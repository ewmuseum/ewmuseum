Never Never Land I: [Part Six]

10 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 10, 2002 What is a pharoah doing in Texas? “Captain!” Captain Hook turned around, stopped by the loud voice which beckoned to speak to him. As he turned, he saw a scraggily looking pirate running in his direction. His pirate’s hat was on a tilt and his shirt was not tucked in. Besides that, it was abhorrently dirty. This was a slacker pirate – the kind that Hook despised. Unfortunately for him, his ship was full of them. “Are you the one calling out my name, you worthless piece of scum?” Hook asked of the pirate. He (uhm, the pirate) stood there, silent and unmoving, afraid to speak again. “I told you to speak, imbecile! You obviously called out my name for a reason.” “Yeeeeesss….captain…” He stuttered along. “We’ve put Lexian and his…plant, in the guest cabin for now. They’re locked up nice and tight.” “And you’re sure they won’t escape?” Hook asks. “Positive, captain…” He says with a smile on his face. “But if they do, we know what to do.” He points to his sword, smiling from ear to ear. Hook chuckles. “I’m sure you do. Let me ask you your name, sailor.” “Gash, sir.” The pirate states proudly. “And may I say it has been a pleasure to be working under your hook.” “Sailor, you make me proud.” Hook says, looking down at his sword. He unsheathes it, slamming it into his gut. “But don’t you ever, ever interrupt me again.” He pulls the sword out, leaving the pirate dead on the deck. Some others come by and pick up the body quickly. “Such a shame…” Hook says walking away. Smee appears from the back of the ship, watching the men carrying Gash’s body away. “What happened to him?” “He interrupted me…” Smee sighed. “Captain, I know you disapprove of rudeness, but that is the fifth man you’ve killed this week. At this rate, we won’t have enough pirates to swab the decks!” “I will not tolerate rudeness, Smee. Never. Not in this lifetime. These pirates need to shape up or ship out, and by god it will be by my hand. Besides, if this ring truly does work, we won’t need pirates, will we?” “I suppose not, Captain.” Hook grabs Smee by the shoulders. “Smee, this is a joyous time for us. We’re finally going to have a chance to take this stinking island away from those nasty Lost boys. And once we discover the truth about the ring, we can make this Pirate Island. It’ll be our Never Never Land. All of the books and fairy tales will have to change.” “I’d love to see that, Captain…” “Of course you would, Smee. Who wouldn’t?” “I just have a quick question. What about Lexian? What about the Lost boys? You know they’re going to try to stop you.” “Of course they will, Smee. But in the end, they will fail. Lexian is as sick as a dog. He can’t fight, not to mention walk. And those Lost boys are no match for the Ghost pirates. Smee, we are on the brink of victory. Let’s go savor it. I want to have a drink with you.” Smee looked shocked. “A drink? With me, captain? We’ve never had a drink together.” “There’s a first for everything, don’t you think?” Hook and Smee walk away, heading towards his cabin. As he does, the scene shifts to a private cabin elsewhere on the ship. You see the door, guarded by a pirate, who is asleep against the hard wood of the ship. Then the scene shifts inside, revealing someone we know quite well. “Bobby…Bobby…” “Wha…” Tom Lexian kicked Bobby the Cactus’ pot once more, trying to wake him up. As he kicked the pot, it rolled farther away from his foot and down towards the door which was directly in front of him. Bobby slammed against the wooden door, in which he responded, “God dammit.” “Sorry,” Lexian admitted, trying to grab the pot with his feet now. He stretched as far as he could, but he couldn’t reach. He sighed, giving up. “Out of all the things you could have done, you decided to kick me over to the door.” “It seemed like a good idea,” Lexian replied, leaning heavily against the wall. “I’m psychokinetic, but I still don’t have hands, Tom. I can’t open the door.” Tom looked around the room, trying to find something else to use to escape their prison. They were locked in a small room, which appeared to be a guest cabin. Tom was chained to the bed, which was directly opposite to the door which seemed miles away to him. He was chained to the bed post with very strong chains. It amazed Tom how he couldn’t even cause a dent in them. His feet were also bound, but with rope. On the walls were fantastic paintings of the Never Never Land waterfront, but Tom couldn’t enjoy them. He was still worried about how they were going to escape. The last thing he remembered was being dragged into the room. He was so groggy, he couldn’t even attempt to fight back. He could feel the pirates tie him to the bed, bind his legs, and then he heard a loud thump. He assumed that was Bobby being thrown back into the cabin with him. Tom wasn’t sure if that was a blessing or a curse. Still, one question remained… …where was Belly and Bats? “Please tell me you’re figuring out a way to get out of here,” Bobby said, flying into the air and landing upright once more. “Quiet,” Tom replied. “I’m still thinking…” “Oh, didn’t we wake up on the wrong side of the bed? I guess that tough guy routine really is working.” Tom scoffed. He wasn’t trying to be tough, but be smart. Nothing would be served by sitting here on their asses, waiting to be saved like a damsel in distress. No, they would have to fend for themselves. After all, he was brought in here to stop Hook, not get saved from him. “Bobby, stop playing around. We need to get out of here.” “YOU need to find a way off of that bed. Need I remind you that you’re still strapped like a mummy in a coffin.” Tom tugged away at the chain, which wouldn’t budge at all. They were strong. He clanged them against the metal pillar of the bed’s legs, which continued to spark. But no dents could be found on the chains themselves. Never Never Land metal was made for eternity. “God dammit,” Lexian cursed, sweating profusely trying to break the chain. “It’s not breaking.” “Well, try harder.” “This isn’t like baking a cake. If I try harder, it will hurt more and I won’t get anywhere.” “Get a clue, Tom. Everything hurts. You can’t give up, okay? I don’t want to be stuck here with that dead body on the bed in this room for the rest of my cactus life.” Tom looked at Bobby oddly. “What are you talking about? I’m not dead.” “Why don’t you take a gander at the beauty on the bed.” Tom looks up, seeing a skeleton laying on the bed. It appears to be a female, as the skeleton is decked out in a dress, but Tom couldn’t be certain. The skeleton was chained to the bed, as if locked up for years and years. The bed spread was completely black, except for a tinge of yellow that seemed to be emitted from the dead body. “She didn’t look like she had a good time…” Tom responded, staring despondently at the corpse. “What do you think she was in for?” “Who knows? These are pirates. They don’t need a reason to kill people.” “Good point.” Lexian pounds the chain once more, still to no avail. Bobby just laughs. “Tom, you’re doing it all wrong. You can’t fight force with force. Use your head.” “Maybe if it wasn’t throbbing so much, I would…” He complained. “I still feel sick. It’s getting better, but this Blueberry disease isn’t agreeing with my body.” “That’s Dewberry.” “Whatever.” A situation so critical, one could light a match and the entire place would burst into flames. One scenario, Tom Lexian doesn’t get out. If he doesn’t he most surely will die along with him, Never Never Land. If he does, there is hope. One can only pray. A girl for every man...and every corpse. Formulate a plan, Tom. As hard as I tried, no ideas came to me. Here I was, the man that always seemed to get out of a pickle like Macgyver in a pretty hairy fix, and I was now trapped in a cabin with my cactus and a corpse. Funny how things like this always seems to come full circle. I banged my hand against the door once more, letting out all of the frustration that was pent up in my chest. Sometimes you need a shot in the arm to wake you up from what appears to be a bad dream. Unfortunately, my dreams are always my own reality. Suddenly, the door swung open, knocking me down to the cold, hard wooden floor of the cabin. As I looked up, I saw a rather nasty looking pirate staring down at me. He unsheathed his sword and pointed its tip right at my throat. I swallowed slowly as I watched the blade come closer and closer to my throat and stared the pirate directly into his eyes. He was around my height, although he seemed to have about forty or fifty pounds on me. His gut hung out from his untucked shirt, revealing a stomach about as hairy as his head. His face looked unclean and his beard was as thick as a lawn which had been through weeks and weeks of unrelenting rain. I could even smell him from where I was sitting. It was the stench of an unshowered body. “What the hell do you think you’re doing?” I demanded, grimacing in pain. “I don’t think you’re in any sort of position to be demanding anything of me,” The pirate answered back with a toothy grin. “If you didn’t notice, I’m holding the sword.” “Lucky for you then,” I retorted. “If I were holding it, you would be on the floor and I would be standing before you.” It was a ballsy thing to say, but I was feeling rather ballsy at the moment. Not only that, but perhaps a little of stupidity. I took a deep breath as I watched his face change from overconfidence to pure rage, and that didn’t bold well for me when he was indeed holding a sharp weapon to my throat. “You will apologize for that,” He answered back slowly. “You will apologize and I will consider not slitting your throat this very second.” I gulped, but tried not to show any fear. “I haven’t even apologized for shoplifting when I was in elementary school, so I know damn well that you won’t be getting an apology any time soon.” He bit his lower lip. “The captain ordered me not to kill you. He wants to do it himself, but I’m willing to take the consequences for the sake of watching you die.” It was flattering to some degree, but I don’t think I should let that get in the way of avoiding a very painful death. The sword was only a few inches from my throat, and even if I surprised him with a quick move, one swipe and it would be all over. “Leave him alone!” Bobby shouted from the bed. That’s it Bobby, threaten the dangerous man with a sword. “Shut up, you stupid plant!” The pirate shouted back. “When I’m through with your keeper, I’m going to cut you into ribbons and feed you to the sharks below!” “Just try it, junior,” Bobby answered. “But you won’t get very far with that little butter knife you’re holding.” I sat there perplexed. What exactly was Bobby trying to prove here? “You’ll see,” He laughed. “Once I’m through with Lexian, you’re dead meat.” “No, you are.” I batted the sword away with my hand, gashing a cut on my right hand. As he tried to balance himself again, I leapt on him and laid an elbow directly to his nose. He yelped out in pain as the blood began to drip onto his shirt and onto mine. I swung at his face again, but he blocked it, and knocked me clear across the room with a right cross of his own. I fell backwards, holding my jaw which felt like it had been unhinged. As I stared up at the wall, I could feel my head spinning and I was quickly losing my bearings. I could hear him getting back to his feet and getting ready to pounce on me. “Owwww!” He yelled out in pain. As I opened my eyes, I saw him pull out a spine from his back and throw it to the ground. “I’ll get you, you nasty plant!” He bolted towards Bobby with his sword drawn, getting ready to slice and splice my poor little companion down, but I got back to my feet and stopped him. We struggled for the sword momentarily, but he laid a kick to my chest which sent me flying again. This time his attention was solely directed to me, and I had nowhere to run. “You don’t know how much I’m going to enjoy this,” He said, bring the sword above his head. “I’ll see you in hell…” WHAP! The pirate fell to the ground unconscious right before my eyes. I looked up to see a skeletal corpse standing there with Bobby in her hand. “Well, I guess he should think twice before threatening a cactus again, huh?” The corpse said. “You bet,” Bobby said. “Now, would you mind putting me back down?” “Oh, sure.” The corpse placed Bobby back onto the hard wooden deck next to me. I stared at the corpse with a blank look on my face, unable to say a word. She still had long blonde hair, but she was nothing but bone after that. Empty, drunken eye sockets and grayish teeth made up her face. At least she was wearing a dress – not that she was hiding anything. “Tom, what’s wrong?” I looked over at Bobby with an incredulous look. “Did you just ask me that? What the hell do you think is wrong? I’m standing in front of a corpse!” “Uhm, excuse me, but I prefer to be called one of the undead.” Great, a politically correct corpse. Since when did we play that game here in Never Never Land? “Yeah Tom, what’s your problem?” Bobby asked. I gave Bobby a stern look. “Look, I’m sorry. I just haven’t ever seen one of the undead before.” “I’m sure you have,” She answered. “You just didn’t know it. Let me introduce myself. My name’s Hilda. And you are Tom, correct?” “How did you know that?” Hilda just shrugged. “Well, I was just laying there on the bed while you two were talking. I may be dead but my ears work just fine.” “So you know why we’re here then?” She smirked and began pacing around. “Tell me if I got the gist of it. Let’s see, you were brought here by Tinkerbell on behalf of Peter Pan to come to Never Never Land and stop Hook from taking the legendary ring which unleashes the Ghosts of Never Never Land and simultaneously gives Hook control of the entire world. You were later captured and brought here where you were going to be executed at Hook’s discretion.” “Whoa.” Bobby said, obviously amazed. “And you also leave the toilet up, which really annoys Nat.” “Whoa.” Bobby and I said in unison. “But what are you doing here?” I inquired. “What were you doing chained to that bed?” She sat down on the floor with us and sighed. “I’m not even sure how long ago it was, but I guess it must have been at least thirty years. You see, I’m a Corean. I live underground with the rest of my people because we were tired of being treated like dogs here on the surface with all of the pirates and lost boys. Well, after a long time, Coreans became deathly afraid of the light and we loved the dark. I was a little different though. Ever since I was a little child, I would sneak out from the encampment and take long walks out onto the forest and watch life go by on the surface. One day, during one of my routine trips, I was stopped by a pirate raid. They were destroying a local village which apparently didn’t pay their taxes to Hook. I was captured and brought directly to Hook.” “Weren’t you scared?” Bobby asked. “I should have been, but Corealians live a fairly boring life. I spend a majority of my day attending to plants and vegetables while preparing a local band of boys for their annual choir performances. But anyways, Hook saw me and immediately recognized that I was a Corealian. We usually have pretty pale skin.” Well, obviously. “He wanted me to tell him the secret locations to the Corealian home base, but I wouldn’t. I knew that if he ever got a hold of that information that he would go out and enslave all of my people. I couldn’t let that happen, so I did not reveal anything to him.” “That was pretty brave of you,” I said, a bit of respect being paid to her. “I suppose,” She replied. “You should have seen the way the pirates were laughing and hounding at Hook when they realized that he wouldn’t be able to break me. Hook has a way at getting whatever he wants, and he was fairly annoyed that I embarrassed him and made a mockery of his leadership.” “So he locked you in this room…” “Unfortunately, yes,” She said. “I remember staring up at the ceiling and wondering when in the hell someone would come and rescue me. I guess it was just wishful thinking. No one knew that I was here and I had kept my whereabouts from my family. I was left here to die.” “But why aren’t you passed on?” Bobby asked. “I mean, you’re just a walking skeleton.” “You ever watch that television show, ‘Unsolved Mysteries?’” “Yeah.” “It’s sort of like that,” She said. “I remember the exact moment when I died. There was a bright light at the end of this tunnel, and I was running towards it. Just as I was getting there, I stopped running, and started walking backwards. I realized that I can’t let it end like this. That would have meant that Hook had won, and I couldn’t let that happen. As God as my witness, I was going to make Hook’s life a living hell at no matter the cost.” “Wow,” I said. “You had a destiny.” “I sure did,” She replied. “That is until I realized that I was locked in here. You two were the first prisoners placed in this cabin since I was left here. I guess Hook began his new policy of immediate executions, but he was saving you two for some much more dramatic event.” “Lucky us,” Bobby joked. “Anyways, I’m sure he’s forgotten that I was ever here. Even so, I haven’t forgotten anything, and I’m going to help you two escape out of here and we’re going to take down Hook.” “How are we going to do that?” I asked. “I mean, we can’t even find our way out of this cabin, let alone take on Hook. Hell, I still haven’t met with Peter Pan yet.” Suddenly, a bright light flashed before us and a man in a sky blue cloak appeared. He had a long white beard and a cane. “Did someone call for a miracle?” I suppose I did.
