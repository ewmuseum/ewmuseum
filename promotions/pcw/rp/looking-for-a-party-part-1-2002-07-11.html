Looking For A Party [Part 1]

11 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07/11/02 City: New Orleans, LouisianaTime: 1:23 AMDate: 07/12/02 As the time to PCW Tuesday Turmoil nears, the more the pressure weighs in on Jordan X Shabazz’s shoulders. Ever since his first PCW Pay Per View debut loss to Mark Makonie, people have been saying that he suffers from the pressure. The pressure that weighs in on his shoulders whenever he has a title shot, or a HUGE match. John Crichton and the Television Title are two things that have been on Jordan X Shabazz’s mind. But in order to take the pressure off of his shoulders, he went out to a club to party. Jordan saw it as the only way to get his mind off of the match. Going to this club. The problem was, the club was in the heart of Louisiana, New Orleans, and Jordan was lost. The scene opens up with a shot of the dark skylines of New Orleans. When you look down from afar, you see all the lights blazing, advertising this and that. As we get lower to the streets of the nice during the day naughty during the night city that is New Orleans, we realize that we are in the seedy part of town. The place is a mess. Syringes on the floor, hookers patrolling the streets looking for a John, drug dealers sitting on their stoops waiting for the junkies to come around. It truly is a sad case. We suddenly see a black rented Cadillac Deville 2002 pull up on the street. This is a shock for the people in the neighborhood. Nice car usually meant the pimp, head dealer, or rap superstar returning to his roots. But in this vehicle is none of the three. Instead, in this very vehicle, the members of the PCW’s newest stable, The Uprising, sit in the car. Yeah, I know, we’ve all been lost before, and usually you are told to stay in a lighted area, in a good neighborhood. But Shabazz and crew are doing the exact opposite, practically begging to be robbed. Jordan peers out through the front window of his car, looking for some sign of life. He spots it when a young looking teenage male run up to him, both hands in his black hoody. Jordan lowers his window to speak to the kid. Jordan X Shabazz: “Hello, I’m lo…” Before Jordan can ask the kid for some directions, he is cut off. The kid looks at him wearily, as if he hasn’t slept in hours. Kid: “What do you need, sir? I got a dime bag, a…” Jordan looks in the back seat where Rush Mason sits polishing his Extreme Title, even though it’s very dark. Jordan X Shabazz: “No the fuck…” Rush Mason: (Still polishing the title belt) “Yeah he did. He thinks we’re here to buy drugs.” Jordan looks back at the boy in utter shock. He was sure that when they drove up that they were on Martin Luther King Boulevard. He raised his head out of the car window and he looked at the street sign. It said Martin Luther King Boulevard, as he expected. Jordan X Shabazz was now incensed. He gets out of the car and he walks back to the sign. The kid that was leaning into the window of his car asking him what kind of drugs he wanted to purchase now, is following Jordan towards the sign. Jordan X Shabazz: (Angry) “YOU SEE THAT? WHAT DOES THAT SAY?” Jordan grabs the kid by the front of his hoody, bringing very closely to the sign. Jordan X Shabazz: “Read the for me.” Rush Mason and Thug roC are in the car, doing different things. Thug roC is bouncing to the Nas playing in the car, and Rush, obsessively, is shining his Extreme title, trying to take away the dirt that has devalued the gold. The kid stammers out an answer. Kid: “M-Martin Luther K-King Boulevard.” Jordan looks back at the kid. Jordan X Shabazz: “And you’re out here selling drugs on this street?” The kid looks saddened, sort of like saying ‘I have no other choice.’ Jordan speaks again before the kid can reply. Jordan X Shabazz: “You’re selling drugs on Martin Luther King Junior street. You know what that does? That’s a slap in the face to Malcolm, Martin, Marcus, and myself. We work hard so young punks like you can walk the streets without getting stopped by the police. You’re the reason that I’m forced to work hard in the wrestling business to try and win some gold to gain some credibility.” As soon as Jordan says the last few words, the kid is shocked and confronts him. Kid: “Uh… why do you need credibility.” Jordan looks the other way, completely shocked. He turns back to the kid. Jordan X Shabazz: “No the f*ck you didn’t! I can’t believe you said that… I NEED the Television Title for credibility. Credibility that I’ll use to get your black ass an education. That way, as Television champion, I’ll be all over your screen, advertising N.I.G.G.A and Civil Rights in general!” The kid looks at Jordan as if he’s crazy, and Jordan thinks about what he is saying verbatim from the point of view of a 15 year old. He lets go of the kid’s shirt. Jordan X Shabazz: “You can leave…. And if I EVER catch you selling drugs on Martin Luther King Boulevard ever again, I’ll murder you with my bare hands.” It wasn’t the best thing to say to the youth. But it would suffice. Jordan X Shabazz was steaming mad that the legacy of a man that has helped so many, has turned into a mockery. A disgrace. A horrible place to hang out at nights. Shabazz understood that people had to do what they had to do to make money, especially if they were black. But if you’re going to sell drugs, don’t sell it on the land where a man who literally gave his LIFE for you has a commemorative street. Jordan X Shabazz walks back to his vehicle where Rush now sits in the front seat with Thug roC at his side and a map being shown of the city of New Orleans. Rush uses a pin to darken a certain spot. Rush Mason: “Get in the back, Shabby, we’re going to a party tonight.” Jordan X Shabazz begrudgingly moves to the backseat as Rush starts up the vehicle, and they head off into the night, going to this important party.
