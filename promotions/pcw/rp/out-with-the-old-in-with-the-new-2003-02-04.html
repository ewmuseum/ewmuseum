Out With the Old, In With the New

4 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/4/03 Out With the Old, In With the New The scene fades in with a pile of smoke and fire thickening within the deep wood near Romantico’s palace. The flames are heavy and bright and are seen ripping through everything in site. Deep within the heart of the fire are long handwritten pages, slowly turning into dust. As the flames flicker, El Romantico appears behind the large pile, just throwing in paper after paper. As a deeper look is taken to the paper seem to be poetry written by a younger El Romantico. Everything from verse, to rhythm, to script, to narrative, were all just burning away? The fire reflected in the eyes of El Romantico giving him a demon look to himself. Romantico seemed so focused and determined to kill these old memories that not even a bullet to the head would cause him to break it. What are you doing, sir? Romantico turns around with ink slipping from the surface on his finger do to the deep heat next to him. Romantico sees a small little boy with very rancid clothing and in poor conditions. As he drops his eyes he sees feet filled with mud and bloody toes. What brings you here? I saw a fire and I was cold. I thought maybe I could come here and add some warmth to my frigid body. May I please gain some heat, kind sir? I am very cold and very hungry. I will not ask for anything more and do not request a dime. Just some warm air is all that I am after. Do as you please, I do not mind your presence as long as you do not make oneself a disturbance. The boy drops on his knees and kisses of shiny beautiful shoes of El Romantico. Romantico swipes him off and steps back, looking right back at his fire. Romantico picks up another pile of papers when he is interrupted. I could not thank you any more, kind sir. This means so much to me. As I said, do not disturb me. I will stick you back in the slum you came from. I am sure not a soul would miss your horrid body. I am sorry. The time passes as Romantico continues to throw in paper after paper. Romantico does it so quickly and thoroughly that he forgets how big the flames are getting, and burns his finger. BLOODY MARY! The boy jumps up from his position near the fire, and grabs Romantico’s hand placing a leaf around it and tying it tightly with a strand of straw. He drains the blood and holds it lightly over the flame to clean it. That should make it better sir. Romantico surprisingly looks over at the boy. This time instead of going back to his duties, he asks the boy a question. Child, where has such a lower class boy as yourself learned the skills of a doctor or medical professional. By no means is it really that much of a big deal. You see my mother was a great doctor back in Madrid. Many went to her and she was rich and famous. That was until a terrible disaster took place. What disaster do you speak of? About one year ago my mother had just disappeared. My father has already died in the service, and no orphanages would take me in. So now I live here day by day, but do not be fooled for I am an educated child. It is just the way things turn out some times. As Romantico hears the stories about a boy and his mother his heart drops and his emotions show. He tries to work past them and keeps on his tough and rigid look, not breaking at all. Well not everything turns out like you want it to. I certainly agree with that. Romantico takes a deeper look at him as he shakes and pivots over and over. His lips are blues and his eyes look up practically drained of life. How do you live out here, day by day? I take it one moment at a time. Romantico takes a sudden flashback as his body trembles from the last remark “So how do you survive out here?” said Romantico’s first recruit, who picked him up off of the streets in Barcelona. “I take it one moment at a time.” I sharply and sadly replied. Back in the previous scene with the fire, Romantico stands their lifeless. Are you all right sir? I am fine. Romantico shakes himself off and takes out five hundred euros. He places it next to the boy and then walks off into the woods. Things will get better. Gracias senor, this must be an act of God. It’s an act by myself, not one by God. Romantico then walked off into the forest, contemplating the way he is leading his life.
