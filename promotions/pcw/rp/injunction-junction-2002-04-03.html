Injunction Junction.

3 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4.3.02 “All rise! Circuit court for district 11-2-3 is now in session, the honorable Judge Waylan Meyers, presiding.” The bailiff finishes his speech just as the judge walks through the door into the courtroom. He stops before stepping onto the bench and looks about the room. We rejoin our Big and Bold hero, Big Alan Steele, as he stands impatiently next to his trusted manager, Sue Saksnussum. He fidgets about, shifting his weight, his eyes darting across the room at the adjacent table. There stand a pale pack of lawyers, all with dark hair slicked back, all with matching black suits and charcoal ties. If one were to look quickly, they might be mistaken as identical. All face the judge with impassive expressions, none even blinking. The judge sits, prompting everyone to also take their seats. Steele growls, again stealing a glance across. One of the lawyers catches Big Al’s eye and lightly hisses, a fang where his eye-tooth should be… Steele eyes widen in alarm as he tugs at the sleeve of Sue. Big Alan Steele: (whispering) “Lady Saksnussum! I believe our adversaries to be… unholy creations from the fiery abyss of Hades!” Sue: (harsh whisper) “What do you want? They’re lawyers! Now hush up so I can pull this thing off!” The judge settles on his bench and adjusts his robes to comfort before proceeding. A court representative takes his cue and slaps a manila folder on his bench. The judge whips out a set of glasses and opens the folder. Judge: “Okay, let’s see here… Steele, Alan vs. Healey, Dr. Rupert. An injunction, eh? What seems to be the case here Miss… Saksnussum?” Sue: (standing) “Well, your honor, first off I would like to express my dismay at your granting of the Healey lawyers a court room time of 7 pm. I find this to be extremely detrimental to my case, your honor, which required the utmost expediency. All that was accomplished was allowing them time to bring forth all sorts of legal gymnastics to the table.” One of the gaunt lawyers from the other side stands, addressing the judge. His words are smooth, lulling all into a kind of stupor. Lawyer: “Your honor, my colleagues and I take great offence at Ms. Saksnussum's accusations. We merely requested the time as we all suffer from a very rare form of albinism, making sunlight dangerous for us. As far as ‘legal gymnastics’, we find the term itself preposterous and we aim to show merely facts that shall prove their case to be frivolous beyond a doubt.” Sue: (rolling eyes) “That’s ridiculous!” Lawyer: “We have claimed this as under the Federal Disabilities Act Amendment of 1999, which includes our particular skin condition among its listings.” Judge: “Ms. Saksnussum, he is right, I was forced to grant this time to the Healey team and I ask you to stand down on your position, lest you be held in contempt. Now, please get on with this.” Sue: (sighing) “Very well, your honor. My client, Alan Steele, was illegally released from his contractual duties on Sunday evening, May 31, 2002. As will be shown in subsequent proceedings on the matter, he was released not because of poor performance or any other negative job related problems, but solely on the basis of illegally leveraging my client into a contract of lower wage scale.” The lawyer on the other side stands, finger raised in the air. Lawyer: “Objection, your honor. He was not illegally fired. The clause was written into his contract over 8 moths ago, a fault entirely on the head of his MANAGER. Furthermore, we merely offered him a separate contract upon his release to KEEP him with the company. Not leverage. Truthfully, we are pleased with his participation in Primetime Championship Wrestling or without.” Sue: (yelling) “Liars! That clause was not a license to fire people on a whim! It was intended for use only when PCW was in true financial crisis, as in Chapter 11!” Lawyer: “Your honor, the clause was indeed intended for PCW in financial crisis, but not in bankruptcy. Instead, it’s intent was for times of PCW being in the RED, not necessarily meeting the stringent requirements of bankruptcy in a court of law.” Sue: “Bah! Then I should hope that they would be able to produce the books from PCW accounting offices that would show conclusively that PCW was, in fact, losing money at the time of Alan Steele’s release.” Lawyer: (coughing) “Well, you honor, I’m afraid that is impossible. You see, there was an unfortunate fire in accounting…” Sue: (screaming) “A fire the local Marshall labeled as ‘suspicious’ in his report!” Lawyer: (hands up, shrugging) “Your honor, the fire is beyond the control of my defense team and the investigation is ongoing. We cannot be held accountable for that as such.” Sue: (red faced) “But all the more reason to grant the injunction allowing my charge to continue wrestling at his former wage scale. This court needs more time to find out all the mitigating circumstances surrounding his departure form PCW.” Lawyer: “But there isn’t irrefutable proof…” Sue: “You don’t NEED irrefutable proof in an injunction! Just probable injustice on Healey’s part!” BANG! BANG! BANG! Judge: “Order! Order in the court! I won’t have the two sides engaging in a mud fight. I would appreciate if each would allow the other to speak at length before presenting a counter-point.” Steele again steals a glance across the courtroom, again catching the same eye of the lawyer before. The lawyer grins, revealing his odd fangs, glistening in the light. The man then runs his thumb across his throat, his long black and pointed fingernail tracing a second smile, before discretely pointing back at Big Al. Steele grumbles and tugs at Sue’s sharp looking outfit. BAS: “Sue! Yon lawyer is undoubtedly possessed by the demon of Hades! I can see it in his deadened eyes, and smell it in the courtroom!” Sue: “Smell it? All I can smell is their overpowering aftershave… Aqua Velva! Yuck!” BAS: (taking in deep breath) “Smells like… DEATH!” The other lawyer at the head of the table gestures grandly at our two heroes. Lawyer: “Your honor, this is clearly a case of disgruntled workers trying to get a manner of revenge on a man that they feel wholly responsible for their less than dignified departure from PCW – Doctor Rupert Healey. I submit that this case has no merit and that you squash their attempt of getting back into PCW for possible sabotage of shows, other PCW workers and making a negative impression altogether on revenues. Your honor, do not allow these ingrates to carry on their almost assuredly terroristic plots.” Sue: “WHAT?! Then why is it Steele was offered a contract immediately after?!” Judge: “Yes, I would also like that explained.” Lawyer: “Simple. PCW would have liked to continue his employment at the status he has EARNED – low card, thereby earning a low card wage scale. It was for PCW’s current financial benefit.” Big Alan Steele roars, knocking over the table, stunning those gathered. Sue stammers in slack jawed confusion. The lawyers of Doc Healey turn as one, tight smiles etched into the faces. The bailiff pulls out his gun. The judge bangs his gavel hard on the bench. Judge: “ORDER! Order in my court! Bailiffs, restrain that man!!” The bailiffs start forward towards Steele, but a cold stare leaves them frozen in their tracks. Steele points a meaty finger at the apposing lawyers. BAS: “Lord of Justice, I beseech you to grant me the authority to rid this world of the foul villainy perpetuated by this brood of contemptuous demons, flaunting the very gifts that the gods of Olympus bestow upon the Earth! I beg of you to grant me the power to rend their un-living souls back to the bosom of their creator, Hades, thus purging the lands of their dark, and malicious deeds! Hi-Keeba!” Judge: “Wha-?” Sue: (mortified) “Uh… he believes the defense team to be blood-sucking vampiric undead, your honor.” Judge: “Well, what does he want? They’re lawyers.” Lawyer: (grinning evilly) “Your honor! This is the same type of behavior that can be expected of this character in the future should you allow him to continue to work in PCW! This simply is unacceptable!” BAS: (growling) “Silence, foul scourge of decency!” Judge: “Mr. Steele, you stand DOWN! Should you continue your tirades, I shall be forced to weigh in favor of Mr. Healey’s team of lawyers! Then you won’t be getting paid the full amount of the original contract!” BAS: (waving wildly at the judge) “Tis not about the MONEY! I care not for the root of all evil! I wish to continue my ascension of the rungs of PCW’s Olympus unfettered by the tyrannical pitfalls of a madman seduced by his wanton power!” Judge: “Wait a minute… let me get this straight…” Sue: (shrill, ear piercing) “AL! You moron! I care about the money!!! It’s important!!” Lawyer: “See?! If the money did not truly matter, and it was only the thrill of competition, then Steele surely could have resigned right away!” Judge: “Indeed. The clause in the contract, though a bit vague, can be interpreted as when the company is losing money, not in bankruptcy…” Sue: “Oh no…” The lawyers on the other side grin maliciously, the one making eye contact with Steele during the injunction hearing licking his lips. Steele’s eyes narrow dangerously. Judge: “… and the plaintiff was offered another chance to sign.” Sue: (sputtering) “But the contract clearly states…” Lawyer: “Your honor! Not only was he offered a contract to resign, but the opportunity to RECLAIM his old contract, via a wrestling match which he, regrettably, lost handily.” BAS: (booming) “Lies! Twas fixed!!” Lawyer: “If you were as good as you claim, you would have won.” Judge: “It’s all inconsequential. I have to rule in favor of the defense.” BANG! BANG! BANG! The judge slams down his gavel. Sue: “NOOOOO!! My money!” Lawyer: “Excellent. And now, you, ‘Big’ Alan Steele... We have a special gift for you from the venerable Doctor…” The group of ‘lawyers’ extend their claws and display their glistening fangs, hissing at our hero. BAS: “Hi-Keeba!” Lawyer: “Feel the power of attorney!” Steele rips a leg off the overturned table, snapping it across his knee, forming two sharp, wooden stakes. Big Al crouches low and circles around, the group of seething lawyers following suit. Seemingly at some unspoken signal, Big Al and the lawyers leap at each other… Bailiff: “All rise!” Steele stops short, poised over a lawyer, wooden stake almost piercing his blackened, unbeating heart. The lawyers stop too, one with his mouth wide, fangs shining next to the exposed neck of Big Al. Bailiff: “This session of circuit court for district 11-2-3 has ended.” Steele and the lawyers slowly separate, eyes wary. Big Al backs towards Sue, putting an arm in front of her. BAS: (snarling) “You and your accursed kin have won this round, but you have not heard the last of Big Alan Steele! And by the gods I’ll give you all what for!! Hi-Keeba!” Lawyer: (straightening tie) “We await the challenge, Mr. Steele. Until next time.” BAS: “Soon… so very soon…”
