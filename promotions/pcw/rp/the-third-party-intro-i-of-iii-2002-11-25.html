The Third Party (Intro: I of III)

25 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 25.11.02 It's quite obvious: Abner is the man. Is there any known being cooler than a redneck/ex-con/cult leader-turned pro-wrestler? Ha, I think not. I mean, c'mon! This guy is amazing……“Quite the character” is an understatement! And of course, if you'd care to dispute this point, he'd inevitably reply, 'That's horseshit!' And indeed, what you said would in fact be horseshit! Sweet, huh? You see, just don't argue with His Ab-nesty. With cunning replies like that, you can't but help but have respect for this 'Hick from the Sticks'. And many people do..... And so, to start off our intriguing tale of redneck fantasies, let us begin with a brief history of our heroes past..... Shall we? Abner, overlooked, and underrated by the masses was destined, yes, destined to become a success. You see, no matter how little he tried, and no matter how many times he failed in life, he couldn't possibly be as big a failure as what society expected him to be. And as far as 'professional' wrestling goes, by his late teen years, none of the locals in his area could stand in his wake. As far as one could tell, from his initial appearance, this tobacco-chewing, beer-drinking, bar-hoppin' brawler wouldn't get very far in life. But those who knew him better knew otherwise. This small-town, subsistence farmer from the backwoods of Vermont was due to rise up in the world of professional wrestling, and for better or for worse, that's exactly what he intended to do. Abner had educated himself out of the depths of redneck society. Numerous winter nights were spent curled up with a book in hand, lying on the cold, hard wood of an old metal spring mattress. Up and down, up and down he would bounce; the old, almost worthless piece of furniture was no friend of his. His parents' creaking log cabin was also of no use. But somehow, and only God knows how, Abner had survived. Abner mastered the three ‘R’s’ of a good ol’-fashioned Yankee education: readin’, ‘ritin’, and ‘rithmatic, at a fairly young age. With a (somewhat) complete understanding of basic education, as he grew older, it was apparent Abner wasn't dead last on the intellectual totem pole…..but certainly on the lower regions. But as the years went by, he got smarter and smarter, and before Abner knew it, years after his family dubbed his knowledge as 'worthless', this 'good-for-nothin’ ”, had made something of himself….. Abner was off to......hmmm.....college? No silly, the bar! Yes, that’s correct, the bar! And yes, the mood of the story changes here..... That's right, starting at the beginning of his sophomore year at Hicksville High, Abner was officially the school drunk, and boy, was he proud. Those were some of the most self-fulfilling moments in his whole life. Looking back now, Abner takes pride in his accomplishments. So yeah, as any halfway intelligent human being would guess, Abner never made it to college. Night after night of late hour partying and drinking with friends, put Abner in quite a delicate position. Tending to his studies was now a secondary concern to him, and his grades reflected this change in heart. But of course, there were always early signs of this…… From his childhood terrorizing of the neighbor's dogs, to the famous 'playground incident' on the first day of kindergarten, Abner's folks knew that there son had an.....er.....umm......rebellious side. You see, his parents, though they were decent enough folks, didn’t really pressure their child hard enough. Oh sure, there was the average elementary school lecturing, and of course the typical fatherly-son reprimanding......but none of it ever really paid off. Abner's mother was very level-headed, but she was too easy going. For example, one time Abner threw a lighted M-80 into the neighbor's window, and for a single night, Abner was severely punished: he went without desert. But the truth be known, later on his mother finally snuck him in a piece of pie….. Abner's father was kind of a lazy, ignorant, welfare drunk…..yeah. And though he was never a “terrible” parent, he wasn't a very good one either. He supported his son's love of the booze in his (crucial) teen years, and more than once bought a keg for Abner and Company......So nice people, sucky parents. A common story in present day America. So it's pretty safe to say that early on in life, Abner had no good role models to look up to. And Abner, like the rest of his generation, was fairly independent-minded. Besides from the typical teenage pop-cult influences (which all new-age children give into), Abner really was himself. No one could control, motivate or even influence the independent being in Abner. Quite a stubborn individual you know. And what a guy this individual turned out to be….. Seeing the other average Vermont families of the day turning toward a more urban, civilized society, Abner inevitably turned in the opposite direction. His counter-establishment, anti-authoritarian values threw him across the opposite side of the fence…..and landed him in the house of a pit-bull. The authorities, parental, civil or even of peer groups, couldn’t control him And his natural attraction to all things involving war, death or violence, made him even more of a rebel. And rebels, as you know, get in trouble…… Abner’s eventual fate would be to end up in prison….. Everything just seemed to point in that direction. Instead of making good, well-educated decisions, he did what his ‘gut’ told him to. And his choice in friends was horrendous: he chose to hang out with the other town “hoodlums”. With war always on his mind, the rowdy rednecks and hicks of old Vermont made far more preferable company than the intellectual ‘bore-whores’ as Abner so affectionately called the intellectuals of his high school. You see, Abner really was a natural anarchist. Independent, emotional, and somewhat mentally clouded, Abner was always one step ‘ahead’ of the establishment. The boys loved him…..the gals really loved him…..and the authorities loved to hate him. As fate would have it, Abner turned out to be a rebellious teenager who somehow managed to bring his pre-adult principles (or lack thereof) ahead with him into the ‘real world’. Hehe…..as if his childhood were a dream. But anyway, Abner was certainly a revolutionary. A rebel without a cause some may say, and I just the opposite. But perhaps he’s most accurately, as put by music star Tom Petty, a “rebel without a clue”. For Abner really was, and still is, a clueless, brainless, militant rebel….or is he? But at least, to give the devil his due, Abner thinks that what he does is right….. ……And Abner thought hanging out in certain “social groups” was the right thing for him to do….. Now whether official, unofficial, or even superficial, Abner wanted to be part of group that caused destruction. And that group was called the Third Party….. To His-Abnesty, Third Party was a childhood dream come true. It was a physical embodiment of all that Abner held to be true. Meeting in a stranger’s old brown shed, ten o’ clock, on every Thursday night, a group of buddies would get together and engage in relatively ‘unusual’ activities. Odd rituals would be performed, scheduled fights would take place periodically, and some men would be made, and others broken. It was quite a spectacle. For a newcomer, the experience of “the Club” was highlighted by a certain joy: the senses being touched in the most fascinating of ways. A two-sided (I’ll explain later), slightly insane individual, Cornelius Greyhend, along with Abner started this extraordinary “club”. “Corn” as they called him, was the brains of the operation, and the young, resourceful Abner, was the bronze. Many a young Vermont, teenage redneck would be recruited by Abner. Let it also be noted at this time, grade-wise as unsuccessful, and unimpressive he was, Abner had a great deal of common sense, was a relatively good “people” person (with hicks anyway….). Besides for the hicks, college students, bar-hoppers, and rowdies of all shapes and sizes would come to join this most peculiar “club”. Third Party was a successful business…..but what exactly was it? Third Party was an object of many faces. Consisting, at its peak, of close to sixty members, it was a relatively large “family”. Officially, it was defined as an “intellectual discussion group”, “sports club”, and a “community recreational club”. But on the inside, it was nothing short of a hotbed of dissident anarchy…..and spiritual corruption. Third Party engaged its members with near religious tones. And consequently, those who entered the Party were loyal, and they were loyal to the grave….. All except Abner that is: the first one of the few who ever tried to leave the Party, and the only one to leave it “unharmed”. Why did Abner leave? Well, deep into the Party’s second year of being, the two founding fathers had a slight…..disagreement. One night, after hours of drinking and “prayer”, two of the members engaged in an unscheduled fight. Needless to say, the results were brutal. The climax was the gruesome act of one member smashing his chair over the skull of another. So, as you might guess, Cornelius, the “intellectual” of the two was appalled. He took this specific incident seriously, and the club even more seriously. The disgusted Corn pushed for the abolishment of the “sports” and “recreational” aspects of the club, in favor of a purely “intellectual” one. “Intellectual” to your typical Third Party member of course, consisted of nothing more than drunken talk of how the government “sucks”, how the Sox could “whup” the “Yankees’ “arse”, and how “Bass Masters” should be featured on network programming. You know, typical native (or is it naïve?) Vermont conversational subjects. But some of them took it seriously. A few intelligent members, mostly college students that is, felt a deep devotion to the intellectual aspects of the Party. But not all. After heated discussion on the issue, Abner, backed by a handful of hicks, decided to “override” their intellectual leader. And there was no room for compromise….. With knowledge on diplomacy known only on a minimal (and at that, militant) level for these people, there was little hope of compromise. Abner, the rowdy redneck that he was, only came to the club to “have a good fight”, and many others felt that way too. A quarter of the club backed Abner, a quarter Cornelius, and about half were neutral (or too drunk to choose sides). Come time for the final decision, they decided to finish it the Third Party way: a wrestling match. No-holds-barred, one-on-one, no outside interference was the only way to go. And so that’s what the match was. And boy, what a match it turned out to be, and what a turning point in the Ab’s life….. Abner, a young kid of 19 at the time, seemingly had things going in his favor. Though the twenty-five year-old Corn was the elder, Abner had a lot more experience. Corn had never fought (in a Third Party ring that is) in his life, while Abner on the other hand fought every opportunity he had. Over the last two years, Abner became someone who was comparatively a seasoned veteran. He started out just slightly above average, but quickly matured. By the time the night of the match was set, Abner had the best record in Third Party, at 49-11, and hadn’t lost in over six months. But Cornelius Grayhend had something in-store for Abner….. Now, for the first time in this hick’s life, his skills were truly about to be tested…..
