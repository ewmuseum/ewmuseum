Warcraft: Tides of Lexian [Part One]

11 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 11, 2002 Playing Video Games As An Adult I had been up all night, in a world where the sun set once every three days, and I was surrounded by swash buckling pirates and children that never aged. While that may sound appealing to some, I could deal without the weirdness all the time. Wait a moment, I don’t think I was supposed to remember that. Aliens and their time technology were supposed to wipe away all of that, right? Right? We’ll just pretend like we forgot again. (Begin forgetful phase) I awoke in my bed, staring up at a ceiling that looked like it could have used a cleaning thirteen years ago. The once sparkling white tiles that made up the surface were now charred black with soot and dirt, much of which was permanently etched into existence there. I wish I had kept up with the cleaning, but I had other important duties to conduct – like taking out the trash. The truth was that I was never a heavy chore freak, although Nat was. Speaking of the goddess herself, I hadn’t seen her all night. She was working a case downtown, covering the local gang activity that has increased in the Market district. I thought it might be dangerous for her down there so I bought her a can of pepper spray to use in case she got in trouble. She looked at me with those beautiful eyes of hers and told me if she got in trouble, she would just use her head, not a can of herbs. So she left it here, much to my chagrin, and now I carry it. Sure, I’m a wrestler, but an advantage is always needed. I got up from my bed and looked around. Light was slowly streaming in from the window, and from the looks of it, it looked like a beautiful day. A great day to go out for a quick jog and just enjoy the scenery. Ring Ring! That’s when the phone rang. I looked over at my clock to see that it was only 7:30 am. Who in the world would be calling me at this god awful hour? It had better not be a telemarketer, because that would just ruin my day. The worst part of it was that you could hang up on them, which amounts to a certain amount of satisfaction, but in the end, there are a hundred more where that one came from. I stretched a bit and got up to pick up the phone, which was sitting on the edge of the bed. The battery read low, but I knew I would forget to hang it back up to recharge. I huffed into the phone and wiped the crust from my eyes. “Hu-llo.” I murmured into the phone. “Hey Tom, you sound pretty shitty.” Now, I wonder who could be greeting me in such a manner. “Sean, is that you?” “Yeah dipwad,” Sean replied. “It’s frickin’ 7:30 am in the morning. What are you still doing in bed.” “Jacking off, Sean.” “I knew it.” Sean O’Reilly – or the Impaler, as I called him. No one knew what the meant, except us, because it was an early joke from the early days of my wrestling career. He was always a free spirit, and had this asinine idea about getting up as early as you could to enjoy the day. I never got up before 10:00 am, although this was a pretty damn fine day to be getting up early for. “What are you up to?” I replied, trying to keep the conversation alive. Don’t you hate this awkward pauses? “Well, I was just getting up and I was having myself a cup of coffee…” “Yeah…” “And I was thinking to myself, it’s been a long time since I talked to Tom,” He said, continuing on with his rather pointless story. “I thought it would be nice just to talk and reminisce about old times.” “Get to the point.” “I want to play Warcraft II right now.” Click. I dropped the phone on the floor and ran over to my office desk to boot up my computer. As I watched the Windows 98 screen flash before my eyes, my hands shook with anticipation. Excuse my excitement, but this wasn’t just any game we’re talking about here. It was WARCRAFT II. WHOO! Now, I know what you’re thinking. You’re saying to yourself, “I can’t believe Tom Lexian, Legend Inc. himself, would be getting excited playing a five year old video game. He’s a wrestler, a legend…he should be out winning more titles!” Well, screw that. I’m going to play Warcraft II. You see, me and Sean used to be WCII (That’s the shorter acronym that only cool ‘Crafter vets use) gods, and we’d rule over the Kali players that claimed to be superior. They thought they were cool because they paid a twenty dollar Kali registration fee to beat up on other poor, unsuspecting souls, but little did they know that myself and Sean were pretty damn good as well. Not only that, but also happen to have twenty dollars to blow that week. The strategy of it all was just completely mesmerizing. Orc or human? The races were supposed to mimic each other, but we all knew that wasn’t true. Orcs had an incredible advantage in bloodlust, a spell that caused triple – YES TRIPLE – the damage done to your opponent. What was the humans response to this? SLOW!?! The ‘slow’ spell was useless, although it was generally agreed that some of the best players were those who could ravage the Orcish hordes using the Noble humans. Of course, I went the easy route. Sean wasn’t so smart. He was hell bent on proving that he was the best, so he went about mastering the humans. I guess we made a good team, balancing out the spells and knowing our game plan well enough that not even the most seasoned duos could collapse us. I opened up my CD-ROM drive and inserted my autographed copy of WCII. As soon as I saw the dialog box open to ask me if I wanted to play, I immediately clicked yes and I was launched into the Battle.net servers. Once Warcraft II B.Net edition arrived, the newbie flesh that came along with the storm was more than enough for myself and Sean to lick our chops on. One team after another would challenge, but we would drop them like insignificant flies. Garden of War? No problem. Plains of Snow? Piece of Cake. No Way Out of This Maze? A maze…phhhm. We would not be stopped, and today would be no different. I logged into my screen name Primal Kaos, and immediately entered our super secret chat room, “WCII Gawdz.” That’s where I found Sean waiting for me. Lusty Ogres: Wuz up dude?Primal Kaos: Not much. U?Lusty Ogres: Ready to beat some newbiez azzzzz!Primal Kaos: Dude, we so ownz these foolz.Lusty Ogres: =PPrimal Kaos: Let’s go man. I can’t wait any longer.Lusty Ogres: Meet me in one of the game rooms. We’ll find someone there. So off we went, ready to find our victims. We found these two guys, ‘newbiez’ of course, and off we went on high resources on Plains of Snow. This would be too easy. I knew what build order I would pursue. It would be a two farm—barracks—farm—smith build, allowing for upgrades and simultaneous unit pushing. Something you need when you’re rushing your opponents. I decided not to build a lumber mill immediately, so I would forgo a tower at my home base for the sake of pumping out a few more grunts. I was so offensive minded it was scary. While I was busily constructing my arsenal and war machine, Sean continued to message me. Lusty Ogres: Dude, what are you going for?Primal Kaos: I’m gonna rush them with mass grunts. You?Lusty Ogres: Mass rush with footmen. Where are you at?Primal Kaos: Bottom right corner. U?Lusty Ogres: Top right corner. One of them must be below me. We’ll rush top and bottom together.Primal Kaos: Sounds good. Get a catapult while you’re at it.Lusty Ogres: You lazy ass. You get it. I sighed, queuing up a catapult once my fourth grunt had emerged from the barracks. I upgraded my weapons to +3 and prepared to move forward with the assault. We would look for the spot where our opponent probably left a spot open in his wall-in, and march through it and hit his gold supply. If he was smart, he would have left a peon or two out to continue building. I was betting that they weren’t. Suddenly, I got a message from Sean. Lusty Ogres: Dude, I have like eight footmen at level 3 already. What do you have?Primal Kaos: Dude, only four level 2 grunts.Lusty Ogres: WTF!Primal Kaos: You told me to build a catapult!Lusty Ogres: WTF! Where are your micromanagement skills?Primal Kaos: We’ll go once the ‘pult is done. I waited anxiously, and finally the catapult was ready and roarin’ to go. I sent my men up there to meet with Sean’s, and we were ready to attack. Right before I could initiate the attack sequence, Sean began jibber jabbering with me. Lusty Ogres: Tom, I think we should take a moment of silence to remember these poor souls before we demolish them.Primal Kaos: What? Since when did you become the sensitive type?Lusty Ogres: I don’t know. But I think we should, after all, it would be the gentlemen thing to do.Primal Kaos: You need to stop going to church, my friend.Lusty Ogres: Have mercy on their souls, before we destroy them, as Tom and Frisco will destroy Golden Game.Primal Kaos: Oh yeah, I forgot I had that match.Lusty Ogres: How could you forget? It’s for the PCW tag team titles!Primal Kaos: Bobby forgot to remind me. It just sort of slipped my mind.Lusty Ogres: Sometimes I don’t even think you have a head, Tom. I ignored his comment and prepared my men. I ordered the patrol command and suddenly my Orcish horde ran head on into the fray. They came upon a pretty well constructed wallin, blocking off the entire base using farms and two barracks. My guys began slicing and dicing away at the barracks with no one to defend. Not even a tower for my catapult to take aim at. That was awfully weird. That’s when I watched as my catapult burst into flames. I scrolled over to see eight grunts finishing off what used to be my catapult. I realized that I was now out numbered two to one. Things didn’t look good already. Shit, I murmured. I pulled my four grunts out of the fray and had them run over to the side to avoid being creamed. As I did, I messaged Sean. Primal Kaos: Hey, I could use some help here.Lusty Ogres: So could I. I scrolled over to see Sean’s footmen being pummeled by a horde of grunts, all level 4. I cursed out in frustration and realized that we had been duped. All of this time, we thought we had an upper hand, but our opponents were even craftier than we were. Primal Kaos: Sean, we’re in trouble. Big trouble.Lusty Ogres: My town’s undefended, and I’m out of footmen.Primal Kaos: How did that happen?Lusty Ogres: I don’t know. I think we’ve lost. I banged my hand against the screen so hard that it seemed to just jump out at me. That was when one of my peons landed on my desk. Gosh, that’s odd. It looked at me. “Who are you?” I looked back at it. “Who are you?” “Never mind that. I must be back to work or else the base master will have my hide!” It jumped back into the screen, which emitted a bright white light as he leapt through. I had to shield my eyes from the pain. When I opened my eyes again, you wouldn’t believe what I saw… …and yes, it will be lame.
