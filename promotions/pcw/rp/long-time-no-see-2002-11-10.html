Long time, No See

10 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 10th, 2002 The door swung open, and to his knowledge he was being taken right into the main training area. His eyes glazed the walls, as the torches burned in their slots in the wall giving off light to the floor below. The guard was leading the way, along side was the small child, who he meet outside. He was still dripping from the storm the brood over head, but that was the least concern at the moment. The girl seemed out of place in world like this. When he was here, so many years back, there where only a select few that gained entrance. Now everyone was equal, and one by one they trained together as one fighting unit. He didn’t really notice anyone else, but kept his eyes locked on the light head, barreling down the side of the walls until it stopped at a large arch. The guard stopped, took out a key and gently slipped the key into the lock. He turned and moved back from the door. The large doors swung open slowly, allowing the light to past through. The brightness blinded them all, but carefully they moved on. The guard pointed to a stairwell on the other side of a large arena, and what appeared to be a training facility. “The council is waiting for you,” uttered the guard as he showed them the path to the stairwell. He bowed to the little girl and quickly made his leave to the doorway again, closing it behind him as he passed. The little child continued on her way to the stairwell. She looked back noticing he wasn’t moving. “Excuse me Mr…..” Her voice trailed off. “Lavagna. Vilas Lavagna,” she answered back, still gazing at what he had come to see. She nodded and slowly began to walk again, but noticed he was not following her way. He was still focused on the training facility and the people trying to become what ever there hearts desired. She began to grow impatient, tapping her foot on the ground and trying to move on. “Are you scared to see the council,” she asked, which brought a sudden jolt from Vilas head towards her. He started to move on, still carefully watching the action that was going on and trying to not make a sound. Even for being late at night, there was still classes going on that need silence. Children, adults and even seniors circled around one instructor all trying to gain extra knowledge. She seemed different from all them, but in a way very left out. Even though classes were optional, you would think someone as young as her would attend them all. “This place has toned down. No violence, no peace, just calm silence.” She turned around and looked up at him, wondering again what he was saying. She had been her almost her whole life and everything was the same to her. She shook her head and continued on her journey towards the stairs. Vilas just laughed and continued on his journey. A few of the students peered over at him, but that was normal since outsiders were not welcome. But then again, he was an outsider for the beginning. Vilas looked at them and nodded in acceptance, but they quickly went back to their studies. Vilas continued on his journey, following the small girl. She never looked back and for a split second took her eyes off the opening and looked around at the crowd that had gathered for today’s lessons. “You shouldn’t look at them. That will only take them away from their studies,” commanded the little girl, startling Vilas into a small smirk. They continued on and reached the opening to the stairwell. She waited for him to catch up then started her way up. Vilas looked at her and quickly ran up and pulled her down the bottom. “Where do you think your going,” he asked peering down at her, while still gripping her shoulder. “We are going to the council. Isn’t that why you came here in the first place.” Vilas shook his head and moved the girl aside. He started up the stairs and she followed. He looked back and she stopped again. His eyes narrowed and he began to breathe heavy. “You don’t understand, I am going to the council, not you,” Vilas shouted, pushing her farther down the stairs. “I thought you wanted me to help you?” He shook his head and answered, “No you helped me get in. I will go the rest alone. Besides you should be in your classes anyway.” He pointed towards the opening and the little girl looked back and forth. She lowered her head and slowly crept back down the stairs. Vilas shook his head again and continued to march his way up the stairs, looking back now and then making sure he wasn’t being followed. The stairs twist and turn till the top is reached. That’s when everything changes, and it becomes a whole New World. Paintings of all the past students who have represented the temple and Thailand hang on the sides on the walls, with a red rug going through the center and candles lining the hall. Vilas looks at the paintings and reads the notes left at the bottom. He reads the small notes and moves on until he comes to his picture. Just as he remembered it but with no notes. “These are some of are finest students,” a voice says, calmly walking through the same hall as Vilas. His clothing gave himself away as one of the council, but still it was unusual to see them outside their chambers. Vilas didn’t look up, but yet continued to look at his picture in the gold frame. The councilmen walked up to him and gazed at the picture himself. He looked at Vilas and then back to the picture. He smiled and placed his arm around Vilas shoulder. “You have finally come how after so many years,” he shouted with excitement, as a large smile came across his face. Vilas looked at him with a serious express, freezing the man in his tracks. “I came to speak with the council,” said Vilas, passing by the man heading towards the doorway that lead to the chamber room. “I’m sorry but they are all asleep. Come back tomorrow,” the councilmen said, still looking at the pictures that hang in the walls. Vilas smirked and shook his head. He marched over the councilmen and turned him around, throwing him up against the wall, and getting into his face. He had him shaking in fear, as Vilas took his hand and gripped it tightly. He lifted it up in a threatening manor. “You left me waiting outside for three hours. You took five years of my own life and used it for your own. Wake the bastards. Their sleep means very little considering what you have put me threw,” Vilas shouted, slowly lowering his hand and releasing his grip. The councilman let out a sigh of relief and quickly brushed away the sweat that had dripped from his forehead. He fixed his clothing and looked at Vilas with a snobbish fashion. He gracefully walked by Vilas and headed towards the chamber doors. Vilas waited for him to speak but no words came. “Will you arrange a meeting,” he asked, hopping for some sort of responds. “The council will not see you. Do remember young Lavagna, that we do have rules here. There is no way out.” Vilas staired the man down, but the councilman turned away and headed back for the door. The door closed behind him and the councilman went out of sight. Vilas smiled and looked back at the door. He nodded in acceptance and started to laugh. “There is always one way in. I will get what is rightfully mine.”
