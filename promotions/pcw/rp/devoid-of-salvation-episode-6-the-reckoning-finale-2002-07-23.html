Devoid of Salvation: Episode 6 - The Reckoning (Finale)

23 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 23 Shanahan realized what needed done. And this was the time to do it. He gripped the hilt of the dagger, its onyx-carved form adorned with the emblems of many now-familiar deities. Hands trembling, beads of sweat now slithering down his brow, he thrust the blood-tipped blade into the pink flesh. Though he felt the Chaos’ body convulse violently as the dagger plunged deep into His lower abdomen, he already knew the effect it would have before looking up. His attack was more an act of defiance than a true attempt at inflicting damage on the eternal monstrosity. The blood that now slid through his fingers and dripped down his wrist was merely a side effect of taking on the Earthly form of the ancient pharaoh. Life could not be taken from his foe so easily; chillingly, chances even were that Nyarlathotep could never die except by own divine will. Such were the fruits of godhood. Shanahan felt despair and dread before lifting his gaze to meet the spectral red eyes of the insidious foreign god, yet at the same time feeling a sense of relief and limited achievement sweep through his being. Undoubtedly he would be punished severely for his insolence, but in the face of impending demise he had managed to defy his omnipotently powerful aggressor. Nyarlathotep shed the building rage in his scorching red eyes, leveling that disheartening stare he wielded into that of his defiant attacker. Blood trickled from his mouth, dripped onto the rock at the feet of the rebellious mortal with a subdued, yet distinct echo. The human attempted to maintain a demeanor of outward calm and composure, but the Chaos could detect the terror festering within his heart and mind. “Your will is that of steel, mortal,” replied the cosmic fiend slowly, stretching back his lips to reveal polished white incisors, gleaming with an almost unearthly radiance. “Admirable really. Foolish, yet admirable nonetheless. Most men in your situation would have claimed their own lives.” He nodded toward the dagger, which still skewered his lower innards. “And as you could tell, many other nuisances before you have accepted my most generous bargain. Pity that your asinine sense of rebellion has clouded your judgment. But then, so is the nature of the human race…..” Uttering forth a great sigh, the one known as the Crawling Chaos retreats back several paces, the dagger slipping out of his wounded gut and left in the still hand of Shanahan. The god appeared both repulsed and pleased, and Bruce realized Nyarlathotep was torn between his desire to destroy the Boston resident and the urge to offer a meek reward. Both were common prizes to those who challenged the Old Ones, most often dependent on the said deity’s current mood. Thankfully, prayers were on the side of Shanahan that day. “Your fortitude astounds me, boy,” boomed the demon as He paced opposite the room to glower balefully on the still crouched Boston thug, the gaping wound closing and mending even as Bruce looked on in awe. “I revel in having such a resolute foe, especially one of mortal blood and your bastard ancestry. Indeed, so much like your predecessor. Always steadfast, always persevering was that Crowley….” The avatar of Nyarlathotep appeared to pause momentarily and reflect, casting a smug grin in the direction of the glaring Shanahan before resuming. “For your firm dedication, I bestow on you but a morsel of my blessing. Throughout this next week, a part of I shall be with you. I realize your greatest passion is your occupation, the bloodshed you inflict in the ‘wrestling ring’. I peer into the innermost desires of your heart and touch on your emotions, and I find you want nothing more at this point than to claim what you allege is your ‘rightful crown’. You yearn for the title of Extreme champion in your petty wrestling organization.” Again the deity was playing to the longings of Bruce’s soul, toying at the very thing which now defined his existence. He lived wrestling, slept wrestling, breathed wrestling. Ever since his arrival several months ago he had been consistently denied what he craved, and now his fervor to victory had reached an unprecedented zenith since the day his signature had been scrawled on that contract in PCW Headquarters. Nyarlathotep felt this, discerned his passion for success through all the other hate-triggered aspirations in his black heart. Thoughts of murder, deceit, treachery and ire plagued the majority of his thoughts, and the Chaos waded through these to reach his most cherished objective. He himself was a god of evil and a symbol of anarchy and ultimate destruction, like that of a beekeeper tending to the sweet nectar of mankind’s own making: that which would lead to the race’s final downfall. He revered death and reveled in war, using the unpredictability of man to further the causes of He and the other Great Old Ones and lead the way toward the prophesized Cleansing. But with the penultimate degree of godhood he enjoyed came the capacity to swallow pride and appeal to his target’s wishes. He could obviously use this as a gateway to Shanahan’s annihilation. By granting what he so longingly wanted, he could take grasp of the mortal’s soul and seize his very essence. And he could make it work to his advantage. Nyarlathotep was one who enjoyed the thrill of the hunt and slowly gaining control over his adversaries. To blatantly smite his rivals wasn’t nearly as much fun as wrenching their hearts out after earning their greatest confidence. Besides….was it not much more gratifying to use your enemies to further your ends as opposed to swiftly ending their lives? “For your valor, I will help give you what you want,” came the proposition, the words reverberating through the chamber in the echo-composed voice of a hundred begotten souls. “Come this ‘Wrestle Vessel’ event you anticipate so eagerly, I will guide your efforts in the ring. My own energies will be rather subtle, yet they should be more than suitable to accomplish the task at hand. Especially if you truly are the messiah of ‘extreme wrestling’ you so often claim. Kramer Wild Wing will be decimated, Flaymin Aymin will be crushed, and Rush Mason will be vanquished. You will have the trophy you value so highly. Until such time, I ask but one request.” Bruce narrowed his eyes, rising to his feet warily as the Chaos dipped his head ever so slightly with a royal air of dignity. “What’s that?” sneered the bitter PCW star. “Until your triumph on that evening, you must relieve yourself of any duties to the Blackhelm Coalition, the Miskatonic scholars and all other societies you are likewise affiliated with. You will not hamper yourself with the bantering of those fool intellectuals or the militant actions of your brotherhood. It will be but you, I, and those three lowly ingrates in our crosshairs. Though I despise all you stand for, Bruce Shanahan, I am obligated to do all in my power to aid you in your quest for your prize. I have given my word, and I am bound to it. And until that goal is met, I shall not relinquish my power to helping do so.” Nyarlathotep was a god of deceit and sedition; his word was compliant with His own convenience. Yet more lies….. Inside, Shanahan realized how fragile the word of the Chaos was. He knew Nyarlathotep’s track record for fulfilling promises was less than adequate, but his rationale was impaired for his own insatiable hunger for that which had eluded him since his debut in Primetime Championship Wrestling. He saw only the objective, looked past the obstacles and into the golden light of his dreams. Ordinarily a noble choice, yet rash when dealing with a devil the likes of the Messenger of the Outer Gods. Shanahan accepted. And Nyarlathotep rejoiced. “So be it,” nodded the blasphemous thing. “Go forth, Bruce Shanahan. My guidance shall accompany you on that night. Your strength will wholly be that of your own. But your wisdom in combat will be one with the Gods…..” The fiend dissipated from sight, a wavering silhouette lingering in his wake. As he did so, his surroundings were enveloped in a heavenly light of azure blue and scarlet red and the grimy, blood-soaked floor faded away only to be replaced with the glimmers of sunlight poking through the canopy above, his surroundings murky and saturated with the incessant squawking of exotic birds….. * * * The thunderous wail of the modified minigun muffled out the death hymn emanated as the Cthonian monstrosities burrowed up and through the floor to devour their raiders. Sensaki’s customized Winchester sawed-off rang out repeatedly, the wiry Asian pumping as quickly as he could to ward off the approaching threat, shot ripping through the scales of the squid-headed serpents. Norton drew back toward the south tunnel as his comrade took point and downed an oncoming beast with a short-range blast to its steepled head. “If we get outta this one alive,” shouted Norton before opening up again with his bulky firearm, “then I ain’t never going to Sea World again! F*cking fish-headed freaks!” Sensaki barreled forward, clearing a path for Norton who mowed down a trio of the slithering creatures as they hurtled forward from the right flank. Thankfully, their ranks seemed to be thinning out and the space ahead was clear. A few more turns would bring them out at the second level elevator, and soon afterward they’d reach the surface out of this God-forsaken pit. Sensaki spun around, leveling the sawed-off muzzle of his shotgun at a Cthonian looming directly ahead. As his finger closed on the trigger, all went black and it felt as if he were caught up in a cyclone, spinning uncontrollably and his stomach twisting into contorted knots. * * * Meanwhile, not far from the position of the Sensaki-Norton led Team Alpha, Stryker and Slade had to contend with their own problems. They had been virtually pinned down, all three intersecting tunnel trails brimming with the chants accompanying burrowing Cthonians, their mantra nearly driving the hardened soldiers mad. Stryker’s last recollection was the rattle of his final emptied magazine as it clattered onto the floor, a pair of the abominable monsters careening vertically through the floor beside him and bearing down with flailing tentacles and terrible hisses. Slade spun to protect his commander with the final ammunition in his weapon as he too felt a rush himself hurled off his feet, violently spinning in a torrent of infinite darkness…. * * * Vanessa Wilder, Second Lieutenant of the United States Army and officer of the Delta Fox regiment, awakened to find herself sprawled out on a broad rock overlooking the entrance of the cavern system. Below she could perceive the outlines of several other human forms, most groggily drawing themselves up in a confused stupor much like she was. Sergeant Norton, the barrel of his empty mini-gun smoking at his side, awakened only a couple feet away from the brawny Lieutenant Ryder Slade. Slowly, amongst the brush and tangled undergrowth of the dense forest, emerged the rest of the team. Threy rubbed their heads as if just awakening with an intense hangover, groaning and dragging their exhausted bodies into the thin rays of light protruding through the foliage overhead. Slowly the battered battalion assembled on the bank of the wide stream that ran below, Norton and Wilder taking the opportunity to rinse their faces in the sparkling and untainted waters. Stryker glanced around, leaning on his dirty and groove-ridden rifle. “F*ck!” he spat, running one hand through his sweaty hair after whipping his dented helmet onto the ground. “What the hell happened in there?!” His team was at a loss, most still jaded by the loss of the twenty-plus soldiers they had taken in as perimeter guards. Apparently none had escaped alive, or at least if they had then they were yet to show their faces. The mission had been among the more catastrophic in recent memory, and things had gone awry so quickly it was nearly impossible to discern the catalyst behind it. “I think its safe to assume Kafelnikov ain’t coming out,” sighed Stryker despondently. “Though to be honest, I could give a damn about any metals. Am I the only one that thinks its downright absurd to send nearly thirty special forces into a cavern to look for a damn metal?!” “Things have been fishy in this outfit for a long time,” responded Sensaki, musing on the bank of the stream as he changed his socks. “Too many disasters and irrelevant missions. I don’t understand it.” Stryker put a hand to his chin, rubbing the rough stubble beginning to grow. “Those bastards on the council have some explaining to do, that’s for damn sure…” Just then the realization hit him, and the captain cast his eyes left and right in panic. “Bruce?!” he cried. “Where’s Bruce?!” The others just then noticed their civilian counterpart was missing, and anxiety blossomed through the group. A hand flew to Vanessa’s mouth as grief began to flood her emotions. She had been the last to see him, and had sprinted off down the dug-out underground corridors fleeing from her pursuers while leaving the entranced Boston native crouching silently on the floor. “No….” hissed Stryker, clenching a fist. “How could we drag him in like that?! We should have known….” “Look!” Norton jutted a finger to a ridge at the edge of the clearing the stream ran through, noting a figure outlined against the setting sun. Its back seemed to be turned to the group, but by the height and girth of the figure, it was obvious it was Shanahan. As their shouts rose up against the olive trees and the sapphire-tinged sky, Shanahan gazed down on his cohorts. At his feet was the rifle he had been issued for the expedition into the caves, not one shot ever fired. His eyes were full of spirit, the drive to fight that defined the Boston-bred time-bomb so well. And behind those eyes, was another gleam. An alien flash that came and went intermittently like the wind. He grinned and waved down on them as a red glint shimmered momentarily in his determined eyes. * * * (Briefly excerpted from the memoirs of Bruce Shanahan) Even now I feel his power. Even though I was granted but a fragment of his strength, I can sense there is nothing to fear. The knowledge of a million races is encased in my brain, the drive and inherent brutality of the most ruthless forces in the universe. Though I abhor that vile Nyarlathotep, his minions and his masters, there comes a time when a man’s greatest enemy must become his greatest ally. I have no intention of helping that sinister thing from beyond the stars. This alliance is temporary and strictly business, but simply to spite my critics and my doubters in the locker room I feel compelled to go by any means necessary to usurp Mason. I’ve gotten sick of hearing his praises sung by the jaded populace of this organization; alas its only natural to trust such lies when you have an entire crew of brown-nosing chumps jammed down your throat like so many of the reject-laden locker room. God, what fun it’ll be shoving their words back down their arrogant throats. Lousy f*cks. My time has come. Wild Wing is going to be crucified. Aymin is going to be dismembered. And for the coup de glace, I’ll take Mason’s head home and place it above my cracked mantle. Along with his bloodstained Extreme championship, which I shall once again restore a sense of dignity and reverence to. No more paper title rules by teenybopper-appealing pretty faces or corporate puppets. I'm taking this sh*t back where it belongs. Time to re-establish the Baron of Brutality. Let the Era of Extreme reign yet again….
