Over the river and through the woods, to mother's house we go

9 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9-9-02 RP 2 Turmoil 10th When we left off earlier, Teresa and her not so trusting traveling companion, Jack was on a plane heading to Nashville Tennessee. Jack had bought tickets to the Titans game and to his dismay; his plan to get her mind off Scott didn’t work. Instead, she called her former love and found a disturbing truth, which for Jack is becoming to expectant. To tell a long story short, Titans did when their first game of the NFL series. With Jack locked in the bathroom of the plane, he wasn’t coming out any time soon, especially after Teresa placed all his luggage against the door, keeping him from opening it. With the lying butt hole out of the way, Teresa enjoyed an afternoon of sweaty men running back and forth over a 100 yard field, two hot dogs and a plastic glass or two of her favorite beer. After a fourth quarter touchdown resulting in the big win, Teresa took a cab to a certain place just outside of Nashville. A place away from the city and most civilization. Teresa went home. Not much had changed over the past few months she’d been away from her old farm home. Now days her mother along with a few hired hands tend to the cattle and crops. Since Eric, her brother has disappeared and her father passing away so many years ago, Mama Tomas has no choice but to hire help to keep her 100 plus acres kept in its best. As long as she has men tending the fields, she does the canning and cooking for them. She enjoys their company as well. A few of the men’s wives helps the elderly woman cook and clean. The taxicab stops at the gravel driveway leading to the two-story farmhouse. The same apple tree stands tall to the left of the house and the old chestnut tree still towers on the right side. Teresa pays the cabby and slings a bag over her shoulder before making the trip to the front porch. Mama Tomas is sitting in a rocking chair snapping green beans. The old woman’s eyes grow big and she drops the bean in her hand onto the wooden porch. Teresa drops her bag on the porch, hugs her mother tightly then the two women walk inside. The front door takes you into a small room you see a wooden staircase leading to the three bedrooms upstairs. To the right is the living room and to the left is the den. A coat closet is also in the room and a large kitchen to the back of the house. Teresa and Mama Tomas walk into the kitchen where her mother pours her a glass of ice cold sweet tea. “I wasn’t expecting you so soon. It’s a nice surprise. How is my baby girl?” Teresa leans against a counter holding her glass of tea. Mama Tomas takes a seat at a kitchen table enjoying a glass of tea as well. “Jack found ticket to the game yesterday so you can thank him for the ride home.” “Speaking of the fine feller, where is Jack?” Mama Tomas has always liked Jack and probably always will. No matter what Teresa tells her, the old woman has it in her mind that he will be her son in law some day. Poor woman. Teresa rolls her eyes and looks out the back door into the woods behind the back yard. “He didn’t complete the trip. We had a bit of a fight and I’m seriously thinking of sending him back to Texas. I’d rather not talk about it.” “Oh nonsense child! All men screw up. It’s in their nature. You wouldn’t believe how many times I would’ve liked to ring your father’s neck. Give the boy time, he’ll straighten up.” Teresa turns her head towards her mother. She should’ve known not to bring Jack up, but they have this conversation every time she comes to visit her. Teresa simply shakes her head thinking it a lost cause. “I’ll tell you one more time Mama, Jack and I are or will never be anything. Right now, he’s a pain in my side and I’m making plans to get rid of it. He’s a lying deceiving, self centered bastard that needs to be hog tied and beat to death with a bat.” Teresa finishes her glass of tea and places the glass on the counter. She walks to the refrigerator for the pitcher of tea. Mama Tomas gets that motherly expression to her face watching her grown baby girl. “Watch that tongue young lady. I didn’t raise a foul-mouthed daughter. Don’t you think I won’t still wash that mouth out with soap.” “Sorry Mother. That man makes me so mad sometimes. Why I even thought about taking him along with me to PCW is beyond me.” “You still love him. That’s why, Sweetie.” Teresa sighs. This woman never gives up. “No, I quit loving that man long time ago. Let’s talk about something else. I don’t want to get angry in front of you again.” The last time Teresa got angry in front of her mother, they were at a hotel room. Mama Tomas had paid Teresa a surprise visit. Something was said and Teresa went off showing her mother her violent temper for the first time. It hurt Teresa afterwards realizing what she had done. It hurt Mama Tomas just as bad to see her own daughter in such a state of rage. Just an inherited trait carried over from Teresa’s father unfortunately. The two women spend a couple of hours catching up on lost time and the local gossip. Mr. Thatcher from down the road has been seen visiting the widow Smith from the Deep Draw community while the preacher from the Full Gospel Church resigned his position due to rumors of adultery and drunkenness. It has even been told that he preached a sermon or two while intoxicated. Later that night, Teresa and her mother along with the hired hand, sat down to a delicious meal of the best meatloaf ever created, homemade mashed potatoes with gravy, green beans fresh from the garden, along with corn on the cob. Dessert was Mama Tomas’s special chocolate pie. Teresa will stay the night with her mother then come morning make her trip to Oregon for Turmoil.
