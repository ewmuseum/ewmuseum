Robin Hood, Robin Hood, Riding through the bronx

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 27th November Time 1300Date November 27th FF#3 Kai looked down at the helpless Libby as she touched her reddened cheek, she was different. Kai noted how she was defending her fellow companions. He liked her attitude. As time stood still, Kai found himself delving deeper and deeper into Abner and PCW as a whole. He noted Abner’s superior experience, he had been around, nevertheless Kai new that his euphoric blend of speed, agility and superior technical skill would perhaps make up for his lack of big time wrestling experience. He would have to research Abner further, his character, his demeanour, his style his…everything. Nevertheless, Abner intrigued Kai, he was the first ‘redneck’ that he would have ever met, let alone fought. This in its own was captivating. Kai’s deepest thoughts were intermittent at the best of times…as he felt himself connecting to them a shrill yelp procured Kai’s attention. He began to watch for a moment. When Reyes raised his hands to strike Libby again, he stepped in between them and did something with his hand that Libby could not see. The tall man stepped back, white around the mouth, holding his wrist. “I said that was enough.” Kai said softly. He raised his voice and addressed the women. “We are going to leave you now. I want all of you to sit straight up facing the pole, hold out both hands at your sides, palms up.” Paco Nunez, who had rifled the lockers, took a small plastic tube out of his jacket pocket, flicked the tip of it with his fingernail, and carefully opened it. “Keep your hands still,” Paco said as he moved around the circle, squeezing a couple of pearly drops into each hand. “Now, hold hands with the people next to you. That’s it, nice and tight.” After ten seconds had passed, Kai said, “That is Kuuuraaaazy Glue, ladies. It says right on the label, ‘Cyanoacrylic Glue. Bonds skin instantly.’ If you try to get loose before somebody comes up here to get you, it is going to tear the skin right off the palms of your hands. If you just sit there and wait patiently, when the ladies from the next class come in, a little nail polish remover will fix you up as good as new.” He started to turn back to the others, and then turned back to Libby and said with humour, “You know chubby, you have a lot of nerve for a helpless female.” “Stuff it,” Libby snapped. “It can’t take a lot of nerve to do what you do. And I’ll bet you’re scarred and battered under all them clothes.” She regretted the words before they were past her lips… Kai looked at her, strangely silent, sporadic thoughts, “I think you have us under some kind of misconception. We do not do this for our own gratitude lady; we are doing this to help the needy of New York, its not all Porsche’s, Burberry and Gucci you know. There are people dying each day, because rich snobs like yourself are to clouded in your own worlds, you have never really seen before. I am just doing my part for the community, which is far from what you have ever done.” Kai breathed deeply after his rant, knowing he had opened the eyes of at least one person in New York today. Arden… The next… Checking the street through the peephole in the door, the three men left Muffy Grant’s Slimtronics Salon, at thirty-second intervals, each going his separate way. Kai Xao Kua pulled his hood off from his head, and let it fall onto his back as he walked towards Fifth Avenue. When he got to Fifty-third Street, he dropped his gloves casually into a garbage bin. Alone among the three thieves he was more comfortable with the whole deal. He winced as he recalled the remark of the chubby girl, and reached to touch his forearms to touch the smooth scars…they were the price to pay for freedom. He pulled his hand away self-consciously. It had been one year, when he had left the monastery. When he felt the need to discover himself, the same result would occur on Friday, yet it is more likely to be less painful. When he got to Fiftieth Street, he turned west again and went into the local theatre. They were playing James Bond, Die Another Day. He watched for a while then went to the men’s room. He sat down in a stall and locked the door, then went through the contents of the shopping bag that he had taken from Muffy Grant’s. In accord with his usual procedure, he pulled on a pair of white cotton gloves before going through the wallets and purses, carefully removing and counting the money and discarding the rest. There were half a dozen watches. One was initialled. He threw it back into the bag. He smiled. The women in metallic grey would sure be pissed, he thought to himself. In her wallet, there was a picture of her sitting on a white sand beach somewhere in the bottom of her bikini with her tits out in the breeze. Next to her was a heavyset man in his mid-fifties. He was known as ‘the ticket’ the same principal in whom Kai would use Arden, was in principal here. A huge gold medallion gleamed on his hairy chest. The girl in the picture was obviously using the man to catapult her to wealth, hence the link between Arden and Kai. Kai, the youth, would exploit the aged, Arden, to make sure he started with a bang. The last of the loot was the chubby girls wallet. She had fourteen dollars, a visa card, and an employer ID with her serial number, picture, home address and telephone number. He looked at the card carefully, considering perhaps revenge. It was a breach of conduct to keep anything that might serve to connect them with a crime… Matsushima, for whom Kai worked, was stern about work rules as he was about the observance of philosophy of personal conduct that bound the group together. As his chief disciple, more was expected of Kai. Nonetheless, with a last glance at the fair, round face, he slipped the card into his pocket. She would become his personal project. Kai peeled off his white gloves and dropped them into the bag; he peered out of the crack in the stall door. He was alone. He opened the trash barrel where paper towels were thrown, and dug a hole in the crumpled mass. He stuffed in the shopping bag and made sure it was completely covered before he replaced the lid. Then he went out and watched the movie. He had the whole afternoon to kill, Arden to research, Arden to beat and Arden to make aware.
