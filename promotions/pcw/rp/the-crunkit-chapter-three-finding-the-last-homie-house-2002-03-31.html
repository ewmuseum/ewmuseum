The Crunkit Chapter Three: Finding the Last Homie House

31 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/31/02 The next day, Rushbo and his companions were not merry, and were not joyous as they had thought they would be about the journey. There was no dwarvish singing, no joke telling. The same went for the next day, and the day after that. The small band realized that danger could be around every turn, could be watching them with glassy eyes from deep inside the woods. They no longer made fire when they needed to camp, they slept under the stars. Food grew scarcer and scarcer, until the fact became that the ponies had more to eat than Rushbo and his companions. One morning, they came across a river. Rushbo: You’ve gotta be kidding me. We sleep on the ground, we’ve got barely any food, and now we have to ford a river. Maybe this is what white people do for fun, but I’m telling you, if I don’t eat some real food soon, then there is going to be an ass-beating going down. Sethin: Burglar Rushbo: What? Sethin: Dude, you bitch and whine a lot. Rushbo: I’m not what you call the outdoorsy type, okay? Coren: You’re not what I’d call the enduring type either. Juandalf: Cut it out. Trevin: He’s kinda pissed huh? Rushbo: Yeah, he lost his tag titles back in the real world. RPWRITERSMACK! So, the party forded the river and when the reached the top of the slippery bank on the other side, they could see mountains on the horizon. Rushbo looked at it with wide eyes, amazed at the size of the mountain Rushbo: Does this mean we’re done? That’s THE Mountain? Juandalf: By no means, these are the Misty Mountains; the Lonely Mountain is still far off. We have much traveling to do, let’s get going. Rushbo: We always have much traveling to do. My ass is so painful that I think the black is peeling off. Rolling their eyes, the rest of the group continues on, ignoring Rushbo’s whining and complaining. Juandalf led the way towards the mountains, the rest of the ponies following behind Juandalf’s giant white steed. Juandalf: We can’t miss the road now, because I personally do NOT want to have to hear Rushbo complain about being lost, because I might have to stuff him into my saddlebags. Rushbo: Oh, I see, now we get to the hazing part of the trip? Ignoring Rushbo, Juandalf continues to explain his plan to the dwarves. Juandalf: We’re also going to need food and some rest where we can be fairly sure we’ll be safe. We can go to the house of Elrond. Rushbo: Sorry, I’ve sworn off Scientology and L. Ron Hubbard’s bullshit. I’m Crunk til I die now. Trevin: *cough * Krayzie rip-off *cough* Juandalf: Not L. Ron, Elrond. That’s the Last Homie House before the Misty Mountains. Rushbo: Homie House? Sweet, I don’t have to hang out with you white people anymore. I mean, gee I can’t wait to get to the rest spot, how about you buddies? Rushbo now has an idiotic grin across his face as his pony speeds up, heading towards the Last Homie House. However, the House was still far away. As the journey progressed, all signs of life left. Trees and animals seemed non-existent and it seemed like days were spent going up one steep rock slope. No water could be found anywhere, and the group was reduced to sucking on moss for moisture. Now the group was beginning to become discouraged, for they could’ve passed by the house in any number of hidden valleys or hills, and they neared the Misty Mountains with every passing hour. Juandalf insisted that he knew the way they were going, so the group followed, as he did seem to know his way around the area rather well. Suddenly, they came to slope so steep that they almost went tumbling down. Juandalf: Here it is! We’ve arrived! At first, the other four could see nothing, but as they looked down into the valley, they could see what he was talking about. There was a valley with waterfalls and a rocky bed of water flowing across the valley. Rushbo: Finally. Now how do we get down there? Juandalf, not bothering to respond, started to slowly zigzag his white steed down the hill. Rushbo never forgot, in all of his days, that slow and terrifying trip down the slope. Any slip of foot or misplaced pebble could mean the doom of them all and their ponies. Fifty meters separated each person so that it would be possible to stop a fall. However, they didn’t fail, for the sweet warm air became warmer as they got closer to the bottom. At last, they reached an open glade that lay above the stream’s banks. As they reached there came a song from the elves among the trees. I can feel it comin’ in the aiiiir toniiiiiiiight hold onnnn Rush: AAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAGGGGGGGGGGGGGGH! Suddenly, a small elf popped out from the trees. Elf: Just kidding. So the two groups mingled and laughed in the trees. Rushbo could soon see many of the elves, from behind the trees, and he noticed that they were actually white. He whispered to Juandalf during one elf song. Rushbo: I thought you said it was called the Last Homie House? Juandalf: It is. Rushbo, now thoroughly confused, didn’t bother thinking about it anymore. He just thought about the luscious foods that he would eat, and how good a bed would feel against his sore back. The dwarves however, didn’t join into the fun, for dwarves and elves are none too fond of each other. Later that day, during all of the nonsense that was going on, a young looking man walked out. Man: Look, ‘tis a Crunkit on a pony. What a sight that is? Rushbo: Note to self-Continue to dislike white people. Juandalf: Elrond, it is wonderful to see you. Thank you for your hospitality. Elrond: It is my pleasure. So, they ate dinner there, but they couldn’t stay for Smaug would learn of their quest soon, and they needed for some sort of surprise to steal from the blood-seeking dragon. Thus, they set off, and they came to a small river. The only way across was a small path only wide enough for one. So, each rider led their pony across by the bridle, and then saddled onto the beasts, ready to ride. As they rode off, they heard. “Don’t let Rushbo eat all your cakes, for he is too fat already.” Rushbo: HEY! I’m muscular okay. I didn’t get to be 3’7 and 80 pounds by eating. I have a fitness regimen just like everyone else. And just as they were leaving, they saw the elves put on their regular clothes. To his shock, he saw them walking around in do rags and one pant leg rolled up jeans. Rush: Homie House? I should’ve known it was wiggers all along! The quest continued, although Rushbo still would’ve liked to stay with those wiggery little buggers. Strange as it was, the Homie House kind of reminded him of Crunk End and the Shire. During their stay at Elrond’s house, the three little people had feasted and slept but Juandalf and Coren had been doing some extensive research on the map to Smaug’s mountain. For Lord Elrond knew all kinds of ruins, and could read almost any writing, even that of the ancient tongue of Mordor. Juandalf first showed him the swords that had been taken from the trolls. Elrond: My friend, these are no swords of troll origin. These were made in Gondolin, for the wars of Goblins. This, my friend Coren, is the sword Crumbtaker, because it makes those cute little crumbs that come off of muffins. Coren: Excuse me? Elrond: Whoops, my bad. This is the sword of your ancestor. It is called Orcrist? Coren: Why does it have that name, Lord Elrond? Elrond: Well, when a man loves a woman, wait no, everyone reaches an age where they, no that’s not right either, I guess it’s called Orcrist because BIG SWORD O’ DOOM~! had been taken. This sword is the goblin-cleaver. Juandalf: What is the other weapon you hold? Elrond: This my friend, is the legendary Sword A La Poisson. Juandalf: Sword of the Fish? Elrond: No, maybe that’s wrong. Ah, here it is. This is Crunkiller, the legendary sword of the kings of Gondolin. Coren: I shall keep Orcrist, and hopefully, it may cleave some goblins, or at least some nice little muffins soon enough. Juandalf: Where might these trolls have obtained such fantastic weapons? Elrond: I cannot say. There are probably plunder from plunderer’s plunder and so on. They were made by my Elvish brother’s in the west, that is all I know. Juandalf: I have one last favor, old friend. Elrond: Show me the map. Elrond held up the map to the mirror and explained to the wizard and the dwarf. Elrond: Moon-letters. Not usual runes, they can only be read by moonlight. I will try to read what I can. Juandalf: What do they say? Elrond: They say “Your brother Raoul is a fish.” No, wait that’s just some ale I spilled. It says “Stand by the gray stone when thRush knocks, and the setting sun with the last light of Durin’s day will shine upon the keyhole. Juandalf: That must mean something about the Lonely Mountain. Coren: Durin was my long ancestor, of the Longbeards! Juandalf: Then what is Durin’s Day? Coren: When the last moon of Autumn and the sun are in the sky together, we call that Durin’s day. However, in these days we cannot know when this will take place Juandalf: Perhaps this will help us anyway. Any more writing? Elrond: Yes, it says “Beware of Moose when the sun sets under coyote’s rectum.” Or wait, is that more ale? Juandalf: Coren, I think we should go and leave Elrond’s ale fantasies to him. Thus, with the whole story known to the group, they rode, off towards the Misty Mountains and whatever else lay ahead. And what lay ahead, was more than they could’ve guessed at this time. Still, they rode, towards the land beyond.
