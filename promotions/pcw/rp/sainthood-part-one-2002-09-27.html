Sainthood (Part one)

27 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/27/02 Screams filled the room........a form dropped, kneeling. A grin crossed his face, which you might have expected from one such as he......he fit the mold rather well. A rather large man, young.........though we cannot see his eyes, his expression below told the tale. His smile. His wicked, broad smile....... What makes evil, you might ask? Is it not perspective, merely........isn't it what you make of it? What would you call evil? Is it this boy? The child who sat on the floor.......what was really wrong with him? Was he so horrible? He cradled her head in his lap, rocking back and forth......if her eyes could see, they might have reflected sympathy. If they had not been torn from her skull, they might have shown emotion other than the simple and dead shocked stare. His grin vanished.......he wept as he sat with this girl, close to his own age in his arms......he remembered...... Harsh words, screamed at him from a blurring darkness. Ever taunting. What did I do to deserve this? What? WHAT? Back to the forefront......shocks of stringy black hair hung before him. The boy absently brushed them back.......bent over and kissed the form he had ravaged. He snuggled close with her, his tears falling and mixing with her blood........he cried out to the gloom. WHY!? But no answer is forthcoming. Snow fell on a lonely police station.......its barren walls reflecting grime, disuse. Scheduled for wreckage soon, the old building was spared due to no other reason than it was a landmark.......standing long enough to be remembered by the old timers, but still maintaining enough charm to catch the attention of the young. Inside the halls things were a little better.......maintained, renovated. The members of this station pitched together, and their efforts showed......the years vanished inside of it. If one didn't know better, they would think it was new........a mission accomplished. Through these halls, and to a room where the door eternally remained open. Footsteps fall, shuffling along.....delicate, feminine. A woman backpedaled through, carrying her load of paperwork as carefully as possible though she is clearly overloaded. Stumbling, she spun and planted the pile on a nearby desk.......behind it an older head peered. 'You okay Bev?' Bev grinned sheepishly at the fellow before her. The lines on his face showed through, though he never lost his sense of humor through the many years. She'd always liked him.......and she believed he knew it, too. Bev: Yeah John, it's okay. John brushed back his hair, ignoring the fact it had long since receded, cropped close to compensate. He looked to the long pile of legwork on his desk, now grown even larger......smiled and shook his head. John: It's never quite done, is it? Bev: Will be soon for you though. The old cop's expression froze for a moment.......in an instant Bev regretted her choice of words. Bev: I'm sorry......I..... John: It's okay. You're right......my retirement's coming up very soon. There won't be much more need for any of this paperwork, eh? Again the elder smiled.....though it was tinged with sadness. He would miss this line of work......even after being relegated to the desk, he loved the feel of it all. The feel of making a difference. Bev: John.....? Blinking slowly, he turned to her. What Bev saw filled her heart with an unbelievable sadness.......and he knew it. His eyes had grown accustomed to picking out small details in the expressions of others......some talents never leave you. John: What's wrong, Bev? Tell me. Bev: It's......it's nothing. I just got this feeling.......I would....... John: What? Bev: That I'd never see you again. After you left here for the last time. No surprise came over John Morgan's face.......instead it was as if he knew. This strange reaction broke Bev out of her daze, she raised an eyebrow. John: The thought had crossed my mind as well, Bev. The secretary opened her mouth to speak, but the aging man rose. Before she could say anything he put his arms around her, holding her close. In that moment doubt vanished.......sometimes, when you're close enough, you don't need to say a word. He raced through the darkness, though he wasn't moving. Looking down, he could see no motion save the futile pumping of his legs......why? Why was he unable to move? Finally realizing he could not escape, he fell to his knees. Nothing could save him......beyond all redemption. It was coming closer now. Heavy footsteps.......he cried. Tears streamed down his face, his broken and bloody face......why? Why is this happening to me? The boy tried to wipe the tears, with the two remaining fingers of his right hand. All that remained, all that he had left.......the others were taken from him. His very fingers......ripped, smashed. Again he asked what he had done to deserve this.......and his answer trundled towards him in that godawful, all consuming black. 'Dad........' It leapt upon him, crushing him. Beating him, slamming his head off the cold, cold ground. He begged, begged for oblivion to come but it would not.......there is no salvation. No hope......abandon. Lost child. 'No......no.......no.' Abruptly he awakened to find a form indeed atop him, though this was different. Its listless, lifeless body was weight on him, nothing more.......the young man threw it to the side in terror, taking a moment to look at it only after the initial terror. Corpse. Girl. Another. Somewhat absent mindedly he looked at his left hand, a blade clutched in his mangled grip. Congealed blood clung to the steel, indicating age.......he had done this some time ago. How long had he been out in the woods? How long had she been dead? No answers were forthcoming. Only the howls of the wolves, the beckoning of the cold, cold December night. His tattered clothes caught on twigs and branches, but he paid no mind as he ran. His blood seeped from cuts, but these mattered not, no.......nothing mattered. Nothing mattered but the escape........but there was no escape from the visions. The memory. Beating him. Into the eternal earth. 'John, take a look at this.' Rummaging through the endless parade of paperwork, Bev Carter happened upon something that caught her eye. Though murders were fairly common in a large town such as this, she stopped short here. Perhaps it was the description.......the brutality which has such a way of fascinating humans. Perhaps mere coincidence......in any case she stopped short. John: Bev? The old man leaned over, peering across the shoulder of his secretary of so many years. At first merely curious, still sporting his look of slightly tired humor......however as he saw what she saw, his humor quickly faded. John: ......what......? Bev: Look at it. John held the manilla folder closely, flipping through the various pages of the report.......scanning quickly with a highly trained eye. His expression grew steadily gloomier, as he read......and became downright morbid as he reached a stark black and white photo. Bev: Didn't you know this girl? Morgan peered closely at the girl in the picture.......pretty beforehand, long golden hair running down to thin shoulder blades. Now mangled......the after photo resembled little the previous creature. John remained silent, not answering Bev as he examined the crime scene photo. Her eyes stared forward, open eternally, unmoving.........slashes covered her body, showing clearly through her torn open clothes. Broken and lifeless......raped? The autopsy report said no. And yes he knew her......he knew her face. John: It's......Sam. Quietly the officer said her name........he remembered. Flashed back, upon happier times.......a young girl playing. Her teenage years just beginning, she danced with a smaller child.......as on a porchstep nearby John Morgan watched. He smiled, the lines on his face a little lighter.....the years a little less advanced. He watched his daughter frolic with her neighbor.......and he wished for it never to end. Abruptly the memory faded for John.......realization set in that it was indeed over. His daughter now a young woman herself......and Sam, that sweet neighbor who had once given her such happiness now laying broken. Shattered. A picture, a memory, never to be anything more. Bev noticed the anger setting into her longtime friend's face.......she gently took the folder away from him, setting it to the side. John: I.....need a smoke. Morgan rose from his seat......the middle aged woman watched him leave the room. Then after a long moment, not even knowing the reason, she followed.....outside that station she found him attempting to light a cigarette, only to be foiled by a faulty lighter. It sparked, sparked, but no flame came forth......until another lighter came close, igniting easily. Bev: Need help? John: Mm. John Morgan took a long drag from his cancer stick, exhaling and watching the smoke drift. The cold air played havoc on his knee, sending shockwaves of pain through it but he paid it no mind.......merely looked on. Bev: ......I'm sorry, John. John: Don't be. These things happen.......it's in the line of work. Bev: But not on a personal level. John: Always on a personal level, Bev. Always. Throwing the cigarette onto the ground, Morgan stepped back into the station........after an eternal moment, Bev pulled her jacket closely against the biting December wind and followed.
