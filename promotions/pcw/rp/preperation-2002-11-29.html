Date: 29th November Time Classified29th November FF#7 When Dennis had presented tribute to Matsushima, he had squatted before the master in his office to receive praise and to make obeisance. He had seen the drawer in which the plans for operations were kept. The night after the punishment he climbed out on the ledge of the window in the dressing room and hung from his tiptoes and fingertips four storeys above the concrete till everyone left. As his extremities numbed, and death beckoned closer, he welcomed it all… His search for identity had come to a dead end…dead…end… Without the activity provided by the collection of tribute, Dennis Holling’s source of life-giving energy receded into memories of successful adventures, Matsushima left before his grip gave out. He opened the window, stole the plans for the Angles, and shinnied down the drainpipe. He took them to a nearby Xerox shop, had them copied, and then crawled up the system of pipes and window ledges, his fingers bleeding, and his life hanging by a thread, to return the originals. George Pfister, the other white member of the Matsushima clan, was also its youngest adherent. He was big, young, and strong. He came from Seattle. He had left when he was fourteen, a runaway from a drunken mother and a bestial stepfather. He joined the hundreds of thousands of youngsters who swirl like flotsam in the tides of America’s human sea, living and dying beneath the surface of society and largely invisible to its members. Kai had picked him out of the middle of a fight. George was almost too willing. Matsushima said that he would take the greatest patience of them all, because while he was young and therefore could be formed to the clan’s needs, he would be very slow to learn. Dennis Holling’s and Kai said that they had secret orders from Matsushima to exact tribute from the locker rooms at then Angles. That evening Pfister did not show up at the academy. ~**~ Kai sat on the stoop of the dojo; wondering how would the robbery come off… Betrayal… Backstabbing… Matsushima would never know it was them… or would he? ~**~ Security at the club had been tightened since the episode at Muffy Grant’s, as at every gym and health salon in New York. The number of uniformed security guards had been doubled to six. They patrolled to six. They patrolled the street level and the sidewalk around the club endlessly. One stood, sick in hand, at the side of the pretty brunette who handled the door and checked the membership cards. There was one even stationed on the tennis courts on the roof. ~**~ The correct death is written in.I will fill the need.My bow is stiff.My bow is in readiness.I am the bullet and the hook.I am cocked and held ready.In my sights I carve himlike a sculptor. I mold outhis last look at everyone.I carry his eyes and hisbrain bone at every position.I know his male sex and I domarch over him with my index finger.His mouth and his anus are one.I am at the center of feeling. Give mehis flag and his eye.Give me his hard shell and his lip.He is my evil and my apple andI will see him home. Kai Kua stood against a rusty column in the 125th street subway station waiting for a train. It was after ten. He sighed. His ear pained him, and his hip was defiantly sore. He had a busy day planned, and would have preferred to be in bed. He had nothing to read. When he had sat on the bench to wait, an old man offering a disgusting proposition, and the second time by a young man who expected to take his wallet had accosted him twice. A single glance dissuaded him. In his boredom, he patted his shirt pocket where he had kept his short supply of opium, however in his personal cleansing, he had rid of the toxin. The search for a spiritual path is born out of suffering. It does not start with lights and ecstasy, but with the hard tacks of pain, disappointment, and confusion. However, for suffering to give birth to a genuine spiritual search, it must amount to more than something passively received from without. It has to trigger an inner realisation, a perception that pierces through the facile complacency of our usual encounter with the world to glimpse the insecurity perpetually gaping underfoot. When this insight becomes apparent it can manifest into a crisis. It overturns accustomed goals and values, mocks our routine preoccupations and leaves old enjoyments stubbornly unsatisfying. Kai had hoped that his perception of America would not mean that his hiatus from the monastery would transform into…into an American. At first, such changes generally are not welcome. We try to deny our vision and to smother our doubts; we struggle to drive away the discontent with new pursuits. But the flame of inquiry, once lit, continues to burn, and if we do not let ourselves be swept away by superficial readjustments or slouch back into a patched up version of our natural optimism, eventually the original glimmering of insight will again flare up, and again confront us with our essential plight. It is precisely at that point, with all escape routes blocked, that we are ready to seek a way to end our disquietude. No longer can we continue to drift complacently through life, driven blindly by our hunger for sense pleasures and by the pressure of prevailing social norms. A deeper reality beckons us; we have heard the call of more stable, more authentic happiness, and until we arrive at our destination, we cannot rest content. Kai’s thoughts began to manifest; growing increasingly in complexity, his comprehension of the Buddhist way of life meant Kai could ascertain spiritual enlightenment, and therefore be in peak physical condition. He had often heard that phrase, ‘peak physical condition.’ Yet, Kai thought, how could one imply they were perfect when their mind is scathed by corporate sponsorships and impurities? He shrugged and drew in his duffer coat, Mother Nature would be denied any satisfaction tonight, and he pulled his hood up onto his head, which was covered by a black beanie. His pants reached down to his shoes, a pair of tatty Nike running shoes. Kai frowned… he was being faced with a challenge that he had not an experience of. Abner… Remembering the Shaolin way his thoughts transverse on how he could face this difficulty. His breath materialised in front of his face, as the cold began to set in. It is just then that we find ourselves facing a new difficulty. Once we come to recognize the need for a spiritual path, we discover that spiritual teachings are by no means homogeneous and mutually compatible. When we browse through the shelves of humanity's spiritual heritage, both ancient and contemporary, we do not find a single tidy volume but a veritable bazaar of spiritual systems and disciplines each offering himself or herself to us as the highest, the fastest, the most powerful, or the most profound solution to our quest for the Ultimate. Kai sought perfection, spiritually, mentally and physically, he already was the ultimate in his own mind; confronted with this melange, we fall into confusion trying to size them up- to decide which is truly liberal, a real solution to our needs, and which is a sidetrack beset with hidden flaws. A train finally rattled into the deserted station; it seemed like an eternity had passed of deep concentrated thought. The car smelled of urine. A bag lady, the sole occupant, looked up, fear emanating like a beacon from her rheumy eyes. She gathered her possessions around her. When Kai sat at the other end, she breathed a sigh of relief. Had he really become that intimidating, he spoke obscenities in Chinese about the stereotypical racist woman. Knowing he could do nothing about it, he slumped back into the worn and travelled chair. She breathed a sigh of relief, and relaxed her grip on her assorted bundles. At Eighty-sixth Street, the train sat for several minutes, with its doors open. Kai began to think that this was not the place to evaluate the various spiritual disciplines in terms of how he would live his life from these moments onward. Kai moved restlessly, toying with intricate details, he then sprang from his seat and jumped on to the platform just as the doors were closing. He trotted up the steps to the street and began to walk east. At First Avenue, he turned south, and continued towards his humble warehouse abode. Where was he going initially? He had become sidetracked in thought, something that had not occurred since his days at the monastery. He would try to iron out this lack of concentration by a strict two day fast, in total meditation. Boredom… But for now… the robbery must go ahead.
