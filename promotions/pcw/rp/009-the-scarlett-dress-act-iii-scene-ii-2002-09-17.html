009 - The Scarlett Dress; Act III, Scene II

17 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09.17.02 Bourbon StreetNew Orleans, Louisiana 1:15 amTuesday Peter Evans slurred down the street, heavily drunk on a case of scotch found in his right hand. He happened to joke around, laughing incoherently about politics, the Gay Mafia, and various potholes in his eyes of noteworthy figures around the New Orleans area. His black denim jeans happened to drag along the sidewalk, pushing over people whoever was walking in his way. In the mind of a drunk, Evans had no pleas for help, nor was he not one of those loser drunks. He drank like a victor tonight, after beating his boss and being called a hero for it, nobody could deny him of that. 'Hey, watch out buddy!' one pedestrian slapped Evans on his back, as he trotted drunkenly down the street. It was only hours ago that himself pioneered the 'Fight Club' boss fight scene, only in the litteral sense. 'Shit on a brick!' Evans blurted out loudly, getting raucous cheers from various men and women bordering Bourbon Street. He threw his bottle down to the ground, glass scattering around the sidewalk and one landing in his palm. The detective chuckled about it, before flicking out the shard out of his hand. Blood slightly moved out of the wound, but nothing could slow him down. Evans, it all of his stupor hadn't been paying attention to the back alleyway only a few steps away from him. A man leant back against the wall, his eyes closed and seeming to be in ecstasy. Down below him past the standing man's ankles, a woman appeared, wearing a scarlett red dress and her hair tied back in a bun. 'Hurry up.' he whispered to her softly, as she continued for some reason. It didn't matter in her mind, just a simple fifty dollars to be placed in her bra, to be kept for safe keeping and not to be mugged. Shortly, she was done, and the man rolled his pants back along his hips. 'Thanks!' he whispered with a smile on his face. She scoffed, 'He wasn't all that good.' she clicked her teeth, saying it like a pro. As her face appeared in the dawning bright lights and roaming people, it was certain April Jackson was on the streets for this night not to even joke around with people, business came first. Evans happened to catch her out of his left eye. 'Hey April!' he shouted out, loud enough for a few dozen people to turn their heads into looking at the two of them. April muttered to herself, Oh my God! I hope he doesn't bring himself over here. She quietly strutted down, with the heels of her shoes clacking against the rubber pavement. Sure enough, Evans obliged with a running start, reaching her in mere moments. 'Dammit!' April told herself, before changing her tone. 'Hi Peter,' she told him with disdain in her voice.Peter laughed, 'I didn't know you came out here at night!' he stumbled around before catching himself. April forced a laugh to Peter, before trailing off, 'Yeah.' 'So, how's life?' he asked. 'Not bad,' she responded. 'How's yours?' 'Oh you just don't know!' he slapped her shoulder with force, sending her back a bit. 'I mean, I hit my boss Larry in the face, and he fell back.' he retorted, sounding like a child and saying it real fast. 'You did?' she asked, seeming to be enthralled by the story. Peter eyed her, 'Of course guy! What you think I wouldn't be telling you the truth? I'm too good for that.' he said that with confidence. For the first time in a good while, he acted without the strict demeanor that police work happened to be giving. 'Oh, and what are you doing out here?' 'Nothing.' He tilted his head. 'Come on, we know that you were doing something.' 'You don't want to know.' For the love of God, I swear I'll punch him in the face if he doesn't back off. April didn't look like much of a bully over people with her petite size and structure. 'I had nothing to do tonight,' she lied. 'I just went to the club with a few people and had fun.' 'Right,' he said sarcastically. 'Fun...' 'Fine then, be argumentative about the situation.' He shook his head, before moving away from her, brushing up against her shoulder, before retreating back with skill. He acted almost as if he were a brash teen out of high school. Some people wanted him to act just like that in life. It just didn't work like that however. 'Why are you drunk?' April questioned. 'Why am I drunk?' he asked her question to himself. 'Well...because I can!' he joked, strumming away the chords of 'Fell In Love With A Girl' by The White Stripes. April placed a fist under her chin, before tapping her foot impatiently. 'You just won't answer the question will you?' 'Do I feel like answering your questions woman?' he questioned. 'NO!' 'Shut up, peon.' she hailed a taxi, before Evans grabbed her forcefully by the arm. 'Let go!' she squealed out, with Evans not letting go. It didn't even turn to his mind that he needed to stop, his drunken and erratic behavior even out of control for the people that are common drunks along Bourbon Street. What is he doing? April began to question herself into Evans' actions, before pulling herself off of him and smacking him once in the mouth. The relflex of it sent him into a frenzy as he moved his head back into position, starring down the woman in front of him with murderous eyes. 'You little bitch!' He charged at her, knocking her against the cab she had hailed only minutes ago, before backhanding her down to the concrete. Her hushed cry for help made her realize that she couldn't control some people, even when they were drunk about and completely out of their minds. Evans recoiled back with a snarl on his face as he spat out blood draining from his bottom lip. Pedestrians around him happened to look at him with disgust, even shout a few things at him. He shrugged it off, before he heard the sirens above him, howling with a wolf's wail of the night. Stopping himself, Evans began to realize what he had done, and took his black leather jacket and shrouded his face, but the name calling wouldn't stop. 'Rapist!' one man shouted from the balcony, he had seen the incident from the fifth floor of his apartment building. He tossed down a cup filled with left over beads from Mardi Gras and hurled down towards Evans' head. It couldn't be unnoticed, not by any person who happened to be on the street. 'How dare you treat a woman like that?' 'Yeah!' another shouted, causing Evans to reel back in disgust and horror. He whispered to himself, 'Stop it, please stop!' They wouldn't let up, the accusations continued around the entire block, and all of it was centered around the very fact that Evans assaulted this woman, not for any one reason or repair of his consicous. It didn't help, and by sunrise, this would be the main thing in the Daily Herald. He began to run, faster and faster until his legs pumped acid and his breath stank only of the sour steel mill waste left out by companies. Evans didn't care, as long as he was far as he possibly could be from April, that mob, and the newspapers. Now he was on the receiving end of the stick, that odd feeling when you're confidence shrinks from that highness that you ended losing after just one single moment. He began to cry, the tears that even the woman on the ground began to wipe hers away. The fear of it all began to dawn on her and she was beginning to loathe her job, even hate it. The truth shall set them free. Westlake ApartmentsNew Orleans, Louisiana 3:19 pmTuesday I stepped up towards the door, patient in waiting and not rushing over the excitement pulsating throughout my body. A simple knock on the door would be needed, just to make me relax about certain things. Here I was, about to meet my bigger sister for the first time in my life and I'm sweating bullets for it! I raised my hand up to the door, waiting in wait could be an upsetting uproar thanks to the actions I've happened to be reading about. The door swung open, without me even realizing it. 'Oh no.' she shrieked, with a dark blue ring around her left eye. She stared at me, but tried to scream her head off wanting to single me out on her hallway. Since relocating from her former apartment complex, she'd been dodging people all day. I felt bad for her, in a way. 'Ssh.' I silenced her with a finger on her lips. 'Can I come in?' 'Why? To ask me questions and bargain me off for your cheap thrills?' I smiled, 'No, because I have some real news to tell you.' 'Like what?' she asked. 'If you don't let me in, you'll never know.' I played with her, before she snagged me inside of the room. She slammed the door behind her, 'Okay, who sent you? Times, Press?' 'None.' 'Well then, come on and spit it out.' I took a seat on the plush bed they had given her with the room service. To be on the streets for a majority of your life, you surly did happen to have various obstacles not even in your way. Not that this life is for me anyway, but its how she lives, and I have to oblige that. 'Look, your father-' She interrupted me, 'Don't speak of him, I don't know him and I never want to see him.' 'Well, you will have to learn of him anyway.' I repeated. Soon, I would have to burn the question out of her. She nodded, 'All right. What about him.' 'Your father, is my father.' She opened her mouth in a gasp, only feeling that she wasn't hearing the truth that I know and now she happened to try and comprehend. 'Why would you tell me something like this?' 'Because,' I began with tremble in my voice. 'you might be in danger.' 'Danger?' she questioned. 'You mean like someone trying to kill me danger?' I smirked, 'Would I lie to you?' 'Okay,' she sounded a bit down, or maybe just shocked that I was her brother afterall. She began to talk a walk towards the door when the knock came at the door. My eyes lit up like a Christmas tree, wondering who it happened to be. 'Who's there?' she asked anxiously. The knock came again, now judging by my thoughts of how most shoot ups inside of a project building happened to occur, two knocks means that its coming, and one happens to mean its safe. I lunged for April, getting her to the ground, before the gunshots started ringing out in both of our ears. It played by requiem, hitting everything in its path, from the bed sheets to the mirrors in the room. She screamed, like I'd normally would do in the situation, but you have to believe that no grown courageous man would do such a thing in front of a woman, let alone his big sister. God, that feels odd saying that. She scattered herself under the bed, thinking it was to be safe. Back then, I would have done the same thing, but when I opened the door, the hushed silence of gun power flew into my nostrils, and that empty cry for help a gunshot gives was absent. I thought everybody was all right and nobody was injured. Checking myself, I saw no bullet wounds, no holes in my clothes. 'That is one strong liquor cabinet.' I assured myself as I called out to April to come out and see that everything was fine. She didn't hear my calls. I called out again to her, even louder than the first time, 'April!' it rang out, but she didn't hear me. I looked under the bed, her hiding spot from the gun shots and I stood back in horror. Something happened to her, and I couldn't even speak it into words. 'Help!' was the only thing I could shriek at the moment as my flesh and blood lay there, face down in the carpet. Helpless. With a bullet in her chest.
