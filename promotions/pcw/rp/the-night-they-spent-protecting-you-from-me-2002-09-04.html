The night they spent protecting you from me.

4 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/4/02 -Duke sits in his presidential Front Office in the headquarters of PCW Plaza. Duke sits back in his big leather chair and smokes a cigar as his eight hundred dollar ostrich skin boots rest on the desk. He is wearing all black and has a cup of coffee on his desk in front of him. Ty Jackson the travel director for PCW sits in Duke’s office as the bullsh*t.- Williams: Hey hoss I don’t care how much they’re paying me I won’t play Utah. -Ty sighs as he tries to reason with the stubborn, set in his ways, forty two year old veteran- Jackson: Utah is an untapped jackpot with stupid fans dying to pour money in the kitty. Williams: Jive on Jackson but I ain’t playing Utah. Nothing but rough times hoss. Jackson: What’s so bad about Utah? -Duke kicks his feet off the desk and sits up right as he sits his coffee.- Williams: Hoss, I'm for law and order, the way that it should be. But this story is about the night they spent protecting you from me. Someone called this me outlaw, in some ol' magazine New York sent a posse down like i ain't never seen. -Ty listens in as he loves to hear the old wrestlers stories.- Williams: It was 1982 and Nancy Reagan had a hard on about saying no to drugs. Like that rich bitch really cared. Jackson: I remember that. Williams: You know the law is for the protection of the people and the law was coming down heavy on those who enjoyed recreational enhancers in the privacy of there own home. Jackson: Minding your own business shouldn’t be a crime. Williams: Right on. Now in ’82 I was in the WWF and not doing very much. I was also in the heaviest point of my drug use in my life. Fifteen grand was going up my nose every day. Jackson: Jesus. Williams: You said it hoss. I thought I was Jesus and invincible. You showed me water and I was walking on it. Now I’ve been clean of all drugs since 1996 so I have no problem taking about this. The fat ass fans didn’t figure it out but I have been. Anyway you remember a goofy wrestler named Jim Dugan? -Ty smiles.- Jackson: HOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOO!!!!! -The Malice Man laughs.- Williams: Yea that was him. Dumb as a fucking rock. Anyway his father was the Sheriff of Glen Falls, New York. Now Jimmy is the guy who first got me hooked on Cocaine. At this point I had been hooked for about a year. Now about a month before the infamous bust I did an interview with Bill Apter for ProWrestling Illustrated. He heard about my problem and I alluded to it in the interview. So they threw my picture on the cover and I was labeled wrestling’s real outlaw Duke Williams. I all of a sudden became a cult hero for the young guys of the 80’s drug culture. As a matter of fact if a store sold drugs on the side they would hang my poster up in the window as a symbol. -Duke points to a framed picture of him with long hair dressed like a Hell’s Angel on the cover of PWI magazine from July 1982.- Williams: Of coarse the cops caught on and I became a prime target. They wanted to bust me and make an example of the wrestling star, slash drug hero. So that brings us to Salt Lake City for a WWF house show. At that time I was feuding with a young Randy Savage who was a few years older then me. Before the show I had a few words with a local cop who called me a hippie. After the show I was to forget all about it. -Duke takes a sip of coffee as Ty looks on attentively.- Williams: Duggan just got back from visiting his daddy in New York and stolid a few bags of coke from the evidence closet. We we’re going to party hardy in this small motel eight in Salt Lake City. I mean fuck what else is there to do in Utah? Jackson: You have a point. Williams: We had done a few lines and the rest was in a bag on the table and we hid the bulk of it in the ventilation system. The WWF always tapped our work so we hooked up a play back device to watch our matches on. We were rapped up in our wrestling, that's why we never saw, the cars pull up, the boys get out and the room fill up with law. They came pounding through the back door in the middle of the match. They got me for possession of something that was gone, long gone. -Ty breaks in.- Jackson: What do you mean long gone? Williams: As soon as they crashed in Duggan dove for the coke, tossed it into the john and flushed. They never found the coke in the air conditioner shaft. So all they got us on was a little residue and the stuff in our system. Simple possession of something that was gone. -Duke points to a picture of him in black shorts being escorted away with his hand cuffs behind his back.- Williams: It was the DEA who tried to bust me in Utah and failed.. It totally backfired on them cus when I beat the possession charge that picture of me in cuff’s elevated my statues even higher. -Ty smiles- Jackson: So those t-shirts and Duke bongs on E-bay are legit? Williams: Damn right man!! It was Cheech, Chong, and Duke Williams. -Duke laughs and points out a picture of himself with those two drug icons.- Williams: Vince had to let me go from the WWF and gave Randy Savage my push. I went to Japan and wrestled as Elwood Williams the American Outlaw. My gimmick was somewhere between country pimp or motorcycle outlaw. I always wore a DEA hat to the ring and a shirt with a big pot leaf on it. Those were the days. Jackson: So you don’t do Utah? Williams: Now you jive hoss? Jackson: Yea I understand. So when did you come back to the states? Williams: In the 1990’s when it all cooled out and I was long forgotten. Jackson. Damn. -Duke takes a sip of coffee.- Williams: That’s why I don’t do Utah and that’s why I don’t respect authority. Jackson: But you are the authority now? Williams: No I’m still the same pig down in the poke it’s the rest of the people that our problem. Everybody is fighting for something but no body knows what that something is. They are lost souls with out the mental capabilities to understand that. You see guys like Kannon and ladies like Brandi and Ellie get it. They understand the purpose of fight for what is right. That’s what we do. We our the savior’s of this company. We have already cut cost nearly in half. We’re doing what’s right they just don’t understand it. We our unsung heroes. -Ty just nods his head to move the conversation along.- Williams: XK is PCW’s first real champion. All those before him were just guys passing though. Names on a wall that mean nothing. There history from this point forward is erased. I don’t like authority and that’s what Rent-a-dolt claims to be. Jackson: I guess he does. Williams: Your damn right he does!!! That mask, that attitude, fighting for a cause he doesn’t understand. He’s as gullible as sh*t and about as bright. He was the laziest member of the Malice Crew the world has ever seen. A disgrace to the ring and to everybody in the back. No wonder the boobs in the crowd cheer for this loser. He’s so simple even they understand him. Jackson: What about Darkstar? Williams: A modern day outlaw trying to be a role model for the kids. At least I know I was a low life when I was one. They kid is damaging all those freaks who dye their hair and pierce God knows what. All of them Darkstar included are losers. Freaks is all they are and all they will ever be. All they have to live for is the hope that their parents die and leave them money. Really they are just worthless. It’s a pity really. Oh well what are you going to do. Jackson: Duke I have to fly. Williams: Hasta hoss. -Jackson exits THE FRONT OFFICE- =w=
