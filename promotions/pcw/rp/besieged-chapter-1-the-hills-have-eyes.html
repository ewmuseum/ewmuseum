Besieged: Chapter 1 – The Hills Have Eyes

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 27 of 2003 A.D. “Nice little lodge, captain. Sure we can’t just say screw the mission and take a little vacation?” Captain Stryker grinned, peering around the den of the bungalow with luggage still dangling from his hand. Lieutenant Jago Sensaki mounted the steps with the mammoth Ryder Slade in tow, leaving the captain momentarily alone at the threshold separating the living room and entrance corridor. Christian Norton slipped through the door and trudged past the commander and into the den itself, a heavy burlap sack slung over his right shoulder. The regiment commander remained steadfast as he peered despondently through the far kitchen window and into the primal wilderness of northern Vermont. The landscape, wild and uncultivated by the progress of civilization, was breathtaking and the weather was pleasantly tinged with the tell-tale elements of early spring, but still Marcus despaired. Upon that mountain looming to the north, and somewhere amongst the brush and tangled growth of the forest, one of his greatest foes now camped. A foe that was once his friend. And one that had once saved his sanity, and perhaps his life. “Bruce,” he thought silently to himself with eyes narrowed, “someday I pray you will renounce the dark ways and dispel the hatred from your heart. Things don’t need to be this way…..” The interior was sparsely furnished and devoid of common conveniences save electricity and an old-fashioned phone, the latter of which Stryker had been warned had been known to be cut off during periods of bad weather. An antiquated radio served as the other lone medium between the remote cottage and the outside world, and typically the only one if indeed the phone would be cut off, but the equipment Stryker and his Delta Fox team carried assured they wouldn’t be handicapped by such circumstances. The team set out unpacking their belongings for the next several days where upon they would remain at the Blue Enoch Lodge, gathering intelligence on the cult based among the piney thicket of the hills and possibly launching a quick strike against their isolated ‘estate’ if necessary. They bore various scientific equipment, provisions, munitions and clothing to last the week, and Sensaki had brought with him a small library of occult texts as reference against the things the Sect of Black Wisdom reputedly commanded when the light was vanquished and darkness came. In light of the deaths of the Muldoon couple, rumors had spread rampantly around the county about dark things lurking the hills on certain accursed nights, but to Delta Fox these rumors were not mere gossip but hard truths. They had faced malign things in the past and were now acquainted with secrets most men were fortunate enough to remain ignorant to. And it was their sworn duty to maintain that veil of ignorance. The metallic clatter of pans and dishes emanated from the kitchen where Lieutenant Vanessa Wilder was organizing utensils in the cramped pantry, a pot of fresh coffee brewing across the bare counter. She whipped her light blonde hair back and sighed indignantly as a throng of pots tumbled from the shelves and into the sink with a prolonged clatter. Stryker, passing by, lifted his head and glanced into the kitchen, making momentary eye contact with the beautiful female officer. She rolled her eyes at the mishap but offered a meager smile before setting back to her duties. The captain chuckled to himself before continuing on, slowly opening the screen door and stepping out into the chill northeastern night with a windbreaker to shield him from the frigid breeze. His eyes transfixed on the ridge jutting out over the peaks of the pines, the background bathed in the orange lambency of the setting sun. He thought of the local tales of great bonfires seen on starry nights atop the ledge, and the perverse chants and otherworldly piping accompanying their fury. A hand rested on his left shoulder, causing Marcus to start nervously. Sensaki took his commander’s side, a mug of coffee in his hand. Steam wisped and curled up from the black liquid, dissipating into the atmosphere as the lieutenant took a cautious sip. “Gotta admit, captain, Vanessa can make some good coffee.” “She’s good for more than just making coffee, thankfully,” replied Stryker. “Damn fine soldier, especially for such a gorgeous woman. Sometimes I don’t think we would have survived for so long without her being there in the clutch.” Jago silently nodded agreement, casting his eyes on the heavens over the mountain. He grinned and let out a sigh, enraptured at the brilliant glow painting the skies and casting the already dark highland in feral shadow. “Whatever could have driven Bruce into this frame of mind?” pondered the Asian aloud. “I never believed he would embrace the darkness, and least not so ardently. It’s just surreal really.” Stryker bit his lower lip, shuddering as a brisk breeze swept by. The shudder wasn’t a consequence of the wind as much as it was recollections of Bruce’s fervent, ireful rants he would typically break into before his turn to the gospel of the Old Ones. “He always had a reason,” analyzed the young officer. “The hate was there, the man had an aura that reeked of contempt. He resented society, Jago, every damn thing about it. Had no qualms about seeing civilization’s destruction…..hell he craved to see it. But somehow, and for some reason, he kept it all concealed within himself. Bruce has a conscience, or at least had a conscience. The dark ones though…..they found his Achilles’ heel and they played it up. They used it to turn a dire threat into one of their most invaluable mortal agents.” Sensaki shook his head warily, mournful of the situation. “Maybe there’s hope?” “There’s always hope.” Stryker turned his head and gazed at his subordinate. “I don’t wanna kill Bruce,” he calmly iterated. “Not unless all alternatives have been depleted. But if it comes to that…..then it will be done. In this current mindset, he’s too much of a danger to mankind to subsist. The ideas he harbors…..they could bring about the annihilation of man if they come to fruition.” Sensaki casually sipped from his coffee again, echoing a dry chuckle. “Humanity is already teetering on the brink, captain. He’s just needs to give that final nudge….” “Captain!” The cry came from the porch where the massive Lieutenant Slade now loomed. Stryker turned to look at his associate while Jago continued to admire the horizon. “Equipment’s all in place, Marcus. Sergeant Norton just set tripwires around the perimeter so no one is entering or leaving this area tonight without our knowing it.” Stryker nodded from afar, tapping the Asian on the shoulder as he turned on his heel and began his pace back toward the lodge. Sensaki fell into stride beside him, traversing the brown lawn and ascending the creaky porch steps before finding the others resting comfortably in the den. “Everything’s in place?” inquired the commander. “Yes sir,” Slade grunted. “All the equipment has been installed upstairs and the property has been lined with detection devices to set off an alarm if we have any trespassers. Emergency generator is in place and working up to standards.” “No contact with the town of Brayston?” Stryker asked with false hopes. “None. State police investigated the town a couple days after contact was first lost, said the people were acting strangely but not bizarrely enough to warrant further investigation. Made excuses about the power lines being toppled in a freak storm, cops bought it. Said they were mending the problem themselves.” “And they apparently haven’t yet?” “No phone calls have made it into the village yet. So if they are fixing it then they’re taking their damn sweet time doing it.” Stryker’s visage took on a quizzical expression. “Doesn’t make sense. From what we’ve been told they know Bruce is a madman, you’d think they’d want to maintain contact with authorities. I don’t like it.” He rubbed his chin for a moment, brooding over the scenario. Instincts told him to fear the worst, and he had come to trust his instincts by now. Something simply didn’t smell right about the whole situation. “Nothing changes,” he concluded after momentary contemplation. “Same plan as before, one person awake at all times. I’d wager money that Bruce knows we’re here by now, and the man is as shrewd as a fox when he’s protecting something. I don’t intend on trusting any electronic security. Understood?” “Yes sir,” everyone chimed in. “At least one pair may have to share a bed,” he announced. “Unless, of course, somebody is willing to sleep on the floor.” Sergeant Christian Norton chuckled, maneuvering closer to the shapely young Wilder on the edge of the old taupe couch. She cast her eyes in his direction, immediately groaning upon noticing the wily smile dancing on his lips. “What do you think, Vanessa?” said Norton, slipping an arm around the soft shoulders of the female lieutenant while he eagerly eyed her curves. “Wanna run a field test on the quality of the springs?” She rolled her eyes, sharply elbowing him in the ribs and sliding out of his embrace. “Drop dead, Christian,” she replied with an irritated mumble. “Lieutenant Wilder will have her own quarters,” interjected Stryker with a tepid laugh to break the tension. “God knows we don’t need any distractions. Am I right, Sergeant Norton?” Norton, still eyeing Wilder, glanced at the commander. “Huh? Oh yeah, of course, captain.” Stryker, grinning spiritedly, glimpsed the dying light over the mountain to the west. Darkness was descending on the shunned countryside and the inevitable was almost upon them. “Alright. Do as you will, but at first sign of anything suspicious, be on your toes. We should have affiliates from the Wilmarth Foundation organization arriving to aid us tomorrow, but tonight it’s just the five of us. For now if they’re not Delta Fox, they don’t get in here. And if they don’t have two legs and two eyes, and they don’t fit the furry woodland creature category, they don’t get out of here alive. Comprende, folks? “We got it, cap’n,” Slade said with a nod, adjusting his holstered sidearm. “Good. Consider it a vacation until tomorrow. But if we have guests before tomorrow, then the party’s over. We ain’t in Kansas anymore.” He paused momentarily as an inhuman howl split the night air, its guttural wail echoing the horrors of oblivion.... * * * * * “M’lord? They have arrived at the lodge.” Brother Asenath kneeled at the threshold of the doorway into the attic shrine, a subdued grunt echoing from within. A figure garbed in spangled black robes, that of Bruce ‘Violence Jack’ Shanahan, arose to face his subordinate. A series of obscene runes decorated his forehead and a stoic expression was firm upon his lips. Even now, his mind was ripe with mantras and hideous verses of the darkest scripture. “A part of me mourns, Brother Asenath,” iterated the diabolical cult leader. “Believe me when I say that a portion of me still exists that wishes this confrontation to never come about. It is tragic. However….” He frowned, clutching a leather-bound tome in his calloused hands. “……it simply must be done. The decree of the masters is not something to be ignored. Otherwise the favor I was shown two Sundays ago may not last. And the last thing I need this Friday is for my blessing to be erased. Especially not with that washed-up freak.” Asenath bowed in accordance. “Of course, sir.” “I’ll be leaving Thursday night. You are in charge until I return Friday afternoon. Do not fail me, Brother Asenath, or may Great Cthulhu have mercy on your soul.” “What are your wishes, m’lord?” Shanahan laid the volume down on a table covered in hastily scrawled notes and splotches of pen ink. A candle in the west side of the room cast the side of his face in shadow, further twisting his sinister grin into one of most insidious intentions. “As we discussed. Let the newest servitors of the Old Ones prove their merit the next couple nights. If they prove unsuccessful.....the real fun begins.”
