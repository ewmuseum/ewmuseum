Pharaoh and The Garden of Eden

19 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 19th of July, 2002 Our scene opens up in the lush green Garden of Eden. Yes, that’s right, you read it correctly, folks, our scene does indeed open up in the very same garden that Adam and Eve were evicted from by the big man himself, God. Trees, plants and small interstellar shrubberies enter our vision as we gaze about the amazing botanical ballroom of vegetation; a distinct smell of ripe fruit entering our nostrils as we breath the luscious air of this Godly realm, which could of course be aptly named ‘God’s Backyard’. “How the hell did we get here?!” echoes the voice of a woman… “Oh, don’t worry about that! We’re here for a reason, and how we got here isn’t really that important!” replies the voice of a well-spoken man… There’s a brief pause before: “Honestly!” A tall bramble bush nearby shakes furiously before out from the back of it walks the imposing sight of The Pharaoh and his gorgeous wife, Isiy! Come to think about it though, that isn’t that imposing at all, but its better than nothing, I guess. Well anyway…Isiy and Titu make their way along a small path, perfectly kept and furnished to almost perfect idealness. Its sides are trimmed down to the nearest millimetre and the cobblestone of which it is made out of don’t seem to show any signs of weathering at all. The Pharaoh: “Interesting…the Garden of Eden is almost exactly as I imagined it, perfect in every minute way.” Isiy: “Yeah, even the garden gnomes are sculpted to look real!” To Isiy’s left a pond, with a selection of Garden gnomes surrounding it, is found. They undeniably do look almost real but as people know gnomes don’t exist – they’re not real. As the couple walk on through the garden one of the little men takes off his tall red hat and wipes the sweat from his brow. He looks around cautiously and then returns his concentration to fishing. Our concentration on the other hand now returns to the Egyptian couple moving gracefully through the spectacular Garden of God. Pharaoh: “I’m glad I came here…it’s a soothing place. Finally I can take my mind off of business.” Isiy nods. Isiy: “Finally! I mean, you’ve been talking gibberish about Xavier Kannon for a few weeks now! It’s getting annoying…” The Pharaoh: (Frowning) “Well you would too if you’re wife had been beaten up, you’d been humiliated in front of a crowd in a museum, and you’d been mummified and shipped off to Egypt!” Isiy looks at her husband, guilt clearly present in her gorgeous blue eyes. Isiy: “Sorry, but ya’ know…I get worried, and you thinking about business all the time isn’t good for you…” Pharaoh points to the still healing gash on his forehead. The Pharaoh: “And this is?!” He sighs. The Pharaoh: “If I am to defeat Kannon anytime soon I must concentrate on him…I must find his weakness! But for know I’m here for a reason!” Up ahead a small, but bulky cobblestone bridge can be made out. It flows over a gentle stream underneath and a couple of bright green leafed trees climb high up over the top of it, interlinking and crossing each other in the middle to add shade to the cobblestone floor below. The couple begin to cross it when - what can only be called - a miniature whirlwind picks up, collecting soil and a few blades of grass from around the bridge’s entrance. Pharaoh and Isiy shade their eyes from the blowing partials of muck as the whirlwind begins to take the form of a human shape, slows down and leaves a man, looking a little dizzy and dressed in a long white robe - the same sort a monk would wear, standing in its place. Man: “I still haven’t got the knack for that, yet!” Pharaoh un-shields his face and looks the man up and down thoughtfully. The Pharaoh: “ Err…God?” Man: “Yes! That’s right, I am the one those humans call… God!” The Pharaoh: “Well it’s a pleasure--…” God: “Now what the heaven are you two doing here? You’re Titu Xansiv, right?” The Pharaoh: “Yes--…” God: (Sighing) “So why aren’t you visiting…Duat…or somewhere? A place where the Egyptian Gods live? Huh?” Pharaoh bows his head, a little embarrassed. The Pharaoh: “Well, Horus and Amun-Ra were all booked up for visions today--…” God: (Impatiently) “So, what? You thought you’d come here? To my garden, and bug the heaven out of me all day? What about Seth? Couldn’t he have seen you?” The Pharaoh: (Dismissively) “Seth isn’t exactly a… nice guy to meet…” God strokes his stubbly chin, deep in thought. God: (Helpfully) “Osiris?” The Pharaoh: “He’s the lord of the Egyptian Underworld, sir, I’m sure he has more pressing matters to take care of--…” God: “What? And I don’t? Look, Titu, I know you’re like all high and mightily thought of by Horus and all his buddies, but to tell you the truth you aren’t very well thought of by myself…” The Pharaoh: (Shocked) “Well, why?!” God: “Well mainly because you just came to my garden without even asking me!” Xansiv laughs. The Pharaoh: “That’s a pathetic excuse--…” Gods face grows red and furious, his eyes blazing…literally, with spiking flames of red and orange fire. God: “How dare you call me pathetic, Mortal!” The Pharaoh: (Stepping back) “Hey, hey, calm down, I was only kidding, geez!” God’s eyes slowly return to normal, as does his facial colouring. God: “Ok, answer me one question, then…” The Pharaoh: (Folding arms) “Shoot…” God: “How did you get here?” Pharaoh looks at Isiy for a little help, but of course his wife can’t help him out, she doesn’t have the slightest idea how they got to The Garden of Eden. Pharaoh neglected to tell her this fact, most likely because he himself didn’t know. The Pharaoh: “Well actually, I don’t really know…” God: (Smiling) “Good! If you can’t remember how you got here, then that means you won’t be bothering me again!” The Pharaoh: Not necessarily--…” The expression God gives Pharaoh at this moment renders the Egyptian to stop in mid sentence. The Pharaoh: “Err. Yes! We…won’t know…how to …get here…again…” Isiy: (Interrupting) “Err, yeah, well maybe we should be going, then. What do you think--…” God: (Bellowing tone) “Yes! Perhaps you should!” The Pharaoh: “Now hang on one minute!” God looks a little shocked. The Pharaoh: “God, today I came here, with my beautiful wife, to ask you something…” God: “Oh?” The Pharaoh: (Proudly) “Yep! I came to take TheTest, God…with your permission of course?” The big man himself looks puzzled. God: “The Test?” The Pharaoh: “Yeah! The one, ya’ know, where you have to not eat that fruit, and stuff?” God smiles. God: “Oh! that test…” Pharaoh nods. God: “Well then, follow me…” God turns and walks over the small bridge, a beaming smile upon his face. He knows too well that if Pharaoh can’t resist the fruit, and he won’t of course, it’s the fruit of Eden dammit, then he’ll be kicked out of the garden…Then God can go back to gardening! I mean those damned conifers need watering, and after that he’d have feed the fish in the pond…and after that, well…lets just say the rose beds are getting out of hand… And they say that God has it easy, so much that they know…huh? God: “You aren’t afraid of snakes are you?” The Pharaoh: “No, why?” God: “How about talking snakes?” The Pharaoh: (Beginning to get confused) “Err, no…why?” God doesn’t answer, he simply continues walking through the luscious Garden of Eden. As he walks however it seems that more grass grows under his step, very unusual thing to happen, that is. But then again…he isGod! The Pharaoh: “Are we nearly there yet, God?” God: “No…” The Pharaoh: “Are you sure?” God: “Of course I’m sure, I made this garden!” The Pharaoh: “‘K” Minutes pass before the trio enter a passage carved into a tall stone cliff face. Pharaoh gazes around in amazement at the wondrous surrounding of the precipices inside. A bright beam of light floods down through the roof of the cave-like place illuminating a tall tree, bathed in various bright fruits. God: “Gaze upon these fruits and resist temptation…” Isiy: “They look…so…yummy! The Pharaoh: “No, Isiy! You mustn’t!” God smirks as Isiy walks slowly towards the tree… The Pharaoh: (Rushing to his wife’s aid) “No, No, no!” Pharaoh pulls Isiy back from the tree but he himself is suddenly drawn towards the brightly coloured fruits. One of them catches his eye; it glows unnaturally with a lime green light, and tiny, tiny skulls can be made out if you look carefully enough…but typically Pharaoh doesn’t look carefully enough… The Pharaoh: (Turning to God) “ Can I have this one, God?” God: “No! That one will kill--…I mean Yes, yes of course…” Those strawberries in the fields will need some work too, he thinks… Voice: “Go on, take a bite…” Pharaoh’s attention is diverted from the fruit upwards where he sees a snake dangling from a twig near his face. The Pharaoh: “Goddamit, God! You’ve got a snake in your tree!” Pharaoh takes off his sandal and belts the snake over the head with it; the creature flops lifelessly to the floor before evaporating abruptly in red flame. God looks shocked. God: “You…you…you killed him?” God gulps. God: “You killed Lucifer!” The Pharaoh: (Puzzled) “I killed who? I killed the snake, God, I thought you’d be pleased!” Pharaoh sighs. The Pharaoh: (Grumbling) “Some Immortals these days!” Suddenly a bright white light flashes over the area, brightening the cave considerably. Everyone, even God, shades their eyes as the glow slowly diminishes. Pharaoh looks up to find a guy, dressed completely in red and black, a pair of horns protruding from his head, and a tiny folk-shaped tail flowing from his ass, standing in front of them. Satan: (Looking down at the dead snake) “Ah, dammit! I liked that body, too!” The Devil clears his throat… Satan: “Ok…here goes…(Bellowing) Titu Xansiv! By killing the snake in this tree you have resisted temptation! This tree is now free from temptation, my friend! You have done what no other man has done before!” Pharaoh blushes. The Pharaoh: “Well geez, thanks…” Satan notices God. Satan: “Hey, God…” Isiy: “I thought you guys hated each other?” God: “Bah! That’s what the church says!” Satan: (Walking towards God) “Yep, they say all these nasty things about me, and I mean, if the world didn’t have evil it’d be unbalanced, right? And someone has to do it, and that guy’s me!” God: “But in actual fact, Satan ain’t such a bad guy.” The Pharaoh: “Oh?” Satan: “It’s the damn media, they hype it all up, ya’ see” Pharaoh scowls. The Pharaoh: “Damn them to hell!” Satan: (Helpfully) “That can be arranged” The Pharaoh waves a hand at Satan. The Pharaoh: “Nah, I don’t think its necessary.” Satan sags. Satan: “Aww, I really need some good old-fashioned people to damn in hell, too…” The Pharaoh: “Well, guys, since I’ve resisted the fruit I think my wife and I will be getting going. ” At last, thinks God. The Pharaoh: “I have a match pretty soon, and it’s going to be a tough one, I actually have to team up with my worst enemy… *sighs* Hey, Satan, are Frisco or Xavier Kannon on your list for damnation?” As if by magic a checklist appears in The Devil’s hands. He scrolls a black nailed finger down the many names in front of him and then looks up. Satan: “Not for another twenty to thirty years…” The Pharaoh: “Ah well…I suppose one can only hope, huh?” The devil nods. God: “I’ll show you the way out.” The Pharaoh: “Thanks…” The trio of God, Isiy and Pharaoh walk out of the cave leaving Satan to look over his list. Satan: (Surprised) “Dale Winton?!” Our scene returns to God as he looks down at a small shrubbery in his garden. God: (Sighing) “Caterpillars are deadly at this time of the year!” He looks back up at The Pharaoh and Isiy. God: “Well anyway…I’ll show you the way out, folks…” God points a long finger towards a seemingly normal looking tree. Slowly but surely the sky parts ways and what appears to be the living room of the Xansiv mansion appears. Pharaoh smiles. The Pharaoh: “Well I guess this is good--…” God: (Impatiently) “Yes, yes, just go, will you…the parsnips need tending to!” Pharaoh shrugs as Isiy walks through the portal to the mortal realm, Pharaoh follows her as God sighs… God: “Well at least the snake’s dead, it’s been pissing me off for a while now, anyway…” The scene fades to black as God walks off, apparently to tend to the parsnips.
