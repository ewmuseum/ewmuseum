Aforementioned Warning, Part II

8 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11.8.02 Downtown Chicago… “I think we’ve seen this before…” -Letting loose a frustrated sigh, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins shoots Lacey Reinking a flat stare, more than implying that she keep her comments to herself. Walking about the bustling downtown area of Chicago was tricky even on the calmest days, but tonight was made worse with the drizzling rain. People always seemed to travel in the rain.- -Clutching his black leather jacket tight, Rollins ushers his blonde accomplice past the little café they had passed three times now, a clear signal that they had taken a few wrong turns. Grinning smugly, Lacey grips her pink umbrella tighter as she rolls her deep blue eyes at The Renegade.- Lacey: You know, we could ask for directions… Renegade: (harshly) No. Lacey: But we’ve… Renegade: No. Lacey: I …! Renegade: You’re getting your hair wet. Lacey: … -Jerking her umbrella back over her head, Lacey sticks her lower lip out in a mock-insulted manner, walking ahead of Rollins stiffly. Shaking his head stubbornly, The Renegade kicks along across the sleek pavement, the headlights of hundreds of yellow cabs refracting off the wet surface. Searching for some recognizable landmark, he desperately searches for the radio station he is scheduled to be interviewed at in half an hour, actually enjoying the lack of interference from his beautiful partner.- -The silence doesn’t last long.- Lacey: What’s the radio station’s call numbers? Renegade: (absently) Talk Radio KKTD 90.73. Lacey: You know… maybe we should go back and try to find that café we’ve passed a dozen times now. -Rollins sighs, his concentration now hopelessly broken.- Renegade: We don’t have time to grab a bite to eat, Lace. I have an interview in thirty… Lacey: No, you’re not getting it. We’ve been looking for this radio station for almost an hour now, and all we’ve been able to find is that café. Maybe, just maybe, if we look for the café… Renegade: … then we’ll find the radio station by mistake? (he grumbles) How Taoist of you. Lacey: Well, it doesn’t look like your “I’m-A-Stubborn-Man” routine is going anywhere, now does it? -The Renegade rolls his eyes, but he knows the battle is lost. Arguing with her is like screaming at a brick wall… only less productive. Smiling in self-satisfaction, Lacey turns around and begins to walk in the opposite direction, cutting through the sea of busy tourists rushing to get out of the rain. She tosses an arrogant grin back at Rollins as she pushes past him, pausing only a moment to allow him to follow. Looking to the bleak gray sky in frustration, The Renegade grudgingly falls behind her, ignoring the stream of water that soaks his shoulder-length brown hair.- -Five minutes later, the large building that houses publication of the Chicago Tribune looks both Rich Rollins and Lacey Reinking in the face… and two doors down, the sign dwarfed by the Tribune’s, is KKTD 90.73. Beaming with pride, Lacey tries to give an “I-Told-You-So” look to Rollins, but he refuses to meet her accusing stare. Pushing in through the revolving glass door, The Renegade hurries to make the interview.- ~~ ~~ ~~ “This is Cindy Freedman, resident Wench of the Windy City, ready to take your calls and questions. Next, we’ve got the one and only Rich Rollins in the studio, local Superstar of the vaunted Primetime Championship Wrestling. Rich, how are you this evening?” -Flipping his microphone on, Cindy Freedman looks to Rollins expectantly, brushing a strand of curly red hair out of her eyes. Swinging the hanging mic closer to his chair, The Renegade smiles in response.- Renegade: I’m doing all right, Cindy. It was a little wet finding this place, but I made it. Cindy: Good to hear. So tell us… how do you feel finally making it into PCW? -The Renegade shrugs, his voice laden with confidence.- Renegade: It was a given, Cindy. In this business, the crème always rises to the top. It was just a matter of time before PCW noticed me, and they finally did. I’ll tell you though, I haven’t done half as much traveling my entire career as I have these past three weeks. We just finished up a tour of Asia, then the Middle East. Tomorrow I fly to London for Friday Fusion, and I’m pretty excited about that. Cindy: Take in a little fish ‘n chips, eh? -Rollins laughs, shifting to a more comfortable position in his chair.- Renegade: I just might do that. Cindy: (shuffling some papers) Speaking of Fusion, you’ve got a match with PURE newcomer El Romantico. Many think you might suffer your first pinfall defeat tomorrow night… your thoughts? -The Renegade slowly leans back in the stiff folding chair the studio had provided for him, letting his eyes stray to the racks of tapes and posters on the wall in the small recording station. He chooses his words carefully, speaking with a precision and matter-of-factness that raises Cindy’s eyebrows.- Renegade: I had one motivating factor that brought me into PCW, and his name is Skylar Green. The End did something few men have accomplished in pinning me, and the fact that it was in the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament irks me to no end. I told him that payback would be a bitch, but he didn’t listen. Now he’s runnin’ to the tag division, and I know it’s because he’s afraid. He’s afraid of what I might do, and after the thorough beating I gave to him last week… I don’t blame him. I’ll get my revenge, one way or another. Cindy: And his Extreme title…? Renegade: I’ve already told him that his gold means nothing to me, other than the fact that he owns it. I want it because he has it, end of story. Bob Vurdo and the four other rent-a-wrestlers that End has to plow through on Friday haven’t got a prayer, ‘cause it’s me who will knock that blind bastard off his perch, not them. Cindy: And what about El Romantico? He’s a veteran of the sport, and seems more than ready for a showdown tomorrow night. -Now it’s Rollins’ turn to raise his eyebrows. Licking his lips, The Renegade responds not unkindly, but with a steely resolve underlying his words.- Renegade: And what, you think I’m new to the world of Sports Entertainment? Hell, most of the guys around here still call me a rookie, when I’m anything but. El Romantico may have won some junior title over in PURE, but this sure as hell ain’t PURE. I defeated PCW mainstay Jon Kano and fellow PWA student Mark Amulet last week when nobody thought I could… what makes this Mexican lover-boy any different? The way I see it, anybody other than Skylar Green steps in my way, they’re holdin’ me from my goal. I don’t like being held back, Cindy. Unfortunately for Romantico, that anger and frustration is about to come out on his heartbreakin’ backside, I promise you that. -Flipping a call board switch, Cindy pulls her mic closer as she responds, brushing a stack of papers aside.- Cindy: We’ve got callers on the line, so you ready to field some questions? Renegade: Let ‘em loose. -Grinning, Cindy flips another switch as the call board lights up.- Cindy: You’re on the air… any questions for Rich Rollins? Caller #1: Yeah, I’ve got a question. Rich, I’m a big fan, I followed you through GCW and I think it’s great you landed in PCW. Renegade: (smiling) Thanks, man. What’s your question? Caller #1: I just wanted to give my condolences, man. I mean, with your brother on trial for murder and all… it has to be hard keeping up with PCW’s schedule. -A misty haze crosses The Renegade’s eyes as Cindy looks at him, concerned. She had worried about this; they had agreed not to discuss his brother’s trial in the interview, but open callers were impossible to predict. Hovering her hand over the kill switch, she waits for his nod to cut the caller’s line.- -Instead, he takes the call.- Renegade: I lived with my mother and brother Jason until I was seventeen. That’s when I left to pursue my career as a professional wrestler, ‘cause I couldn’t live on welfare checks from week to week like we had been. That’s why my brother hated me: he resents my success and the life I can afford to live. Because of his jealousy, he snapped… he started murdering innocent people to get back at me, playing me like a fiddle the entire time. I helped the police catch him, I’ll testify against him, and I hope he burns in hell for the things he has done. Next question. -Her mouth open in shock, Cindy Freedman refuses to believe what she just heard. She herself had been one of the victims, true… but she had gotten lucky. Many other people hadn’t. But sending a man to die, especially a blood relative, was almost impossible for her to imagine. The stony resolve in Rich Rollins’ eyes are enough to motivate her to kill the caller’s line.- Cindy: Folks, this conversation needs to stay wrestling-related. If you have a question for The Renegade as a wrestler, by all means, give us a ring. But any other comments or questions need to be saved, ‘cause this just ain’t the time or place. -Cindy flips another switch, linking the next caller in. Shooting Rollins a wary glance, she continues.- Cindy: This is the Wench of the Windy City, you’re on Talk Radio… you got a question for Rich Rollins? -A long pause follows. Her brow furrowing, Cindy looks into her microphone as if it holds all the answers, brushing her hair behind one ear.- Cindy: Hello? -static…static…static…- Voice: (over phone) What if they caught the wrong guy, Richard? -The very sound of the man’s voice sends a chill down Cindy’s spine. A quiet tune filters in behind the static, sounding vaguely like The Beatle’s Helter Sketler. Instantly tensing up, Rich Rollins looks out through the thick observation glass at Lacey, who wears an uneasy frown not unlike his own. Taking a deep breath, The Renegade responds.- Renegade: Nobody calls me Richard, pal. Who is this? Voice: (laughing) That’s exactly what I won’t tell you. Renegade: And why is that? -A long pause stretches out like an eternity, the phone’s static filling the small broadcast studio.- Voice: Let’s be honest, Richard. You’re brother isn’t exactly the smartest man under God’s sun, now is he? You really think he could have masterminded all those grizzly slaughters? Or even have the stomach for half of them? Renegade: What the hell do you…? Voice: You’re next, Mr. Superstar. Click. -An eerie dial tone fills the room, leaving Rollins and Cindy wide-eyed and confused. Breathing heavily, Cindy quickly tries to recover for her listening audience.- Cindy: I’m sorry folks, we’re gonna have to cut to a commercial… -Bodily tossing his rigid chair into the unbending observation window, The Renegade swears.- ~Fin~
