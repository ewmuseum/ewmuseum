Emotionless Gift Giving

27 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/27/02 Moments Prior To The Surprise Present. McGlocklan stood backstage, a pack of cigarettes held in his right hand, a Zippo in the other. Raising the cardboard to his mouth, he pulled loose a cylinder with his teeth and with a click, lit the end. A pop ensued; combusted tar crackled into the air and hovered above his nostrils, the scent of menthol caressing the hairs. Inhaling deeply, he flickered the gray smog on his tongue before blowing a ring of toxins into the air. Smacking his lips together, he spat a wad of phlegm from his mouth and lifted a black, fingerless gloved hand to the filter. Grasping it casually, he pulled the cigarette from his mouth and shoved the ash against the painted wall of the Alamo Dome and flicked it to the floor. He reached behind his back grabbed hold of the handle of his claymore, unclasping a buckle with his free hand. The leather strap to his sheath came undone and trickled to the floor, and the sword was removed from his back. Resting it against the floor, he adjusted the collar to his brown leather trench coat. “What was the signal again?” he asked, turning his golden eyes towards Chris Matthews. Matthews shrugged, running his hand through his hair. “Some kind of a chair shot… just keep watching,” he said, his eyes remaining on the monitor that lay next to them. Atop the cage, Kannon lifted a chair from the rafters and clashed it against the metallic support beams, sending a wild vibration throughout the arena. Matthews nodded and stepped forward, walking through the frame of Gate A and into the crowd of people below. McGlocklan cracked his neck and followed after, shoving people away as they grabbed at his clothing. Mordred followed, his shaved head gleaming under the spotlights that rained from above. “What the hell?! Get security out here!” shouted Kern, the microphone of his headset shaking above his earthquake of a mouth. Marx sat back in his chair, watching as the legion of RUSH wrestlers hopped over the barricade and began to scale the side of the structure. “I know some of these…” he stated, a look of shock on his face. Fake snow drifted down from the ceiling and to the floor below as the McGlocklan stood at the bottom, looking on as Kannon, toppled Mason with a chair shot to the cranium. His face remaining emotionless, he watched as his former boss toppled to the ground. With his arms folded, he glanced as Matthews who nodded and made towards the ring. Caleb followed suit, sliding under the bottom rope and grabbing hold of Mason’s head. Lifting his new found enemy up, McGlocklan slung Mason across the top rope and dropped down, planting his baldhead into the mat with authority. “This… this is a mockery!” swore Kern, beads of spit forming at the corner of his mouth and splattering towards the ground. Hauling the load over towards the middle of the ring, Xavier dropped to his knees and covered Mason and the freshly Alpha Omega’d End for a pair of three counts. With their jobs done, the two left the ring and waited outside. They watched on as Kannon mustered up his strength for the Hallmark and the victory. Helping their new employer to his feet, the dragged him up the ramp, his arms raised in celebration, blood and false snow plastered to his face. Fast Forward. >> McGlocklan sat in his chair, a lit cigarette hanging out of his mouth, and a bottle of Guinness resting between his legs. In his hand was a Zippo lighter, the other a remote. With a click, the footage on the television reminds, scratchy voices and men running backwards ensuing. The soft fizz of carbonated alcohol hummed in his ears, the foul stench of month-old cigarettes gliding above an ashtray. With a swig of the dark beer, he smacked his lips together, tasting the mellow, bitter liquid on his tongue. Tossing the empty bottle to the floor, he hit play on the remote with his thumb, the events of Alliances occurring yet again. He sat still with golden, careless, carefree, indifferent eyes watching the action unfold. His mouth remained straight, his eyes un-arched, and his emotions as dead as a cadaver’s. Emotionless… emotionless… emotionless… emotionless… “I love presents,” stated Jeff, his eyes twinkling with delight.Who doesn’t, Marx? Who doesn’t?
