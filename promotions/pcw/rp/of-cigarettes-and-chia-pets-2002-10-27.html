Of Cigarettes and Chia Pets

27 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10.27.02 An apartment complex in West Chicago... “Dude, tell me this isn’t the sexiest lookin’ hedgehog you’ve ever seen!” His face split in an ear-to-ear smile, Greg Smith produces a small plant-looking object from behind his back, holding it aloft for his roommate’s benefit. Hunched over on all fours, the tiny green organic statue of a hedgehog rests in Greg’s eager hands, its exposed backside covered in fresh blades of newly-grown grass. “I think I’m gonna name him Harvey,” Greg beams, setting his prized Chia Pet on a normally sunny ledge next to the window. Most days the rising sun shines into the room from the east, but today an overcast sky deprives the tiny plant-pet of its mandatory daylight. Greg’s roommate Johnny, however, just crosses his arms and rolls his eyes. “Christ Greg, you ever wonder why your parent’s never let you have a dog?” he asks, looking at their newest addition with more than a little uncertainty, “I mean, you can’t even remember to feed yourself half the time. I’ll give Harvey the Magical Hedgehog over there two days ‘fore he croaks.” Greg looks indignant, taking the slight as an insult directed at his beautiful new hedgehog. “I’ve got it all taken care of, Johnny. Look, the guy at the plant shop gave me this,” he retorts, reaching into a back pocket. Fumbling around his wallet, Greg finally produces a small bottle of Miracle Grow and holds it up triumphantly for his friend to see. “Harvey will want for nothing. He’s a good hedgehog.” “Whatever, man. If you want to raise a Chia Pet in you likeness, more power to you.” And with that, Johnny shuffles over to the couch set in the middle of their shared apartment and gropes for the remote control, pulling out a cigarette without giving Harvey a second thought. Lighting up, he turns the television on to the Travel Channel, becoming instantly engaged in the wonderful cuisine delicacies of Budapest. Mumbling under his breath, Greg returns his attention to Harvey while snapping the cap off of the Miracle Grow, ignoring his roommate’s disinterest. With all the delicate precision of a plastic surgeon, he squirts the liquid onto his hedgehog’s bushy green back, watching the clear chemical dissolve instantly into the soft soil. “Greg?” “Hmm?” “What are you doing?” Johnny asks over his shoulder, still attentively learning about Budapest dining. Greg shrugs, still squirting the Miracle Grow across his plant’s grassy blades. “Taking responsibility for my hedgehog,” he answers simply. “Christ, would you stop calling it a hedgehog?” Johnny says, turning around to face his roommate. “You’re acting like it’s a real animal. It’s a fuckin’ plant, Greg! And you’re gonna drown the damn thing if you keep squirting that shrubbery steroid shit on it!” Abruptly snapping the cap back on the Miracle Grow, Greg sets the can next to the Chia Pet and cracks the window open a little, letting some fresh air in. Turning around with a self-satisfied smile, he dismisses Johnny’s flat look with a sigh. “You’re just jealous ‘cause you never had a dog, either,” Greg snaps. Johnny just shakes his head sadly, turning back around. “Whatever, man. I just think someone needs a nap.” Completely giving up on this pointless argument, Greg looks his prized new “pet” over once more before stalking from the room, heading for his bedroom. Finally alone, Johnny stretches out on the couch, flicking his cigarette behind him and out the window. Almost, that is. Bouncing off the glass of the partially closed window, the still-burning cigarette butt dives straight into Harvey the Hedgehog’s plant vase, landing square on its green backside. It doesn’t take long before the fumes from the excess Miracle Grow begin to react with the business end of the smoke. ~~ ~~ ~~ O’Hare Airport, Northwest Chicago… -Stepping out of the flight gate with his black wrestling duffel bag slung casually over one shoulder, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins takes a deep breath of the familiar American air, more than happy to be back on home soil. Dressed in a simple pair of button-up black sweat pants, a white tank top and his usual black leather jacket, Rollins tightens the knot on his red bandanna out of habit, scanning the crowd in the busy airport flight deck.- Woman: Rollins! Over here! -Raising her arms above the milling crowd, Lacey Rienking pushes her way past the hustling throng, tossing her mane of golden blonde hair out of her face. Meeting her beaming face with his one of his own, The Renegade plows into the crowded hallway, embracing her with his thick arms.- Lacey: You did so good, Rich. I watched you on satellite from one of my girlfriend’s places. I don’t think Mark Michigan had a clue what hit him! -Slightly wincing, Rollins reaches his free hand up to his covered head, feeling the sting where it had taken four stitches to sew up the gash left from Mark Michigan’s underhanded chair attack.- Renegade: I don’t know, Lace. He did one helluva number on me after the match. Lacey: Pshh, please! You kicked his ass, Renegade. He was just bitter. Renegade: Well, his bitterness leaves quite a mark, I can tell you that much. It doesn’t really bother me, though. If he wants to take out his frustrations of inadequacy on me, let him try it a second time. It was just a good warm-up for my match with The End and his Extreme title. -A dark, glowering expression flashes across Rollins’ face, but is gone so quickly that Lacey wonders if it was even there. A quizzical look fills her gaze as she matches The Renegade’s hard stare.- Lacey: My my, aren’t we quite the optimist? Never mind about the #1 contender match on Tuesday, you’re just going to fight End? Renegade: You’re damn straight I’ll fight him. He’s the only man to pin me since last May, and it’s been eatin’ at my brain ever since the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament. I was so close, Lace. That tournament was supposed to be mine, and that blind bastard just came and took it from me. I don’t even care about his Extreme belt… I just want his ass in the ring, no ifs, ands or buts. Lacey: Keep that temper in check, tiger. People are starting to stare. -With an effort, The Renegade realizes he was almost yelling, and a few airport patrons were indeed giving him strange looks. Allowing himself to be led, Rollins follows along as Lacey walks him into the massing rush of humanity, heading for the exit.- Lacey: So tell me, Mr. “I’ve-Got-A-Perfect-PCW-Record,” have you ever been to Rome? -The Renegade smiles, his mind flashing back to his days with another company.- Renegade: Yeah, I have. My first GCW World title was won at the Coliseum nearly two years ago. It’s a beautiful place, what with all the rustic Roman Empire scenery and all. (he shoots her a wry glance) Why do you ask? Lacey: (shrugging) Oh, I dunno. I just heard it was a real romantic place. -A mischievous grin spreads across Lacey’s face as she looks up at him, her big blue eyes wide with flirtation. The Renegade laughs, but is cut short as the pain in his head swells.- Renegade: Absolutely not, so don’t even think about asking. I’m not taking you on the road with me. Lacey: (pleading) Oh, c’mon! I’ve always wanted to see Italy, Rich! Why can’t I go along? Renegade: Just take a good look at my head, Lace. The PCW locker room ain’t the safest place for a woman like you to be hanging around. Lacey: Oh, so Ellie can do whatever she damn well pleases while the rest of the women sit at home and… cook or something? -Rollins stops dead in his tracks, turning Lacey so she can look straight at him. Speaking in a very deliberate, slow tone, The Renegade responds very matter-of-factly.- Renegade: Hey, look at me. Really, look at who I am. I’m not the CEO of PCW, now am I? That would be Xavier Kannon’s job, and he can have it. But since this is his game and he makes all the rules, Ellie can make her own choices on what risks she takes. Hell, did you watch Red October? Clyde nearly decapitated her pretty little head with a chair shot. I won’t, and I mean absolutely won’t let that happen to you. -Puffing out her lower lip, Lacey dons a mask of mock-disappointment as she puts her arm through Rollins’, once again walking through the jungle of airport patrons.- Lacey: Fine. (playfully grinning) We’ll see where you get to sleep tonight, mister. -Laughing, Rich Rollins scoops her lithe form over his shoulder with one arm, ignoring her startled bursts of insulted giggling as he carries her through the airport.- ~~ ~~ ~~ An hour later at Lacey’s apartment, West Chicago… Lacey: What the hell happened here? -Her eyes wide with surprise, Lacey watches as a squad of firefighters valiantly battle against the licking tongues of flames shooting from a third-story balcony. Turning her head, she notices that The Renegade’s look is more reserved, almost amused. After all, it isn’t his apartment building.- -Mixing into a small gathering held back by police barricades, Lacey and Rollins watch as three of Chicago’s Finest hold a powerful water hose in place, desperately trying to douse the fire before it spreads. Looking closer at the burning balcony, Lacey shakes her sadly.- Lacey: That looks like Johnny and Greg’s room. They used to conveniently show up at the pool every time I went to sunbathe. Stupid horny men. Renegade: You know, if this place is gonna be crawling with firemen all night… we can always head over to my place. -Lacey smiles, turning her bright blue eyes on Rollins’ face like a hawk looking for a meal.- Lacey: I thought you’d never ask. ~~ ~~ ~~ Sitting with his head in his arms, Johnny moans on the gritty cracked curb while a police officer questions Greg, the heat of their house fire infuriatingly hot on the back of his neck. Johnny had never considered himself a violent man, but at this moment in time, he really wanted to kill something. Something small, green, and hedgehog-shaped. “So, you say it wasn’t an electrical fire?” the female officer inquires, jotting something down on the note pad in her hand. Turning her eyes on Greg, she waits for an answer. Greg sheepishly looks to his feet, avoiding the officer’s stare. “No… it was my hedgehog. Um… I think Johnny is more suited to answer this one. John?” -Digging his forehead deeper into his folded arms, Johnny speaks hollowly, his words mumbled. “You see officer, it was like this…” ~Fin~
