Whiskey Bent and Hell Bound

10 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Title: Whiskey Bent and Hell Bound Featuring: Duke Williams Date: 03/10/03 Corn Cob Blues by John Hartford plays in the background of Duke’s private gym in the basement of his colonel mansion in Missoula, Montana. John Hartford is a great singer songwriter who fell through the cracks of commercial success. Somewhere between Roger Miller and Bob Dylan with a twist of bluegrass, like Duke he never fit in. “I’ll never die now, for my image and words will be remembered forever on classic wrestling shows and history channel specials. Pop icons never die they just fade away into obscurity to be asked as the 100,000 question on some futurist game show.” Duke says while lifting a weight on the bench. Duke is lying on his back lifting up about three hundred pounds with out much effort as he talks and thinks things over. He’s been spending a lot of time thinking as his time draws to a close in PCW. It’s been a long ride with lots of ups and downs. “I’m going to live forever. I’m going to cross that river. I’m going to catch tomorrow night. This Sunday I’m going to win the PCW world heavyweight title not for my fans, my legacy, or my ego. I’m going to it for myself. When I leave here nobody will ever find me but I’ll always be around just like the work I leave behind me. I career that spans more then a lifetime.” Continues Duke in the mist of working out. Duke’s been called everything from the Merle Haggard, Mark Twain, Teddy Roosevelt of professional wrestling to simple a lovable loser. His favorite nickname he’s acquired over the years has been the Hillbilly Delhi Lama. Always standing up for the workingman and the simpler ways of life that has been his motive of operation and always will be. “I started an out as an old chunk of coal but I’ve been pure plumb polished and look at this diamond now!” Reflects the Malice Man. Duke puts the weight down back on the rack as he sits up on the bench and cools himself with a towel that are branded with his logo. Duke’s son is now a wrestler in an up and coming promotion. Although they have a strained relationship he’s proud of him boy. The tradition lives on in the offspring of the Malice Man and that reassures Duke. “To let something go you’ve worked so hard at building is almost impossible. Artists have died with paintbrushes in their hands finishing that final stroke of that life’s work. Sunday I finish my portrait but do I finish it with masterpiece or an eclectic piece of shit that will soon be forgotten like the smoke of burning trade towers in the minds of anti-war protesters?” Dukes says in a serious low tone. Duke is finished working out and ready to retire. No more long hours in the gym and worrying about a game plane. Just Jeopardy and martini’s from here on out. Not a bad way to go out if you ask me. “If I’ve said it once I’ve said it a hundred times this Sunday get ready to be sent Whiskey Bent and Hell Bound one last time.” Duke says while looking into the camera with a cold stare. =w= ---- View Duke Williams's Biography
