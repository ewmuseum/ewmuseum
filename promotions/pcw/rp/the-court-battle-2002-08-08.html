The court battle!!!

8 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 8, 2002 {{{{Scene}}}}The scene opens up on a perfect mild hot summer day. Semore Smoke is standing outside the Atlanta, Georgia courthouse and is looking around at the people swarming into and out of the courthouse. Some involved in other cases, and some that is involved in his case. Smoke is wearing a business type of attire, and Mick Palmer by his side wearing a similar ensemble. They both look to be serious for this case, the whole time Smoke has been saying that he didn’t do it, but Mick has been a little skeptical. Looks like this court battle could be the worst battle Semore’s ever had to battle, because if convicted, he could serve from seven to ten years in prison, missing out on his PCW career for sure. Smoke looks at his watch and then looks at Mick with a look of shock and horror on his face… Semore Smoke: Mick! We’ve only got fifteen minutes! Let’s get going! {{{{Semore runs up the stairs towards the courthouse and Mick follows suit. They both dodge several people as they make their way to the entrance of the courthouse. Smoke runs up to the entrance double doors and opens them, he runs into the vast courthouse and looks around the lobby area. He finally sees several people he recognizes, in fact they’re the people who that were working security and pulled him aside. Smoke follows them, knowing that they’d lead him to the room where his court case would be taking place. Smoke looks back to make sure Mick is behind him, and indeed he is. They cease from running and start a steady walk behind the others as they all walk down the hallway. They turn onto another hallway and the first door on the left they turn into. Smoke follows suit as does Mick behind him. The courtroom is surprisingly crowded with people. Smoke didn’t expect many people to show up for a petty crime such as what he’s accused for. But in fact several had, many of them probably wrestling fans who came to get a glimpse of their favorite wrestler as he battles the court system just like he would if he were against Scott Taylor again, or maybe Lance Perfection. The four guards from the airport turn and head for their lawyer and seats towards the front of the court on the left. Mick sits down in the audience as Smoke turns to the right and sits down beside the lawyer that the PCW had appointed him for the case. Smoke knew the lawyer as they had spoken on occasions, but this was the first case that he would be doing for Smoke, so he didn’t want him to screw it up. As soon as Smoke sits down the bailiff standing in the courtroom asks...}}}} Bailiff: If everyone would please rise for the honorable Judge Lucas. {{{{Smoke stands up as does his lawyer and the rest of the courtroom. Then a man of slight build walks into the room wearing a long black robe that is accustomed with all judges and he walks up to his seat and sits down. He bangs the gable down on his work area in front of him and announces to the court…}}}} Judge Lucas: You may be seated. Today we’re here to judge the court hearing between Semore Smoke and the state of Georgia, which will be represented by it’s D.A. as well as the four guards who reported Mr. Smoke in the first place. First I’d like to lay down the law to you two groups, first off, if you lie to me and I know you lied to me, then you can bet your chances are swaying me with a decision are slim to none. Now I’d like to the D.A. of Georgia to stand and present his case to the court against Mr. Smoke. {{{{The D.A. who is dressed much like everyone in the formal situation of the court is dressed stands up in front of the judge and the courtroom. He then speaks aloud so everyone can hear him…}}}} D.A.: Today we’re hear to put a man away who has not once, but many times in the past broken the laws with his drug antics on TV, as well as other places. You see, Mr. Smoke here is a wrestler in Primetime Championship Wrestling, a company which prides itself on the entertainment of it’s viewers. Now, one of their top draws to many youngsters in the crowd is Mr. Smoke, who uses drugs influence and other things to buy the crowd over for him. Mr. Smoke has been indicted on drug cases in the past, but none serious enough to put him away for good. But this takes the cake, these four men to my left found Mr. Smoke looking suspicious at the Atlanta Airport as he was flying back in after an event the PCW had held in Jamaica earlier that week. The day was August 3, 2002, and the time was around noon. Semore was walking through customs looking suspicious as these men put it and they pulled him aside, considering what happened last September 11… {{{{Semore’s lawyer, who’s name is James Macklin stands up in front of the court and yells…}}}} Macklin: Objection! What happened last September 11th has nothing to do with my client’s case. Judge Lucas: Sustained. Please leave certain events such as that out of your argument, but you may continue. {{{{The D.A. clears his throat and gathers his thoughts…}}}} D.A.: Thank you Judge Lucas. But the extra security measures had them check his bag. Where in a secret compartment they found a stash of approximately 2.5 kilos of Marijuana in his bag. These four men then cuffed him and escorted him to the security outpost where the police later came and picked him up. So therefore Mr. Smoke was charged with smuggling illegal drugs into the United States of America. That is all I have to say. {{{{The D.A. sits down in his seat and looks over at Semore and winks and gives him a smile as he looks to think he has it in the bag. James Macklin stands up and walks around the table where he was sitting at. He then looks around the courtroom with a smile of confidence on his face…}}}} Macklin: Ladies and gentlemen of the courtroom, Judge Lucas, I would like to present my case against this one. My client might have been pulled over and the bag that was beside him might have been carrying illegal substances, but I’ve got evidence that shows that not only was he wrongfully accused but also that the bag was not his bag. Now, of course I bet the prosecution doesn’t have any similar evidence, and the only reason we do is because we claimed it first, we’ve got witnesses for it as well. Now, even though my client was wrongfully accused, he went to a rehab center to show that if he had done such a crime that he was willing to seek help for it. Now, today my job is only to prove my client’s innocence, nothing more. So without further of due, I would like to call forward my first witness you honor. Judge Lucas: You may proceed. Macklin: Thank you, I’d like to call forth Mr. Mick Palmer! {{{{Mick stands up from the mass crowd watching the court case and walks forward. He steps through the double barricade door’s that stand between the judge, as well as the others involved in the case and the crowd. Palmer then walks around to take his oath, where the bailiff is standing there with a Bible in his hand. Palmer places his hand on the Bible as the bailiff ask him the same question that everyone hears…}}}} Bailiff: Do you swear to tell the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth, so help your God? Mick: I do. Bailiff: You may proceed. {{{{Palmer walks around the Bailiff and steps up onto the witness chair and sits down. Macklin walks around Mick and looks at him, and then starts off with simple questions…}}}} Macklin: For the record, could you please state you whole full name. Mick Palmer: Michael John Palmer. Macklin: Thank you, now, how long have you known my client? Mick Palmer: For about two years now. We met when he was younger, I was still working in the business and he was just coming into it. I was working in a federation called the People’s World Wrestling Federation and was working under a name known as Mick “Hardcore” Holly. Macklin: Now, Mr. Palmer, when you say the business you mean the wrestling business, right? Mick Palmer: Yes sir. Macklin: So, does my client seem like a person who would smuggle drugs into a country? Mick Palmer: No he doesn’t. In fact Simon over there wouldn’t hurt a fly other then someone in the ring. Of course, he’s broken a few laws, but who hasn’t? Someone has at least jay walked, probably rode their bike on the wrong side of the road, or participated in underage drinking and smoking. This world is full of a lot of people and I can bet that none of them have never ever committed a crime in their whole life. But Simon wouldn’t do anything that illegal, it’s true, he does have a history of Marijuana use, but he’s been long over that. In fact he went to rehab sessions earlier this week that I joined with him as a witness. The guy is totally changed. Macklin: Thank you Mr. Palmer, your witness D.A. {{{{The D.A. stands up and walks up to Palmer with a grin on his face. He looks at Palmer and then back at Smoke and then his grin changes to that of confusion and then he asks his first question…}}}} D.A.: Now, you called Semore Smoke, Simon is that correct? Mick Palmer: Yes it is, that’s his real first name Semore is just his middle name. D.A.: Ah, I just needed to clear that up. So, where were you on August 3, 2002 around noon? Mick Palmer: Well, I was at home working on some things that go for a federation that I’m a part of. D.A.: Ah, so if you were not there, then how would you know that Mr. Smoke did not commit this crime? Mick Palmer: Because Simon is a guy who would stand up for himself if he was committed of a crime and take responsibility of it, now if he says he didn’t do it then I can bet you he didn’t do it. D.A.: Ok, fair enough, also you said you were working for a federation. What federation would that be? Mick Palmer: New True Internet Championship Wrestling. D.A: Yet you’re supposed to be retired from in ring competition right? Mick Palmer: I am, I’m working as the President of the company. I’ve been retired from in ring competition for over a year now. D.A.: Ah, I’ve also heard that you’ve written a book, in fact I’ve got it right here. It was never really famous, in fact it was only published in the Wilson, North Carolina area, but I managed to get a copy of it. Now, here is a passage about when you were still working with PWWF and it’s when you first met Semore Smoke. May I read it aloud? Mick Palmer: Sure, go ahead, I don’t see anything wrong with it. D.A.: Thank you. “Simon showed up in my life for the first time on this day, of course I had just lost to D2K in an I Quit match, but this man said he was a huge fan of mine, so I had to meet him. He looks to be a bright young man with quite the future ahead of him. But, when I saw him he was smoking a cigarette. I didn’t approve of such things, but I couldn’t argue with my biggest fan, could I?” So Mr. Palmer, you met Mr. Smoke and he was smoking a cigarette, right? Mick Palmer: Yes sir, but I kind of used creative license in that passage. D.A.: Creative license? Please explain what that is. Mick Palmer: I made up some of it. D.A.: Ah so you lied in your autobiography, now! I ask you in front of this courtroom, what did you lie about? Mick Palmer: Well, it wasn’t right after my match with D2K, it was after my match with Soul Grinder when I beat him for the second time. {{{{The courtroom burst into laughs as they realize that what the D.A. was expecting was that he would say that Smoke was really smoking a joint, but that wasn’t true. Semore smokes cigarettes as well. The D.A. looks depressed as he sees his case falling through for some reason, he looks at the Judge and then at Mick Palmer and says…}}}} D.A.: No further questions. Judge Lucas: Ok, Mr. Palmer, you may be seated. Mr. Macklin, you have any other witnesses or will you present your evidence? {{{{Macklin stands up and walks around the table once again…}}}} Macklin: I will bring forth my first piece of evidence, which the head of the evidence department at the Atlanta Police Department will gladly bring down. {{{{A police officer walks into the room and he’s carrying a video in one hand and is rolling a bag behind him. He walks through the barricade doors and to the front of the courtroom. He hands the videotape to Macklin and leaves the bag on the floor beside the evidence table. The bailiff wheels a TV to the center of the room and puts a video into the VCR. The D.A. has a look of disbelief on his face as he realizes what Macklin is doing. Macklin sticks the tape into the VCR and hits the play button, he also cuts the TV on, and lets the court view the video…}}}} Macklin: Ok, now this is the footage that the security camera in the airport picked up, now why no one looked at this before, I have no idea, but this is about ten minutes before my client walks through customs, in fact, he has just gotten off the plane. There’s my client getting off the plane and he’s wheeling his large suitcase behind him. Now, I’ve set this up so that several cameras’ footage stick to him the whole time, and the time at the bottom of the video will show that it is only a split second later. Just watch the video and it will explain itself. {{{{The video shows Semore walking through the crowded terminal as he heads towards the customs station, he looks at something off camera and walks over there. The camera angle changes and like what Macklin had said, the time at the bottom didn’t change, showing it was right at the exact second. Smoke walks up and leaves his bag beside himself as he buys a hotdog from a vender man who left his station and walked around with the movable hot dog vender station. Smoke pays the man, then someone bangs into him, now the man gets in the way and it goes by fast, so you can’t see anything…}}}} Macklin: That’s where it happened, now this man was a professional so you can’t see anything, but that’s where the man switched bags with my client, now I’ll rewind the footage and show it in slow motion, which this VCR allows. {{{{Macklin hits the rewind button and stops it as Smoke walks up to the vender, he then hits the pause button and continuously hits the play button, which causes the slow motion. The man walks up to Smoke and bumps into him, then you can see the quick switch, behind that man was his own bag, when he hit Smoke he twirled, left his bag and grabbed Smoke’s and took off, the incident took less then two seconds, but in slow motion you can clearly see the switch off. As the evidence is seen by the crowd, you can hear several “oohs” from the crowd, as if they were at a wrestling event and had just saw something truly amazing. The D.A. just slaps his forehead as he thought he had an iron clad case…}}}} Macklin: Now that that evidence is through and done. I’d like to present the bag that the police had confiscated with all the drugs in it. Now of course the marijuana has been cleared out of it. But the bag had something else that no one picked up on, except for us when we inspected the bag, of course in front of the police department’s staff, so we didn’t put this there. They are witnesses to it and are here, but to make this short, I trust that you will let me present the evidence without the witnesses. Judge Lucas: I would love to see these officers, please let them come forward. Macklin: As you wish your honor. {{{{The deputies who are standing at the door entrance walk out and call for the four officers who are witnesses. Then four officers, two of them who look to be veterans of the force, and two others who look to be somewhat new walk into the courtroom, they walk down the aisle and through the barricade, they then all place their hands on the bible and take the same oath…}}}} Bailiff: You swear to tell the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth, so help your God? Officers: I do. Bailiff: You may continue. {{{{Since there are four officers in all, they stand in front of the witness stand as the PCW lawyer puts the bag onto the evidence table in front of them. As he opens the bag he asks the officers…}}}} Macklin: Now, as respected officers of the Georgia PD. Did I or anyone else place anything in this bag, or take anything out? Officer 1: Of course you didn’t take anything out, the police department took all the drugs out, but other then that, everything is the way it was before. {{{{The officer who spoke up is standing on the far right and the others just nod with him, with no reason to lie the Judge believes them. Macklin whips open the bag and shows that it is full of men’s clothes, of course. But he holds the shirt up in front of him, and the shirt is very small, small in fact that Semore couldn’t fit in it. He puts the shirt on the table and grabs a pair of pants, he shows them in the air, and in fact they are a lot shorter then Smoke is, it’s obvious that they are. He then finds what he was looking for, he pulls out a pencil he had in his pocket and uses it to grab the garment. It’s a pair of underwear, he shows it to one of the officers at the front, including the bailiff and he asks…}}}} Macklin: Now, what name is on this pair of underwear? Officer 3 and Bailiff: John Jordan… Macklin: Now, apparently the man who made the switch with my client was so ignorant to leave his underwear with his name in them. Now after I did some searching, I found this man, and he’s here today in the courtroom… {{{{As Macklin says his words, a man who jumps up out of his seat cuts off Macklin, he’s of slight small build and looks to be about five foot seven or eight. He makes a break for the door but is caught by the two deputies standing their, he’s then cuffed and carried out of the door. Macklin smiles as he looks at the judge, who is shocked, and then the D.A. who looks similar…}}}} Macklin: Now, I think that incident pretty much explained itself. But you see, that was John Jordan right there, and he’ll be charged for this crime, not my client. All this evidence points to him. All the state of Georgia had on my client was a history of drug use, the word of four guards saying he was looking “suspicious” and then the bag that my client also thought was his, until it was opened in front of him. Now, I ask for an immediate dismissal since you’ve seen the evidence. {{{{The judge smiles as he bangs the gable on it’s base and announces to the courtroom…}}}} Judge Lucas: I rule in favor of the defendant, Semore Smoke did not commit this crime. Now let’s all go take a short recess. {{{{Cheers erupt from the crowd, as they realize their favorite wrestler didn’t commit the crime. The D.A. and Macklin shake hands, as does Smoke shake the hands of all four guards. They all apologize to him, and then walk out of the courtroom. The D.A. kicks the table, then grabs his briefcase and walks out of the courtroom through the same door the others used, which is the one those only personnel or people involved in a case may exit out of. The other door is crowded by too many people as they try to leave, many laughing, other’s not looking so surprised by the outcome. Smoke turns to Macklin as he is surprised by most of the evidence that his lawyer presented in front of the court in his defense…}}}} Semore Smoke: Whoa James, you took me by total surprise, I thought I was going to be dead for sure. Macklin: You have to just believe in your lawyer, and I’m the best on the PCW payroll. Best thing was, that all the evidence was legit, none of it was faked or anything, so you were clean the whole time. Well, don’t want to take up anymore of your time, I have to go anyways. Semore Smoke: Come on, stay with us, we need some other people to celebrate this victory with. {{{{Macklin is turning away and getting his briefcase as he answers…}}}} Macklin: No, this is your moment, enjoy it. I’m already paid for this, so go celebrate, I’ll look forward to watching you wrestle this coming week. {{{{Macklin is headed towards the personnel exit as Smoke then yells at him…}}}} Semore Smoke: Well if you’re not going to party with me, then at least let me tell you thank you! So thanks! {{{{Macklin is out the door as Smoke finishes, Mick walks up to Smoke and hugs him, Smoke would normally push away, but Mick is just congratulating him on the win, then as the camera fades to black during the whole hug scene, Smoke realizes something…}}}} Semore Smoke: I didn’t have to go to those stinking rehab sessions after all! {{{{The scene then turns to black with the words “Semore Smoke is Innocent” in white at the end, they too fade as well….}}}}
