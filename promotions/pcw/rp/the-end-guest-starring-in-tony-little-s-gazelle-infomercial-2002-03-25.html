The End guest starring in 'Tony Little's Gazelle' Infomercial

25 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/25/02 The scene opens inside of a small studio with cyan-blue walls and somewhat inadequate lighting. Certain areas of the stage are elevated higher than others, the middle left up about four feet, with another platform only 2 feet up; on more platform to the extreme right is one foot high, while the central platform of the stage is left alone. On each of these platforms there is a black machine that is shaped like a hydraulic treadmill with no tracks, only a harness and foot rest for the legs and handlebars that extend up from the solid metal frame of the harnesses. In the back, the sign “Tony Little’s Gazelle” is prominent in white italicized letters as big as a person’s head. In the back, The End is sitting in a meditative position, but he is definitely not of clear mind. The PCW press agent is with him, a scrawny man of 38 years: 5’9”, 124 lb., wearing a drab brown suit and holding a briefcase. “You said I’d be doing a commercial with Tony Hawk…” Forsaken says plainly. He’s trying to keep a pending outburst in check, upset at the press agent for lying to him. “And anyway, how’s this helping me with anything? I’ve got a match with Stormbringer at Spring Fling, and a 4-way match with Clyde, Rush, and Chet King tomorrow. Inane infomercials with a has-been fitness trainer doesn’t do a thing for me.” The press agent spits back, “I don’t know what you think you heard, but I specifically said you’d be doing a commercial with Tony Little. And I can’t say anything much about your matches, but you’ve been booked to do this show, and I’m going to make sure you do it.” The End turns his head to the press agent and takes a darker tone. “You want a commercial with Tony Little? Very well… but never again….” Standing up, The End moves to his spot off camera, where he waits for his cue as he thinks…. The End:“I win consecutive matches against Clyde and Darkstar, and I’m rewarded with a four-way match this Tuesday. Stormbringer loses consecutive matches against Clyde and Badge, and he couldn’t even finish his match with Charlie Loc, and he’s rewarded with a night off. Did he earn it? Or are the bookers trying to tell him that he’s not good enough to be involved in Turmoil?” Onstage, Tony Little, a man that looks like Crash Holly with a long blond ponytail and wearing a blue shirt, and black pants, baseball cap, and shoes, calls for The End. The End:“If such is the case, then why would he be given the chance to face me at Spring Fling? So he’s the only other person from the last incarnation of EW to remain in PCW and he’s living in my shadow, that’s no reason to be bitter. I kicked his ass at March Madness when he wanted to help out Jeremy Howard, and did his job for him. Should he be angry? He’s so inept that he should be thanking me for taking care of what would have just been for him loose ends never fully attended to.” Once again, Tony Little calls for The End. The press agent taps The End on his shoulders. The End clutches the press agent by the throat and tosses him away. “I shall go out,” The End says in a very deep voice, “when I so choose.” The End walks out onto the stage, dressed in his new ring attire: a standard black ninja-looking suit with white forearm pads and shin guards. His hair is trimmed and tied back in braids, and the mask covering his face now looks better than that C-3PO gold mask he used to wear. The End steps out near Tony Little, and his female co-host. The woman’s name is not known, but she wears a pink sports bra and exercise pants and looks about 40. Let’s just call her Suzanne Somers. Tony Little:“Well, End, we were beginning to think we lost you. Had trouble finding the place?” The End growls at Tony Little, prompting his silence. Suzanne Somers walks over to The End and pats him on the shoulder. The End quickly says, “Don’t ever touch me.” Suzanne Somers withdraws her hand, then continues with what she was about to say. Suzanne Somers:“So, what has the Gazelle done for you?” The End replies, “Nothing. I’m in my prime physical condition by my very nature. I don’t need an exercise machine to do what healthy living has already accomplished for me.” The End stops and does a double-take. He ‘eyes’ Suzanne Somers before saying, “I thought you sold your soul to the Thigh Master manufacturers?” Suzanne Somers brushes off the comment with a shrug. Suzanne Somers:“Business hasn’t been that good lately, so I have to move onto something else.” Tony Little breaks in. Tony Little:“Let’s introduce a man that does use the Gazelle very often. Here he is, former WWF wrestler Adorable Adrian Adonis!” In his old wrestling attire of purple tights that can hardly be seen beneath his still lingering layers of fat and flab, Adonis comes out onto the stage. Ratings begin to drop through the floor. Suzanne Somers can’t bear to watch Adonis waddle his way out to the stage. Taking matters into his own hands, The End immediately delivers a superkick to the jaw of Adonis, sending him head over heels and landing on his chest. The End then locks on his submission finisher Never Again. “Come on,” The End says, barely audible, “let’s hear that snap.” While ratings are back up to normal, Tony Little insists that The End not kill Adonis on the air. The End drops the hold, and moves over to the exercise machine. “You know what?” The End says, “I’m very eager to get out of here. So I’ll play along for but a moment, and say something to my opponents for Tuesday Turmoil while we’re at it.” The End chokeslams Tony Little off of the Gazelle he’s been on for the past twenty minutes. Little hits the floor with a thud. The End climbs onto the machine and begins using it in the forward motion while speaking into the camera. The End:“This machine works out the entire body, from the arm muscles, pecs, abs, right down to the glutes and the thighs. That’s very important, because my opponents might need to use this machine. First, there’s The Rush. He’s muscular, but he could stand to lose some of it as it impedes his movement in the ring. He stumbles around awkwardly, and looks just ridiculous trying to execute the cruiserweight moves that someone under 230 pounds like myself has no trouble performing. Rush, your bulk gets in the way of your athleticism. You don’t need to be so big… I mean, really, what’s it gotten you? Nowhere in PCW, and Xavier Kannon had to carry you in NTICW. You might as well try out something new….” The End switches to the backward motion, the camera zoomed in on his body. Off-camera, tech crew are brought in to remove the unconscious Tony Little and Adrian Adonis from the stage. Suzanne Somers is eyeing The End in a sort of attraction. The End:“Before I continue, do not look at me like that, Somers. You’re nowhere near my type; simply put, I’m better than you.” Suzanne Somers runs off to the back crying. Ratings for the Gazelle are at an all-time high. The End continues. The End:“Next, we have Clyde. He’s only about 6’1” but he’s just as bulky as Rush. That’s not good. Apparently, he’s called the Burned Man. Guess those aren’t calories you burned off, were they? I am already 1-0 against Clyde. Why? Because I am a better wrestler. I’m more athletic, more agile, and stronger than Clyde could ever hope to be. He depends on weapons and extreme matches to win. I use my own body, and only my body, as a weapon, and that’s the most effective and most dangerous thing I can do. Clyde, I hope that you’ll put up more of a fight than you did last Tuesday. I bet you’re watching the replay right now, wondering what you did wrong. Here’s a little hint: the first step towards failure was getting in the ring with me.” The End hops off of the machine. He walks into the camera and finishes his monologue. The End:“Chet King, you shall be spared a bit of verbal bashing, merely because frankly I’m not even sure if you work for PCW anymore. I haven’t seen you backstage, and if you are booked for a match you probably leave right afterwards rather than doing what the rest of us do and sign autographs and talk with fans. Add to that the fact that the supposed ‘Chat King’ has been uncharacteristically silent as of late… and, you’ll understand if I won’t consider you a presence in the match, let alone a threat. On second thought, Chet King, you won’t be spared. You’ve become little more than a figurehead of PCW’s past nowadays. Sure, you’ve held the Cruiserweight Title, the Continental Title… but where are all your accomplishments now? Where is your spotlight? Kept in a closet of faded memories somewhere in the shack that you call a home….” The End takes off his mask, showing his empty eyes to the camera, the optical devices so small as to not register on camera. The End:“My opponents are as mentally blind as I am physically blind, desperately grasping to delusions of greatness. They merely have their eyes closed to the reality of their lives. When I walk into the arena on Turmoil, put on my gear and head out to the ring, there will not be three opponents standing with me, but three wayward souls that but need enlightenment…” The End turns away from the camera and walks off stage left. His voice can be heard as he departs, and the studio lights are dimmed. The End:“And they will all be Bobby Stormbringer to me….”
