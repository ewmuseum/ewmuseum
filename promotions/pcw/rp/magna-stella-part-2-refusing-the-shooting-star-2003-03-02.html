Magna Stella - Part 2: Refusing the Shooting Star

2 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3 - 2 - 03 Daniel’s The beautiful, blood red color of the neon sign emits a soft glow around the perimeter of the huge oak doors of the 5 star restaurants. Complemented by the beautiful white bricks that form the entirety of the structure, the sign serves a beacon to draw those far and wide from the city to the red felt corridors of the upper-city eatery. The rich and famous, important and extravagant, all stop by the taste the finer delicacies of world-class chefs while attending to pressing business in the bustling metropolis. Due to the exclusive nature of the restaurant, it’s usually void of the every day man, and thus, is rather empty. The common man just has no purpose in such a place. But that won’t stop us, Dorn Tracer smiles to himself, wearing a grin fit for a lord full of royal confidence. Donned in his sleek trench coat, a pair of fine black Wolverines and matching black pants, he stands defiantly before the pair of large wooden portals that lead to the wonderful smells within. Around his waste, glittering like a fine diamond is the Rising Star Championship. “Beautiful place, Jessica. Fit for a champion, I’d say…” The grin on his face clearly depicts his arrogant state, and Jessica merely rolls her eyes. “Don’t get too sure, Dorn,” She exclaims, her lips hinting at a quaint little grin. “You never know, they may have some huge bouncer that’ll be ready to toss us out if we just barge in.” Knowing she rights, Dorn’s grin slowly melts from his face and is replaced by a straight-lipped look of grim reality. “Why are we here, then?” Dorn inquires, suddenly pondering their purpose. His friend had promised him help in a matter that needed none, and he only came along to appease her. But now that they’ve arrived at their destination, Jessica believes they won’t be able to get in. What’s the point? “I mean, are we going to dine outside?” Rather amused, Jessica gives a little giggle and covers her mouth with her first three fingers, trying not to laugh out loud at Dorn’s cute puzzlement. Noticing the perplexed, if not annoyed, look on the Astronomer’s face, Jessica halts her amusement and says, “Of course not, Dorn. I’m just saying, you shouldn’t be so sure that someone’s just going to let you in because you wear a championship around your waist, you know?” She can only hope he understands, because that’s phase one. He’s of course a champion, but that doesn’t make him royalty, contrary to what the Rising Star Champion believes. And believing himself to be one of the most important people in life today is definitely what Dorn thinks, “Blasphemy, Jessica! I wear glittering gold and fine expensive leather around my waist! They should be nothing but impressed upon my arrival, correct?” He’d rather answer his own question himself, for he knows Jessica’s answer. “Not exactly…” She exclaims, raising an eyebrow and shooting her friend a bemused look. The more arrogance that emits like UV rays from Dorn, the more she begins to loose confidence that breaking En’Flarim’s spell over his consciousness will be easy. At first, he gave in almost willingly after she stated he needed help, but now… I think he’s starting to believe I’ve tricked him. “Look, Dorn. This is a five star restaurant with reservations MONTHS in advance. Unless you’re someone who reserved a seat, they’re not going to let you waltz in despite your title.” Simply not acknowledging such rubbish, Dorn shrugs his shoulders and folds his leathered arms across his chest in a sulking, childish manner. “That’s stupid, Jessica. Any fat white bitch can wear gold around her neck and get in, but the minute someone with a truly wonderful prize steps to their door, they turn it away like a wayward raccoon? Blasphemy! Let’s go in!” And with that, ignoring Jessica’s grabbing attempts to stop him, Dorn swings the mighty wooden doors open. Immediately, a rushing blast of warm, humid air bursts forth from within the restaurant and snakes over Dorn’s body like a large cloak. The welcoming smell of various foods and delicacies fill his nostrils, beckoning him like an irresistible finger. Taking the aromas in like a much-needed breath of air, Dorn gives a wanton smile and steps onto the expensive red carpet and into the extravagant restaurant… and is instantly stopped by a large black man in a black tuxedo, standing in front of a wooden pedestal. “Name, please.” The large man demands gruffly, his black mustache waving along with his lips. The golden chandeliers hanging from the ceiling reflect their elegant glow above his baldhead, forming a crown of light. Dorn isn’t deterred by the fact that the man wants some identification and proudly slaps the face of the Rising Star Title, which glows softly in the restaurant light. “Right here, sir. Dorn Tracer, PCW Shooting Star champion. Now, if you’ll excuse me…” The Astronomer tries to brush past the hulking, 6’7’’ black man, but the bouncer reaches a huge hand out and stops him again. “Do you have a reservation?” The bouncer inquires, with a hint of anger building from deep within the confines of his esophagus. The persistence of this little man with an extremely large, gaudy looking belt doesn’t please him at all. The said little man smirks irritably at the bouncer and folds his arms across his chest, “I don’t need one. I represent the elite of the most successful wrestling federation around, and that should be enough. If you don’t believe me, call my boss Syko on his cell. The number is-“ Not really caring, the black man clears his throat and folds HIS huge, burly arms across his chest and stares down at Dorn, burning a hole through the Astronomer’s leather coat with a look of malice and irritation. “Sir, if you don’t have a reservation, then you’re not getting in. I don’t care who you are or who your boss is. Now, if you’ll please step away, that’ll be enough.” Truly enough, the black man DOESN’T care whom Dorn is and is actually amused at the shining championship belt around the Astronomer’s waist. Dorn, though, isn’t too happy at all. “This is blasphemy!” Dorn cries, a little too loudly for anyone’s liking. Jessica, who has remained outside, slaps herself in the forehead and shakes her pretty little head in dismay. “Alright then, I’m part of an occult that deals with Astrology. I can have the very Gods of the night sky grant you unlimited power if you let me through!” Dorn obviously doesn’t realize how foolish he sounds, and the bouncer breaks out in laughter. Unable to control himself, the black man slaps his huge thigh and gives a hearty, belly-shaking laugh. His body quakes like the San Francisco fault line as the words of the Astronomer run through his head. Dorn can feel the rage building within his championship body and clenches his fists tightly, turning his knuckles ghostly white. He thinks it’s funny I follow Astrology… If I was some kid-raping Christian priest, I bet he’d kick himself for not letting me in! It’s crazy! Dorn can hardly keep himself steady as the black man laughs for a moment more, then finally calms down. Wiping a tear from his burly face, the bouncer clears his throat and takes a deep breath, regaining his iron composure. “I’m sorry, sir, but that won’t get you in either. Now step away, or I’ll be forced to toss you over the moon, okay?” Amused at his own comment, the bouncer cracks a grin and returns to his defensive stance. About ready to remove the title and slam it across the bouncer’s face, Dorn steps forward with a infuriated look on his clean shaven face. Uh oh… Jessica gasps and steps forward into the restaurant. “Wait!” She exclaims, trying to get Dorn’s attention. She then turns to the bouncer and pleads, “Don’t hurt him, he’s with me!” The Astronomer suddenly whips around and gives his friend a bewildered look. The bouncer takes one look at Jessica and smiles warmly, his brown eyes brightening. “Ah, Ms. Carter! How are you?” The black man inquires, the tone of his voice appealing to her as if he’s known her for years. “Wonderful, I trust?” The young Jessica Carter blushes and nods at the burly black man, “As always, Macklin. And how are you? Is your mother still sick?” The bouncer known as Macklin gives a short, despaired sigh and nods his large, almost melon-like head. The muscles in his bulging neck twist and crane as the mighty cranium of the bouncer dips forward. “Unfortunately. But she’s getting better all the time!” Macklin exclaims, almost like he’s trying to convince himself. Jessica smiles warmly and nods, then suddenly remembers Dorn, whom’s standing with his arms folded across his chest and a pouting look on his face. She’s not sure if it’s from the lack of attention or if it’s because she stepped in. “Oh yeah, sorry about my friend here, Macklin. He doesn’t mean any trouble.” She then turns a confirming eye to Dorn, who turns away. Macklin merely nods. “I understand. But he needs to work on his attitude a little,” The mighty, giant bouncer shoots the Astronomer a harsh look, which isn’t met at all. He then turns back to Jessica, wearing that smile on his face again. “Will you be dining here tonight?” At the very sound of that, Dorn’s face snaps to bewilderment and he stares at Jessica, who just grins at Macklin. “Yes sir! Is that alright?” She asks politely, despite knowing the answer. Macklin nods and she claps her hands together once, signifying her pleasure. She turns to Dorn with a smile, but the Astronomer just gives her a dark, brooding stare. Her smile sort of melts into a frown, but she beckons for him to follow as she starts off into the restaurant. Dorn obliges and starts off down the hall, giving Macklin a dirty look as he goes by. The bouncer just smiles. Catching up with Jessica, the two walking to the huge, elaborate dining hall of the restaurant. Fine, polished oak tables with silky white clothes dot the red room, complemented by beautiful pieces of art upon the walls. Tall, colossal windows line the red walls, letting the beautiful rays of the sun pour forth into the room, which is fairly empty. A group of Japanese men in suits sit near the upper right corner of the room, possibly being businessmen. A man dines with a woman on the opposite end, laughing and drinking merrily. Other than that, void of people other than Macklin and the waiters. Dorn and Jessica make their way to a small, round table close to them and Jessica carefully sits down into the beautifully crafted chair. Dorn removes the Rising Star Title from his waist and slams it down with reckless abandon upon the dining table, startling Jessica and making a couple of glasses of water quake. He then removes his coat, revealing a white t-shirt, and slings it over the back of the chair. Finally, the Astronomer seats himself. Noticing the irritated look on Dorn’s face, Jessica clears her throat and leans forward on her elbows, “So, you hungry?” A cheap-stock attempt at breaking the ice, but it’s better than nothing. It doesn’t really work, however, and Dorn gives her a dirty look. “You know him? He inquires darkly, the anger hissing between his teeth. Jessica nods and looks over at Macklin, then back at Dorn. “Yeah, he used to work for my father before Dad passed away…” The end of her statement seems to send a wave of sorrow through Jessica’s body, slowly breaking down her cheery demeanor. She gives a prolonged sigh of despair, but returns to the topic. “I helped him get a job after my father’s company went bankrupt. He thinks he’d be having to deal cocaine to pay for food right now if it wasn’t for me!” The statement seems to cheer Jessica up a bit and she giggles, obviously amused. Dorn grunts angrily and just shrugs. “Whatever, and no, I’m not.” The Astronomer spits gruffly, answering her earlier question. “I’ll just have a glass of white wine.” Jessica nods, and signals for a waiter to come over. She relays their drink order to the clean-shaven Hispanic man, then turns back to Dorn. With an inquiring look, she decides to dwell deeper into the topic in the back of her mind. “What made you think being the Rising-“ “Shooting.” Dorn corrects her, before she can finish. “…Shooting Star Champion would get you into a place like this?” Jessica asks, trying not to anger him too much or make him feel like his title isn’t worth anything. Dorn gives a shrug and cracks his knuckles. “Why wouldn’t it?” He shoots back, turning it around on her. “Like I said, if I was some fat bitch with gold around my neck and a lovely dress on, I bet he would have let me in with a big fucking smile on his face.” The venomous tone in the Shooting Star Champion’s voice unnerves Jessica, as does Dorn’s next comment. “He also laughed at me when I told him of the boons that respecting me and my religion would bring. He’s lucky I didn’t break his neck and just barge my way in.” Sensing the wave of rage building within her hot-tempered friend, Jessica bits her lip nervously and tries to progress with the conversation without angering Dorn more. “I understand, but I also warned you it wouldn’t get you in. That’s what I wanted to talk to you about, Dorn. Lately, after winning that championship and falling that En’Flarim woman about, you’ve been…different…” Her voice trails off and she tries to find the right words to do Dorn’s demeanor justice. The Astronomer, however, merely shrugs her muscular shoulders. “I don’t think so,” He retorts, really not believing her. “I’ve just been more assertive. And you see what it’s gotten me.” Dorn slaps the beautiful, jeweled face of the Rising Star Title and gives a cocky smirk. “And besides, I’m not hurting anything.” Horrified at that comment, Jessica’s jaw drops and her eyebrows draw inward towards the bridge of her nose. “Not hurting anything?! You call trying to sacrifice the mailman to her not hurting anything, Dorn??” Her frantic, yet quiet tone doesn’t really pierce Dorn’s stalwart mental defense and the Astronomer just shrugs once again. “Not really, he was a douche anyway.” At that moment, the waiter returns with glasses and their select choices of wine. Setting the bottles down, he bows and return from whence he came, promising to return shortly. Dorn pours himself some white wine and sips it meagerly. “I don’t understand. I’m just serving my Goddess unquestioningly, like any deity-fearing religious man, what’s wrong with that?” To be frank, Jessica believes there’s a lot wrong with that, and the expression on her face clearly says that. She sighs and tries to rethink her strategy, guessing that the front door is permanently locked. She might have to appeal some other way… Perhaps I can warm him that his career is in danger, maybe that’ll help. “Well, for instance, you’ve built up quite an arrogant attitude. It’s attracting the likes of that guy that confronted you the other day, and it’s going to get you into trouble.” It isn’t merely a coax attempt, but actually reality. Dorn, however, isn’t fazed and begins to grin at the thought. “Oh, that Wolfson kid? The boss gave me him next Tuesday. I’m not really worried. He’s got a mouth on him, and I’ll have to teach him some respect, but he’s nothing to worry about.” Dorn takes another sip of his wine and slowly rattles the glass around, his mind wandering. “My arrogance doesn’t bring about swine like him, so what do it matter? He’s just some ignorant, blasphemous fool who thinks he’s got some weight to throw around, despite being greener than a crisp counterfeit bill. It’s not a problem; so let’s forget about it. All this talk of jobbers and my attitude is making me hungry, so let’s order!” And with that, Dorn completely forgets the conversation and scouts the restaurant for the waiter. Jessica sighs and leans her head against her open hand, propping herself up with her elbow. This is going to be harder than I thought… [End.]
