To The Reservations: The Farm (Part 1)

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March something Eddy was become a Native American. Eddy could feel how the culture came through with him. He was driving to the Indian reservations when his stomach was giving him language he needed food. So, he dropped by a little cafe called 'Joey's' to grab a bite for breakfast. It was a small, clean, quiet place. Some 60's music played as Eddy walked it, greeted by the squeaking of his boots against the newly wiped floor. The tiles were of red and white, and that decorated the whole cafe. The counter, floor, the tables...The walls however were a tannish color. Eddy sat down alone at a table. The table was newly cleaned, but he did notice an open jelly packet towards the far corner. He strummed his fingers to the oldie tune as a middle aged woman with short brown hair, a white apron and blue clothing underneath came by with a pen. She walked up to Eddy. 'Hello. What would you like to drink?' 'Milk would be good.' 'OK, I'll be back', she said walking away. Eddy sighed and leaned back in his chair in thoughts of his upcoming match versus Skitzo. Skitzo, in Eddy's mind, was an immature man. He often used sexual comments to add some flavor to his promos but it came off more sickening then funny. He told Eddy 'he'd shove a dildo so far up his ass he'll lose in his own pile of sex juice'. Eddy just thought 'Sheesh man...Why couldn't you just say you'd kick my ass?'. Oh well. It didn't matter to Eddy. He had confidence in himself winning otherwise. The title was so close to him and he could almost touch it. He'd love to have the title too. The pride of having a title on your waist or over your shoulder was a pride Eddy never has felt before but has come close to. For example, he fought El Romatico for the Cruiserweight. Eddy won the match, not the title. El Romantico attacked Eddy with a weapon, making Eddy win the match by DQ but not get the title. However, in the 'Extreme' stipulation, regulations wouldn't be like that. You were SUPPOSED to use weapons. SUPPOSED to knock your opponent upside the temple with a 2 by 4 or clock him with a chair. SUPPOSED to make him bleed. So, Skitzo could use and many weapons as he wanted, but the deal is it was allowed. Eddy had a tendency to run into extreme, hardcore alleyfight-esque moments. He was in New York, of course, and got mugged in an alley. Eddy fought back to keep the mugger from stealing his things, and Eddy won anyway. But the mugger was pretty damn tough. Eddy also had practiced the extreme way of life in gym. Because, like for Fusion, he knew he'd need the same skills soon enough. The woman came back, milk brim full in her hand as she sat it in front of Eddy. Eddy was about to grasp it to take a sip when the woman asked another question. 'Ready to order hon?' 'Yeah, sure...' 'What would ya' like?', she said in a thick country accent. What should I have.... 'Let's see....2 scrambled eggs, 2 strips of bacon and toast.' 'Would you like our Touchdown plate?' 'Wha?', said Eddy confused. 'What about a Field Goal plate?' 'What???!' 'Don't want that either? We have a good First Dow-' 'Do you guys just do the exact opposite meal names of Denny's?', Eddy interrupted. 'Yes...Shut up', said the waitress walking off. Eddy sighed and leaned back again. He then changed his thought of Skitzo to a thought of Mark Makonie. Eddy never liked Makonie, ever. Since the first match of PCW when Eddy redebuted against Makonie and won, he could tell something about Makonie was evil. He was right. The man was a true Neo-Nazi and hated everyone not white. Eddy, being Hispanic, was not liked by Makonie. In the second time they faced off about a week ago, Makonie beat Eddy by DQ. Eddy attacked the ref, even though that wasn't his forte of things to do. He was always trying to inspire the young, and attacking refs didn't seem like it. But, Eddy did have a reason to. The ref, a good friend of Makonie's named Liebig was putting everything in favor of Makonie and being biased. He slow counted Eddy's pins, and fast counted Makonie. Finally, snapping, Eddy punched him, losing the match by DQ. Just before that, Eddy was attacked by Makonie in the locker room as well as his father, who is in the hospital for broken bones. It really made Eddy mad, and Makonie was the man in PCW he held a vengeance for. He also wanted to avenge his father and beat him, like his father told him many times in the hospital. Makonie's judgemental views could be changed with Makonie looked at the champion's and saw 'Young Tiger= Extreme Champ'. Eddy never liked any cockiness in himself, and didn't enjoy people being cocky, however if it DID happen and he won he'd feel that way. Makonie would feel ashamed he doubted another race. He didn't know if Makonie would stop his views, but if he did it would be nice. Eddy thought Makonie's head was too hard and was too ignorant to care, but Eddy would win the Extreme title to say just that. If he could beat Skitzo, it would rule. The lady came back with a plate of food and sat it down in front of him. She walked away quickly without much realization of Eddy as Eddy grabbed a fork and began on his eggs. The eggs were okay, nothing special. The bacon he didn't like. They were extremely chewy and raw just like Eddy despised them to be. The toast was just toast, so he didn't have any complaints for that. He grabbed a butter and knifed it onto the toast and spread it, biting into it as he sat it down. He looked outside. The sun was just beginning to rise. In fact, it wasn't even beginning, but it was the moment right before it began, where you could tell it was coming but it wasn't there. A happy medium in a sort of way. Eddy finished his eggs quicker then normal, only ate 1 strip of bacon and 1 and 1/2 pieces of toast. He stood up to pay. He saw a sign on the wall as he stood. For compliments, tell cashier. For complaints, press here (red button) Eddy looked and saw the red button was actually loaded on a mouse trap and if he did press it his finger would get stuck. He chuckled mildly as he reached into his pocket to pull out money and walk toward the front cashier. The cashier was an even older lady with thinning gray hair and big round glasses. Eddy pulled money from his pocket and sat it down, hander her the receipt for his food. She looked, totalled, and gave Eddy $12.50 back for change from his $20. He counted to make sure the amount was correct and began to the front door. He stuffed the money into his pocket and began to open the door and got a breath of fresh country air, looking across the street to see cows mooing and other domesticated farm animals around. He smiled timidly at the sight and began toward his truck, opening it up and stepping in. Eddy turned the key, and all he got was dead chugging. He tried again, and then realized he was dead on gas. He felt stupid because he always forgot to do so. He looked around, but any nearby gas station was many, many miles away. Eddy tried again, and nothing worked. 'No...Please don't die on me out here...Urgh...' The car refused the start as Eddy gave up on the 5th try and stepped out with a hopeless look on his face. He was stranded nowhere with no gas, and had nothing to do. The same thing happened to him recently. He felt very stupid. He walked around his car, pacing in thought. 'What can I do...' He sighed and stood there hopeless when he heard a voice yelling. 'Hey son! You lost?', said the voice. Eddy put his head up to see a farmer across the street, leaning over the fence. 'Not lost, but I'm out of gas and can't go anywhere.' 'Ain't that so? That's a bitch. Here, hop over here, I can help ya'.' Eddy smiled. 'Seriously?' 'Sure...' 'Thanks', said Eddy graciously. Eddy never had a good experience with most farmers, but this one seemed trustworthy. Eddy jogged across the street to an open fence that the farmer had opened. Eddy walked through with the smell of cow dung. Eddy tried holding breathing from his nose to stop the smell from entering again. The farmer was a pretty tall man with brown hair, overalls and a red shirt underneath. His voice was country and pretty thick, but it truly wasn't over done or anything. Eddy saw cows grazing around. He looked around when the farmer began talking. 'So...Where ya' headin' son?' 'The Indian reservation.' 'I know where it is! But don't be a-leavin' so quick, have some food.' 'I already ate', Eddy said, trying to be nice. 'Realla'? Well, can ya' help me on my farm work a bit?' Eddy didn't really care. 'Uh...Sure. I guess.' 'Great. You can get the chicken eggs.' 'All right...Where's the coop?' 'I'll show ya.' They walked across the grassy fields and in front of them was a red barn. Eddy heard chicken's clucking and yapping inside. The farmer opened a door to get in. 'Oh...I forgot to tell you my name. My name's Eddy', said Eddy nicely. The farmer smiled and extended his arm. 'The name is Robert Barnes, but you can call me Bob. Nice to meetcha.' 'Same here.' Bob walked inside as Eddy followed hearing chickens clucking. Eddy began walking when he felt a peck on his ankle which hurt. He jumped and turned. There was a black, burned chicken with burnt feathers and an evil face. Next to him, a younger, still evil looking chicken stood. Eddy looked. Bob noticed Eddy looking at them as Eddy stared. 'That one right there's named Clyde. Next to him is Bethany', Bob commented. Bethany clucked up to Eddy, pecking him and clucking noises which sounded exactly like 'DIE DIE DIE DIE' 'Hehe...That Bethany can be a bit mean when she wants...', Bob said. 'Uh....Yeah...Anyways, they are annoying me...' 'Nuttin' much you can do.' Eddy sighed. 'Well, wait', said Bob. He grabbed both and flung them out the door, shutting it quickly and nailing a board on it. Holes began popping in the door as Eddy looked confused. Eddy disregarded it as he started gathering eggs in a basket Bob gave him. After a bunch, he handed the basket to Bob and nodded. 'There.' 'We got a bunch this morning! Yippee!', said Bob as Eddy ignored him and sat on a bale of hay. 'Wait here, I'm going to milk da' cows.' Bob walked off as Eddy sat on the bale, bored. He stood up for a moment, looking around. Then he began shadowboxing. Eddy sent a couple of punches around to practice. Eddy swung and kicked a bale of hay, but on top of the hay looked like a jug of gasoline. The gas tipped over and fell onto the floor, spilling out. Eddy was in shock. 'Oh no!' Eddy saw Bob's footsteps coming back as he opened the door and looked in. Bob grabbed a cigarette and began lighting it. Eddy dived. 'NOOOOOOOOOO!' Eddy crashed into Bob as the cigarette flung out of his hand and landed somewhere else. 'What the hell is wron' with you boy?', said Bob. 'Sir, I knocked over your gas-' 'Hey! You even poured water for the chickens! Nice job!' 'What?' Eddy turn a glance at the 'gas' and realized it was just a jug of water, nothing more. It actually tipped over and fell into a pail where the chickens drank from. Eddy chuckled to himself at the incident and brushed hay off him, standing up as Bob patted him on the back. 'Well done.' 'Yeah...Thanks...', said Eddy stupidly as we faded out.
