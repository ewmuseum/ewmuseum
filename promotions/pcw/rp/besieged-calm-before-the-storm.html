Besieged: Calm Before The Storm

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 24 of 2003 A.D. (OOC: Apologies for jumping so far ahead in time after my last installment; I may go back to that one as a prequel of sorts eventually) “Ph-nglui mglw’nafh Cthulhu R’lyeh wgah’nagl fhtagn” Crouching before the deformed pulpit atop Fire Ridge, and enveloped in the darkness of midnight, the demented high priest of the Sect of Black Wisdom incessantly recanted his black mantra into the eternal blackness overhead. The stars themselves seemed to revel blasphemously amidst the unholy hymn, glistening vehemently in the still heavens. The altar, crowned by the malformed ankh denoting the notorious cult, was tinted orange and red in the glow of the torches forming a tight ring around its obscene bulk. Shanahan’s guttural gibbering was accompanied by an equally vile chorus generated by his followers. Thunder echoed in the distance, lightning flashing in its wake. The chants reverberated through the shunned wood and down from the ridge into the steep valley below, and some among the ranks of the cult would say that something seemed to answer the calls that ominous night. Shanahan’s eyes rolled back into his head and his lips parted to form a grotesque snarl, slowly warping into what could only be called a morbid type of grin as a bead of sweat began to course from his brow. Upon the altar rested that abomination forged beyond space, the metallic yellow box that housed the dark gem. Wrapped in the radiance of the torchlight, its exterior shimmered fiercely as if alive and with the sinister colors of an alien spectrum. Shanahan’s eyes moved to the relic, watching as the jewel pulsated with an unearthly glow. A steady rain began to trickle from overhead, almost immediately altering into a torrential downpour. The water cascaded down the contorted visage of the diabolical cult leader, coursing down the contours of his twisted face and onto the collar of his robe. Again the thunder struck, and Shanahan believed he could hear a voice instilled in its roar. DESTROY Somewhere, above the monotonous chant that rose and fell with the now howling wind, there iterated an audible guttural cackling. While those of the Sect of the Black Wisdom denied being able to detect such a vocalization, Shanahan adamantly proclaimed the phenomenon as an omen from the Old Ones. And with his mad gaze deviating between the Shining Trapezohedron and the clouded heavens, he slowly shifted his focus to the bare valley below. His eyes fell on the Blue Enoch Lodge, and the rays of light swinging around on the roof. “You’ve done well to defy me, Marcus,” he muttered under his breath, arising from his stance before the pulpit and adjusting the belt of his robe. “But your luck has run out.” He pivoted slowly around to eye his followers, whose heads remained bowed in solemn prayer. The faintly-luminous halo-crowned star designating the cult’s second-in-command decorated the head of one, that of Brother Asenath. Bruce scowled in the direction of his subordinate, letting out a deep growl and turning back to the valley. “However I am not as inept as my lieutenants, Marcus. My PCW ambitions are done for the time being, and you will curse that truth. The Black Pharaoh requests your presence. His dominion is death, and I’m assured you are long overdue. Say farewell to your good fortune, old friend.” A grin crept across his face as thunder crashed yet again. DESTROY * * * Stryker pursed his lips, glancing uneasily at Lieutenant Vanessa Wilder. She returned his gaze with a gesture to the darkened forest and a tightening of the grip on the rifle resting in her soft hands. Stryker could see rustling and stirring beyond the dim bulbs of the porch. “It’s been dark for hours. Why haven’t they moved yet, captain?” Marcus shifted his attention to the unnerved orator of the comment, Dr. Allister Kilborn of the Wilmarth Foundation. Spectacles were poised on the bridge of his nose, and he had been wringing his hands seemingly for hours now as Stryker’s Delta Fox unit surveyed the perimeter of the property. Fright were visible in his light brown eyes. “Mind games, doctor,” iterated Stryker, his eyes returning to the blackness. “Bruce commands a force that thrives in darkness. At night he has the upper hand, regardless of what little gadgets we toss out there to turn the tide or what matters your team takes during the day to secure things. There are more efficient forces under his control than mortal men. The very armies of the void are at his disposal. Those are the spoils of worshipping such things as he does. And sharing their viewpoint……” “Military tactics, perchance? Playing your game?” Stryker issued a meek nod in response. “Possibly. It’s hard to judge his ambitions or what drives them….” “Of course, captain. Mr. Shanahan is criminally insane. His tactics are nearly impossible to predict or even label.” Marcus shook his head in dissension. “I don’t know if he’s as deranged as he’d have us think. We all know there is basis to his convictions, but not even we know how far the realities of it extend.” Dr. Kilborn shook his head, fidgeting with a piece of electronic equipment and a screwdriver. “Perhaps, but is he the central figure that he has christened himself? Bruce Shanahan truly believes he’s going to single-handedly ring in the Apocalypse. I cannot say I believe that. He’s evil, yes, but I don’t know if he has the tools to fulfill his desire of eradicating humanity.” Ryder Slade lumbered into the room, pitching an empty styrofoam cup to the floor. His rifle was cradled in one massive arm. Dr. Kilborn smirked and glanced uneasily at Stryker. “Um……you are aware that firearms are rather ineffective against those things he conjures on that mountain, correct?” The Delta Fox captain glanced out among the illuminated grassland that formed the front ‘yard’. Still nothing beyond the wavering shapes prowling the shadow just beyond the light. His eyes turned back to that of the doctor. “Well aware, doctor. These are more for his more human lackeys. We have other means of dealing with the ‘other’ force.” A sound like that of a wolf howling echoed from somewhere atop the hills. Immediately the wind began to do likewise, and all within the lodge were abruptly brought to alert. The rain, which had only begun minutes earlier, ceased to a meek drizzle. The clouds, which had eclipsed the stars for the past several hours, now glided across the sky to impart their collective glimmer on the countryside. And above Fire Ridge, silhouetted across the now disclosed moonlight, was a frenzy of dancing shadows and a wall of towering flames. Stryker’s team stood at positions throughout the lodge, with Norton and Sensaki posted on the upper floor and the others on the bottom. Kilborn and his scientific team from the Wilmarth Foundation crept uneasily throughout the old building, themselves beginning to feel what years of acquired instinct now beset the Blackhelm team. Stryker could make out a single static shadow amongst the foray upon the ridge. And it took only a glimpse to realize the form’s identity and intentions. “Fuck….” growled Stryker. “That son of a bitch….” At that point, the very forest seemed to come alive around them. Men garmented in robes charged from the shadow and into the light, flanked by other, darker things. Marcus reached for his rifle, readying to shout orders. Before he could do so, a lone shot was fired from the second floor. “Blitzkrieg!” And the chaos ensued....
