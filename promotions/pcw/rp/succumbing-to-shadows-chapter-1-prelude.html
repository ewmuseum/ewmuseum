Succumbing to Shadows: Chapter 1 - Prelude

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 2nd of 2003 A.D. What is it you wish of me? Merely to fulfill your promise. Even now, fear plagues the ranks of PCW’s lowliest. Exploit that fear and develop the faith, Shanahan. Use the method established by heathen Christianity. “What is that, m’lord?” Breed anxiety and horror. Warn of the consequences of not following Us. They will follow like the terror-stricken sheep. 'M'lord, is their devotion a guarantee?' Bruce...do you truly believe that men would follow the teachings of Christ based sheerly on love and adoration? Surely you have gained more knowledge about humanity than that….. “Indeed so, Lord Nyarlathotep. But do you believe it will work? Will the PCW faithful offer such little resistance to our new order?…..” Humanity as a collective species falls victim to the vices of pride and conceit. Those feelings would initially cause them to doubt and ignore our gospel, and to that point your reasoning is unflawed. However, by that token, they also embrace the values of self-preservation. They will overcome that pride if threatened with the eternal torment awaiting them beyond the realm of death. The Christian belief plays off the same premise, manipulating worshippers into reverence through ‘campfire’ tales about lakes of fire and flesh-ripping demons of the underworld. Many eventually come to love their ‘glorious Father’. But first, they must question how they want to spend afterlife. “Hence you wish for me to use the same tactics of gathering support? Threats of unending pain in death for those who refuse the doctrine of the Old Ones and Outer Gods?” It’s not a difficult concept, Bruce Shanahan. Do you truly believe that Christianity would be the dominant faith of the western hemisphere were it not for men standing at the pulpit every Sunday, crying out the repercussions of Earthly sin and the horrors of Hell they invoke on the wicked? Most of these people don’t love their messiah, Shanahan. They love the promises of heavenly jubilation and the feasts assured to grace the table of the divine, and they abhor the notion of burning perpetually in some abysmal furnace. “This is not true in all cases. Some truly do feel devotion to their supposed creator and savior. To sway their convictions will be far from a painless endeavor…..” Then simply destroy them. We have no time to manipulate brainwashed drones. Such ludicrous devotion has been foreseen, don’t toil away your time on such cretins. “As you wish. And what of Duke Williams? He has come to be my first obstacle.” The mortal is far too stubborn and self-righteous to entertain the true gospel. I invite you to try, Bruce Shanahan. But I feel your efforts will be only in vain. If that becomes the case, then annihilate the insolent mongrel. And spare all remorse. “Of course, m’lord….”--------------------------------------- Shanahan cast his beady eyes on Wyatt Mansion, the accursed structure silhouetted ominously against the gleaming winter moon. It had been his abode for months now, and within its hallowed walls Bruce had come to grasp an ever deeper understanding of truth. The mansion also had been a focal point of local folklore for decades, reputed to have once hosted the notorious Wyatt family of northern Vermont. The Wyatt clan had slaughtered numerous locals for the purpose of unholy rituals they carried out in the deepest reaches of the woods, before being lynched by an angry mob nearly one hundred years ago. Even today, locals avoided the forbidding dwelling and would cross themselves at its very mention. The forces now housed in its tainted walls were no less diabolical than the degenerate Wyatts’. And while the evil within was no less potent, its approach was far more terrifying. The Old Ones and their Earthly vessel, Bruce ‘Violence Jack’ Shanahan, saw to that. There would be no time wasted in the coming weeks, and there would be no quarter given to those who refuted the teachings of the Necronomicon. Insolence was simply inexcusable by edict of the Old Ones. “Master Shanahan?” The meager voice from the shadows interrupted Bruce’s moment of reflection. In spite of himself, he couldn’t help but express a slight half-smirk. A solitary footfall broke the long silence with the snapping of a twig. “Brother Norrys, why must you tremble every time you mouth my name? Do I frighten you?” His head pivoted to the left almost robotically, neck muscles taut and movement deliberately protracted. Brother Norrys was another of Shanahan’s youthful students of the black arts who had taken up arms under the cult banner, and since day one had exuded an anxious and almost paranoid personality. In all truth, the raven-haired savant of the supernatural did harbor fear for their high priest, and he was not alone. Shanahan’s presence portrayed that of a man possessing an inhuman sobriety; he never laughed and never smiled, but always appeared to be in deep thought or preparation for God knows what. He had an evil authority hovering about him, adding a further foreboding depth to the enigmatic leader that not even his subordinate Brother Asenath held. That trait was what kept his followers in constant reverence to him; the fear he generated garnered their respect. “Yes, Master Shanahan. To an extent….” “And so it should.” The eerie presence and the inherent fear that permeated the atmosphere in his company were only two of his more peculiar attributes. The other was the power he wielded. And the fact that he realized it. “What do you want, Brother Norrys?” “Actually I come requesting advice, master.” This solicits a haughty laugh from the robed eminence of the Sect of Black Wisdom. The laugh wasn’t hearty or jovial; it was as sinister and malign as his typical character. “You are curious whether your heart is in the right place, Brother Norrys?” For the first time, the grim Shanahan rotated to face the befuddled youth. A thin ray of light from the mansion porch shed light on his bleak features, lips pursed and brow ruffled. “In a sense, master. . . . “To question one’s faith is a logical step at one point or another in life,” Shanahan elaborated. “Everyone simply has their own way of interpreting reality, and they need a method of substantiating their existence.” “Is that why you started the Sect of Black Wisdom, Master Shanahan?” The high priest’s eyes shimmered fiendishly. He could still recall that fateful night in Deerfield Sanitarium when it all changed…..
