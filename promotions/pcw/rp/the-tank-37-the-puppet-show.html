Date: Alliances #1 ﻿*********The Tank __37—********* The Puppet Show I can’t stop dancing because you can’t stop playing.I can’t stop movingbecause you can’t stop pushing.I can’t restbecause you can’t resist.I can’t resist because you can’t rest. I have transparent little stringsand you have a steady hand to pull them.And the next time I see the sunit’ll be from your engulfing shadow.You can make me hatebut you can’t make me whistle. All the times I’ve seen mushroom clouds,they’ve never looked so hollow.I never meant to swallow liesbut times called for impending action. And the next time I see you danceI’ll hold still and look for a giant hand that can’t resist stealing your rest. ~Leigh Donovan Sunday, November 17 - Day 136 It’s been three weeks since the death of Geoffery Rankin, and in that time, Unit B has gonewithout a manager. Rankin’s authority immediately fell to Unit B’s head C.O, BenjaminGraham, known to the prisoners as Ahab, who was nearly crushed by the weight. Juggling thepressures of being head C.O along with the decisions that used to be passed to Geoffery Rankinhas turned Graham into a bitter old man. He’s been noticeably pale lately when he comes to work, wondering just what horror lies aheadin the day. Unit B has never been worse either. Fights break out every day and there have beenfour stab wounds inflicting in the three weeks since Rankin’s death - two of them fatal. Leigh Donovan plans to push that number to three. The young Australian has changed more than perhaps any prisoner in the Tank’s hundred yearhistory. He came in as a rich and powerful rockstar - his albums, ironically, have sold moresince his incarceration. He was convicted, like most rock-and-roll superstars, for possession ofillegal substances, but if one good thing has come of his time in the Tank, it’s the fact that hehas, indeed, finally kicked a four year heroin habit. Drugs mean nothing now. He lost his taste for them during one of the many nights he spentlying in his own urine and vomit while crouching in the darkest corner of the hole. It was thehole, after all, that changed his entire outlook. Now, it’s all about power for the twenty-six yearold Australian - and not becoming subservient to anyone. That’s why he’s been sharpening a moderately large chunk of steel for the last month - JeremyDoris, known to his Thug gang underlings as J-Thug, must die. Upon J-Thug’s death, Leighwill not only be rid of his most obvious menace since entering the Tank, but he’ll also make aname for himself with Harvey Blake and the other Aryan gang members. That may have beenthe greatest change of all in young Leigh Donovan - his transformation into a walking-talkingNazi propaganda machine. A thick black swastika blocks the skin over his heart as he shaves away at the side of his chunkof steel with a stone that he was able to break out from the wall in the cafeteria. The blade wassharp enough to penetrate a human body weeks ago, but the opportunity for Leigh to cunninglymurder the massive J-Thug has yet to present itself. Luckily, Harvey Blake has been patient. His Nazis have been battling with the Thugs fordecades, a few more weeks won’t make a difference. “I thought you finally got some sense and were going to call off that plan,” a voice breaksthrough the silence of the dimly lit cell, startling Leigh. He immediately hides the blade in theback of his pants and looks up to find himself in the company of his cellmate, the Tank’sother celebrity, three-time former PCW Champion, Jason Snow. “Don’t fucking sneak up on me!” exclaims Donovan reaching up to brush his fingers through hislong blond hair, but finding only the short crop of brown that he was left with after the infirmaryshaved his head a few months back. “Like it makes a difference,” shrugs Snow. “With all the shit that’s been going on around herelately, the C.O’s probably wouldn’t give half a fuck if you’ve got a semi-automatic up your ass.” Snow pulls himself effortlessly up to his upper bunk and leans back for an afternoon snooze. Parole is coming soon for Snow, and he’s been trying his best to keep himself out of trouble,though with the chaos that has become the trend in the ever-volatile Unit B, it’s becomingincreasingly difficult. “Sometimes I don’t know if it’s the fact that the C.O’s are too busy tokeep their eye on everyone without Rankin, or if they just don’t care what happens because noone’s around to discipline them.” Leigh’s not listening. He’s gone back to sharpening his blade on the lower bunk of the cell. Before long, the sound of stone on metal draws Snow’s attention. “You’re really planning to gothrough with this?” Snow says with a hint of a smile. “You do realize that you’ll have a dozenlarge black men bashing your head off the floor before J-Thug even hits the floor.” “Fuck that,” Leigh says in a low voice. “I can take care of myself.” Somehow, through his ever-growing dislike for his cellmate, Snow has found himself growingsomewhat protective of him. Leigh Donovan is in way over his head, Snow knows, but nothingwill stop him from making an attempt at what may be an impossible goal. Sunday, November 17 - Day 136 For three weeks, Tim McDonald, leader of the incarcerated Italian mafia, hasn’t spoken to hiscellmate, Vincent Giovanni. The two have, after all, been plotting each other’s deaths for quitesome time. Trying to stay one step ahead of the other and thwart his plans becomes terriblyinconvenient over time, Tim McDonald is noticing. He almost regrets ever getting involved inthis mafia lifestyle - with Scottish heritage, he should have known how hard it would be to workhis way up in the Italian’s version of organized crime. Of course, that’s what lead to DougHattoni’s murder, which in turn lead to all this trouble with Vincent Giovanni, who was hired bythe Hattoni family to enter the Tank under cover and seek out the people responsible for the lateDon of the Hattoni family’s death. Over the past few weeks, however, Tim has been cooking up an explosive plan that will finallyput an end to the situation. And in the gamble that he’s preparing, the stakes are high and eitherhe or Vincent Giovanni, his nemesis, will walk away from their dangerous game of mind pokerwithout so much as the shirt, or skin, on their back. With the spikey blond tips of his dark roots bouncing along with his stride, Tim hurries to theC.O station where Benji Graham is at the controls, as usual. “Graham,” Tim speaks up, leaningover the desk and trying to keep his voice out of the earshot of Graham’s co-workers. “I really don’t have time for your shit, McDonald,” Benji frowns. “Why don’t you just go backto your cell for a while. I’ve got enough things on my mind without having to think aboutcovering my tracks about all the shit you’ve had me into over the last few months.” “This is different,” McDonald says in a level voice. “No schemes. No plans. I just have astraight-forward request.” Graham’s eyebrows lower in suspicion - he knows that Tim McDonald wouldn’t be able to keephis hands clean in a car wash. And at the moment, with one prisoner murdered for every weeksince Geoffery Rankin’s death (over three times the usual rate), he doesn’t need any moreworries on his shoulders. “Say it,” Graham says in a loaded voice, “but if I think you’rescheming, or dicking me around, for one fucking second, McDonald - not only will I get you outof my face, but I’ll take you to the fucking hole and kick your skinny ass while I’m at it! I don’thave anyone to answer to for that kind of shit anymore, you know.” Tim takes the words seriously and it takes a moment for him to regain his confident grin. “Noworries, Ahab,” he says, referring to the chief C.O of Unit B by the nickname given to him forthe scar that runs from his face down his shirt. “This has nothing to do with you. I’ve got someinformation I want to put forward - I want an appointment to speak to the Warden.” Again, the middle-aged C.O’s brow furrows and he gazes for a long moment at McDonald’ssmooth young face - the poker face of all poker faces. Questioning McDonald’s sincerity,Graham asks: “How serious are you about this, McDonald?” “Very fucking serious.” Monday, November 18 - Day 137 The office of the Warden is decorated, for the most part, with bookshelves filled with endlesspages of law that even the Warden himself finds boring. He has yet to read most of it. But still,it’s good for decoration, he thinks. Now, as he finally gets a chance to meet the heavily reputed Tim McDonald, he’s not sure whatto think. McDonald, who’s spent just over four months in the Tank has already taken control ofthe Italian Mafia-like gang that runs the entire place and is known as a dangerous customer. Things always seem to turn up in his favour, whether or not it looks like he has anything to dowith it. The untrained eye might think him lucky - but Gavyn Miller knows that Tim is apuppeteer, tugging away at the strings of the Tank and making its prisoners dance to his tune. He doesn’t look like much, thinks Miller as he stares across the heavy oak desk that provides acenter piece to his office. If Miller didn’t know better, he’d think the young man was justanother wanna-be street thug. He’s a lot smaller than the Warden expected too - not so much inheight, as Tim McDonald is actually quite tall, but he expected the man that leads the Italians ofthe Tank to not be nearly so slim. His build is average, really, thinks the Warden. If not for thecrop of bleached blond hair that spikes out from the top of his brown roots, he wouldn’t standout at all. But most surprising at all, to the Warden, is the fact that he can’t see a thread of Italian heritagein the man’s youthful face. When he originally heard that Tim McDonald was runningthe Italian gang after Doug Hattoni’s death, he thought the young man probably had at leastsome Italian blood in him. But now, upon seeing him, nothing of that nature is evident. “Mr. McDonald,” the Warden sighs, tossing the young man’s thick file onto the desk in front ofhim. “It’s rare that I agree to meet with prisoners, you know - but the Benjamin Graham stuckhis neck out for you. And because of that, this had better be important.” “I’ve got some information,” says Tim as he sits in the chair opposite the Warden with his handscuffed behind his back. “Big shit.” “Would you like me to send for some police officers?” asks Miller, leaning forward. “Or maybethe FBI?” “Fuck that,” McDonald spits. “I’m going to say this shit to you. You tell the guys you have totell, and that’s that. There’s no way their going to even get close to my name - you got that?” Miller sits in silence for a long moment. “You got that?” repeats McDonald. “I can’t risk a dirty cop handing my name over to the wrongpeople. You’ve seen the movies - you know how this shit works. I’m going to tell you somethings. You’re going to go to the authorities and relay my story. Tell them that one of yourprisoners came forward. Nothing else. Ok?” “Ok,” sighs the Warden, reluctantly. “Promise me,” McDonald says, his eyes burning with seriousness. “Give me your fucking word,Miller.” “Ok, ok!” says the Warden as he stands up from his seat and turns his back to McDonald. “But Ihope you know the cops aren’t going to be able to do shit if they don’t get the informationstraight from the horses mouth. They can’t put me on the stand to testify about things ‘aprisoner’ told me.” “You won’t go on the stand,” shrugs McDonald. “Lets just call this an anonymous tip.” “Alright,” sighs the Warden as he takes his seat. “I’ll bite. What is this magical information.” A smile appears on Tim’s face and the intensity drains from his brown eyes. “Three years ago,”he begins, three police officers were killed on duty in Miami, Florida.” “Didn’t they link that to the Hattoni family?” the Warden asks with a perked eyebrow. “A link isn’t a conviction,” says Tim with a smile. “I’m not going to tell you who did it, and if Iget word that you tell a soul that I know who did it, I’ll have a riot started in every Unit in thiswhole fucking prison - don’t test my power, Miller. Don’t even try.” “Alright,” Miller says with his hands out. “We’ve been through this. You’re name won’t beuttered.” “Good,” McDonald says, leaning back in his chair. He gets back on topic: “The court couldn’tconvict the murderers in the case for only one reason - there were no bodies. When you’re upagainst multi-million dollar lawyers, it gets a little tough to prove a person is really dead whenthere’s no tombstone to support your case.” “I remember the case,” Miller nods. “Are you saying you know where the bodies are?” “Those and twenty more,” McDonald nods. “You got a map of the USA handy?”
