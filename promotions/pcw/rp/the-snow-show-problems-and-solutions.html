Date: Season I, Episode 9 ﻿************************************ The Snow ShowSeason I, Episode 9“Problems and Solutions”************************************ Location: Morravelli’s Restaurant - New York City Date: January 6, 2003Time: 7:40 pm Justin Hattoni sat leaned back in his chair and stared across the table at his opponent for the day- John Mossilano, the man who had sat at the helm of the Mossilano family for the last thirty-five years. Hattoni was only thirty-three now. “You’re father and I did business peacefully for years,” John said as he stared across the table atthe young Don of the Hattoni family. “And never - never - did he ask me for a piece ofsomething that had nothing to do with him.” Justin’s eyes narrowed as he stared at the old man across from him. John Mossilano was at leasta year or two past seventy, and it was obvious that he didn’t respect Justin’s youthfulness. Johnhad to work his way up through the family before he was able to take over - he was alreadynearly fourty years old when the Mossilano family was given to him, and he changed his name tomatch it. Hattoni was born on his throne, and had been running things for years while the oldDon rotted in prison. Now that Doug Hattoni was dead, the family was completely owned by hiseldest son, Justin, who reigned with fire and passion. Sometimes, common business senseeluded him, at least in John’s mind. They weren’t the only to at the table. To each man’s right sat his accompaniment. PeteyMoseriole, or Petey the Vice as he was commonly known, sat on the right hand of Justin. Hekept himself silent throughout the meeting. His arms stayed folded over his chest, and his curlyblack hair was slicked back nicely for the occasion. To John Mossilano’s right was someone that neither Justin nor Petey had ever seen. He was abroad man, somewhere in his mid fifties, with a nearly bald head and a large, impeding, nose. Like Petey, he kept silent. The meeting was held in a small Italian restaurant, after hours, where they knew they could bealone and unobserved. The owner was a close friend of both men, and they trusted himimmensely. “You say it had nothing to do with me,” Justin said cooly as he turned his gaze to his fingernails, “yet we both know that it was a job that my boys had been planning for weeks - monthseven.” “And then your boys handed the job over to us,” said John as he pointed at the table. “Theycouldn’t handle it, and my men could.” “The planning and the ideas came from my people,” Justin said as he raised his eyes to meet theold man’s. “They want a taste of it, and I think it’s fair.” “You’ll get nothing!” John said in a final tone. Justin let a fierce glare escape him, and then concealed it quickly, returning to his usual calmexpression. He knew that John would never have talked that way to his father, Doug Hattoni -John wouldn’t have dared. Doug Hattoni may have ruled peacefully over the family, but heruled with an iron fist. “I don’t understand the problem,” Justin said after a long pause. “I can’t believe that you wouldspeak this way to any of the other families. The Vurdos, the Imperiolis... and were my fatherstill alive, you wouldn’t speak this way to me either.” There was a long moment of silence, and Justin knew that John was admitting guilt, if only tohimself. “Look, John,” Justin said as he leaned in. “I’m not asking for much. Just a taste - that’s all. Justsomething so my boys feel like they’re getting their due - so they feel respected, John. That’s allI’m asking from you. Respect. And in this case, respect can be shown in the form of a couple ofgrand.” “You’ll get nothing!” repeated John. His eyes were harder now. Another long silence ensued. “You’re telling me,” Justin finally said, “that your crew made a three million dollar heist, andyou’re not willing to part with twenty, maybe thirty grand, to some people who helped out? Isthat what you’re telling me, John? Because if it is, I have to tell you, I’m disappointed.Very disappointed.” “I’ve said all I have to say.” John stood up from the table, and walked away. Justin allowed hisdeep eyes to harden as he brushed his right hand through his short-clipped grey hair. *** Location: Morravelli’s Restaurant parking lot - New York CityDate: January 6, 2003Time: 7:50 pm Justin and Petey walked slowly into the parking lot to their cars. John Mossilano’s car was longgone, but his companion’s car remained. “This makes me fucking nervous, Justin,” Petey said as he looked around the empty parking lot. “That second cocksucker - what was his fucking name? - his car’s still here. Why don’t you letme give you a ride back to your place. You know, for safety reasons.” “Fuck that,” Justin said as he lit a cigarette. “John Mossilano doesn’t have the balls to set up ahit. Not on me.” Petey looked over his shoulder one final time, and there, walking around from the back of therestaurant, was the man who had been accompanying John Mossilano. Instinctively, Peteypushed his way in front of the young Don. “What the fuck is this!?” Petey shouted across the parking lot. “Easy,” Justin said from behind Petey. “Keep your piece where it fucking is. I don’t want ashootout here in the middle of a fucking parking lot.” Fortunately, the stranger from the Mossilano family raised his hands above his head in a gesturethat he meant no harm. “I just want to talk to you guys,” he told them as he approached. “I justwant to talk.” “I thought your boss did all the fucking talking inside,” Justin said sharply. “My boss speaks for himself,” the man shrugged. “As he advances in years, however, it seemsthat his words speak for less and less of what the family actually feels. My name is TommyMossilano - I’m a nephew of John’s.” *** Location: Snow Residence - New York SuburbsDate: January 6, 2003Time: 7:55 pm Her eyes were a strange painting of anger, sadness, and confusion. Sitting in the center of a sofa,Krista tried to restrain herself from crying. Why was Snow so adamant at getting involved in herlife? Sure, it was fine sometimes, but he had a knack for overstepping his boundaries. So didher sister, who sat next to him on the arm rest of his chair. Raven’s mouth was pursed, thoughKrista couldn’t tell who the sour thought was aimed at. “He’s not the type of guy you want to get serious with, Krista,” Snow told her with a sigh as heleaned back in his chair. He didn’t know what else to say to her at this point. “I don’t think that’s for you to decide,” said Krista with hard eyes. She was inconsistent withher feelings about her brother-in-law’s intrusive ways. At times, she appreciated it, and at othertimes, like this, she hated it. “You don’t know him,” Snow answered. “I know him as well as you do!” Krista said, louder than before. “He’s my boyfriend! He’s only your business associate!” Everything about her words were sharp and biting. She first referred to Marty as her boyfriend, and then verbally slapped Snow in the face with theword ‘business associate’. Both women in the house now knew the business that Snow was in. It was obvious. He sighed hard and lowered his eyes on the younger sibling of his wife. “I’ve got reasons forwhat I do,” he told her, attempting to be gentle with his words. He failed miserably. “And Iknow things. And I know that this guy is no good. I don’t want you seeing him, Krista.” Raven’s eyes suddenly turned to her husband, and Krista knew which side of the fence she wason. Even she saw that Snow was being unfair and controlling. “Wait a minute,” Raven said as her eyes hardened. “What are you saying here, Jason? Are yousaying that Krista can’t date this guy because he’s a low life mafia thug, but it’s ok for me to bemarried to one!?” Finally, she had addressed the feelings she had long had. She didn’t knowjust how deep her husband was in the gangster game, but she hoped that he wasn’t over his head. She couldn’t picture him ever doing something too serious, of course. Then again, there was a side to her husband that Raven didn’t know or understand. He was akiller now, but she was blind to it. He wanted to keep her that way, and in truth, she wanted tostay that way. “You can’t stop me,” Krista said finally as she stood up from the sofa and marched defiantlytoward the door. “Where are you going!?” Snow yelled as he leapt from his own seat, startling his pretty youngwife. “You can’t control me!” Krista snapped over her shoulder as she grabbed her coat and openedthe door. “Hey!” Snow yelled, pointing an index finger at her strongly. “Where are you going!? Don’tyou fucking walk out of here! Don’t fucking do it!” He lost the argument, and it was exclaimated by the slam of the kitchen door. With that, Kristawas gone. “So what!?” Snow whirled around to face his wife. “We’re just supposed to let her leave!? Justlike that!? Off to God knows where!? She’s going to his place, you know!? Thatmotherfucker!! Fuck, Raven!” “You don’t talk to your wife like that!” she said with blazing brown eyes. For a moment,he felt dwarfed under their intensity. With that, she turned her back to him and walked away. *** Location: Morravelli’s Restaurant Parking Lot - New York CityDate: January 6, 2003Time: 8:00 pm Tommy Mossilano proved to be smarter than his uncle, at least in Justin Hattoni’s eyes. He wasalso much less trust worthy. But that didn’t make the innuendo of his words any less attractive. “You know,” he began to Justin as he lit a cigarette of his own - Justin’s was burnt low now,“John’s been in power a long time. A long time. But, the family feels that every so often thingshave to change. Sometimes, I have to wonder, if old John wasn’t around right now, how closewould the Hattoni’s and the Mossilanos be? Huh?” Justin nodded, unable to believe his ears. “But you know how things are,” he said with a sigh. “Just remember, that not many in theMossilano family feel the way John does. And even fewer would seek vengeance, if somethingwere to accidentally happen to him.” And with that, the stranger walked away to his car. Left under the dim light of a street, Petey and Justin exchanged a surprised glance. “You think it’s a trick?” Petey asked finally. “Why would they do that?” Justin said as he finally began to regain his cool. “They’d have adead Don and a war on their hands. Nobody wants a war.” “So what then?” Petey asked. “Are we going to take him up on his offer.” Justin remained silent, but his eyes danced under the orange light.
