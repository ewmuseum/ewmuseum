Win or Victory?

3 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 3, 2003 Begin Transmission. SOCIETY’s education on women must continue. Despite not really needing it in two strong performances, last week was a winless one for SOCIETY. Winless, but not victory-less. The difference? Allow the Order themselves to explain it.) “Mr. Chang,” the Conformist said, slightly irritated, “I really haven’t been doing very well lately.” (SOCIETY’s head, for the first time, was hung low. He was no longer proud of himself; proud of his cause. Chang looked across at SOCIETY, and quickly decided to diffuse this emotion.) “On the contrary SOCIETY, you have been doing quite well.” “But I have no victories to speak of last week.” (Chang shook his head.) “SOCIETY, you must understand that your definition of victory, and PCW’s definition must be two completely different things.” (SOCIETY cracked his knuckles to relieve built-up pressure, but even at this obviously innocent reasoning, Chang cowered slightly, before regaining his composure and continuing.) “A win, by PCW’s standards, requires you to force your opponent’s shoulder to the canvas for a count of three, or to put them in such severe pain that they are forced to give up on the spot.” “And, I did neither of those!” (SOCIETY’s dejection was evident on his face.) “You did not let me finish, SOCIETY! THOSE victories are nice, but they are not vital!” “But,” SOCIETY inquired, “Do I not need victories to obtain championship belts?” “I never WANTED championship belts. Or needed them, for that matter. That was always YOUR idea, SOCIETY. But, it is a flawed one.” (SOCIETY stared at Chang, trying not to let the little, tiny, microscopic bit of anger that he felt pour through.) “Then, Mr. Chang, why am I here?” (For some reason, SOCIETY suddenly felt as if he wished he could have screamed that to Chang.) “You are here to make people conform! THAT is the only measuring stick of victory that we can use! THE NUMBER OF PEOPLE YOU BLOODY AND FORCE TO CONFORM!” (Chang’s grin is wide at the thought..) “And, I suppose that I begin with Lacuna Debris.” “Precisely, SOCIETY.” “That shall be no problem AT ALL.” (Chang studied SOCIETY’s face. He seemed more confident than usual, more prepared…and even a bit more angry.) “Why are you so confident in this particular battle?” “I would rather my reasoning remain exactly that. Mine.” (SOCIETY turned abruptly, and left, leaving Chang alone in the room.) “That’s not like SOCIETY. Something is wrong. Something is dreadfully wrong with my creation. And, when something is wrong with my creation..” (Chang opened up his wallet, $10,000 lighter thanks to the fine he had received at the hands of PCW. He counted out several $100 bills, but the amount was nowhere near what he usually carried with him.) “Something is wrong with the Order. I must go and take care of this immediately!” (Chang turned, headed out of the room right after SOCIETY, worry on his face.) End Transmission.
