Date: 10/15/02 Countless people walk the streets and through the park, blurred together into one multi-colored mass. The sky is overcast, though the clouds are thin enough to allow for the blue sky and sun to creep through. There is a breeze that meanders through the concrete labyrinth of the city and whisks through the park with a sort of carefree feeling. Ninja #41 sits on one of the cleanest benches in the world in Sapporo City Park, by the road that leads to the business district. His right leg crossed over the left, he reads the daily newspaper. The front page headline? 'Crazed man claiming to be The End's half-brother killed in violent shootout with police.' Man:'Ey.' Designation: End Promo, Audio Only {Athazagoraphobia: The fear of being forgotten/ignored/neglected.} Amazing, the lengths to which people will go to get everything that they want all at once. I see the frantic jockeying for title shots *coughjamieceltcough* and for some reason I'm reminded of a game of Hungry Hungry Hippos. The sensible person would be satisfied to allow everyone their equal fair share (and no, I'm not talking about the Red Army communist issue). Observing the way people (not just in the PCW, but anywhere in the wrestling industry) work, it's not about fairness or sensibility. If you can, you grab all 20 of those little plastic marbles, leaving the other three players with nothing. But what did it benefit you to grab more than the others? Winning the game? The game is pointless, and one play is never any more important than the others. Some like to brag about their many title wins (thank god Frisco's gone), or at the least pay the announcers not to let us forget it. To a point there's nothing wrong with that, everyone has something they've done that they're proud of. But to dwell on the past (even a good past) is nothing but a crutch, something to limit oneself from their true potential. For one who lives off of past accomplishments is doomed to never surpass those deeds. While it is nice to have a garage full of those iron pyrite (You think that they actually waste REAL gold in the belts they let you keep?) championship titles, we don't know if you won those titles through set up matches (New Breed PCW World Title Whoring), or maybe you suck and you just won against someone who sucked more, somone with no talent whatsoever (Lance Bell winning the always defunct, now RETIRED Rising Star championship from Red Rock when Red Rock had the clap so bad he could hardly walk)... And as for those persons who may be new to PCW, whether by weeks or days, you may doubt yourself in such a match as the Black Friday Mall Brawl. You've earned nothing, have no ring rep... just the jolly green rookie. In effect, the rookies have no past and are striding ahead by walking backwards, hoping to catch a glimpse of that past as they work to create it. This fear is not as pronounced as it is in the veterans, though. The Snow's and Clyde's, the Miyagawa's, Akiyama's, Duke Williams', Darkstar's, Syko's, Tim Walden's... The rookies know what it's like to be ignored, it's fresh in their hearts from very recent experience. The veterans, however, most likely have not had to have that feeling in a long time, but they remember it and fight tooth and nail never to feel it again. People: don't worry about it. What you don't know, what you have yet to understand, is that YOUR CAREER IS ONLY AS IMPRESSIVE AS YOUR NEXT MATCH. So it's up to every one of you to decide if in this next match taking the place of Fusion, if you're going to be more concerned with what you haven't done or have already done, or if you're going to be dealing with the present and the future. An interesting story I heard once at a bus station, there was an old man, a vagabond if anyone still uses that word besides me, who was waiting for a bus, he said. He'd been waiting for years, but was harmless enough and had become something of a fixture in the station. I sat down near him while awaiting my own bus, and he just came on over, friendly old guy, and started talking to me. This was the story he told me... 'A long time ago, there was a man who declared at a very young age that he would not die until he had done everything he could in life. The Fates had been kind enough to allow him this trivial human pursuit, and had not cut his string until it was complete.' 'Well, it took him 100 years, by that time he was one of the oldest men in the entire country. And he one morning after writing a play, he realized that he had done everything that he could do in life, and would soon be visited by the messenger of death.' 'He didn't want to die. Though he'd fulfilled his original goal and had nothing left to do, he had grown quite fond of living and wasn't ready to give that up.' 'Well, this old man, in his lifelong studies, had learned that the messenger of death always came from behind to bring a person to the underworld, both as a symoblic reflection of one's life catching up with them, and also because he was actually afraid of humans seeing him. So, the old man had developed a way to prevent Death from sneaking up on him, just in case Death came early.' 'He rigged a mirror harness all around his front and top of his body, so that when he wallked around, he would be looking behind himself the entire time. He didn't need to see in front of him, he knew where everything was. Moving around was like a routine for him.' 'Well, he was walking around that day after completing the play, when what happened but that he suddenly died. His strand was cut, and here was Charon was rowing him down the river Styx. Naturally the old man found this quite perplexing and asked Charon what happened.' 'Charon said that the old man had a flawed plan. He said that a normal person would be equally concerned with the past and the present, and though occasionally look over his shoulder, would more often look ahead to the future. But the old man could only live for the past because he felt he had no future to look forward to, that he had done everything.' 'When the old man said that he had, Charon disagreed with him.' 'No, Charon said, you said that you would not die until you did everything in the world that there is to do. But what you did not realize is that there are infinite things to do in the world so long as one has the imagination to try it. But you believed you did everything, and only concerned yourself with the past through your mirrors.' 'Well, Death and the Fates, seeing that the old man limited himself to what he could do, decided that it was time for him to pass on to the afterlife. And because the old man only looked back and never forward, it wasn't the past that caught up with him, but the future that he ran into that was his undoing.' 'Had the old man merely kept one eye behind him, and one eye ahead, he would have lived forever...' You're probably all bored that I'm not talking about you, but the old man in the bus station did have a good point to the story, one you all would do well to take to heart. You fear being forgotten. You want to remain in everyone's thoughts and minds forever. But it is VERY unlikely that that will happen. The overwhelming odds are that you will be ignored and forgotten. Accept it and move on. A padded resume or a blank resume, it doesn't matter. Years from now it will mean the same thing: nothing, because the people who pay to see you perform will most likely have moved onto something else *cough*KANO*cough*. And by living for the past and not the future, you only make it easier for them to move away from you toward someone else who, to borrow a phrase from a human crash test dummy (so says the nVg) working in CT, 'lives for the moment.' Don't remind us of what you've done. Just wrestle and show us what you can do now. Please, Smoke... Just shut up and wrestle...
