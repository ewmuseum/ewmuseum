TEC: Odd Jobs

24 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/24/02 {If you recall from TEC: Lamar's sick, then you'll know that Misfits Richard, Slade, and Drek Redaxe had to get jobs to afford to travel to the Land of the Lepers to get medicine that would cure the strange disease plaguing Lamar. In the meantime, The End is off doing his own thing, winning matches and trying to train to win the March Madness Rumble for it All. He’s on a hot streak so far, his singles wrestling winning streak now at 4 wins, beating Mark Estep before the last PPV, Banshee and THE Multiple Man in the March Madness mini-tournament, and WWA no-show and US champion Nick Extreme in the first round of the GTT. Now, he teams with Drake Maxwell, his second round GTT opponent, to face Xavier Kannon and Bobby Stormbringer on Sunday Sabotage. But enough about The End, this isn’t his RP. It’s the Misfits’ time to shine. Now we go to one of those Misfits, Richard the Scruffy-Bearded Man (who looks very much like X-Pac, quite to his chagrin), as he ventures out into the job market to ply his skills in some trade in order to get money to help Lamar. Let’s see what he’s up to.} Richard: Ice Cream Man It is daytime. The sky is gray, with some white clouds; the day is still warm, though. Richard drives through a suburban neighborhood with his ice cream truck. Well, it’s an ice cream truck today, anyhow. Usually, it’s a meat-hauling truck, but it’s all they could give Richard on short notice. The truck is yellow. The wheels are spiral-swirled yellow and red, and across the side of the truck’s freezer section is a pink and green sunburst pattern, a huge steak (see, I told you it was a meat truck) in the middle of it, with the word “Meats” above it in yellow letters and in blue-green some Japanese characters written below it. The front of the truck has been made to look like a face. So along Richard drives in his spiffy pink Good Humor Man suit, with white gloves, red bowtie, red and yellow yin-yang pin and a strange pink hat (the company’s logo is an eye with strange characters above it). Yes, Richard feels strange wearing what he is, but he’ll do anything to help out his sick friend Lamar. Passing a white two-story house with red shingles, a little blonde girl in a pink dress (accompanied by her white shirt, brown slacks wearing father) comes out waving a dollar like a flag; Richard stops. The girl approaches the window, which Richard has rolled up so the air-conditioning won’t go to waste as slow as he’s driving. Sound still passes through the window as easily as it does air. “Why, hey there, little girl,” Richard says through the glass, cheery as he can be. “What would would you like?” The girl thinks about it for a second (odd how the children aren’t provided some type of visible menu, but I guess that’s not important. “Ummm…” the girl says in more of a quiet aside to Richard, putting her right index finger to her chin as she stares at the ground thinking. “Can I have… a ice cream sandwich?” “No,” Richard says quite succinctly. “Why?” says the little girl, a slight frown on her face. Richard replies through the glass, “Richard’s truck only carries Choco Tacos, Crunch bars, and frozen honey bars.” The little girl nods her head. “I see,” she says. “So,” Richard continues, eager to get this girl to buy something, “can Richard get you a Choco Taco, a Crunch bar, or a frozen honey bar?” The little girl quickly answers, enthusiastically, “Crunch Bar, please!” “Oh, I’m sorry,” Richard says with a genuinely apologetic tone, “but Richard is out of Crunch Bars.” “Then,” replies the little girl, “can I have a Choco Taco?” Richard shakes his head behind the glass, even though the tint prevents him from being seen. “Richard has no Choco Tacos, either.” At this point, the girl’s impatient father interjects. “So, let me get this straight;” he begins, his balding head sweating beads, “my daughter’s choices are ‘or a frozen honey bar’? Why even mention those other things?” “Because,” Richard says, adopting a matter-of-factly voice, “I’m supposed to say everything we will in case you want it next time. Now can I get the little girl a frozen honey bar or not?” The little girl shakes her head in the negative, saying, “I don’t like honey.” “Okay then,” Richard says. He waits for the girl and her father to back away from the truck; when clear, he drives away. As he passes by a two-story yellow house with a red-shingled roof, a bigheaded little boy wearing a pink shirt, navy blue shorts, and red shoes, runs out with his black pug and stops the truck. “Well, hello,” Richard says in a pleasant voice, the windows to his truck still rolled up. “What can Richard get for you?” The little boy responds in an eager, high-pitched voice, “I wanna fwozen honey bar, pwease.” “Okay, let Richard get that for you,” comes from the truck, Richard going into the back to get the ice cream. While the little boy waits, a pale-faced dark-haired teenage girl wearing a long ragged black dress walks by. A few moments later, Richard emerges from the back with the frozen honey bar, and rolls down the window. “Okay, that’ll be 50 cents,” says Richard, holding out the ice cream in his left hand (the ice cream held with Richard wearing a hand puppet of Abraham Lincoln for some reason), ready to receive the money with his right hand. A terrified shriek from the little boy is all that Richard gets for his trouble. The pug begins to bark furiously, attempting to jump and attack Richard. The weird goth girl just stands there, hands at her side, the dress and black boots clashing like the English and French at Waterloo. The little boy picks up his dog, and runs back into his house screaming, “MOMMY!” the whole way back. His door is slammed harder than would be expected for such a small boy. Richard is hurt by the boy’s fear in him. He asks the goth girl what he did wrong. “Was there anything,” Richard begins, “Richard did to frighten the young child?” The goth girl just stands staring at Richard for a moment, her gaze seemingly piercing into Richard’s soul. This is alternated every few seconds with her looking up at the top of the back of Richard’s truck. Then, after ten seconds of awkward silence, the goth girl begins to laugh uncontrollably. Richard is quite confused. “What is it?” Richard asks. “Why are you laughing at Richard?” The goth girl tries to calm down her laughing; when she does, she says, “Well, there’s either two things that scared that boy away: one would be that huge Mask of Death you’re wearing over your head.” Richard touches the giant skull mask he decided to wear today as a novelty. He takes off the skull mask, sad that he unintentionally scared a small boy who just wanted some ice cream. Richard’s eyes begin to tear up, as he realizes that he’ll be reported, and lose his job. And if he loses his job, it won’t look good for him if he tries to get another one to get enough money to help Lamar. Richard looks back at the goth girl, who is still laughing a bit. Without any prompting, she continues on her point. “Or,” the goth girl says, “it could be that giant bee that’s hanging onto the top of your truck.” Richard pokes his head out the window, and sees the bee. It’s about the size of the boy. Richard screams, gets back in his car and rolls the windows up, and then speeds away, pulling the ice cream truck out from under the bee. The bee (mutant produced by toxic waste dumped in the forest), smelling the frozen honey bars, landed on the truck while the boy was ordering his ice cream. The now-angered bee flies off after the truck, Richard doing his best to shake it. After a 30-min chase through the neighborhoods, Richard finally stops by a river, opens up the back of the truck to allow the bee to get inside, and calls the police to deal with it. The police release the bee and shoot it dead with hundreds of bullets. People from Ridley’s come to collect it for an exhibit in Orlando. Richard sits down by the truck, disappointed and a little tired. As most of the police are leaving, a chubby police officer in a black uniform walks up to him. “May I have some ice cream?” says the officer in a voice that even sounds fat. Richard looks up at the man with a half-indifferent, half-upset look. With a bit of acid in his tone, Richard says, “Do you want a frozen honey bar?” The officer replies, “No.” Richard then turns his gaze to the ground, but gives a forceful, “Then feck off!” {Well, Richard’s venture into the job market ended with a bit of disappointment, but he did manage to make some money for his friend Lamar. While Richard sulks back at his place, we’ll check in with Drek Redaxe as he attempts to get into the real world with a job.} Drek Redaxe: Photographer The sky has cleared up, but it’s late afternoon, so it’s not as bright. A country road is the setting for this particular section of the RP. A grassy field, with scrub brush and a tree here or there, lies in front of a quaint brick cottage, the red roof standing out against the full green trees in the background. Further in the distance, a brick wall arched into a gateway leads to an orchard, and further still are rocky foothills. The road is solid, but dirt. There’s some rocks and scrub strewn along it in somewhat of an organized pattern. The photo is simple in Drek’s mind, but everyone else is a little bit confused about it. Confused and worried as well, the picture is one that is indeed quite dangerous. Riding in a red wooden wagon used for hauling grain is a group of babies all wearing Huggies brand diapers; all of them are Caucasian babies. One baby is holding a stick. The child sits at the front of the wagon, and with the stick points the wagon onward down the road. Here’s the scary part – the wagon is being carted along by a 8-foot-long millipede, a leather strap connecting it to the wagon. Weird, huh? Drek takes his pictures with a Nikon. His assistant stands wide-eyed at the sight he sees. “I can’t believe this!” says the assistant. Drek ignores the assistant; in fact, he send the assistant away. For 10 minutes, he shoots the scene from various angles. When finished, he packs away his camera. The buyer of the photos drives up to the scene in a red Porsche Boxster. He wears a light brown suit and a red tie. His short hair is slicked back with gel. With confusion and fear, he looks at the set of the photo shoot. “WHAT THE HELL IS THIS?” exclaims the buyer. Drek, crouching down to his pack (he’s not that short), looks up at the buyer. “Something wrong?” he asks, not sure what the problem is. With wide, wild hand-gestures, the buyer strides up to Drek and stands over him, talking down (not just by height) to the miniature photographer. “We hired you,” the buyer begins, “to shoot some advertising photos for our business. Now, I don’t know what kind of mind you have, but this is not the way we envision Red Lobster!” “It’s just impressionist art photography.” “No! It’s weird and sick… is that a giant millipede?” Drek looks over at the millipede and smirks. He points over at it with his thumb as he turns his attention back to the buyer. “Yeah,” says Drek, “I found it in the forest right by these large cans of smelly green fluid.” The buyer stops himself for a moment and rubs his eyes. Then, he picks up as normal. “Look,” the buyer says, “we want a wholesome image for Red Lobster. But you can’t honestly expect me to walk into a board meeting with pictures of this. It’s pointless and disturbing… oh my God, the giant millipede is eating the babies….” The buyer says the last line with more speechless shock than horror. Drek looks back to the millipede, which has slipped out of its harness jumped into the wagon. It has already ingested two babies, and is in the process swallowing the third. Drek runs over to the wagon and shouts, “Bad, Fluffy! Bad!” The millipede, apparently named Fluffy, spits out the baby, and hides under the wagon. The buyer rushes up behind Drek and starts yelling some more. “ARE YOU MAD?! I SHOULD HAVE YOU ARRESTED! WHERE DID YOU GET THOSE BABIES?! YOU’VE GOT A LOT OF EXPLAINING TO DO.” Drek, tired of the buyer’s bad attitude, looks at Fluffy. “Fluffy,” Drek says, waving his head at the buyer, “Dinner.” Fluffy comes out from under the wagon and engulfs the buyer, slowly eating him alive. Drek grabs his camera and leaves the buyer to be eaten by Fluffy. Drek returns the babies to the dumpster from which he found them. {Well, that was Drek’s job experience. Now, let’s see what Slade is up to.} Slad: Auctioneer Open in a plain white room seats 100 people, all bidding on priceless antiques up for sale in this auction. The pieces include sculptures, paintings, books and journals, and other items. Standing at the front, on a 1-foot high makeshift wooden stage, calling from behind a podium of the same wood, is Slade wearing a black suit and tie. An assistant, who just moves pieces in and out, brings up a miniature version of The Thinker. It comes to Slade to call this piece. “Uta ondfsda uhbfahs djh ziuj n dfbnd,” Slade says. The bidders look around in confusion. “G odjnk jib,” Slade continues, “iu sad ad adfs.” Not one person in the crowd understands Slade. They don’t do anything but speak amongst themselves, asking what’s going on. Slade taps his gavel on the podium to call for silence. Then, he continues. “Mnubn I dyagdana auowifh rgbn vzn oids faba,” Slade says. The room is silent. Slade says once more, “Mnubn I dyagdana auowifh rgbn vzn oids faba.” A man in the back assumes the meaning of Slade’s last statement, and raises his bidding paddle. Slade acknowledges him. “O apasdmm cb,” exclaims the sleepwalking Slade while pointing at the man, who turns out to be Donald Trump, wearing a long black coat.. This goes on for quite a while, people bidding even though they have no idea what is being said. Finally, after three minutes of confused bidding, no more hands are raised, and Donald Trump wins the sculpture. He goes to collect his purchase as Slade walks out the door with $300 to go toward helping out Lamar. The last thing he hears as he leaves is Donald Trump shouting, “Nooooo! I didn’t know I bid my soul! I couldn’t understand him! Get away!” Back at End Manor, the Misfits stand over Lamar’s bed, the sheets still pulled over his body. He’s getting worse, and he’ll die if he doesn’t get the treatment he needs. The Misfits get together and check on all the money that they made. Richard pulls a 50-dollar bill out of his pocket. “This is all I made,” says Richard, still wearing the ice cream outfit (at least he’s ditched the hat and skull mask). Drek snatches the $50 out of Richard’s hand and puts it in his pocket. “Hey!” Richard exclaims, wondering why Drek took the money.” Drek shrugs his shoulders. “Hey,” he says, “I lost $50 today, so this’ll make up for it.” Richard shakes his head in disappointment. Turning to Slade, he says, “How much did you make?” Slade pulls out three $100 bills, and puts them down on the table. Drek grabs one. Richard tries to stop him, but Drek recoils. “I’m not going to take it,” he spits at Richard. “Have some trust in me.” Drek holds the bill up to the light. And nods his head. He grabs another one and nods. On the third one, he shakes his head side-to-side and crumples it up. “What are you doing?” Richard asks. “‘Tis fake, the other dollar.” Drek says. “We only have $200 for Lamar.” Richard sighs heavily. “Dang,” is all he says. “Well, we’ll have to make due.” Slade holds up a bus schedule he had in his pocket. “Spj fj aiwme muxnads ijpokz,” he says. Richard perks up. “Greyhound bus fares save the day!” The Misfits charge out of the house with their $200, prepared to go to the Land of the Lepers to get Lamar the medicine he needs to get better. With that, we’ll leave. The End picks Walden up and slams him to the ground with an Endslam. Leo gets up and is met with a kick to the chest or an Endkick and The End climbs the turnbuckle.
