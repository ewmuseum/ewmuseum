Becoming Mad II: Introduction

13 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5-13-03 “New York?!” Exclaims A.J. Trinidad into a cell phone whilst walking through a run down airport. “Why the hell would I be going to New York? I have a match in Afghanistan this Friday. Well, I guess we all could be mistaken here can’t we? Your man with his purple folder will just have to wait.” Trinidad clicks his cell phone closed, but it’s not a cell phone at all, in actuality it’s nothing more than a compact makeup case, and what in God’s name Trinidad is doing with that, we may never know. The airport is almost vacant, due mostly to the late night hour or the constant terrorist threat, however, some brave souls manage to continue to commute across country lines during this trying time. Dust floats around thick enough to consider blanketing, and a load of farm animals wait off in a distant corner while they’re herder plays an old school upright version of the Atari classic video game “Joust”. “What the hell?” A.J. silently questions to himself while moving closer to the luggage claim. A few patrons covered in bed sheets meander around in the background as A.J. Trinidad stops to scratch an annoying itch on his foot that had been present since the mid-flight showing of “Summer School”, having to remove his shoe and dance the one legged dance of peril in fear of his foot catching some foreign infection if it dare touch the ground, he feverously scratches away until something catches his attention. A man, dressed in a neatly cut black suit stands about fifteen yards away from him in the direction he was heading. This would not seem so odd if it weren’t for the colorful purple folder he was carrying under his arm. “He couldn’t have known to meet me in Kabul” A.J. says to nobody in particular. “Could he?” A.J. carefully places his foot back into his size twelve loafer and continues on, paying careful attention to the aforementioned man with the purple folder. Moving painfully closer to a potential confrontation with each step Trinidad begins to speed up in an attempt to blow by as quickly as possibly and he did just that, but as soon as he passed the man, he was halted by his voice. “Mr. Trinidad I presume?” asks the strangely eloquent man whilst reaching a hand back as he turns around to meet Trinidad who has seemed to turn on his heels at the same time. “You can’t be the man I was supposed to meet in New York City?” Trinidad professes as he cautiously extends his hand to meet the free hand of the man standing in front of him. “One and the same” the man acknowledges. “Some meetings in our lifetime will happen regardless of change of plans and other circumstances that may arise.” Trinidad looks to his watch, then over his shoulder noticing that anyone present at the airport prior to this anomalous meeting has since vanished, or at least found somewhere better to be. Breaking his grasp with the stranger Trinidad continues walking towards the baggage claim mumbling incoherently under his breath, “this can’t be happening, this can’t be happening, this can’t be happening.” As A.J. peeks back over his shoulder, he notices that the man too has disappeared. Turning his head back to face front, he takes a deep breath as he continues walking but again stops in his track, this time by his own accord. The stranger is once again standing immediately in front of him and wearing a menacing smile on his visage. “What the hell do you want from me!” A.J. yells to the man standing calmly in front of him. “I desire nothing of you young Trinidad, I am here to assist you.” The stranger serenely replies. “I have much to tell you about your upcoming matches in PCW as well as your overall future in PCW, but we have a schedule to keep so I suggest you take your bags and we maintain a swift pace.” Trinidad looks up to the stranger who is now holding his bags and rubs his eyes in disbelief before reaching out and forcefully yanking them away from the man. “Why would I trust you?” Asks Trinidad in a continually calming voice, “I mean, I have just met you and now out of the blue you just expect me to walk off a bridge with you?” The stranger prompts A.J. to begin walking along with him to the doors leading to the outdoors, then opening the door and inviting A.J. through he begins to explain. “I know you must be hesitant to follow, and that is understandable, but I carry with me a very important message for you. A message from St. Jon.” A.J. immediately halts halfway through the door, “St. Jon? How do you know of St. Jon?” “There are many things I know young Trinidad,” The stranger begins. “When the time is right, you too will know just why I know what I know and what relevance it bears to you and your future. For the time being can we please just get in the cab and get the hotel. Time is of the essence.” Bewildered by the man’s knowledge of his former trainer and mentor from back in Argentina some 8 years prior A.J. mindlessly opens the door of a taxi cab allowing the stranger in first, then sits himself in the back seat next to the stranger. “Where ya headed?” the cab driver asks to the back seat. “We’re going to the Kabul Komfort In,” Trinidad replies still half in shock. “We?” the cabby questions, “I only see one of you.” Disregarding the cab drivers last comment for a minute out of pure mystification, Trinidad just nods and hands the man a wad of rolled up bills, “Yeah, we.” The taxi cab pulls off into the night as the camera fades out showing a more broad picture of the city of Kabul before losing the taxi cab in the evening commerce of traffic below.
