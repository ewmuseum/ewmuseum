Los Hermanos Attack (Part 1)

26 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/26/02 Adios Al Ghetto, Hola A La Ciudad I walk down the streets of a city that was once a beautiful work of art. It used to have such history, such beauty, and now, well now it’s just a slum. Gangs and corrupt families have taken the city over. Gunshots are my alarm clock. But now I leave this place. I’ve waited for this day to come, and now it has. You see fighting is what I do for a living, but I do it for the money. The money that allows me to step up from a place like this and lead my life into a more peaceful and serene place. Barcelona is split into two sections, there’s the West side, and the Upper East Side. The Upper East Side has always been the predominantly European Spaniards, who were well to do and have good jobs. The west side was a mix of the Spanish from South America and the poorer European Spaniards. When I was little I would walk to the edge of the West side and stare at the big houses in the East side, wishing I could one day own one, but now I finally have the chance to do it. I was at Alliances, I did my job and helped my team, so now I get the rightful pay I deserve. It’s a sunny day, and the cool fall breeze is finally settling in. The “For Sale” sign in the front is finally gone, for I have bought the house I used to look at every day when I was a young child. It isn’t so much the house that makes me happy; it’s the fact that I’ve reached a point in my life I’ve always wanted to reach. Well here you go Mr. Borro. All you have to do is sign some of these papers and the house is all yours. We’ve set up a team of movers who are exporting all of your possessions to this house as we speak. Thank You, I’ll be finished signing these in no time. You see to get the house legally; I had to use a real name. And since there was none I knew of I used Borro, seeing as Juan’s father was my legal father making Borro my last name. I’d just sign first initial R. Borro. I signed the papers and handed it over to the more than kind woman selling me the house. I hope you’re pleased with your new home. You wouldn’t believe how pleased. That night I packed all of my possessions into my new house. I looked around the house and was nothing but pleased. That night, I turned on my new fireplace and sat there alone looking at poem’s I’ve written through my life. I looked through every single one of them, and they all had the same dark and moody tone to them. I never had anything happy to write about it seemed. Maybe it was because nothing made me happy throughout my whole life. It was just a bunch of fill in for miserable years; God put me on this earth for. They all began the same way. This dark light is haunting me… Sadness made a home in my soul… A life that’s not worth living… As I looked back on these poems I noticed how young and not open to the world I was. I was just keeping my self-locked up so I couldn’t see all the wonderful and majestic parts to life, but now I see them. Why did I attract so many negative feelings before? All I know is I hope negativity has finally left me, for I can’t take another travesty to occur in my life. I laid back my head into the warm and soft carpet. I let my guard down and felt grateful for everything I’ve ever had, whether good or bad. I soon learned, I should have never left my guard down. Later that night I went up to my new balcony near my master bedroom and sat there looking up at the stars. Everything seemed so simple. This was the way I was supposed to live all along, why did it take this long for me to get there. My life had finally gotten to a good point that was until faith came into play. You see I wasn’t meant to have the peaceful and loving life I desire, or at least the way things seemed to turn out I didn’t. As I stood there with a smile on my face, I heard the engine of a powerful car from a mile away. It was inching closer, so I peeked over the balcony to see what was going on. I then saw a red and black mustang from the West Side drive in and stop in front of my house. Rich son of a bitches gonna get what they deserve, Hermanos style. The hoodlum then threw a lit cigarette in the tree on my front lawn. The tree engulfed in fire and I couldn’t believe it. This haunting hatred of the past was following me wherever I went. The anger rushed through me and my body was uncontrollable. I stood there with a straight face not moving a muscle. I looked into the tree on fire and it reflected off my eyes in a satanic manner. My fingers became numb and my knuckles were ready to feel blood. Let’s get the fuck out of here esse, these rich bitches will call the police on us. The two men then got back in their car and raced off, but I wasn’t about to let this go unpunished. I wanted justice and I was going to get it. I ran downstairs and grabbed my coat. I then left the house and jumped in my Cadillac chasing the car with the two hoodlums. I don’t know what was driving me, but it was something beyond what I’ve ever felt before. I got my hopes so high, just to be crushed by these little bastards. They were going to pay dearly, and I made sure of it. They didn’t realize I was following them of course, but I never took my eyes off of their car for one second. They finally arrived at there poor excuse for a house and they went inside. I parked across the street and nonchalantly walked up to their front door. *Knock* They quickly responded to the door in the negative manner I expected them to respond in. I could barely hold the anger inside me. What the hell you want with me? Do you know who I am? Mother fucka, I don’t care who you are, do you know whom your talking to here? I’m talking to a young and obviously stupid young boy. What’d you just call me? I’m a fucking Loc Hermanos brother. Do you know what that means? It better mean your going to pay me for that tree you set on fire in MY property. You are one crazy motherfucker. The gang member then takes out a knife and draws it closer to Romantico and near his neck. Do you wanna die? I finally released my anger as I uppercut the gang member in the nose, took the knife out of his hand, and stabbed him in the foot. Ah Dios Mio my foot, you fucker. I didn’t stop there as I bashed his head into my knee three times, drawing a large quantity of blood. The other gang member in the house then came out and saw his friend lying in a pool of blood. What the fuck! He then drew a gun from his pants, but before he fired I jumped down and grabbed the knife from the other mans hand and threw it at the other man right before he was about to shoot, hitting him in the thigh. He dropped the gun and I charged him bashing his body into the wooden house. I then grabbed his gun and whipped him across the face. My anger has finally worn off as I stared at the two bloodied men, hoping they’ve learned their lesson. I then got in my car that night and called the fire department. They set out the fire and when I arrived in the house I received a phone call. To whom am I speaking with? Los Hermanos are after YOU! *Hang up* TO BE CONTINUED
