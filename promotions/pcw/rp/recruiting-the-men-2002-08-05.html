Recruiting the men

5 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5th of August, 2002 “BANANA’SH! GET YOUR FRESH BANANA’SH HERE, FOLKSH!!” (The gruff voice screeches out over the bustling streets of Cairo and merges silently with other similar chants. Pharaoh looks over towards the man, eyes stern and firm. The street was mainly made up of cobblestone flooring and tall yellowy buildings that formed a simplistic skyline across the morning sky. It’d been a long walk into the city of Cairo but they’d got there eventually and had even booked themselves to stay in the Cairo Plaza Hotel only a few blocks away… Pharaoh walks steadily towards the hunched man and smiles as he finally arrives at the feet of his fruit stall.) “Yesh, folksh, how can I help you?” The man speaks with a lisp and beams a wide smile as the two Egyptian’s look over his array of rich and lush fruits. At length Pharaoh stares up from the apple in his hand “I’m looking for a man who may be able to help me with something.” “Oh?” The grubby little person grins wildly. (Pharaoh nods, a sly smile beginning to form on his face.) “And what might you be in need of help with, shir?” (Titu’s face lights up. With his eyebrow raised he leans forwards, pulls the man’s head towards his and utters: “I need to find the best warriors Egypt has to offer!”) “Well, shir…that could be tough. Egypt ishn’t renowned for itsh brilliant army.” (Pharaoh’s brow wrinkles as he pushes the man away from him.) “Bah!” he sighs. “Egypt was once such a rich and fruitful country! What the Duat has happened here?!” (Helpfully, the man leans forward, raising a finger at Pharaoh’s chest.) “Well the taxesh have been terrible lately, shir.” He sighs, shaking his head in disgust. (Pharaoh looks at him with a concerned expression about his face.) “And why are you telling me?” He says carefully. (The man folds his arms and teeters his head to the side.) “Well you are our prime-minishter, milord… Sho I thought I’d drop a hint…ya’ know?” (Pharaoh’s face twists in disgust.) “I am not your prime-minister, you fool! Why, I am Titu Xansiv, a well known celebrity here in Egypt!” (The man leans against his stall, rubbing his chin in thought. His eyes meet Pharaoh’s as he studies Xansiv’s face.) “Well I thought I recognized you and looking at the way you’re dreshshed I figured you were our ruler, ya’ know?” (Pharaoh shakes his head to and fro.) “Well you were greatly mistaken, my friend. But tell me…where can I find the best warriors in Egypt.” “Well,” the man begins, as though reminiscing over past events. “There is the old ‘Shphinx’s Head’ down in the shuburbsh, and of course just around the corner there ish that new pub…the…the,” the man strokes his chin again. “Oh yeah! That’sh right, it’s called ‘The Pyramid’sh Vantage Point’… Nice place, only been once myself…but anyway you should find some lone-sharksh down there, Mr!” (Pharaoh nods before removing a large brown leather bag from the mysterious recesses of his fabled loincloth. As the bag jingles towards the fruit stall owner a thought crosses the man’s mind… ‘Who’d of thought I’d get a bag full of gold for shimply telling a shtranger where to find shome bounty-huntersh and shuch?!’) “How much are those apples, my good man?” (The stall owner sags in disappointment.) “Twenty piecesh of gold…each…” he sighs glumly. (Titu’s eyes widen in dismay.) “Twenty?! Good Gods, man! You really expect me to pay--…” “Milord, that’sh how much they are, sho…” “Look I’ll haggle with you? Ok?” (The man drops his head in shame.) “You know…ever shince Monty Python’sh life of Brian was releashed ush shtall ownersh have been having a hell of a time trying to get people to realize that we don’t haggle?! They’re twenty piecesh of gold or nothing!” He bellows. (Pharaoh lets out a deep sigh and produces forty pieces of gold from the bag.” “I’ll take two, then…” (The man smiles smugly and takes the gold from Titu’s hand with a mumble of delight in his sale. Isiy grabs the two apples from the rest of the lush fruit on display as the couple set off back down the crowded street.) “So which one of those places are we going to go to?” says the young Egyptian beauty. (Pharaoh doesn’t speak for a moment and simply continues his journey down the cobblestone ahead.) “Well,” he says at last. “This ‘Sphinx’s Head sounds like a place where the ‘regulars’ might hang out. Perhaps we’ll find some experienced warriors there?” (Isiy looks on down the road.) “I have a bad feeling about this…” she sighs. Later that evening… “So I said to him: ‘Why don’t you just go an’ buy the other one? It’s in much better quality and to be honest the one you’ve got there is poor compared to the craftsmanship you can get on that one…’” mumbles the alcohol fuelled voice of Billy Crysp, Cairo’s most prominent lone-shark. “So what did he say?” asks Arnie Stubborn, a well-known trader in downtown Cairo, and one of Billy’s buddies. “Well,” begins the lone-shark in a low voice. “He didn’t have a chance, see. ‘Cos that was my diversion, wasn’t it?! He turned around for a second and then I took of his head with my machete! And that was the end of that!” he smiles proudly. (The group of beer-guzzling men sat around the table relaxingly fell back into their chairs as Billy finished another of his brilliant stories, a short cheer going up as they returned to their drinks. The tales had become the main attraction in The Sphinx’s Head over the years and everyone loved them, even the older one’s that had been told at least thirty six times in the last two months alone never seemed to lose their appeal.) “So, Billy,” exclaims Arnie. “Tell us about the time you and Freddy Longbread took on those Argentine bastards and you--…” (Arnie is cut off in mid sentence when suddenly the door of The Sphinx’s Head creaks open and in walks a tall dark stranger - a man unknown to the regulars - and a beautiful young vixen just a foot shorter than her accompaniment. The two move uncertainly through the crowds of staring people.) “Excuse me but I’m looking for the man in charge around here…” he says at length, trying to look away from the gawking eyes of the tavern’s regulars. (The landlord stands thoughtfully and then realizes that the tone in which the man spoke would immediately rule him out as the man who the stranger wished to see, so he inconspicuously sits down again and sags on his stool. Triumphantly Billy stands, his chest sticking out and his face stern and proud. Slowly he walks up to the man, towering over the stranger by at least a foot and a half.) “I’d be the one you’re looking for, stranger!” he says gruffly. (Pharaoh smiles.) “Good... I wonder, though…are you an experienced fighter?” (Billy laughs boomingly, turning to the other men in the bar with humoured eyes. They return his chuckle with one of their own as Billy turns back to Titu and giggles.) “My good man, do you not realize who I am?! Why, I’m Billy Crysp! Egypt’s greatest and best lone-shark!” the monster booms a long and intimidating laugh. “ Now tell me…what exactly do you want with me?” (Pharaoh strictly looks up into the fierce eyes of the towering fellow in front of him. He was perfect…one in a kind. He looked, sounded and smelt just like the kind of person he needed to lead his battalion - and he was a lone shark too, which was an advantage in its own right.) “I need help, Billy…” (Crysp laughs in the same booming way he always did. Pharaoh doesn’t seem to see what’s so funny and coughs lightly in a vain attempt to catch the monster’s attention. Billy looks down at him firmly.) “Have you ever heard of a man named Francisco Cassius Valerius?” asks The Pharaoh. (Billy’s face wrinkles in thought. He’d heard that name before but couldn’t quite but his finger on it…) “The Argentine diplomat?” he says at length. “Quite so.” Titu sighs. “Yeah,” Crysp begins. “I know him, only by name of course, but I do hear he hates us Egyptians and he’s got quite the force to back up whatever he says. Isn’t he running for president of Argentina soon?” (Pharaoh nods.) “I’m afraid I have my disagreements with him and unfortunately he has an army – as you say - of personal goons to carry out whatever he wants. I fear that I’m next in line to be executed as such…and I need help…fast…” (Billy breathes heavily for a moment, staring methodically in Titu’s direction all the while.) “Look if you’ve got the money then I might be able to help you out,” he says at length. “I’m guessing you’ll need a few more men than just me, though… right?” (Pharaoh breathes a deep sigh of relief and nods.) “Yeah, and I have the money too… We’ll settle the exact amount later on if that’s Ok with you. The only thing that matters is that I know I can trust you,” Pharaoh looks on hopefully. “Can I have that trust?” (Bill cracks his knuckles.) “I hate those damned Argentines as much as any of us Egyptians. And since you’re my new boss I think you can trust me, Mr?” (Billy waves his hands in the air as though shaping dough that isn’t there and never was.) “It’s Xansiv. Mr Xansiv…” Pharaoh informs. “Ok, Mr Xansiv, you’ve got yourself a deal… as long as you keep to your side of the bargain and pay up.” (Pharaoh nods in agreement.) “And I’ll pay up as long as you help me…” He replies with a pearly-white smile. (Billy grins smugly.) “You can trust me, Mr Xansiv.” (Pharaoh nods thoughtfully and begins to leave for the Sphinx’s Head’s door.) “I’ll meet you in the town square later this evening, my Friend. I trust you’ll be there at twelve sharp?” He doesn’t even turn to see Crysp’s reaction. “Of course.” Says the lone-shark. (Pharaoh and Isiy leave the pub headed for the Cairo Plaza Hotel as Billy Crysp returns to his pint of bitter at the table.) “So what’s gonna’ happen, Billy?” Asks Arnie Stubborn with a look of concern about his face. (Ignoring Arnie’s question Billy stands and says:) “Who fancies going on an adventure?” (This one, however, would be his toughest yet…) Even later that evening… (Twelve O’clock… The bell in the clock tower in the town square signalled it as though it had never been this time before – as though the world had never seen twelve O’clock in its long and complex past. The streets of Cairo were silent now. All the market stalls were closed and their owners had long gone home. In the more popular areas of Cairo, however, party’s raged on throughout the night as young women and handsome men met in clubs… Pharaoh looks down at his watch.) “They should be here soon.” “I thought you said to them to be here at twelve sharp?” says Isiy, looking at her nails. “I did, my love - and I trust they’ll be here. And besides the bell hasn’t even struck twelve yet.” (Pharaoh looks down one of the countless streets leading off into the darkness ahead.) “They’ll be here,” he says uncertainly. “Honestly…” (Ten long and strenuous minutes pass before Isiy talks again, an amount of sarcasm present in her voice.) “Oh yeah, they’re oh so reliable,” She groans. “Cummon, I’m going.” (Isiy looks sideways at the hopeful face next to hers and sighs.) “We’ll just have to find help somewhere--…” (Suddenly the sound of marching echoes through the alleyways. Pharaoh looks down smugly at Isiy’s startled face.) “Told ya’ they’d come!” (Before long the face of Billy Crysp comes into view and behind him a battalion of twenty men follow loyally.) “What the hell?!” Says Isiy, looking the men up and down. (Her initial reaction to the men in front of her is just. None of them actually look much like fighters and many of them just appear to have come along for the ride.) “What do you think, then?!” says Billy, a smug grin spread over his face. (Isiy is about to speak her mind when Pharaoh nudges her in the ribs.) “Owe!” she moans, rubbing her side. “They look…great…yeah…great, just great!” smiles Titu, trying to cover up his actual thoughts on the people in front of him. (One of them men raises a hand) “Err excuse me…” (Pharaoh looks over at him.) “Yes, my friend?” “Erm, are we gonna’ get benefits from this?” (Pharaoh sighs.) “I’m afraid not.” (The small man frowns.) “Fuck this then.” He shouts, walking away. (Billy winces.) “You’ll have to excuse him, sir. He’s quite awkward when it comes to that sort of thing…” (Pharaoh’s face twists in thought.) “Is there any way to get him back?” “Not unless you’re offering benefits, sir.” (Pharaoh shakes his head in disgust.) “Ok,” he starts. “Has anyone else got a question?” (A tall man raises his arm.) “Yes, you!” “Will we be done by tomorrow morning? The wife doesn’t know I’m out and she’ll--…” “I’ll tell you what, shall I? Why don’t you just go home, huh?! This job is going to take a long time to complete! Months to complete perhaps! And if you impress me I may employ you for a further time afterwards! So get your frickin’ minds sorted out on what you’re gonna’ do here or leave…OK!” (Billy smiles. He could see that Pharaoh was of leader material from the moment he saw him and he’d just been backed up on his claim. After a moment or two a few men walk away from the team leaving sixteen of the Egyptians – excluding Billy - left.) “Ok, now that we’re over the complications I’ll inform the rest of you of our mission.” (And so Pharaoh told the others of Frisco and his army of Argentinean soldiers. The men listened hard and took in mostly everything their new leader told them – they tended to ignore the bits about obese coach drivers and rude hippy’s - and as Pharaoh finished his speech a small cheer went up, as though Titu was been praised like a god.) “So are you with me?” he shouts. “Yeah!!” They respond in union. “Then our mission begins! But now, my friends, we must all travel back to America where I am scheduled to face off against our enemy in the squared circle!” (A mighty cheer goes up for Xansiv as he begins to walk away down the cobblestone street.) “Err, sir?” asks one of the sixteen soldiers. (Pharaoh turns with a smile.) “Yeah?” “Do we have to pay for our own flight tickets?” (Pharaoh sighs.) “No.” he says wearily. (The man turns back to his comrades.) “He says no, fellahs!!” (Another cheer goes up as the scene begins to fade to black on the smiling face of Titu Xansiv…)
