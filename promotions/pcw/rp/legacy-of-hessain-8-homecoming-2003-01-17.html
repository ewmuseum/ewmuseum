Legacy Of Hessain 8 - Homecoming

17 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 17/01/03 Recap: After defeating the master of the mind, Skitzrapto, Hessain has restored The Pillar Of The Mind, as part of his pledge to Aimziel. When all 9 pillars are restored, Aimziel promises peace for the vampire Hessain. The next target is Magnumek, the protector of the corrupted circle that is bonded to the Pillars. He exists in an icy fortress to the north, past Hessain's very own hometown... * * * Legacy Of Hessain Part 8: Homecoming I had been trudging these roads for too long... only the wolves that fell to my sword were my company, as well as the occasional deluded bandit that dared cross me. Bandits were a common problem along this road, being the path to my birthplace - high Coorhagen. Second only to Willendorf in stature, Coorhagen was a city rich in splendour and were it a person, it would be represented by myself in my former existence - a loyal knight, wealthy and proud. Yet as I approached the outskirts of the place, my heart - if mine still beats, that is - froze. The irony of it all was almost too much. If I had indeed been a representative of the city in my former life, then I was so now. There had been whispers of a plague that had stained a few towns to the far east of here, but never could I imagined my dear Coorhagen falling to such filth. In the spots where their hearts once beat, the dead lay with their faces covered in a green slime. It wept from their eyes like diseased tearsm clogging their mouths and noses. Breathing would have been an impossibility with this horrific deathly substance infecting someone. I also took a further blow when I sliced open a vein to feed from these corpses - the blood that would have nourished me was nowhere to be found. Instead, I found only the vile liquid. Worms and maggots feasted upon the rotten skin and flesh. A pity that even the lowest of these animals would be able to have a banquet on this festering cadaver, but I would have to find an alternate food source. I turned past an empty and cold Blacksmiths, only to find that a mountain of heaped empty husks were in my way. Children, ladies, knights, even the few beggars that had lost their fortunes were mingled in the wall of disease. Whether this was a random dumping place for the bodies or a wall made strategically to divert and detour any survivors, my path was blocked. I would need to find an alternate route. Suddenly, I hard footsteps behind me. I reached inside myself for the power that lay dormant and once more felt my body change into the disguise that I had stolen from the gyspsies. My appearance changed to that of a peasant, just in time as a quartet of people in thick, black cloaks emerged from a house. Their faces bore odd masks that resembled the beak of a crow. I was unsure if they were part of some cult that brought the plague or otherwise... until, that is, one offered me the same mask and robe. 'Away from the damned, good man, lest you be as they lie! Here, wear these robes, they bear the scents of the flowers and oils that keep the plague out!' one of them commanded, leaving me no option and draping the garment over my head. The mask was heavy and the beak was bent downwards, containing a strange liquid that smelled sweet. Nevertheless, it also provided cover for me and I could drop my magical disguise, saving me the magical energy that I need to empower my spells and forms. When I had dressed as they did, they ushered me into the house they had come from, closing the doors behind them tightly and bolting them shut. Security, or paranoia, was a main factor among these people. My Coorhagen had become a very changed place. The house was stripped of all decoration. Only a trap door that led into the cellar prevented this room, indeed this house from being as empty as those lifeless husks outside. The four rescuers went first, beckoning me as they went. As I followed them, I was amazed. The cellar led into some underground sanctuary. Tunnels streamed out from all directions like the veins and arteries of the body, a myriad of man-made caves and passages. These must have been the rumoured tunnels of Coorhagen. Their purposes were long forgotten but they now seemed to be a place to harbour the survivors of Coorhagen's plague. Perhaps I may even see some old faces among these people if their masks were ever removed. 'What brings you to Coorhagen, traveler?' Asked one of the crowd. Even my ears could not pick out which of the heavily-clad people said it. 'I seek a path to the bastion of Magnumek' I replied. My response must have triggered something inside them. The tones in their voices suddenly became hostile and like stone. 'What business have you with the Serafan Lord?' One of them asked. 'I seek the murderer's heart!' I cried. It was a good thing that I had already cancelled out my own spell of disguise and that these gigantic robes were masking my identity, as this act of rage would have surely shown my true face. Their suspicion was raised regardless, it seemed, as one of the crowd shook their heads. 'You'll have difficulty doing that... HESSAIN...' Curses. They had somehow seen past both my magic and my robes. 'Magnumek sent you? It doesn't matter, I shall send you the same way as this town - to the Underworld!' I yelled, discarding the robes that offered no protection from the eyes of the surviving townspeople nor the plague that had no bite. 'It makes no difference what you do to us, vampire. Magnumek has no heart to speak of... and now, nor do we.' the main speaker said, stepping forward with a melancholy sigh. Removing his mask and robes, he showed himself to be like Magnumek, a spiritual being clad in physical armour. His face was obscured by shadows and his helmet, now merely a shade. 'Keep your distance, wraith! Your lack of body does not mean you lack a soul that can be shattered!' I bellowed, raising my blade. The ghostly being shook his head once more and began to explain. 'You are mistaken, Hessain. We owe the master our lives. When we were victimised by the plague, thousands of lives were taken. Coorhagen took but one week to turn from a thriving city into a lifeless corpse. That is when Magnumek sent a messenger with an offer. If we would give him our servitude, he would take the souls from our weak, fragile bodies and bind them to immortal vessels. The plague no longer affected us. In return, all we had to do was bring in each new traveler or surivor and convince them to join us, or cast them into the death-filled streets. However, the master also said that we were to expect one eager to meet with him. It would seem that we have found or quarry...' I paused for a moment. I had no choice but to admire the cunning of this Magnumek. It was death either way for the people of Coorhagen - one would be as a rotting carcass, the other as an immortal slave of an iron terror. Magnumek not only held the life of the Pillar Of Conflict in his gauntlets, but also had a personal grudge now dur to enslaving my hometown. 'Very well, former kinsmen and fellows - I shall redeem your souls and set them free in the name of Coorhagen. VAE VICTUS!'
