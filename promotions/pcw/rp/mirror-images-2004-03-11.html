Date: 03/11/2004 PRELUDE: NOCTURNE IIAlone in the far cornerOf a world of dreams.I listen to the plaintive whisperOf sweet memories with wings,Clipped with time flutteringA sedate, soothing rhythmThrough this cool flesh sliding,So namelessly familiarOver this landscape of looseTranslation. She kisses me,Slowly forces herself through me;My trembling assembles a tacit myth:This is of significance.We move in lasting time,Resisting a sense of desperate secrecy.Then, somewhere far below us,The strangers, the rest of them,Begin to stir. I need not askShe stay until I forget.And we are laughing,The day has come too soonAnd we are sobbing.This is a stranger's clutch;so little time to learn so much,As if no other night could bringSuch a futile, fleeting touch.And because she sighs,And because she sings…As she becomes my pain,I must steal the night again.But I know, inside my black heart,If I try and feel her soul once more,I’ll only reach for empty dreams,And realize…That she was never there at all. SCENEDead, decaying leaves whip up around an otherwise silent street corner of Nashville, Tennessee. The city, usually bustling with life, is somewhat desolate against the frosting chill of the night. The city mirrors that of a ghost town, as all signs of life seem to have been vanquished by the evils that lurk beyond the shadows, waiting to pounce on the weak and the innocent. Old Man Winter’s dying grasp has the Volunteer State’s heart clinched in a desperate struggle between light and darkness, one which it can never recover from. With nothing more than his whims and hopes riding on the breeze beside him, the austere Dom DeMerius walks along the street, not bothering to sidestep the piles of dead leaves and trash that has magically appeared on the streets of the city out of nowhere, as if the demons had intentionally left their playground a mess for the rest of the world to fix. With his hair slicked back and his pair of sunglasses tucked safely into the breast pocket of his blue sports jacket, DeMerius walks with an intent purpose to let everything go on this blistering cold night. His hand aimlessly drops down to his right hip, out of instinct, faintly reaching for the switchblade he would carry with himself back home in Canarsie, and wondering faintly why it wasn’t there anymore. The lone light on the barren avenue radiates up from a hole in the wall type of joint, the Mirror Image Sports Club. With little to do between now and his appointment to cripple Joe Mac, DeMerius begins to walk cautiously to the establishment, letting his hand rest on a wad of $100 bills in his right pocket, wishing he’d had the foresight to go in packing something a little more powerful than the Swiss blade he had tucked below. Despite being out of Canarsie for nearly a decade, he still couldn’t shake the feeling that bars were bad places to be… alone. DeMerius: What a joint, must’ve been a femmina reale to get this piece of shit off the ground. What the Hell am I saying… femmina reale, bitch? What the Hell is wrong with me tonight? Dom slowly opens the door inward, cringing a bit as the rusty slab squeals and creaks on its rusty hinges. He takes a quick glance around the joint, and gets enough of an idea about his new environment to do a double take of the setting. A lone bartender stands across the way, aimlessly drying out dusty shot glasses, while a few old television sets struggle with themselves, trying to decide whether to allow the Virginia/Clemson game continue airing, or to air the static race that seemed to be winning out. The bar was disturbingly empty to be a Thursday night, with not a single soul apparently choosing to lose him or herself in a stupor of unimaginable proportions. DeMerius quietly moves down the empty bar, eyeing the one seat that hadn’t been ravaged by a couple of drunken fools, and immediately tapped his fingers on the bar, startling the errant bartender. The old man, dressed like something out of a Goodfellas sequel, adjusts his thin-rimmed glasses and gingerly limps over to DeMerius’ position at the bar.Bartender: What’ll ya have, stranger? DeMerius: Whatever you’ve got. Budweiser, Coors Light, Sam Adams, doesn’t matter. I don’t give a damn, frankly, and neither should you! Bartender: Sorry, I meant no offense. I haven’t seen you around here before… you new in town, or are ya visitn’? DeMerius: I guess you can say I’m on business. A very macabre business that you probably don’t want to know about. Not that I’d give you the time of day to discuss such matters, but eh, it’s what a guy’s gotta do to make some dough, capeesh? Bartender: Ah, you must be that Yankee fellow, DeMerius aren’t ya? Yeah, I heard about your group being in town. Damn fine wrestling organization, if you don’t mind me telling ya. I’ve been following the business for awhile now, well before my days in WW II. Yes sir, I was following the sport back when Lou Thesz and George Wagner were lords of the ring. Tell me, son, have you ever met Thesz? I heard he was quite the cona… um, what’s that word that means your good at something, son? DeMerius: Connoisseur, Thesz is dead, and no, I won’t discuss my misadventures in this damn business. Now would you please get me a drink, before I shove my foot so far up your ass, you’ll have to peal it out with a bottle opener! Bartender: Alright, alright. Kids these days, the whole lot of ‘em, bunch of ungrateful little punks... The barkeep reaches across the counter, grabbing the one clean glass left sitting there. He starts to fill the glass with an unknown brand, almost painfully slow at that while DeMerius tries his best not to climb over the bar and mutilate the old bastard. If there was one thing he hated more, it was having to disfigure stupid people before they could be of any use to him. Without another word, the bartender slides the glass carelessly towards Dom, who scoops it off the dusty bar with little inhibition. He quietly sets a c-note on the bar, pushing it towards the brother of Moses without really caring about petty things like change. DeMerius: A token of my appreciation for not getting yourself hurt. Now don’t bother me again, or I’ll stuff a few more bills into your widow’s pocket at your funeral. Dom cautiously takes a swig from the glass, relieved to find something similar, if a bit stale, to Budweiser in his hand. He sits in complete silence, lost in the low hiss of the broken television set mounted onto the wall. So much so, in fact, that he doesn’t quite realize where he is until the TV slowly flickers… and then cuts itself off, immune to the world around it. The sudden cease of sound is enough to draw his attention away from the chiseled, carved up bar long enough to notice that he’s completely by himself now. The bar’s lights dim to an almost trembling pitch, as DeMerius quickly begins to realize that he might have set himself up for a trap. His hand absently makes a move for his pathetic army knife… but he stops, frozen in a perilous mix of both fear and anxiety at what lurks directly behind him. Without a breath escaping his body, DeMerius can only sit petrified as a cold, icy wave of fear rolls down his spine like Lucifer himself. Only one person in the entire world could make him tremble in fear, and he’d been dead since 1995. Without even having to look beside him to acknowledge his presence, Dom’s long-since murdered father sits down beside him, his attire eerily similar to Dom’s own threads. DeMerius: Father… Father: Son… For a brief second, all Dom can do is try not to lose his composure like he’d promised himself he never would. Despite all his shortcomings as a human being, he’d never once mourned the death of his parents, but his anger had been there, and it’d kept him warm. But now, for some reason, with his father sitting right beside him, all Dom could do was sit there in an awed silence, waiting for the withering bartender to wake him up from his somber nightmare. DeMerius: You died? I had to bury you… You fell? Father: I rose, Dominic. I’ve always been with you, Dom, whether you’ve realized it or not. You still need me to be your guide, son. More than you’ll ever know.DeMerius: I’m… I can’t find my calling. With you and mom, I couldn’t quite understand what the world had planned for me. I guess I do now, don’t I, Pop? Father: You’re here to make us all proud, Dominic. You’ve got your family’s reputation to fight for, each time you step into that ring. Your mother and I were both very proud of you when you stood up for our family, and volunteered to sacrifice your early years to help us stay afloat. I know my demands on you weren’t logical at the time, but can you see now why I was so tough on you before? You understand now why you have to be remorseless and cold-hearted, don’t you? DeMerius: I… I don’t know, father. I can’t shake the feeling that I threw something special away for nothing but bloodlust and anger. I can’t, just can’t get the notion out of my head that I was taught wrong. Father: You were taught with the best we could manage, Dominic! Life isn’t meant to be a bowl of cherries for everyone. You think Ethan Richards had to put up with your problems, your downsides? Never! He was a rich boy that had everything handed to him on a silver platter. Just like that punk, Joe Mac! He’s a tight ass, he’s nothing but a joke to your sport! DeMerius: But father… Father: No more of your excuses! I will not allow you to wreck our family’s proud heritage! You’re a disgrace to your mother and I, and I wont allow you to dishonor us any further! DeMerius: Yes, father. I’m sorry. Father: Dominic! Dom, my boy… don’t worry about it, son, you’ve done the best you could under the circumstances. I didn’t make it easy for you, and it’s gotten you this far as a result. You should take some solace in what you’ve done in your lifetime. You’ve had a great career, son, one that you can look back on in your dying days with some degree of respect for. DeMerius: I still have fights left to wage, father. You speak as if I’m done with wrestling! What a crazy notion that is. Father: Not another word of this wrestling nonsense! I’ve put up with this long enough, and I wont stand by and watch you make a fool of yourself here in this PCW organization! You’ve lost once too often, and now you’ll have to suffer the consequences! It’s time you went back to Canarsie and paid for your sins! DeMerius: I don’t know… I don’t… No. Father: What did you say? DeMerius: N- No. Father: Dominic? DeMerius: No. No. Father: Son, what are you- DeMerius: No. No. No. NO! You son of a bitch, no! This is it, you worthless imbecile! I am sick and tired of having to lug around the guilt of watching you die at my feet any longer! I am a fighter, nothing more, nothing less! I will not live to see the day when kids come in my thrift store, snickering to themselves that I fell before I could cripple Joe Mac! I wont allow it! I have to make him bleed, father, I have to make him suffer! It’s what I do, goddamnit! And not you, or Richards, or Roberts, or anyone can tell me different! I will kill my dreams for you! My dreams are going to kill me, that’s it! I’m so sick of trying to love my parents, that I’m almost ready to give you up now! You ruined me, father, so now it’s time for me to ruin you! I… I hate you, I hate you so fucking much, it nearly breaks me down. I wanted to mourn, I did, but I couldn’t stop wishing that I’d been able to watch your pathetic plea for help just before darkness stole your hopes! All I can do is lace my boots each time out, and pray that I can make them beg for mercy just like I wanted you to. They’re my scapegoats, my tools to release my frustration that you’ve burdened me with, you son of a bitch. I’ve envisioned every single way that I wanted to hurt you, to hurt our family legacy. I couldn’t give a damn about Joe Mac, but at Turmoil next Tuesday, I’m going to make him scream a bloodthirsty scream that was supposed to be on your tab! Leave, please, before I make you regret the day you ever got that whore of a wife of yours pregnant! Dominic quickly lets out a brief sigh before he stops, dead still, waiting for his father’s retort. His anger begins to build inside of him, such a blind fury that he’d never imagined in his life. Dom’s rage began to boil over to the breaking point, to where he finally couldn’t take it anymore. DeMerius: No, you wont escape from me now, not when I have you here in my grasp. It’s time to make you pay, Joe Mac. Father… Dominic swings wildly to his right, catching nothing but air as he twists to the ground, face first in a layer of dust. He pulls himself up, flailing wildly in a stunning silence, blinded by his rage. No matter how hard he swings, visions of his father dance right in front of him like an elusive dream, brushing past him without a care in the world. He continues to wail away, oblivious to the eyes staring at him. He swings… DeMerius: No, you son of a bitch, no. No. No!!! Dom thrusts himself forward… and promptly feels the crunch of glass splintering into his flesh. The warm, elixir-like blood that had been boiling a minute earlier begins to cool as it trickles down his hand and wrist. Dom manages to grimace at the pain for a moment before he realizes where he is. Out of nowhere, dozens of people have gathered in the club, looking on in stunned amazement at the scene on hand. The old barkeeper had been replaced by a rough looking fellow, the television sets were new and working. It was if he’d been lost in a dream world. Dom looked forward at the mirror he’d punched through… and saw his father staring back at him, wearing the same grimacing frown that Dom suspected he’d been wearing. DeMerius: Your clothes, your hair, your swagger. My voice, my dreams… myself, alone, the whole time. Dom looks down at his bloody hand quickly before looking away, smearing it all over the cracked glass, watching as his father’s reflection faded away in a smear of blood. DeMerius quietly rises, taking in one last look before he quickly makes for the exit, not caring who stopped him or how the poor bastard that tried would die for his trouble. He stumbles out onto the now bustling street, lost in an inferno of noise that starts to send his world spiraling out of control. He drops to his knees, clutching his wrist, trying to reach above him to some lucid ending that didn’t exist. DeMerius: Help me… it won’t end, not before Joe Mac bleeds for me! Let me reach my dreams, for Heaven’s sake, don’t take it away from me now… Dom continues to reach upwards, dimly aware that the darkness was growing around him. The noise began to reach a fever pitch, as he struggles to see what is happening to him. His hand slowly loses it’s stains of blood, instead taking on a white bandage wrap in it’s place. Dom closes his eyes for a second, trying to brace for the end… and jolts wide awake, realizing that he’s reaching up towards the ceiling of his hotel room. The white, faintly stained cloth on his wrist was murky but visible enough in the pale moonlight that was filtering in through the blinded windows. His ears buzz with the strangling noise of his nightmare, but it soon subsides into nothing but utter silence. He has the impression that he’s not alone, and let’s his hand fall beside him, coming to rest atop another person. He looks beside him, noting the buxom young redheaded woman that lies solemnly beside him, like an angel that had been sent to save him from his madness. Dom quickly rises without stirring her, moving towards the door to his hotel room balcony, desperate for some fresh air. He pushes through the door, wincing a bit at the cold chill in the air, and almost turns back in spite of the very same start to his nightmare. DeMerius: No, it wasn’t a nightmare. It was my life, and it’s a life that I’ll have to live with till the end of my days. He was right, just like he always was. Every time I close my eyes, I’m going to see you dancing in my head like a splinter of the mind. But you wont go away, so I’ll just have to find a use for you down the line. Joe Mac, though, is a different matter entirely. You’re like a stain on my shoe that I can’t seem to get rid of, no matter how hard I try. I’ve known you for awhile now, Joe, but I still can’t seem to disconcert my energy to finishing your pathetic life off. I know that when you arrive in Hell, you’ll thank me for showing you the error of your ways, but in order to do that, I’m afraid that I’ll have to martyr you on the grandest stage of them all. I know you can hear me, Joe Mac, just as I can hear you. We might be thousands of miles apart, but I’ll always be watching you from the shadows, somehow. I’m like a nightmare that you can’t stop dreaming about, Joe. I’m that cold breath of fear that sends chills up your spine. And believe you me, Joe, I will do worse things to your spine than make you tremble with it! Oh, your day will come, Joe Mac, your day will come. People like you are the reason I’m still around in this business. It’s nothing personal against you… that’s not entirely true, because it’s people like you that make me want to lose faith in the pathetic shell of humanity that we have left… Dom fishes into his back pocket, aimlessly pulling out a scrap sheet of paper and his old ballpoint pen that had somehow ended up with him at one point. He couldn’t think of anything better to do than to write a letter of regret to Joe Mac’s family… but nothing came to his mind, other than his need, his strong desire to inflict pain on the man. You’ve got to bleed for me, Joe, I hope you understand. If you don’t, it’ s your problem, not mine! I’ve got to feel your bones break by my hands, you pathetic fool! I’ve got to be able to prove to myself that I can take your soul and cripple it beyond repair, just like I say I will. You may think you have a chance to defeat me, but I know more than you’ll ever hope to learn about this game that we play. It is a game, you know. It’s the ultimate game of skill, where one wrong move can cost us everything, Joe Mac. We’re all going to lose some day, but as long as I have a breath in my body, I’m not losing tomorrow. Unlike you, I’m strong! I’m tough! And I can’t wait to get my hands on your skinny little carcass at Turmoil. Ah, the hour grows late, Joe! Time to catch some shuteye, my friend! Have a wonderful Friday in paradise, wherever that might be my friend. Because when you land in Nashville, your hours are officially numbered. You better start praying now, because I promise you, I will never forgive myself if I don’t cripple you for good on Saturday! Enjoy walking, you son of a bitch. Enjoy the moment, because it will be your last… Dom rises from his brooding perch on the balcony, unaware that he’s written anything on the paper. He drops it to the ground, watching it get blown to the end of the ledge before he walks inside. The woman stirs in the bed, as DeMerius only glares down at her, wishing to God that he didn’t have to leave right away. He reached down into his pocket, hoping to fish something worthwhile out… but only came up with a family picture that he’d taken when he was a child. His mother and father were standing in front of their store, a look of sorrow plastered on their faces… Dom quickly turned away from the picture, tearing it to shreds as he walked back out onto the balcony, tossing it into the wind and not looking back. He took one final glance at the note he’d absently written, noting that he’d left a message for everyone to find lying on the balcony before heading back inside, closing the door behind him. Shutting the evils of the night off, leaving them to fend for themselves in an uncaring world… “To whom it may concern: I’m sorry for the things you have to go through, but I don’t regret inflicting them in the least. I’m sure you understand that a part of you will have to die on Saturday. If not, well, life isn’t always fair is it? I hope that you can look past this one day, and understand that you did nothing wrong, nothing to deserve this. Just look back and be thankful that I didn’t kill you when I had the chance. Go in peace, Joe Mac… Sincerely, Dominic V. Demerius”
