The Madness Subsides...

17 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3 - 17 - 03 So March Madness is over. The chaos has subsided and new doors have been opened, others closed finally. The much-hyped PPV has come and gone, but not without tragedy, victory, pain, and pleasure. Like most key points in someone’s life, it’s a two-faced monster that looks in both directions equally. One eye stares down the road of pleasure and victory, its half of the face beaming with wondrous thoughts of truly amazing events to come. The other stares down the beaten path of tragedy and pain, grimacing with disgust and squinting its red iris into the distance, as if it’s ready to burn the contents behind in a fit of raging fire. The monster looks in both directions equally, neither getting an advantage over the other. Thus is life, and thus was March Madness. Dorn knew going into the PPV that there would be set-backs to his grand journey to the ultimate gold, and as he calculated, there truly was. Not so much a number of setbacks, but one critical one. He had done the unthinkable. The unfathomable. The unmistakable act of… Losing. Though to the Astronomer, defeat atop the mountains of greatness was becoming commonplace. The ironic proposition was this: He had lost his chance originally to be in March Madness to one Chris Storm, which dealt a heavy blow to the confidence of the Astronomer. Upon winning the Shooting Star Title, though, he own that chance back. This is my chance to gain the ultimate trophy! Dorn exclaimed within the depths of his own mind, his inner voice ringing with joy. It will be the final testament to Astrology and make the blasphemers BELIEVE that the Astrological religion is indeed great! No such thing occurred. He was flung over the proverbial cliff, plummeting to a concrete death below. Though in body, he was perfectly fine albeit being a little sore, but in mind, he had splattered like a water balloon upon impact. It had crushed his spirit temporarily, and didn’t give him great hopes for the future. Would he be able to guard his Shooting Star Title with the same zest and spirit he had before? Dorn didn’t know, and certainly had faint hopes about it. But why is he taking the defeat so badly? Sitting alone in his cramped, cold arena locker room, Dorn is the now the last one inside the place of battle besides the ring crew and the staff of the venue. Sitting upon an old wooden bench and supporting his battle-weary head upon his clamped hands, Dorn just stares at the blank white wall, as if looking for an answer. The wall merely stares back, void of communication with Dorn through body or mind. Receiving no solution from the wall, Dorn turns his gaze to the side and sighs. ‘Where did I go wrong,’ His voice is heavy with puzzlement and sadness, obviously still disturbed by his untimely defeat at the hands of pawns and blasphemers. ‘I performed the ritual and carried the burning torch of En’Flarim’s will within me, ready to do battle. Yet, I lost…’ It confuses him to no end, for he believes that victory should have come with ease to one such as he. He picks up the Shooting Star Title from beside him and cradles it to his bosom, almost as if ghouls within the room are reaching out with their gnarled, transparent fingers and trying to whisk it away from him. The Astronomer sighs again, his voice as heavy as a 5-foot block of lead, and pulls himself up with unbelievable strain. Looking towards the exit of the locker room, he thinks to himself, Like leaving the med care building during a war… wounded and spirit-broken, it’s time to move on… And with that, Dorn picks up his Shooting Star Title and leaves his temporary arena lodgings. His next destination… San Diego. [End.] So March Madness is over. The chaos has subsided and new doors have been opened, others closed finally.
