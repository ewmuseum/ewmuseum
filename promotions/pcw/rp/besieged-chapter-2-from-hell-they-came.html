Besieged: Chapter 2 – From Hell They Came

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 3 of 2003 A.D. “Hey Vanessa…..sure you don’t wanna take me up on that offer? It still stands……” “I said drop dead, Christian.” Stryker lounged doggedly in his quarters in the Blue Enoch lodge, sipping cautiously at his cup of coffee while surveying the exterior through the aura of dim lamps. Night had fallen on the quiet countryside, and the sounds and signs of fledgling spring were conspicuous by their absence. The wood beyond the lodge property was deathly quiet and still, not even an intermittent breeze to interrupt the ominous calm. A wave of coffee lapped at the brim of the mug, burning his upper lip. He cringed momentarily, wiping the hot liquid away with his forefinger and glancing into the next room where Norton continued to badger Lieutenant Wilder. He chuckled at the mock lover’s quarrel emanating forth through the door; watching Norton’s shameless come-on lines always served to amuse the commander. Stryker failed to notice Slade make an appearance as he slipped into the room from the adjacent hall, his girth eclipsing the flickering light of a candle set across the room. . “No action yet, Marcus,” boomed the towering behemoth. “Quiet as a grave out there.” “I’d much rather it be the inverse,” scoffed Captain Stryker. “I hate to think what could be lurking in the dark right now, among those trees. Those things Bruce worships……they revel in the darkness the same way we survive in light. Anything that takes shadows as refuge like that chills my blood.” “He knows that, captain. Do you really think he’d dare stage anything as long as even a glimmer of sunlight peeked over the mountain? Those followers of his wouldn’t mind, but you know that’s not the bulk of his force.” “Men hold superior numbers for him, lieutenant. But the true strength of his force is in those things he conjures from beyond the world of man. Most of those under his sway in that blasted mansion are neonates to the black arts, hell most of them are still kids searching for identity and purpose. I dunno if any of them are more than 25 years old. And that’s the most despicable thing about it.” Stryker set the cup down, eyeing the steam wafting from the surface and dissipating into the air. Through the steam he gazed into the gentle eyes of his subordinate. “Bruce is criminally insane, but his followers aren’t. It’s unfortunate that he’s gone so mad that he’s stooped to brainwashing confused youths into carrying out his deranged bidding. They don’t receive any gratitude for the crimes they commit on behalf of those wretched beings, there won’t be any reward for them no matter what sort of propaganda he feeds them. There’s no sanity in a man who worships such gods as those he does…..” He trailed off, pausing as a gentle chatter arose from outside. Wilder and Norton also became silent, wandering into the same room as he and Slade while gazing forlornly out the windows. They all sat in silence for nearly a minute, all the while the tone not subsiding. “The whippoorwills…..” Stryker pursed his lips, his hand clutching at the mug of black coffee while he glared uneasily into the darkness beyond the window pane. The song of the whippoorwills wafted throughout the bungalow, their hellish anthem infused with menace. “You’re scaring me, captain,” commented Vanessa. “Why?” “Because you’re trembling. I never see you trembling.” Stryker noticed for the first time the coffee that had spilled over the lip of the cup and onto the flesh of his violently quivering hand. He now became conscious of the burn and eagerly wiped it away as Ryder rose, drawing his sidearm and grunting his disapproval. “Ryder!” exclaimed Marcus. “Any contact with Brayston yet?” “None, captain,” replied the giant as he checked a laptop computer screen. “From what we know, Brayston may be a ghost town by now. They’ve isolated themselves.” “I don’t like this one bit….” sighed Sergeant Norton. Stryker knew of the whippoorwills and their ominous song; the captain was well-versed in the folklore of various regions, as much of it wasn’t the collection of old wives’ tales and ghost stories one would initially believe. Myths and legends had their roots, and some were far more sinister and grounded in truth than was comforting to know. “It’s starting,” he breathed quietly to his subordinates. “What’s starting?” inquired Slade. Marcus could only shake his head, peering longingly into the blackness for something. He preferred the attack to come over the suspense of waiting. It was inevitable anyhow. “I don’t know. But if I know how Bruce and his cult work, they aren’t going to wait until tomorrow when we have numbers. He wants us out of the way tonight. And I hear their battle hymn….” He nodded in acknowledgment of the whippoorwills’ steady song flowing on the now-stirring wind. Wilder, Norton and Slade paused, straining to hear anything the birds’ calling may have been concealing. “Spread out, take up your positions!” ordered the captain. “Lieutenant Slade, watch the surveillance monitors! Sergeant Norton, Lieutenant Sensaki is downstairs, give him the heads-up. Instantly the group spread out to their duties, leaving the commander alone momentarily to go about his own tasks. He grabbed a heavy duffel bag of supplies and, slinging the object over his left shoulder, plodded down the wooden steps with a series of heavy thumps. He crossed swiftly into the sparsely-decorated den and unzipped the bag, drawing various pieces of equipment forth and dropping it onto the table. They had expected a fight. Captain Stryker? My, my……been a while has it not? Stryker could hear the voice and he detected the malice instilled inherently within. “Bruce?” It didn’t have to be this way, Marcus. Believe me…..I didn’t want any of you involved. But to further the greater cause sometimes sacrifices must be made. And the desires of my flesh are insignificant compared to the whims of the masters. “Enough people have been hurt by your blind ambitions, Bruce. We’re here to stop it. And I promise, that we will do.” This is between myself and society. I have no qualms about closing the final curtain on mankind and its perpetual plague of an existence. Humanity’s creation is the only folly of the daemon-sultan’s magnificent feats throughout time. The species has grown weak and it’s time for humankind to go the way of the Dodo bird. Stryker, himself seething at the sound of his one-time accomplice’s words, cast his eyes into the expansive blackness beyond the front stoop. He could faintly spy the crawling forms of various entities that writhed and flailed grotesquely just beyond the range of the tripwires and surveillance cameras. Monstrosities whose quirky movements defined their sheer blasphemousness were poised just beyond the edge of the property, lurking along the edge of the forest. “Cut the shit, old friend,” hissed Stryker audibly from his position in the den. “We all know this goes beyond whatever philosophical trash you spew as a front. This whole crusade is just your own valiant attempt to extract revenge for Carlisle and everyone else you knew who died in Boston. You just happen to have the backing of dark gods who seduced the evil in your soul and made you their bitch.” Stryker slammed a clip into his rifle, his mind echoing with the diabolical cackle of his former ally. The officer could only sneer as the retort came telepathically, baleful and hostile. Pfft, fine. Sure I despise my own species. But who wouldn’t after knowing the things I do? The very knowledge of the cosmos has been opened to me, Marcus. You’re no different than the other peons who have defied me and the Old Ones! You fail to see the big picture. Tragically, you only see what is so graciously laid out for you, and you shut out those things which you claim have driven me mad when you know they are as much reality as the firearm you now clutch in your hands. This has all evolved beyond a craving for petty vengeance, Marcus. I have chosen to embrace my destiny, for unlike you, I am not foolish enough to resist fate. I accept it with open arms, and as such my eternal reward will be all the more extravagant. “You’re not fooling anyone but yourself and those kids you’ve imprisoned with your lies. Only reward you’ll get is eternity in some dark void after you’ve fulfilled your purpose.” Silence! You’ve run out of time. The Wilmarth puppets cannot save you now, Marcus. By the time they arrive at the lodge, nothing will remain but your ravaged carcasses. The air will be ripe with the succulent aroma of death, and you will have joined the vast legion of souls who now follow our masters in the great beyond. “I’ll be damned if I ever let those things have this Earth. Tell me Bruce…..why don’t you just dispose of us yourself? Why spout empty threats from a thousand miles away and send your mindless lackeys to do your dirty work?” I have other fish to fry. The glory that I will offer the Old Ones Friday night when I crush three of my organization’s premier denizens in one fell swoop will be utter bliss, and it eclipses quelling the dry efforts of military outcasts. I extend good luck to you, old friend. If you somehow survive, maybe we will meet again someday. Stryker couldn’t suppress a grin as he visualized the contorted face of his enemy, wherever he now was. “You can count on it, you son of a bitch.” I wouldn’t count on it. The Black Pharaoh’s children have come to visit you and your boys, Marcus. Mind your manners and go entertain your guests. “What??” “Captain!” Slade’s exasperated cry reverberated through the lodge, prompting Stryker to shift attention from the mind-sendings of Shanahan back to the situation at hand. He scowled and dashed through the hallway to take the side of his colossal comrade “We’re being hit from both sides,” growled the captain as he gazed at the approaching mob. “He’s sending those demons from the north woods, and his horde of mindless servitors coming from the south. That’s what happened to Brayston, Ryder. You were right, it is a ghost town now. Slade frowned, sliding a heavy chair in front of the door. “How so?” he asked, nudging the furniture into its place. “There isn’t a soul left in that entire village. The bodies are there, but they’re only shells now. Bruce has become their puppet master. The irony there is that he too is a damn puppet…..” “What’s the plan?” Stryker gazed intermittently between the north and south ends of the lodge. He could see the burning torches and lights clutched in the flailing arms of the Brayston mob, and likewise could feel the approaching presence of those things of the dark the cult had summoned in that accursed mansion atop the mountain. Stryker, well-learned in the arts and tactics of battle, deliberated several moments before returning his eyes to his massive ally. “It’s going to be a long night, lieutenant. Put on a fresh pot of coffee, grab a couple belts of ammunition, and get the spotlights moving. If we can hold out until morning, we should be fine.” “How are our chances?” Stryker pursed his lip, his mind still plagued with the words of the man who was once his ally. He knew his philosophies, motives and ambitions. And the feeling had become mutual. “I don’t know……”
