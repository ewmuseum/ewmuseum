Facing Evil

25 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 25.12.02 Pharaoh stood still in the light. It washed over his face and body like the waters of the Nile and it cleansed his mind so that his thoughts could concentrate on one single subject. Clyde. There was also the matter of DarkStar at hand, but Pharaoh knew the Demon was the force he needed solely to think of. Why, in the face of defeat, had Clyde repaid Pharaoh’s good deed with a chair shot to the face? Why had DarkStar returned and helped Clyde, the beast, to utterly decimate Titu after he’d gone out of his way to help the Champion keep his gold? Pharaoh shook his head. From his limbered frame hung black cloths and cloaks adorned with hieroglyphic symbols of an age long past. The Gods he worshipped day in and day out had not been kind to him on the eve of his return, but from the beating he had endured he felt he had leant sometime indeed. ‘You can never trust anyone in this business and from this day forth I won’t.’ He wouldn’t make this same mistake with Jason Snow. The up-and-coming Sunday would see him face off against DarkStar and Snow in a number one contender match for Clyde’s world title, it was a bout he couldn’t afford to lose. And so he contemplated the outcomes. The alliance between the Demon and the Mourning Star cancelled out the possibility of DarkStar taking the victory – it was more likely that he had pain and torment on his mind than a chance to win the title. So logically Snow seemed to be the only man worth watching. But Pharaoh didn’t wish for a new rivalry to form. Clyde and DarkStar were enough for any man to handle, and besides, it was greatly rumoured that neither were mortal, and after some of the things Titu had seen of the Demon he didn’t underestimate this claim for a second. DarkStar had survived Death itself, and the man who ironically had killed him in the first place was Clyde… What on earth was he up against here? Duat itself?! Looking on through the private temple, Titu picked up the sight of Amun-Ra situated against the very far wall of the peaceful sanctuary. The statue had cost much money, but as a messenger from the Gods it was well worth the purchase. He walked slowly towards it as a duo of Egyptian guards, heavily armoured, but in the dress of men from ancient times parted their halberds in unison to allow the ominous presence of Xansiv into the main chamber. Noticing the look on his brow, and also the gash upon his forehead, the men - persons of great size and most likely of a well experienced fighting background - turned to one another, waited for their elder to pass and then whispered: “What’s up with the boss?” The second sighed, shaking his head and looking behind him to where Pharaoh trod lightly on the stone floor, “Clyde.” His reply was flat and outright, as though the name was an answer alone. His companion’s brow furrowed, “Clyde?” “You know! The PCW world champion.” informed the most knowledgeable of the pair. “Oh.” The silence for the next few moments was only interrupted by the gentle flow of footsteps further down the hall behind them. Then the guard whom first dared to ask what the matter was let his voice drift again. “The gash?” this comment seemed not a question at all, but more a statement that he felt needed to be backed up. As an employee of The Palace, he had obviously heard of Titu’s return to PCW, but up until only a few moments ago hadn’t leaned of the actions that took place at Decimation. His comrade nodded knowingly, fixing his gaze back on the corridor up ahead at the same time. The second guard nodded also. This he considered was all he needed to know at present, and - unless something dire occurred very soon - probably ever would. The bastard, Pharaoh’s anger had somehow kept to itself. He didn’t quite know how or why, but the only snapping that had ensued since he returned home to California had taken place inside his head. You think you can get away with this? He probably did. The Demon wasn’t someone who went home after kicking the crap out of a defenceless human being and then had second thoughts about it. Clyde was a killing machine – the perfect killing machine, and by the Gods he didn’t even care. He showed little, to no emotion. Well, we’ll see about that, Clyde! We’ll see what happens to you once I’ve got past your little crony and Snow on Adrenaline! Just you wait, you heartless son of a bitch! Now if he could only suppress his anger and direct it’s energy to something more productive…like praying to Amun-Ra, or even KICKING CLYDE’S ROTTEN ASS! Ok…deep breaths… A moment passed. What the Duat am I thinking! I have to control myself. You’ll get your chance, Titu; you’ll get your DAMNED chance. He just couldn’t understand what had happened! Still, he should never have trusted something as evil as the Demon. It wasn’t so long ago in fact that Clyde had dropped a ton of lighting rig on him! How could he have been so stupid? Bah! I don’t care what it takes, but by the Gods I’ll get my own back on the twisted freak. Surely the Gods would know what to do! And even if they didn’t come up with the answers at least he’d have some time alone to think things through properly. He hadn’t done that in some time, think things through. Ever since El Diablo banished him back to Egypt he’d planned his revenge and as soon as he gets it the damned tables are turned against him. That wasn’t right! It wasn’t supposed to happen like that! Well not for much longer. As soon as Adrenaline was through he’d be crowned number one contender to the title and then the tables would once again be turned – this time in his favour. This, he felt, was his only way to get any sort of closure on the champ. And once he’d backed Clyde into a corner he could pounce, take out his anger, take back his dignity, and even capture some gold in the process! It all worked out in his favour…just the way he liked it. Titu grinned. Slowly he approached the giant figure. Amun-Ra, highest deity of Egypt, looked down upon him with the face of that of a human being. Unlike many of the other Gods, Amun-Ra was gifted with a visage that didn’t resemble any animal - this separated him from the rest, keeping him the apex of the group. “Oh, Amun-Ra, what must I do to be rid of these demons?” The question itself was ironic. Though he had not meant to use what was most likely Clyde and DarkStar’s best definition, they acted as the thorn in Pharaoh’s side, and so represented the demons in his career. He wished for them to be away, but for him to be free from their ever-increasing grip he would have to do battle with them, earn their respects, or - dare he think it? – kill them. Whatever the solution I must think beyond them; after all, I have much work to do. Indeed so. The PCW championship could, with work, soon find itself around the waist of the only Egyptian to ever grace the prestigious federation, but with it comes much responsibility and respect. Did Pharaoh really want all that pressure? I guess I’ll add that to my list of worries too. Of course first he actually had to win the title and foremost defeat two extremely worthy adversaries to earn a chance! It was all too much! Up until now he hadn’t even considered becoming the champion of PCW. Clyde had been his only worry, and though he hated what DarkStar did to him days earlier in the week, his thoughts laid beyond the pawn in the Demon’s plan to the master of the game. What to do? At last, he realized: I must face evil… and overcome its power. He rose to his feet and left the temple. The guards watched on…
