TEC: En Route

25 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/25/02 “Ow!” “Get off of me!” “A nas dunau din casdc apiuahc isdfvin!” “That was uncalled for, Slade!” “When is this plane supposed to land anyway!” “Richard doesn’t know. Everything is so dark.” The End:“Wait, weren’t these guys supposed to be on a bus? The last Misfits RP hinted at it.” Handler:“Hey! I can’t change things up if I want to. And you’re not even in this RP, so be quiet!” The End:“Geez. I haven’t been in like the last two or three RPs you’ve done. Am I still your character?” Handler:“Shut up!” The Misfits are stuck in a large crate, having mailed themselves to the Land of the Lepers via UPS to get there. The plane ride has been cramped (obviously), but also cold and wet (the air-conditioner is turned to full blast and seeps through the cracks in the crate; Drek drools when he sleeps). Up in the cockpit (there would be too many buttons to describe individually, let’s just say that there’s a lot of frickin’ buttons, levers and switches, okay?), there are two chairs. In one is the co-pilot, wearing a blue aviator’s suit. He is drinking out a bottle of vodka, and keeps yelling, “Don’t touch my money, ass!” The pilot is passed out on the floor next to the cockpit door from imbibing too much alcohol. Of course, given this situation, neither man is flying the plane. Who is, you ask? Why, it’s Juju the Albino Iguana, of course! Juju is sitting in the cockpit, his scales somehow insulating him from the air-conditioning that is set at 55 degrees for some reason, odd because as an iguana, Juju is cold-blooded and would be less active in this temperature. On Juju’s head is the pilot’s hat. Juju took the hat as soon as the pilot passed out. Juju stands in the pilot’s chair, flying the plane as best he can. A voice comes over the radio. “UPS 926,” the medium high-pitched voice says in a slight country drawl, “we have you plotted heading for the Land of the Lepers, is that indeed your intended desitination?” Juju grabs the radio, hits the talk switch, and talks. “Wonk,” says Juju. “What?” the voice asks. “Wonk!” Over the radio, a few laughs can be heard. The man on the other end put it on the speakers at is tower. “I can’t understand you. Look, the Land of the Lepers is a dangerous place! Don’t go there!” Juju shouts, “WONK!” into the radio, and turns it off. But enough of that; let’s skip ahead a little bit. {Time Compression: Two Hours} The pilot and co-pilot are in the cargo area of the ship. Both are very, very drunk. Pilot:“You know, I betcha $100 this here little ol’ iguana will hit the ground before this big crate here.” Co-Pilot:“You’re on!” “WONK!” is all Juju says in a very agitated wonk. The pilot opens the cargo hold, the plane above the Land of the Lepers. It’s on autopilot; at least Juju had the foresight to do that before the pilot snatched him out of the chair. The pilot holds Juju out of the hold, and the co-pilot, a big muscular guy, pushes the crate to the opening. Pilot:“Ready? 1… 2… 3!” The pilot let’s go of the iguana, and the co-pilot pushes the crate out of the door. {Time Compression: 30 seconds} “I oijp dfnk aio jmnadjkn?” “Richard agrees: Richard does feel as though he is lighter.” “That’s means we’re falling, you idiots!” “Falling?” “Mumphklix?” “AHHHHHHH!” “AHHHHHHH!” “AHHHHHHH!” There is the sound of rustling leaves and cracking branches. Finally, a loud, hard thud; then only silence. {Time Compression: 1 hour} The Misfits wake up inside of their box. The UPS people dropped it out of their plane because they do not wish to enter the Land of the Lepers, or deal with its people. And no, for the moment, I will not be any more specific than that. Drek uses his axe to hack through the crate. Outside the box, the Misfits Slade, Richard, and Drek, all wearing the same thing they wore in “Odd Jobs,” (shame on you if you didn’t read it) stretch out, their bodies cramped after the long plane ride to get to the Land of the Lepers. I’ll leave it at that… wait, I never described what Drek was wearing. Well, Drek wears wolf’s fur tunic, bearskin boots. Nothing else. The forest they landed in is dry, dense and dark. The Misfits can hardly see in front of them. There are the sounds of various birds and primates singing and howling in the night. The canopy of trees overhead is too thick to see through. Drek, a dwarf soldier, feels the ground. “Vibrations,” Drek says, shushing the other. He closes his eyes so he won’t fool himself. He also keeps his head still so he won’t pick up sounds to confuse himself. Drek jumps up and points to the southwest. “That way,” he says in a determined voice. Drek leads the group, cutting the brush out of his way. Of course, he is shorter than the others, so he only cuts the brush out of his way, while Slade and Richard still get bush and braches in their faces (which wouldn’t be a problem in itself except for the ants…). Slade makes a suggestion. “J ahsdu nmoiadsoin k cl?” he says. Drek looks up at Slade, the question addressed to the dwarf. “I’m not sure,” Drek says, “that you can lift it.” “Hui jckplx,” Slade responds, waving off Drek’s comment. Drek holds up his hands in his right hand, offering it to Slade. Slade grabs the axe with his right hand. As Drek lets go, the weigh of the axe overwhelms Slade and he falls forward. Drek has to dive out of the way to avoid being cleaved by the axe. Slade grabs the axe with both hands, the axe slightly imbedded in the ground, and pulls at it with all his might; the axe won’t budge. Richard offers to help Slade, and together he and Slade pull on the axe as much as the two possibly can. However, the axe still does not move. “Stand back, ya pansies,” Drek commands. “Let a real dwarf lift it.” Drek waves the other two Misfits away. Exhausted, they back up. Drek places his left hand around the axe, and with the greatest of ease picks it up out of the ground and slings it over his shoulder. The other two Misfits are amazed. “Asjud,” says Slade, “nquecnlerpy ikj albhdcn c peoirwef ifxusrm a.” Richard nods his head. “Richard agrees,” he says. “Richard never would’ve thought that your axe was so heavy. You’re really strong.” “Aye,” Drek gloats, placing his right hand on his hip, and staring up into space as though he were a memorial statue. “Well,” the scruffy-bearded one says, “Richard needs for the brush to be cleared faster. Richard has an idea.” Richard takes out his cigarette lighter and a small whiskey bottle from his pant pocket. He pours the whiskey onto some bushes, and lights the lighter. Drek’s eyes grow wide very fast, and he rushes from his spot ten feet away from Richard to stop him. “No!!!” Drek yells. Slade stands back, knowing he can’t stop Richard in time. Richard steps back, and he tosses the lighter down into the whiskey. Immediately, the line of alcohol erupts into a fire. The flames quickly spread to all the other trees and bushes. In but a minute, the entire forest is up in flames. {Time Compression: 1 hour} The Misfits sit in a wooden cabin on a plain wooden bench in front of a wooden desk. Sitting at the desk, wearing his khaki-colored park ranger hat and green pants, his shoeless feet folded across each other on the desk, and his shirtless stomach bulging, is Smokey the Bear. Smokey eyes the Misfits as if he were going to maul them. Smokey:“I’m usually a personable bear, but you’ve got me a little rustled. Don’t you pay attention to the public service commercials? ONLY YOU F*CKERS CAN PREVENT FOREST FIRES!” Slade tries to explain what happened. “Hjwax kbblstx,” he says in an apologetic voice. “Gropula nffer—” Smokey interrupts Slade with anger in his insulting voice. Smokey:“WHAT THE HELL DID THAT THING JUST SAY?” Drek jumps down from the bench and stands up to the desk. A loud roar from Smokey the Bear makes him get back up on the bench, and say his piece from there. “We ask you forgive us,” Drek begins, pointing his right index finger at Richard, “for our companion is a little dumb. You see, Richard here wanted to clear a path through the forest…” Smokey takes off his hat and throws it on the desk. He points at Richard with his entire left claw. Smokey:“This is your fault?” Richard sinks his head into his shirt, confirming Smokey’s statement with a weak, “Yes.” Smokey reaches over the desk, grabs Richard by the neck, and pulls him over to the other side of the desk. Smokey throws Richard down on the floor and then begins mauling him. “Excuse me,” Drek says in a careful voice, “but would you please not maul our friend?” Smokey looks back at Drek, in his eyes a bloodlust that could not possibly be sated by just Richard alone. Smokey:“YOU WANT SOME, TOO?” Drek promptly quiets himself. “Ajhu hfiuasdn liunwe zc,” says Slade, standing up with conviction. Smokey:“WHAT?” Smokey looks back at Slade, but continues clawing and beating Richard. “He said,” Drek translates while in his seat, not daring to move, “that we were just trying to get to the Land of the Lepers.” Smokey hears this and stops mauling Richard. He calms down for a little bit, and licks Richard’s blood from his claws. Smokey tosses Richard back to the bench, and sits down in his chair with his back straight and paws crossed. He puts his hat back on as he speaks to the Misfits in a diabolically evil tone. Smokey:“Hm… the Land of the Lepers, huh? That’s worse than anything I could do to you… Tell you what, I’ll tell you how to get there. Go out the door, and turn left 70 degrees. Head straight until you hit the boundary to their little area.” Slade nods and says, “Koioa jnasdfjni jkasdh hoern bzcxhb?” Smokey looks at Slade with an expression of confusion. He looks to Drek to translate. Smokey:“I don’t follow.” “He said,” says Drek, “we’ll have a hard time seeing. Could you give us a flashlight so we don’t trip and injure ourselves?” Smokey:“Fine.” Smokey opens up a drawer on the right side of the desk, and rummages around in it a bit. He comes up back with a flashlight. Smokey:“Here’s a solar-powered flashlight.” Drek takes the flashlight, but looks at it with his own confused expression. “But,” he begins, “it’s still dark outside.” Another loud, angry roar from Smokey the Bear sends the trio running outside. It’s still dark and dry. The fire is out, with help from Captain Planet. The smell of ashes and embers is still fresh in the air, and the air isn’t quite clean enough to breathe without problems. Drek tries the flashlight anyway… it doesn’t work. Hadn’t been charged in a good long while. “Llih linasfon pji iuh m itfsarf,” says Slade. “You mean he doesn’t act like that in commercials?” “No,” responds Slade, in one of the few things he can say in plain English. Walking along the path Smokey set for them, Drek looks up at nearly unconscious Richard while Slade carries him across his shoulder. “Dumbass,” Drek says with acid.
