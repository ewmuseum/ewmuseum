Date: February 9 of 2003 A.D. “I fear that is impossible,” the doctor iterated matter-of-factly. “Until you overcome this affliction, you must remain under our care. It is hard to tell how you may react if we were to release you back into the world while you harbor these cravings for vengeance.” “You have no choice in the matter, doctor.” The doctor couldn’t help but suppress a wide grin at this revelation. “Oh really?” he scoffed. “I, the head physician at this facility, have no say in whether or not you go free at your leisure?” The smile Bruce expressed in retort was morbid and not subtly perverse. “Tomorrow afternoon at 4 pm. At that time, I will be released. That has been affirmed.” Normally Okoye would have retaliated with laughter and dismissed the mad lunatic. But something he couldn’t explain brought fear to his heart and caused a cold sweat to break out on his brow. He could only stare at his sinister subject with skepticism. And to his chagrin, for some reason even that was declining..... ---------------------------------- (Loosely based account of the events of September 4, 2002 from the statements of hospital personnel and various records) Dr. Okoye nonchalantly checked his watch and extended a brazen smile. It was nearly 4 p.m., the forecast time Shanahan had claimed he would be spontaneously freed from his ‘chains’ in the Greensburgh sanitarium. Samsanov remained unwavering in his passion to keep the insane Shanahan locked up until his wild yearning for vengeance was curbed. He had yet to openly embrace the initiative and thus his revelation was undoubtedly going to prove false, as chances of the doctor spontaneously changing his outlook in the next few minutes were very slim. The doctor lifted his eyes to rest on his immobile patient, though taking care to avoid his wicked gaze. Though strictly a man of science and ever the skeptic in respect to the supernatural, he could detect something vastly unholy lingering in those sockets. He cautiously avoided eye contact with his mad patient, fearing the repercussions on his own sanity. The Boston native had an inexplicable aura, that much was undeniable. As was the diabolical intent which tainted it. “The time has almost arrived, doc,” Jack uttered flatly. His manner contradicted the furious gleam that raged in his eyes and that element in itself brought a menacing air to the New Englander. As Okoye stood gawking through the barred window and across the waters of the sun-kissed bog flanking the institution, he truly couldn’t decide what was worse: the evil in his patient’s eyes or the dreary tone shrewdly disguising the dark mania he harbored within. The physician’s only comfort came from the knowledge that Bruce had been securely bound in the straitjacket now enveloping his bulky form. And he was assured that personnel had checked the apparatus and double-checked it to ensure his safety while the pair remained alone in that barren, solitary chamber. Okoye tried to focus on the rays of the setting sun as they reflected off the waters of the marshland. As he did so, a buzzer echoed over the address system. “Doctor Okoye?” came a tinny female voice. “Yes?” he replied with nary a tinge of feeling. “Doctor Houghton wanted me to ask if you needed anything before he left for today.” “No, I’m fine.” “He wanted me to express his concern to you, doctor. He told me he has a very bad feeling about what you’re doing.” A dark cloud swept across the sky, temporarily blotting out the sun and casting the old structure in shadow. Shanahan uttered a menacing laugh at this phenomenon as Okoye attempted to blot this unsettling omen from my mind. He cleared his throat and turned away. “Doctor Houghton always has bad feelings, Kyla,” he snorted in response. “He’s a good doctor, but a superstitious nincompoop. I’m perfectly fine.” He glanced down at his watch again. “We’ll be done with this ‘session’ very soon. Please leave us until then, Miss Geoffrey.” “As you wish, sir.” Okoye braced himself and locked his eyes on Shanahan. The maniac’s head was bowed and a slew of unintelligible monosyllables rumbled forth from his throat. He twitched abruptly in his straitjacket with eyes shut and muscles taut before suddenly erupting forth in what could only be described as a seizure. Okoye looked on spellbound as Shanahan quaked and trembled violently in his chair but suddenly lacked conscious mobility to take action; it was as if he were hypnotized by some unearthly force and then compelled to watch the birth of some new form of life. He didn’t know why, but for some eerie reason in his own mind he likened the sensation to that of watching a butterfly burst forth from its cocoon….. The episode ended as quickly as it had begun. Shanahan nestled back into his chair with head lowered, his breathing somewhat erratic but otherwise serene. Okoye watched quietly another moment before his senses returned to him in a snap and he blinked a couple times to shake off the effects of the incident. “The reason I summoned you in here was to prove your theory was a figment of your own mind,” remarked the doctor. “It’s nearly 4 p.m., and you should know I have no intention of discharging you from this facility. Especially not after what just happened.” The laugh this raised from the patient sent shivers down the spine of Okoye. It was an uncanny laugh, evil and sparked by whatever hellish influences plagued his unstable mind. It was the laugh of a man reveling in victory. “You poor fool,” Shanahan mumbled. “Oh how I pity your ignorance. My friend, if only I was still embraced by that which is placid and conventional. But alas, chaos and ruin have chosen to adopt me into their fold. And my sanity was the sacrifice….” “You make no sense, Bruce,” replied the physician, now feeling unsteady. “Not even you know what you’re saying.” Not even Shanahan’s binding could comfort Okoye. An unnatural chill had flooded the room and startlingly seemed to emanate from Bruce. “On the contrary, Samsanov,” the patient continued. “Entire realms of knowledge have been opened to me. Your taking me into custody proved to be my deliverance. Within these walls of the insane, They have bequeathed the most hallowed of wisdom upon me. I now know the truth.” “And what truth is that?” Okoye saw what he felt were the very fires of the abyss rage and churn in the madman’s eyes. And he thanked God he wasn’t driven mad at the sight. “Mortality is fleeting. But the Old Ones are forever.” The lights flickered momentarily, drawing a bemused glance from the anxious physician. Shanahan was again dead silent. This perturbed silence was broken by a voice over the speakers, just as the lights in the room failed and the room was bathed in darkness. “Doctor Okoye! Power across the facility has gone down!” Not even the orange glow of waning daylight permeated the room. Okoye answered the female clerk quietly and veiled his apprehension as much as possible. “Get someone on it please, Kyla.” The emergency light switched on, bestowing the room with a meager red glow. Okoye wasn’t concerned with this however. His eyes were directed outside, past the metal bars of the window and what lay beyond. And beyond there lay only darkness. “An eclipse?” he wondered to himself. “I didn’t hear of one today….it’s too damn early to be nightfall…..” “By the Old Ones’ whim, there is no daylight.” Okoye’s rage seized him as he spun to face the blank face of Bruce Shanahan. “Bullshit!” he roared with hatred finally seething to the surface. “There are no ‘Old Ones’! It’s a fabrication, an illusion! You’re a fucking lunatic and they’re a creation of your damn mind! Can’t you see that?!” “Denial is a safety valve, my dear friend,” Bruce countered almost mockingly. “Your lack of faith really disturbs me, doc….” “Doctor!” Kyla’s voice echoed over the PA system again. Okoye mumbled despairingly to himself as he glimpsed out the window. Shanahan merely smiled. “The patients are going crazy! The powers out and some of the doors are opening! Jesus what should we….?” Samsanov was mystified, Kyla’s voice barely registering in his head before the the interocm was cut off in a hiss of static. All he could see were the shadows moving beyond the walls of the asylum. Spawning in the expansive bog he watched as a vast army of them twitched and shirked against the outside emergency lights, lurching and crawling toward the institution through the murky water and reeds sprouting forth. “Trick of the light,” Okoye thought to himself with a grin, vainly trying to convince himself of such. “Too many madmen….nerves….that’s all it is….” He turned to face Bruce. “Yes, yes….it’s all you. You’ve made me paranoid. There’s nothing out there. This power outage….it’s mere coincidence….” He cackled like a madman himself. “Besides…..I ain’t letting you go! You were wrong, Shanahan! I’m not releasing you! Your prophecy is fucking wrong!” Bruce’s reaction was that of stoicism. He seemed callous to the physician’s words. “Heh….I never said your approval was requisite, Doctor Okoye. The Great Old Ones don’t require the endorsement of mortals. You are a pawn. And you always have been a pawn. And now……the game is nearly over. Checkmate.” Terror the likes of which Dr. Samsanov Okoye had never felt gripped his racing heart. Dread and shock consumed his mind as he watched Bruce Shanahan, incarcerated in the straitjacket that had been double and triple checked for security, draw himself to his feet. A baleful grin crept across his lips as he looked the doctor in the eye, and let the outfit slide from his shoulders and hit the floor with a jingle as the metal locks hit the concrete….
