Shattered Dreams

16 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8-16-02 It was my dream; my opportunity and I blew it. When it comes to competition sports I have been underestimated in every game I have ever played. I have been put down by just about everyone that has crossed my path when it comes to a competition sport. Coaches would yell at me to push harder and I did and they would see me, as a shaky individual and that I cannot survive in society. I have always used those words to feed my adrenaline and to pump me up before an event. I want to teach those that put me down a lesson, a lesson that do not always underestimate the mind, body and heart of a individual, because you will never know when or how it will strike back. You can’t let it get to you or it will destroy you, so I have just kept my chin up and I have proven people that what they say about me is wrong. With those words I became a hell of athlete, something that pushed me everyday that I could succeed. With the hard work and the determination I began to see my dream coming ever so closer to me. I played football since I was seven years old playing in the pee wee leagues doing my best to make my parents proud. My father was a former football in the NFL, he played for the New York Giants as there tight end, his career was ended short due to badly torn ACL that could not have been repaired back in his day so he had to retire at the age of twenty six years old. I was always told by coaches throughout football that the only that I could succeed was to gain some muscle because I was such a tall kid for my age and I was very thin. By the age of twelve I began working out at a gold’s gym down the street from me, I worked out gaining more muscle until I reached High School. I could see huge improvements in my game; I was faster and harder to tackle. I became a hell of a football player now and was doing great each game in the middle school level. Then came the beginning of my downfall… It all started in High School Football, I was the starting halfback as a freshman. I could run up and down that field like a stolen car on the highway. I average about one thousand seven hundred and fifty yards a year throughout my high school career. With those statistics brought talent scouts from different universities to come and watch my games. I had Penn State, Ohio State, Notre Dame and Nebraska just to name a few who were watching my ability. They came to every game I had senior season and they would sit there with a clipboard while holding a stopwatch watching every move that I make, making shore that when it comes down to crunch time I don’t fuck it up for everyone else. It was a good year for me; I received a total one thousand eight hundred and seventy two yards in my final year at my high school. As a team we had a perfect season, we won or division championship and then we later went on to win are section championship which advanced us to the state championship level a feat that our high school has never completed. That was the way I wanted to prove myself in front of those scouts, the big game, New York State High School Football Championship on the line and me playing the game of my life. I knew if I could help carry the team to the championship that I would be playing division one college football the next fall on live television, going for the NCAA National Championship something I have desired my whole football career. The team did great in the tournament; we advanced all the way to the championship game. We hit some rough spots along the way but overall we played a hell of a tournament and I was carrying the team as far as I could possibly go. I was averaging a game twenty-eight carries for two hundred yards, with two touchdowns. I even played a little tight end so the newest freshman sensation that was following in my footsteps at halfback could play. The scouts loved it, they came up to me after every game to shake my hand and tell me what a hell of a job I did. They even asked me what jersey size I was. I couldn’t believe what was happening to me at the time. A city boy who grew up with two working parents that did there best to support their family was getting look upon by division one schools to play college football for them. I was on cloud nine and I let it get to my head. My parents were excited that I was being able to play for a college that would pay for my entire scholarship because we did not have enough money to pay for a college like the standards of a Notre Dame and a Penn State. I looked upon the opportunity as me getting into the NFL, my parents looked upon it is as a way to get my degree. We received packets in the mail from all different colleges willing to give me a full scholastic scholarship to play football for there college. My parents would read each packet and always tell me what go resources are at each school. I would just stand there nodding my head, thinking about the next move I made in a game and what kind of parties I will be going to. I hit a huge brick wall when my parents asked me what I will be majoring if I go to a college. Mom: Have you looked at these packets yet? Yeah shore I have. Dad: You haven’t look at them yet, I bet. I have been real busy with football right now, the state championship is tomorrow. Mom: Honey, what’s more important to you, an education or the game? Right now I’d have to say the game tomorrow. Dad: Well you do realize that football isn’t always going to be there, don’t you? Yes I do Dad, but this is my dream, to play football the rest of my life and make it into my occupation. Dad: We understand that, the only thing we have a problem with is that incase something happens to you like with me, then we want you to receive your scholarship so you have a cushion incase something does happen. Dad don’t worry, I’ll be fine. Dad: That’s not the problem, I don’t want to see you doing what I’m doing when you screw up your body and can never play football again. If I didn’t listen to the NFL scouts then I would have had my degree in business, but I was stupid and left in my third year of college for the NFL Draft. I regret ever doing that and I don’t want that to happen to you. I get the picture Dad, I won’t let you guys down. Mom: We just want you to have a back up plan incase something drastic happens. All right, I will pick a major and I will study hard. I promise. Mom: That’s all we ask for. I understood what they meant and I was always interested in doing radio broadcasting or communications like a sports anchor or something of that nature. But I had no time to think about my future now, the present was less then twenty-four hours away and I needed sleep tomorrow for the state championship game against Albany High School. One of the top teams in the nation, but that didn’t scare me, I have been waiting four years for this opportunity and it won’t slip away. It was game day parades in the morning, luncheon at midday and the big game at night. Scouts wishing me good luck and telling me that they would be watching approached me before the game. I was very excited cause this was my chance to make national acclaim on a stage like this. New York State was known for good High School Football programs and some of the best players in the nation have come from New York State school systems, so it was very assuring that when I step onto that field I could be making history for myself and paving the path to my next step. The whistle blew and the ball was kicked off, the start of the game. It was really a miss match of teams as we were known for our offense and they were known for their defense that would make an interesting game. The first half ended in a tie. In the first half I had twelve carries for sixty-four yards, pretty good against a top defense but I needed to show the scouts that I was unstoppable. The third quarter wasn’t easier we were knotted still at zero. The fourth quarter seemed like forever until they scored on a forty-yard field goal with three minutes to go. We need to move down the field and fast. So I took upon myself to return the punt and the coach allowed me to. I ran down the field breaking tackles and was finally run down at our forty-five. The next play I want the ball so they gave it to me, I found a whole up the middle and broke away to there thirty. They then made a quick pass that got us down to the fourteen. We were pumped as hell and I told the coach to give me the ball again cause I wanted it so badly. This is where it goes all down hill… The ball is handed off to me I make my way through a whole down to the ten, the to the five and I see the end zone is open. Little did I know that the two safeties were on each side of me and tackled me in the middle like I was a sandwich. While they tackled me on the side, there two hundred and fifty pound outside linebacker comes up from behind me and jumps on my back. I fall to the ground and I feel a sting go throughout my body. I was stuck under a pile of these guys, glassy eyed and could barely move. The refs pulled them off and noticed that I was hurt. The paramedics came out and put me on the stretcher taking me to a hospital where I later found out that I had two broken legs, a bruised collarbone, a bruised spine, bruised lungs and to top it all off I had a broken shoulder. Then I later found that we lost after I left which wasn’t good news, they lost on an interception the play after I left to the hospital. Did I ever hear from the scouts again? Nope, they couldn’t wait long enough for me to go through rehab and get better. But what the doctor basically told the scouts was that my career was over in Football. The only time they visited me was to check with my doctor. He was right in the end, I could never play football again, my dreams were shattered and all hope was lost.
