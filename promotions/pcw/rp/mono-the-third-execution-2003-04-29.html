Mono:- The Third:- Execution

29 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 29/04/03 The constant rain, added with the now darkness of the night sky, had turned the night into a bleak and dirty affair, especially as Lacuna had locked her car and left through the grimy back streets, heading towards the large neon sign illuminating a small area of the dark alley. ‘Temptations’ read the sign. It was Jade’s strip club, well; it used to be Jade’s. Lacuna was sure she was here, she had to be. It just all seemed to make sense at the moment. Every single thing that ran through her head was clear, every thought made total sense. She was scarily focused on every minute detail she had to remember. She had never felt like this before, and it scared her. The feeling that there was only one outcome was growing, as was her confidence, but something small was niggling at the back of her head, like something that she knew was wrong, but she had cleared her head and tried hard to forget any prior doubts, thus this emotion confused her more. She didn’t doubt Jade was here, but something was telling her she wasn’t. “Well, only one way to find out” she muttered under her breath as she took a sharp right and turned down the side of the rough bricked building. The large men on the door scared her, rain soaked yet still then large lumps in their pockets insinuated weaponry, and she didn’t want trouble yet. Leaning back against the sharp brickwork, she sheltered the blue print map she had dragged from her pocket under the large flap of coat from the now torrential downpour of rain drenching everything in its path. “There should be a ladder right about… oh, behind me” she shrugged as she turned to see a large rusted steel ladder attached to the wall, and she shoved the map roughly back into her pocket before jumping up and taking a hard grasp on the slippery metal and slowly climbing the large spaces in between each rung. It was slow at first, the climbing that is, but it sped up as she began to reach the top of the ground floor, which seemed mightily high for a single floor, it must have had a very high ceiling inside, but she had only seen the outside and the first floor, and she finally reached the top of the ladder which seemingly ran to the middle of the first floor, and as lacuna stepped out onto the makeshift gantry at the top, she heard something that made her blood run still. Voices. No, not inside the building, but actually inside her head. Yeah, seriously. “She isn’t here, turn back” the voice commented and Lacuna shook her head violently, as if to get the sound out of her ears or something. She was somewhat famed for being slightly insane, she regularly had discussions with herself in dreams and has heard voices before, but not when she was about to break into a building and very rarely did what she heard speak in coherent sentences, usually riddles were their selected medium of contact. But now there were actually voices on the inside of the door, and she regained her composure. There was no other way to get inside the building without going through the heavily guarded front and rear entrances, and the handle on this door, pending further inspection, was heavily rusted and may well fall apart upon contact. So she did what just what any burglar with a high social class would do. She recreated the Irish Burglar joke. She knocked on the door of the building she was trying to rob. There was silence from inside, and she pressed her ear to the cold rusted metal of the door. Inside, there were indeed voices, and they were seemingly discussing how on earth someone had managed to knock on a door linked to an unused alley while climbing an unsafe ladder and standing on a makeshift gantry that not only shook in the wind but also had strange habits of falling apart in bad weather. “It’s impossible” beckoned one, his voice deep and gruff “Do you know how high up we are here?” “I’m sure, you know, I’ve never heard anything that clear before. I swear!” this voice was brighter, and somewhat younger sounding, probably a teenager still. By the sounds caused by their feet, they were probably approaching the door now, and the footsteps sounded cautious. So, just to add some confusion, she knocked again. “There!” shouted the younger voice excitedly “You had to have heard that! Three loud knocks!” “Okay, why not have a look if you’re so damn sure about that” grunted the other man angrily, he was clearly not best pleased at the situation, he seemed like the sort to crave normality, whatever that was. “Why me?” hesitated the younger, seemingly worried about what lurks behind the door, with full rights to when Lacuna is standing their, a shotgun slung over her shoulder and voices in her head. “Because you are the moron who is hearing the damn knocks, now get on with it” and with his negative berating of his younger counterpart, he could distinctly be heard handing over a key. Then, somewhat unsurprisingly, the key turned in the lock mechanism, and Lacuna ducked back to the side of the door, which opened with a loud creaking, and a small head, decorated in pimples and spots and coated in grease signified the arrival of the younger guard. Lacuna smiled. He was using under developed teenagers to guard Jade. This would be easier than she had imagined! She reached forwards hastily and grabbed the child by the collar, flinging him easily over the broken rail and down to the alley below where he landed hard on the concrete. It wasn’t high enough to kill him, but still, it must have hurt one hell of a lot. Lacuna ducked back behind the door and smiled to herself. She needn’t have worried, she was good at everything, and now she knew that it involved breaking an entry. “What the?” It was the gruff voice of the older man now, surprised as his friend had just disappeared off a ledge, and he too stuck his head out, in his right hand was a hand gun. “Bloody hell!” shouted Lacuna, and she kicked out her right leg angrily, knocking the gun out of the guys hand. She then followed it up, bringing her leg around and clubbing it around the face of the guy, who fell to his knees deftly, he reached out for the gun but Lacuna noticed in time, stamping her foot down on his hand, and by the sound of it, breaking a couple of fingers at the same time. She kicked him in the face angrily, his head rebounding from the white plastered wall, spraying a thin layer of blood around as she did so. “Where’s Jade?” shouted Lacuna, her voice full of venom as she reached forwards and grabbed a small hand around his throat. “Jade? Haven’t seen her for weeks!” he was panicking, but his voice sounded honest enough “Lies! Now tell me!” she banged his head into the wall again. She was fuming now, she couldn’t be wrong! “I’m not lying! She really isn’t here!” he whimpered as Lacuna got further into his face. “Told you so” It was the voice in her head again, and she really didn’t want to hear it right about now. She had things to do, and that did not include arguing with herself over whether Jade was here or not. She shouted loudly, which was pretty stupid as she was not legally supposed to be in the building, and she stormed off around another corner into a dark corridor. Upon further examination of every room she could find, there was nobody to be found. Not even another guard she could take her frustration out on. She was heading back to her car. She had given up now; she had given up on Jade altogether. Her life was over, she had lost the only person she had been close to, just like she had lost her parents, and as she turned a corner and unlocked the car, she heard the voice in her head again. “I told you that I wasn’t there” “I?” she questioned. What did the voice mean by that? She sat down in the car and pulled the seat belt over her shoulder. She was a failure again, and it didn’t look like she would ever tie down a friendly relationship with anyone. Then she saw the eye, glowing in the darkness behind her. It was Jade. Her one bright white eye was glowing with an inner cleanliness, it seemed somewhat clinically bright. Lacuna also noticed the white streak in her hair had become brighter. “Jade!” she shouted, not caring who heard outside of the car. “I told you I wasn’t there” her voice was cold and uncaring, avoiding all emotions. “You told me? How the hell did you get inside my head?” she was too confused to take in what exactly she was saying at the moment, but all she wanted to do now was go home “Just drive, I’ll explain later” So she did, and that was that.
