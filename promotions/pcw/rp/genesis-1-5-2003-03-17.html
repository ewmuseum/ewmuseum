Genesis 1.5

17 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3-17-03 Genesis~ Definition: An event that is a beginning; a first part or stage of subsequent events. In a dark room, a dark man sits, a dark shadow dances on all the while at the will of the flickering light of a dying candle. A dark mystery lay beneath an otherwise dark façade, linked directly to the dark scene. Redundant? Damn straight! “What the hell!” A.J. Trinidad laughs out loud to his self as he turns the light on. “Um, you certainly are a strange fellow Mr. Trinidad,” a middle-aged man dressed from head to toe in a navy blue business-like suit holding a microphone. “You say strange, I say exclusive.” A.J. responds wiping a tear from his eye caused by the sudden outburst of mirth. “Anywho, I just always wanted to do that. Saw it done so many times with the dark room and candle, thought I’d amuse myself.” “You definitely stay true to your track record Mr. Trinidad, I’ll give you that.” The reporter replies swiping his comb-over from one side to the other. “And just what track record is that?” A.J. questions. “That you are a jokester, a funny man, a real gag reel.” The reporter professes. “Hey! I resent that!” A.J. almost yells back in the direction of the microphone-wielding suit. “I can be serious, all too serious I’ll have you know.” Covering his face with his hands, A.J. reappears with a stern, stupidly serious look grafted across his mug. Holding this pose for minutes without saying a word A.J.’s phony façade has the reporter man completely puzzled as how to approach this interview until A.J. breaks his act. “You see, totally serious, completely and entirely thought-provoking, wouldn’t you agree?” Trinidad asks while taking a sip from his half empty glass of water. ‘Half empty, there is an expression used all too often in this god forsaken world’, A.J. thinks to himself as the reporter lashes through a slue of questions that interest him about as much as a colonoscopy on a warm summer’s day. “The PCW?” finishes the reporter. “Huh, what?” A.J. replies snapping out of his daydream. “PCW, why PCW, and why now?” The reporter shortens his question, and repeats. “Simple” A.J. begins. “Life grew stale without wrestling. Sure I have a beautiful girl friend and an awesome kangaroo, but there’s something to be said about walking out to thousands of screaming fans night in and night out.” “Or booing.” The reporter interrupts. “What the hell are you babbling on about?”A.J. demands. “Just simply stating that you, A.J. Trinidad may not always get the screams here in PCW that you’ve gotten in EWE or DWO. The past few DWO members to grace PCW were terrible, terrible disappointments.” The reporter counters. “True, ya got me there,” A.J. laughs, “but look who you’re talking about. Jackson Rust, he’s just an ass clown, born an ass clown, and will die an ass clown. I have a funny family tree theory on the Ass Clown family strand that Rust fell from, but we’ll save that for another day. Adam Stryker, he was, and will remain a good friend, but for reasons unbeknownst to me, he dropped the ball. Callisto Bane, was she in PCW? “Ummm, absolutely not!” The reporter takes offense to the mere mention of the name. “Oh,” replies Trinidad again snickering, “last I heard she was serving time for impersonating a famous author, or maybe a not so famous person. I can’t recall.” “Can you honestly say that your time in the DWO served you no bit of good what so ever?” The reporter pushes on with the interview.” “Honestly?” A.J. questions. “That’s what I said wasn’t it?” The reporter comes back with in a harsh tone. “Well then, let me think about tha… No!” A.J. spouts out, not actually thinking about the question one bit. “I’ve honestly had more legitimate training with the kangaroo back in Australia. He’s a little perverted, and has a drinking problem, but he’s one hell of a mean sumbitch.” The reporter stalls for a second searching around the room as if though he hadn’t actually prepared thoroughly for this interview. Noticing the championship belts on the walls, along side a picture of an old man dressed in an all white robe. The man wears a certain look on his face that automatically demands respect, his long, white hair flows slightly in the breeze from wherever this picture was taken and the photograph gets lost in the infinite sand in the background. “The picture, on the wall, of the old man,” the reporter inquires, “who is that man? “He was a friend.” A.J. cuts his answer short. “Was a friend?” the Reporter digs for a deeper answer. “St. Jon.” A.J. mumbles more so to himself than to anyone else letting his head fall in sorrow. “His name was St. Jon and he was like a father to me. He taught me everything I’ve ever needed to know about fighting, winning, and most of all, pride in myself. I never got to……” A.J.’s head falls completely into his hands, his raven black hair hangs over his face to conceal his emotions. “Never got to….what?” interrogates the reporter taking a more somber tone. “Never got to say thank you, never got to say goodbye, never got to fulfill my prophecy.” A.J. staggers through muffled breaths. “So that is what has driven you into PCW, the will of your teacher?” “What has driven me into the PCW is the genuine opportunity to supply closure! I don’t expect to hang any more belts up on this wall; the gold means nothing to me! Sometimes when you win, you really lose, and sometimes when you lose, you really win. The world of competitive fighting is on a grandiose scale, one much higher than I ever expect you to understand! One, greater than I truly expect anyone in PCW to even begin to fathom! Fact of the matter is, many a man can hide a scorned past behind a keen smile, but for one to actually wear that smile and truly recognize that it comes to him or her naturally, is what life is all about! “So you hide behind your joking and playing around, you hide your scorned past away in this matter?” The reporter asks not fully grasping where A.J. is going with this. “No!” A.J. responds frustrated. “I hide nothing! I embrace my pass and thank the stars every day when I awake for the time that St. Jon and I spent together on this earth. With that said, I can’t bear the thought of living without the belief that I’ll never see him again, never walk along the ridge during dusk listening to his stories laden with knowledge that no man, woman, or child will ever know again. I strive for acceptance into the same afterlife that has found St. Jon so again we can be together; I have so much more to learn. “Wow,” the reporter responds bedazzled. “I never would have imagined you were so deep.” “Well, there is much to me that people don’t know, and most likely never will.” A.J. states looking up into the reporter’s eyes. “One thing I was taught in the early days with St. Jon is that you can see through to a man’s soul through his eyes.” “Oh yeah?” the reporter again wonders aloud. “And what, I beg, do you see in my soul?” “I see a battered soul, punished, one of great confusion, frustration, but over all, acceptance in what is real. I see a man who doesn’t love his job, hell; he doesn’t even like it, but understands that he must provide for his two sons and daughter. Lemme ask you something? A.J. finishes with a question of his own. “Go ahead.” The reporter welcomes the question, still a little confused about A.J.’s theory of his life. A.J.’s piercing green eyes scald the pupils of the reporter’s as Trinidad searches more deeply into the man’s soul before continuing. “Is it as hard as you make it to be, being a single father, raising three kids, with no wife at home to speak of?” The reporter simply sits in awe, not wavering his gaze from that of Trinidad. “It’s not your fault, you need to let go of her. Death holds no fault, it simply happens. She’s moved on, you must as well, for the children’s sake.” A.J. informs the reporter reaching out and grabbing his hand. “If you believe you will see her again, then it is destined to be so.” The reporter allows a tear to break from his creased eye before breaking his stare with Trinidad, “How could you have known?” The reporter mumbled in a broken voice. “More is revealed when you truly learn to look and listen, not simply just see and hear,” A.J. states while comforting the reporter. “Cut the tape” the reporter demands! The scene fades to black~
