Mono:- The Fifth:- Helplessness

22 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 22/05/03 “I only asked a simple bloody question, moron, is he there or not?” Lacuna fumed, her hazel eyes gleamed with frustration as the cut glass voice on the other end of the line delayed and delayed. So far all this son of a mongrel had done was delay, and Lacuna needed the answer. She pressed the phone harder to her ear, so hard that it indented her finely sculptured cheek, causing the blood to rush to the surface and illuminate it with an ungodly red glow. “I have no time for your idiotic games, dipshit, now answer my god damn question! Is he there or not?” There was the sound of shuffling on the other end of the line, as the man, evidently in his twenties and speaking in strong Received Pronunciation, searched for something. The burning glow in Lacuna’s eyes began to grow, developing a fiery tendency, which one could expect to fire out at any second, scorching anything in its path. “Sorrow moved out long ago, ma’am, one would expect him to be living in a box somewhere, for isn’t that where riff-raff like that sorry excuse for a man usually ‘hang’ as you so eloquently put it?” Lacuna tried to calm herself, closing her eyes to the early morning sun and pointing her head to the clouds while calmly counting backwards from ten under her breath. Strangely enough, and to her much surprise, it actually worked a little, the fire behind her eyes reducing itself to an ember that glowed rather than roared. “Well is there –any- way in which I could contact him, then?” the calm tone could not help in the hiding of the obvious bitterness as she imagined tearing the limbs from this jumped up monkey-boy. “One would suggest the Yellow Pages, now could you please leave me alone and let me get back to sleep before I call the police? That’s a good girl. Ta-ta now” and with that, the phone was hung up and Lacuna found herself listening so intently to a dial tone. “Fucking jumped up moron! Angelus would have him his P45 as soon as looking at him!” but she also knew Angelus would have never left that house without reason. Him moving out could not have been a choice; he loved the place too much for that. People wouldn’t break such a strong feeling for no reason, and Angelus was as stuck in his ways for a reason. Glancing around nervously, as if for any sight of Jade, she began to jab her finger at the keypad again, this time with far less excitement and far more anger than the last time. Swishing back her raven black hair, she eyed up the sheep which had given her the thought to ring Sorrow with a sharp, cutting glance. It made her feel better, strangely enough, although the sheep just looked back blankly. “Brook? Get me Sorrow’s number from the Internet, and don’t keep me waiting if you value your job” she ordered, the feeling of power seemingly restoring her confidence. Brook hesitated “Haven’t you got it? I remember putting it on your phone for you, ma’am” “Look at it this way, would I be wasting my time on you if I could get a hold of him?” she stopped abruptly, as if waiting for an answer from Brook, surprising the middle aged man as he usually doesn’t get chance top answer when Lacuna is in a bad mood. “Err…” He tried to get out, but Lacuna didn’t give him chance to even start his sentence before she interrupted. “The answer you are looking for is ‘No. Ma’am’. Now find me the god damn number before its more than your job on the line.” And she hung up the phone, feeling thoroughly more at home with herself. Being on the receiving end of a lashing over the phone was not her cup of tea, nor was being insulted by some upper class moron. She wasn’t upset, however, just very angry. She had nearly forgotten that Jade might well try and do what felt like killing her with some strange, mystical power again. She opened the door to the silver Mercedes and fell inside uncomfortably, landing hard on the soft leather, but bent in an awkward angle. Swinging her legs round, she closed the door loudly and sat in the relative darkness of the small sports car. She still couldn’t believe that Angelus had moved. He was the kind of man that, once settled, would be content with whatever he chose. Where he was had no bearings on his feeling, yet the comfort of his surroundings would soon become his shell from the world. He rarely changed his shell. And the phone rang. She flicked it open idly, her mood severely soured by the earlier events, but the slow spread of a smile could not be hidden as Brook read out an American address, clearly and precisely. So, she thought while driving along at great speed, Angelus had moved to America. Maybe this was a good idea for his career. He always valued his career above all else, and maybe that was his reason for moving. The things that confused her most was that his house was on a street. Sure, that’s fine with most people, but Angelus had more money than she did, and he despised human contact, so why he chose to surround himself with people in the centre of a damn city was anybodies guess. The cars navigation system told her that she was close to her target, and she was quite upbeat. She had not seen Angelus for a long time, and although it was hardly going to be a pleasure, she was renewing an old acquaintance and she was looking forwards to it. As she turned a sharp corner the navigation gave a beep, a quick glance giving her the indication that she had arrived at her destination. “Jesus, Angelus, what has become of you?” she muttered under her breath as she stepped out of the car, locking it quickly as she precariously eyed a gang of youths on the corner of the street who jeered loudly as she turned towards the small block of flats before her. They weren’t even nice flats. The old brickwork was stained in a torrent of graffiti and years of abuse from the elements and coated in a thick layer of grime. Once, she imagined, it would have looked nice, maybe even attractive. There were a set of heavily eroded stone steps leading to a pair of modern doors, the ones that are unlocked from the flats themselves. Hastily as if put of by her surroundings and the kids glaring at her, she approached the system of buzzers and pressed down hard on the one marked Sorrow. “I don’t want any” came the reply, said in a stone cold voice, tinted with an English accent. Lacuna smiled. He hadn’t changed a bit. “Why here, Angelus, why forsake the house?” There was a clank as the doors opened. He didn’t even reply to the question as the line went dead and she stepped into the neatly decorated hallway and began to ascend the stairwell. “I almost thought you were visiting out of the kindness of your heart for a second. I should have known all you would come here for would be regarding… her.” Sorrow spoke in a deadpan voice, not even looking at the intimidated figure of Lacuna who had settled down in a small chair in a dark corner of the room. He was pacing angrily up and down the dirt-caked carpet, his black locks of hair swinging from side to side as he turned and paced back in the opposite direction. His eyes were closed tightly as he paced, his giant arms crossed tightly over his muscular chest. His face was stretched and tired looking, quite pale in the half-light. “I really need your help here, you are the only one who can help me get through this” she pleaded. Angelus hadn’t changed. He didn’t like to help unless he was the one being benefited. Society had made him that way. He stopped pacing and open his deep brown eyes for a second, glaring right through Lacuna as he walked to a corner clad in shadows and emerged himself fully in the darkness until he could not be seen at all. “What happened?” “She disappeared, and I went looking for her, silly of me really, but she was my only friend. I couldn’t lose her that easily. I tried to break in the club she owned, did in fact, but she wasn’t there. The whole time I kept hearing these… voices in my head telling me she wasn’t there. I went back to the car, and there she was. Her hair had gone white and her voice had changed, and she told me to drive. I just did, I couldn’t do otherwise.” she explained hurriedly, she was well aware that there were details she had missed out, but she didn’t feel there was time to sit in Angelus’ dank flat explaining it all. “Is that all?” “No, not quite” she hesitated, well aware of how stupid it sounded in her head “On the way back, she went a little mad, blocking my thoughts. I tried to think of your name, as someone who would help me, but she flipped, some strange sort of electricity ran through me, buckling my muscles, but she fell unconscious, and then I did too. When I woke up, she was gone.” The sound of flesh rubbing softly against flesh insinuated he was stroking his chin, a habit all to regular for Sorrow, but he was too hidden in shadows to actually prove he was. “ I don’t know, I really don’t know. You must fid her. She is capable of too much to be left alone. She brainwashed me, costing me every penny I owned when I lost my job thanks to her and some stupid injury. I would also wish you god speed in finding her before she finds you. I have no clue what it is she is after, but expect the normal Jade to turn up sooner or later. You will think you have got somewhere. Never think you have got somewhere. This is all I know, unless you want to go out and kill Chryste, which was her problem last time, just find her before she finds you. Now get out, if she traces you here then I may have to kill her. She did too much to me for it to lie dormant. Go.”
