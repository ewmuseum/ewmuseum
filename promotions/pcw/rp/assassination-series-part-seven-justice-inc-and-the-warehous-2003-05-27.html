Assassination Series Part Seven: Justice Inc and the Warehouse O' Doom...

27 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 27/05/03 The entrance to the warehouse was relatively quiet, despite the size of Warrior’s mouth and the manner in which they gained entrance. The team had moved to the back of the building and Blind Rage had simply walked through the wall with considerable ease, which disappointed Warrior somewhat. He still favoured the giant wooden horse idea, but then again he would. Upon reaching the inside of the warehouse, they found themselves within the confines of a dark, but narrow corridor. The width of Blind Rage’s massive frame almost touched each wall of the corridor and he also had to lower his head a little to avoid smashing it against the ceiling. Blind Rage took a few steps along the corridor, before realising that Warrior wasn’t following him. He turned back to see nothing, except the glow of the afternoon sun, splash against the inside of the corridor wall in a rough shape of his own large frame. “Warrior? Where the hell are you?” Blind Rage whispered. With that Warrior popped his head in the corridor and eyed Blind Rage up cautiously. “I can’t come in!” he replied. Blind Rage grew angry. “What the hell are you talking about?” “I can’t come in because I’m afraid of the dark.” Blind Rage sighed. “Don’t worry about it, stay close to me…the corridor’s pretty narrow anyway.” Warrior’s eye’s widened. “How narrow?” “Pretty narrow.” Warrior backed off a little. “I definitely can’t come in then!” “Why?” “Because I’m claustrophobic too.” Blind Rage couldn’t contain his rage anymore. “Will you get your ass in here?” Warrior sighed then, reluctantly, stepped into the corridor and took up pace behind Blind Rage. Blind Rage took another step forward and felt his foot, and then his leg slipped through the floor, as the concrete under footing gave way. A large chunk of the floor fell endlessly to the pitch black pit below, but Blind Rage managed to grab a hold of the corner of the concrete floor as he fell. Warrior just stood there shocked, as Blind Rage pulled himself up from oblivion and clambered back onto solid ground. “Well Bugger me!” Blind Rage gasped, as he pushed himself up from the ground. “What in the hell is going on here?” Warrior asked, seemingly astonished at the event that had just transpired. Blind Rage eyed his partner up warily. “It seems the corridor has been booby trapped.” A smile developed upon Warrior’s face. “I bet you’re wishing we had gone along with that wooden horse idea after all, huh? Doesn’t seem so stupid now does it?” Blind Rage sighed. “I beg to differ. Tell me, if you’re so damned afraid of darkness and confined spaces, how the hell did you think you were going to cope in that damned wooden horse.” “I didn’t. I thought you would have done quite well though.” “Oh thanks.” “So what do we do now o’ mighty leader?” Blind Rage sighed. “We carry on.” Warrior simply stared at him for a second. “Say what now?” “We carry on.” “Listen buddy, you might be next to indestructible, but I ain’t, so count me out!” Warrior turned to leave, but Blind Rage grabbed his Lycra collar and jumped over the five foot gap in the floor. Understandably, Warrior screamed the entire way across, which seemed to take forever. “What the hell did you do that for you loon?” Warrior asked, in a decidedly angry manner. “We came here to do a job. We ARE going to do it if I have to drag you there myself, now come on!” Blind Rage replied, now becoming unreasonably angry himself. Warrior visibly deflated at this command and reluctantly follows Blind Rage along the narrow corridor. Blind Rage paused for a second, and Warrior accidentally bumped into the back of him, which caused him to stumble forward a little. Consequently, he stood on a section of the concrete floor, which also sank upon the pressure of his footstep. However, this time the step ceased and Blind Rage didn’t fall through the floor. Blind Rage angrily stared at Warrior. “Now look what you’ve made me do!” Warrior takes a step back, looking confused. “What did I do?” “You bumped into me and me stand this step!” “You can’t blame me…I didn’t even want to come this way!” Warrior declared. “Besides, nothing’s happened to you?” Blind Rage suspiciously looked around. “You’re right!” Confidently, Blind Rage removed his foot from the trick stone and took a step forward. Then he heard the noise. He turned, as a dozen or so darts fly towards him. He manages to evade a few of them with his superhuman reflexes, but the rest hit his near-indestructible torso with little or no effect. “Phew.” Blind Rage exclaimed, checking to see if his vitals were still intact. “That was close.” Blind Rage turned to face Warrior, unaware that just ahead of them a door had quietly opened and a rather large object had begun to head their way. Warrior saw the giant metal ball first. His eye’s widen at the sight of it and quickly he pointed it out for Blind Rage to see. “What the…” Blind Rage said, as he turned to see the giant ball heading towards him at a fairly fast pace. Warrior closed his eyes and waited for the inevitable to happen. And the inevitable did happen, except it didn’t transpire in the way Warrior fully expected it to. Blind Rage’s overpowering strength was more than a match for the giant ball. Blind Rage effortlessly stopped the giant ball in it’s tracks and propelled it back where it came from. The ball smashed through the wall and startled all three of the occupants. Never let it be said that Justice Inc never could make an entrance. It was time for the party to begin.
