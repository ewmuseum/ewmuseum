The Ghettos of Seville

15 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/15/03 The Ghettos of Seville The cold air whooshing by my feet, I look around and see a paper bag roll over my footstep. Dabs of alcohol still present as the aroma fills the air. Music is all around me, loud harmonious chants that could be heard all the way up to the heavens. I look on the ground in front of me before every step avoiding the vomit and waste all over the dirty pavement. Faces followed by faces, followed by more new faces. Everyday a different face appears, but the same remains. All are dirty, poverty stricken, clothes is weary, face is rough, and eyes are wise. They know the streets because they are the streets. Some come to Seville and see beauty and history, but these people see an empty future. Seville isn’t the great wonder people speak of but rather a pit of depression for those looking for a new life. So what do these people do in a place where nothing can change, they FIGHT! Day by day, hour by hour, the cold, the warm, at worst, at best, they give it their all just so those already wise eyes can live another day. Poverty isn’t just something in Seville poverty is everything in Seville. When I was a little boy I used to take bus rides down here because I learned it was the best place to make some money and find some shelters for a place to stay. What I found was a world of sadness. It was so lifeless and so desperate. I stood there for a while, but the mood drifted me away. Every night I dreamt of the sad faces, midnight fires, screams of illness, and tears of discomfort. So as I come back here four years later, I see nothing has changed. Different faces? Yes. Same Misery? Definitely. I wish it could all change, but the ghettos of Seville will always remain the same. Please sirs, I beg of you, lend me some money so I can eat tonight? I turned around and looked the man in the eyes. He avoided eye contact because I knew he was scared. I grabbed him face with the dirt from his cheeks leaving an imprint on my fingers. Leave here! Never return. I then grabbed his left arm and held it out. What are these marks and lines on your arms? Wait don’t answer, I know what they are. So you’ve showed me that you have the money to shoot up on heroine, but you don’t have the money for dinner. My friend right there is enough money to buy you a filet mignon for two. Do you have money for me or not? I have more than money I have my words. And even though you may not see it now, you will soon enough. Don’t talk to me like I’m a young fool. I’ve been on these streets for years, boy. Your age is just a word. You have no depth and nor does your mind. And may that be the same message for all of you. I looked around and saw the careless faces as they continued with their everyday activities as if I was nothing but another tile in a ceramic floor. I was where all of you were just four years ago, but I didn’t give up, I never even let it enter my mind. Sure I felt bad sometimes and thought I was granted a hellacious life, but I never let that get in the way of my dreams. Go to hell! No, I won’t go to hell, but you my friends you are already there. That’s right, this existence of yours is a hell on earth. I’ve said my peace. I knew my words were just a blur in their minds, but years went by with those words bottled up inside. I had finally released them and I was grateful that I had done it. If I gave money it would have just added to the hurt. It was a moment of joy, but a decade of tears. So rather I spoke the truth leading to a moment of hate, but a decade of wisdom. I am a young man, but that does not mean I am just any young man. I’ll make a change, and when I do I’ll remember the lives I’ve changed. I made my way out of the area, but before I was finally gone I noticed a woman sitting on a curb with a coughing baby in her hand. I went up to her and then realized that my approach may have not been the right way. Strict words won’t change them; it will just dig them farther in their hole. Leave here and always love him. I handed her twenty euros and pointed to the bus. She didn’t say a word, because she didn’t need to. She just followed what I said and was on her way. Right when I turned my back she called out to me. Yes? Thank You. Two simple words, but they were two simple words I starved to hear. Life goes on elsewhere outside the Ghettos of Seville. All it takes is a point in the right direction. The End.
