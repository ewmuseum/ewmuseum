Date: .... Eddy yawned and arose from his slumber inside a trucker’s 18 wheeler. The trucker was named Jack, and like always, he was smoking his cigarette. Eddy then pulled himself up and looked outside. This wasn’t Wyoming. It was more snowy, higher elevated, but didn’t know what it could be. 'Where are we?' 'Coloradda. Gotta make muhself a stop at this truck stop here, you won’t mind waiting for 15 mins, wontcha?' 'No…' Jack put his truck down near a big gas station. He stepped out. 'Didn’t really pump muhself up to well. Gotta pump more. Be back.' Jack slammed to door and walked up to the gas station. Eddy got out as well as a cold burst of air hit his skin. He shivered and looked around. It was Colorado, mountains were all over the place, it was snowy and cold, and you could feel an altitude difference. He had many memories of Colorado. Battles, mysteries, so many things he could remember. He went back into the car, grabbed a slip of paper and a pen, and pondered for a second. This is rather pointless and out of boredom, but doesn’t it seem memories are one of the best and worst things to have? In the good fashion, memories make us feel good inside, knowing all the time we have done something well and did something great, and those memories we cherish as we love back on them. I can remember how proud I was when I won my first match in wrestling. I always look back upon as a landmark, a goal, a point of time I can love. But then, on the bad side… Memories kill us. We remember everything bad we’ve done, like we cut a friend off or didn’t do something we should’ve done. Then, you find out that friend died or the thing you didn’t you was pretty big. I can remember past relationships, and I can’t enjoy the one I’m end without the thought of a past, ruined, and rotten one. You try your best not to remember, but we have them in our mind and they can’t be out Amnesia would be very good and very bad too have. We can get over the past, but we wouldn’t have even points of our lives we can actually remember that was good. So, reader, be in love with your memories and be hated by your binary past. Confusing, strange, and makes no sense to what is trying to come through. Make the best of your life. We all probably have done bad things in our lives, but just remember, even bad side has a good side, and every good side has a bad side, and nothing will be perfect unless you try your best to make it like that. Understand your memories are truthful, and don’t be dishonest with yourself before anything else, because inside, it always rains. Just try. Try your best. Whatever anybody else says, try your best. If anybody puts you down, try your best. Believe in yourself in EVERYTHING you do, but don’t have the biggest ego while doing it. Right after folding this paper and putting it into his pocket, Eddy fell asleep again. Very quickly too, because Jack didn’t even arrive back in the truck yet. About 10 minutes after that, Jack came back and started the 18 wheeler. He pull it out of the truck stop and out on the freeway. Eddy, awoke just a few minutes later, and stared outside. Then, he sighed, lied down, and fell asleep once again. He wasn’t even tired too. Eddy didn’t wake up in a boring brown fields or a mountain area, but his eyes sprung open to night time. Or very early morning, whichever it was. He glanced, Jack was asleep. Snoring like a pig. To the left was a truck stop and sleeping point. Eddy looked straight ahead, and chalked his eyes to an amazing site: Dallas, Texas. Also his hometown. Also, where his depressed sister lived. Eddy knew his sister would have answers for his dying parents questions. Skylar had said he talked to her and he was told then Eddy’s parents were dying. Eddy knew he had to get to his home and talk to her. He jumped out of the truck, without Jack even knowing, and looked around. It was cold, and Dallas was straight ahead of him. He had to walk along the freeway at midnight in order to get to Dallas Without thought, he ran. He ran full speed ahead. He didn’t look behind, or look above. Just straight in front of him, a bright city was there. He ran down the freeway. He ran with no jacket. No sweater, just a short sleeve when it was 25 degrees. But Eddy couldn’t bother caring. In the distance, a silhouette of black was running. The silhouette was there momentarily, then it disappeared behind fog and drizzle of the cool midnight air.
