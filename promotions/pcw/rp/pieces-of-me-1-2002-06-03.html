Date: June 3, 2002 Berkeley YMCA Berkeley, CA June 4th , 2001 - 10:47 am Splash, splash... The pool at the local Berkeley YMCA was lightly filled this day. It was a weekday, but usually this early in the morning, the pool would have brisk business with the everyday goers and hardcore swimmers getting up early to fill their day with a swim. Tom Lexian was in the fast lane, all by his lonesome, swimming freestyle. Although he was by himself, he still circle swam, swimming up the right lane and coming down the left lane. He found it easier to swim in this fashion, just in case another swimmer came into his lane while he was going through his routine. This made it less likely that they would inevitably run into each other. The chlorine of the pool had a light scent today. Tom liked that - whenever it was too strong, usually on a cold day, it would sting into his eyes and make his overall swimming experience less desirable. His eyes were especially sensitive to the stinging effects of chlorine - his goggles were his lifesavers. They were Speedo brands, just the best brand to keep the chlorine and swim water out of his eyes. They were boasted to be fog proof, although as far as Tom knew, no goggles were truly immune to the fog. Tom decided to do one more lap, and then call it quits for the day. He stopped at the far wall, pulling the goggles off his face, and looking around the pool deck a bit. There were only four other swimmers in the pool with him, all of them in the slow lanes. Tom didn't feel any superior because he was in the fast lane, but he felt that he had the speed to be able to keep up with the other lane swimmers. Besides, the fast lane was located in the middle of the pool, so it wouldn't have the 'pool pull' of the walls, drawing in the swimmers closer and closer to the lane lines. He gazed up to the clock, which read 10:50 am. Only ten more minutes and the pool would be closing anyways, so some of the classes could be held. Tom took a deep breath and stretched his arms. They were feeling a bit sore from so much arm work he had been doing in the pool, but that was only from rust. He hadn't gone swimming in a very long time, mostly because he just didn't have the time to pound out the hours in the pool. He remembered when swimming dominated most of his time in high school. Being on the team, he'd have to practice everyday, the first being the worst. The endless cramps, sore joints, and hard breaths only served as a reminder of how out of shape he really was. By midseason he'd be back in form, swimming better than ever, and being at one with the water. Some people laugh when they hear someone referring to the water as their home, but Tom felt that it was the only way to be a successful swimmer. You had to be able to go with the currents and feel the water, otherwise the resistance would be like swimming against a brick wall. He put the goggles back down on his face, and ricocheted off the wall to begin swimming. The water felt smooth and clear as Tom stroked slowly through the water. Although he was in the fast lane, he felt no pressure on himself to go any faster than he wanted to. There was no one behind him or in front of him to break up his focus or concentration, and this was just a warm down lap. As Tom came into the wall, finishing up, Tom heard a whistle blow and he stuck his head up from the water. The lifeguard had blown his whistle, signaling to all of the swimmers that it was time to get out of the pool. Tom obliged, and quickly made his way onto the pool deck. He grabbed his bag and towel which was on the far wall. As he dried off his face and shoulders, the lifeguard came over smiling. 'Did you have a good swim, Tom?' Tom nodded, wringing the water out of his ears. 'It wasn't too bad actually. Especially considering how rusty I am.' 'Yeah, it's been a few weeks since I've seen you down here,' the lifeguard remarked. 'Has it been pretty busy on the road?' 'A bit,' Tom replied, feeling a bit guilty. He knew that his time spent away from the house would only make it harder on him and Nat to maintain their steady relationship, but he knew that she had a stable career and could take care of herself. 'I have to say, I really enjoy watching you work on television. Most wrestlers don't come out in public very often...they're afraid of being bombarded by fans and the like.' Tom laughed. 'I don't think I'll have that problem, considering I don't have any fans.' 'Bah. You have plenty of admirers. I mean, who hasn't heard of the name Legend Inc.? 'Not many, I hope,' Tom said chuckling. 'How has business been around here?' The lifeguard shrugged, looking around the pool. 'It's really been off and on. I would think that this place would really start picking up during the summer, especially since school is out and everything, but it's been slow around here. I guess people have been going off to vacation or what not.' 'I saw a bunch of kids in front of the YMCA when I was coming in. I'm guessing those are the summer kids.' 'Oh yeah,' he said, scratching his head. 'We have a ton of those YMCA campers this year. I hope they didn't get in your way as you were coming in.' Tom didn't have much problem getting in, as he did trying to drive past all of the double parking parents that were dropping their kids of. He had encountered this problem a lot when driving near schools and such, but that wasn't too bad of a problem considering most of these schools were located in residential areas. Unfortunately, the YMCA was smack in the middle of DownTown Berkeley on a single lane street. 'No, not really.' 'Good,' he said, showing a relieved look on his face. 'We've been getting a lot of complaints recently about the kids going around the entire building, messing around and fussing about. It's been a bit of a nuisance. I'm just glad that I'm not on staff for the kids this year.' 'Well, kids will be kids.' 'And they also will be annoying,' the lifeguard responded. 'I must have been handling at least twenty kids at one time. When two of them weren't fighting, they were destroying the locker rooms, or getting stuck in the toilets....' The lifeguard sighed as he began to flashback to some of the worst experiences he had as a lifeguard. It was probably a very stressful job, and Tom was thankful that he didn't have to do it, although he felt like he was a babysitter sometimes in the wrestling locker rooms. Crying, bitching moaning...it was all in the job description when you're a wrestler. This wasn't the third grade anymore - no one was going to hand you a kleenex and wipe your nose for you when you're done crying. The locker room was instantly the first place a wrestler went to vent off his frustrations, which is why nothing in there is breakable. Well, except for mirrors...those seem to be broken with just about anything. If the lifeguard thought that children were annoying, maybe he should see what it's like to be a wrestler. 'Well, just be thankful that some other people are handling that side of things now,' Tom said, smiling. 'Damn straight,' he replied. 'I'm out man,' Tom said, extending his hand to the lifeguard. The lifeguard nodded his head and exchanged hand shakes with Tom, noting that he hoped to see him again next week. Tom replied that he hoped so too, but he couldn't promise anything. Tom grabbed his bag, heading into the locker room. As he opened the door, steam already began to pour out from the showers which had been fired up from everyone leaving the pool. Tom chose a stall near the door. He placed his bag on the rack, and turned his shower on to the hottest setting. As he waited for the water to heat up, he looked about the shower room to see that all of the showers were on, but no one was using them. It was an odd sight seeing all of them pouring water but no one under them. Tom went around shutting down all of the faucets, a bit concerned about water consumption. As he turned around back to his own shower, a little boy stood under it. He was completely dressed in his own clothing, which was now soaking wet. He was a young hispanic boy, with dark brown eyes and midnight black hair. He watched Tom's ever move, and even his facial expression, as he couldn't believe what he was seeing. 'What are you doing?' Tom asked, grabbing the boy from under the shower and placing him against the wall. Tom took a towel from his bag, wiping off the boys hair and face, hoping that he wouldn't catch cold. 'What's your name?' The boy was oddly silent, and would not answer any of Tom's questions, no matter how simple they were. Tom got done trying to dry the boy, and ushered him into the locker room area. Grabbing his bag, he corraled the boy towards his own locker. Tom noticed that the boy was barefoot, who wore a Transformers t-shirt and faded black jeans. Tom unlocked his locker, instructing the boy to take a seat. He opened his locker, searching for his extra towel that he kept in case he needed it. He found it under a large amount of clothing that had begun to stockpile in his locker. He gave it to the boy, who continued to look at Tom as if asking his own questions. Tom continued to get dressed, finding an old t-shirt and a pair of shirts for the boy to wear, and gave it to him. The boy didn't budge, just sitting there staring up at Tom. He was beginning to think that the boy was deaf or worse, but he didn't know what to do. Perhaps he was lost and he couldn't find his father...but why was he clinging on to him like a lost puppy? He finally got dressed, and as he was putting his shoes on, he looked back at the boy, who was still soaking wet and staring up at him. Tom shook his head slowly, taking his shirt and shorts and placing them back into the locker. If he didn't want to use them, then Tom supposed that they should go back into the locker. No use wasting them. 'Kid, why don't you talk to me? I'm trying to help you.' The boy opened his mouth, but then shut it. Tom was beginning to get excited, as he was actually showing signs of life. The boy began to speak, and then suddenly burst into laughter. Tom was stunned. 'I got you good, didn't I?' The boy said, still rolling around on the ground in laughter. 'Do you think that was funny?' Tom asked, obviously annoyed. 'You think playing around in the showers and pretending like you're deaf is a joke? I was trying to help you.' 'I know, I know,' the boy replied. 'But I couldn't help myself...I had to see for myself if you really were the person to help me.' 'You need help? That's hard to believe,' Tom said sarcastically. He picked up his bags, and headed past the boy, heading out of the locker room. The boy stood up suddenly, following behind Tom. 'Where are you going? You can't go! I need your help.' 'You showed me that you don't need anyone's help,' Tom replied, not even looking back at the boy. 'Why don't you try and fend for yourself for once?' The boy watched Tom stomp away angrily. He came down the hallway and then quickly disappeared out the door. He suddenly realized that it perhaps was not the best idea to have tried to joke around with him, although he was told that Tom Lexian was a comical person. The boy shrugged, deciding his next course of action...
