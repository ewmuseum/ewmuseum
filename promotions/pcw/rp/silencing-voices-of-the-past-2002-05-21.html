Silencing Voices of the Past...

21 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5-21-02 So, Heaven Rayne has finally made it to PCW, after spending her time in the Academy. Now that she’s made it to the Primetime, she should be happy and ready to go out and give it her all, right? Wrong. Having a match against Jaden and Anna Keyson in the very first Friday Fusion, since the evolvement, isn’t really on Heaven’s mind at this point in time.... The day is an average spring day. It is not too humid nor is it too chilly. The setting is Heaven Rayne’s backyard. Heaven is sitting on her small wooden deck with her legs stretched out, a scrapbook in front of her. Her shoulder length sandy blonde hair is pulled back at the sides with a silver barrette and the only makeup she wears is a touch of mascara accenting her beautiful sapphire blue eyes. She is wearing denim blue jean shorts and a plain long sleeved light blue t-shirt with navy blue sleeves. She is bare-footed and the warm sun feels good on her toes as she wiggles them in the wind. She smiles to herself as she flips through the tattered pages of the large, ugly, brown family scrapbook. The pages have gone yellow from the acid in the paper and the pictures still appear clear, though the people in them and the places they are…look very outdated. Heaven laughs happily as she reminisces through all of the history. It has been three weeks since Heaven first learned she is an angel. After all of the shock and stages of denial she has come through, she is now beginning to accept it all. Now she is just curious and eager and trying to recall everything she can about her childhood and how normal it all once seemed. She thinks maybe by studying her past, she can somehow find missing pieces to the puzzle. Maybe, she can evoke some things from her memory that would prove she is an angel, things that she overlooked as an innocent, naive little kid. She flips to the twenty-third page, and the picture on the bottom right hand corner shows her in her pink and white striped bathing suit. She is wearing orange, plastic child floating devices. Her hair is wet and stuck to her head as she smiles for the camera. She looks as though she is having the time of her life splashing through her little blue baby pool at six years old. Heaven recollects that this picture was taken the day after she crashed into her grandmother’s porch with her bicycle. Her parents bought her this new bathing suit to cheer her up. “If I remember right….yeah,” Heaven says, with her left eye squinted and her head tilted to the side, trying to confirm her thoughts. “I did get a black eye from that bicycle incident…my whole face was scratched up too, cause after I hit the porch, I fell sideways into the shrubs. I’ll never forget that.” Heaven studies the photograph more closely to spot out any scratches or bruises. There are none seen to the eye. “Maybe…,” Heaven says, “This wasn’t the day after that bike crash. Well, I was pretty sure, because I only got to wear this swimsuit once since my mother unintentionally put it in the pile to give to charity. The day I wore it was the day after I got it, and I got it the day I wrecked my bike.” Heaven realizes that this is a picture of her the day after wrecking her bike into her grandmother’s porch, and there is no shiner on her eye and no scrapes. This is proof that what the angel in white told her was true. Heaven sighs and continues to flip through the scrapbook. After a while, her legs become sore from the position she’s sitting in, and she is uncomfortable from sitting on the hard, wooden deck. “For as long as I’ve been sitting out here, it’s a shame that it’s not a hot enough day to get a sun tan,” Heaven says to herself as she stands up and stretches. She brushes of the back of her legs and yawns. She bends back down to close the scrap book, intending to close it, but something catches her eye. She sits back down with the book. There is a picture of her and Mick, her childhood next door neighbor sitting on a yellow and white striped beach towel. They are eating popsicles and wearing brief little summer clothes. Heaven estimates the two of them to be about four years old… A tear drop emerges from the corner of Heaven’s left eye, and drips down to land on the picture. Heaven becomes disheartened yet again, thinking about her friends. She wonders about Sarah, marveling if she is still in a coma. An idea comes to Heaven after that thought… “Now that I’ve learned the facts about myself, I’m no longer so angry with fate. I can no longer blame God for Jason dying, given that I know the truth about him. I don’t think it would be quite as painful to go home now. Maybe I should go home for a visit, and find out about Sarah. If she is out of her coma, I’m sure it would brighten her day to see an old friend. After all, she and I are the only two that survived the car crash. Surely Sarah is out of the coma by now, it’s been two years. Hmmm…I wonder. Anyway, I’ve seen my mom. Maybe I should visit my dad while I’m in town, too. I’d sure like to hear his point of view of how I came to them as a baby,” Heaven’s plan evolves as she sits and thinks. She decides that she will go home for a visit. She stands up once again with the dilapidated old family scrap book in her arms. She opens the patio door and steps inside to find Cloe, her kitten, has been taking a nap under the deck. The sound of the door opening alerts her, and she is now running in the house ahead of Heaven. Heaven walks inside and closes the door. She sits down at the kitchen table in the dining room, joint with the kitchen. She places the scrapbook down and grabs the small, yellow tablet of sticky notes and a patriotic looking pencil from the center of the table. She begins to make a list of things to do and people to see while in Nevada. TO DO LIST: • Unpack at home • Find out about Sarah • Get flowers and balloons for Sarah • Buy flowers for Jason and Mick’s graves • Visit their burial sites • Visit Jason’s family • Visit Mick’s family • Visit Sarah’s family • Get dad away from he attorney’s office long enough for a talk • Shop with mom • Go by grandparent’s house • Go to church on Sunday Heaven looks up from her writing, satisfied with her list. She feels proud of herself for the last item she added to her list and figures as long as she’s letting go of old grudges, she might as well bury old hatchets. She will go to church with her family while she’s in town. She hasn’t been to church for two years and who knows? It may be pleasant. A smile comes to her face as she realizes that she’s becoming so mature about everything. She walks over to the kitchen counter and grabs the black cordless phone from the base, straining to remember her parents’ phone number. Giving up, she goes into her bedroom to search through her dresser drawers for the little sheet of paper containing the number. The scene skips ahead to the next day. Heaven appears cruising though Las Vegas, Nevada, in a black jaguar with a drop top, large wheels, and shiny silver spokes. Inside is tan leather interior. Heaven has just established a new sound system in her car, and she is blaring music to block out the stares of the people that admire her expensive car as she passes by. The loud music also helps to block out the bad memories of the past from the sin city. She pulls up to her parents’ house having been on the road for four hours. She is relieved to get a rest as she opens her car door and surfaces from the inside. As typical of spring, the weather is always unpredictable from one day to the next, and today it is windy and a little chilly. Although Heaven wore shorts yesterday, today, she is dressed in black pants and a thin red sweater that ties at the top. She is wearing black dress boots and black rimmed sunglasses. Her blonde hair is loose, and with the wind is wiping uncontrollably around her face. Heaven pushes the small black button on her key ring with a white illustration of a car trunk. Before she has a chance to get all of her luggage out of the trunk, her father appears at the door of the large white house. Here stands a man she’s always admired, but a man who has spent the last ten years of his life trying to climb the success ladder instead of being the family man he once used to be. Russ Rayne is a tall lean man, with a large receding hair line, and his black hair thin at both sides, and a round patch on top. As he was the day Heaven left Nevada two years ago, her father is in a gray suit and black tie. He comes down the four broad stone steps to help Heaven with her suitcase and small bags. “Hi, Sugar,” says Heaven’s dad, using the old familiar pet name he did when Heaven was a little girl on his knee. “Hi, Daddy,”Heaven answers quietly with her head down. She wonders how he can be all smiles and act like the loving father he once was before the work took over. In her mind she thinks how the present expression on his face reminds her of the one he wore when she used to come home from grade school. But then again, by about the fourth or fifth grade he became less and less daddy like, and by the time she was in junior high, they almost never spoke. Sadness fills Heaven at this reflection of the past, but she is interrupted when her father clears his throat. “So, you want help with your things? Or, are you going to just stand there staring?” He asks in a tone of voice that startles her and makes her realize how much time she is wasting by daydreaming. “Yeah, I could use a little help,” She says, finally realizing what is going on. She turns around and lifts the open trunk lid, pulling out a large green suitcase with “Heaven” printed in gold letters, and three small blue plastic bags of extra stuff she has brought along. Her father picks up the heavy green suitcase as Heaven shuts the trunk lid and grabs her three small blue bags. They don’t speak at all until they are inside the living room. As Heaven sits in the familiar beige recliner, she grows uncomfortable, trying to decide what to say to begin a conversation with her father about her past. She has so many questions left unanswered, and so many things to say to him, as she tries to conjure up something to say. The scene jumps to Heaven Rayne’s parents’ house, and the setting is the front room. Heaven is still trying to summon the words to begin all of the questions for her father that she holds inside. Her black sunglasses are now pushed up as she sports them on top of her head, resting neatly on her dark blonde hair. She sits in the tan recliner which was her favorite piece of furniture while she was growing up. As she sits, she nervously fidgets with her hands that are daintily painted with red polish. Heaven clears her throat and shifts her eyes away from her hands and toward her father on the couch adjacent from her. “So,” she says in a voice showing that she is trying to be casual and cool before getting into the great issues she is determined to eventually discuss. For now she is going with small talk as she continues with, “I see you and mom have done quite a bit of renovating in the two years I’ve been gone.” “Yes,” states Russ Rayne, “In fact, that decrepit old chair you are sitting in is the only piece of furniture we kept. Your mother insisted on keeping that ratty chair, because she said it held sentimental value, being your favorite. Heaven laughs remembering all of the nights she spent curled up on this very chair reading or watching television. Her father smiles and looks uncomfortable realizing that with the lack of interest he has shown in his daughter for the past ten years, he has no idea what to say to her. He very much wants to have a relationship with Heaven, but he really doesn’t know her in the way a father should know his daughter. Heaven’s dad begins asking her irrelevant questions in order to break away from the abrasive silence. As he does so, Heaven’s eyes wonder around the room inquisitively, exploring the new decor. The once white walls are now darker, almost a caramel shade, and there is a modern boarder around the top and bottom of the ceiling. A man-made fireplace has been added in on the far right wall, and the curtains have changed from big and frilly and country blue, to simple and straight and a dark purple. The furniture is now dark oak, and the dark shades and warm hues look excellent, but Heaven feels sad remembering the way everything was decorated when she grew up. It appears to be a different house. After a few minutes, when it seems her father’s rambling has come to a halt, Heaven decides it is time to get down to business and say the things she has intended to say. “Dad, did you know that mom came by to visit me a few weeks ago?” “Yes,” he looks down as though he is ashamed. “If you are sore at me for not coming along, I apologize, but I was just occupied. I hope you understand.” “Oh….yeah dad, I understand, that’s not why I mentioned it,”says Heaven a bit nervous now, actually bringing up the dreaded subject. “I just wondered if mom had discussed anything with you about the visit.” Russ appears nervous as well, now becoming aware of the fact that Heaven is about to bring up the one thing that, unfortunately, he has always known would eventually come up. “Yes, actually, she did. I was wondering if you would want to talk about that.” “So, is it true, dad? I’m I really a…an angel?” “Your mother brought you home one night…she was dirty and beat up. She told me the story of the man who mugged her on the streets of New York that night and the angel who saved her life and brought you. At first the story was a bit hard to believe, as I’m sure it was hard for you to accept as true at first as well. But your mother could not conceive children and I…I was very desperate to have a child. She and I were at the time seriously considering adoption. So naturally, I very much wanted to believe her. It seemed that our prayers had simply been answered, and that you were a miracle. In time, it was proven that you weren’t an average child. When most children get hurt, their parents kiss their “boo-boos” and they feel better, thought the cuts and scrapes don’t really go away that easily. With you, it seemed that even though the kisses we gave weren’t what healed you, your injuries went away just as quickly as though they were healed by the kisses. You never stayed hurt for very long and nothing unpleasant ever fazed you much. After a while, to tell you the truth, we sort of forgot about you being an angel. We just considered you our own flesh and blood and went on with our lives. Then out of nowhere when you are twenty years old, your mother claims she has had a dream that it is time to tell you the truth. She said the same angel from twenty years ago came to her again. This time she said that you are coming to the age where you are a fully developed adult, and you will not age anymore, as the angel said would happen eventually all those years ago. She said it was time to tell you, because you would be alarmed by your inability to age pretty soon. That’s when the memories came back…” “Dad,” Heaven says, breaking up his speech, suddenly feeling a bit angry on account of the one thing that did affect her negatively as a child, “If you were that thankful to have me, having thought you wouldn’t get to have children, how could you forget that easily and become so careless?” “Honey, people have a way of just accepting things and certain things just become ordinary and taken for granted. Yes, we should have been more thankful to God and to the angel who protected your mother and brought you to us, but we are only human. We make mistakes,” her father says, obviously misunderstanding her. “I’m not just talking about that. I’m talking about how involved you became with your career and how little time you actually spent at home after I was…what, ten years old? By the fourth or fifth grade you were never there after school, at dinner, or at bedtime. The only time I saw you was in the morning at breakfast, and even then you couldn’t be disturbed because it was so important to read your “Wall Street Journal” and make sure you didn’t miss anything important that your big shot buddies would be discussing. And you never came to see me do anything of importance, like win awards, or sing in the second-grade play. You worked even when you didn’t have to, and I never understood it. It hurt me to see you go from such a fatherly man to so cold towards me. It broke my heart…I never had the courage to bring it up to you, but now that I am an adult and I’ve been put through a lot of hard things, I finally can face this, and I can confront you. I feel like I need some answers.” “Wait, wait, wait…did you come here to ask me about you being an angel, or to dig up old hard feelings at me for being a bad father? Look, like I said, we make mistakes, and Sugar, lately I’ve been having a lot of regrets about the mistakes I have made. But don’t judge me, Heaven, until you’ve walked in my shoes. I was thankful that I had you, and I loved my family very much…so much that I wanted to provide for you and your mother along with making you proud of me. I thought if I dedicated my life to providing for my family, they could have nicer things and they would always have every thing they ever needed or wanted. So, I spent all my time doing so.” The more I buried myself into the work, the more successful I became at it, and the more I realized that I even enjoyed it. I was becoming a very good lawyer. I was getting to be very professional, and I was well liked too. I was being accepted with very well to do people and it was exciting, and I enjoyed it. I guess eventually, I did make the career itself a higher priority than my family, and it wasn’t until your car wreck that I realized how much I took you for granted.” Heaven still wants to shout things out, like why didn’t he ever take her to therapy for her legs after the wreck, if he was so sorry? Why did he continue to bury himself into his work? But…her heart softens, and she decides against saying any of those things. “Daddy,” Heaven says, calling him as she used to when she was the little girl who was close to him. She now feels a little sympathetic with her father, and at the same time she feels sad. “I would have understood if you would have sat down and talked to me, and explained the reasons you had to work such long hours. I would have understood if you would have spoken more than two words to me a week. But, Daddy, I just flat out felt ignored after awhile.” “I’m sorry… I realize now that I was wrong in the way I handled things, and I hope you can forgive me, Headoo…” Heaven hears the sincerity in his voice. “You didn’t have to be a big time lawyer to impress me. All you had to do was be my daddy, and I would have been proud. I didn’t care about having everything I could need or want. What I needed and wanted most was my daddy to be around like he was when I was little. Instead, I had everything money could buy, and was still unfulfilled. Then Jason came along, and he made me happy despite everything…but that was ended, too.” I got everything together, and decided to pursue my wrestling dream. I got hard and cold against everyone and everything until I discovered the truth…about me being an angel. I decided that you are the most serious and professional person I know. If anybody is telling me the truth, it’s you. I knew that, and that’s why I wanted to hear your side of how I came to you as a baby, and how I grew up as a normal kid when I really wasn’t. I knew that even though the story sounds silly, if you say it’s factual, than it must be. That’s why I came home for a visit. I needed you consent to this being true, and I figured while I was here, it would be a good time to confront you about the way you indulged yourself in work for half my life. After all, now I have the guts to do or say anything, that’s the type of person the events in my life has made me.” Her father looks down, and Heaven thinks she sees a single, small tear slide down the bridge of his nose as he quickly lifts his glasses and wipes the evidence of remorse from his face. “I’m so sorry, Heaven…so sorry I wasn’t around.” Heaven hops out of her tattered old recliner and steps across the room to the couch where her father sits, giving him a hug, feeling relieved just to do so. The embrace is very comforting, and makes Heaven feel the way she did when she was a little child, very secure. “Hey dad,” she finally says, just to break the silence as she is suddenly reminded of something. “Did you ever hear what happened to Sarah? Did she ever get out of her coma?” “No, unfortunately, last I heard she was still in the coma. I’m sorry, dear...” Heaven rises to her feet. “Well, I think I’m going to go lie down before supper. Is my room still there?” Heaven asks jokingly because of all the remodeling she’s seen so far downstairs. “Sure thing, you know if your mother had to keep that ratty old chair on your account, she wouldn’t dare touch your room.” They both laugh faintly. “You know, we sure do miss you here, Heaven…” With that the scene fades to black as Heaven goes upstairs to lie down. When she wakes up, she has a sense of comfort, because she feels like she has patched things up with her dad, removing old skeletons from secret closets. Also, she has spoken to him about being an angel, and because he has said it’s true, she knows deep in her heart that it must be. Now the rest of her visit can be enjoyable. She will now be able to relax when she and her mother take their trip to the mall. She just has a few more things to get taken care of. She really wants to visit her grandparents because she feels guilty for not seeing them for two years. She wonders if they have any idea about her being an angel. She also needs to go to church on Sunday before she leaves. As for now she is trying to decide whether or not she should visit Jason, Mick, and Sarah’s families. She really thinks she ought to show her concern by visiting, but she wants to do it without stirring up any pain. Heaven hurries to the beautiful large bathroom that she used to call her own. The bathroom has a large double sink and the bathtub is luxurious with jets. Heaven smiles to herself as she fills the bathtub with warm water, adds bubbles, and turns on the jets. “No place like home,” says Heaven, smiling to herself, realizing that she can truthfully say that without reliving old haunting memories. Now that she knows the truth about Jason, she no longer has to mourn over him, and making peace with her father seems to make up for any sadness that she ever felt as a child because of his neglect. Heaven now feels that she is complete and can go on with her life and her wrestling career, in PCW. For the last few weeks, ever since Heaven discovered her identity, she has been taking a certain concept into deep consideration. She has wondered if she really has a calling to be an angel. She had already accepted the idea after her dream the day that the angel in white spoke to her, but after hearing her father testify its truth, she has now erased any doubt that had ever crossed her mind. Heaven has a thought… “If…I fulfill God’s plan and do the work I am called to do as an angel…I know it will help me to overcome any sorrow and guilt I have left from the horrible, horrible night of June 17, 2000, when we had the terrible wreck that shattered and changed lives. At least I know it will make me feel so much better. It will silence my voices from the past….those voices being Mick and Sarah’s. I have to. I have to do as I am called….” Fin
