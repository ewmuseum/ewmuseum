Return from the Brink

20 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.

In his stomach, knots of nervous energy tumble furiously like a washing machine on spin cycle. He smacks his tongue against the dry roof of his mouth and a slight ringing noise like that of metal chimes is heard as the ice in his drink clinks against the glass. For some reason, Jim thinks about ass porn. Then with a disgusted look on his face he downs the shot of Maker’s Mark with sickening ease. Everything has gone against the Tombstone, Arizona native recently. While his half-brother Ben Nighthorse Campbell went out in the world and did some good during Redemption Song’s self-imposed exile from PCW by speaking on behalf of the National Organization for the Advancement of American Indians, Surge himself had allowed his self-pity to take control and instead he did nothing. Well, that’s not entirely true. He worked off a lot of his anger in the gym. And he worked out a lot of anger in the bar. Jim (slight slurring drawl): Hey man, I think I need one more for the road. The bartender looks up at Surge, the long oak bar is empty save for the bartender’s old cocker spaniel that lies down flat and sat at the feet of Jim’s barstool. The Irish bartender shifts uneasily at the cowboy’s request and takes an anxious drag from a Marlboro cigarette before answering. Bartender: Uh, Mr. Surge, I don’t want to sound rude but you told me to tell you when it was 10am because you said you had to go meet your brother or something? Surge closes his eyes and gnashes his teeth in anger before letting out a thunderous sigh that would be appropriate of Bill Clinton being dragged away from a sorority party. His hand no longer shaking, he raises his head and opens his eyes, letting the bartender see his demeanor shift to one of casual friendliness. Jim: Thanks then, partner. He’d probably put some sort of Indian dance curse on my soul if I didn’t get there to pick his sorry ass up. Jim throws a twenty-dollar bill onto the sturdy oak bar and gets up, petting the dog on his way out. Outside, a driver is waiting for him in front of a black BMW limousine. Jim can’t quite help but chuckle as he gets into the limo. What an absolutely absurd year. Plucked from the depths of independent wrestling obscurity by an international sports marketing firm owned by his uncle, thrust into the spotlight through the untimely death of his uncle as Chairman of the Watkins Group, set-up to take the fall for burning Churches in Canada by his own manager and lawyer and ditched by his longtime valet Misty in favor of Cowabunga Connections’s Totally Tubular Tommy. A self-imposed exile seemed like the only option for the deflated cowboy to get his shit back together and focus all of his self-pity into a rage. A deep, overriding rage that was now dripping from every syllable he spoke, dripping in every bead of sweat, dripping like the blood that would be shed in PCW upon Redemption Song’s return. Jim gets into the limo and the driver hands him a fax. Jim: What the fuck is this? What the FUCK is THIS?? Driver: Hey, it just came through and I thought you’d want to see it as soon as possible. Jim looks down at the fax that shows that Redemption Song is scheduled to participate in the Alliances 2K2 Main Event – with Cowabunga Connection as their teammates!! Jim: I can’t believe this garbage! This was probably that damn Indian’s doing! What the hell does he think he’s doing? This will not stand, man. All this behind my back crap just won’t stand! Driver: I feel for you Mr. Surge. Should we get to the airport now? Jim: No. Screw that redneck. If he has something to say, he can come here and find me. Surge opens the limo door and heads back into the bar. Bartender: Forget something boss? Jim: Yeah, forgot how much of an asshole my half-brother is. Just always using the Redemption Song name to promote himself and whatever other crap he’s up to with the teepee building and whatnot. Bartender: So I just saw a commercial for that Alliances show? They didn’t mention you on air but there was a picture of you on the screen. Jim: Shut up please and get me a Sierra Nevada. Bartender: I’m just saying, maybe this is a chance for you to clean up a bit and make a name for yourself. You’re always going on about being undefeated CHZ Tag-Team Champions and how no team out there now can do what Redemption Song can do. Why do you keep sabotaging yourself? Jim: Shut the hell up Pete, and get me a beer. This ain’t a tea party, I thought this was a bar. Bartender: Just think about what I said, mate. Jim: Yeah, I’ll think about it.
