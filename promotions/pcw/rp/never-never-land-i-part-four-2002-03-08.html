Never Never Land I: [Part Four]

8 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 8, 2002 “How much farther?” Tom Lexian’s knees felt weak, as if they were ready to double over and fall off. Not exactly a pretty image, but it fit the way he was feeling. They had been walking for hours, although it reality it was only about thirty or forty minutes. “Not too much longer,” Belly answered, who was surprisingly not breathing hard or sweating at all. At his size, he should have been a wreck. Instead, it was Lexian who was the wreck. He continued to stop for a moment and re-gather his composure, falling behind and stumbling through the rough vegetation. At one point, he went down on one knee, closing his eyes and beginning to wheeze. “Tom, Tom…what are you doing?” Bobby asked, who was being held in Lexian’s arms, but rolled away as he fell to the ground. “I don’t feel good,” He replied, with a painful grimace on his face. “I’m not breathing right…” “You’re telling me. I can hear your lungs just taking in 200 cc’s of oxygen there. At least, I think it’s oxygen. No telling what these Never Never Land dodos use for breathing.” Lexian laid down on the wet grass, leaning his head on a nearby tree trunk. His eyes are closed, as his breathing slows. “I don’t understand…I’m in great shape.” “I told you to lay off all those twinkies.” Bobby remarked. Lexian rolls his eyes, but doesn’t quite have the energy to chastise Bobby. A few moments later, Belly and Bat come back after realizing that Lexian had been left behind. “What’s wrong with him?” Bats asked. He poked Tom in the shoulder with his finger, which Tom slapped away quickly. Bats whipped his hand back in pain. “Ouch!” Bats exclaimed. “Gosh, I thought he was unconscious. His senses seem to be working just fine, though.” Belly scratches his chin and bends down near Tom’s body. He places his hand on Tom’s forehead, feeling his temperature. He then rubbed Tom’s cheeks together, and they instantly colored red. He then took Tom’s hand and slapped it, immediately causing Tom to cough. Belly shook his head. “What?” Bobby said. “What is it? Why are you shaking your head?” “He’s got the Dewberry sickness…” Belly stated sadly. He shook his head in despair as he said it. “Oh no!” Bats replied, shaking his head as well. “Not the Dewberry sickness!” “What in the hell is the Dewberry sickness!?” Bobby screamed, trying to figure out what they were talking about. “It was discovered a short time ago,” Belly said, beginning to retell the history of the disease. “One of the lost boys came back to the encampment just like this. He was tired, out of breath, dizzy….we thought he had just been hit by some pirates or something. But it lasted for days and days. He was twitchy, just like Tom is now, and had a fever. We called it the Dewberry disease.” “But why?” “Because eventually, he’s going to have blue dots all around his body for a time. That’s when you know you’re near healthy again, but it’s a scary stage.” Lexian began to stir, moaning in his near sleeping stage. Bobby sighed. “This doesn’t look good. Not good at all. How long is this going to last?” “A few days probably,” Belly answered. “We’ll know more as time goes on, but for now, I wouldn’t hold my breath.” “Don’t let him operate heavy machinery,” Bats said, reminding Belly. “Remember what happened last time?” Bobby snickered. “What happened?” “The last Dewberry boy who operated heavy machinery wound up cutting a new door in Peter Pan’s room. Peter wasn’t too happy about that.” “I bet.” Lexian stirred again, this time, with his eyes open. He struggled back to his feet, using his legs to push himself alongside the tree’s trunk. Belly tried to help him, but Tom brushed him away with his free arm. “Don’t touch me,” Tom said groggily. “I’m supposed to be a tough guy now, so I can’t have you lost boys helping me at all. I’m the man here, okay? Now, let’s go find some pirates and beat them up.” Tom smiled as he said that, but tumbled back down to the ground, landing directly on his face. The thud was rather loud. “Well, well, well…” A voice called out from the distance. Bats, Belly, and Bobby (The triple B’s) turned around, all to watch Captain Hook and Smee, his ultimate crony, step out from the woods. He was decked out in elegant attire. A long, red suit and a smashing hat which went vertically a good foot and a half. And on his side was his swash buckling sword, probably used to slay at least a thousand Lost boys. And of course, the hook itself. It glistened brightly, revealing beauty and danger at the same time. “HOOK!” Belly and Bat yelled out in unison. “Hook?” Bobby said disbelieving. “As in, Captain Hook?” “In the flesh, my friends…” Hook said with a broad smile. He looked around for a few seconds, feeling bedazzled. “Okay, who in the bloody hell said that anyways?” “I did,” Bobby replied. “Who said that?” Hook repeated, now becoming a bit paranoid. “Show yourself!” “I’m right here!” Hook is genuinely annoyed now, and grabs Smee by his shirt. “Do you hear those voices too? Is it those Lost boys playing tricks on us again?” “It could be sir,” Smee said, strangely afraid. “But I didn’t see their mouths moving. How could they speak if their bloody moves weren’t moving? Can fairy dust do that?” “What do I look like? A fairy expert?” “It’s the cactus,” Belly said, his hand on his sword. “He’s talking.” Hook laughed loudly, almost pushing Smee over in the process. “Child, are you off your bloody rocker? How in the bloody hell can a plant be speaking to Hook…or even, Captain Hook! It’s bloody impossible!” “You can bet your bloody butt I’m talking, you ignoramus.” Bobby retorted with a chuckle. Hook gasped. “Smee….Smeee….Smee….that plant is talking. How is it talking?” “I…I dunno, sir.” Smee answered truthfully. “I’ve never seen anything like this before. Plants don’t have mouths.” “What kind of sorcery is this?” Hook demanded of Belly and Bats. “Are you so desperate to destroy me and the ring that you would create freaks of nature using fairy dust and household plants?” “You’re the freak of nature, Hook,” Bobby replied, laughing all the while. “I mean, you don’t even have two hands. You have a hook! Go back to the circus.” Hook shook with anger as his face billowed with fury. Smee tried to calm him down, tugging away at his sleeve, but Hook merely shoved him back down to the ground. Smee went flying into the shrubbery. Both Belly and Bat unsheathe their swords, trying to fight off Hook, but Hook responds with an odd smile. “You think you can handle Hook?” He asks, his sword now actively displayed. “Let’s see you try.” “In the name of the Lost boys, we will destroy you!” Belly lunges forward with a sword strike to Hook’s head, but he ducks, quickly sending a kick to his gut, which causes Belly to double over. He responds to the kick with a knee shot to the face of Belly, who lands on his back unmoving. Hook spits on Belly’s body, turning his attention over to Bats. “My young boy,” Hook said mockingly. “What are you planning to do to the Hook? You want to kill me? Is that it? Are you going to try to take me out?” Bats stood frightened, looking down at his fallen friend, Belly, from time to time. Hook sneered. “Don’t look at him! Look at me! I am your enemy, Bats. I am the man you want to kill. Can you do it? Can you kill me?” “I will kill you…” Bats said firmly. “Then let’s see you do it…” Hook answered. He lunged forward with an overhead sword shot, which Bats is able to block, and replies with a swiping shot to Hook’s arm. Hook staggers back from the cut on his arm, and grabs at it, feeling the blood drip from the torn fabric. Hook looks back at Bats, who is still nervous, but ready to attack again. He smiles, wiping his hand off. “That was good, Bats. Maybe I underestimated you.” “Underestimate this…” Bats replied, swinging wildly at Hook’s head. Hook blocked the shot, and kicked the sword away, swinging Bats around and placing his hook dangerously close to his throat. Bats shook in fear, as he can feel the cold steel of the hook encircling his veins and arteries. “You see, Bats,” Hook begins to say. “I am a swordsman. And you’re a boy. And you are good, very, very good. Peter has taught you well. But you see now, you are not better than me. None of you are. And when this is all over, I shall kill you all. Do you understand that?” Bats didn’t answer. Hook smashes the blunt part of the hook against Bats head, knocking him out cold. He gets up, now glancing over in Bobby’s direction. “Well, if it isn’t the cactus…” “You stay away from me,” Bobby says, a hint of fear in his own voice. Bobby wasn’t usually scared of anything, but watching Hook take out those Lost boys without any problem was distressing. “Don’t be afraid, my little cactus,” Hook said, sarcasm dripping from his voice. “I shall not hurt you, yet. I know you’re a pet of Thomas’. If I hurt you know, it would take away all of the fun we’re going to have.” “What do you mean?” “Oh, you’ll see,” Hook replied, a smile crossing his voice. Hook sheathes his sword, not feeling the need to have it out against a plant. “You like surprises, don’t you? I have the biggest surprise of them all coming soon. I’m sure you, Thomas, and all of Never Never Land will love to see it. It’s to die for.” “Nice foreshadowing, you pirate scum,” Bobby shot back. “We already know your plans. You’re trying to bring the Ghost Pirates back.” Hook laughs, his voice echoing in the woods. “That’s SO not everything. I thought the Lost boys had more intelligence to figure out my plans. That’ll make the surprise much more sweeter than. Tell me, young cactus, what is your name?” “None of your damn business.” “I will call you Leafless, then, since you have no leaves.” “Of course I don’t have any leaves, dumb ass. I’m a stinkin’ cactus.” Hook’s smile quickly disappeared. He stepped forward towards Bobby. “I tire of your sarcasm and your words. If you were any other man, I’d have slit your throat by now. But you somewhat amuse me, so you shall live for now. But you will die like the rest.” Hook kicked the pot towards Smee, who was gathering the weapons from Bats and Belly. He took out a small bag and placed the weapons and Bobby in to it. “And here is the mighty savior…” Hook said, walking over towards Lexian, who was sitting near a tree, almost unconscious himself. “He doesn’t look too good, does he Smee?” “I reckon not,” Smee answered with a smile. “What’s wrong with the savior?” Hook said, crouching down. “Is this the same man that out danced Preston Pot to save the life of Peter Pan so long ago? Is this the same Thomas Lexian that was destined for such great things?” Lexian’s eyes opened a bit. “Up yours, Hook.” “So he speaks,” Hook says. “You know, I have waited to get my hands on you for a long time. I wanted to see if you were as good as Pan, or Pot, or even these Lost boys. It’s a shame you’ve been afflicted with this disease. I guess you’ll have to die without a fight.” “I’ll fight, and you’ll lose,” He answered back, trying to sit up. As he did, however, he got stomach cramps, and began to writhe around on the ground. Hook smirked. “Save the tough man routine for a later day, Thomas. Today is not yours. You will die, like the rest, and Never Never Land will be mine. Pray to whatever gods you believe in, Thomas. Your hell is now your reality.” Hook walks away, as Smee comes over to bind Lexian’s feet and arms. As he does, he whispers to Lexian, “…and hell hath no fury, like Captain Hook.” Lexian groaned. “…he will die. I promise you.” And then he was unconscious.
