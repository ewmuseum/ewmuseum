Time to Think (Part Three)

undated

Date: Oct 25/02 The sun was beating me down with it’s warming rays of suppression, but I didn’t mind it none. I had nothing to do but sift the hot, white sand between my toes. I was totally at ease, as I watched the hotties in bikinis run along the beach, and splashing in the clear, blue water. Suddenly, the air was filled with a horrible dinging sound followed soft voice . . . Pilot - “Ladies and gentlemen, this is your Captain speaking. We will be landing at the Paris Charles de Gaulle International Airport shortly. I would like to ask you to please return your seats and food trays to their full and upright position.” The sunny beach began to fade away, and left me with a cold, cramped inside of the aircraft which was currently transporting me from New York to Paris. Groggily, I awoke from my slumber, and did as the Captain had asked. It was another fifteen minutes until the plane actually landed, and another ten minutes til I had my feet on solid ground. Man did ever feel good . . . ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Yet again I had to wait . . . this time for my baggage to be unloaded from the plane. It took a good half hour to do so, but I was in no rush . . . well, I really was in a rush, but when you see a line of French soldiers, all of whom were carrying automatic rifles, you suddenly realize you don’t rush for anything. Eventually, I was on my way out the airport door and flagging down a taxi. One of the dozens of taxis that were waiting outside was kind enough to pull over in front me and pick me up. With my broken, I often apologized for my poor grammar, I directed him where I wanted to go . . . and that was the train station. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ I had thought my speeds on the New York highway was incredible, but you should have seen this cabby blaze down the streets of Paris. I swear his foot never left the gas, and never touched the break. He cut of other drivers left, right, and center . . . he used every back road and alley he knew . . . it was like a chase scene right out of James Bond or something. I can’t even say we got there eventually . . . he practically made it to the train station before I even asked. That was by far the fastest I’d ever gone in an automobile . . . but it was also the last I’d see of an automobile for another few days. From the train station, I would be on tracks all the way to Istanbul, Turkey. Sounds like a nice, long sight seeing tour of Europe, right? Nope! To me, it just means another day or so of sitting around, getting motion sickness . . . not so fun sounding now, eh? Anywho, I got my bags out of the cab’s trunk and paid the driver, leaving him a tip that he was less than enthused about. I made my way into the train station, and managed to board my train without any problems. I was all set for my next big journey. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The train was about halfway through Yugoslavia, when I woke up on the second day of my train-ridden existence. The thumping of the train’s wheels clucking over the railway ties had brought me to an early awakening, and man was I grumpy. Let me tell ya . . . Europe is nothin’ more than trees, grass, and farms. This was not my kind of a place at all. I could have cried I was so bored. Believe when I say, if you ever get a chance to visit Europe . . . DON’T! Well, anywho . . . I was awake for most of the second day, which didn’t help my boredom much. Playing solitaire over and over and over again can get pretty boring after the first sixty times or so. However, my trip wasn’t all bad. The kid behind me was pretty bored too, so he decided to kick the back of my chair none-stop for hours on end. Usually someone would find that enraging, but not me. It was like getting a back massage for free, and my knotted back muscles liked that very much. Anyway, rather than taking in all the sights of South Eastern Europe, I decided I’d take that time and rest for the show. Little had I realized, that the trip from Istanbul to Ramat-Gan was a two-day excursion by automobile . . . and I was guaranteed beforehand that I would not be able to sleep while the military style bus transported me through the war-torn Middle East. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ I had made it to Istanbul, and unloaded my luggage with no problems, and I was now on the twelfth hour of my bus ride to Ramat-Gan. We were a few hours into Syria, and we had already had several close calls with land mines and guerrilla soldiers. I was exhausted from my travels, but I couldn’t sleep at all. The skies were filled with the sounds of bombs and gunfire . . . and fear of never waking up kept me from closing my eyes. The war-torn countryside was also a sight to see. It differed greatly from the grassy knolls of Europe. Just about every mile all you could see was dirt, dust, and bomb craters. The villages we passed through looked so devoid of any kind of quality of life . . . just makes you stop and wonder what the world is coming to. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The first day of bussing was met with success, as we were not hijacked, or bombed. It was six in the morning on the second day on the bus and we were a short few hours from Ramat-Gan. Like Syria, Israel was also desolate and barren. The same bomb craters, and dust plains went as far as my eyes could see. This was definitely a place would never want to live in, or vacation to . . . and the sooner I got to the show, did my thing, and get outta here . . . the better. With only a day until my debut match in PCW, I had a lot to think about. As the bus sped down the dusty highway, many things passed through my mind. Would I be good enough to defeat Rich Rollins? Would I be able to impress my peers, and maybe make a few friends? Did I leave the iron on? My mind was just racing around in circles for the rest of the trip . . . but for an instance, my mind went blank. When I saw the huge, developed city of Ramat-Gan sitting in the middle of a barren wasteland . . . I could only think . . . If these people can build something like in the middle of this hell hole, then maybe, just maybe, I can defeat Rich Rollins? These overcame all odds and built a fantastic city like that . . . maybe I can overcome all odds and pull an upset victory over Rollins? Only time will tell . . .
