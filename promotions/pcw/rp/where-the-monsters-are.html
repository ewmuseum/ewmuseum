Where the Monsters Are.

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: not entered In the beginning, life was fairly simple. Sure, there was ugliness. Wars. Famines. Plague. Heck, we have all that right now, plenty of it right under our very noses. But it was simple – as a human, you did your best to eke out an existence, breed, and cultivate a linage. It’s the biological way. Societies were built and felled. Religions came and went, few surviving the scouring hand of time. All the while man progressing, evolving, discovering his world and himself. Technology, low and high, proliferated and made life easier, more manageable. Man became master of his domain. Then… HE arrived. Landing smack dab in the middle of a booming nation known as the United States of America in the mid-1930s, an alien with superhuman powers. Superman. Ugh. Gifted with powers unimaginable, he set about as a tool for good and righteousness. He quickly gained fame and notoriety, as well as created a tide swell of justice perhaps never before seen. He set about righting wrongs and using his alien powers to fight crime in an attempt to create a utopian society, as he saw was his responsibility. What a dope. The balance of human good versus evil was thrown. With this great and mighty beacon of justice of an alien source, the tips of the cosmic scales dipped heavily towards that which was considered good. Ordinary human villains became easily dismissed by this veritable unstoppable force. The karma of Earth shifted, for what is a gentle ying without a raging yang? So, the flood of supervillains upon the Earth began. But with the scales so unproportionally tilted to the side of good, I mean, let’s face it, ole Supes is downright indestructible, indefensible, and undefeatable, it wasn’t so much as a flood, but a raging tsunami of devilish ne’er-do-wells. And they came from all over; all walks of life and unlife… Other countries. Other planets. Natural mutations. UN-natural mutations. Self-afflictions. Accidents. The Netherworlds. Other dimensions. Even the realm of the unliving gave birth to the hoary denizens of the criminal underworld hell-bent on undermining the normal human way of life, built up over centuries of evolution. Fortunately, this was also an age of heroes also choosing to rise up to fight the good fight. But Superman’s mere presence always tipped the scales in the bad guy’s favor. They fought valiantly, sidekicks at their side, hoping to stem the never-cresting tide of super powerful villains. For a few decades, America had a host of champions defending her from drowning in a pit of ceaseless treachery. But then what happened? Comic books! Cartoon deals! Merchandising! Movies! The heroes took their money and ran with it. Really, who can blame them? At the time, the sums of money offered were enormous, well suited to an affluent lifestyle. Once sedated with piles upon piles of hard cash, it’s hard to think of anything but which bank offers the best interest rate and which politician will offer the greatest tax cuts. After all, their exploits lived on in media, fueled by the heroin-addicted writers and artists of the time. Al; you had to do was show up and collect your check in your costume for being you. Justice took a back seat to money, as per the usual. But the flow of villains refused to staunch. Dateline: 1956, Southern California. Trying to cope with the massive influx of now ex-superheroes into the sunny SoCal region, namely Beverly Hills and Hollywood, which just barely precipitated the ungodly transposition of all the various supervillains and their underlings, Californian lawmakers came up with a bold plan. Supervillains rarely worked alone. Massive egos must be sated with a spawn of criminal subordinates, creating a corporation, of sorts, in villainy. These evil super men created their own underworlds, placing themselves naturally at their behest. This allowed for invisible tendrils of the organization to fund their greater plans with smaller workings of crime, all to be attributed to the mastermind at the top. California lawmen began to infiltrate these gangs undercover. Their mission: get close to the heads of these criminal organizations and take them down with a newly discovered and experimental procedure: Cryogenics. Brave lawmen were forced to do many things they weren’t proud of, some driven to the fringes of lunacy by the dark deeds forced upon them, just to get close to the supervillain running the show. Once shown the inner sanctum, they would await an opportunity to get close enough to use a chemical so expensive, so super-cooled, and so potent, that a single drop would instantly render a mad villain incapacitated for 6 hours – more than enough time to send in a SWAT team and transfer the baddie to a proper storage containment unit. From there, a hasty trial would be held underneath public radar, the containment units ruled ‘proper and necessary restraints’ in dealing with an individual with such catastrophic powers. California has no death penalty. Also, the ‘arrest’ was often a hasty matter, done whenever and wherever possible, making the accumulation of actual evidence a problem. California judges, lead by the inexorable Judge Gerard Sexton, refused to condemn ANY man to an eternity of frozen imprisonment based upon such scanty evidence. Still, they saw the situation as it was and the maximum sentence was given on the charges allowable, making for heavy sentences indeed. Funded by the booming American economy as well as siphoning funds from such other top secret provisions such as the Area 51 Project, California prepared to send its supervillain problem into a sort of frozen stasis, hoping it would last forever. But it wasn’t life without parole. It couldn’t have been, based on the charges levied, so it couldn’t last forever. But still, what parole board in their right mind would release a diabolical supervillain bent on world domination and unleash them upon an unsuspecting populace? Who indeed? Flash forward to the present. Funding has dwindled as costs soar. The ancient and under-serviced Cryogenic chambers are over-flowing with villains, stacked like cordwood. The use of subversive moles inside criminal organizations has long ceased due to law enforcement budget restrictions. And it’s parole time. “All rise! This criminal parole hearing in the state of California for prisoner number 098430-25 is now in session.” A darkened room, the smooth walls carved from the very rock of the Earth, a mile below the surface. Two men and a woman sit behind a stone desk, each dressed alike in a collection of grays and blacks. They sit, grimly, across the room from a small contingent of heavily armored soldiers as well as the frozen bodies of some of the worst scourges known to mankind. “Well?” The people at eh table look at each other, cold sweat beading on their foreheads. “We can’t just release him…” “What other option do we have?” The female parole officer chimes in, “These reports indicate storage space is at a premium, as well as funding. We can simply state good behavior…” “Good behavior?! He’s been frozen solid for 50 years!!” “He hasn’t gotten into trouble, has he? Besides, we only have him on a slew of minor charges.” A tense pause. The man on the right breathes a heavy sigh. “I agree that are options are limited, but it would be unconscionable to release this… this…” All three look down at the cryogenic containment unit propped up before them. A giant, purple creature sprouting great ram horns from his head and a monstrously muscular body held up by cloven feet. His blood-red eyes are narrowed, his fangs frozen in a snarl of hate and surprise, going to his icy slumber knowing he was betrayed by a member of his own organization. The parole board gives a collective shudder. “This thing… is it even human? Can’t we just put him to sleep like some deranged mongrel?” “A judge ruled in ’59 that he was, indeed, human and, while psychopathic, mostly sane. Real name is Clive T. Daniels…” “AKA The Red Scourge, able to summon swarms of farm animals to his aid in battle.” “Red?” A shrug from the female member, “He was colorblind. No one had the guts to tell him he was purple instead of red.” “And he gained his powers through an accidental radiation leak from the nuclear power plant that was near his farm.” “It seeped into the ground well. They found him mutated and barely alive, in the barn, smeared with animal growth hormones… ” “Ewwww.” Suddenly, a young soldier bursts through the entrance door, waving a paper with PCW markings aloft. He grins widely as he addresses the board. “I’ve got GREAT news!” “Hi-Keeba!! Tis good to again be allowed to honor the gods in their sport of choice!! Huzzah on this day, for Big Alan Steele returns for the glory of Olympia!!” We now rejoin our Big and Bold hero, Big Alan Steele, as he postures in the living room of Stately Steele manor. A board, though trusted manager, Sue Saksnussum looks on, yawning unappreciative of her charges growing enthusiasm. “It’s a screw job. It always is,” she manages between yawns. “Perhaps… but perhaps this is the opportunity to show Lord Frisco the power of good. To show him the honor in gladiatorial combat and the pride one receives from a job satisfactory,” Steele ponders openly, “I shall see this as possible avenue to direct the lost Frisco into the light of the Olympic gods, allowing him to make his way in the world as an ally, rather than a diabolical foe.” “Screw job,” Sue repeats. Steele shrugs. “ROOOOOOOOOAAAAAAAAAAARRRRRRRR!!!!!!!” A car alarm whines outside Steele’s palatial home, as the The Red Scourge overturns an Audi and gives a feral cry. People scream in fear, running en masse as the monster stomps his way through Maplewood Drive, outside of Stately Steele Manor. BAS and Sue move to the foyer and peer out a front window, nonplussed. Sue grimaces, “Another one? That makes the third supervillain this week!” “Excellent!” booms Steele with a hearty laugh, “More training that is proper and right for my upcoming match Friday!” Sue gets a far-away look, thinking, “You know… it seems like they always seem to show up JUST before you matches… like it’s planned or something…” “Why, tis merely the god of Olympia, sending forth adequate sparring to allow me to attain maximum efficiency within that most scared of disciplines, wrestling!! Zeus doth take care of his son…” “Well, I don’t like it,” frowns Sue, “There’s always the chance you could twist and ankle or worse… break a finger!” “Silly mortal Sue!!” laughs Big Al, delightfully, as he cracks his knuckles, “I fear NO man, be him molded in the form of gods, or twisted into the perverted abomination as you see destroying our front gates!! They cannot hurt one protected by the gods as well as destiny!” As Big Al strides to the front door, Sue puts a cautionary hand on his shoulder. “Just be careful… I need you out there on Friday, fit and ready for treachery.” Steele merely grins as he flexes his huge muscles, preparing to exit. “Hi-Keeba!”
