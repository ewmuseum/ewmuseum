The Bellzier Chronicles: Fade to Black [Part Three]

2 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 2, 2002 Good Luck SupermarketOakland, CA A new voice cried out to Tom Lexian, beckoning for him to head out of the super market. It was like a wild fire in his brain, flashing in front of his eyes as if he was watching a movie. Suddenly his past was now his present, and he had the choice to either face it head on, or duck his tail between his legs and escape the confrontation. Tom stared at Mai Le, a young Vietnamese woman who stood in front of him as he made his way into the Good Luck super market. . She had on a white t-shirt and light blue jeans, anchored with a red apron. It was a standard uniform for most of the people that worked in the market – casual, comfortable, and not too flashy. Mai was one of the only girls that Tom had been acquainted with when he was young. Most of his friends were other boys from the neighborhood who he would ride his bike with or just walk around the market place having fun. Mai was like him – the odd person out. She was the only child to her widowed mother, who had come over to America along with his own mother and father. Mai’s dad had served on the police force in Ha Noi, a South Vietnamese city which had been in a virtual tug of war between American forces and VC forces. Most citizens of Ha Noi had fled the fledgling war land, taking to the jungle for refuge and hoping to make it out of the country with any American ships that were bound for a country either than their own. Mai recalled the troubling night that they had when Mai’s mother had decided to flee the city that they had lived in their entire lives. A rain storm had recently pulled into the area, pushing rain soaking showers and howling winds into the war laden area. Soldiers tromped around the battle field, now muddy and tired, trying desperately to avoid showers of deadly gunfire, and now, mother nature’s fury. Those veterans that fought on that auspicious night often joked that it was like Mother Earth was looking down at the world, and was furious at what she saw. She saw her children fighting amongst themselves, using her own natural resources that she provided, to make weapons of mass destruction to take other lives. The rain were nature’s tears at what she had seen, and the wind was a cry of agony as she watched lives being lost over such petty interests. The storm would last well over three weeks, causing homes that had not already been destroyed, to be trampled by the high winds and unbearable conditions. The ground itself, now rain soaked and quick sand like, seemed to envelope whatever was foolish enough to step upon it. Soldiers from either side would try to sludge through the mud, hoping to get to higher ground, but would be slowed down by the mud’s impediments. In war, being still is almost as deadly as holding a live ground. Those who were not quick enough would be killed instantly by strafe gunfire. So there they lay, two immovable giants, unable to approach one another, yet able to keep each other at bay. In the middle were innocent bystanders – the citizens of Ha Noi – who could only watch as their beloved city fell to ruins before their eyes. Mai’s mother had seen enough. If they stayed longer, they would only lose their home and their lives, so she decided to move from the city now. The night brought cover from prying eyes, although mother nature found a way through the darkness. Although they could not escape the storm’s wrath, they could at least flee from the gunfire. The random, strafing gunfire often ended at night, leaving both combatants to take a rest from their warring ways, leaving the trail towards the jungle clear. A few other families had gotten together and agreed to make the dangerous trek through the jungle together, although they all agreed that they must flee immediately and not delay any longer. The longer they waited, the greater the chance that the storm would let up and the ground warfare would begin again, leaving no one standing in their wake. That night, Mai’s mother and father got into a heated argument. Mai watched from the poorly lit doorway of her room, as her parents fought over whether or not they should stay. Mai’s mother begged her husband to think of the family, and argued that they had no future if they decided to stay in the city. Mai’s father was a proud man, who had lived his entire life in Ha Noi. He loved the prestige and the honor that his father had brought to the city as a leader in the community, which was why he had become a police officer – he vowed to protect the city no matter what the cost. Mai’s father refused to leave, and argued that the city’s integrity and honor was at stake. He could not leave. Mai’s mother, obviously upset, called him a coward, and took Mai by her hand out of the house. Mai looked back, only to see her father, heart broken and confused, watching his only daughter leave his life forever. The staging point for the escape was at a nearby house, which was the closest home to the jungle line. There were approximately twenty people making the escape, although they knew that there wouldn’t be twenty people in the boats as they left. The survival rate would be low. The rain poured on harder and harder, seemingly knowing that violence would erupt on the battle field that night. The wind let out another mournful cry, as if foreshadowing the inevitable conclusion that lives would be lost. Hand in hand, Mai and her mother followed the escaping group out to the jungle, running faster and faster through the city square. Mai found it difficult to run – she only had on a pair of thin sandals, and her feet continued to sink into the mud, deeper and deeper. She could feel herself falling, but would continue to regain her balance, keeping her eyes on the jungle in front of her. Fifty meters…forty meters…thirty meters…twenty meters… And then the screams began to echo out… Gunfire erupted from all sides, suddenly catching the group unaware. Mai watched on as three of the people running in front of her suddenly collapsed to the ground, a red smear of color trickling down from their fallen bodies through the aqueous mud. Mai screamed, suddenly tripping on the mud herself, falling face first into the ground. She couldn’t breathe…the mud and rain was choking her, not allowing her to breathe. This could be the end of her life - right here and now - twenty meters away from freedom. Mai wanted to scream and cry for her mother, but could not even hear herself shouting for help. Her mother pulled her out of the mud, shouting for Mai to get up and run. Mai had mud smeared all over her face and eyes. Whenever she tried to look forward, all she would see was a blur of colors and a flash of light occasionally. The flashes of light were gunfire. Mai’s mother, panicking and realizing that their lives were now at stake, looked desperately over at the group she was running with to call for help. But no one was listening. Bodies were lining up the trail that they taking to freedom. Several of their group mates had been shot, lying face down in the cold, unforgiving mud. Mai’s mother watched as a few of the people ran into the forest, never looking back. Those were the smart ones – looking back would only hasten your death. A few family members stayed behind, trying to help others that had fallen. One memory that had been forever etched into Mai’s mother’s mind was watching a woman desperately drag her husband’s body through the mud, whom had been shot. Her face, soaking wet from rain drops and tears, and laced with despair, cried out for her husband to get up. Mai’s mother knew what would happen next, and she closed her eyes as the woman suddenly flailed back wards, falling to the abyss of mud and dirt, next to her husband. The situation was suddenly becoming more dire as time progressed. Only a few other people were still trying to make it to the jungle, and they were all on their hands and knees, crawling for the line. Bullets were flying everywhere now, and the situation was turning ugly. Mai’s mother began to clean off her face from the mud, using the rain to her advantage to help wipe off all the blinding dirt that afflicted Mai. Every second that they spent not running, was a second off of their lives. But running into the hail storm of gunfire was the last step they would have to take. Mai wanted to run back for the house – she didn’t want to go forward anymore. But Mai’s mother gripped her hand fast, and they began to run for the jungle once more. Mai was afraid to open her eyes – she was afraid of what she might see. So she ran blindly, only being guided by her mother’s hand. She could hear the barrels of the guns firing off, coupled with the thundering claps of lightning, and suddenly Mai opened her eyes. They were getting closer to freedom - she could see it. And then she slipped. Mai cried out in pain as she fell once more to her knees. She was so tired, so weak…she could not run anymore. Mai’s mother grabbed her into her arms and began running with Mai in her arms. Mai could almost smell the desperation and the fear that was being emitted in the air. She thought they wouldn’t make it. She thought it would be impossible for them to survive to make it o the jungle… The Mai looked back, and saw her father running out, along with some other men from the village. He had a hand gun and began firing it towards the enemies line. Mai and her father suddenly had eye contact with one another, and Mai reached out her hand to somehow grab onto her father. Mai’s father continued to fire shattering bullets into the enemy, unknowing whether his shots were causing any damage. His only function was to draw fire away from his wife and child. He looked over to Mai one last time, shouting, “Run!” “No!” Suddenly the jungle enveloped herself and her mother, and the last memory Mai had of her father was watching him fall to his knees, continuing to fire his weapon valiantly. With all that Mai had been through, it was a wonder that she had been able to live such a normal life as she had. She was rough and strict, as always. Even as a child, she had a serious demeanor that had resulted from her tumultuous past, but she always had a sensitive side for Tom. He remembered the nights they would spend, just talking about her past and her memories of Vietnam and her father. Tom was amazed by her maturity at such a young age, and she was a model for him to follow. Although serious, she had a fun loving side which he reasoned existed well before the war. But the events that had taken place during her life had buried that fun loving soul deep within her, putting up a wall that could not be broken down. Only occasionally would she lift her iron curtain of emotions and share them with Tom. So there was, still so beautiful, yet as serious as he remembered. The shock on her face made Tom smile, because it told him that she was happy to see him. If she hadn’t, she wouldn’t have cared very much for his appearance. Mai stopped, staring at Tom carefully. Suddenly her eyes bulged wide and she dropped the dough roller on the ground, placing her hand over her mouth. “Oh my god…Tom, is that you?” Tom nodded slowly. “It’s me alright. Tom Lexian – all grown up now. It’s been awhile Mai.” “More than awhile…” Tom sighed, closing his eyes. “A very long time…”
