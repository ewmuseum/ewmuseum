Beyond Duty: Touching Into the Unknown

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 26th of 2002 A.D. “Fan out! Blue squad, your area is Bravo Sector! Red squad, we got Gamma Sector!” Shanahan rotated erratically left and right in his chair, eyes glued on the hazy screens of New Miskatonic’s underground surveillance chamber. Miskatonic staff were spread through the room, taking notes on various readings gathered from gauges implanted on the suits of the unit whose activities were now displayed on the video monitors. Solemn men garbed in standard military dress also permeated the room, Bruce’s fellow disgruntled associates of the Coalition brotherhood. The member of the temporarily disbanded Delta Fox regiment swiveled to eye Dr. Davids, the operations chief of the Miskatonic contingent. “My presumption was that all activities of the Coalition were placed on hold until the trial of Captain Stryker was closed.” Glancing through thick-lensed and wire-rimmed glasses, Davids met Bruce’s presumptuous stare and replied with a meager grin. He returned to scrawling notes with a stubby pencil. “You assume correctly, Mr. Shanahan. This operation is being conducted without consent of the High Council. In a sense…” He glanced up momentarily, taking in more data on the dim monitor. “….you could say this one is rogue.” A thick hand fell on the shoulder of Shanahan, jarring the ever-tense Boston thug. He shifted his eyes abruptly from the meticulous Miskatonic professor and met eyes with the dutiful Major Hawkins, a temperamental yet respected officer within the military-partitioned Blackhelm ranks. His manner was brusque, but Bruce managed a somber smile at the twinkle in the commander’s eye. “I have a good reason to believe you helped plan this one, Major,” proposed Shanahan. “Damn right,” snorted Hawkins, crossing his arms before gazing at the surveillance monitor designated to Captain Lisbon, commander of the France–imported unit they now tediously followed. “Don’t get me wrong, those bishops and priests running our little group are honorable and upright men. But sometimes that pious devotion steers them the wrong way…..they’re just too f*cking passive. They’ve let themselves get virtually walked on since this new order of council members took their oaths, and they always justify it by claiming ‘God will fix things when the time is right’. Well to tell the truth, I’m getting impatient and it’s time for me and God to start running in different time zones.” Bruce couldn’t withhold a snicker. He had sensed the same apprehension in Craven the past evening when they spoke in his apartment; Randall had truly felt an appeal to join the Blackhelm ‘rebels’, but as always had insisted his faith would see him through. It was his lone Achilles’ heel: his ambition was held forever in restraint by his spiritual devotion. “Anything abnormal, doctor?” inquired the major with a curt glance. Dr. Davids pursed his lips and studied his notes before replying with an shake of the head and negative grunt. “Environmental readings all average, nothing to worry about at the moment, Major Hawkins,” reiterated the scholar from the charts and graphs on his clipboard. “Temperature maintaining steady, atmospheric pressure stable, no sudden changes in motion activity in the immediate area. The past disasters have always come on the heels of such abnormalities occurring in the immediate vicinity of the active units. None of those conditions exist, major. Everything is currently running on schedule and according to plan, no deviations necessary at this time.” Shanahan’s eyes fell on the monitor of Lieutenant Winslow, its image jarred as the camera mounted on the lieutenant’s shoulder bumped against a jagged protrusion projecting from the cavern wall. Static and a momentary flare of snow flashed across the screen. “What’s the purpose of this operation, Hawkins?” asked Shanahan, facing the solemn-eyed Army officer. “Exactly where’d ya get the idea to send these guys into the caverns of the inner Amazon? I doubt ya just poked your finger at a globe and said ‘Eureka! They’ll go here!’” Hawkins made no effort to withhold an amused snicker. “It was a mission listed for in the near future on the ‘itinerary’ the High Council issues to all commanding parties,” informed the robust officer. “It simply hasn’t been approved or given the go-ahead yet by those faceless buffoons. Given our theories and speculation, we think this is a perfect time to test our suspicions.” Bruce’s demeanor turned grim. He scowled, watching the thin light of Winslow’s shoulder lamp dance sharply over the crags and impressions in the caverns. “I know what the suspicions are. But what if your suspicions are wrong?” Hawkins bit his lip tersely. He clasped his hands across his abdomen, gingerly rubbing a tarnished silver ring on his right middle finger. “Then the blood will be on our hands this time. And I will mourn the losses as I have in the past.” A hiss of static broke the tense silence between Shanahan and Hawkins. The major’s head swiveled stridently left to the monitor of Lisbon, whose thick French accent now flooded the room through a series of speakers as the feed from his camera illustrated darkness ahead, beams of light intermittently penetrating the blackness and glancing off the drab, sandy walls of ancient basalt. “We’re entering a narrow tunnel. Not sure how far it extends but all is quiet right now. Red Squad is moving ahead to scout.” Three of his subordinates slid past him in the narrow underground corridor, forming a single-file formation as the light reflects awkwardly off their jet-black helmets. Lisbon’s rough hand briefly passed over the image to adjust the lamp. There was uneasy silence in that cavern as the unit pressed ahead, a lingering dread far from alien to the battle-hardened soldiers present in the musty air. Loose dirt swirled into the air and danced in the rays of the lights. “What of Captain Stryker?” solicited Hawkins to Bruce without raising a brow or shifting his gaze from the monitors. “Any word on his ‘trial’?” Bruce sighed longingly. Stryker had all but disappeared after his departure to meet with the Council following the Tropical Massacre. Ever since then a subtle anxiety had arisen in his gut, feeding his over-active imagination. He hoped the suspicions held by the rogue faction were nothing more than trite and ridiculous notions, but that instinct he knew all too well said otherwise. However, he knew to bring that particular matter to the attention of Hawkins would only result in a further wedge being driven between the sides, as Hawkins’ nature was akin to Shanahan’s. “Nothing,” came the simple reply. “I imagine whatever happens with him will be over soon and this entire thing can be resolved.” “What of your allies?” “Waiting. They have nothing outside their duty in the Coalition. Simply waiting for things to pass.” “What in God’s name?....” The astonished curse harvested the attention of all those in the chamber. Hawkins leaned forward to stare at the monitor of Lieutenant Barslett, the point man at the front of the line. His camera was overwhelmed in static as it slammed against something to the right and it shuddered without cease for several seconds, shifting out of focus. The others darted indistinctly in all directions, over the walls to either side and over the perplexed expressions of comrades. Major Hawkins leaned forward, a bead of sweat coursing down his brow. “Lisbon!” he shouted. “What the hell’s going on?” “We’ve just entered some massive chamber, major, this thing is huge. The tunnel has opened into some broad cavity in the recesses of this cave system. And…..damn, there’s some weird sh*t in here, major.” Hawkins raised a brow, inching by one of the professors as they busily recorded readings. “Define ‘weird sh*t’, captain.” “Take a gander for yourself.” There was a pause as the lead camera finally shifted back into focus. Hawkins gradually recoiled in slight surprise, the lights passing over the outlines of a deformed, yet hauntingly majestic steeple towering in the middle of the open grotto. Beneath the steeple was an altar surrounded in pylons of a shimmering black metallic core, instantly recognized by Shanahan as the same alien substance materialized in his dreams in the past. Atop the pylons were oil-laden nests for torches and ceremonial fires. The light illuminated the gleaming pylons and the gaunt monoliths hovering in the background flanking the steeple, elucidating the succession of symbols and occult glyphs engraved in sequence down the sinister structures. Hawkins snorted. “You just walked into a temple in the heart of the Amazon cavern system. Apparently somebody worships something there. Who does the worshipping and what is worshipped is anyone’s guess. Comb the area for anything of value, report back ASAP.” “Affirmative.” Hawkins set his eyes on the floor while Shanahan watched the monitors with interest as they investigated the area, pushing aside rocks and examining the crude marks and emblems adorning the bulky obelisks and globular columns. “This place may be worth perusing, Bruce,” commented the major. “How odd though….a temple in the center of a subterranean cavern in the center of the Amazon rainforest. Doesn’t make any sense.” “Especially not with craftsmanship like that,” added Shanahan. “Or using that eerie metal to make a pillar for that matter. I ain’t no tribal linguist and I sure as hell don’t know their writing for spit. But I’ve seen pictograms and marks like that enough to know they’re not carved by any rainforest tribe for any primitive god.” “Any ideas?” “Besides getting the impression we’re not dealin’ with your typical headhunters....no. But I think your boys better pick over that temple with a fine-toothed comb. They're gonna find something worthwhile.” 'How do you know that?' 'A hunch, major. Just a hunch.'
