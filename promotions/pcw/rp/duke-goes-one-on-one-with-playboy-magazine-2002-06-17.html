Duke goes one on one with Playboy Magazine

17 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6-17-02 Dateline July Edition PlayBoy Magazine Al Larcher interview with Duke Williams Hundreds of miles away from the nearest arena, crowd, or town with a population over three hundred people. Stands a colonial mansion that looks like something out of a civil war novel. An American flag flies high above the front lawn for the entire world to see. The mansion belongs to long time wrestler and talk show host Duke Williams. I met Duke back in 1984 when I was a young man after a WWF show. He was young and green and so was I. Although I had no interest in meeting the rookie wrestler I got his autograph and continued waiting to meet Dusty Rhodes. Dusty never came and over the years I lost that Duke Williams Autograph. Now eighteen year later I sit down with the wild-eyed veteran to talk about his storied career and life in general. He has gone from rookie nobody to a celebrated champion, high paid pitch man, and PTC radio host. I sat down with Duke in his office in his home in Montana. The room was filled with animal heads starring down at us. PB: Thanks for giving us your time Duke. DW: Yes sure. Thanks for interviewing me. PB: You seem like I nice guy. I saw family pictures decorating the halls, your drinking ice water right now, you say please and thank you. How different are you to the guy who we see in the ring ? DW: Elwood Williams who is sitting here today isn’t much different then the guy in the ring. Elwood is not as vulgar, violent, loud, or sinful as Duke. I am however very similar in my beliefs and ways of doing things. PB: What are some of those beliefs? DW: I do respect and understand the working man. I think he is America and not the corporate boobs like the Arthur Anderson’s of the world. I think the wrong people get the wrong credit in this world of ours. That needs to change. PB: You’re the president of the PCW, if you were the president of the USA how would you change things. DW: Increase the minimum wage, police our tax monies, instill the trust of government back into our youth though reform. PB: How did you get involved in pro-wrestling? DW: By accident really. My career in the NFL was short lived. I loved to play music and perform in concert and entertain. I also loved to be physical and fight so I went to Japan and for four years I was a shoot fighter. PB: Dangerous stuff. DW: I broke a lot of bones and I have the tattoos and scares to prove it. PB: What sent you back to the US? DW: I missed home and my body told me no more. PB: So that’s when you took advantage of your degree and got into radio. DW: Yea I did some radio work in the US and a local wrestling promoter for the NWA hooked me up with a ring announcing gig. PB: That was short lived? Duke laughs DW: I was six three and three hundred pounds. When they saw that it didn’t take long for them to talk me into the ring. PB: How long did that last? DW: Maybe four years. I don’t really remember. PB: Did anything come of that? DW: Not really. Lots of great friends and loads of knowledge. PB: What kind of things did you learn? DW: IF you wanted to hang out with the veterans learn respect, then learn how to hold you booze. Also watch your wallet during the card games. PB: You packed it up and went to the CSWA? DW: Yea but that was short lived. I wrestled under the name Winston Wolf during my first run there and Duke Williams during the second one. PB: Why did you leave the first time. DW: I wasn’t ready yet so I went back to the Indies then to the WWF. PB: How could we forget the rodent? Duke laughs DW: Why do we remember? PB: The mask and ears were true late eighties at its best. DW: Yea vince made me the Rodent and that how I got paid. I got to fight wrestlers like Randy Savage, Andre The Giant, and the legendary Hacksaw Jim Duggan. PB: That was the funniest feud I’ve ever seen. You and the giant. He was afraid of rats and you would chase him around throwing cheese at him. DW: Can we move on?? It wasn’t funny when he caught me. Duke and I share a laugh. PB: So then you left the WWF and joined up with a young promoter named Jax. DW: I joined the ICWA and my career was underway. After floating around with other characters and bad gimmicks I decided to be myself and wrestle as Duke Williams. PB: Where did the nickname “The Malice Man” come from? DW: The dictionary. I was a bodyguard for Kris Kristofferson when a guy attacked him. I beat the hell out of the guy and Kris started calling me Malice Man. The name was cool after I looked it up in the dictionary and it stuck. PB: So the ICWA was really becoming the federation of the future and you we’re the man. DW: After proving myself and win belt after belt it was clear I was that company. They built the future on me and we had a lot of success. PB: You as the man didn’t last long did it? DW: No, it didn’t. I won the world title and decided I wanted some time off. Something I didn’t understand at the time. You don’t win a title just to take time off or retire. I was guilty of it. I would never do that again. I win titles to defend them not walk away from them. However I did and I happily did the job to Tony Rolo. PB: The specialist. DW: The specialist at shady deals and bullsh*t maneuvers. When I came back he did everything he could to make sure I wasn't involved in an angle with him. Keeping me away from the world title cus he didn’t want to lose. He feared the Malice Man and had Jax on his little pinky finger. PB: But you stuck around? DW: I did. I was weak minded. I was convince that if I went anywhere else I would have to start over or would be buried by another promoter sending a message to Jax. So I put up with it. PB: When did you finally decided to leave? DW: I was the president of ICWA or I think it was IFWC at that point. I had the angle going for me. I was working harder then my opponents. Rolo was not in the picture he was on vacation. There was a tounament for the title. I should have been booked to win it. There was no reason why I shouldn’t have been. I don’t complain when I lose 9 out of 10 time but this was the tenth time. I confronted the owner and he said I should have won. I was confused then I saw Rolo came back and he was the number on contender. So basically he was planing his return and didn’t want me involved with the title so they gave it to a half ass jobber that Rolo could easily beat. I did everything for that company. I turned heel cus Rolo refused to be anything but I face. In the long run I got screwed. PB: Would you ever go back to Jax? DW: Not unless PCW fired me and I had no choice. I never say never but it’s a pretty definite no. I think he’s retired anyway. PB: So how did you come to PCW? DW: There were a few feds around at that point all jockeying for the top position. When I was in fedwars and so was PCW I always admired Tyler Nelson. So when it was time to jump ship he was the first guy I called. He hooked me up with the booker and after a shot conversation I was the newest member of PCW and Tyler Nelsons stable. PB: Did you have to start over? Were our fears coming true? DW: Yes and no. I wasn’t headlining any cards but I also wasn’t in a training fed. I was treated as a low midcarder but not as a rookie. A lot of the older guys were critical of me because they had been burnt by other free agents in the past. I understood there feelings but I didn’t really feel all that welcome. PB: You thought about retiring at that point? DW: Yea I really did. However I fought through it and came to realize that the criticism I was feeling was in my own head. It was not perpetuated by the veterans but my own paranoia. I was welcomed I just wasn’t used to the backstage ways of doing things. Lots of sarcasm and good hatred insults is all I was facing. So I adjusted and things changed. PB: You came into PCW as a heel? DW: That was one of the problems. Ninety percent of my career I’ve been a fan favorite. The only time I was a heel and it worked is when I acted as a born again who wanted to clean up wrestling. PB: Republican mouth piece Alan Keys was your manager. DW: Yea Alan and I worked together and started to clean things up. I was the first wrestler to ever say f*ck on tv. SO it was only logical I clean it up. PB: Why didn’t you use that in the PCW. DW: Before leave the IFWC I was a face and ended that gimmick. Plus the WWF had already ripped it off with the right to censor. I did it first but nobody in PCW would have know that and thought I was stealing for the WWF. So I came back as good guy Duke only I was a heel. PB: Confused the fans a bit? DW: Yea I think so. They didn’t know weather to cheer or hiss so they did nothing. PB: No response is killer. DW: No doubt. So I started to turn face with Jeremy Howard and the Nelsonless stable. Things started to shape up and I won the continental tile from Boda. PB: A proud day? DW: Very. PB: Then in started your Vampir fued. DW: I like Vampir as a person but I hate wrestlers who cut promos with out mentioning there opponents. It doesn’t make sense to me. He was one of those guys. I’m sitting here trash talking him and he’s talking about some stupid f*cking story. I ended up losing to him after giving my all. I felt at that point it was time to say so long. I’m going to wrestle until I’m not longer a world title contender. I didn’t feel I could move up at that point so I move out. PB: You ended it sort of? DW: I talked to the owner of PCW about exiting the ring but not the spot light cus I didn’t want to lose PCW Malice. PB: You were retiring why is that? DW: I knew I would be back and I knew if I were to come back a few months later my show slot would be gone. So Ron asked me if I wanted to commentate on the shows. How could I pass that up?? That’s what I wanted to do. So I did that, then after heated debate on Malice I started running for president. PB: You regained your popularity with out being in the ring? DW: It was the damnedest thing but that’s what happened. My back returned to old form and I knew I wanted to be back. PB: You said you would not wrestler if you could not work to the world title picture. Has that changed? DW: It has not changed the day I’m not a contender is the day I’m done for good. I feel like I can win the PCW title. I'm not sure I can do it today, tomorrow, or six months from now. But the day will come. PB: You looking to win it then retire? DW: Hell no!! I retire when I’m not a contender. When I become champion I continue fighting. When I lose the title I continue fighting. When I’m all used up and not viable anymore I’m gone. PB: PCW is you last fed? DW: I think so. PB: What do you think of the current PCW talent. DW: Anybody who makes it to PCW is talented and I have much respect for them. PB: Are you better then them? DW: At my style I’m the best. However everybody has different styles. I think I can take anybody any week. On the same note anybody can take me out any week. So it depends on the day who is the best. PB: Will you ever leave wrestling? DW: No when I retire which I don’t fore see for many years I’ll commentate. PB: Malice till the day you die. DW: Right on hoss! =w=
