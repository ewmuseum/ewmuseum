And the Trigger was Executed

16 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6.16.02 It was a soft, reposeful spring landscape, as lovely as a dream, and as lonesome as Sunday. The air was full of the smell of flowers, and the buzzing of insects, and the twittering of birds, and there were no people, no cars, there was no stir of life, nothing going on. The road was mainly a winding path with hoof-prints in it and now and then a faint trace of wheels on either side in the grass -- wheels that apparently had a tire as broad as one's hand. The gray of the morning has passed, and the sun is high over the distant horizon, which seems jagged, whether with trees or hills, for it is so far off that big things and little are mixed. To the right of the roadway is the outside of an abandoned warehouse, top to bottom, steel blue paint, fading and peeling from this place's battered walls, the logo of the company that stood once proudly at the top of its frame, suffered the same hurricane treatment as the building itself, for only a few letters were left in it. The bush around the warehouse hasn't seen the blade of a machete; the place is now a broken, run down mess, to say the least. All around appears calm, nevertheless, as the birds chirp their merry songs and the newly hatched nibble on remains of larva to soothe their young appetites. It’s a beautiful sight, to say the least. The street is in the middle of a calm and tidy suburb. Trees line the side of the road, every one of them enveloped in green buds ready to bloom for the spring season. No houses in picture for miles, just a beautiful mid-afternoon day. The sun, high in the azure blue sky sparkles down on the small street, and a little breeze gusts by, taking some excess leaves with it. Slowly but surely, the mid-afternoon day begins to dim out of view as the moonlight hits the brim of the sky peering down on the world from above. The streetlights come on and shine down on the calm street and everything seems to still be perfect, with nothing to worry about. Sure, there are no birds singing their songs, but they’re snug away in their nests awaiting tomorrow’s day to begin their same routine, with the crickets singing them to sleep. A heavy cloud passes across the face of the moon, so that it is in complete darkness. It seems to be almost complete darkness, for the rolling clouds obscure the moon. ...The peace of the street is soon demolished as a black 1995 Suzuki Aerio SX four wheel drive hatchback base comes into view over a slope of a hill in the road swerving back and forth through the lanes. The scene cuts to the inside of the veering car to reveal a gorgeous female of average height, with mid-length brunette hair to her shoulders, in which is covered with a sleeveless black dress up shirt with white buttons, and a tight black leather skirt with a red zipper along the side. Her knee high boots lay the pedal to the metal while she takes another swig of the lucid bottle of Jim Bean and wipes a tear from her eye. She smears her black eye shadow in the process, but gives the impression of melancholy and depression. “Why did I have to be so stupid and do something like that?” The woman cries her expressions under her sniffles. “Please lord, forgive me. I have committed adultery on my husband, but I know I was wrong. Really, I do!” The woman turns saddened frown into a small smile. It doesn‘t look like much of a smile, but it seems to be the best she can do, trying her hardest not to be at a state of complete depression. She takes another guzzle of the bottle of hard liquor, but doesn’t stop drinking until she had endured every last drop into her system. She lay her head back with the bottle lifted high, letting the drops of liquor trickle calmly into her opened mouth and down her throat. She gives a frown and turns to her right throwing the bottle in the floorboard, making a loud shattering noise, interacting with at least four or five more fifths of liquor. From the brown paneled glove box, she reaches in and pulls out her last bottle of Jim Bean. She looks up at the road with a frown to open up the alcoholic drink... SCREEEEEEEEEEEECHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHH! BUMP! The sound of tires bouncing off of the asphalt under them is heard as the car tries to keep its balance on the road. Finally, it comes to the stop and the woman driving slams her head forward against the dashboard, instantly knocking her unconscious. There is a cut above her eye as thick warm blood begins to slowly seep down. The glove box spills open and a small red-laced book falls onto the passenger seat. The book opens up from the impact of the crash. --Dear Diary: April 24, 2001 It’s a beautiful day out. The smell of the flowers is overwhelming, and the day is perfect to be outside. Nothing can go wrong today. The last time I wrote in this, I explained the whole wedding between Ben and I -(you know, the greatest day of my life.) It was great! Tomorrow, we will tie the knot and get married! Isn’t it exciting? He’s the man of my dreams and I will NEVER do anything to hurt him. It was just so sweet the way he proposed to me. I know that I've told you a billion times, but I never get tired of reliving the beauty of it all.:) There we were, out to dinner at the Grande Café in Minneapolis (one of the fanciest restaurants in the city!) We had a huge dinner and then went outside for a walk around the city. It was in January, so the weather was quite chilly, and I was in a small dress with no coat, freezing. Like the gentleman that Ben is, he gave me his long warm coat to put over me so I wouldn’t be so cold...and he bared the temperature, because he’s one of those tough men. Well, anyways, we were walking to his car, and out of nowhere he just told me to put my hand into the coat pocket....so I did...and I found the ring! It was so romant.... A gust of wind comes through the cracked windows, flipping the pages in the diary. The weeks and months go by rapidly, and become gone with the wind. The diary has a thick layer of paper, so it can hold a large amount of information...evidently, since the next entry in the memoir is a year later. --Dear Diary: April 18, 2002 God! Sometimes, I hate Ben! It’s only a couple days until our one-year anniversary and he doesn’t even care. All he does is go out and get drunk with his friends, do drugs, or leave the house. I never even get to see him. AND, this is the man that I want to spend the rest of my life with and bare children with!? The other day we got in an argument...I lost my temper and threw a wineglass at him. It was stupid of me, because I knew what was going to happen. I’ve had to wear shades for a week, now. I know that he doesn’t intentionally mean to hurt me, and it’s usually my fault. I typically deserve it, but it’s just not right for him to mistreat me. It’s just so frustrating to live with a drunk...hold on, he’s at the door. I’m tired of this shit! He came in here yelling at me for no reason and threw me on the bed, because I told him no! I’m going to leave tonight, and get rid of all these problems. I’m done trying to impress him! Misty As if on command, the same gust of wind comes back...only flipping a couple of pages this time. --Dear Diary: April 23, 2002 I can’t believe that I screwed up! I was all into getting rid of the problems the other day, and what do I do? I go out, get drunk, and fuck some guy! I’m such an idiot. He’s never going to forgive me, and I’ve screwed up our whole lives. Just because I went out to some bar, and figured...hey, I don’t need Ben anymore. So, this guy offers to take me home, and before taking me home, we decide to go to a hotel. I’m a fucking dumbass! What am I going to do with myself if Ben finds out? I’m going to have to go out again tonight...it’s one day before our anniversary; I need to drink away my problems AGAIN! Please, Ben, just forgive me. I never meant to hurt you, and I just want our anniversary to be the best. I just want to have a beautiful life with him and beautiful children. I screwed up...we can fix your drinking problem, just try for me! If he doesn't’ take me back...I will kill myself, if I’m not already dead. The woman that was driving, now known as Misty, raises her head up slowly off of the steering wheel. Her forehead seeps blood at the top, an ugly wound with a big cut. She reaches to the right to shut the glove box, holding her injured gash with her left hand. As she closes it, she looks confused, and reopens it, looking around for her diary. Her diary is neither in the glove box, nor on the passenger seat anymore...but a note card is that states: Thank you for coming to the Anger Management/Under the Influence Class. We hope to see you next week, Ben. With those words, she begins to weep more than she ever has, knowing that Ben has been trying. She looks all around the right side of the car, and the backseat to search for her diary. As she turns her head slowly to her left, a blue factory shirt comes in focus with the nametag of Ben on it. His face is over the top of the car, so there is no sign of what he looks like...but a ruffled sniffling sound is heard. As soon as she sees him, she starts to cry. As she cries, everything seems to go in slow motion...as the diary falls from Ben’s hands....and a chrome revolver comes into view. And the trigger was executed. End
