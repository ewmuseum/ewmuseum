Date: Insert Date Here A black screen. Nothing can be seen, nor heard. Suddenly, there is a blinding flash of light, like a camera’s flash, which quickly disappears to reveal a weathered photograph. The photo shows a much younger Andy Murray, standing beside a similar looking child, possibly his brother Shawn. Behind the two children, two adults stand, a man and a woman … Andy’s parents. All seem to be dressed casually, and appear to be having a good time. Behind them, we can see a gushing waterfall cut through the centre of the forest backdrop. A family picnic. ”Lies … Pain … Deceit … Torture … These are the days of my life,” the voice of Andy Murray says in the background in an emotionless tone of voice. The picture stays central to our view for a few seconds, before a small flame appears on one of the corners. Slowly, the flame increases, burning the picture. More smaller flame begin to appear, as the picture folds and twists under the immense heat. The photograph turns to ashes, as the burning begins to die down. Suddenly, there is another blinding flash of light, and the scene slowly fades into a primary school classroom. The class is full of little children, who sit watching as the 40+ year-old female teacher dictates some sort of language lesson. ”The early years … Oh how delightful” Andy says in the background. We begin to home in on the teacher, who appears now to be writing something down on the black board, with white chalk of course. ”And so you see, there are 3 different tenses used in English. The past tense, which is speaking of things that have already happened, the present tense, which is used when speaking of things that are happening right now, and the future tense, used for speaking about things that are going to happen. Now … Who can give me an example of a sentence in the past tense …” says the teacher, turning around to face the class. Several members of the class raise the arms into the air, and several, of course don’t. ”Yes, Andrew,” says the teacher, everyone in the class lowers their hands. ”Yesterday, I came to school,” says a small boy, black hair, blue eyes, dressed in uniform. It becomes clear, that it is a much younger Andy Murray. ”Well done Andrew … Now …” the teacher says, slowly the scene slowly fades into a playground scene, probably from the same school day. Girls practice skipping, younger children play hopscotch, and the majority of the boys seem to be playing football (or soccer) on a poor, concrete pitch, large stones used instead of goal posts. In fact, this game of football looks more like a football riot, but hey, it is little children after all. We home in on one end of the pitch, a rabble of kids appear to be around the ball. Standing right in front of the goal, arm raised, is Andy Murray. The ball manages to escape from the crowd, and rolls straight towards Andy, who takes a chance, and strikes the ball straight at the goal. It trickles past the goalkeeper. Andy grins in celebration, his friends congratulate him. ”I had all I wanted. A great education, lots of friends, and a warm, friendly place to come home to after school. Oh how things have changed!” Andy says in the background again. The scene slowly changes again, this time, showing a slightly older and more matured Andy, walking up a street on a sunny day, with a group of friends. These are the streets of Westhill, the suburban area of Aberdeen, Scotland, where Andy lived back then. The group are chatting, and joking amongst each other, rucksacks on their backs, probably on the way home from a day at school. They reach a corner, where Andy branches off from the group, heading one way, with the others heading the other way, probably because they live in different areas of town. ”Brother … Why couldn’t you have stayed?” Andy says in the background, this is obviously the day when Andy’s brother disappeared. Andy continues to walk a short distance along the street, before finally reaching the door of his semi-detached house. Andy pulls the door open, and walks inside, along a short corridor, and through a door into the family kitchen. Andy sees his mother sitting down at the table, crying. Across the table, sits a police officer, trying to calm Andy’s mother. ”What’s going on?” the young Andy enquires, a questioning look across his face. His mother wipes a tear from her eye, before speaking to her son. ”Andy, would you take a seat please.” she says. Andy drops his bag on the floor, and walks across towards the table, before pulling up a chair, and sitting down. ”Andy … It’s about Shawn.” ”Shawn? What did he do?” ”Andy … Shawn has gone. He was off school sick today, and when I came back from shopping, he had disappeared …” Andy’s mother says, barely holding the tears back. The young Andy’s face quickly explodes into a fit of tears, the police officer tries to contain him and his mother, to no avail. Slowly, the scene changes yet again. We are in the living room of the Murray family, at night. Again, Andy looks like his about 1 or 2 years older, and sits on a couch, watching television. A young, childminder sits on another couch, watching television with Andy. There is no sign of Andy’s parents. ”Ah yes … The day it all fell to pieces” Murray says in the background, this could possibly be the night when Andy’s parents were killed in a car accident. Suddenly, a phone rings in the room. The childminder stands up from her chair, and walks across the room towards a small table, where the ringing telephone sits. ”Hello?” she says, lifting the phone to her ear. Andy sits watching the television, paying no attention to the television conversation. ”What? Are you sure … Oh … My Goodness, this is terrible … Well what am I supposed to say to him … Alright … Goodbye …” says the childminder, before gently placing the receiver back down. She walks across the room, standing between Andy and the television, before clearing her throat. ”Andy?” ”Hey, I’m watching that!” ”Andy, I’ve got something to say … You’re parents have been involved in an accident. That was the hospital … They both died.” the childminder says hesitantly. A sheer look of shock spreads across Andy’s face for a moment, before it violently changes into a look of pure rage. A seething, and crying, Andy jumps up from his seat, before grabbing his head, and falling to his knees. ”Why oh why!?” Andy shrieks in a fit of sorrow. Slowly, the scene begins to change from that scene of deep unhappiness, to a cold, black room, which sort of resembles a cell. The room is sparsely furnished, with only a chest of drawers, and a bunk bed. Andy Murray sits in the corner of the room, curled up, head in his hands, crying. ”This is where things changed … This is when I turned to the darkside.” Murray says in the background. Suddenly, a creaking of a door can be heard opening, as a care worker walks into the room. This must be the orphanage where Andy spent a lot of his wasted youth. The worker, dressed in a white uniform, walks over towards Andy, a tray of hot food in his hands. He kneels down when he reaches Murray. ”Come on now Andy … You’ve got to eat” the care worker says, suddenly, Andy swipes at the care worker with his right arm, knocking the try right out of his hands. The roasting hot food splatters all over the worker, who shrieks in pain, as the burning kicks in. ”CAN’T YOU JUST LEAVE ME ALONE!?” Andy yells, as the worker runs out of the room Once again, the scene changes, again, we are in a darkened room. A white, pentagram is marked out on the centre of the floor, candles at the corners of inner star. Slowly, Andy, from about 7 months ago, walks into the centre of the pentagram, wearing a black trench coat, black fishnet shirt, and black jeans. Andy flashes a grin to the camera, before lowing his head, as if he was praying. ”Satanism … Dark days indeed. But finally, they are behind me. Now, I am a nomad soul, a wandering heart, a misguided being. Where does the future lie? Where will I be in a years time? We’ll see … We’ll see … “ End.
