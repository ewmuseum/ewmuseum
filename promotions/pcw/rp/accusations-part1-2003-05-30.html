Accusations, Part1

30 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 30th May 2003 [ ] – Sivasian speaking directly to the reader, his thoughts. :: :: - Action, and reflection on other stuff from a narrator Krikorian: ‘’ – Someone speaking [ It was weird, for the first time I felt as though I’d let the Society down. At the pay per view, at my first pay per view, there was a match to decide who, out of me and Syn would progress through to the final of the rising star title match. Guess what happens? I get screwed directly out of a one on one situation. Still , though, it was a mocking of me in the first place. They credited me with a 3 wins and 4 losses record, when in fact I was 4 and 4 all along, I complained to Chang, bitched, moaned, whatever you wanna call it. He understood, he fully understood. People are our there to get us, to get back at us, and those who do not wish for us as the Society to succeed will try anything to try and get us kicked out of the PCW, out of the world spotlight. Would you want people with such believable and radical extreme beliefs, all of which can be backed up, to be shown in front of your kids, to you in person? No, I’m not surprised I wouldn’t either. ] [ Chang decided to treat me, he decided I knew best. I could go anywhere, but since we were in the Middle East, it had to be somewhere around there. Now, I could have gone to Dubai, to Saudi, somewhere with exotic hotels, but after seeing the financial peril that the Society of Order and Logic was in, I decided to head on over to Iraq. I had one of the highest members of the Order, who had particular computing skills, to get me a journalist badge. Whether it was true or false didn’t bother me. I was in. There was a deeper message to me than just having a holiday. What could I do in Iraq? Yes, I could relax, but I could also check up on some American soldiers, people who you’ll know are people I do not trust. And guess you what? It happened again, people tried to rip me off, to get me thrown from the Order, it’s pathetic, and fake accusations were flying. ] :: Sivasian Krikorian walked through the door of his Iraqi hotel. Beside a small man hurrying across the street with a large golden statue he had obviously stolen from a palace of Saddam, there appeared to be no one else about. A small, deserted truck, red, but dusty, stood ahead of Sivasian as he strode out of the doors. The sun shone, it was baking, and people could have cooked eggs on the bumper of that truck, he thought. He repositioned his sunglasses. People in PCW not only lead to fame, but being paid a hefty fee from radio and television stations back in the Ukraine and Russia to do various things for them helped his financial situation. Under Society law, however, he had to pay 90% of his takings towards them, bolstering them even further. :: :: Sivasian saw a man come running towards him, a small, hunched backed man, he looked incredibly ugly. His teeth stuck out in all directions and he made Sivasian shiver just thinking about him. The small man, his dirty brown eyes staring at Sivasian, cupped his hands. He wore ragged green clothing, ripped, torn, and with blood stains over his arms. :: Poor Man: ‘Please sir, have some money for my family. Food and medicine for me to buy with.’ :: Sivasian never gave to the poor. Hell, back in Kiev as a kid, he was never helped by anyone, even when he was hungry and desperate. He had built his reputation up himself, and made money on his own. Even this, though, didn’t stop him in such a situation. Sivasian is usually hated, but this act of kindness shows he is just a human after all, even if he chooses to harm woman, and openly commits to a ridiculous cult. :: Krikorian: ‘Er…I’ve got a little spare cash, give me a moment.’ :: As a typical suede wrestler, Sivasian pulls a wallet from his back pocket. He flips the leather wallet open and rakes through notes and notes. He shakes his head, and flips open the coin section, before delving a few pennies onto the floor for the man to pick up. Suddenly though, the old begger swipes forward, grabs the wallet and sprints. Even though he is 15 years older, Sivasian gives chase, forcing his sunglasses onto his face. He finally captures the man up over 100 yards of sandy road and drags him down by his legs, flooring him. He snatches the wallet back, and kicks the begger. :: Krikorian: ‘Assholes, I’m never giving to the poor again.’ :: The begger looks towards Sivasian with dog pup eyes, hoping the man can forgive and give him some more money. Sivasian, however, shakes his head and has had enough. :: Krikorian: ‘No asshole, you’ll be getting no money from me now. ’ :: Krikorian, in a typical heel act, kicks the man into a slump once more with his over priced leather shoes, and slides the coins out of the mans torn pocket, before throwing them far into the distance. Seeing some candy lying in his pocket too, Krikorian shrugs, and pulls this out. He opens one rapper, slides the sweet into his mouth, before spitting it out. :: Krikorian: ‘That’s terrible. Reminds me of Aunties sweets, horrid, horrid things.’ :: He throws the candy down on the floor and smashes it with the sole of his shoe. Thoroughly satisfied with the work he’s just down. Sivasian looks around and shrugs. :: Krikorian: ‘I was going to pay him for directions towards the American troops too.’ :: Suddenly, he needn’t ask for directions. A heavy black hand grips his shoulder tightly. Sivasian peers over his shoulder, and, in one of those action movie type things. Contemplates hitting him with an elbow and sprinting. However, another 2 soldiers appear around his front. Sensing that he may try something, the original soldier grips harder and speaks in a deep, confident, booming voice. :: Soldier #1: ‘Sir, can you come with us, we’ve got some questions.’ :: Sivasian shrugs, pauses and wonders. Suddenly, he believes they may have seen the incident with the Iraqi man. :: Krikorian: ‘This must be a mistake, for he took my wallet first, you see.’ :: The solider sounds and looks confused. :: Soldier #1: ‘Sir, this has nothing to do with a wallet, or anything of that matter. This involves the death of one of our fellow American soldiers.’ :: Sivasian’s face suddenly turns red as he feels the important and seriousness in the voice of the solider. :: Krikorian: ‘A death you say?’
