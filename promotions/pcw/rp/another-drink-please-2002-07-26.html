Another drink, please

26 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 26, 2002 'Bartender, I need another one...' I pushed aside the glass that was once filled with beer and waited for the next one to roll in. There were four other glasses just like that one lined up on the other side, all falling victim to my inhuman thirst. The bartender was a relatively old man, perhaps around fifty, and he was drying a glass as I called out for him. He walked over, shaking his head. 'I think you've had enough, friend.' I snorted in retort. 'I'll tell you when I've had enough, bartender. Now, pour me another one.' He took a long stare at me, probably wondering if he should do it or not, and finally gave in. 'Fine, I'm not your father. But I'm telling you that you've had enough.' I watched the bubbly paradise refill in my glass and I smiled gleefully. 'My father doesn't drink. Neither do I. I don't think that'll stop me tonight.' It poured down smoothly all the way down my throat, and I lapped it up like a dog. I wasn't a drinker, in fact, I hated it. But when you're feeling low down, and you need a pick me up, a drink is a sure fire way to spice up your evening. Maybe hardcore drugs would give me more of a kick, but with random drug testing going on in the ship, I doubt I would be lucky enough to get by. The bartender took a stool next to me. 'You look like you could use a friend right about now. What say you?' I took a last gulp of alcohol and put the glass down, wishing I had some more. 'I think we all need friends. People to share our sorrows and our worst nightmares with...' 'What about your good times?' He asked. A grimace crossed my face. 'Good times? Now there's something I haven't seen in a while. Almost forgot what that feels like.' The bartender wiped his hands on his pants, nodding his head. 'I see guys like you all the time. You come in here, have a few drinks, and try to drown your sorrows in the alcohol.' He shakes his head. 'It never works though. Not that I've ever seen.' 'I had friends,' I admitted. 'But I pushed them away. I don't want them get hurt for what I have to do.' 'That's always the trick isn't it?' He replied, seeming sympathetic. 'You need friends to be there for you, but you need to keep them at a distance so you don't suck them into your own black hole.' 'What do you usually do?' I asked. 'I think we all have acquaintances, but true friends...now those are rare. Do you have anyone like that?' I nodded my head. 'Yeah, yeah I do. And he's coming soon.' 'See, there you go,' He said with a smile on his face. 'Things are getting better already.' He patted me on the shoulder and went back to his business. As he left, a PCW representative walked up to me. 'What do you want?' I demanded. 'Darkstar and his friends are heading over to the bar,' He explained. 'In order to avoid an incident, I recommend that you return to your room.' I got up, smirking to myself. 'Of course. We wouldn't want to steal any buyrates from the glorious PCW pay-per-view.' I turned around to the bartender. 'Thanks a lot, buddy.' He nodded. 'Good luck to you, Mr. Lexian.' 'So you know who I am, after all.' 'Sure I do,' He said. 'I know everyone on this ship. I'll be sure to say hi to the Dark Age for you.' 'You do that.' I walked away, hearing the distant voices of the Dark Age approaching the bar. Just a few more days, boys. Just a few more days.
