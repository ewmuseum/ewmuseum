Date: not entered Charlie Loc seems to pride himself on being able and willing to take a life at any moment. Heh. He seems to forget that I can be quite ruthless. I could recount some stories to you of people whom I've sent to the next life. Loc wouldn't know of any of these people, because it was before his time in PCW, and besides, these people weren't important to PCW in any way (but to draw a comparison, I didn't hear anyone crying when Juan Borro died). Here are but some of the lives I have taken. Dead men don't tell tales, but their killers do. I tend to give recollections in the first-person. Helps me remember things better. Hope you don't mind. The first person I killed (on the PCW payroll) was some young punk. Don't know his name, didn't need to. He was a waste and a burden on society. He never would have made anything of his life. And to think all I was going out for was an evening walk when I heard a woman's screams... I wake up some time in the middle of the night. I know this because I hear little activity on the street two floors below. I live above a marketplace that closes when the sun goes down due to of a recent crime wave that's hit the city. Call it cabin fever, but despite the risks involved in leaving the house (or staying home for that matter), I feel the necessity getting some fresh air and getting out of the house. Just in case, I grab my sword, which is concealed by a bamboo cane I use to help me walk around, and leave my house. Since I'm blind, I don't keep much furniture in my house, so getting in and out is a breeze. Getting out of the building is usually a breeze, too, as the elevator is two doors to the right of mine at the corner of the hall. Tonight, however, my neighbors had a fight. That much I surmise from the numerous haphazardly packed boxes sitting out, lining the hallway. When I trip over one of them, the husband apologizes. I don't know what he looks like, but based on his timid, bland voice, he's in his mid-to-late thirties and works an unimportant, non-upward-mobility desk position in some office building. In spite of the boxes spread about the hall, I make it to the elevator without suffering any injuries from falling on my face (though that does happen a few times; I should use the cane more as a walking stick if I carry it around so much). I press the button for the elevator, and my index finger sticks to the button; some kids put gum on it. From the smell, it's Bazooka Joe. I call the elevator and it comes to my floor. I get inside; I'm alone, but there's the fresh smell of urine. Ugh, some people.... Once I get to the bottom floor, the path to the door is clear. There's yet another man thrown out of his home, however, right outside the door. I ask the man in somewhat of an aside, 'Can't any of you people work these things out with your wives?' The man replies that he's called Philip many things in their 10 years together, but never his wife. I didn't want to hear that. I walk on, hoping to forget that line. I've always walked that street to get to the bus stop, but I still don't know all the details of the street. I guess they're not important. The street is almost silent, but I hear voices from the bus stop. Two voices. The first is a young kid. Must be just out of high school. He's in a hurry. The other one is a woman in her early to mid-twenties. Her tone is frantic, defensive; she's getting mugged. I make a beeline for the bus stop. It's close by, and there's no streets to cross or obstacles to dodge between my building and the stop, especially at night. At the bus stop, a woman is struggling with the mugger to keep her purse. Normally the best thing to do if someone wants your purse if to give it to them. This woman is either very brave or very foolhardy (two words which are pretty much synonyms). The mugger notices me, but he's set on getting the woman's purse. He takes out a knife and cuts the straps, wresting the purse from the woman's grasp, the mugger runs away like a bat out of Hell. His loud footfall makes it easy to follow him. The woman screams 'He's got my purse!' I know, lady, be quiet. The guy knows I'm following him. Tries to outmaneuver me, but I just follow his steps exactly. Easy to trail someone when your sight doesn't get in the way. I follow him down an alley; the footfall echoes and bounces off of some high brick walls and a trash dumpster or two. The mugger runs into a dead end; his breathing is no longer rhythmic, and his steps circle in place. I catch up, and his breathing turns my way. My size makes him back up slightly, but he's a scared, cornered animal: he's dedicated himself to getting away with that purse. He takes out his knife again and steps toward me. 'Just go away and you won't get hurt,' the kid says in a nervous, squeaky voice. He's a child in a world of men. I hold my position; he's more afraid of me than I am of him. That's what they say about forest bears or something, isn't it? His knees knock together loud enough for me hear them, yet he continues his forward advance. I wait for the right moment... and then spin 360 to my right and release my blade from its sheath. The knife drops to the ground along with the purse; the mugger falls to his knees (my intuition tells me that he holds both hands up to his neck in a futile attempt to block the profuse bleeding). The swiftness of the cut results in little blood upon my blade, so I don't bother cleaning it, I merely return it to its sheath. The young man faintly begs for help. 'Please,' he says. Now, he wants help. Mercy. Pathetic… Later that night, I got back into my house having disposed of the mugger (and somehow getting a date out of it, but the victim turned out to be an old friend) and was going to go to sleep. But things were changed around a little once I found a bag at my front door. I go back to my apartment, but instead of turning in for the night, something comes up (literally something just shows up at my door) and I have to go back out and deal with it. I dressed in rubber pants, flood boots, and a raincoat before I came out, since the rainstorm passing through is covering the entire city. I walk out to the dark forest on the edge of the city, the misting rain beating upon me the entire time. I make my way in between the trees carrying a Jansport bookbag on my back. The wind howls, and lightning flashes brightly across the sky as I continue his long walk, guiding myself along the uneven ground with my walking cane. I come to a steep hill. The grade of the slope has to be at least 65 degrees downward. To make the trek downhill more difficult, the rain makes the ground slippery and muddy, and there are rocks jutting out of the hill. Despite my condition, I manage to navigate the terrain without hurting myself. I get to the bottom of the hill safely. There's more forest in front of me, I can hear the rainfall in the trees ahead, and the flow of the wind is broken. I pull the falling bag back upon my right shoulder, and diligently stride forward. 'Achievement is the third motivator. Everyone wants to achieve, but almost everyone is too lazy to do so. There are two kinds of people: achievers and non-achievers. The achievers, the minority, get what they deserve because they work for what they deserve and more. The non-achievers get nothing because they wait for what they feel that they deserve to come to them. I reach a clearing of sorts; a field of tall grass three feet high. Without slowing down, I continue on my journey, still carrying the lightly pack bookbag upon my shoulder. I get to what I approximate to be the halfway point of the field. I stop for a moment to check my bearings, then turn to my left and I start walking again. The rain is starting to slack up. There's still a fairly strong gust blowing. As I walk, I hear thunder in the distance, rolling my way. I pick up my pace to a light jog. I come to a lake; I here the falling rain beat upon the surface of the water. It's one of the most soothing sounds you'll ever hear. Many small, heavy rocks lay on the shore. I move to the edge of the lake and kneel down to remove the bag from his back. Although a pocket of a thunderstorm is about to move over the lake, I take the time to sit still and absorb the energy from the atmosphere. With my left hand, I tap the bag. It is coarse to the touch, and soaked with rain. The rain pours down now, harder than before. The thunder is getting closer by the second. It's almost directly above me now. I stand up and sign the inverted cross upon my chest. remove two vials, one of balsamic oil and one of olive oil, and sprinkle the oils upon the bag. With that done, I lean over to pick up the bag. The saturated bag is a little heavier no than before. I windup the bag in a clockwise motion with my right arm, turned toward the lake. 'You shall not be a burden to anyone,' I say as I toss the bag into the lake. It hits hard on the surface of the water about 60 yards in from my estimation. Without any further thought or action, I turn and leave the lake. Without any more stops, I go back to my apartment. I take off my gloves and my raingear, and fall into bed, worn out for the night. I turn on the radio mounted on the wall by my bed, and listen to it as I drift off to sleep. '... Police continue the search for the missing son of Congressman Albert Gosser-Haan,' it says. 'While there have been many leads reported to law enforcement agencies in the area, nothing conclusive has yet turned up. A teenage girl by the name of Tiffany Morgan has stepped forward saying that her gang was involved in the kidnapping of the child, and that she was responsible for keeping the child until a ransom demand was made. She admits that she abandoned the child in a residential apartment building, hoping that someone would find the child and report it in, but no one has yet stepped forward claiming to have any credible knowledge as to the whereabouts of Congressman Gosser-Haan's son. We'll bring you more information as it comes to us.' Though no one in PCW at the moment knows this (though Hessian and Vampir Nosferatu are familiar with it), I used to do some 'work' for Satan, when I was made to believe that my resurrection had anything to do with him (I would later find out he just took advantage of me, that punk bitch). And so, Satan, through his servant Mephistopheles (Earl in the corporeal realm, a balding middle-aged accountant with a heavy smoking habit), would give me assignments. People who were going to Hell anyway, and I was just speeding them along to their destination. And one such assignment was a crooked insurance broker named Herbert Duncan... The loud speaker wakes me up, the conductor letting everyone know that Sanadista Park Station is coming up in about a minute. I took a train to get here, because a man by the name of Herbert Duncan has been assigned to me by Mephistopheles to be killed. His cheating insurance practices have caused the deaths of 31 people. At the station, I call a taxi to pick me up and take me to the Marriott Hotel about two blocks from Frenchman's Creek. The driver sounds like Sanjay Petal, someone who really annoyed me when I saw his promos, so I ignore his attempts at conversation and order him to my destination. At the Marriott, I leave the driver his fare and walk the rest of the way to Frenchman's Creek. Outside Frenchman's Creek, there is a guard post that I must get past. I must wait for a vehicle large enough to hide my body comes by for me the slip through without being noticed. To my good fortune, after about five minutes of sitting on the sidewalk, a UPS truck comes up to the ground level guard station. I hide behind it on the passenger side, and wait for it to be cleared for entrance. A short wait, and then I run alongside the slow moving freight truck until I'm inside the gated community. There isn't much activity on the streets of Frenchman's Creek, save for a few joggers that just say 'Hey' to me as they go past. Most of the people are either at work, or inside their houses, hiding from the world they want to feel superior to. Duncan's house is supposed to be the only house with iron bars surrounding it, a gated house within the gated community. He's paranoid. He's guilty. I go around the bars, checking to see how tall they are. The bars are only 9 feet high, but they have barbed wire at the top, so I can't go that way unless I want my clothes getting stuck on the wire. So instead I check for any loose bars. There are none. Determined to get in, I notice two helpful things when reaching inside the bars: a long rope hanging on the bars, and a puddle of rainwater on the ground, left over from yesterday's storm. I don't care who put this here, but I can use it to my advantage. I grab the rope, soak it in the water, wrap it around two bars, and twist the rope tight, so tight it bends the bars into each other. When the two bars are touching each other, I soak the rope again, move over to the pair of bars and twist those together too. It doesn't take long, and I create my own way in. My position is roughly 100 yards from the house, 200 feet from the front gate. I slowly approach the house, listening to my surroundings carefully. I hear a low humming sound coming from two directions in front of me. Laser tripwire connected to an alarm. I hop over the lasers and continue to the house. Having made it this far, I don't mind just walking through the front door. It's unlocked for some reason. Maybe someone just went inside and forgot to lock the door behind them. I don't really care; it's my way in. I hear light, rhythmic breathing coming from a room at the end of the hall to my right. Someone is sleeping in there, perhaps Duncan himself. I lightly run down to the end of the hall and nudge the door open. When the door is open just wide enough for me to fit through, I enter and close the door behind me. I move up to the breathing and locate the head of the person. I gently feel the face and hair. Duncan's wife. I can't go skulking all about the house looking for Duncan, so I have no choice but to wake her and have her give me the location of her husband. With my right hand over her mouth and nose, and my left hand holding her arms together, I wake Duncan's wife. The End:'Where is Herbert Duncan?' Martha Duncan:'In our bedroom at the top of the stairs.' I tell her to leave, and move to the door. I am about to open it when I hear two voices and footsteps approaching the room. I quickly jump out of the first floor window and crouch under it. Robert Duncan:'Hey, mom. Paul and I wanna go to the beach in the Boxster. That's no problem, right?' So, Duncan's a family man. I feel no different about him. He's caused 31 families to lose their loved ones. They must be avenged. Martha Duncan:'Um… sure, that's no problem. But first, could you close the window for me?' She tries to tell them about me. Her intonation changes; the volume of her voice fluctuates. She's nervous. Paul Duncan:'What are you talking about, mom? You know the air's broken. You should leave the windows open until the guy gets here later today to fix it.' Luckily, the teenage sons are idiots. Robert Duncan:'We're gonna take the car, so just try to keep cool until we get back. See ya!' The sons leave, and I climb back in through the window. I tell the wife I'll cut her throat if she ever tries anything like that again, but it's an idle threat. I just can't hurt women. Of course, she doesn't know that. I go out of the room, which I guess to be the study, and head down the hall until I reach the stairs. The wife doesn't come out of the room behind me. I head up the stairs, but hurry back down at the sound of an approaching servant. I hide in the coat closet at the foot of the stairs until the servant passes. When the path is clear, I head back up the stairs to the bedroom. In the bedroom, I hear water running in the shower. A man is singing along to [i]It's Raining Men[/i] by The Weathergirls on some radio station. Rich people. I quietly open the door and move in. The air is thick, and very hot. The steam's most likely so great that I wouldn't be seen. I detect Duncan's exact position, his voice rising above a glass partition about five feet away. Herbert Duncan:'What are you talking about?' I honestly believe he has no idea what I am talking about. The End:'Thirty one, Duncan. That's how many lives have ended because of your greed.' Herbert Duncan:'What?' That was too slow, too high in pitch to not be a nervous attempt at defense. The End:'This house, your cars, everything you own; all of it was purchased with money that never belonged to you.' Herbert Duncan:'I run an honest business!' They always play the honest card, even though it's not true. I'm surprised he hasn't asked the usual 'Who are you?' and 'How did you get in here?' The End:'Did it feel good going to the funerals of 31 of your clients. Did it at all wrench your heart to see 31 families shattered because you had to have an extra $20k a month in income?' Herbert Duncan:'Those clients died of natural causes!' Duncan's lied to others so much he believes his own lies. The End:'Natural causes that could have been prevented had they had enough money to get the treatment that they needed. Natural causes that you are in a way linked to. You killed those people, Duncan.' Herbert Duncan:'No! I'm just an insurance broker! I get paid to set up claims, and get a commission from those claims!' He's breaking down now. The End:'That commission is not collected from your clients, and you know that. Your love of money has ruined you, and killed innocent people. For that, there is no forgiveness. Goodbye, Herbert Duncan.' In a few quick moves, step in the shower, spin Duncan around, and slit his throat. He dies silently. And how could I forget this man? He was a PCW intern. This man was another of my 'assignments.' See, Loc? I've killed, but for a reason. He left a recording of his thoughts on his last day alive (for some reason), so I'll go ahead and play them now. I woke up this morning, ready to start the day refreshed after last night at Greg's party. My girlfriend is still lying beside me. She had even more to drink than I did. She's gonna feel one bad hangover today. Lucky for her it's Saturday and she doesn't work on weekends. She can stay home and recover. I, the more unfortunate of the pair in terms of work, have to leave for the office, even though I went a little overboard at the party. I can still taste the alcohol on my breath. Brushing my teeth helps that matter. After a quick shower, I put on my favorite blue shirt and some jeans I found in the dirty clothes hamper (they look dirty, but they smell alright), and string up my nearly worn-out Nike Air Jordans (99, I believe) and head out the door with a cup of coffee in my hand. Mark Spades came back the next match and beat Rage to win the Hardcore Title by slicing his wrist open with a knife in a First Blood match. Rage had to be rushed to the hospital. And then, it came time for my match. 'At the cemetary, go to the rear plot that is second from the middle path. There should be a small statue of an angel there.' I get out of my car and head to the plot that The End wants me to go to. The End:'After about 30 minutes of fighting, Blade and I were left, Diello, The Crow, Buried Mother, and Apocalypse all eliminated. I had just knocked Blade over the top rope to eliminate him and retain the title. That's when Buried Mother came up from behind and executed a Coffin Drop on me. It's his version of a Sleeper Drop.' This rain's not getting any better. At least I'm almost at the plot now. The End:'I tried to fight out of the move, not expecting to be attacked after the match was over, but Buried Mother locked it in tight. But instead of just attacking me, he botched the move. He pressed his shoulder into the back of my neck, and when he dropped me down, he threw his shoulder forward, bending my head forward farther than it was meant to go. I went limp, and I couldn't feel anything. I saw blood pooling around me on the ground; it was hard to stay awake. From the back, I saw emergency crew rush down the ramp to help me.' I stand in front of the plot, the angel statue right where it should be. I look at the headstone, and my mouth drops wide open. I can't believe what I see. 'Seppuku' Skylar Green, born April 29, 1977; died September 2, 2001. The End:'Buried Mother killed me that night. And that's where my story ends. Your story, I'm afraid, must also end here. Thank you for listening to my tale. I hope yours is recounted in a favorable light.' The dirt plot stirs. From the ground, something penetrates upward. It flies at me faster than I can dodge it. My head… splitting. View is distorting; the world cut in half. A hand, then an arm… then, a body rise from the plot as I fall to the ground, my sight going red. I'm dying. The last thing I see is a sword being pulled from between my eyes. And then everything goes black. (The news later that night aired the following top story.) News Anchor:'Police are still investigating the death of a PCW press room intern. He was found inside Pellen Cemetary, buried in a plot belonging to another person. When police searched his house for clues as to who would kill him and why, investigators found thousands of hours of child pornography kept in the basement, including a camera and bed they believe were used for making their own videos. The man's girlfriend, who ran an after-school daycare for children between the ages of 5 and 12 out of the apartment they shared, is believed to have lured the children to their house. Police also found hundreds of thousands of dollars in order forms for the pornography in the home computer files of the victim. We'll bring you more information as it comes to us….' So, you realize now, Charlie Loc, that you are not the only one to erase the life essence of another human being. I've done it. Multiple times. But I've also done it up close and personal, and was told to by higher powers. Even so, don't think you're going in against a green rookie in the street game on Fusion. Have a nice day. It may be your last....
