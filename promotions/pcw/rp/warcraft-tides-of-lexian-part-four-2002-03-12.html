Warcraft: Tides of Lexian [Part Four]

12 March 2002

Date: March 12, 2002 A Dungeon for every Goblin The trolls were gruesome looking, but I kept myself from staring too much. They didn’t look like Cero at all. There faces were disfigured, with partial masks hiding one half of their faces. But from the half that I could see, there were vicious scars and charring from the skin. I had to hold my lip in order not to say something. One of the trolls breathed down my neck as if I were carrying some secret weapon that the Horde needed to win their war. Anytime I tried to turn around, he would be there, staring at me and making sure I wasn’t trying anything. He didn’t make any bones about it – he didn’t trust me. The other troll seemed a bit more laid back, although I’m sure if I did something that he didn’t like, I’d find an axe in my chest. We came upon a wall with a shield and two axes going across it. I admired the design and chivalry of the art – it was oddly noble. The troll that seemed to be shadowing me saw the decoration and spit on it, leaving a milky yellow fluid dripping from the handle of the axe. The other pressed a brick on the wall, which drew inwards and caused the wall to begin to move, revealing a secret passageway down into the dungeons of the palace. I was impressed. After all, you only see something like that in the movies, right? The troll behind me grunted, obviously ordering me to go down the dark staircase. I sighed and began walking into the corridor. Spider webs littered the entire stairwell, and I looked closely to see if I could find the creators of the webs. I didn’t see a single spider, which made me nervous. Maybe I’m a little too inquisitive, but what other animals create webs? The stairs seemed to go on forever, and they didn’t go straight down. They wound around and around in a spiral shape, which could make even the most seasoned veterans dizzy. I had experience though. I was one of those kids that loved the merry go round, and when it came time to spin around in circles and see how clumsy we were, I would always win. But I wasn’t seven years old anymore. In fact, I was about twenty years older. My old eyes couldn’t take the strain. Whistle. Whistle. I stopped right in my tracks and listened for it again. The troll behind me growled and told me to keep moving, but I couldn’t. My instincts told me something was coming, and I wasn’t about to let some burly thing let me walk right into my own demise. “What is he doing?” The disgruntled troll asked the other. “He’s just standing there.” “Didn’t you hear?” The troll replied. “There was some sort of whistling sound.” The disgruntled troll slapped the other across the face, leaving him reeling against the wall. “Stop being such a coward. You’re beginning to remind me of the peons, always afraid of being in a conflict. If you are so afraid, why don’t you join them as they cut down some timber or mine those infernal gold mines.” “I am not afraid of anything,” The troll announced, somehow finding his backbone. “Good. You’re becoming the apprentice that I knew you would be. There can be no fear amongst the troll kin, my friend. We are not the grunts, who fight amongst each other and try to lead the Horde. We know better. We both know that the Horde could not survive without the stability that the troll kind gives the orcs. And when the time comes to control these lands, the trolls will have power.” “Who else will lead? The ogres?” The troll joked. Even the disgruntled troll had to chuckle. “I’m afraid not. Even the poor ogres have a hard time remembering where they are, not to mention having to run the Horde. No, we will be the leaders of a new revolution, and we will make sure not to forget where we came from.” “Excuse me,” I interrupted. “I’m sorry to break up this plan to take over the horde, but could you please shut the hell up? There’s something down here.” The troll grinned, showing his pointed teeth. “Is the human afraid? Are you worried about what might be down here? I don’t care if you are from this world or not, or what kind of powers you might possess. I will kill you.” “Just try it, two face,” I dared. The troll growled and hit me with the blunt side of the axe, sending me tumbling down the stair case. I had been dazed, and I knew that the trolls would have a field day tearing me a part now. I could kick and scream, but I doubt that would do any good now. Whoosh!” Something moved past me, and it was fast. As I looked up, I was horrified to see a human sized spider flocking towards the two trolls. The first troll looked shocked, but the older one drew his axe and prepared himself for battle. The spider transformed into a semi-human looking building, but it’s eight legs which it held at it’s side gave it away. It was a female, and it looked mean. She drew her sword and dared the two trolls to attack her. “Back away from us, she devil,” The troll said, crossing his axes together. “The undead have no place here. This palace belongs to the Horde.” She just laughed. “This entire world belongs to the Undead, my trollish friend. You made the mistake of getting here before us. Now you will pay a dear price for your trespassing.” “You think you can stop us?” The troll said. “There are two of us and one of you.” She unleashed a web from her arm which caught the younger troll in it’s grasp. The troll tried to escape, but he wasn’t going anywhere. The web uncoiled and drew the troll towards her, and he landed at her feet. She smiled and drove her sword into the troll’s heart. He screamed for a moment, but then he was still. The older troll growled deeply. “You are truly evil,” He said at last. “I am evil?” She questioned. “Your Horde kills innocent people everyday, all in cold blood. You expect me to learn moral values from a person like you? There is some hypocracy in that statement, don’t you think?” I began to slink down the staircase, wanting to get as far away as possible from this powder keg of a situation. I knew it would only get worse from here, and after the victor got up, they’d have a meal out of me. I turned around and ran down the stairs, faster and faster, until all I could hear was the clanging of their weapons together in the distance. I had never run like this before – it was the adrenaline and the rush of dying that drove me. And then I fell, flat and hard, and I rolled down. Finally my body stopped looping around and fell against the flat surface of a floor. I could feel a huge knot on the side of my head and I moaned softly. As I awoke, the sight of a small goblin filled my view. The goblin smirked. “Welcome to the dungeon.” What happened to Sean? There was screaming. It was his own, but then it wasn't. With his screaming came confusion at that revelation. The terror and confusion mixed together and his mind went reeling with the sensation. Vanji's body convulsed when he woke and he almost rolled onto his own shuriken. The smell was the first to assault him. The smell of panic sweat permeated and defiled his clothing. Fear in itself was disgusting, the smell that accompanied it was worse. It was an animal instinct to be afraid. Animal, and thus, less than human. Vanji had seen many of his victims cower in fear and bargain for their life. They weren't human when they did this. Vanji had seen it too many times. But he had been the one cowering just now. Vanji knew it wasn't him that had been afraid though. During his training Vanji had recreated his self, his mind. Taking the role of a surgeon Vanji had sliced away the emotions of fear, pity, and hope, the scalpel being his own will and the stitches his self-discipline. But this had changed long ago. Vanji had gotten involved in the defense of a small village against an Orc raiding party. He had done this to come to the aid of a client at the time and because the Orc's had destroyed the horse, and everything in its saddle bags, he had owned at that time. In the course of the battle Vanji had been wounded internally, by his own rage induced rampage, and several organs were ruptured. They were healed when another hired man of Vanji's client used the souls of the slain to feed, and thus heal Vanji. There were Orcs, Trolls, Taurens, and even Humans who had died in the attack. He had never counted how many there were and he didn't care. The fact that they existed was enough to defile him. The real problem though, lay with the human or humans inside. Constantly during his waking hours, they affected him with their the emotions would feel in whatever situation Vanji had gotten himself into. As though they were trying to make Vanji feel the emotions he should be feeling. They had been there when Vanji discovered where Risknack was from and had caused his fear and the panic sweat. They had been there in a thousand encounters with his own mind. The stitches have burst and the severed emotions were regenerating themselves. Every morning it was a struggle to reach the emotional emptiness Vanji strived for when he used to wake with it. And that struggle continued throughout the day. Vanji immediatley sat up straight, legs folded beneath him, his buttocks on his heels, and began his meditations. Pushing back the memories that were not his own, he could never destroy them as he did with his own memories, he first cleared his mind of all thought. The emotional turmoil that now came every morning swirled inside him and pushed to overcome him now that the barriers of thought were down. For the walls of thought are what keeps man from ever experiencing our emotions in their true form. Vanji then practiced the methods of reaching inner peace that had been taught him. Fighting against the peace he forced upon it, the turmoil seemed to rage and swell even greater, but it eventually conceded. It always did. With tranquility restored, Vanji removed even that. The distractionless void he strived for was reached, and Vanji was unfilled to emptiness. The only emotion he ever allowed was rage, and that was always kept in check so as to keep it from disrupting his thought process and making him do something stupid. But rage had its uses, and it was so easy to vent, to let loose with his anger, that he didn't fight it. With emotion defeated, he stood up and prepared to leave his camp. As he did, he noticed another body laying in the distance. He just shook his head at the sight of another casualty. Who was this? Another innocent soldier, giving his life for the sake of the Alliance? The worst was when he found an average peasant who was in the wrong place at the wrong time. The Horde have no respect for life. As Vanji made his way to the body, he was shocked to see that the man was still breathing.
