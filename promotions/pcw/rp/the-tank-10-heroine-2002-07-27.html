The Tank 10 - Heroine

27 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 27, 2002 You can take my carsYou can take my wordsYou can take my freedomBut don’t take my heroine You can take my clothesYou can take my skinYou can take my bonesBut don’t take my heroine You can take my heartYou can take my passionYou can take my loveBut don’t take my heroine You can take my lifeYou can take my sinsYou can take my soulBut don’t take my heroine ~Leigh Donovan Wednesday, July 24 - Day 19 Life in the Tank isn’t easy for anyone, but it’s especially difficult when you’re branded ‘prag’ - a slave-like person who’s only purpose is to serve his master or masters. This is the life that Leigh Donovan now finds himself living. On the outside, Donovan had everything - fame, fortune, women... now, the Australian vocalist struggles to scrub the grime off the toilet of the cell that he shares with the massive J-Thug. And worst of all, he’s craving a fix like never before. J-Thug knows where to get the drugs, Leigh thinks - J-Thug knows everything about this place... this hell hole. But everything seems so much better when stoned, according to Donovan. Sweat trickles down his forehead and runs the length of his nose. Heroine was his drug of choice on the outside - his wife. He wrote songs for heroine - powerful love ballads that made him millions. Heroine kept him away from everything that tried to tear him away from his life of luxury and excess. Heroine was everything he needed - everything the people who ‘loved’ him swore it wouldn’t be. He needs it now. And there, lying in top bunk of the cell, is the mighty J-Thug. He knows where all the drugs are. He knows everything. If Leigh plays his cards right, he can know too. Playing his cards right is the difficult part. But it’s all worth it in the long run. If he can just pry his consciousness away from this hell hole for a moment, it’s all worth it. He can still taste the semen from the last time he ‘played his cards right’. J-Thug snickers to himself - he knows what Donovan is going through and soon enough, he will toss the puppy some kibble. But for now, it’s just too much fun for the massive leader of the Thugs. Soon, however, he realizes that he’ll have to give Leigh what he craves, or he’ll find it somewhere else. He wouldn’t like having to kill the young man - skinny people with long blond hair are hard to come by in this dungeon. “Get off the floor, prag,” J-Thug says, closing the book that he’s been pretending to read for the last half hour. “Give this to that motherfucking spick across the balcony before he comes here tonight bothering me.” With that, J-Thug tosses the book on the floor where Leigh picks it up. He hurries out of his cell in search of Miggy, who he’s seen wandering the Unit each evening with his little cart of books. “I haven’t seen you around,” Miggy greets Leigh with a smile when the young man peeks his head around the barred gate of the old Latino’s cell. Miggy’s smile is reserved for people he doesn’t consider dangerous, and compared to most people in the Tank, Leigh Donovan looks like a puppy. “Kinda new around here, mate,” Leigh says, popping fully into the cell and holding up the hardcovered book - ‘Tales of Life.’ Leigh tosses the book on Miggy’s bed and then points to it, speaking in his thick Australian accent - “Cellmate wanted me to bring this here over to ya,” he says, “You know, the guy they call J-Thug.” “Oh,” Miggy’s smile fades, “everyone knows him. How’s he treating you?” Miggy’s voice is filled with concern. Leigh is so young to Miggy - youth has always brought out tenderness in the old man. Some say it’s because he felt, and still feels, so guilty for murdering his own child so many years ago. By looking at him now, one would never know him capable of such a crime. “Treating me?” Leigh says with a shaky voice. He looks over his shoulder, careful to make sure J-Thug or one of his loyal Thug gang members aren’t too close by. “He’s treating me ok... I guess.” “I’ve seen guys like you before,” Miggy says empathetically. “I know what it’s like for you. Up until last week, I too, lived in fear of people more... physically... dominant than me.” Leigh can’t help but indulge his curiosity. “Until last week?” “Yes,” Miggy nods. “The Whites used to torment me because I’m Latino. Recently, they gave me quite a beating.” The old man’s story is true, Leigh notices - the proof is still on his face in the form of unhealed cuts and swelling. “What’d you do?” “I got a friend,” Miggy smiles. He then points down to the main floor of the Unit where Jason Snow is watching television beside one of Doug’s Italian gang members. “A friend who knows how to handle things like that.” “And everything’s good now?” Leigh asks, glancing from Snow to his own cell where J-Thug must still be lying on his top bunk. “I mean, they don’t give you a problem anymore?” Miggy shakes his head in a negative response. Leigh turns away from the old man and begins the journey back to his cell - it always seems too short. Out of the corner of his blue eye, he watches the young lion-like man below, in front of the television. Maybe now he won’t have to ‘play his cards’ right, and he can still get what he craves. *** From the Office of the Warden “Drugs come into the Tank via one Doug Hattoni,” starts the Warden from behind his desk. “Everyone knows it, but no one can do anything about it. We can’t catch the bastard. He was slippery on the outside, and even though he’s considerably less staffed in here, he’s like a fucking fish.” “He was good in the seventies,” the Warden sighs. “Now... he’s unstoppable. I like to think that I run this prison. But each and every one of the inmates in these walls will tell you that the man with the drugs runs the prison. ...Why?” “In the Tank, we don’t have Big Macs. We don’t video games. And we sure as fuck don’t have Britney Spears.” The Warden stands up and faces the far wall, his hands folded behind his back. “What we have are drugs, drugs, and more drugs.” “The man with the drugs,” says the Warden turning around to drive his point home, “runs the prison!” *** Thursday, July 25 - Day 20 “How can he protect you from them?” Leigh asks Miggy as he follows the old Latino on his nightly book run. “I mean, he can’t be with you all the time, now can he, mate?” “Oh, no,” says Miggy, pushing his little cart of books back toward the library - the books have to be back by six-thirty, since he has to pass through the gym to get there, which closes along with the library at seven o’clock sharp. “He doesn’t stay with me all the time - he just knows when to show up.” “How so?” “Well,” Miggy begins, pushing his little cart past the television, nearing the pool table where the snickering Nazi’s are beginning to vacate for their nightly workout, “during most of the day, I can avoid them on my own, you see. It’s just at night... well, why don’t you let me show you.” A moment later, the little Latino and his rockstar companion are at the main gym door where a C.O looks down at his clipboard, “Nightly book run, Miggy?” “Same as always,” Miggy responds. He then points back at his companion, “Leigh Donovan - he’ll be accompanying me on the run. He hasn’t been put in the inmate-work program yet, so I told him he could help me out.” “Just for something to keep m’mind from drifting, mate,” smiles Leigh before the C.O waves them through. As soon as Miggy pushes the heavy steel door open, Leigh understands perfectly. Behind them, he can hear the large group of Nazi’s chattering as they approach the C.O. Ahead of them, at the far side of the gym, the Thugs - including J-Thug - are joking while they work out. And there, alone in the middle of the gym is Jason Snow. Snow’s work out is much more serious than the other inmates’. He’s been gaining weight at an accelerated rate since entering the Tank thanks to his new training routine. And in the Tank, one’s appearance can win a fight before it starts. J-Thug, some thirty feet away, is a perfect example of that. His massive structure intimidates everyone in the Tank. Snow knows that his current position as Doug Hattoni’s head muscle requires him to have a similar physical presence - one that invokes fear. As Leigh and Miggy make their way past Snow, they can hear the Nazi’s starting their workout behind them. Jason Snow doesn’t give them a look as they pass - J-Thug, however, does... *** From the Office of the Warden “So if a prisoner can’t offer another prisoner drugs,” the Warden says, leaning on his desk, “then just what can he offer? Protection is one thing. The more physically dominant prisoners can always offer protection. The only question left is... at what price?” *** Friday, July 26 - Day 21 Just moments after the morning buzzer signals the opening of the cell gates, Jason Snow plants himself in one of the uncomfortable metal chairs in front of the television. He’s wearing the same denim jeans that he’s been wearing for three days now - hygiene has become the least of his worries, and he’s only allowed two sets of clothing... he hates doing laundry. Besides, a few minutes from now a pair of armed guards, who’s paycheques are signed by Jason Snow, will arrive with an orange jumpsuit that he’ll have to wear as he’s transferred from the Tank to Friday Fusion. Once he gets there, a PCW employee will have a fresh change of clothes waiting for him. As for now, there’s nothing to do but wait and watch television. His fellow inmates are watching sports highlights - they always do in the morning. Soon, the young lion realizes that he’s not alone. Sitting just a few inches to his right is Leigh Donovan, whose eyes are glued to Snow. “You want something?” asks the young bearded lion. “I, uh...” Leigh hesitates, brushing his shoulder length bleached blond hair away from his eyes. “I wanted to talk to you about something.” Snow shrugs - “Talk.” “Well,” Donovan begins, sitting up straight in his chair, “I’m going fucking nuts, mate. My cellmate, mate - he’s a monster. But he’s got the drugs, mate, and I need the drugs. So what can I fucking do, right mate?” Leigh realizes his words might not be coherent to Snow as soon as they come from his mouth. “What do you want me to do?” Snow asks, looking over at a clock beside the control center of the Unit - just after seven. “Well,” Leigh hesitates, nervously brushing his hand through his hair again. “I hear you help people out. You know, people in my situation. People who need... protection.” “Protection?” Snow snickers, glancing up at the cell that Leigh shares with the monstrous J-Thug. “You want me to protect you from J-Thug?” Leigh is ready to plead - “I can’t take him anymore. I just can’t! He’s horrible! You don’t know what it’s like!” Snow waves the young rockstar off as his own smile fades, “I can’t help you.” “But you can!” pleads Leigh. “Go away.” “You have to help me!” Leigh continues, “You have to! He makes me... do things. I don’t even fucking want to talk about it mate! But I need your help. I need it.” “You want my help?” Snow says to the young rockstar, turning to glare into his eyes. “I need it!” “Then talk to Doug Hattoni,” Snow shrugs. “I won’t fucking move without his say-so.” “But you protect that old Latino fuck!” sneers Leigh in desperation. He draws yet another of Snow’s icy glares, “That’s different,” the young lion says. “I work for Doug Hattoni. My services to Miggy are - lets just say - a part of my contract.” Snow leans in a bit closer to the young Australian rocker, his intimidation in full effect - “Now,” he says in low, dangerous, tones, “like I said, if you want my services, go see the Hatt. Honestly, I don’t give a fuck what you do. Just make sure you do it somewhere else.” Finally, Leigh realizes that he’s trying the patience of the one man in the cell who can do anything about the monster that terrorizes him every night - J-Thug. Quickly, he stands up and hurries away from Snow, craving a heroine fix so much his hands are shaking and he feels like he’s going to vomit. He’s seen Hattoni in the cafeteria and knows that he rarely leaves his cell. Now that his cellmate, Bruno, has past away, Doug shouldn’t be hard to catch alone either. And then suddenly, the only thoughts that Leigh is capable of thinking are large, black, and dangerous - J-Thug grabs him by the color and pulls him under the staircase, out of the C.O’s sight. “What the fuck were you doing talking to that motherfucking white boy?” J-Thug demands in his unreal low tones. “N-nothing!” says Leigh quickly. Physically, he’s no match for the nearly three hundred pound J-Thug, so Leigh simply allows himself to be handled. “Nothing my ass!” “I swear!” Leigh says, a little too loud for J-Thug’s liking. “We were just talking about what it’s like to be, you know, a celebrity - here in the Tank.” “Don’t think I ain’t fucking watching you, prag!” whispers J-Thug. His eyes are large and intense. “If I suspect for one fucking second you’re trying to plot against me, motherfucker, you won’t live to see morning. And you know I can make it happen - you fucking know it!” *** From the Office of the Warden “Nigger, Jew, Spick, Cracker, Coon, Hebe, Honkey, Gook,” the Warden says as he stands up from his desk and puts his hands in his pockets, “Get used to it. That’s how things operate here in the Tank.” “Sure, in most cases, the prisoners don’t exclusively associate with the members of their own race - but when the chips are down, the thing they can always trust about a person is the color of their skin. Does a black person choose to help another black person, or a white person? On the streets, you can argue both ways. But this is the Tank. A different set of rules were established here a long time ago. And everyone who wants those rules to change, ends up changing themself.” *** Snow left for Fusion a few hours ago. Must be nice to have a D.A itching to put you in one of his prisons so much that he lets you serve inconsecutively, thinks Leigh as he cautiously makes his way to Doug Hattoni’s cell. If not for the stomach-turning heroine craving, he probably would have heeded J-Thug’s words and let it be. Leigh finds the fat Italian sitting on his top bunk, like always, in the middle of a game of solitaire. “Mr. Hattoni?” Leigh says just loud enough to get the aging man’s attention. “And you are?” Hattoni replies without looking up. “Leigh,” says the twenty-sx year old Australian rockstar, “Leigh Donovan. I’m pretty new here.” “Donovan,” Doug says to himself, “You got here a couple of days back, am I right?” “Yes sir.” Doug knows about all the comings and goings in the Tank. It’s a good way to keep up on possible customers. “What do you want?” “I need some help, sir,” says Donovan. “Everyone needs help,” Doug says, still not looking up from his cards. “What I need to know is what kind of help you’re looking for.” “It’s my cellmate...” “Jeremy Doris,” snickers Doug, surprising Leigh with his vast knowledge of current events in Unit B. “What about him?” “I need protection from him,” says Donovan, nervously scratching his stubbly chin. “Protection?” Doug says in a serious tone, finally turning his eyes to the young rocker. “Protection from a man like J-Thug - that don’t come cheap.” “I’ll do anything, Mr. Hattoni, anything!” Leigh is getting used to pleading - the Tank has been less kind to him than most. “I just can’t take him anymore. If he finds out I’m here, he’s going to kill me!” “You’re probably right,” Doug says heartlessly. “And on top of all that,” says Leigh, tears welling up in his eyes, “I’m craving heroine like there’s no fucking tomorrow, mate. I wake up in sweat. I’m throwing up all over the fucking place. I just don’t know what to do about all this. I just don’t know!” Leigh has never been so desperate in his life. Since becoming a rich and famous rockstar with his band, Cataract, everything the young Aussie has wanted has been handed to him. “I’ll be honest with you,” Doug says, turning his attention back to his cards. “I can’t do anything about J-Thug. As you may have heard, a dear friend of mine was murdered not too long ago. Murdered by those...” Doug hesitates as emotion builds inside his fat old body, “by those fucking Nazi’s. And believe me, I will have my revenge against those responsible - even if it means starting a fucking war. Now, what you’re asking me to do is disrespect J-Thug, and thus, start a war against the blacks as well as the whites. Even if you could afford the protection you seek, which I doubt, I think you can see that I would be hesitant to offer my services.” Doug turns back to Leigh and watches him fade to a pale grey color. There’s no hope left, thinks the young Aussie. The only good thing about getting beaten, molested, and maybe even killed by J-Thug would be the fact that it takes his mind off the devastating heroine withdrawl. “However,” Doug says when Leigh is about to leave, “I believe that I can help you with that other problem you have. The cravings. You’ll find one of my men in cell 28 - it’s a popular place around this Unit. He’ll fix you up, so long as you have the cash.” *** From the Office of the Warden “How powerful are drugs?” asks the Warden. “More powerful than Big Macs. More powerful than video games. More powerful than Britney fucking Spears. A hardcore addict will risk everything - fucking everything - just for that next hit. That next high. He throws consequence out the window and goes for it. That’s why Doug Hattoni runs the Tank. Prisoners throw consequence to the wind for what he can give them.” *** Leigh rummages through his cell nervously, hoping J-Thug doesn’t return from his nightly work out early. He must keep money around somewhere. Under his mattress? Under the sink, maybe? Nothing.Leigh wishes that he’d brought cash to the Tank with him. He had no idea prisoners were allowed to bring, and spend, money. He regrets the fact that he walked in with nothing but the clothes on his back as much as he regrets getting caught a few months back with more drugs than a police quarantine. Finally, the rummaging pays off. Tucked into an extra pair of J-Thug’s boots, Leigh finds a roll of green and white bills. One step closer to freedom.
