His Story, Part #1

12 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12th May 2003 Back in the day, the USSR was a collaborations of nations today, built on communism. Whilst the parents of Sivasian Krikorian were not fully behind the communist government, they shut up. Hell, if you spoke out, you got sent to Siberia, with minus 65 degree temperatures and no chance in hell of getting out. You worked and worked and worked until you died. If you didn’t work you died, see the catch here? Sivasian bounded along, it was 6:30 in the morning, and his mother had sent him out to get some meat in the forests of Urk for dinner time. If he brought her something really special back, he could have extra cereal for breakfast. Sivasian wore a thick black coat, it was so comfortable, and at the time, no one cared about the anti fur trade. Hell in the mid 60’s no one cared about anything but survival. He bounded out of the small family home, and out onto the street. His thick bear skin boots, made by his father himself, kept him warm at this time of the year. The streets of Kiev where thick with snow, and the filtered ice below his feet cracked and crunched under his hug tight boots. The bitterness and cold didn’t get him at first, with the extra weight of his coat, he felt at ease, at a great temperature. This Sivasian was mentally strong, he had to be. He saw his mother suffer through illness and his father suffer for years down the pits, since he could remember his father had come home sick and ill every day, just to put bread on the table. He readjusted the gun on his shoulder. He had slung it over his 6 year old head and it rested on his back now. The gun, made in his uncles woodwork and metalwork shops, made from the finest Urkan timber, was a high quality gun. In latter years it would never let him down, ahead of these modern day weapons. The timber shone on the bright white ground, like a sheet of white laid across the city. The city was still being redeveloped after the atrocities of World War 2, and Sivasian spoke with the men working there for a few minutes, they gave him bread and hot soup. He smiled as he walked past the posher parts of the town, and pushed his by now red nose against the window of the cake shop, salivating over the freshly baked cakes within. One slice would be his fathers weekly wage, and he could only dream. He carried on though, the air now starting to bite in to his skin. He shuddered slightly and tightened his coat closer. He entered the forests, and tried to find a spot which could shade him from the awful weather. It was cold, even colder than he had seen before. A place no one would want to be, the complete oxymoron of hell, and yet it felt like it, as his eyes watered, and his nostrils had crystallized inside their small tubes of nose. He lay down, making sure the coat took the full brunt of the freezing cold, as he slowly drew the gun from his back. His father had given him a few coins to buy something for himself after his journey, but it slowly fell out of his pocket into the white snow. Sivasian looked around quickly, any money found was anyone’s to keep. He scampered, the money, his fathers love it seemed, made him feel wonderful, it was important. He felt like a sorry child, looking for almost pittance in the ground. Tears welled up in his eyes, and he stood crying for minutes. Finally he consoled himself with anger, and dropped to the floor. His father had also given him 6 bullets. Sivasian lifted the silver tipped bullet and slotted it into the holster of his gun, and pulled back the cock. Thoughts ploughed through his mind of hunger. His father had worked 2 extra hours in the mines for money to give to Sivasian, and as those coins laid still in the ground, he had wasted his fathers time and money. He was, his father told him, to spend the money on chocolate for Sivasian and his sister. He tightened his fist and surveyed across the forests and landscape. He saw nothing but snow for now. He had done this so often though, that he knew how the animals reacted and acted. He smirked at the apparent thickness of animals, they didn’t know such a well trained, at least he thought, shooter was lying 20 meters from them often, until BANG! The bullet penetrated their brain and they collapsed. As he looked ahead, Sivasian heard sounds, sounds of humans, a possible thug squad? The army men getting money from poor families again? He pulled the gun back, and slung it round on his back again, before pulling a hunting knife from his belt. He held it to his hand to check for sharpness, before scurrying off to stand against a tree, hoping the snow blowing heavily over the forests would create a diversion, or hide him completely. The noises got closer, and when he saw the dark blue and green uniforms of the army officers he gulped hard. They were renowned for creating taxes ‘on the spot’ and taking money from poorer families in the region. If you didn’t cooperate, you were shot , and deemed to have ‘discussed the downfall of communism’ within their ears. He looked around, desperately trying to make out more of the figures coming towards him. He stooped down, and quickly piled some snow up , to make a small hill of snow. He quickly swings his gun round and lays it on the ground. Sivasian drops to knees, hoping the soldiers don’t hear him in the almost silent morning. He sweeps the snow over and covers it. He pushes his back once again against the tree. He was used to this feeling, the feeling that adrenaline gives him. It makes him hot, yet outside his body is freezing, his skin is freezing, yet his inside is emotional and adrenaline warm. He’d been through this many times before, but this time the soldiers came closer than ever. A bit TOO close for him. He knew his whole families future and his future relied on him not allowing them to find his rifle. The one thing his family had cherished and kept, the one thing which was a constant flow of food and, when additional was caught, of food. They could sell the wild forest deer meat for a lot of money to the top Kiev restaurants, to places where the posh people ate. The people he HATED. Suddenly, as his thoughts flooded his brain, the soldiers approached. Sivasian panicked and tried to step away towards another tree, but he had been court. ‘Hey son, where are you going?’ asked one of those soldiers. Sivasian had split seconds for thought and an answer. He hoped he could find something in a brain which was smarter than most children his age, in fact smarter than a large majority of the Russian and Ukrainian population. He thought about the soldiers weaknesses, about their hatred for certain things, and one thing stood out above the usual stuff. Sivasian raised an eyebrow slightly. ‘I was just err…thinking about how I’d love to kill one of those Americans, the assholes, they don’t belong to be here in the land of Ukraine.’ He says, smiling sweetly towards the soldier, still thoroughly expecting them to beat him senseless with a sharp shot from the butt of their rifle. Instead, one of the soldiers pulls a shiny new coin from his pocket. Sivasian notices to be at the higher end of the Ukrainian money market, and could buy a lot with it. ‘Well kid, fight on, we want those Americans beat to a pulp, and kids like you will serve this country proud in the future.’ He says, handing the coin over. ‘Thank you sir.’ Says Sivasian, putting the coin in his pocket, ‘good day, sir’. The soldiers, three of them, nod and continue walking, as the thumping heart of Sivasian continues. ‘Someday, someday,’ he says, ‘I wanna be just like them, killing Americans.’
