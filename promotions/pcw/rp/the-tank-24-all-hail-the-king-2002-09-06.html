The Tank 24 - All Hail the King

6 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 6, 2002 ﻿*********The Tank __24—********* All Hail the King It’s the way I’ve never seen a mirror with mudand you’ve never seen a mirror with mud.What’s the world like for those with muddy mirrors?Is their mirror muddy or is our sight impaired? ~Leigh Donovan Tuesday, September 3 - Day 61 Lying on his back in the hole, wearing only the tattered pair of jeans that no C.O was braveenough to take away from him, Jason Snow pumps stomach-crunches in rapid-fire. Sweatbegins to form small pools in the crevices of his well-shaped abdomen as he pushes himselfharder and harder. Snow’s new obsession with physical strength has grown out of a need to look as physicallyintimidating as possible for the most part - however, there’s also the fact that he needssomething to do to pass the time, and pushing his body to its limits seems like the mostconstructive and worthwhile activity. His hair, moist with sweat, collects the dirt and grime on the floor of the small filthy cell. JasonSnow is not the man he was when he entered the Tank - his values have changed. Now, insteadof living a life of excess, the only excess available to him is survival. Even that doesn’t seem so valuable anymore. His sister-in-law, Krista, hates him. She may not have said it, but he knows. The way shelooked at him, or rather, the way that she wouldn’t look at him. Then again, he thinks, maybe that’s best. Maybe she should cut him out of her life. Other than for money, he’s really not much good to her now. It seems that every time their pathshave crossed over the past six months, the results have been disastrous, despite the best ofintentions. Snow’s a one-man wrecking crew, and that’s not only when he’s in the ring. He’s also left hisfamily, and himself, in his own wake. All lives are destroyed, and he’s not sure he can evermake things right. Maybe it’s not even worth trying. Snow sits still for a moment on the floor of the dirty cell with his arms draped over his pointedknees. He stares up at the corner between the ceiling and the front wall of the small modern daytorture chamber and his eyes begin to drain of hope. All that’s left is a hardened machine of destruction in the form of a golden haired lion. It nolonger knows hope, nor does it see anything beyond these walls. And finally, Snow realizes that maybe his purpose in life isn’t to make the world a better place. Maybe, in the movie of his own life, he’s not the hero. Maybe he’s the villain. Suddenly, the door crashes open, sending a blinding light down on the hardened figure of JasonSnow. A C.O, with his nightstick already in hand, steps in though his features are unknown toSnow thanks to the sudden burst of life. His voice, however, is clear as day. “Prisoner,” he saysto Snow, tapping his nightstick in his open left palm, “your time’s up... right in time forTurmoil.” Snow can’t help but close his eyes, which are still stinging from the mace that was sprayed inthem on Sunday. He holds his bulging right arm up to shield his sensitive eyes from the blindinglight. “On your feet,” the C.O orders harshly. “I don’t want any trouble out of you.” *** From the Office of the Warden “King Midas had good intentions,” the Warden shrugs. “All he wanted was to turn everything togold. Soon, however, he realized that maybe gold wasn’t so great after all. What happened withhis family? Did they appreciate the fact that he was turning all of their belongings, maybe eventhem, into gold?” “Well, what would happen if one day Midas told them, ‘Fuck you! I want thegold’.” *** Wednesday, September 4 - Day 62 It’s been well over a week since Doug Hattoni’s death, and ever since, Tim McDonald hasprospered wonderfully. Everyone knows that he’s the man to come to when they want drugsnow, and even though he doesn’t have his own pyramid of dealers beneath him, like DougHattoni did, he knows that in due time, he’ll have every bit as much power as the old Sicilian. What’s more, McDonald’s mysterious persona has made him a feared man within the walls ofthe Tank. No one suspects McDonald of the murder of Doug Hattoni, but they have beennoticing the sudden disappearance of the rest of his enemies, most notably, every other might-bedrug king of the Tank. “Timmy,” a voice interrupts young McDonald as he’s watching early morning sport’s highlights. “We’ve got to talk.” Tim turns around to find himself staring at Tony Lakarni, one of Doug Hattoni’s most influentialsoldiers. “Tony,” McDonald nods, turning around in his seat to face the much larger Italianman. “What’s up?” “Some of the guys and I have been talking, McDonald,” says Tony as he takes a seat beside Tim. Immediately, Tim begins to worry, though he tries not to let it show in his face. “About what?” “About Doug Hattoni,” says Tony in a quiet voice. McDonald hopes he isn’t sweating as hewonders if this Italian knows about McDonald’s subtle hand in Doug Hattoni’s death. “Doug was a great guy,” McDonald says quietly and respectfully with a nod. “I really learned alot from him.” “I know,” Tony says, putting his hand on comfortingly on Tim McDonald’s shoulder. “And youwere very close to him - he told everyone that you were his ‘protege’.” Tony is a tall thin man with pitch black hair that he wears slicked straight back. He’s no longera young man, at thirty-seven years of age, but he’s still considered ‘young blood’ in the Hattonifamily. Tim McDonald has heard rumours that when Tony gets out of prison, he’ll be a mademan in the Hattoni family. This is the type of man that Tim McDonald wants to prove himselfto, and ultimately, climb over in the powerful Hattoni family ladder. Now, McDonald only wishes he knew what the next words from Tony’s mouth will be. DoesTony know? And if so, how much does he know. He soon learns that Tony knows nothing at all. “And it’s because Doug thought so highly ofyou,” Tony says, “that we want you to lead the Italians in the Tank.” Tim smiles, but has to ask - “But why me? I’m not Italian.” “What? You think we didn’t notice?” Tony says with a chuckle. He then gets serious and leansin close to Tim. “Look, the facts are simple. With Doug in the ground, no one brings moredrugs into this motherfucker than you. That makes you potentially the most powerful man in thewhole fucking prison. We see that, Timmy. Every fucking Italian in this shit hole sees it. Anddon’t think we don’t know that you did small-time work for Doug on the outside. All that shitcounts, Timmy - all that shit. Every rock you sold out there, everyone who put in a bag - they allscored you points. But when you got in here, and the man himself, Doug Ha-fucking-ttoni, putyou under his wing... the Italians can’t ignore that.” Tim McDonald smiles and nods, but says nothing. “Listen, kid,” Tony says quietly. “Out there, I was pretty hot shit before they locked me up. Hattoni’s family were making a leader out of me. But you... I ain’t fucking nothing compared toyou. But I see that. And when you get out, they’ll see that too. You’re going places, kid. AndI’m not talking about being a fucking henchman.” “What are you talking about?” “High up, kid,” Tony nods with a smile. “As high up as you can imagine. But first, you proveyourself to us in here. Hattoni had a lot of motherfuckers in here, and I’m not just talking aboutthe Italians. I mean legit Hattoni family members, Timmy. And when they get out, they’ll talkabout you. Just like I’m going to talk about you.” Tim stares silently at the tall Italian. “Lead us, Tim,” Tony asks again, staring straight into McDonald’s blue-green eyes. “Lead us.”
