Diversion I

15 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 15th September 2002 The scene opens with Redemption Song sitting in front of a fire pit in the North woods of Wisconsin in a town called Cable. The camp is very crude - just two tents, a fire pit, a supple of firewood and a rental Ford Bronco with the back open revealing a healthy supply of blankets, marshmallows and bottled water. In an effort to save some finances in the wake of the imposing Front Office Tax, Jim Surge and Ben Nighthorse Campbell cower near a freshly built fire and warm themselves as the cold front and winds begin to howl across the dark, lonely Midwestern lakes. Both wearing brand-spanking new blue PCW sweatshirts and their normal trousers (jeans for Surge and those loose-fitting tan things for Campbell), the half-brothers enjoy the warmth and savings of a cool early fall fire. Jim: I love the autumn. Ben: Technically it isn't fall until September 23, the Autumnal Equinox you know. Jim: You damn Native Americans, always so technical about everything. Ben flashes a bit of a scowl back up at the cowboy as he lets out a big sigh. Ben: Yeah, yeah... and from listening to your history books you would think that we all were bloodthirsty savages looking for whatever reason we could to scalp your asses. Jim: Heh... I wouldn't go that far now.... Ben (interrupting): Like hell you wouldn't! If not for the stupidity and pride of the independent tribes, we would have banded together and crushed you. Remember when the filthy colonists were able to repel the Hessians because the colonists were involved with the land and the Hessians could care less? Jim: I thought the Hessians were all killed in their sleep on New Year's Eve thanks to George Washington's tactical genius. Ben stands up, pulling a red-and-blue woven burlap blanket around his shoulders and looking back at his half-brother with utter disdain. Ben: Ah yes, American genius. The same genius that wants to 'save the world' but tried to utterly annihilate any American Indian in its way under some false propaganda that we were 'a threat.' That was a smart tactical move, eh? Jim shifts his weight uneasily in front of the fire and looks up to catch his half-brother's eyes. Campbell looks back down at him with the hatred of the ages and Jim, despite the fire's physical warmth, feels a complete cold shiver wash over his body. Jim: Yeah, well, I don't really know too much about that. Hey man, now that Fusion’s over, why don't we both take off and go back to my ranch in Arizona and rest up for Turmoil? Ben: The infamous Steel Bell Ranch? Remember what happened last time? Jim tries hard to swallow back a smile as he indeed does remember the bad vibes between Jim's older brother Virgil and Ben. Jim: Virg said he was really sorry about all that and that next time you came you wouldn't have to eat your dinners out in the pig peg with the hogs. I think he was actually being sincere. Ben: You cowboys are all the same. Just whatever you need to do so that you have fun... damn everyone else and thousands of years of tradition. Jim shifts uneasily in front of the fire before attempting to switch the subject. Jim: While we're here... what are we going to do about Turmoil? Ben just folds his arms and shakes his head at the gall of his half-brother to start talking about wrestling when there is still so much to figure out regarding their own feelings towards each other. But Nighthorse just lets it drift for now, knowing that sooner or later, the deep-seeded ill-will that the half-brothers naturally have towards each other and each other's culture will come to a head. This night, in front of the roaring fire pit, was not the time. It was a time instead to recharge energies and prepare for the long and torturous rise up the PCW ranks until they were wearing the Tag-Team Championship belts - not the quick rise to what seemed to be a 'setup-to-fail' early non-title shot. Ben (whispering): Focus... we must keep our focus. Jim: Hello? You there? You falling into one of those crazy trances? Ben steps forward and puts a very firm hand onto the shoulder of his half-brother, Redemption Song's powerhouse Jim Surge. An expression of deep and solemn respect quickly fades away into a flash of pop culture brilliance. Ben: Why don't we hold a contest? Jim's eyes immediately light up. The cowboy springs up to his feet and places both of his own hands onto the shoulders of the Apache Ghostrider. Ben: A valet contest. We’ll audition a bunch of different chicks out at your brother’s ranch. Jim: He’s your brother too. But I think this sounds great. Maybe we can get a little free publicity from all this. Ben and Jim look at each other with a smile. At the dawn of a glorious and punishing career, the two half brothers enjoy a moment that they will long remember. After sufficient time passes, Jim and Ben make the necessary mobile phone calls and book themselves the next available flight to Arizona.
