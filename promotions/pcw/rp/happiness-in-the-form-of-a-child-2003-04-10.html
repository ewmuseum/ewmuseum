Happiness in the form of a Child

10 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/10/03 Twinkle Twinkle Little Star, how I wonder what you are. The melody coming out of four year old Evan’s mouth as he painted himself a picture with watercolors brought back memories in my mind. It was back in August of last year right after my brother Brandon had passed away that I two found myself singing that tune over and over again to comfort myself the way a young child comforts it’s fear of the dark. Each night I would look up to the stars and remember what had happened that crushing August night and how it should have never occurred. Like a broken record the words from my brother, “Don’t worry about me. I’ll be fine.” echoed through my head. And now those words had come back and haunted me once more, not in the tone of Brandon’s voice though but in the tone of the man who was at one time supposed to be my husband, Chris Storm. Ever since the wedding between Gaz and I that was never supposed to happen Chris had become that broken record and repeated that same response, “Don’t worry about me, I’ll be fine.” Don’t Worry, Be Happy Evan chuckled to himself as he painted a smiling face on the paper with a mixture of yellow and black paint. It might have seemed foolish but perhaps I should take the advice coming from the mouth of the four year old. If I could get my mind off Chris and losing custody of both Christa and Alex perhaps I could get happy and everything would change for the better. Then perhaps if I could make everything better I could beat Gaz, Nerezza, and even Chris and capture that number one contendership for the television title. “Tessa I’m all done.” Evan said as he took the piece of paper sitting on the table into his hands and held it high, proud of his work. The crooked lines of black and yellow paint were spread all over the paper and slowly dripped down the paper making an even bigger mess of what Evan considered to be a masterpiece. Yet through this all the paper still managed to maintain the form of a smiling face, with black streaks running down the sides that could be perhaps mistaken for hair. It had reminded me of my own life as of late. Perhaps that just like the black paint overcoming the paper yet still remaining in the form of the smile I could do the same with my life and somehow overcome these obstacles thrown in my way and walk out of them all with a smile on my face knowing that I was the number one contender for both the cruiserweight and television titles. But what would Chris think of this all. Would he take it as me trying to get rid of him now that Gaz was forced into my life because of a prank gone wrong? It wasn’t like I was trying to toss him aside and out of the picture, I just needed to prove to the world that I was more than one half of the Schmoopies and he needed to understand that. I couldn’t live my whole life attached to his back, especially if his back was going to be turned to me and my ideas because of a name I wished not to bear. So instead of going upstairs and begging Chris once more, I came up with an ingenious plan that would involve the picture that Evan had just painted for me, and the power that a child carried that could render even the most heartless of people on their knees shedding a tear. “Hey Evan, why don’t we give that picture to Chris as a present.” I suggested, looking the picture over and then spotting a marker which too would be used as a tool in my plot. “Sure.” He agreed the way any child would to anything someone older to them suggested. “Good, why don’t you write a little something on the back of it for me.” I said, manipulating the child like a puppet on a string. “Okay.” He agreed, taking the blue marker from my hand and flipping it over to the blank side of the paper.” “How about you say, Dear Chris, We love you. Please feel better.” I said, sounding like a preschooler myself with the simple sentences and phrases used. “Okay.” He agreed once more, taking the marker to the paper. Instead of writing the actual words though it was scribble in which the only word that could be read was the name Evan. He then handed the marker over to me and said “You sign too.” With the simple motion of my wrist I signed the card and thanked Evan with a hug, whispering in his ears to deliver the card to Chris, knowing very well that Chris couldn’t say no to Evan seeing as though Evan was like a son to him. Now for the hard part, getting Chris to come downstairs and listen to me. I could only cross my fingers and hope that my plan would work, and that Chris would be won over by the cute side of not only Evan, but by the softer side that dwelled over me the majority of the time.
