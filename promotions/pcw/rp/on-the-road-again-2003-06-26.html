On The Road...~AGAIN~!

26 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 26.06.03 It’s been close to half a year since we’ve last seen the likes of Abner. Now, returning to the PCW, he is alone. Duke and Thomas, and the rest of those affiliated with the Redneck Alliance are gone. He’s just recovered in a small hospital, back in his home state of Vermont, after a freak accident involving a chainsaw. He’s knee’s quite sore, and though his heart aches, grudgingly facing the loss of his former PCW companions, his spirits are high and his confidence glows. “Ha”, Abner spits, kicking the side of his tire, “Now I ‘member why ya only cost me fifty bucks!” Abner shrugs, taking the advice of his doctor, and not letting his anger pump up his stress level. The flat figure of the limp circular mold of cheap black rubber is slowly hissing out its last breaths, forcing out the air that is its blood. And soon, it will be dead. And for once, Abner just doesn’t care. Popping open a cold one, he leans up against the truck and chugs down a beer. “Ah, that’s the stuff!” Abner shivers in delight, tossing the empty can aside, and pulling out another. A few moments later, that ones gone too. And then another and then another, until finally, the entire contents of the six-pack of Budweiser is gone. He feels better now. Much better. He reaches into his side pocket, relying on the comfort of cash to keep him cool. It’s working. He steps back and smiles at the old body of his junk pickup. A late-eighties model Ford F-150, this old truck has well over 150,000 miles on it, and a whole lot of memories. The tires gone flat (Abner hasn’t changed them in years), but besides for that, she’s in pretty good shape. Abner straightens out his dusty old John Deere hat before getting in his truck. The grimy green and yellow cap is two sizes too small and rests on the top of his head, closer to that of a decorative figure than to a hat. A lot has changed since he last stepped foot in a PCW ring, and as he gears up the bang-wagon, heading down the winding dirt roads of his home, his mind is filled with unlikeabe images of guilt and regret. As former Director of Redneck affairs it was his duty to keep the members in line…keep the family alive. But now they’re gone, all gone, and there’s nothing he can do about it. His career, like that of his driveway, has been short and straight but as the long and winding road unfolds before him, he knows that they’ll be change. Change. A natural element that all rednecks hate. He absolutely despises the word. Change. What an evil, evil word. When Abner thinks of change, he thinks of the events and circumstances surrounding them that have changed and altered his life. Most of them bad. Complication. To him, complication, the lack of love and simplicity which he favors, brings chaos. The government, the law…the PCW front office…just the whole system, any part of it, or, for that matter, any system besides for the natural system of life…love, nature, beauty…are the only good things. He’s been abused, he’s been in jail, he’s been beaten down like a dirty dog…and he’s been in the ring. But nothing, nothing has prepared him for change. Every day, a new and entirely separate event, it seems to him, as someone who cannot make connections, that life simply doesn’t make sense. “No, ah…” Abner leans out the window of his truck to spit out some tobacco juice, “it just doesn’t make any sense…any sense at all.” Peering out his window, all of a sudden, Abner slams on the brakes. He launches forward in his seat, and smashes his head against the dashboard. Fortunately for Abner, his time in prison (and months in PCW) has done him well, and he simply shakes it off. “Damn it!” His steering wheel has gone slightly crooked, and a small, three-inch long crack runs diagonally down the left side of his windshield. “Ah!” he screams, punching the seat. He never wore a seatbelt in his life, and this time is no different. Luckily, his out-of-date truck doesn’t have an airbag either. So without having to worry about these things, he forces the wheel back in place, and kicks open the open the door. Fists held at his side, hoping to God he didn’t kill someone (‘cause if so, he’d kill ‘em again), he smiles as he walks around the corner of his truck, relaxing his fists and letting his arms fall to his side. Only five or six feet in front of him lies a tan colored pile of fur. The lifeless, curled up body of a full-grown Whitetail Deer lies still in a pool of blood. “Well now” Abner chirps, “I reckon I found some dinner!” Hands on his hips he looks back in forth from the dent in the front of his truck…the crushed center of his old, rusty metal bumper…to the deer, to the bumper, and then back to the deer. And finally, with his minds made up, Abner walks over and grabs the deer by the back hooves, and begins to drag it back to the truck. “Huh” he grunts, “this ain’t so bad.” Abner lifts up the deer and tosses it into the back of his cab. For once, there’s a change in Abner’s life that doesn’t seem to mind. “This ain’t too damn bad…” He gets back in the truck, slams the door shut, and starts up the engine. “Well maybe it is time for me to get back in the ring, after all.” And he speeds off down the road, wind at his back…
