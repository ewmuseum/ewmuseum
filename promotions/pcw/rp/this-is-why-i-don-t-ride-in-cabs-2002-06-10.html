This is why I don't ride in cabs

10 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6-10-02 Brandi walks down the street nervously after receiving the call from Lindsey about an hour ago. Her head is throbbing, but that will soon go away as those painkillers the doctor prescribed to her seem to be working marvels. She’s taken a couple every day now and her headaches have virtually disappeared. The nights of Los Angeles are pretty cool, even during the summer, so Brandi has on a pair of shorts and a tight blue t-shirt. She welcomes the cool breeze on her exposed skin. Brandi- What could Lindsey want? I’ll bet she wants to lecture me about leaving Malvain at the restaurant by himself the other night. I’ll just have to explain the situation to her. Surely she would have never eaten dinner with a vampire. Newspaper blows past Brandi’s feet as she hurries down the crowded streets of LA. She rounds a corner and nearly collides with a man leaning against a wall, playing his guitar. Brandi- Oh! I’m really sorry. Man- It’s alright. The man continues playing his sorrowful tune and Brandi digs through her purse to find a coin to throw in his open case. She continues down the street, walking past a dark alley. She hears a noise and freezes. Voice- Miss! Miss! Come here for a second, would you? Brandi looks around. The voice was coming from the back of the alley. Brandi stands in the opening, trying to squint through the darkness to find the owner of the voice. Brandi- What do you want? Voice- Just come here for a second and I’ll show you. Brandi backs away from the alley slowly. Brandi- No…really that’s ok. Brandi turns around quickly and continues walking. She looks back behind her for a minute and is glad to find that the voice from the alley hasn’t followed her down the street. Brandi- I hate walking around LA at night. Gee, I hope Lindsey isn’t mad at me for leaving that Malvain by himself. Maybe that’s not what she wanted to talk to me about. Maybe she just wanted tickets to a show again, or something. She was pretty excited when I told her that I won my first match back against Jaden. She did get mad though when she saw Bethany come out at the end. Maybe she just wants tickets to Violence Jack? Brandi hurries out of the side street and is thankful to be on one of Los Angeles’ busier roads where streetlights shine down on everything, and there are no surprises. Cabs rush past eager to get their fares for the night. Brandi whistles and waves her hand in the air. Brandi- Well, let’s hope all she wants is tickets to Fusion. We’ll soon find out, I guess. A cab inches to a stop at the curb in front of Brandi. She opens the door and hurries inside. Inside, the cab smells no better than it looks. The smell of booze, body odor, and cigarettes float up to Brandi’s nose. She rolls down the window, only to find it stuck a little more than halfway up. The cab driver turns around to face his new found passenger. He wears a dirty baseball cap that was, at one time, blue. His breath smells like tobacco, and his long beard has food stuck in it. Brandi shrinks against the door of the cab. Driver- Where to? Brandi- Starbucks. Driver- I’ll have you there in a flash. Brandi is thankful when the cab driver turns back around and pulls out into the road. She sighs and tries to relax. Brandi- It’s just nerves, that’s all. It has to be. I’m just nervous about my second match back. PCW has been taken over two shows in a row now, so I guess I’m just trying to figure out what’ll happen on Friday night…if Bethany will be there. Brandi tries not to think about her match on Friday night. Instead she turns to the side and looks out the window. The cab driver is going slowly through narrow back roads. Brandi looks outside with concern. She’s not quite sure where they are. She can’t remember any of the streets she’s traveled on since they left. Actually, they’ve been going down back roads ever since they left. Brandi knocks on the plastic partition between the driver and herself. Brandi- Hey! Where are you going? Driver- I’m just taking a few shortcuts to get you there a little faster. Brandi- Shortcuts? Brandi grows more worried when the driver doesn’t respond. She looks out the window and can’t help but notice that they’re gaining speed rapidly. The safeness the streetlights had brought her is now disappearing far behind them. Brandi sinks into the seat, not knowing what to do next. Brandi- Slow down a bit, would you? This only seems to make the man drive faster. He turns around for a second and Brandi can almost swear she sees the man laughing. He whips around the corner and takes the cab for a wild ride through an alley. There’s a loud crash of trash cans flying over the hood of the cab, that makes Brandi jump. A car turns in front of them and the cab driver manages to veer out of the way just in time. Brandi- Do you think this is some kind of sick joke or something? The man in the front seat ignores her question and continues to face forward, watching the terror he is causing the people on the street, not to mention Brandi. Brandi- So, what are you going to do? Rape me?…That’s not going to work. I’m a professional wrestler. I get paid for fighting people. I don’t think you’d be able to do it. In fact… The man ignores Brandi’s attempts at a threat and he turns onto a street blocked by orange pylons. He’s driving down the middle of a farmer’s market, sending people scattering for the safety of the sidewalks. Various types of fruit hit the cab, giving it a splatter paint effect. The cab driver turns on his windshield wipers to clear the mess off the window, but this only makes it worse. He veers back and forth down the road. Brandi bangs her hand against the partition. Brandi- Hey! Stop the car! I’ll walk. The cab driver shakes his head. Driver- Oh, no you won’t. Brandi looks on in disbelief as the man drives the cab up a curb and through a small park. Grass flies up behind the car as he does donuts through the grass. Brandi closes her eyes, hoping that this is a dream and isn’t happening. When she opens them, she only finds that this is happening, and it’s not a dream. The man is sending ducks flying as they hurry to get out of the way of the madman. Brandi closes her mouth tightly, afraid she’s going to be sick. Finally, the man returns the cab to the road and continues to drive, acting as though nothing’s happened. Brandi- Could you stop the car? I think I’m going to be sick! The man ignores Brandi’s pleas. She tries the door, only to find that it’s somehow been locked from the outside. She scoots across the vinyl seat to the other door and tries to open it. It too, has been locked on the outside of the cab. Driver- Would you just sit still? We’re almost there. Brandi- If you’d just stop the car now, I’ll pay you what I owe you and I’ll be on my way. Driver- I’m sorry, Miss, but I can’t do that. Brandi- Why not? The driver has gone back to ignoring Brandi’s questions again. She sits there in the seat, frustrated. She tries to slide the plastic partition open, but it won’t budge. The driver notices her doing this and turns towards her. Driver- I wouldn’t do that if I were you. He speeds up, turning down yet another back road. Just when Brandi thinks she really is going to be sick, the man surprises her by slamming on the brakes. Brandi is thrown forward and he head bounces off the plastic partition. She leans over in her seat, holding her head. When she realizes that they’ve stopped, Brandi looks out the window. There’s nothing to be seen but a dumpster at the far corner of the empty ally. The driver turns towards Brandi. Driver- This is where the real fun begins. He slides open the partition, and tries to slide his head and arm through. Brandi kicks the man on the head a couple of times, before turning her aim for the window next to her. One good kick later, the window shatters, sending glass flying through the ally. Brandi squeezes through carefully, but ends up cutting her hand on the glass. Brandi- Ow! Damnit! She stops for a minute, staring down at her hand. It’s impossible to see anything in the dark, so she gives up and turns back towards the cab. Inside, the driver is squeezing his way back into the front of the cab. Brandi runs down the ally, towards a streetlight at the end. She hears the door to the cab being closed, so she runs a bit faster. Driver- Wait! You forgot to pay the bill! By now, Brandi is out of hearing range. She welcomes the light and the safety of the people hurrying about. She turns to her left, and walks off quickly keeping her head towards the ground. If her head had been up, she would have seen that she was just in the back ally of Starbucks, where Lindsey was waiting.
