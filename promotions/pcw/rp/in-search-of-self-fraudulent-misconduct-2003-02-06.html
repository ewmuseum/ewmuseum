In Search Of Self: Fraudulent Misconduct

6 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06-02-03 The Basement. Hotel management had thrown Skitzo into workman’s quarters. In the early 20th century when the hotel was new, folk employed by the hotel were allowed to stay in a small 10ft x 12ft compartment in the basement. The location of the room meant that it was always warm, being situated next to the boiler and having pipes running through the walls of it. Skitzo had set up a nice little home for himself here, regardless of the circumstances surrounding it. So there he was, squashed into this humid, warm little room in the corner of the basement, while his room was cleaned out and the hotel was awash with disinfectant after the sheep scandal. The act had gotten his name in a couple of the local newspapers, other than that, people knew it was just another little Skitzo thing. He wasn’t persecuted of course. This was worse, he could smell the stale warm air as it invaded his lungs, and resorted to covering his face with one of his shirts. This kind of smell was worse than any other. It was a constricting odour. Choking. As you breathed in it felt like your lungs were closing up. Alternatively, Skitzo could easily leave his little room and venture out into the wide-open world for this or that, but he reckoned it best to lay low until the disinfectant had cleared and his name wasn’t being splashed about so much. For what was left of that day, he spent sleeping in his quarters. Better to adjust to them than try to leave and be restrained, forced to stay in the room. There’s nothing worse than being panicky or frustrated in claustrophobic conditions such as this, it only aggravates the sensation of constriction. Hotel staff was cautioned of this man, it didn’t matter that he was a wrestler; in fact in general wrestlers are looked down upon in most places unless they’re vastly popular like Ivan Stanislav or Xavier Kannon. Skitzo wasn’t so popular, sure he had fans in general, but when a wrestler like Skitzo is going about his business in public, the usual jibes come out from various entrepreneurs and such. “Get a real fucking job, you fake! Anyone can pretend to hit someone, you just get paid for it!” Other such insults were far more vulgar, but in any case this was the bare reality. When a superstar isn’t off cutting promos in exotic locations or getting up to something weird and wonderful – such as the escapade with the dead sheep – he has to deal with life. Paying bills, having a social life, putting up with the bullshit from people who don’t understand the ‘magic’ of wrestling. This was the culmination of generations of men fighting one another for supremacy, or some other meaningful purpose. The entire concept goes back to the days of Neanderthals and Cro-Magnon man, Homo erectus and other such early models of man. The argument of ‘real sports’ is based on how a caveman lived his life. Eat. Sleep. Drink. Mate. Hunt. The five basic activities man occupied himself with, which of course stem off into more and more. In terms of hunting, man would either run, swim or fight. Therefore it’s argued that these are the only true forms of sport. The Olympics, supposedly showcasing these sports, have come through the years allowing more advanced forms of the sports in, such as rowing and long jump, shot-put and javelin etc. The Olympics has really lost touch with sports, but for example, Greco-Roman Wrestling, Swimming and Running. Those were the main sports. Wrestling is a real sport. Fuck anyone who says it’s fake. Over the years wrestling has been given a glamorous and glitzy look, all for the purpose of entertainment. When you look past the pyro and the music, the flashy outfits and the videos, what are you left with? Wrestling. It might look fake, it might very well be fake, in their eyes, but they are not the ones risking their necks to take a piledriver every night or to fly high above everyone and come crashing down with a shooting star press. It’s the moves that keep the sport alive. People are wowed by the powerbombs and moonsaults and slams and splashes. In essence Skitzo never gave a shit what people like that thought, he loved doing what he did, and no one could tell him any different. So there he was in the basement, more time had passed and he was getting bored. Usually when he was on the road he took a notepad with him and just wrote down any general feelings or dreams or nightmares he might have had. Being a schizophrenic in real life is much different than playing one on TV. The Skitzo everyone sees in PCW is a great exaggeration of William Williams. Although not apparent, the schizoid is there in him, and he loves exploiting it. This time he had forgotten the notepad. A couple of weeks ago he had visited Louisiana to deal with business at home, his home in the Bayou D’Arbonne. Leaving again to join the PCW road crew on the way to the next venue, he had neglected to lift his notepad, and come away with nothing. It wouldn’t be right to just go out and buy one either, he liked to keep his thoughts to one diary as it were. With no immediate family his thoughts and feelings were important to him, and recording them was a way of keeping him going as he spent most all of his days with PCW. The evening was approaching fast and Skitzo had spent a good portion of the day dwelling in this nook of the hotel. Of all the places they could have stuck him, it had to be in the dankest, most uncomfortable room in the house. Suffice to say Skitzo was getting a little pissed off. Finally he opened the little hatch-like door and moved into the basement. Stretching, he yawned and scratched his butt. Looking out across the field of supplies, utilities and tools, he spotted the stairs leading up to the lobby of the hotel. A plan! Moving back into the little room, Skitzo packed up the shirts and things he had taken out of his bag and zipped it up. Grabbing his jacket, he slipped on his sneakers and left the room, slamming the door behind him and moving across the basement. He took a deep breath looking up the stairs, and finally made his way to the top. Opening the door, he made a beeline for reception, and wasted no time in addressing the receptionist. Skitzo: Oi, woman! Receptionist: Excuse me sir; aren’t you the man whom the manager wished to stay in the quarters below? The lack of people in the lobby at the time made it pointless for Skitzo to shout and cause abuse, so he addressed her like any gentleman would. Skitzo: Yes, I am. For your information, I am now leaving. I came here with the intention of spending a week soaking up the atmosphere, but your establishment has been nothing but a disappointment. I ask you tell the manager I would slap him in the face I am that disgusted at this establishment, but I wouldn’t want to get any scum on my hands. I bid you good day, ma’am! Leaving the receptionist jaw dropped and shocked, Skitzo turned and left the hotel. As soon as he knew he was out of sight of the receptionist, he began sprinting down the lamp-lit street to a nearby public toilet, ran in to a cubicle, locked it, and five minutes later emerged wearing one of his fine suits he had packed. Looking in the mirror, he ran his comb through his hair until it was suitably presentable. With his kitbag packed and zipped up, he left the toilet and made his way back across the street. Stopping behind a parked car, Skitzo watched as surely enough, the receptionist from the hotel exited and made her way back home, having finished her shift. At the same time, another woman was making her way into the hotel, nodding and saying hello to the woman who had just finished her shift. Skitzo cleared his throat and followed her. Entering the hotel, he stood at the reception desk and waited, until finally the new woman on duty appeared, smartly dressed in uniform. Receptionist: Good day sir, welcome to the-… Skitzo: Please, if I may interrupt you. My name is Adrian Paul, I’m here on business, none of the hotels in town can meet my requirements, and this hotel is my last option before going back home and wasting time here. Can you help? The receptionist thought hard. The name Adrian Paul rings a bell, and anyone who knew Skitzo and Skitzo’s appearance could put that name to his face, which was the basis of his plan anyway! Receptionist: Certainly sir, and may I say I’m sure I’ve heard of you somewhere before… Skitzo: Let me refresh your memory… Skitzo cleared his throat once more and put on perhaps the worst Scottish accent known to man. Skitzo: ………Aye, there can only be one…Highlander! Receptionist: Oh my! Mr. Paul! It would be my pleasure to arrange for you to stay here! At present, two of our four suites are free, would you like me to book you into one? Skitzo thought it over very quickly. Skitzo: Certainly! Would cash be an appropriate form of payment? Receptionist: Firstly sir, how many nights would you be wishing to stay until? Skitzo: I should think until the 11th… Receptionist: Ok sir I’ll just enter your details into the computer here-… Skitzo: Oh one more thing! Receptionist: Yes? Skitzo: Well being a celebrity as I am, you know, I normally get the odd fan following me and pestering me and such, would it be possible to be booked under a false name? Receptionist: I’m sure that would be quite all right for someone such as yourself sir, do you have a name in-… Skitzo: Williams…William Williams! Receptionist: How very…Scottish! Skitzo: Yes…Scottish… The receptionist keyed in the last final details and looked back at Skitzo with a keen smile. Receptionist: That’s a 5-night stay in one of our master suites…$249.99 please! Skitzo felt himself blushing, but quickly jammed his hand into his pants pocket and removed his wallet. Opening it up, he spotted $120 and his MasterCard. Thinking to himself, he eventually grabbed the card and handed it to the receptionist. She swiped it and watched as the details appeared on screen; again a smile came across her face. Receptionist: Sir, this credit card is under the name of William Williams? Skitzo: Ah yes, don’t worry about it! I use it all the time to prevent anyone from taking my real credit card details and stealing money from my account! Receptionist: Very clever sir, but may I say our establishment hire the trustworthiest staff, you have no fears here, and I hope you enjoy your stay! The receptionist took a large key from the rack behind her and handed it to Skitzo. Receptionist: Shall I call someone to take your bags up for you? Skitzo: Not necessary thank you I think I can manage! Turning away, Skitzo almost burst out laughing as he made his way to the elevator. Stopping abruptly, he turned back around and nodded at the receptionist. Skitzo: Sorry, I never caught your name… Receptionist: Oh, my name is Shirley, Mr. Paul! Skitzo, with a sly smirk across his face, stepped into the elevator, and gave Shirley a wink as the doors closed. Skitzo: Thank you Shirley…thank you very much! The doors closed and the elevator began to rise to the top floor, and smiling like a wily fox, Skitzo did a little dance. Skitzo: Thank you very much you stupid bitch!!! Skitzo is movin’ on up!!! HA! HA! HA! HA! HA! BOOM! BOOM! Finally he could lavish like a king in the hotel’s most prestigious room as the countdown to his March Madness qualifier bout on Turmoil continued, and after two days consisting of dead sheep antics and fraud, whatever could Mr. Psycho be up to next?
