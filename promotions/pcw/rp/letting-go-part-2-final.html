Letting Go (Part 2) Final

Date: 24-March-03 FAX RECEIVED The digital display on Jonathan’s Fax/Printer/Copier/Scanner reads the reassurance that his facsimile transmission of his life’s work has been reached on the “other end”. In a way, Jonathan feels like he’s transmitted his soul. The transaction is almost finished though, this is no time for regret. Ok, let’s look through the process. I fax that, and then they fax me the signature back, I take it to the bank and it becomes official within a week. Only two employees, it won’t be hard Jonathan. Jonathan is in the business office portion of his restaurant, located behind and to the right of the kitchen. The office is not very large; there is a small desk that almost extends from wall to wall, a leather swivel chair, and not much else than walls. The desk is adorned with very little, other than some connections which snaked towards the back of the desk and underneath for a laptop and the aforementioned mammoth Hewlett Packard Fax/Printer/Copier/Scanner. Jonathan sits back in his chair and looks out through his open door to the sitting Antonio who is still perched atop the kitchen’s counter thumbing through a magazine, pretending not to be concerned. He does seem to be paying attention to the words, trying to evade a beast of anxiety by hiding himself amongst the tall grass of clauses and punctuation. I wonder how he’ll take it. I wonder if it’s going to be a pain to him. I wonder if we’ll part ways peacefully, I wonder if he’ll gush and talk about how I was the best boss ever. I wonder…and now…I’m just gonna find out. Jonathan has been standing next to the mammoth Hewlett Packard paper adornment device for quite a while and the blood has started to settle in his legs. This perhaps aids his urge to tell Antonio the news of his employment and of the restaurant’s future. Jonathan shakes off the uneasiness after having stood for a while, in addition to the tension he’s been feeling for the past few days as he walks towards his chef and friend, Antonio. Antonio looks up from his magazine at hearing Jonathan move, as if he had not been paying attention to the words at all and merely waited for the sound of Drachen’s movement. He smiles at his boss, and then does not as he sees the look in Jonathan’s eyes… ------- INCOMING FAX The display reads now. Jonathan sees a form almost identical to the one he has transmitted earlier, except bearing additional adornments of hardly readable signatures in certain places. He peruses the forms, knowing that they have been properly signed, but wanting not assurance of this as much as escape from the thoughts in his head. Dammit, did he have to take it like that? Did he have to? These words run through Jonathan’s mind as the scene replays itself. Antonio’s departure just will not leave his mind. His last words, why did he have to choose them? ‘Sure thing boss.’ Those weren’t parting words, those weren’t the words of two great friends. Those weren’t those last movie dialogue lines of two heroes about to break their alliance and go into separate worlds. Jonathan looks down at the desk below the contracts, frustrated. He realizes quickly that he cannot distract himself from these thoughts. His eyes seem replete with sadness for some reason, and yet relief. This feeling of relief is magnified upon Jonathan’s thoughts and he begins to think a bit further. That was just, as if he didn’t care. He said it that way on purpose; he said it because he knew being blank would be more hurtful than anything. He realized that letting this all mean nothing was all he could do in order to move along, and I suppose I understand that. I suppose it helps more than just him, I suppose it helps me. I suppose this will let things move along, that I’ll be fine after this, having left what is behind, behind. I suppose that Antonio just walking out like that forever, with hardly a word, was the best thing he could do. It shouldn’t be like that, but it is. The parting is not sweet sorrow; it is nothing, because neither the sweet nor the sorrow can be dwelled upon in order to allow us to progress. Maybe this is all just attaching too much meaning. Maybe it’s time I just let it go. Just let it be. SUAN LE! As Frank would say. Let it be. Jonathan stops his thoughts, and focuses on the contract before him. He is alone in what used to be his restaurant. This is Drachen’s last hour, certainly not it’s finest, but its last. However, it is not the last hour for Jonathan Drachen. He thinks of that as he picks the contract up from the desk. The sale is complete, the fund transfers should begin tomorrow. Drachen’s is a thing of the past. History is officially written. Jonathan takes the contract out of what was his business office and turns the lights off. He cleared his personal effects out of the office and the restaurant earlier in the week. His briefcase is the only personal item left in the building. It still lies upon a table in the dining area where he left it. Jonathan moves towards the briefcase, picks it up, places the document inside and shuts it. As he does this, he gets a good view of the whole restauarant. He looks at the former arcade, at the kitchen, the pick up counter, the dining area, the restrooms, the delivery counter, even the ceiling and then all of it at once and over again. In essence, he’s taking a mental photograph. This is the only remembrance Jonathan needs. He breathes out heavily, as he’s been holding his breath in for a while. Perhaps out of respect, perhaps out of other reasons, how can he even know? Jonathan then, turns towards the door, looks upon it and smiles. He walks the last walk towards the door and pushes it forward, it yields, and he is staring into the early afternoon sky and the world. He enters the world again as he locks the door with the keys he will soon give to those to whom he has sold a piece of his soul. Not once during the process, though, does he ever look back into what he is leaving behind.
