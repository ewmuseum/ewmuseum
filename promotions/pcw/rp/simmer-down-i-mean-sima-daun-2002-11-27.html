Simmer Down! I mean, Sima Daun...

27 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 27/11/02 In an effort to soothe my anger, I've followed up on a lead from an old friend. The journey took me to the stone steps of the temple of Sima Daun. Like most of the temples, it is only accessible by walking up a massive flight of steps. Each one must be taken one at a time, for it is said that those who skip from one step to another are skipping a spiritual link. Even though it was deserted, I wouldn't fly up either, in case one of the monks saw my wings and more to the point, the cold mountain air chilled me to the bone. My wings would probably freeze instantly. Step one. The second my foot touched it, I felt somewhat... addicted. It was like I had to go on, no matter what. Following the legend, I took every single step one at a time. This would have tired an ordinary man out to breaking point and even with my enhanced endurance, I found little comfort in my boots. Every step had to be taken, or whatever it was I searched for would be lost before it was even found. It dawned on me the monks of Sima Daun enjoyed their privacy, perhaps even usurping the faith people put into superstition. I, however, was not one to back down. After climbing the stone staircase for what seemed to be hours, I arrived. Grand in structure and marvelous in it's ancient glory, the temple of Sima Daun boasted massive gates of red brick and golden bars. For a moment, I envisioned that I had arrived at mankind's final illusionary paradise. My legs were racking me with a pain that not even my advanced metabolism could keep up with and my mouth yearned for a drop of sweet blood. Alas, no such drink could be taken with me and for a few days, my vampire side would have to be suppressed. Not even my precious Rezz Bull would be with me. It was to be a true change for me... I looked around for a means of entering without raising suspicion. The only thing I could find was te doorbell, or more precisely, a gigantic gong. The rod which one used to strike it was half my size and as thick as my legs combined. Setting my bag aside, I picked up the rod with both hands, both arms, even, then spun around. The lack of impact merely caused the gong to swing to and fro gently. I turned, my arms straining underneath the weight of it, then staggered as I struck the back half. THAT was my cue. With all the strength I could muster, I slung myself in a circle, slamming the gong with the rod. The noise from it was intense. I was about to find another weakness of my species. It would seems vampires have an aversion to heavy noise. It was amplified by the vampire inside me screaming in pain, causing me to drop the gong and sink to my knees. If the monks had not heard the sound of the gong, then those gates would have certainly opened for the screams that WE were emitting. Minutes passed as I recovered and the gate began to clink and clank. I could only imagine that they were locks, indicating this temple was fortified against outsiders. Through the small gaps between the red bars, I had seen a few peeks inside, but the splendor of the gates was a whisper compared to the full orchestra of the magnificent inner temple. In the middle of a gigantic courtyard, the focal point of the temple lay. It was a statue of a mighty dragon that appeared to be sleeping. Crafted in jade green and regal blue, the leviathan spanned several feet on the ground and the arches of it rose into the air as high as most of the people in PCW. An impressive decoration indeed, but I was not here for sight-seeing. The courtyard was surrounded on all sides by a red, angled rooftop supported by red and gold pillars. On each of these pillars was inscribed writing of some oriental origin, far beyond the comprehension of my minimal japanese. I hoped that one of the monks, wherever they were, spoke english, else my mission to calm myself would only have the reverse effect. I stepped inside the temple, beyond the reach of the doors. Captivated, I focused on the surroundings, failing to notice that behind me, two of the monks were closing those doors. It was only the noise of the locks that snared my attention, a tap on the shoulder, if you will. I turned around and was greeted by these monks of Sima Daun... and received a shock. Both men were like cavemen - the hair on their heads nor faces had touched a blade of any kind in years, although it had the look of a professional model. Both their hair and beards was shiny as though L'Oreal had a Tibet division. Their clothes were pale blue tunics that hung loose and were suspended from one shoulder by a sort of clip. Their feet were shielded from the gravel by extremely comfortable looking sandals and their skin was almost... tanned? I was beginning to think this place was more of a holiday resort or health spa from the smiles on their faces. The biggest shock came when I attempted to read their minds... completely blank. I could hear a heartbeat coming from within them and breath from their lungs, but it seemed that they literally had not a care in the world. I heard a noise from behind me then and quickly turned around. Approaching me were another two men, one of which was a veritable bronzed Adonis. Golden hair sweeping to his waist and the body of an olympic swimmer, compared to his companion. Something struck me that this man was not born in Tibet, as neither were the gatekeepers. Like them, his thoughts were silent even when he began to speak to me in a language I couldn't understand. Fortunately, his smaller counterpart picked up that I was a man of the Western world and translated for me. He told me that I had just been greeted by the Grand Master of the monks, Chi Lao Teh. A very oriental name for a not very oriental person, if you ask me. The interpreter went on to enquire my business. I replied to him that I had been told of the relaxing powers that the place had and that I was in a desperate situation and needed to soothe the monster inside me. A statement not too far from the truth. The interpreter muttered back to his great leader, who echoed a great laugh and beckoned with a hand. The pair turned and were joined by the gatekeepers. My only choice was to follow and see where their path would take me. * * * The monks led me into a room inside the main part of the temple, past the courtyard and the great dragon. Inside was a most unorthodox... yet effective... method of relaxation. The walls of the room were coated with a covering of exotic and exquisite seashells. A cool breeze flowed through and not only cooled the face, but brought an extremely pleasant and almost ethereal sound to the ears. The man known to me as Chi Lao Teh rambled again in his strange tongue, translated to me by the shorter man. He told me that the room was almost an appetizer for their ways and that to continue on, I would have to have an open mind for what I was about to see. They spoke as though they had no idea of who I was. Television was a luxury they could clearly afford with the materials used to craft their temple, but forsaken, it seemed. Regardless, the group pressed on with me following in an almost trance-like state. The next stop was a garden of some kind. Cherry blossom trees lined what appeared to be a natural spring, steaming hot. A sturdy but small bridge spanned a little stream that flowed from the spring. It ran underneath and into what looked like a drain of some kind. Primitive, but from the look on the hair and beards of the gatekeepers, it was apparent they had access to hot water. No animals were seen scampering around and no fish could survive the water temperature but this could have been the place they call Eden. The monks could see the look on my face and I could barely describe it. I'd have smiled if it was possible, but I was in such awe, my face looked as though I was scandalized. If the massive barriers outside were The Pearly Gates, then this little garden was indeed the Paradise Lost... The approach that the monks of Sima Daun took, it seemed, was one that could be represented by the slogan 'Why wait for heaven?'. Indeed, they say that in many a man's case, his happiest moment is when he leaves this world and his soul finds rest. Mine had a festering sickness in it that only imposed living purgatory on me, but since stepping onto the room full of shells, I'd not even heard a whisper from it. Had the peace brought on by this place quelled it's bloodlust? Another thought then hit me. Time was bearing on my body and without a fresh injection of blood, I would rot like a cadaver in front of their very eyes. My stay in this place would not be accepted if murder was part of the deal and having a vampire among them would only disrupt the peace. With frustration building inside me even in this Utopia, I had to inform the translator that I had commitments elsewhere. Upon translating this to Chi Lao Teh, the man himself spoke in a language I understood perfectly. 'No Worries, bro.' * * * As I stepped on my way out of the temple, I prepared to skip as many steps as I could. I only hope that the tape I left them would bring them as much relaxation as they gave me, and the tape it itself had given me too... 27/11/02 Nerezza put down his pen and switched on his walkman, ready to prepare himself for the upcoming match with Dante. To his surprise, he only heard the relaxation tape he supposedly gave to the monks. 'Strange...' the vampire quirked an eyebrow. 'I wondered what happened to that tape Gaz gave me as a joke, 'Ebonics For beginners by Statuz Quo?'
