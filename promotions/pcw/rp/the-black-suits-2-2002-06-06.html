Date: June 6, 2002 Tom Lexian's Apartment 7:00 am Chomp...chomp...slurrp... The morning sun seemed to shine brightly into the kitchen window of my apartment, which seemed to hunt my eyes down and shine directly into them. It was rather annoying, if not a random coincidence. I looked up from the plate I was eating from, wincing as I realized that I had cooked this magnificent non-feast. I had been chomping down on a crisp piece of bacon, although it was a bit too crisp for my tastes. There was only one strip remaining in the package which I had finished out of the freezer, which defeated the purpose of having bacon for breakfast. I mean, you can't just have one strip of bacon for breakfast - that's like only having one whole chicken for dinner. Of course, the fact that there was only one strip of bacon in the house was partially my fault. Ok, it was entirely my fault. In my sudden spurts of hunger, I often pillage the freezer for any food that I can find. It just so happened that the other night I had taken the box of bacon as my latest victim. I hadn't even heated it before - I ate it frozen. It was sort of like a meat popsicle, only more ice than meat. I know what you're probably thinking: 'He's such a lazy bastard that he can't even cook one god damn strip of bacon.' Yes, it's true. I couldn't take a few minutes out my busy life to even heat up some meat for myself. Hey, it wasn't laziness as much as it was pure sorrow. You see, my girlfriend, Natalie Dischner, or just plain Nat, was out for the weekend. She had gone to her cousin's wedding, which was taking place up in Salem, Oregon. I have never met her cousin before, but from the stories and pictures I've seen about her, I hear she hasn't had that much success with men. This would be her third...or maybe fourth, I forget, wedding in the last two or three years. Each time something came up that made the marriage annuled and void. Maybe I was afraid that something dire would occur during the wedding that would force me to never get married...maybe I was afraid that this would push Nat to seriously consider pursuing marriage with me when I knew both of us weren't ready. That carried, with itself, an inherrent problem. My baby's gone! Yes, my Nat baby was gone, and now I was stuck alone in this apartment by myself. It was rather lonely sitting in this small apartment, couped up with no one to talk to. Sean O'Reilly, my best friend, had come over a few times to cheer me up, but even he had things to do with his ex-wife and kids. So I sit here alone, too lazy to make my own food, and not utilizing the awesome powers that my stove and microwave possessed. At late nights, I wouldn't care, but seeing as though I'm pretty big on breakfasts and morning appetites, I wasn't having the best time eating. Hell, I love the U.S., and I have some love for my country of origin, Vietnam, but never in my wildest dreams have I EVER seen so many Canadians who proudly displayed the maple leaf insignia. It was a bit mind blowing, but hell, that's life. We all go on, don't we? I looked back down at my plate, which didn't show me anything appealing that I wanted to eat. I leaned on my hand, trying to figure out which piece of food I should try and tackle next. I had already finished the bacon, but a piece of toast, some scrambled eggs, and a hard boiled egg was all that stood in front of me from throwing this plate in the sink and declaring myself finished with breakfast. Oh shit, the sink... I realized that the sink was still overflowing with dishes. If Nat were here, I would have gotten the scolding of a lifetime, and most likely, would be sleeping on the couch that night. That was Nat's punishment for everything - SLEEP ON THE COUCH! It didn't seem so harsh to all of you, but when you're desperately in love with a girl, you want to be able to snuggle up with her and whisper sweet nothings in her ear. Suffice it to say, you can't do that from a couch that feels like you're sleeping on a sharp, jagged rock. Nat had only been gone for a few days, and sink was already incredibly full. I didn't think I had eaten that much, but I guess I just wasted a lot of plates. How many of you would use a bowl to eat one strawberry? Well, I would. I guess my lack of logic would dictate that with all of the silverware and bowlage I use, I should be doing the dishes more often. I didn't, and now I was paying the price for it. The truth was, I didn't mind doing the dishes, but only when Nat was here. That way, she could praise my work and/or yell at me for doing a horrid job. I'm a listener, so I need that auditory criticism. I poked around the eggs with my fork, not really noticing the egg shells that were floating around in the yellowish mass. I had been getting better at eggs, but I definitely needed more practice. My scrambled eggs consisted of just mashing the eggs together while they were cooking. I was rather certain there was a much more elegant way to make eggs, because I've seen Nat do it, but I had an excuse - I am a wrestler. Ok, so it's not much of an excuse, but it gives me more reason to keep a dirtier apartment. I finally decided that I was never going to finish this meal, and even if I did, I didn't want to be sick to my stomach while I worked out. Which reminded me...I'm late! I checked the clock, seeing that I was already fifteen minutes late for my scheduled work out at the arena. I threw the remaining contents of the plate into the trash, which joined a badly burnt piece of chicken I had thrown in there the night before. One good turn deserves another, I always say. I took the plate and somehow maneuvered it into the sink, which looked precariously balanced. If I hadn't known better, I would have bet good money that by the time I got back, it would have tipped over. Happy with myself that the dishes were safe, I quickly ran to my room, slipping out of the pajamas I was still walking in, and threw on some warm up pants and a t-shirt. The shirt stunk of sweat, but I was once again too lazy to clean it. I would have to remind myself to throw it back into the hamper when I returned. I got my work out bag ready, placing it on the unmade bed which I shared with myself, and placed my running shoes on. They were old 'Rebook Runs,' which were in style for about two weeks, until people realized how lame they were. Perhaps that is why I wore them - either that, or the fact that I had no other form of shoe apparel. Either way, I had shoes. As I slipped the shoes on, lacing them very dilligently, I heard the doorbell ring. I looked up, a bit surprised and wondering who would be coming to my house at this time in the morning. With a bit of a sigh, I grabbed my bag and headed to the front door. The apartment looked like a warzone, but I really didn't care. I dropped the bag near the door, opening it ever so slightly, until I was relieved to see an old friend at the door. 'Hello Suzie,' I said, with a very broad smile on my face. Suzie had been a friend of mine ever since I moved out to this apartment. She owned a small flower shop which was located a few blocks from here. She was probably the nicest old lady I had ever met. 'How can I help you?' 'That's my line,' Suzie replied with an equally broad smile. She looked a bit tired, and I suddenly noticed a small gash on her cheek. 'What happened?' I asked, brushing my hand on her cheek. Suzie backed away from my advances. 'It's nothing. I was just...well, I can't talk about it. Let's just say it involves the police. I came by to drop off these flowers for you that you requested for Nat.' I took the arrangement of flowers, which reeked of beautiful colors such as orange, yellow and red. I knew that Nat was coming back in a few days, and I knew that she would love these flowers. They were not real, however. Nat hated to see actual flowers being cut - she thought it hurt them, them being the flowers. I always argued with her that they're only flowers, but she always countered with, 'How would you like it if someone cut off your arms and showed them to me?' Now honestly, how the hell do you do a comeback to that? 'Thank you, Suzie,' I remarked, admiring the arrangement. 'It looks great. Can you stay for coffee or something?' 'Oh no, I can't,' Suzie quickly replied, looking around the hall way. 'I have some errands to run, but I hope you enjoy the flowers. When you see, Nat, tell her I said hello...' Suzie hurried down the hallway, scurrying away like a little pig trying to get away from the mean old butcher. I say that, considering that a nice, fat plump pig wouldn't exactly be well described as 'scurrying away.' More like slothing around. Anyways, I was a bit surprised appearance and disappearance, and wondered if I shouldn't give her a follow up call to make sure she was completely alright. I knew she was a widower, but she had plenty of friends to keep her company, myself included. I took the flowers in my hand, preparing to stuff them in the fridge. They were fake, so there was no need to put them in water or anything, then again, there was no need to keep them refigerated either. I guess it just made me feel better that they were in the fridge, safe from my non-cleaning ways. As I shuffled them over to the fridge, a small, green object flew out from the flowers and onto the ground. I stopped to see what had fallen out, and I saw an emerald crystal lying on the ground next to my foot. It was sparkling...beautiful...I placed the flowers on the ground, picking up the crystal in my hand. It shined brightly as I twirled it in my hand, and I realized what luck I had struck. This was a million times better than a bouquet of flowers - it would make Nat a very happy woman.
