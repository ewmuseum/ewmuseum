Karaoke Dreams

14 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 14 October 2002 Miyagawa is strolling along a night time main street somewhere in Japan. The lights all around his are flashing in their wonderful colours, and the people are almost in a Times Square sort of rush around the place. Occasionally people recognise him and point and stare, but Miyagawa is just out trying to get some fresh air… even though he’s in one of the most heavily urbanised cities on the planet. He’s wearing his long black jacket, but with the mass of people he must be really quite hot in it regardless of the actual temperature outside. He walks along a few more shop windows as they pass by on his left hand side, not paying all that much attention to them. He starts to pass by a bar and realises that a drink might be a nice thing to do, even though since it’s on a main road he’s going to have to pay half the value of his house in Yen just to get a pint. He walks into the bar and finds a stool at the bar before placing his order and getting it delivered quickly, opting for a vodka and coke instead of a pint simply because well… he wasn’t in the mood. Frisco retired before he could be beaten, you see. Does that count as a count out? Is that an actual victory as such since he is now retired? Who knows? There might never be another chance to defeat Frisco again. Miyagawa: I’m getting too old for this sort of shit. He just says that to himself, not to anyone in particular, but a voice says something from behind him. Voice: Never mind my son, with age comes a sense of wisdom that those who are younger than you will never have. Miyagawa raises an eyebrow before spinning around on the bar stool. He is confronted with something he never noticed when he walked in, and considering the bright colour, he is amazed that he never saw it. Or he, as the case may be, since it’s a Buddhist monk dressed in his regular orange robes. Miyagawa: My apologies sensei, I never saw you there. Monk: No need for apologies my son, I have only just arrived and you of course were already seated. I come for the Karaoke you see. You see, it makes the whole world seem insignificant. Miyagawa is surprised by that statement. Miyagawa: Insignificant? How? The elderly monk smiles, the multicoloured lights in the bar making funny reflections off his shining bald head. Monk: Think about it? Why do people sing Karaoke? Is it for the money that they earn once they have a recording contract? The fame? Miyagawa shakes his head. Miyagawa: But they aren’t going to get a recording contract, or any fame. They just go up there and sing because they want to. Because they enjoy it. The Monk nods in earnest. Monk: Exactly my son. They do it because they want to. When was the last time you did something because you wanted to, not because it was expected of you? When was the last time that you were yourself? Miyagawa sits there quiet for a few seconds, not replying, just thinking over what he could say. Monk: You don’t really know do you? You see what I sense in you is that you are at a crossroads. You have people that rely on you, but are beginning to think that they don’t know who you really are. And at the same time you don’t know who you are either. Miyagawa looks shocked at that statement. Miyagawa: Yes… I guess you are right. I don’t know myself; instead I let other people make me think of what I am. I’m supposed to be Alexander Miyagawa, a wrestler in the current touring Primetime Championship Wrestling company… one of the greatest fighters in their history, second to only Frisco and The Chef in title belts held. And yet… all this time I’ve simply been playing to the crowd being either good or bad. Maybe it’s time to be myself… to do what I enjoy doing. The Monk nods quietly during that statement before responding after it… Monk: But speak to those who rely on you first before making any action. They will no doubt wish to have some input on what is going on, and would wish to determine some course of action. Miyagawa nods once again and gets up from the bar, his drink still sitting there on the bar, completely intact. Miyagawa: Thank you sensei, I will go do that right now. Miyagawa leaves the bar, leaving the old Monk there… but not for long as the Karaoke starts soon after as the people get up and sing, not because they’re expected to, but because they want to. Because they enjoy it.
