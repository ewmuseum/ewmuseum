THE SNOW SHOW: The Dragon

Date: January 19, 2003 ﻿************************************The Snow ShowSeason I, Episode 11“The Dragon”************************************ Location: Marty Verrelli’s Apartment - New York CityDate: January 7, 2003 Time: 10:20 am Black satin runs tangled through naked feet under the narrow slits of sunlight allowed into theroom through the tightly closed blinds. The satin spread itself to blanket its inhabitants at thehips, and up to the shoulder. There, Krista rested her sleeping head against Marty’s chest, andher body, covered only by the black sheet, under his heavy arm. His breathing woke her. At first wake, she was lost. There was nothing familiar to her in the room. The shelves werebare, save for a stack of magazines that she couldn’t see the covers of and a dusty pair of shoes. To her right, there was a wide gap of floor between the bed and the wall, just past the man thatshe was sleeping on. The man!? She suddenly bolted upright, clutching the satin blanket to her neck, and peereddown at the man she had spent last night with - Marty Verrelli. And then, everything came back to her. The fight with her sister and brother-in-law, the tearfuldrive over to Marty’s, and then the kiss... she remembered very little after the kiss - only thefeelings. “Good morning,” Marty said with a sleepy smile. She didn’t know he was awake. She smiled back, but still clutched the blanket close to her neck. It was a smile of the mouthonly, because her eyes couldn’t force one. *** Location: New York City Date: January 13, 2003Time: 2:30 pm In his youth, some friends had nicknamed him The Dragon. In his middle age, the nickname hadstuck more so than his birth name. There were people who assumed his birth name wasdragon. When he looked into the rearview mirror of his Ford Bronco, The Dragon saw only his thickblack Van Dyke, or goatee, as the informal called it. His daughter must have stolen the truck fora joy ride again, he thought as he pushed the mirror back into its familiar setting. A stout cigar jutted from his mouth and from time to time he heaved a thick black smoke outinto the truck - people always complained about the stench that lingered around the interior, hethought with a chuckle. Still, he loved a good smoke. When he started, it was more of anintimidation factor - the cigar made him look older. Now, at forty-six, there was no longer aneed to look older. If anything, there was a need to look younger, but since his hair refused togrow these days, except on his chest, arms, and back, the thought of sending an illusion of youthseemed absurd. Since his hair wouldn’t grow anymore, he did the next best thing - shaved hishead bald. Youthful or not, The Dragon was still intimidating. As he pulled the Bronco to an intersection, it took him a moment to recall which way he wastold to turn. Left, he finally realized - so it was true, the memory really does go with the firstsigns of old age. He pulled the cigar from his mouth, for only a moment, and sighed deeply. When he put the cigar back in, all thoughts of doubt were driven away. Pulling the Bronco around the corner of the intersection, The Dragon immediately saw his manstanding at the corner of an ancient brick building, which faced the street, and the alley betweenit and a windowless cube of stone. If the stone building had a door, The Dragon couldn’t see it. “Must be fucking nuts,” The Dragon said allowed in his impossibly deep, gruff, voice. It was acold day to be dealing drugs out of doors, on the street side. “Stupid fuck,” chuckled The dragonto himself as he pulled the truck up to the curb. He dipped into the breast pocket of his stained green t-shirt and pulled out the photograph onefinal time. It was him, The Dragon nodded to himself as he compared the photograph to the manon the corner. At the bottom of the photo, in thick black ink, was the name: Carlos Santiego. Carlos wasn’t alone today. Two friends accompanied him at the corner. Must have been hisidea of ‘muscle,’ thought The Dragon. With a sigh, the Dragon pulled his jacket on and steppedout of the truck and into the cold bite of New York winter. Carlos noticed him immediately, but in error, didn’t recognize him. He stepped up,accompanied by his ‘muscle,’ and leaned on the Bronco. “You looking for something, man?” Carlos asked as he nervously looked from side to side. “I am,” nodded The Dragon as he began to roll up his sleeves. “Cool, cool,” smiled Carlos. “I got what you’re looking for, as long as you got the dollars to buyit.” “Actually,” The Dragon began while removing his rings - he didn’t want to do too muchdamage, “I’m looking for a man by the name of Carlos Santiego. I don’t suppose you’ve heardof him.” Carlos was speechless for a moment. He knew that this was trouble, he just couldn’t place whattype of trouble it was. And then, as he gazed into the dark eyes of the aging bald Italian,recognition struck him. The Dragon. The man was a legend in the underworld of New York. For twenty years, he had been the mostbrutal and ruthless of the Hattoni family’s thugs. For ten years before that, he had already madea reputation for himself. Floods of stories about this man rushed into Carlos’ head - the time hebroke a man’s arm for stepping on his toe, the time he snapped a man’s neck for reporting late toDoug Hattoni, and of course, there was that infamous time The Dragon pulled each and everytooth from the mouth of another crime family’s top hitman before finally killing him. The saidhe wore the teeth as a necklace, even today. “You’re looking for me?” Carlos finally said. The Dargon nodded. “According to Justin Hattoni,” he began, “you’ve been holding back on...funds. You know the deal, kid - you get the corner to do whatever the fuck you wantwith, and the Justin gets twenty-five percent. You don’t like it, get a new fucking neiborhood.” “Yo man, it ain’t like that!” started Carlos, but The Dragon didn’t care to listen. He grabbed theyoung man by his stubbly skull and slammed his face onto the hood of the Bronco. “You see what you kids make me do!?” The Dragon boomed like a scorned and abusive father. “You see!? All you’ve got to do is play by the rules and this shit is avoidable. If you don’t likethe rules, get the fuck out of the game!” With that, The Dragon slammed a heavy fist intoCarlos’ midsection. “Help!” coughed Carlos, but his cry was in vein. His companions had already fled the scene,and they had done so as soon as they recognized the bald old man with his trademark cigar. “Looks like your girlfriends have run off,” The Dragon chuckled as he gave Carlos a light shove. Carlos fell on his side, still coughing from the punch in the stomach. “Kids these days,” scowledThe Dragon. “Bunch of fucking pussies.” Carlos started scampering away, but soon The Dragon grabbed him by the ankle and draggedhim back to the Bronco. He then pulled the young drug dealer to his feet and cracked him twicein the head, busting Carlos open. “Please, man,” Carlos begged. “Don’t fucking kill me! Don’t fucking kill me!” “You’re whining like a bitch!” The Dragon backhanded Carlos. The impact sent the drug dealersprawling against the hood of the Bronco. Just then, The Dragon’s cell phone rang. “Oh, for fuck’s sake!” The Dragon puffed. He pulled the cigar out of his mouth and opened thedoor to his Bronco. As the phone rang for a third time, he turned back to the beaten young man. “I’ve got to take this - don’t move.” Carlos barely understood him. With some difficulty, The Dragon hopped inside the truck, leaving the door open. He answeredthe phone: “What the fuck do you want, I’m busy!” “Dragon,” Petey the Vice’s voice came through from the other side, “it’s Petey.” “Jesus Christ, Petey,” The Dragon sneered, “I’m fucking in the middle of something here. Canyou call back later.” The Dragon hated cellphones for precisely this reason, but the upperranked men in the Hattoni family, like Petey, insisted that he get one. “It’s important,” Petey said. “The boss wants to see you.” “The boss?” The Dragon’s voice perked. Suddenly, he noticed Carlos was sliding off the hood. “Don’t fucking move!” he ordered. Carlos pushed himself, painfully, back into hisprevious position. “Yeah,” Petey confirmed on the other side. “The boss wants to see you. You know, at theplace.” Of course, the Hattoni family didn’t trust phone lines to give the actual destination ofmeetings. Instead they used code names like ‘the place,’ or ‘the area,’ or ‘the building,’ andeveryone’s personal favorite, ‘where we did that thing.’ “Can’t it wait?” The Dragon said as he looked back up at Carlos. “Like I told you - I’m fuckingbusy here.” “No dice,” Petey answered on the other end of the phone. “It’s very, very, important.” “Alright,” The Dragon answered sourly, “just give me a fucking minute. I’ll be there.” Hesnapped the phone shut and cursed, “God damn it!” *** Location: Hattoni Mansion - New York CityDate: January 13, 2003Time: 3:10 pm “Justin,” The Dragon said as he sat in the head office of the Hattoni family, inside JustinHattoni’s mansion, “correct me if I’m wrong - but you’re talking about whacking a boss here.” Justin Hattoni, sitting behind his massive oak desk, stayed silent. “That’s what we’re talking about,” nodded Petey the Vice, who stood beside a cracked window,smoking a cigarette. “Not just any boss, either - a fucking big one. John Mossilano has headedup his family for fucking decades.” “Dangerous shit,” said The Dragon, as soft as he was able, given his voicebox which was set tobass at all times. “Very dangerous,” nodded Justin. “And you say I won’t be alone on it?” The Dragon raised an eyebrow. “You know I don’t likeworking with partners - they always fuck things up. I especially won’t take kindly to some punkkid on my heels.” “He’s a good man,” Justin said. In reality, Jason Snow had been his first choice to do thejob, and The Dragon was chosen as an accompaniment to add experience. Snow was not chosenfor the job to help The Dragon. “What’d you say his name was again?” The Dragon asked. “Snow,” Petey said after puffing his cigarette. His curly hair gleamed beneath the sunlight thatcrept in. “Jason Snow.” “That don’t sound much like an Italian name,” The Dragon pointed out. “He’s Irish,” said Justin. “Jesus,” the Dragon rolled his eyes. “Now we’re bringing fucking micks into the family!? Whatthe hell, Justin? You’re father would never stand for something like this.” Justin’s eyes lockedon the massive bald man. He was the only one who dared speak to Justin in such a manner, andlikewise, Justin was the only man who dared give The Dragon such a look. “My father,” Justin said, “was the man who brought Jason Snow to the family. But I don’t askthat you follow my father’s wishes, I ask that you follow mine!” The Dragon took a deep breath. “Wait a minute,” the burly man finally said. “Jason Snow - ain’t it his sister that my boy’s beendating?” Justin and Petey exchanged glances. “Sister?” Petey said. “I didn’t know he had a sister.” “Sister-in-law!” The Dragon corrected himself. “Maybe this ain’t such a bad idea after all. Imean, according to what my boy’s been saying about the girl - who knows? Maybe this guy’sgoing to be family some day.” *** Location: Snow Residence - New York SuburbsDate: January 15, 2003Time: 1:25 pm ...Jeffery Howard... ...J S Howard... ...Jerry Howard... No luck. Snow closed the phone back and tossed it aside. It became the single unorganized object in hiswife’s perfect living room as it landed in the far corner. There was so much on his mind lately. The family. The business. And then there was the other business. He sighed deeply and leaned back on the sofa as Krista entered the room from the kitchen andsat down beside him. He was still angry at her for walking out a few weeks back, but there wassomething about the girl that often made him swallow his anger - at least for a few moments. “What are you doing?” she asked. She too had swallowed her own anger toward him. They hada fiery relationship, but neither questioned how much the other cared for them. “Taking a nice and easy day,” Snow said with a sigh. He’d been doing a lot of work for theHattoni family lately - even more than usual. And of course, with Shanghai’ed III on thehorizon, there was the added pressure and the training that came with it. January was a stressfultime for Jason Snow - especially this one. “Sounds good,” the young woman said with a pleasant smile. She was making a real effort topatch things up, it seemed. Even though she swore that nothing happened between her andMarty Verrelli, the two had been on little better than speaking terms since she returned from hisapartment that night. Snow suspected that she had been seeing him ever since, without himknowing. He wondered if Haley would be this difficult when she reached maturity. “So you’ve got a big match coming in a few weeks?” she was trying to make small talk. “You’re probably not going to want to see me for a few days after,” Snow said with a chuckle. “It’s likely going to get a little rough in Asia next weekend.” She wanted to smile, but couldn’t. Usually, Snow downplayed the viciousness of his business (both businesses, actually) around his family. If he was admitting that this particular match wasgoing to be more violent than usual, then there was reason to worry. Quickly, she changed thesubject. “So what do you think of New York?” she asked. Snow shrugged. “I’m not fond of it,” he sighed. “But you do what you’ve got to do, I guess.” Krista was able to smile now. Both she and her sister had already reluctantly accepted the paththat Snow had chosen, even if they didn’t know exactly how deep he was into that path. Theydidn’t know that he was a killer. Even so, it was nice to be on good terms with his sister-in-law again. Still, he knew that shouldMarty Verrelli pop back into her life, those terms would quickly deteriorate. Stories of Verrelli’s ascent in the mafia world were contradicting. Some thought of him as agolden child. Others thought he was a brat clinging to his father’s success. Snow had never met Verrelli’s father, but stories of The Dragon were everywhere. His realname, apparently, was Halfred Verrelli, though those rumours were conflicted as well. Snow didn’t give much care to who the young man’s father was, however. Should MartyVerrelli step out of line, then Marty Verrelli would pay - and pay dearly - regardless of what oldman was looking out for him. Suddenly, the phone rang. “Hello?” Snow answered monotonously. “Snow?” the voice on the other end said. It was Petey. “We’ve got a job for you, kid. Someone’s going to swing by and pick you up tomorrow morning. Look sharp, and be ready.” Just as suddenly as the conversation began, it ended. Snow hung up the phone and took a deepbreath. “Who was that?” asked Krista. His silence told her all she needed to know.
