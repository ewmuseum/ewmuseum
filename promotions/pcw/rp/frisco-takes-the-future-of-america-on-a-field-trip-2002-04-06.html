Frisco Takes the Future of America on a Field Trip

6 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-6-02 (The blazing Arizona sun blinds you as the bright white disc is focused on for about 10 seconds. Zooming out from the source of the Spring warmth, we are introduced to the barren landscape of the Arizona desert. The only things visible is cacti, dirt, and a highway that stretches out into a shimmering mirage a few miles away. A piece of glass on the highway's asphalt reflects the sun's light, shooting a beam of light towards the camera for a brief second. The view turns slightly out of the way of the reflecting light and captures a lizard darting out of the desert and onto the road. The small reptile picks up its head and quickly turns it right to left. The roar of an engine is heard from a distance and the lizard focuses his beady black eyes to the right. The sound of a vehicle approaching grows louder and louder until the lizard scurries away back into the desert. The lizard disappears just as a huge white and sky blue bus zooms by. We are now following the blue and white bus, which more specifically is painted like an Argentinean flag with Mao-esque portraits of Frisco on both sides of the flag's central logo. White letters in the top blue layer spell out the phrase 'FRANCISCO VALERIUS PARA EL PRESIDENTE DE ARGENTINA Y EL SALVADOR DE SU GENTE' and in the bottom layer the phrase 'GANEMOS CON FRISCO!'. Obviously the campaign bus of Frisco, it looks like the finest bus money could buy with a sleek, aerodynamic body, tinted bullet-proof windows, and a machine gun turret on the roof. As the bus is closely focused on, you can see a Hispanic man in army fatigues behind the huge weapon constantly swinging it around searching for targets. On the four corners of the roof, Argentinean flags fly gracefully in the dry Southwestern air. As one of the flags are focused on, we are brought inside the bus, where about 20 small children sit on the many leather couches on the luxury campaign bus. Many of Frisco's secretaries and associates sit up front, keeping themselves busy with papers and phone calls. Our future dictator paces around the front of the room with a glass of brandy in his hand, mumbling to himself as the children in the bus socialize loudly.) Frisco: 'Dios fucking mio. These children's' shrill voices are like a drill in my head.' (Frisco rubs his temples as he brings his trembling brandy glass up to his lips.) Frisco: 'Calmate, Francisco, calmate. They're just kids...future voters...wait, not voters, no more voters when I'm in charge. Arrrrgggggh! Campaigns suck, I hate people. First presidential act--throw them all into a burning pit.' (One of Frisco's party members coughs in a blatant attempt of recognition. Frisco looks up.) Frisco: 'What the fuck is your problem? You got the plague or something? Because if you do, pass it onto those little brats and then kindly throw your diseased self out of my bus.' (The party member--a bald little man identified as Paulo Cortez, Secretary of Paper Towels, Lube, and Foreign Affairs points to the camera.) Paulo Cortez: 'No, no, no. We're on, Senor Presidente.' (Frisco looks at the cameraman and sighs.) Frisco: 'How long have you been taping?' Cameraman: 'Just a few minutes, sir.' Frisco: 'That's not specific enough.' Cameraman: 'About 2 minutes and 32 seconds.' (Frisco shakes his head.) Frisco: 'About 2 minutes and 32 seconds, or 2 minutes and 32 seconds?' Cameraman: 'Uhhh, 2 minutes and 37 seconds.' Frisco: 'Now you're trying to confuse me.' Cameraman: 'No...' Frisco: 'Now you're doubting me.' Cameraman: 'N-..' Frisco: 'No wonder Meanstreak used to beat the shit out of your kind every other promo.' (Frisco's hand goes briefly out of view which results in a shake of the camera and a grunt from the man behind the camera.) Frisco: 'There. Now we can be friends again.' (Frisco sips his brandy and smiles.) Cameraman: 'Ye--yeah, sure.' (Frisco pants the cameraman on the shoulder and walks away to face the children.) Frisco: 'Hey, kids!' (The kids briefly look up at Frisco but quickly return to their prior conversations. Frisco frowns and mumbles under his breath.) Frisco: 'Ungrateful little shits.' (Frisco clears his throat.) Frisco: 'Kids, we're on television now!' (The kids look up at the cameraman and cheer.) Kid #1: 'Cool!' (The children stare towards the front of the bus and wave vigorously.) Frisco: 'Now, kids...' (A loud murmur continues. Frisco looks over to a plump woman who is obviously their teacher. The woman stands up.) Teacher: 'Quiet down, class. You have to let Mr. Frisco speak since he was kind enough to let you on his bus and take you for a field trip.' (The kids speak in unison.) Kids: 'Yes, Mrs. Sayles!' Frisco: 'Thank you.' (Frisco finishes off his fine brandy and sets down the glass.) Frisco: 'Now, my young citizens, I'm sure you're all wondering why you're on a fine bus--...' (A small girl with blonde pigtails speaks up.) Kid #2: 'These have seatbelts. The ones at school don't. My mom always said that we need to wear our seatbelts.' Frisco: 'Yes, this bus has seatbelts and your dear mother is absolutely correct. My vehicles have seatbelts because I care about the lives of my young friends. The buses at school don't have seatbelts because the schools care about saving money, and the government of American doesn't care about your schools. They don't care if a bus crashes and 20 of you kids die...but I do.' (A few scattered claps can be heard.) Frisco: 'In my home country of Argentina, seat belts are a rarity also, but only because we are not yet as rich as your country. Once I am president and we defeat Great Britain, enslave their people, and seize their economy--I guarantee a seatbelt for every moving vehicle!' (Frisco continues his mini-speech.) Frisco: 'But that's not all. No, that's not all by a long shot. The school buses in my country will be driven by a bus driver who will allow your paper fights!' Kids: 'Yay!' Frisco: 'In fact, our bus drivers will supply your paper so you won't have to waste your own--thereby allowing you to keep your own for writing shit down and learning!' (The kids giggle at Frisco's profanity.) Frisco: 'Because I care, kids, because I care. For the lucky children of Argentina, I will throw the first ball of paper!' (Frisco looks around and spots his Secretary of Contracts, Indictments, and Drive-through Ordering.) Frisco: 'Gimme a piece of paper...' (The small pale man in glasses--Carlos Parata looks up and looks around.) Carlos Parata: 'No tengo unas papeles, El Mero Mero...' Frisco: 'Yes, you do, you moron. Right there!' (Frisco yanks a piece of paper off his tray and crumples it up. The secretary's eyes bulge and he begins to sob.) Frisco: 'What's your problem!? I'm trying to amuse the children by showing them what my children will have the right to do and you're sobbing like your alcoholic whore of a mother.' (Frisco un-crumples the paper and reads it.) Frisco: 'What the hell?' (Frisco looks at his secretary. The kids now start talking amongst themselves.) Frisco: 'This is a letter for funeral arrangements for your wife's currently rotting corpse...' (The secretary nods. Frisco frowns.) Frisco: 'You're writing this crap on my time!? On these kids' time!? You sick fuck. I should fire you right now.' Carlos Parata: 'Lo siento! Lo siento!' Frisco: 'Quiet. You'll be dealt with later.' (Frisco shakes his head.) Frisco: 'It's been 3 days since your wife died and you're still crying like a bitch. I don't know if I want your kind on my roster. You're soft. I bet you haven't even got laid since she died. You've got problems, Parata, major problems...' (Frisco looks out at the kids no longer paying attention.) Frisco: 'But that's okay...because you're a Puerto Rican. If you want to become a citizen of the Empire of Argentina, you'll straighten up and fly right, boy.' (Frisco looks over to another party member--Chulo Ibanez, Secretary of Prostitutes, Tobacco, and Importations--who looks like a common Hispanic man except for his bright green suit, feathered derby, and diamond-encrusted sunglasses.) Frisco: 'Chulo, get Senor Parata a date tonight.' (Chulo points to Frisco and nods. Frisco looks back to the kids and clears his throat.) Frisco: 'Sorry about that, kids, but my friend here is going through a hard time. See, his wife of 27 years passed away a few days ago and he's very sad. But since I am his friend I did something very special for him. Tonight he won't have to be without a loving touch. My friend Carlos will not have to think of such sad things as his decomposing wife, as he will be with a new and pretty woman. A woman who will smell of perfume and not of feasting bacteria.' (Carlos Parata moans loudly between his sobs.) Frisco: 'See? He's so very happy now! Because that is my goal, kids. I want to make everyone happy--especially the children of my country. So, just as I was telling you about paper fights on the buses...' (Frisco re-crumples the paper and throws it at a fat kid who wasn't paying attention. The paper hits the boy in the head making the kids laugh and the fatso's lip quiver in embarrassment. Frisco smiles warmly.) Frisco: 'All kids your age will have the right to be happy wherever they are. On buses, at school, at home or in a park--anyone can be happy--provided they're Argentinean. I'm afraid you're all screwed because your parents didn't love you enough to give birth to you outside this country, but there will be hope for millions of others.' (The kids frown in confusion and pout.) Frisco: 'However, don't fret. Once I am president, you will all be allowed to run away from home and gain citizenship in my country! We will of course weed out the trash, so make sure you ask your parents if you will amount to anything or not before you apply.' (Frisco motions over to one of his secretaries, who in turn hands Frisco a Cuban. Frisco whips out his platinum ZIPPO~! and lights his cigar. Frisco puffs as he looks over to the kids who comment to each other about the smoking dictator on their bus.) Frisco: 'Don't let school tell you that smoking is bad. Your country is lucky to have such vast fields of tobacco for production. They make a lot of cash off of it. However, your country is also very stupid, as it doesn't allow the companies to advertise the economy-helping product. Just think, soon you'll be in a country that outlaws toy commercials and you'll have to be 18 to buy a toy because some stupid lab will say plastic causes cancer.' Kids: 'Boooooooooo!' Frisco: 'In Argentina, everyone in here will be allowed to smoke and play with toys all they want. Freedoms will be extended to all age groups and sexes...' (The kids giggle at the word 'sex'. Frisco puffs on his cigar.) Frisco: 'Now, we will arrive in Tucson in a few minutes for our show. You will join 5 other elementary classes from this state and I will field all of your questions and concerns about my campaign. I assume Mrs. Sayles has told you the rules?' Kids: 'Yes!' Frisco: 'Good, excellent. Now, I have a special surprise for you....' (Frisco whips out a ticket for Friday Fusion.) Frisco: 'You will get to attend Friday Fusion tonight to see your favorite superstar defeat oppressors of the Argentinean people!' (The kids cheer loudly and begin to chatter and ask questions.) Kid #3: 'Will Legend Inc. be there!?' (Frisco rolls his eyes.) Frisco: 'Yes, Legend Inc. is among the heathen oppressors of my people.' Kid #3: 'Cool!' (Another kid raises his hand. Frisco nods to him.) Kid #4: 'Is Big Alan Steele gonna wrestle?' Frisco: 'No, he will be rendered unconscious and carried off to Argentina where he will wait for trial and execution upon my upcoming ascension.' Kid #4: 'But he's gonna be at Fusion?' (Frisco grits his teeth.) Frisco: 'Yes!' Kid #4: 'What about Boda?' (Frisco sighs.) Frisco: 'This is not relevant to my campaign. It's a special treat I'm giving you little bast--bambinos. Now just thank me and remain silent until we arrive at our venue. You will be able to ask questions there...political questions.' (Frisco looks around at the kids.) Mrs. Sayles: 'What do we say to the nice man?' (The kids look down and speak in unison.) Kids: 'Thank you!' Frisco: 'You're welcome, but I thank all of you, for you are the future--and you will be among the people who will run this country and will keep the bond between my Argentina and America alive!' (The kids clap and Frisco bows. We are now brought out of the cruising campaign bus which enters the city of Tuscon. The gunner on the roof is now asleep at his post with drool streaming off his face and into the passing southwestern winds. The scene fades into the mountains to the right and into darkness.)
