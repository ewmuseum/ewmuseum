The Wrong Schmoopie (Part One)

18 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 18/03/03 It was times like these when Lacuna found herself wondering about life. Life in general, one huge topic to most, but Lacuna considered it alongside those little chores you had to do for pocket money as a child. To her, this careful consideration was just a chore and had no more importance than washing the pots or cleaning shoes (neither of which she did anyway, why bother when you could pay people to do it for you?) But what was a ‘time like this’? A ‘time like this’ was when a treadmill gently whirred beneath her charging feet, her slim body wrapped in elasticised Lycra that hugged her figure like a second skin. A ‘time like this’ was when her sweat soaked her raven black hair, welding it to her tanned forehead like a black mask. A ‘time like this’ was her daily exercise session. She liked to exercise; it gave her something to do. Rattling around in some giant house with only morons to keep you company would do bad things to any person and Lacuna was no exception. Since developing her money, she became overly focused on developing more money, and when the extra money came in she was back to square one, needing even more of the green stuff. It was an obsession and she knew it, but she had suffered greatly from it. Sometimes she would say it was a good thing, this consequence, but a psychiatrist may give you a different idea. She had grown to detest people. Oh, it used to be just American’s with their commercial attitudes towards everything (yeah, she was a hypocrite too) but now it was everyone within glaring distance. She wasn’t against talking to people; she just didn’t like their company too much. It always ended with her getting bored, or her getting pissed off and hitting someone, or her getting angry and smashing someone around the head with a vase (ah, good times) but whatever happened, she was always the cause of the problems. There was usually a reason that caused her to lapse into these moods; these intense waves of energy that cause her to analyse herself so deeply that it hurts. Maybe its just another character flaw, just another little problem that will plague her until she can be bothered to actually be bothered to try and sort it out. Sometimes she would compare herself to Macbeth, or any other Shakespearean tragic characters. She had her problems and she would generally live with them, for whatever reasons. Then again, maybe she held herself in far too high a regard for someone of her disposition. Maybe she was just that little English girl with all the money. She clutched her hand to the side of her head and blinked violently, pushing the images from her mind. “Will you bloody shut up? Thinking is one thing, but mental rambling? Please!” Yeah, there was mental rambling, but now she was shouting at herself. Real stable Lacuna, real good progress. End sarcasm. She crossed the room, slowly examining each separate torture device. She passed the exercise bikes, the weights, it was all there; each individual pieces to tear away at the sanity until nothing remained. She was the problem, or was she just a symptom. “Oh stop with the mental analysis!” again she shouted at herself as nobody else was in the vicinity She isn’t insane. That was true, she was as mentally focused as she needed to be, and far more so than any insane (or even sane) person needed to be. This was her problem. She approached the flash new ring that stood in the centre of the room, highlighted by four powerful spotlights that hung from the metal ceiling. It was brand new, the latest addition to her multi-purpose training facilities and was yet to be tested. She planted a hand firmly on the ropes and twanged them backwards, feeling the extreme tension of the elastic as the ropes bounced around, casting impressive shadows on the purple canvas mat that makes up the ring. It was impressive, in all comparison it was probably of a better quality than the one used on PWC TV shows. “Time for a little test run I think” she mumbled as she climbed onto the apron and jumped over the ropes with great agility, landing on her feet like a cat on the purple canvas. She glanced around the room, her almond eyes roaming across the large brick walls. She was truly alone and needed the confirmation the quick glance had presented her with. She liked confirmation; it left nothing to her over active imagination. Suddenly, she began to sprint across the ring, her footsteps echoing loudly around the large gymnasium as her feet banged heavily into the canvas. She met the ropes on the over side, turning nimbly and used the elastic as a catapult to gather more momentum. As she returned, she flipped fully in the air, landing flat on her back with a loud yelp. As she lay staring up at the bright strip lighting on the roof, a strange thing happened. She laughed. She laughed for the first time in the day. And as she lay there laughing, laughing at nothing at all, Jade slowly entered the room. She moved slowly from the door and weaved in and out of the exercise machines. The look on her face was priceless, a strange combination of intrigue and suspicion as Lacuna laid in the centre of her ring laughing. Jade swept a hand through her hair and stopped by the side of the large ring, eyeing it with even more intensified suspicion. “What –are- you doing?” asked Jade, trying to keep the humour out of her voice. Lacuna’s laughing stopped abruptly and she sat up with her back to Jade. She ran a hand through her hair. “Where the bloody hell did you come from?” “Nu-uh, answer my question first, ‘Cuna. Were you training? Can I train with you?” Lacuna, who had now swivelled to face Jade, smiled slightly as the shock of her sudden arrival began to wear off slightly. She didn’t quite know how exactly she would answer Jade, it was strange how she would do something and not be able to explain it a few seconds later. It was just something that happened. “I was, err, I don’t actually know. Buy I wasn’t training… I think” mentioned Lacuna unconvincingly, her hand emphasising every point her head tried to make. “So can I not train with you then?” I’m not sure Jade even realised how stupid this sounded, or if she did, I’m not sure she regretted it. Seeing as Lacuna had been lying in the middle of the ring laughing at the ceiling on her own. “ I suppose so, its not like I am doing anything anyway” Lacuna sat back down and gently fell backwards, continuing staring at the ceiling with a renewed vigour. Jade climbed into the ring and joined her, collapsing heavily to the purple mat with a thud. Both women stared in silence for a minute, lost in their own thoughts. Outside, the clouds drifted by slowly, the sun dipped slightly across the horizon, people were born, people were killed, and weapons of mass destruction were created, but all they could do was think to themselves. How limited is human life? There was literally nothing you could do to stop it. We are powerless in our own existence. How sad. That was what Lacuna was thinking about. God only knows what Jade was thinking. So Lacuna, as blunt as ever, decided to ask. “What are you thinking about?” she asked and rolled her eyes to face Jade, who continued staring at the absence of sky. “Nothing really. Pretty neat ceiling though” responded Jade with her typical Jade-ness “I’m angry, and I don’t know why” she changed the subject again, as her mind began to notice quite how broken her narrative was becoming. “Is that normal? Well, it is for you I suppose” Jade smiled and Lacuna rolled her head back to the ceiling in protest, eventually giving in to the humour and smiling back in return. Jade had started to become quite cheeky with Lacuna recently, in the time she had been living in the same house as her they had began to develop a bond, and she wasn’t even going to bother with the anger at the situation. For one of the first times in her life she -allowed- someone to joke at her expense. “What did you mean by that?” asked Lacuna, still smiling at the ceiling “I don’t know, it just seems to happen a lot, you know?” Lacuna did know, and she recognised the meaning of every word as they reverberated around the inside of her head. “Yeah, I know… But I don’t, you get me?” “No, not really” if there was something to say about Jade, she said what she thought. She was honest to the bone. “Forget it then” And silence on the gymnasium again. Outside, the clouds –still- drifted by slowly, the sun –still- dipped slightly across the horizon, people were -still- born, people were –still- killed, and weapons of mass destruction were -still created. And all they did was waste precious minutes thinking. “You know, I think I know what your angry about” motioned Jade, who broke the silence with a slight excitement to her voice “Yeah? Shoot” “Your anxious, about facing Tessa” With a sudden burst of energy, Lacuna sat upright and held a hand to her head “I’ve got it! It’s the wrong Schmoopie!” “Eh?” “Thank you!” And as the pair sat up in the middle of the ring, Lacuna could not help but think it. She could not help but think the impossible had happened. She could not help but think that she had finally made a friend.
