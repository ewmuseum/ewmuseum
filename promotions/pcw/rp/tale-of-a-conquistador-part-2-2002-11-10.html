Tale of a Conquistador (Part 2)

10 November 2002

Date: 11/10/02 A Tale of a Conquistador It was the turn of the century and conquistadors had seemed to be extinct. Spain was still left with one man, sixty-three years of age. His name was the Iron Heart Savio Menta. Savio had fought long and crucial years under the name of Spain. He had conquered territories and made himself known as Savio the Savior of Spain. One day there was a heinous murder that swept the land and all fingers pointed at Savio. Savio swore in the name of God that he had been framed and never commit such a gruesome murder. Even though the people loved him they couldn’t let him go without any punishment. He was sentenced to life in prison and had stood there for twenty years. Savio soon forgot all about his glory, and began to live to die. He didn’t care about a think in the world, and he felt God had betrayed him. What had he done to deserve this? Why was it him that had to serve this penalty? He worked his whole life to get so high in the social scale, and in one short week he fell straight to the bottom. After twelve years of imprisonment the Army of Spain’s director visited Savio. “Mr. Savo Menta, may we have a word with you.” The well-mannered and strong lieutenant stated. “What do you want from me? I am nothing but an old man.” Savio responded as he turned around showing his pale complexion and white hair. “That may be the case, but your country needs you and we are willing to take you away from here.” The lieutenant says with a straight face. The words Savio had wanted to here for such a long time were now words that meant nothing to him. “Why serve a country that persecutes their heroes.” Savio responded with an evil and sinister outlook. “The city has been swiped by a fleet of Moors. They have taken innocent citizens hostage and burnt down homes. We called in the countries defense, but these barbarians have the city of lock. We need help from within the boundaries and we know you’re the man to lead it.” The lieutenant says trying to persuade Savio. “Oh, so in the time of joy I’m a criminal, and in the time of panic I’m a hero, well I don’t play with those cards.” Savio responds quickly. “Your family is held hostage.” The lieutenant says swiftly and reluctantly. With those words, Savio didn’t even think twice. Within ten minutes he was out of the prison and strategizing ways to defeat the Moors. That afternoon Savio led a fleet of three hundred soldiers into the battleground. With little weapons and only sticks and a few swords, the Spanish went into action. With a bad back and white hair, Savio led the way taking out Moore after Moore. The people rebelled and led a counter attack. The distraction opened up a block in the city barricades allowing the Spanish army to come in. When the Spanish Army arrived the Moore’s surrendered and were banned from the country. The city rejoiced and Savio was a hero once again. “Savio, you should be proud, your people love you and you proved us wrong in our interpretation of you.” The lieutenant said proud to be standing in the presence of a warrior. “I didn’t do it for the people. I did it for my family and myself.” Savio said as he walked away and met back up with his family that he hadn’t seen in twelve years. The morale of the story is a conquistador represents and fights for his country, but he fights for himself before anything else. As Romantico grabs his hat and coat, an admiring fan comes up to him. “That was a great reading today Romantico, where do you get your inspiration?” The young man says with a joyous look on his face. “Through a life of pain, my friend. All artists have lives of pain. Even though it’s unfortunate, it’s the truth.” Romantico responds in a deep tone. “Man, I wish my life wasn’t so easy.” The young man says with a look of disgust. “Never wish that my friend, for I would do anything to rewind the tapes and be happy in my early years.” Romantico said as he walked away and left the café. That night, Romantico thought of all the children throughout the world. How gullible and easy to harm they are. He thought about himself at that age and realized he was miles ahead in his bringing. Even though it’s a quality you don’t want to have, Romantico wished for that innocence and stupidity. All he ever wanted was a proper childhood. He then though of his reading and poem and how he relates to Savio. Both had times where they were up and down. Savio was in jail and nobody in the world even cared about him, the same way Romantico was a nobody back when he was homeless and moving from shelter to shelter. Then they had their high points where everyone liked them. When Savio was a hero for saving hundreds of people and when Romantico was a fighting icon. The same final judgments applied for both men. Even though they loved their country, everything they did was for the better of themselves. Is that not true for us all? Even though we like to think about others, in the end it’s for the greater good of ourselves. On Turmoil, Romantico is going to win his match and progress toward cruiserweight glory, not for anybody else BUT HIMSELF!
