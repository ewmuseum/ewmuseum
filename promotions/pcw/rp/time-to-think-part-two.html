Time to Think (Part Two)

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Oct. 24/02 “Well, you just look really tough. I think someday you’ll be a really big star, and you’ll have lots and lots and lots and lots of money” . . . The words of a young boy who didn’t really know me at all. Those words changed my life. Before meeting that young lad, I had all but given up on a future in wrestling. I had already begun my journey from Michigan through Ontario, Canada and onto New York, and I was having doubts. I had decided to stop in Toronto, Ontario to think things through. I could think of little or no reason to continue on with this foolish, spur-of-the-moment change in careers, so I ended up deciding to return home to go back to teaching. However, on my way back to the car, I met up with a young boy named Tommy . . . and his words changed me. Now realizing that complete strangers believed in my abilities, my eyes were finally open. I could be a wrestler . . . I could be a great wrestler . . . I could be a ‘Marvelous’ wrestler. So I continued on my way to New York . . . ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ((On the Road)) Everything seemed more forgiving now . . . like a weight had lifted off my shoulders . . . I had finally crossed the border back into the United States, and I was now halfway across the state of New York. The leaves in the trees were all beginning to change color, but their brilliant colors were masked the eerily green glow of the moon. The heater in my car was busted, and stuck on extreme heat, so I had my window rolled down . . . and crisp autumn air swirling through my hair felt good. The balance of cool air from the wind, and the warming ambiance the boy’s kind words gave me created the perfect balance. I was totally at ease now . . . I knew I was doing the right thing. I felt more energized, like the feeling I get when I hear my music blare through the arena, and the crowd begins cheering for me. However, timed seemed to pass even slower than it had during the first half of my road trip. Just thinking about my match sent shivers up my spine . . . the same feeling I got when I had my first match in the PWA . . . ~ ~ ~ ~ ~((Flashback)) I was so nervous . . . I couldn’t feel my finger or toes . . . the adrenaline coursing through my veins was so thick, so strong it was blocking the blood flow. Never in my wildest dreams had I ever imagined I’d be a student at a wrestling academy. As I peeked through the curtains, I could see the light in the arena slowly dimming and out of no where “Baby’s Got a Temper” by Prodigy, the song I had chosen for my entrance theme, began to play. My eyes bugged out of their sockets, and the sweat fell from my brow like water dripping from a Brita Water Filter. This was my moment to shine . . . I shan’t disappoint . . . ~ ~ ~ ~ ~((Back to the Present / Johnny Law)) Remembering those first few moments as a wrestler brought a smile to my face . . . but none the less my attention was taken away from the road. I didn’t even notice the police car parked on the side of the road . . . the police officer sitting on the hood with radar held high. It wasn’t til about a mile down the road that I finally realized the flashing lights and screaming siren behind me were actually meant for me. I pulled my car over to the shoulder of the road, and smacked the steering wheel in frustration. The officer slowly mad his way up to my window, and in low, demanding voice, he said . . . Officer - “License and registration please.” I rummaged through my glove compartment, found the correct documents, and handed them to the officer. He took one look at my license, gave me a raised eyebrow. Officer - “Mark Michigan, eh?” With my mind on other matters, I had accidently passed him my PCW VIP Pass instead of my driver’s license. I quickly snatched my Pass from him and handed him my driver’s license. Still, with a raised eyebrow, the officer continued . . . Officer - “So, you’re a wrestler with PCW? That’s very interesting . . . I’m a big fan of theirs.” I thanked the officer profusely for watching PCW programs, like a loner sucks up to the cool kids . . . but before I could finish, he cut me off in mid sentence. Officer - “How come I never seen you wrestle before?” I explained that I was just called up from the PWA, and my first show this Friday at Fusion. I told him all about my match, and my stay in the PWA . . . but no matter how interested he seemed, he really didn’t look like he was buying my explanation. Officer - “Well . . . the reason I pulled you over was because there has been a white Honda Accord reported stolen, and I had to pull you over to inspection . . . you know the drill.” The officer handed my license and registration back, and with a sigh of relief I thanked the officer, and commended him on his enthusiasm for his job and responsibilities. And with all that said and done, I was off like a shot . . . back on track to New York. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~((Back On the Road)) After my little run in with the law, the rest of my trip seemed like a breeze. I zipped down the highway like fat kid chasin’ an Ice Cream Truck, and I was on the outskirts of New York City in no time. Navigating through downtown New York proved to be quite difficult . . . like winning the Royal Rumble. Everyone else on the street is against you . . . and it’s up to your cunning and ability to survive, but like I said before . . . nothing could slow me down. I eventually made my way out fo the hustle and bustle of downtown NYC, and was only a few moments from entering the parking lot of John F. Kennedy International Airport. For such a busy place, I never imagined I’d find a parking space so easily . . . but there it was, like God himself obstructed all other vehicles in the world from parking in the spot he saved for me. Finally, things were goin’ my way. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~((Good’ay Mate)) After all that driving, you’d think I would be the most exhausted man in the Northern Hemisphere . . . but to be honest, I didn’t think I’d ever go to sleep that night. After parking my car, and dragging my bags to one fo the many airport hotels, I managed to check myself in the Ramada Plaza Hotel for the night. However, renting the room was a waste of my time and money. I spent the whole night looking out the window, watching intently as plane after plane took off. In roughly ten hours, I would be on my way to Paris, where I’d then travel to Israel by way of planes, trains, and automobiles. After an hour or so of watching the planes take off, I decided to take a walk down to the hotels twenty-four hour bar. As I exited the elevator and entered the hotel’s lobby, I could see that I was far from the only person awake. Travelers from all over were checking and checking out . . . the bar was full of weary tourists, drunken off-duty pilots, and scantily clad women . . . looked like my kind fo place . . . yeah right! I made my way through the mob of drunks, and waited patiently at the bar for someone to serve me. A young man on the other side of the bar looked up at me, and with his Australian accent, he asked me . . . Bartender - “Noisy crowd down here wake you up mate?” I shook my head, and told the bartender my situation and he actually seemed to sympathize for me. I couldn’t believe what I was hearing . . . and just as I was with the young boy in Toronto I was floored by his kind words. Bartender - “Ai mate, I’m a bartender in an International Airport . . . you think I haven’t heard that kind of thing before? Come on, what can I get for you to drink?” I order my usual, Rum and Coke, and the bartender had it in my hand lickety-split. I asked him how much I owed for the drink, but he replied with a simple smirk . . . Bartender - “No charge mate . . .” Once again . . . I was floored by his generosity. I promised the barkeep if I was ever back in the hotel while he was working, I would give him a hefty tip . . . because like the young boy, the bartender also believed I would someday make a great impact on the world of wrestling. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~((Thoughts of a Sleeper)) All these strangers . . . as if they were looking up to me . . . that’s what being a wrestler is all about. I’m a role model now! If I eat my vegetables, then so will my fans . . . If I use manners, so will my fans . . . I’ve truly found my home . . . “Several weeks ago, I was just a lowly teacher trying to find his place in life. I found wrestling . . . Then I was a lowly teacher turned wrestling trying to find his place in the world of professional wrestling . . . Now . . . now I’m Mark Michigan . . . I have found my place amongst the superhuman athletes we call wrestlers . . . and I know what I must do. I can teach others by my action . . . I can finally be a teacher . . . but one thing is for sure . . . with this teacher, you definitely do not want to be kept after class for detention . . .” Naw, that’s way too corny! How could I be taken seriously if I’m standing in a ring spouting of crap like that?! I drop my hairbrush from my lips and turn away from the mirror where I’d been practicing my promos. I had thought being an English major with a degree in teaching would mean I’d be great with words . . . but trash talking in front of thousands of people is hardly like reading poetry to a group of third graders. No matter, I still had a twelve-hour flight across the Atlantic to beautiful Paris . . . I’m sure I could think of something to say to Rich Rollins while I’m way up, miles above sea level. For now, I think I’ll just lay down and try to sleep . . . tomorrow is a big day . . . ZZZzzzZZZzzz . . .
