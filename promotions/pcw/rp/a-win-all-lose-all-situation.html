A win all lose all situation...

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Moments ago. It was to be a last man-standing match and its purpose was to crown a new number one contender for the PCW world heavyweight championship. Up until the past few hours Titu Xansiv hadn’t really thought about it. Yes, it was a match he was to be involved in, and yes it did have huge rewards, but the only real thing he’d gone to the trouble of considering in relation to the PPV match was DarkStar, his most hated nemesis. He sat down in the lounge and looked off into space, blank-faced. ‘What’s the matter, Titu?’ said Isiy, staring at him from across the room. He didn’t answer. ‘Hey! I just said: ‘what’s the matter’?’ – she paused, waiting – ‘Hello?!’ Once again, he didn’t answer. Isiy sighed, took off her sandal and launched it at Pharaoh. It hit him on the side of the head with a crack. He turned sharply, ‘Oww! What in the name of the Gods is the matter with you?!’ Isiy sighed, ‘I just asked you the same thing!’ Pharaoh shot up from his seat, rubbing his head, and stormed out of the room. He returned moments later with a glass of water in hand, sat back down and looked blankly into the liquid below. Isiy waited for a reply…and waited…and waited… …and waited some more. ‘Titu!’ she roared after a while. ‘Can you hear anything I’m saying?!’ Xansiv looked up, wearily. ‘Don’t give me that look either; it’s not going to work this time!’ she frowned. Pharaoh looked back down into the glass. ‘I’ve got a match coming up soon.’ He murmured. Isiy rolled her eyes, ‘You always have a match coming up, Titu.’ ‘Yes, but this one’s quite special. If I win, you see, I will finally be named the number one contender to the PCW world heavyweight championship.’ He sighed mournfully. ‘It’s something I’ve always wanted, and finally I get my chance to shine.’ Isiy bit her bottom lip, sympathising a little. ‘Well…just go out there and win it!’ she was usually fairly good at rallying Titu’s spirits. ‘I take it I’ll need to be there?’ She didn’t really like going to the events, but it seemed to give her husband the support he needed to compete. In truth she didn’t like Titu wrestling, she didn’t see it as a fit job. People died in wrestling, for God’s sake, and some of the more unfortunate were paralysed for the rest of their lives. It was dangerous, and she didn’t like it. ‘Well, yeah.’ He looked up into her deep blue eyes, ‘If you don’t mind, of course.’ In actual fact she did mind. She minded a hell of a lot. ‘Nah, I’ll come along. It’s in China isn’t it?’ ‘Yes,’ said Pharaoh, looking back down into the glass of water. ‘Should be nice…’ There was something much more wrong with him than nerves. Isiy could tell. She always could at times like this. ‘What’s wrong, Titu - I know there’s more. Cummon, just tell me.’ Pharaoh looked across at her, but only for the slightest moment. ‘Nothing; honestly, my love.’ She wasn’t buying that! ‘Titu! Tell me, it’ll make you feel better, I promise.’ Pharaoh seemed to consider this for a moment and then said: ‘Isiy, you do realize that I finally get to battle DarkStar one on one, don’t you?’ His voice was sullen and quite sad; it was as though he didn’t want to fight the demon DarkStar after all. ‘I mean, after all the beatings, all the hate-filled words, and all the blood, it comes down to this…this one last match. And it’ll be chaos, my little jewel – pure chaos.’ ‘Titu, I’ve seen bigger Hebrews than DarkStar and not even the biggest slave ever had the chance to beat down his king. Think of it like that: DarkStar’s nothing compared to you; you’re practically royalty!’ She sometimes enjoyed coming from an angle Pharaoh could relate to. Gathering his spirit always made her feel good, too. She hoped he knew he could always rely on her for moral support. Just as long as the damned Gods were kept out of it that was. ‘True.’ He said at length, ‘But it all comes down to this, Isiy. If I lose then I’m the one who goes away ashamed. If I win then I’m the one who gains a chance to capture a dream. It’s all on the line – everything…is on the line.’ Isiy looked at him in more detail. His face was unsure and timid; he probably had butterflies in his stomach. She rarely saw him under this light - he looked weak. ‘Just because you might lose this time doesn’t mean you’ll never get another chance. Don’t worry…just have faith.’ She must have spoken the right words this time because Titu’s face lit up. His eyes grew wide, brightening, and a smile etched over his lips. ‘Have faith,’ he recited, ‘Isiy, you’re right! I must have faith!’ Isiy smiled in the dim morning sun. ‘In the Gods!’ he added. Her smile vanished. It always came down to this. She should have seen it coming, really. The Gods this and the Gods that. The whole concept was beginning to grow old. ‘With the help of Ra and Horus and of course the fierce Goddess of war, Sekh-met I shall fall not at the hands of the demonistic DarkStar!’ Isiy sighed, her sympathy faltering. ‘’Demonistic’ isn’t a word.’ ‘Well, it is now!’ he beamed. Pharaoh’s voice was triumphant and heroic as though he’d already beaten DarkStar. His face also shone an overly smug, downright dumb and cheesy grin. She shook her head, the sympathy ultimately draining away. ‘Titu, are you actually going to drink that water?’ she said cynically, ignoring his words. Pharaoh looked down at the glass in his hands. ‘Erm—’ ‘If you’re not going to drink it then why the hell did you get it?’ Yep…the compassion was defiantly gone now. ‘Well—’ Isiy scowled, ‘Sometimes, Titu Xansiv! Sometimes!’ Like most women she actually made that sound like a threat. She got up from her chair and stormed out of the room leaving a perplexed Pharaoh staring at the doorway through which she left. ‘Heh,’ he said and downed the water.
