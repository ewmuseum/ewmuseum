Bygone Inscription Chronicles Part 2: Xenophobic Militia

13 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7.13.02 7.13.027:48 pm.Oxford, Mississippi-- “SOLDIER, YOU WILL OBEY MY COMMAND! DO YOU UNDERSTAND OR DO I HAVE TO KNOCK IT INTO YOUR HEAD, PISSBUCKET!?” “YES, DRILL SERGEANT!” That’s the sound of a drill sergeant’s gruff voice teaching the ways of the United States Army to a line of soldiers in uniform. The scene has opened up inside of the US Army base in Oxford, Mississippi. Militia trucks line the base, along with military soldiers, armed with assault artillery. It ranges from white men to black men; all wearing the army coloured attire. The sergeant is a tall African American, sporting a camouflage muscle shirt with the same type of pants. He wears a camouflaged hat, walking with the same pace circling his soldiers. “WHICH IS IT, SOLDIER!? WILL YOU OBEY MY COMMAND OR WILL I HAVE TO BEAT YOUR WHITE ASS?” The sergeant says blatantly to a certain private. “SIR, I WILL OBEY YOUR COMMAND, SIR!” A drip of sweat seeps down the soldier’s forehead, nervous of what’s to come. The drill sergeant walks up to the soldier who stands straight as a toothpick and gets face to face with the soldier. “When I say 50, you give me 50. If I say to run 50, you run 50. If I tell you that I’m going to fuck your mother, you allow it. Do you understand that, soldier?” The drill sergeant says moving his face closer to the warrior’s. The soldier says nothing, as he just stares ahead. “I ASKED YOU A QUESTION, SOLDIER! YOU WILL ANSWER ME!” “SIR, I ASK FOR YOU TO PLEASE LEAVE MY MOTHER OUT OF THIS, SIR.” The soldier says with a serious look in his eye, trying not to upset the officer. “Soldier, if I want to bring your mother in this, then I will. I will do anything, because I am a higher rank than you are. That makes you angry, too, doesn’t it?” “Sir, what makes me angry, sir?” “It makes you angry that a black man has more superiority than a white man, doesn’t it, soldier?” “Sir, no sir,” the combatant says, heaving a sigh. The drill sergeant gets nose to nose with the soldier, staring him in the eyes with fury. “WHAT IS TH...,” the ranked officer stops, watching how the soldier moves his face away from the sergeant. “Sir, you need a tic-tac, sir,” the soldier says with a smile. A couple of the boys in the back begin to chuckle until the sergeant turns around. They shut up as soon as he turns, before he goes back to interrogating the young warrior. He gets even closer to him, this time. “I DON’T GIVE A SHIT IF I NEED A TIC-TAC, SOLDIER. IF I WANT, I WILL TAKE A SHIT AND MAKE YOU SLEEP IN IT. IF I WANT, I WILL SPIT ON THE GROUND AND MAKE YOU LICK IT UP! IS THAT CLEAR!?” The sergeant’s eyes are full of rage. “SIR, YES SIR!” “Now, give me 500 for your actions,” the drill sergeant advises the soldier to do. The head of this line of defense force walks to the front of the Territorial Army in training. He puts his hands behind his back and revolves around, gazing at the sky. “Let me tell you something, soldiers. One thing I don’t like is a racist.........”-- Kris Burden closes his journal and stares across the same riverbank that he’s been for the last few hours. His eyes seem to swell up as he runs his hands through his hair, watching the sun set. “I remember seeing this from a hill when I was a kid...This world has always been full of racists. There’s nothing we can do about it...it’s something that we will always have. There’s a warrior trying to teach the warrior in training the way that it has to be. He’s showing him how to be a man, and yet the soldier has pure hatred in his eyes. I knew that boy when I was a kid. The young man’s father hung a rebel flag in their front yard, and taught his son never to trust an African American. So, he did what he was told and when he’s told a command by a black man, he thinks he can say different, until the man tells him the way it’s going to be or the way he’s going to end up. This frightened the soldier to an extent. Finally, opening his mind to the way that it is now, he loses his sense of bravery to his higher ranked officer...a man that he promised his father he would never trust, a black man. A few days later, I heard that the black man was murdered in his own home with his kids and wife...just the same way that his father and mother were killed. Racists...except, he got away from it. The soul survivor...shot dead in his own home by basically the same actions.” I sigh and get up from my sitting position, putting the black book into my pocket. “Trigger shall do the same this coming Turmoil. He may call himself a warrior...but warrior’s die. I have been burdened for so long, and it’s time for his bravery to take a halt. I can be attacked from behind, just like a man is assaulted in his own home with his wife and kids...I seen it coming. May it be a race thing here in the land of the Dixie...or may it be a warrior turned against one another just for the mind of his own...I’m grasping the moment, and I’m deeming these moments golden...because retribution is what some people deserve. And they called me crazy...Who cares...fuck it.
