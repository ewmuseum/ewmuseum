Disabilites? Wow!

2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10.12.02 “Funny, I always thought that you were the short and no questions type.” the short and stocky equivalent to Dante Jackson stands out, wearing a pink halter with faded blue jeans. “I mean, you’re so just NOT what we’re looking for.” The camera pans around, figuring out a white woman in her early seventies with light faded hair and a stretched out face. A look of shame is upon her face as Tracy leans back in an office chair, a short but subtle posture drawn from her. A quick slip of tea, before she sets the kettle back down, an instant perk up for the day since this Tuesday in Houston isn’t sunny, or happy. It’s raining and boring dull. The former Wrestling Tattler reporter slouches back, her elbow resting upon the head/neck area of the chair as the elderly woman gets up from her seat, audibly sighing with herself. “Oh, and while you’re moving,” the fake prep installs into the woman. “Send the next wannabe in here.” The woman nodded, “Okay…but know that I was a seasoned actress.” Tracy scoffs at the woman, as she shuts the door on her way out. “Damn seasoned actresses, you’d think that no films and eight husbands would kill them by now…” She buzzes on the intercom, reading out something she had previously typed out beforehand, reading it as loud as an Oscar nomination – “Will you send the next one in? The last woman didn’t, guess her hearing aid didn’t get the job done.” The secretary some doors away from Tracy nods her head, before shifting her fingers a bit – ushering in the next young female to be interviewed. Standing at the top of the pack happens to be a lanky brunette, curves like an hour glass with blue eyes and a denim jacket suit combo to match. Her heels clack onto the floor as another woman holds out a long cane like object, tripping the woman up from under her feet. WHOOMP! A sucking sound emits as the woman falls hard on her face, pain induced stares as her neck snaps back behind her, in an Exorcist type of way. The other women in front of her howl in laughter and jokes. The woman dusts herself off, before telepathically slapping all of the women in the room. “Gah!” “Ouch!” “What the fa?” “Jesus!” “Humph,” the woman grunts as she gets back up to a standing position. She laughs on her own as the other women competing for her job happen to be clutching their faces in pain and sudden disbelief. One woman grumbles, “Anybody knows just what the heck that was?” Another woman chips in within record response. “She must be one of those telepaths or something…” The door swings open, as the woman stands in front of Tracy, her hands outreached as if she was Supergirl and Tracy was Lex Luthor. “Can I…um help you?” “Yeah, I’m looking for the job?” A gruff voice, something Tracy definitely wasn’t expecting. Especially from no female as yes there were males that happened to want this position. The Bronx native arches her eyebrows in a surprised manner, before dropping them back down instantly. “What job?” Clicking her teeth, the interviewee yanks her neck forward at Tracy. “The job maybe?” “OH!” Tracy raises in encouragement as the interviewee nods her head in approval. “Well, that’s nice. Do you have any qualifications?” The sky blue eyed woman parted her lips, licking them a bit from not applying chap earlier in the day. “Its like this – I’ve been reporting for a few years, you know getting my name out there and while all this time – people have been warning me for my *BEEP* *BEEP* *BEEP*!” Tracy holds her mouth open, flabbergasted by the woman as she holds her mouth in obvious embarrassment. “It’s my Tourette syndrome.” the woman responds with a nervous chuckle. “Shizzle,” Tracy wonders. “You can’t Snoop Dogg it or nothing?” The woman smiles, “Well no, the *BEEP* *BEEP* is so *BEEP* *BEEP* *BEEP*!” “We’ve gotta help you on that, now don’t we?” Tracy smiles, before the woman gets up from the room, a slight smile on her face. “May I get your name?” The woman’s eyes bulge as Tracy reads this out to her. “It’s Mary *BEEP* Jenkins.” “Well Mary *BEEP* Jenkins, we’ll get back to you on that.” She shortly replies to her as the woman closes the door. Soon from out of the closet appear the one and only – Dante Jackson, a distant laugh and grin appearing on his face. “Well?” he asks his wife. “I’m never being a social worker, too many *BEEP*ing idiots with disabilities! Gosh, I SO HATE THOSE PEOPLE!” “Good,” Dante smiles. “Cause we’re going to an old folks home.” “WHAT?! A FRICKIN OLD FOLKS HOME?! WHAT THE FREAK IS THAT?! WHY THE FREAK DO WE FREAKING HAVE TO GO TO A FREAKING FREAK FREAK!”
