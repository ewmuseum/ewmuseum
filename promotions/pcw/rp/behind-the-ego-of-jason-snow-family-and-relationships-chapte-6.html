Behind the Ego of Jason Snow: Family and Relationships, Chapter 6 - The Monster

Snow doesn’t quite make it to the bathroom before he vomits all over the hallway floor. “Damn it!” he curses, before collapsing to his knees, next to the puddle of vomit. He clutches with long, sweaty, blond hair with both hands and tugs at the roots. A moment ago, he had decided that life wasn’t worth living and that he’s possibly a danger to the people that he cares most about. He can still taste the rust from the gun barrel on his tongue, though now it tastes like rust burning in vomit. ... ... ... It took a few seconds to realize that the gun didn’t fire and his brains, indeed, were still encased by his skull - and even then, he wasn’t sure whether to be upset or relieved. A few more seconds past and he was finally able to unlock every muscle in his body, which had grown incredibly tense in preparation for the bullet. But the bullet never came. “Garbage!” he scowled, tossing the antique gun across the floor of his never-born son’s room. The gun slid hard until it crashed into the wall - firing upon impact. The bullet that was discharged blew a hole the size of a grown man’s fist in the wall to Snow’s left. Snow looked at the hole in shock, his ears still ringing from the shot and his nose picking up traces of burnt gun powder. Only a moment ago, he’d pulled the trigger of the very same gun - nothing happened. He couldn’t help but glance upward - maybe God did care, afterall. Before another thought crossed the young wrestler’s mind, he realized that the majority of the day’s meals were working their way up his esophagus and toward his mouth, and without thinking he rushed toward the bathroom. ... ... ... There are other ways to end one’s life, of course, but the moment has past. Besides, he already pulled the trigger of a gun which he was sucking on - how much nerve could he be expected to have in one night? Slowly, the young man gets to his feet, leaves the vomit, and heads for the stairs. It’s all about David Snow, he thinks. David Snow is to blame for all of this. If it weren’t for David Snow, he never would have inherited this uncanny ability to hurt the people he cares about. If it weren’t for David Snow, none of the past week’s trauma and tragedy would have happened. Snow’s not thinking clearly, and at this moment he even blames his father for the death of his unborn son. It was his wife’s stress level that killed the baby, Snow thinks, then adds vocally, “Stress put on her by me...” It’s all his father’s fault, Snow thinks. Snow never learned how to treat his wife because the example was never shown to him. He learned nothing but violence and hate from his paternal creator - nothing. Snow staggers down the stairs, his eyes glazed over - his father only lives four hours away... it’ll be daylight by then. ... ... ... The sun is new over the Eastern horizon, but Snow has been parked on the side of the street in his rented mustang for nearly an hour, staring up the long driveway at a little white house - he grew up here. It’s been nearly a year since Snow’s last visit to Backwater, and even then, he avoided his parents at all costs. He purposely schedules himself to be out of town on the dates that they visit Haley - his wife has always refused to cut them out of the young girl’s life, as Snow has suggested on so many occasions. That little house brings back so many memories for Snow - so many nightmares. Every nightmare has the same monster, however - David Snow. Snow remembers spending so many nights of his childhood lying on his bed listening to the chaos going on in the other parts of the house. He used to promise himself every night that one day he would come back to this little house and pay his father back for all the pain he caused. As Snow grew older, he saw how childish those promises were - they don’t seem so childish now... ...Not now that Jason Snow finds himself causing people the same pain that he hated his father for causing. David Snow turned Jason Snow into a monster, in the younger’s eyes. Jason Snow hurt Krista, his sister-in-law, physically - David Snow made him do it. Jason Snow constantly hurts his wife emotionally - David Snow makes him do it. Jason Snow may one day hurt his daughter, Haley - He won’t let David Snow make him do it. “I’ll cut out your heart, you old bastard,” he mutters while glaring at the little house. A moment later, he collects his courage, gets out of the car, and begins the tread up the long driveway toward his childhood home. The place could use a coat of paint, he notices - nothing has changed. It’s always looked this way. From the time Snow was born, to the time he moved out at seventeen, to now, on the day he returns as a troubled twenty-four year old man - the house has never changed. Before he gets to the front door, it opens, and a middle-aged man appears - this is David Snow. This is the monster. This is the creator and the destroyer of Jason Snow... hatred burns in Jason Snow’s eyes as he stares at his father. Even in his late forties, David Snow is an impressive man in the physical sense - it’s likely that in his day, he was nearly as physically fit as his son who is known for being a health nut. David’s biceps bulge from beneath his tight black t-shirt - the result of thirty-five years of hard labour in the fishing industry. Much like his son, David has always been a workaholic. There were many nights that David, much like Jason, left his wife waiting by the phone wondering if something horrible had happened to her husband. Years later, when Jason was a teenager, his mother stopped waiting by the phone. Would, one day, Jason’s wife stop waiting by the phone too? The physical resemblance of the two begins with their beards. David’s beard is a fair bit longer and darker than his son’s, but any stranger would recognize the pair as father and son despite the difference in hair colour and personal upkeep. Even considering the fact that David is almost completely bald, while his son has a thick crop of long blond hair, the resemblance is staggering. Snow hates the resemblance, but he knows that it goes much further than physical depths. One resemblance that Jason could never deny are the eyes. David Snow was known throughout his life for having cold, hard, blue eyes - the same eyes that his son has used to stare holes through people in a wrestling ring. But now, as Jason looks at the front step of the house where he grew up, the old man is not looking through the eyes that he remembers - they look so much softer now. Almost human, thinks Snow. But they’re not human, he tells himself. Nothing about this man is human. That’s why no man has dared cross his path in over twenty years - he’s not human. His seemingly superhuman strength made him a legend in this small fishing village, along with his fiery temper and the fact that he was thought to be indestructible in a street fight. Then again, people in this tiny village tell the same stories about Jason from his wild teen years. Much like his father, there’s no man in Backwater who would dare cross Jason Snow. Even before he became a wrestling megastar, his reputation had spread throughout the entire county... ...Like father like son... “Jason?” David says as softly as he can though his unbelievably gruff voice restricts him from sounding overly caring. It hurts him to look in his son’s eyes now... those eyes are so full of fire so full of rage. He remembers how that feels. “Yeah,” Snow nods, his eyes burning a hole through the old man. “I heard about...” he hesitates - emotional subject matter has always been off-limits with David Snow. His son, Jason, knows the feeling - his wife constantly accuses him of backing down from painful subjects. “We came to see you and your family, but you were gone.” Snow remembers - he left for L.A. for his Wrestling God 3 match when he got word his father was heading that way. “I know,” says Jason coldly. David steps down off the steps and approaches his son, looking for a way to make easier conversation, “Christ,” he says while shaking his head, “Haven’t you ever heard of a haircut, boy?” The words are half meant as a joke, but Jason only hears the other half of his meaning. “What the fuck do you care?” he snaps, letting the old man know for certain that his son hasn’t come for a pleasant visit. This is the first time David has ever heard his son cuss in person - of course, he’s watched Snow’s tongue go wild on PCW television, so he’s surprisingly shocked by the emotion that Snow loads into his words in person. “Alright,” David says, backing away from his son while trying to hold his own temper in check, “I know you’re going through a rough time right now, Jason, but -” “Rough time!?” Snow explodes, “You have no fucking idea what I’m going through, old man! Don’t try to get fatherly on me - you don’t know anything about that!” The words hurt David deeply, but he knows that he deserved to hear them. Secretly, he’s always known that one day he would hear them - he knows his son like he knows his own image, and he knew that one day, his son would explode. “Jason,” David begins, his voice full of empathy. “Shut the fuck up!” Snow interrupts a second time, “I didn’t come here for conversation!” “Then what did you come here for?” David asks, trying to hold his voice soft. The question hits Jason like a cannon ball - why is he here? He knew he had to face his father, but what did he plan to do after? Instead of searching for the answer, Snow explodes with pure emotion, “I don’t fucking know!” he screams at the old man, “Maybe I just had to get a look at you one last time. Maybe I’m here to break you’re God damn neck! I don’t know! Maybe I’m here because every fucking time I look in the mirror, I see your face! Maybe it’s because every time I speak, I hear your voice! I hate your face! And I hate your voice! And most of all, I hate you, you old bastard, I hate you more than you even fucking know!” From the time Snow was a teenager, he pictured the moment where he would finally confront his father - he planned it. And now that the moment is here, he finds that none of the planning was necessary. There’s no thought process to convey pure emotion. “I don’t hate you,” David says, lowering his aging eyes to the ground. “I wish I could take it all back now, Jason - but I can’t.” “You’re damn right you can’t!” Snow snaps, “And you can’t take back all the fucked up things that I’m doing either! But you know what!? They’re you’re fault! Everything is your fault! I can’t fucking be happy and it’s all your fault!” “Jason,” David sighs, trying to think of someway to comfort his emotional son - comfort doesn’t run in the Snow family. There’s no comfort for Jason Snow, however. He hates the man in front of him. He hates the man for what happened to Krista. He hates the man for how he mistreats his own wife. He hates the man for making him think that one day, he - himself - might hurt his own daughter, Haley. Jason Snow hates David Snow for terrible deeds that Jason Snow committed. He hates his father because he’s turning into his father... “I’ve got it all!” Jason screams with sarcastic joy lacing his voice. He reaches into his pocket, pulls out a wad of bills, and tosses them into the air, “You want money? I’ve fucking got money! I’ve got a family! I’ve got a career...” he pauses before speaking in a quieter, yet no less hateful voice, “and then I’ve got this fucking thing that you gave me. This fucking thing that makes me destroy everything - even the things I care you. That’s why I hate you, you prick.” Snow is talking about the monster - the same monster that turned him violent against Krista. “I tried my best, Jason,” David says, his gruff voice shaking, “I couldn’t help the way I was. I just couldn’t... I couldn’t... I couldn’t help the way I was.” Snow doesn’t care for the excuse. He stalks toward his father and grabs the older man by the collar of his tight black t-shirt. David’s first instinct is to defend himself, but he’s able to hold down his own fiery temper for the sake of his son. “Come on you cocksucker!” Snow screams, shaking his father by the collar of his shirt, “Hit me! You know you want to hit me! Fucking do it! I dare you! Do it!” Had it been any other man - even his own father - there’s no doubt that David Snow would have lashed out in rage. If he were to strike his son right now, there’s no doubt that the younger, stronger, Jason Snow would win the battle that would ensue - but that’s not why David Snow holds back. “Come on!” Snow screams again. His eyes are closed now, desperately trying to hold back tears that he promised himself, as a teenager, he would never cry again, “Fucking hit me!” he shrieks at the top of his lungs, unable to hold back the tears any longer. They stream down his face wildly as he continues to shake his aging father. “I’m not going to hit you!” David allows his voice to break from its softness and into his usual low, gruff, tone, “I’m not going to hit you, Jason!” “Why the fuck not!?” Snow screams directly into his father’s face. Part of him thinks that David Snow won’t strike him because David knows that he can’t physically take his son anymore. Even in his prime when he was thought to be invincible, David probably wouldn’t have been able to take this bellowing lion - one with all the fiery rage that he, himself, knows only too well. Jason Snow knows that’s not the answer though - odds of defeat would never stop David Snow from engaging in combat. Like his son, David Snow was, and is, fearless. “Because I can’t!” David nearly screams, “I can’t...” Snow stops shaking his father for a moment and simply stares into the old man’s eyes - they’ve changed so much. Snow came to this house to hunt down the monster, but the monster no longer dwells her - it dwells solely in Jason Snow now. He releases his hold on his father’s black collar. He now understands that the monster didn’t start in his father - it was simply passed on to him. And now, it has been passed to Jason Snow. Coldly and silently, Snow turns away from his father and begins walking down the long driveway. There will be no forgiveness today - forgiveness for deeds of this magnitude can not be obtained in a single moment. As Snow reaches his car, he doesn’t look back to see if his father is still there. He now knows that the monster is his to battle. Last night’s suicide attempt was nothing more than selfish - an easy way out. What trauma would it cause if Krista had come home to find her brother-in-law lying on the floor with his brains splattered against the wall? He feels stupid for even considering it. But the monster is part of him now and he must accept that. Maybe all of this time it has been the monster that has made him such a great wrestler - not his athletic ability or drive to succeed. But the monster is taking over now, and Snow has to find a way to co-exist with it while protecting his family from it’s venomous claws...
