Mirror'd Intentions

26 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 26th, 2003 Isn’t it always to the point of mindless self discovery and chewing down on pieces of food do you actually think and repress about your actions? Sure, you can wipe blood on your sleeve, trudge back towards a neutral corner and then begin to take more punishment. And there, the melancholy of what have been pain, carnage and suffering come rolling together to mesh towards one giant whole. Agreed, society and its children is a bunch of screwed up in the head people, but its okay. But I stop right now, picking up the phone and looking over at the caller identification, tapping the phone base with a ballpoint pin, until about the fifth ring or so, I pick up. “Hello?” “Yeah, um Dante? Its Jason, right smack dab in the middle of Cuba where I’m surrounded by chicken shit, and revolution of trying to kill one of Violence Jack’s head ups.” I nod, “So how is it down there?” “To tell you the truth, it sucks!” Somehow, I knew Jason would have told me something like that. It’s been in his system for months now ever since being stationed in Cuba by me to get at Violence Jack in some form or fashion. I sent him to do the dirty work because nobody else would. Tracy can’t leave since the fWo is back in the United States once more. I couldn’t because of a contractual obligation to PCW, limiting my travel time in the process. So I sent him. And man is he going to lay into me when he gets back to the States. “Eh, that’s the point of it Jason, you’re trying to live out there in harsh conditions and then get back here and live under moderate conditions. You’re American in a shitty hole called Cuba. You should be worshiped like a God for Christ’s sakes.” Jason roared back, “Well GOD right now is being treated like a peasant!” I could have felt sorry for him; the prepubescent scent of Cubans around with wiry material and a short pocket could break a man’s spirit. It would have broken mine in a heartbeat. Brimming with success as if it were right now, the chances of owning fine China, woven silk, females above the bountiful, and gold strapped around me as if I were Egyptian. Subtract from that a obscure marriage to my High School sweetheart, a turmoil-ruing two jobs on both aspects of the rich and famous, and not to add towards the lease, a cultist with a social problem climbing upon my heels trying to catch me. Alas, not everything you hear about the wrestling business is all biscuits and gravy. “Its not all its cracked up to be Dante,” Jason told me in quiet and hushed undertones as if his words were becoming more and more under the free speech category. “Cuba isn’t a resort, and if PCW would tour here before Iraq, then it would be much worse.” “You’re fearing yet another bomb attack like the one in Tel-Aviv?” I could tell he was nodding over on the other end of the phone, lining up his Sony notebook, his phalanges clacking away upon the keyboard. He had something to turn in for the Times, all about his sentimental and frustration upon the open country. It was meant to be a classic piece of literature. Not some smoke back writings from a ravenous mad man who became this way thanks to savage natives. He felt cold on the opposite end, before whispering to him and then letting the words catch my ear. “Dear God, if I ever make it out of here, please let it be known that I would make it my wish to never sail again.” “Don’t let ‘em get to you Jase, you’ve got a strong mind and a huge over belly for pain. Take it like a man.” I sounded like an old caricature from one of those Westerns in the forties and fifties as Cunningham could only whisper back, “I’ll be back in touch Dante.” The phone became prone for a couple of seconds before latching it back on the base. I leaned back into my executive chair before there was another knock at the door hollow as it was light. The knob twisted suddenly, jerking in its own axis before the second knock arose my spirits. “Its open!” The right black denim pant leg was the first shown towards my eye as another came behind it. Looking up, Tracy leaned over the table, her buxom body as her attention grabber. “XXL magazine wanted to know if you were free for an interview?” “Tell them they can wait until I get my creative juices back.” “But,” her esteemed whiny voice bucked in, causing a tangible situation. “If you do nothing for the next month or so, your chances of winning any Source award, any Billboard award, any award period, go down the drain.” She had a slight point. “I still say I’m not doing anything.” She growled, trying to be as menacing as she possibly could but I wasn’t buying it. The chances of me losing focus at a time like this were way too risky if I shifted back everything towards whatever beef escalated inside of the rap game. It was becoming nostalgic, because nothing was pure anymore. My love for the game of rap was slowly fading away and wrestling was growing into a bigger and better spectrum. A small yet, obscure feature appeared on her face for what seemed like the first time in ages. A smile. I raised an awkward eye towards her since I couldn’t comprehend the action myself. It was all out of the blue, a obligation most wives would make. Yet none of them actually had the pleasure of having myself for a husband. She nodded to me, “Well, I’ll be at Turmoil. Sitting comfortably on the corner as you win that battle royal.” If the human body ever had its own red button, mine had just gotten pressed. My emotion at this moment was nothing more than complete seriousness, almost Garnett like as it were. I looked at her sternly, pressing my palms on the table before beginning to walk around the room, shallowly breathing. Moving pass her, I looked through our palatial estate with a butterfly feeling. “What the hell do you mean you’re being outside of the ring?” She folded her arms around her chest. I couldn’t back down from her at this point, there was nothing else to do since she had me in check, and you are never to argue with a woman. You’ll never win. I could only shudder in a bad moment. There was nothing here to incise the case that my wife was going to stay around my corner, around the ring, and into the waiting arms of anybody who would want to use some credit of psychologically hurting me. Great. “You can handle yourself right?” I walked through the living room, brushing a bruised right arm of mine past a split hallway. “I mean, a few karate kicks, a few timed ring slips?” She nodded, “Yeah, I think so.” “Okay, well you’ll need to watch to Sherri Martel Guide to Female Manager Heeldom 101 again.” She jerked me by the arm, pulling me in for a long embrace. “I know what I’m doing Dante.” I kissed her on the forehead. I hoped she did know what she was doing.
