From Virtue to Vice: Element 2 - Courage/Valor (Part 1)

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 9 of 2003 A.D. March 4, 2003 Nestled in darkness amongst the hills of Vermont, a singular light illuminated the interior of accursed Wyatt Manor through the western window of the uppermost story. Hideous chants rose and fell with the howling wind from the recesses of that monstrous edifice, their blasphemous syllables drifting through the dreaded hills and raising the maddening song of the whippoorwills in the pines. The deplorable hymn swept through the shunned northern wood as consort to the winds, stirring reports from the near south village of Brayston of grotesque nightmares haunting its residents en masse and formless shadows lurking the avenues of moonless night. The night of March 4th brought unrest to the small village, one of the final outposts of civilization before giving way to the legend-fringed wilderness long feared by locals. One man, local grocer Jude Lambert, went mad that evening as he lay slumbering beside his wife on Wicker Street. Upon awakening he exploded into fitful bursts of dire terror as he recounted visions of Cyclopean cities erected of black metal and amorphous masses gliding in the skies overhead. The most notable of his recollections was the final revelation hissed by the quivering merchant before his ultimate lapse into insanity, his account of a vast flailing demon stretching the range of the heavens and beyond in that his eyes couldn’t observe where its length terminated. Lambert was transported to Rutland for medical treatment when dawn arrived, and with him went three more of the village’s unfortunate denizens as they rambled of equally wicked images. The Brayston community was wrought with horror that day, as the identity of their notorious ‘neighbors’ hadn’t escaped their scrutiny. The Sect of Black Wisdom’s rituals weren’t unknown, but never before had their evil deeds and worship infiltrated the isolated hamlet from amidst the hills. It was an unprecedented breach by the foul theists into the society that ostracized their ways and gods, and the event cast the town of Brayston into alarm from daybreak until nightfall; none doubted the source of their neighbors’ collective madness after hearing the chants and mantras float from the woods that night. And certainly none doubted the madness of Bruce ‘Violence Jack’ Shanahan, the wicked high priest and instigator of the unspeakable faith. March 5, 2003 A moonlight vigil, organized by the First Church of Christ the King, formed a spiritual blockade against the evil that swept from the mountains the next evening. Again the blasphemous chants and hymns wafted down through the skeletal trees and forgotten trails into Brayston in the small hours, their blackest of intent countered by a congregation of men and women poised outside the sanctuary with candles and hymnbooks in hand to ward off the demons the song carried. From Wyatt Manor that cold night came hideous anthems praising various infernal deities, and the lot surrounding the church exuded repentant tones revering Jehovah God and his son the Messiah. Morning arrived and the nearly two-dozen exhausted worshippers finally relinquished their watch. A pious young woman among them, Janet Clement, insisted that she saw Bruce Shanahan, known leader of the black cult, lurking at the fringe of the woods while carefully surveying their vigilance from a trail just beyond the brush. Others in turn scanned the perimeter of the gnarled undergrowth, but her report went unconfirmed and deemed as the delusions of a fatigued mind. Contradictory reports had stated that Shanahan had departed the manor days ago to pursue his professional wrestling endeavors, but that again was an unofficial statement. Moments after the gathering broke reports flooded in of more nightmares and accounts of lingering madness amongst the residents. Lunacy pervaded the elderly amongst all else, and old Gladys Sutter had died of an abrupt heart attack moments after waking with screams of “Daoloth, the Render of the Veils!” and “Shub-Niggurath, that Black Goat and her spawn of the wood!” After hissing a series of hideous unintelligible syllables, Mrs. Sutter shuddered and her heart (mercifully, some would later say) gave out. Mrs. Sutter was never known for her knowledge of such dark things, and how one of her recognized ignorance and lack of education could spout such phrases perplexed those who knew her and especially those who witnessed the happening. Gossip spread that her face took on a joyous and even reverent expression as those final syllables were verbalized before her death. That account was shrugged off as hearsay and exaggeration. March 5th brought panic and outcry to the community, most of who were convinced Shanahan intended on expanding his spiritual territory through his unholy rites and that his efforts were directed at driving away the village of Brayston, if not worse. The First Church and town officials quelled the anxiety by reassuring the residents that Shanahan would be dealt with and told to restrict his activities in proximity to the village. A group of ‘delegates’ were appointed and sent to negotiate and speak with the high priest of the Sect of Black Wisdom, Bruce Shanahan, to suppress fear in the community and hopefully vanquish local sentiment of his diabolic intentions. The quartet of officials and businessmen, accepting their designated duty, ascended into the hills to meet with the wide-feared guru of the dark faith. Afternoon dwindled into twilight, and twilight became nightfall. Darkness encompassed the isolated village of Brayston, and the council hadn’t yet returned. Meeting again in the sparsely-lit lot of the community’s lone house of worship, outcry arose for state police to be alerted and patrols to be sent into the gloom-ridden wood to track down the party. Their pleas were again thwarted by optimistic town officials, and plans were made to wait in hope until morning before taking further, and more drastic, measures; Chet Cole, a war veteran counted among the initial ‘delegates’, was schooled in the arts of survival and thus afforded the missing group a glimmer of hope. The assembly abruptly broke with growled curses from the majority and embittered shouts of “Their blood is on your hands!” isolated among the more pessimistic of the crowd. The council, divided by dissension in opinion, opted to meet alone in the town hall. A faint trail of pitch-black smoke ascended into the sky from the blackened mansion, silhouetted against a waning blood-red moon. The townsfolk, caught up in the unmitigated fear spawned among their ranks, failed to notice the omen. Their conversation, tainted with panic and the presage of impending catastrophe, drowned out the inhuman howls that echoed from the summit of the mountain, the personal symphony of Bruce Shanahan; a symphony forged in a fathomless void beyond the range of conventional knowledge and mortal nightmare……
