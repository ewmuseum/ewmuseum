He has the Gift

18 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3-18-03 The morning sun glows brightly through a partially opened window in A.J. Trinidad’s downtown Chicago apartment reflecting off of a glass of water creating a prism effect on a pasty white wall. Trinidad sits comfortably at a round oak wooden table in his dining room, oddly focused on this glass of water. His glasses sit perched on the ridge of his nose, his midnight black hair is tied back to keep it from straying in his line of vision, interrupting his attentiveness set upon this lone glass of water, filled almost to the top of the glass. Standing next to him, more so perched over his shoulder stands a four-foot tall kangaroo. The kangaroo manages the same attention to the glass of water as Trinidad, if not to outdo him entirely in it’s concentration. The kangaroo, A.J.’s steadfast companion who he calls Crespo, wears a pair of over sized clear-frame glasses as well, obviously more for effect than actual usefulness, but never the less, both are fixated on this glass. A creak can faintly be heard in the background, the sound of a door opening very slowly, followed by the soft scuffling of footsteps taken by feet sliding across the floor rather than heel to toe walking. A beautiful, cherry red haired woman appears from around the corner and is immediately caught off guard by the scene she has awoken to. “Umm, are we taking up a new hobby this morning?” Amira sleepily questions whilst wiping some of the overnight deposit from her eyes. Getting no response from either A.J. or Crespo, Amira treads slowly towards them taking heed of the oddity of the scene laid before her before attempting to win over their attention again. “Hey! A.J.!” Amira yells, slamming both of her hands down on the table in between A.J. and Crespo. “What in god’s name are you two staring at that glass of water for?” “Whoa” A.J. mutters. “I almost had it that time, Crespo.” The kangaroo nods encouragingly as A.J. massages his temples with his index fingers before turning to Amira who is still caught in a state of awe. “What was that honey?” A.J. asked unsurely of Amira’s initial question. “The glass of water,” Amira says for what seems like the millionth time, “what are you doing staring at a glass of water at 6:30 in the morning?” “Oh, that’s easy,” A.J. responds, “I’m trying that trick from that book.” “What book?” Amira asks even more puzzled. “You don’t read.” “Over there.” A.J. motions to a book across the table by nodding his head in the general direction. Picking up the large book from the table Amira turns to A.J. and Crespo with by far the most desperate look for an explanation that one man and his kangaroo have ever received. “The Bible?” Questions Amira. “Yeah, I read it last night,” A.J. says while he starts to look back at the glass of water. “The whole thing?” Amira continues with her questioning. “You read the entire Bible last night? Didn’t you sleep?” “Who has time for sleep these days dear?” A.J. answers with a question of his own. “So much to learn, so little time. We took turns reading while the other one focused on the glass of water, but we can’t seem to make it work.” “Make what work?” Amira asks of A.J. as she thumbs through the Bible now taken up into her hands. “You know, that trick of turning water into wine,” A.J. states in a accusing tone as if everyone should be aware of this marvel. “You’ve been up all night turning water into wine? Laughs Amira. “Or should I say, trying to.” “Well, I thought I had it once. I took a break to go to the bathroom, when I got back, the glass was filled with wine. Turns out that Crespo just poured a glass to humor me.” A.J. shrugs. “I guess he was getting tired, or just playing an overly cruel joke. We have yet to figure that out, don’t we Crespo?” A.J. looks to Crespo with squinted tired eyes as the kangaroo simply turns away staring off into a plain, white wall, playing the innocent. “Are you really sure that pulling all-nighters, trying to recreate a fable from the Bible is the best way to prepare for you match with Seymore Smoke Tuesday night on Turmoil?” Amira asks of A.J. “Say what now about a what?” A.J. asks. “A fable, you know, a fictional story created to supply a moral at the end.” Amira explains. “I’m pretty sure that Jesus turned water into wine only in a story, I don’t think that’s true A.J.” “Can’t be fake,” claims A.J. “If it’s in the book, it’s got to be true, and we almost had it before we were interrupted. Both A.J. and Crespo look to Amira with a glare in their eye before she Pffts them off. “You do know you have a match this week right A.J.? I mean, after not being in a ring for almost a year now, save for your appearance at March Madness, which wasn’t really a debut, so to speak, I’d think you’d want to work off some of that infamous ring rust everyone always speaks of when they return.” Amira preaches in a motherly tone. “Who do I face on Tuesday?” questions A.J. absolutely impervious to what Amira had just said. “Seymore Smoke!” Amira states almost yelling at A.J. this time. “He’s a former champion in PCW, making his redebut against you as well on Turmoil! The man who eliminated you from the March Madness Rumble.” “But I also eliminated him” A.J. boasts staunchly. “You’re such a moron when you try to be funny.” Amira informs A.J., “and don’t you promote this childhood Crespo!” she scolds the kangaroo. Snapping back, holding his paws up in front of himself, Crespo again plays the innocent role before A.J. interjects, saving face for both of them. “Alright, you got me, I’ll take Crespo down to the gym and we’ll work out a little bit in the afternoon. Right after I get this water to become wine” “Christ, you’re hopeless,” Amira retorts. “I’m going back to bed. If you’d like to join me, I might be willing to make it worth your while….” With that said, Amira turns and saunters off back through the door from which she came, closing it softly behind her. “Well old friend,” A.J. says to Crespo, “Sometime we know when we’re defeated.” Before even ending his sentence A.J. is up and almost running towards the bedroom, leaving Crespo sitting in front of the glass, staring at the water, just as we found him when the morning began. All of a sudden a bright light permeates the room, Crespo’s eyes are forced closed as the light becomes increasingly more intense. In a flash, the entire room peaks to a blinding volume, before fading back into visibility. Crespo finds himself sitting in front of a red glass of wine. Astonishment crosses the kangaroo’s face as he peers over each shoulder, confused, as anyone would be by what just happened. Then, in a moment of pure faith, the kangaroo reaches out with his short arms, placing one paw on each side of the glass, he takes a sip from the cup. Pulling away smiling, he suddenly realizes that he is the only one in the room, A.J. will never believe that he has actually turned water to wine, so he drinks the entire cup until the last drop runs down his throat all in one effort. Turning from the table, the Kangaroo hops twice over to a futon, where he lays down his heavy head, and falls asleep.
