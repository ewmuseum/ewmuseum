A Wrench in the Gears: Episode 3 -- Disaster Strikes

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 27/28 “ARGH! Waiter! Why must this food be so sub-par?!” Shanahan lowered his head into his hands, cringing as the voice rang out across the dining hall. A deep sigh rumbled forth from his throat as his head wavered back and forth. “Bothersome insect, is he not?” Bruce twirled his fork through the pasta on his plate, the prongs gyrating and drawing the noodles in to wrap around. His last major meal of the evening and only about 24 hours until, what he had deemed, Judgment Day. “I wish the bastard would just have a heart attack and save me the trouble…” he replied silently in his mind, drowning out the incessant bantering his soon-to-be opponent produced from across the room. It was a blessing that the ‘brass’ had painstakingly made sure all of the next day’s competitors were seated as far as possible from their future adversaries. A preliminary Akiyama-Politically Incorrect conflict would undoubtedly be pretty vicious, and that arrogant Kannon being anywhere near Pharaoh would likewise cause some rather vast damage to the ship. “Alas I cannot arrange that. Not in my jurisdiction, mind you. The realm of death falls outside my sphere of influence, into the dominion of My Liege. My authority doesn’t extend beyond that of chaos and madness, unfortunately….” “And trickery, you ancient devil,” chimed in Shanahan, not bothering to withhold the detest he harbored for the cosmic fiend. The response was a meager chuckle that consumed Shanahan’s entire soul with rage. “Of course. Good to see you’ve studied the materials your affiliates provided you. I am but the Messenger of the Great Old Ones, the right hand subordinate to the divine Trinity of creation, vitality and time. They play the cards….I am merely the hand that holds them.” “You play as many hands as those vile blasphemies churning at the center of creation. You serve them to further your own ambition….they have no consciousness. They are incarnate instinct, and you manipulate them like the strong rape the weak.” “You impress me with every passing moment, mortal,” the echoing voice said in riposte. “To an extent what you say is true….in essence your race bores me. And seeing as the Trinity is content with allowing the cancer of mankind to continue to fester on the world that our kind once ruled, I see fit to take steps to alleviate that situation. But to rebel against my Masters….BAH! You think too much, mortal.” Just then, Bruce only scarcely detected a stifled blast erupt to the north. He lifted his head almost in unison with the rest of the dining hall, following the growing chain of muted explosions that minutely resembled that of hushed rumbles of thunder or partially silenced gunshots. Following the lead of the majority, he joined them in filing out of the room to investigate what was happening. His eyes widened as another spray of light dusted the sky, flooding the darkness in a mix of sapphire and jade. Already the sky was a veritable panorama of color, various shades of reds and whites and blues darting through the skies and painting the heavens radiantly, with the inherent beauty of some celestial-formed artistic gallery. These blazing colors emanated from a source just over the horizon, which also produced a constant accompaniment of muffled explosions to the fiery gouts of light smothering the cloudless atmosphere overhead. “Wow! Fireworks!” Shanahan shot a glance far left, centering his gaze on an awe-inspired trio watching the display. A striking brunette, garbed in a shimmering black and red strapless dress, admired the sky with a rugged young man in a semi-formal shirt and khakis on her arm. A blond-haired child rested cross-legged at their feet, eyes wide like dinner plates at the dazzling exhibition in the skies. Obviously a couple of rich yuppies with the money to afford one of the ‘first-class’ tickets across the ocean on the liner to rub elbows with the company gladiators, while dining elegantly with those bastards Nelson and the Booker (whom he presumed put aside their differences long enough to cater to the guests). More people had already stood on the deck as the display began, and more continued to stream forth to observe the PCW-sponsored festivities. Bruce realized it wouldn’t be a good idea to inform them the ‘fireworks’ weren’t PCW-sponsored. He could already visualize the dire consequences that would be generated by saying what it really was. Even if he candy-coated the truth a bit, it would be difficult to completely alter the events enough to not cause a tinge of panic. Speaking of which, he was thankfully the only one to see the approaching rowboat. Light reflected off the metallic coating of the one-man dinghy, and Shanahan could only barely discern the presence of a lone camouflage-donned inhabitant. Coming out of the darkness it avoided discovery and used the ‘lightshow’ as cover. Out of the public eye, it swept around to the starboard side and Bruce heard it bump against the hull. Hoping he was the only to hear it, he squeezed through the growing throng of onlookers and dashed toward it. Bruce leaned out over the railing, peering down into the darkness to see the weary figure of a soldier. They briefly met eyes, and Shanahan could whatever brought him to the Wrestle Vessel was urgent. Glancing around deck a moment, Bruce spied a tangled ladder of brown rope and yellow rungs and left the side just long enough to toss the ladder over the side. With a sigh of relief, the other’s blood-soaked hands clutched the rope ladder dangling overboard and did what he could to scale the apparatus as the Boston native drew it upward. A Marine, the soldier was brawny and thick and it took a vast part of Bruce’s strength to hoist the messenger board, veins and sinew taut and stretched across his rough skin as finally the soldier cleared the railing and fell onto deck. He leaned his head against the side, and for the first time Shanahan noticed he was wounded as a spreading blotch of red covered the white paint of the hull. Some coursed down in a thin stream to form a small pool, yet groggily he tried to pull himself up. Shanahan pressed his hands down on the young Marine’s shoulders to keep him seated. “You ain’t goin’ nowhere,” he breathed quietly before glancing around. “What the hell’s happened?” “S…sir,” sputtered the wounded soldier with a grimace. “It is not my authority to relinquish such inform…” “Did they send you to find someone?!” solicited Bruce. The other nodded. “Yes…yes, sir. Someone with….connections aboard this vessel.” “Bruce? Bruce Shanahan?” Another meager nod. “Well. ya found him. I’m Shanahan. Are you with Delta Fox? Captain Stryker’s unit? Tell me what the f*ck’s goin’ on over there!” Seeing as the other was uncertain, and rightly so Bruce considered, he fumbled in his back pocket a moment before pulling out his wallet and ID. He flashed the picture to the injured Marine, who nodded approval. His bloodshot eyes closed momentarily before opening again, gleaming with still-present sense of terror. His uniform was stained crimson on the left shoulder, his hair matted down as blood continued to trickle from his skull. “Ambush…..knew we were coming. Don’t know how, operation was……confidential.” Shanahan narrowed his eyes, allowing the wounded youth to go at his own pace, uninterrupted. He was no doctor but he had a nagging feeling the young man was low on time. It was hard to tell how long he had been losing blood…. “Sent men….in submersible. For recon, and bomb whatever was in that damn trench. Onboard cameras sent back…..pictures of what was down there. My God…..” Whatever he had seen caused the hardened Marine to tremble and quiver. The feeling was familiar to Shanahan and thus he felt more than just a shred of pity; the blasphemies and twisted nature of the Mythos were enough to drive even the most hard-edged over the edge of insanity. “How did the captain put it? Ah yeah….’a necropolis for the damned’ he said. A broad tube leading deeper, an abyss with pictures like Egyptian hieroglyphics all around the entrance. Steepled underwater towers, masonry defying human logic, far too complex for construction underwater. Monsters….demons…..Jesus, hundreds of them. All kinds. Reptilian…..with tentacles for arms, serpentine things from the depths of Hell. At least I don’t know….. anywhere else anything like that could have been born. Like….. a bad movie….and their eyes…..MY GOD!! THEIR EYES!!! His own eyes shot open in stark and unadulterated horror, his trembling reaching a point where Bruce felt compelled to place a hand on the other’s shoulder to calm him. He had also seen the eyes, the hideous eyes of all those misbegotten followers of the Great Old Ones. To see a veritable army of those spheres of abyssal red staring up from the deep was something he had never experienced, and he felt sorrow for anyone who did experience such an irreverence. “The submersible….Rivers, Mansfield, Gustavson….it never came up. The cameras….something enveloped the image. Something huge. Tore it to ribbons, shredded and twisted the steel like a cat’s claws rip through yarn. When it finally came up, it looked like….I don’t know, maybe a car Godzilla used for a chew toy. I can only imagine what happened to….” “Go on, then what?” pressed Shanahan, not wanting the other to think too much about it. God knew how stressful on his lunacy it was just to report this much. “They swarmed us. While we mobilized, brought the wreckage board….they came over the sides. Just think….if a man f*cked a monstrous reptile what it would produce. And you get….some idea of what attacked us by the dozens, the hundreds. Firefight…..sprayed in all directions. Some went down to friendly fire in the chaos. Others, ripped….. apart by those awful talons. Had to have went……through a thousand rounds in under two minutes. But for all those we killed, they just….kept coming. One fell, two more rose from those black, churning depths. Climbing the railings, wrenching open holes in the hulls just above….water level to get in. Raided the upper and lower decks. Casualties enormous, two of the fleet sunk. So many losses…..a disaster.” Bruce could hear Nyarlathotep’s vile laugh, sense the hellish rapture the deity enjoyed at the swan song of the countless units aboard those ships. As he thought about it, the Marine struggled to stand. “Where the f*ck ya goin?” asked Bruce, forcing the weakened Marine back to a sitting position. “Gotta go back…and help….” “Hell no, son! Not with a wound like that! There’s a radio in the navigation room, go call for help!” The Marine, his mind decrepit and body ravaged, shook his head before locking eyes forlornly with the bewildered thug. “No reinforcements. All we had….gone into this strike. They ordered it….” “Who the f*ck ordered it?!” demanded Shanahan, fury raging in his voice. Blood had begun to trickle from the mouth of the other, who casually leaned his head back to rest on the side. “Don’t know. Whoever commands the Delta Fox forces. Blackhold or something like that…. Dammit. Yet another blunder by the council. It had happened too much lately, but this was tenfold all the others combined. Shanahan was desperate, his mind racing as he thought of ways to save those who had grown to become his comrades. “For Christ sake, go into the control room and tell ‘em it’s a secret government mission gone wrong and you need to radio for help! A black op or somethin’! “I can’t…psychic vibrations scrambling the radios. We tried, God we tried….nothing worked. All frequencies cut off. By what, we don’t know….” What Bruce wouldn’t give for Rent-A-Hero to still be around, as goofy as that bastard was. He could venture several guesses as to the identity of the Mythos agent responsible for disrupting their communication. And in all cases, he severely doubted if that deranged, spandex-laden lunatic would do a bit of good. The first began with ‘C’… “Stay here,” he commanded to the Marine who had just now, unbeknownst to him, finally slipped into unconsciousness. If he would ever awaken he could not guess at this juncture, but if not then Shanahan believed it would a more merciful fate anyhow. He stalked to the control room, slipping through darkened rooms and up spiraling staircases, through murky shadows and incandescent lights. As he did so, that now-notorious ‘devil on his shoulder’ snapped back to life. “Pity about that young man, eh Bruce? A tragic death indeed, vain and futile. Now, I know where you’re headed, but in case it has slipped that greed-driven little memory of yours then recall we have a deal. You get what you want, I get what I want. You get the proverbial heads of Mason, Wild Wing and Aymin on poles, maybe I even help with Scorpion if you do your job well and I am feeling generous, and I get what I so desire…” “Deal’s off,” replied Bruce scathingly, not slowing down. “What you want is for the men aboard this ship to die, the women to bear the monstrous offspring of Great Cthulhu and Father Dagon. It contradicts our deal, fiend. You win, I lose. I’m pullin’ the plug.” Bruce hurled open the door to the navigation cabin, grateful to find the room abandoned in favor of auto-pilot while the navigation crew watched the ‘festivities’. As he seta bout switching dials and changing the ships’ predetermined course to avoid the carnage ahead, that insidious voice of a thousand tainted souls grasped his attention yet again. “You wretched fool….I do not like my will being challenged. Especially not by petty mortals of bastard ancestry. Rethink this or seal your own fate. If you do not, I hold no responsibility for what befalls your miserable soul.” Right now it didn’t matter. Too many lives were at stake, too many lost senselessly already. The consequences simply didn’t register in the one-track mind of Shanahan. Even if those over the horizon couldn’t be saved, at least the innocent could be. Someone needed to be a savior. And there was no one else around to do it.
