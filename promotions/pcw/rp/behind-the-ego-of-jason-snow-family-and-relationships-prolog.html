Behind the Ego of Jason Snow: Family and Relationships, Prologue

Date: not entered (OOC Note: The following RP's were originally written for RPG3. However, I feel that they are vital in the character development of Jason Snow, and therefore, I'm going to post them here as well.) It always rains on days like this - weather can be so punctual. It’s nearly nightfall, but Snow has no intentions of leaving the cemetery grounds. On this rainy, late Sunday afternoon, this is where he feels that he belongs - as if he’s indebted to sit on the rain soaked park bench until he can no longer stand it. Indebted to someone who never, nor would ever, be granted a proper funeral. He flew in to Saint John, NB, last night from Los Angeles, unsure of what to expect when he arrived. He knew that she wouldn’t be waiting for him at the airport, but it wasn’t until he got there that the realization of her absence struck hardest. The airport, itself, was as busy as he’d ever seen it, but somehow, it didn’t seem real. He wished it wasn’t real. He wished that he would jump up screaming and sweating from this nightmare, but no matter how much he tried to pry his subconscious eyelids apart, the nightmare still surrounded him - his unborn child had died. ... He walked through the airport searching for the parking lot where he’d left his car some three weeks ago, angry at himself for being confused by a place that two days ago he could navigate like his own home. Everything was so different two days before. “Can I help you, sir?” a young, off-duty, flight attendant asked. Snow knew that she recognized incoherent signals in him, but she passed him off as just another junky - they flew in from time to time. Snow looked at her through glazed, hollow, eyes. Normally he hated needing the assistance of strangers. “I’m looking for the parking lot,” he said in a voice quiet enough to hide the shaking. The young flight attendant eyed him over for a long moment, sure to stop and look straight into his floor-aimed eyes. “Downstairs, sir,” she replied, “You’re on the second floor.” Snow closed his eyes and let out a sigh, angry at himself. A moment later, he gave the young woman a quick nod, turned his back to her, and began walking. “The stairs are back there, sir,” she said, pointing behind her, before he had taken two steps. Snow stopped in his tracks, turned around, and silently made his way toward the staircase - still a bit unsure of where they might be, even in such a tiny airport. As he passed, the flight attendant grabbed his arm, “Are you ok, sir?” “I’m fine,” Snow answered. But he wasn’t fine. He was in agony. In his twenty-four years of existence, he had never felt anything quite like this, and the agony confused him. He had found that since his sister-in-law had broken the news to him, he became incoherent to the simplest of questions. Nothing made sense anymore - the flight attendant could see that. She no longer thought he was a junky - a mentally handicap man running free, maybe, but not a junky. Reluctantly, she released her grip on the left arm of Snow’s black leather jacket, and soon after he would find the stairs - and eventually the parking lot. ... His long blond hair is black with rain, as is his beard - he’s been sitting here all day and it seems that the rain has followed. His eyes never seem to blink - never seem to move. The blond beard that he’s been wearing since the second round of Wrestling God 3 is also black now, with tiny beads of water dripping from the individual whiskers. It wasn’t supposed to be this way. Things were supposed to be happy - everything was supposed to be ok. They had no trouble with their first child, Haley - none of these “complications” showed just how violent they can be. That’s how things were supposed to go with this second child, a boy. Plans change. Plans of people, plans of time, plans of God. Something changed the plans, somewhere along the line, leaving Jason Snow in this agony. But he can’t find that sole person, that sole energy, responsible. There’s no one to blame - no revenge to be chased. ... He wanted to blame the doctor. He showed up at the elderly man’s office, soon after driving home from the airport. His wife couldn’t take the explanation anymore - she’d already heard it and repeated so many times that it etched itself on the surface of her brain. But Snow needed the explanation from the doctor - he was supposed to know everything, right? The “Doctor” - the man who, by the power of his education and his tools, could save lives, repair severed limbs, and cut cancers out of the pits of anatomies. “I’m so sorry, Mr. Snow,” the elderly man offered his condolences as soon as Snow entered his small, private, office, “I know how hard this is on you.” “Do you?” Snow asked, wishing he could punctuate the words with a half-smirk - if only his face could remember what a smile looked like. The doctor, sensing hostility in Snow’s voice, removed his glasses and rubbed the bridge of his old nose, “Believe it or not, Mr. Snow, I do,” he began, “Maybe not in the sense that you know - but you and your wife aren’t the first couple who I’ve seen deal with this. And it’s never easy, not for anyone involved.” Snow knew the doctor was right, but didn’t want to accept it. He wanted to believe that he was the only man in the world ever to go through such emotional trauma. That way there was still hope that he could make it right - hope that there was still something, some last resort, that could set everything back on track. “You’re sure,” Snow started, trying hard to control his voice, “there’s nothing more you can do? You’re sure there’s no time left?” The doctor breathed an empathetic sigh, closed his eyes, and shakes his head. “You do realize that money is no object,” Snow said in a more forceful tone, leaning in toward the old, white-haired, doctor. “Money isn’t what your son needed,” said the old man, “Money had has nothing to do with it, Jason...” “Then what was it?” Snow pleaded, barely making any attempt to hide his shaking voice, “If something went wrong, then there must be a way - there must be someone out there who can fix it. You people do these things! You know these things! There’s no part of the human body that you can’t fix up these days! Every time I turn on the TV, I hear you guys talking about cloning, and stem-cell regeneration, and modern day medical breakthroughs! Well where the hell are these things now? There must be...” his voice trailed off as he realized how little control he had over it. Across the tiny office, the old doctor never stopped shaking his head. “The womb is a funny thing,” he said quietly, “and sometimes, horrible things can happen there. They can be caused by health problems, heredity, stress...” Stress? Snow knew that she had been stressed about his absence, asking him every day when he would return home. “Stress?” Snow choked. The doctor nodded, and then changed the subject, “I’m so sorry, Jason,” he said, and then tried to add more comfort, “But look, you’re young! Your wife’s young, and you’re both healthy. You can have children for years!” Snow’s hollow eyes filled for the first time since the news had been broken to him - filled with rage. He wanted nothing more than to strike the old doctor for comments that, for some reason or another, only increased the pain that Snow felt. “Nothing can be done?” Snow asked, as his eyes finally emptied of the rage - he knew that no blame belonged to the doctor. “I’m sorry,” said the doctor, repeating what seemed to be his favorite words. Snow nodded and walked out the door a moment before vomiting. ... It’s almost dark now, and still, Snow has not moved. Somewhere beneath that motionless body, a storm of emotions is ripping through every corner, creek, and gulley - it leaves nothing untouched. He constantly feels sick, unable to eat for fear of vomiting. He doesn’t notice the headaches anymore, because he’s constantly at thought - thinking of both what could have been and what never will be. He’s not handsome today. Somehow, drenched in the rain, with his hair and beard clinging to his skin, every fault in his exterior is noticeable. He looks average today, and years older than the twenty-four that he has lived. ... Krista, his sister-in-law, is only twenty years old. She greeted him with a tearful hug at his front door after he’d returned from the doctor’s office. One look at Snow and his emotionless exterior had set her to tears. She always had the ability to read her brother-in-law, even when she was a thirteen year old kid trying to set him and her sister up. She knew him better than either of them admitted, and was one of the only people in the world who knew how to comfort him. But with all of the cancerous emotions running through his body on that day, there was no room for comfort. Comfort was the dream - reality was the nightmare. She squeezed his hand gently and led him to a seat on the porch where they watched the morning sun rising. “How is she?” was all Snow could ask. “Sleeping,” Krista answered, “Your parents are coming tomorrow, and she’s looking forward to that.” Snow hadn’t talked to his parents since he and his father had a fiery argument years ago, before he and his wife became estranged the first time. Since then, his mother had called a few times, but Snow would always refuse to talk. His wife, a more caring individual, talked to Snow’s mother on a regular basis, but hadn’t had the opportunity to speak to her in person for years. It’s funny how tragedy is the flame that draws the most moths. “She didn’t look well this morning,” Snow said in a whisper. “It’s probably going to be a while before she gets back to normal,” Krista responded in a sigh, “this hasn’t been easy on her - mentally or physically.” Snow nodded. He didn’t expect his wife to be in the best of health, but seeing her lying in bed that morning, uncolored and unable to rise with a lifetime worth of tear-soaked tissues scattered about the room, was far worse than the miserable situation that he thought he’d find himself in. “Are you ok?” Krista’s soft voice interrupted his thoughts and brought him back to reality. “Yeah,” he lied, but she already knew the real answer anyway. How could he be ok? She squeezed his hand a little tighter, but got no response from him. “I’m so sorry, Jason,” she said, crying harder than ever. “Everyone’s sorry,” said Snow, “You’re sorry, I’m sorry, the doctor’s sorry...” He had a point, but lost it somewhere in his words. Krista understood, however, and nodded. “Are you going back to LA?” she asked in a shaking voice. Snow hadn’t thought about that. He’d left in such a hurry that he’d forgotten all about the tournament. Of course, there was still time to fly back in time for his fourth round match against Eli Flair, the tournament favorite. “I’m not sure,” he answered weakly. Krista nodded, giving no hints of what she felt would be appropriate for him to do. “Make sure you say goodbye to me,” she sighed - she already knew his answer anyway. There was nothing Snow could do here in Canada. On top of that, he avoided his parents like the plague. He would return to Los Angeles and meet Eli Flair - all that was left was for him to decide to do it. Krista grabbed his arm and the pair watched the sun climb the eastern sky... ... “You can’t stay here, son,” a voice snaps Snow into the present. Looking up, he sees the night watchman staring down at him, dressed in full rain gear. Somewhere behind the rain clouds, the sun has set over the western horizon. “I gotta lock the gate up,” he adds. Snow nods at the middle-aged man, but doesn’t move. A moment later, the man sighs and takes a seat next to Snow. “You got family here, kid?” he asks. Snow doesn’t know how to answer. There will be no headstone to mark the grave of his deceased child. There will be no place to visit, to leave flowers, to call his child’s final resting place. “I don’t know,” answers Snow, “I don’t know much of anything right now.” He takes a deep breath, looks up into the rain which has been camouflaging his tears for the day, and finally gets up to leave the cemetery.
