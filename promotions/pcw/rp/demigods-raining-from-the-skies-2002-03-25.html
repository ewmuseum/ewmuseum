...demigods raining from the skies...

25 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 25th March, 2002 Scrambled eggs sat on the dinner plate, the spiraling pattern outlined the outer rim while my fork digged deeper into each crevice of flapjacks and harpooning onto the prongs hooking them and then lifting the fork into my mouth and chewing off the flapjacks. This was the day to make or break my life. Picking up a newspaper, I could look deep trying to find the Scorpio horoscope for the month nad it happened to cross me at a moment, the look for which I didn't find anything humorous nor uplifting. 'During this next month, you shall find treacherous findings in your work, while creating something new for your life. There will be that one day this week that shall define you as a person.' My eyes started to twitch vividly as the tune struck me with a odd cord, just as I slept last night with the quizzical feeling at the pit of my stomach, I sensed it right then and there. The light from the window crept in and faded on my plate. Still the words of the horoscope touched me with a sinking feeling. Maybe I was wrong about taking the case. It was like writing on papyrus paper back in the times where Pharaohs dictated the Israelites until Moses let his people go from being a fraud of a prince. I shook the thought out of my head. As I started to walk out of the door, the manager at the diner called out to me, but I barely heard him, deep in concentration on the horoscope. 'Aren't ya payin' for this mack?' I left in complete silence, like a lone ranger clicking his studs along the wooden floors of the OK Carrel, ready for a gun battle with one of the villans of the plains, the dust kicking up behind my every step. A person like myself had a knack for imagination. Stepping through the television station doors, I held my head down low so nothing would run down through me in the single point of no intercourse of conversation with anyone and leave to myself, but first, I had to meet the reporter for whom I was filming outside of the illuminous City Hall building. Knocking on the door leading to the editor's office like I had done the day before, he didn't look surprised to see me. The good old Scotchman shook his head, scratched his dwindling fringe of ruddy fluff, and finally put his reluctance into words. 'Nervous aren't cha?' he told me as he laid down his smoking pipe. His gray beard twitch with a smirk on his face. I could only rellish in the fact of the moment that my fear, trying not to croak out some half hearted apology telling him to stick this straight up. Wondering when I said that, I could imagine his face turning plum colored red and socking me once in the face. Letting my eyes set upon his face, I chuckled nervously towards him before I sighed. 'Sir, I think I might be able to take this and build a career off of it.' He smiled to me and stuck out his hand which I took in earnest and placed in my palm coming up and down with his hand. A smile entered my face as he told me, 'Good decision kiddo, you'll go far in this business.' 'I know that, but what about some of the other things, you know the writing?' 'Writer?' he chuckled to himself, 'You want to be a writer?' 'Why not?'I asked him. 'It's not like I'm one of the more under persuasive writers on staff, every editorial written by one of our writers happens to be shot down with relative ease, and you need a strong, non-liberal like myself to change all of that.' He shook his head in disappointment towards it, 'No, nobody can be a triple threat, and you are just getting used to handling a camera. You think I trust you with a pen? Thanks for the idea, but no thank you.' 'I am sorry for that,' said I, trying to hide my disappointment. 'Of course if I am needed, there is an end of the matter. But the engagement was important and intimate. If I could be spared----' 'Well, I don't see that you can.' It was bitter, but I had to put the best face I could upon it. After all, it was my own fault, for I should have known by this time that a journalist or better yet, a feasible cameraman has no right to make plans of his own. 'Then I'll think no more of it,' said I with as much cheerfulness as I could assume at so short a notice. 'What was it that you wanted me to do?' 'Simple, go out with Eagan and cover the City Hall story, just like I told you yesterday. There's a professor, one of those scientific brain men who happen to have theories about the statuette resting atop City Hall.' 'You mean, I get to have to help interview....'I trailed off when a smile of reassurance grew over my face. 'The very one.' he replied. 'Professor Julius Hawthorne.' I cried 'Aye, and you and Eagan have to do a bang up job or we'll be laughing stocks!' he bellowed out, enough for the entire department to hear him, I couldn't help but smile at what I was about to do. Hawthorne's theories were second to none as he was nominated for a Nobel Prize for his thoughts on the matter that the physical boundaries of matter could be bended. I got up from the rest chair and left out of the office. I was coming out from the news editor's room, turning over my new mission in my mind, when I heard my name called from the waiting-room below. It was a mail room boy with a wire which had been forwarded from my lodgings at Streatham. The message was from the very man we had been discussing, and ran thus:-- Silous, 21, Abraham Street, Alexandria Virigina.- Bring Camera and Film - Eagan As I shook my head, I took onto my alighted path to Eagan's desk, and when I got there, it was a clutter of stacks of paper, a hot lamp which the light bulb fuse was almost out, and a mass of photographs. I saw a folded letter on his desk, the plain white Five Star(r) notebook paper with blue streaks along closed maragins had black ink inbetween a fourth of those lines. Unfolding the paper, I could tell what it said, 'Meet me at the steps of City Hall at 5:30 sharp so we can run the story on the Evening News.' - Eagan The bastard, I thought to myself and shot the paper ball like a jumper in basketball. It went flush straight into the trash can, as I mockingly acted like screaming fans and smiled. Stepping out of the doors, the sunlight cutting into my sight, I turned away to pay off my taxi, the driver of which was very cantankerous and abusive over his fare. As I came back to trying to search for Eagan, he was having a furious altercation with the men who had carried down the oxygen, his little white goat's beard jerking with indignation. One of the fellows called him, I remember, 'Gator loving hermies,: which so enraged his chauffeur that he bounded out of his seat to take the part of his insulted master, and it was all we could do to prevent a riot in the street. These little things may seem trivial to relate, and passed as mere incidents at the time. It is only now, as I look back, that I see their relation to the whole story which I have to unfold. The chauffeur must, as it seemed to me, have been a novice or else have lost his nerve in this disturbance, for he drove vilely on the way to the station. Twice we nearly had collisions with other equally erratic vehicles, and I remember remarking to Eagan that the standard of driving in Alexandria had very much declined. Once, the car brushed the very edge of a great crowd which was watching a fight at the corner of the streets of Lexington and Fordham. The people, who were much excited, raised cries of anger at the clumsy driving, and one fellow sprang upon the step and waved a stick above our heads. I pushed him off, but we were glad when we had got clear of them and safe out of the park. These little events, coming one after the other, left me very jangled in my nerves, and I could see from my companion's petulant manner that his own patience had got to a low ebb. But our good humour was restored when we saw Eagan waiting for us upon the platform, his tall, thin figure clad in a yellow tweed shooting-suit. His keen face, with those unforgettable eyes, so fierce and yet so humorous, flushed with pleasure at the sight of us. His ruddy hair was shot with grey, and the furrows upon his brow had been cut a little deeper by Time's chisel, but in all else he was the Daniel Eagan who had been nothing more than a mere acquantince towards me. 'Finally, you come!' he shouted out towards me as I stuck my head out of the car. He had a school boy Robert Kennedy smile on his face as the news van was right on the side of the left parking lot. The cab drove off as I slammed the door behind me. Shaking his hand, I gave my name towards him and we conversated over the simpler times in the business. A lit cigarette came from his mouth, the nicotine dripping away into the oxygen and light blue skies. He took a seat between the open doors of the news van. Picking up my right arm to look at my watch, it was 9:19 a few hours past sundown and the time of lights, camera, action. 'I must say, Eagan, that your manners do not seem to have improved since I last had the pleasure of meeting you,' said I severely. 'You lordlings are not accustomed to hear the truth,' Eagan answered with a bitter smile. 'It comes as a bit of a shock, does it not, when someone makes you realize that your title leaves you none the less a very ignorant man?' 'Upon my word, sir,' I said, very stern and rigid, 'if you were a younger man you would not dare to speak to me in so a idiotic a fashion.' Eagan thrust out his chin, with its little wagging tuft of goatee beard. 'I would have you know, sir, that, young or old, there has never been a time in my life when I was afraid to speak my mind to an ignorant coxcomb--yes, sir, an ignorant coxcomb, if you had as many titles as slaves could invent and fools could adopt.' For a moment my eyes blazed, and then, with a tremendous effort, I mastered my anger and leaned back in the van with arms folded and a bitter smile upon my face. To me all this was dreadful and deplorable. Like a wave, the memory of the past swept over me, the good comradeship, the happy, adventurous days--all that we had suffered and worked for and won. That it should have come to this--to insults and abuse! Suddenly Eagan was sobbing--sobbing in loud, gulping, uncontrollable sobs which refused to be concealed. My companions looked at me in surprise. I covered my face with my hands. 'You're are such a scared pussy,' The Professor said. 'Only pussies do that!' 'You're ill, old fellah, that's what's amiss with you,' I told Eagan jokingly. 'I thought you were queer from the first.' 'Yeah? Well f*ck you! Your habits, sir, have not mended in these three years,' said Eagan, shaking his head. 'I also did not fail to observe your strange manner the moment we met. You need not waste your sympathy, Ernie. These tears are purely alcoholic. The man has been drinking. By the way, Ernie, I called you a coxcomb just now, which was perhaps unduly severe. But the word reminds me of a small accomplishment, trivial but amusing, which I used to possess. You know me as the austere man of science. Can you believe that I once had a well-deserved reputation in several nurseries as a farmyard imitator? Perhaps I can help you to pass the time in a pleasant way. Would it amuse you to hear me crow like a cock?' 'No, sir,' I said setting him up for another joke, whom Eagan was still greatly offended, 'I'd like it more to see you on your knees sucking one.' 'My imitation of the clucking hen who had just laid an egg was also considered rather above the average. Might I venture?' 'No, sir, no--certainly not.' But in spite of this earnest prohibition, Eagan laid down his pipe and for the rest of our journey he entertained--or failed to entertain--us by a succession of bird and animal cries which seemed so absurd that my tears were suddenly changed into boisterous laughter, which must have become quite hysterical as I sat opposite this grave Professor and saw him--or rather heard him--in the character of the uproarious rooster or the puppy whose tail had been trodden upon. Once Lord John passed across his newspaper, upon the margin of which he had written in pencil, 'I hate being upstaged by punk b*tches!' No doubt it was very eccentric, and yet the performance struck me as extraordinarily clever and amusing. 'Time!' the Professor said behind us, a look of understanding on his face, before he turned his face upward into the dark sky. The glow of red seeped right into his face, and a groan of fear struck his lips. The angel had cometh for him, and in one drop upon the earth, his feet left a large quake along the gravity etch ground for a few seconds before calling out in a voice similar to The Metatron, 'You've been found guility of crimes against the Jade, and now I carry out thou sentence.' In instance, he lifted his stiff gray hand into the air before clutching the Professor by the throat. Wind exasperating from his lungs as the grip along the neck began to tighten, veins in his neck poping and his head becoming a melon red color. Eagan shivered in fear, a cowering stance on his part as my camera stared deeply into the scene viewed from my eyes. The gargoyle took his right hand and dived it right into the body, the thing for which God created for us and deep on his left side from my point of view and started scavenging for something, until he pulled from the body of the Professer and clutched onto it tightly. With an instance, he crushed the Professor's heart, as his slump body fell from the angel's hands, death. 'He's....being killing these people!' I shrieked out, while Eagan made off like the coward that he was but he didn't last long as the van sputtered towards a stop. Looking deep as he frantically tried to gun the van back to velocity, Eagan looked up fromthe steering wheel and then found the angel, starring deeply into his face. 'Oh- -' he trailed off, not even able to finish his sentence as the angel took his fist, and bawled it up. Taking his right arm back, he propelled it forward and ran it right through the glass, his palm going through first and crushing Eagan within his life, becoming a stain on the back of the van. As my camera continued filming it, his head turned from the direction of the now deceased Eagan to my camera as his feet shuffled together. Leaping into the air, he took great flight causing my eyes to look up in my head. A breathing gargoyle stood over my body, as the lens of my camera peering deep into his eyes. He only smiled, before taking my body into the air along with the camera and my body was launched into the front walls of City Hall. A thud falling close to a instant concussion fell against my head, throbbing in pain. My eyes were dilated in sight, couldn't concentrate one single bit as I could see him inching forward, his staff raised high into the air. I could feel a scorching heat coming along my body, as my Disturbed T-Shirt fell open to the sides and a crisp wind fell along my body. The blood flowed through my body, coming close to loss as I felt initals etching into my skin. F. A. I blacked out soon after, but the truth is known, and my prophecy came correct. How odd it comes along my body, and a flesh of pain. Sorrow.
