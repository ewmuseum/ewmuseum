Dorn Tracer vs. The Greek Gods: Part 2, Finale

7 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2 - 7 - 03 “What shall we do??” “I say we find him and put his head on a pike!” “Figures you’d say that…” The arguing has been going on for quite some time between Hera and a tall, muscular God wearing a Spartan-fashion helmet and carrying a long spear. Ever since a basket arrived with the head of their only remaining priest encased within, Mt. Olympus has been in a panicked uproar. Zeus, the head honcho himself, has done little to settle the quarreling Gods, however. He merely sits upon his huge, decorative throne, leaning his elbow on the arm and looking away from Hera and the armored God. “We must do something about the beast,” Roars the armored God, waving his spear in the air. “We cannot allow ruthless murder to run rampant within our grounds!” “I know this, Ares!” Screams Hera, retorting with fury pouring from her nagging voice. Zeus sighs and just shakes his head slowly, full of pity and remorse. Life hasn’t been great for the Thunder God, and now it just got worse. Since the murder of their only remaining priest, the gods and goddesses of Olympus will be turning to Zeus for answers. And frankly, he could care less about the entire thing. The bickering between Ares and Hera doesn’t help matters much either. “But we can’t go hunting for someone we know nothing about! What if we harm the wrong person?” Exclaims the Queen, trying for reason. Ares, however, isn’t willing to listen. “We must simply interrogate and execute all who are suspicious! DEATH TO THEM!” “We cannot do that!” Hera spits, getting rather frustrated. She grits her perfect teeth in pure irritation and tries to dismiss the thoughts of ringing Ares’ neck. “We must wait for him to make another move.” The God of War throws up his hands and shakes his helmeted head, making the large iron protection jerk from side to side. He does an about face towards the door and starts to stride away, mumbling quietly to himself. Hera just rolls her pretty eyes and turns back to her husband, who’s still just moping about… as usual. “What do you think we should do?” She inquires of the God of Thunder. “I know not,” Zeus replies, his voice dull and low. “Does it really matter? No one cares about us anyway. We’re deader than dead. Now CHRISTIANITY is all in their head.” “Oh quit being a baby!” Hera retorts, getting rather sick of Zeus’ crap. “I’m getting rather sick of your crap!” Zeus waves a dismissing hand and twists his head even farther away from her, trying to block her out as much as possible. Hera, sensing the obvious rejection, folds her arms across her chest tightly and rolls her eyes again. “It’s obvious you don’t about your own KINGDOM…” She mumbles, turning away from her pitiful husband. Things haven’t been so great for Mt. Olympus, and they’re going to get much worse quicker than Hera or Zeus or anyone thinks. For only a couple feet away from reaching the Olympus gates is the mysterious assailant known as Dorn Tracer. Climbing the rough slopes of the mystical mount, the Astronomer has but one goal in mind. The death of Zeus. Making the ascent with renewed vigor, Dorn gropes around for a foot hold and continues to make his way up the slope of the mountain, nearing the gate more every second. The great golden entrance to Olympus rises above everything else on the moment, like a glow titan over the land. Its shine burst through the clouds surrounding it and touches the face of every man, woman, and child down on Earth… those very people call it the Sun. And this is what angers the Astronomer. This gate, somehow or another, blocks out the true power of the Great Sun from touching the people of the Balkan Peninsula. He is to remedy that, by the decree of the great Goddess En’Flarim… Back on Olympus, though, they know not of their plight. They merely stand… or sit… about, wondering what to do about what they consider a minor threat. Ares has returned the throne room, more willing to listen to reason, but still up to speaking his suggestions. “Perhaps a trap?” Inquires Ares, not really WANTING that. “We could fool this assassin into our clutches and deal with him from there.” Hera just cocks an eyebrow and bites her upper lip, trying to decide. “I’m not sure. But I guess we have time, since he’s probably miles away…” Almost as if on cue, an explosion of mammoth proportion rocks the throne room, sending everyone to the floor including Zeus. Bits and pieces of the ceiling begin to fall in, making it rain marble. The sound of the explosion echoes throughout the throne room, like the angered growl of a huge animal. “WHAT WAS THAT?” Shrieks Hera, still belly first on the floor with her head covered. “ARES, GO CHECK IT OUT!” The God of War, face down on the marble, suddenly looks up and gives her a horrified gaze. “You’ve got to be kidding! There’s no way I’m going out there!” “YOU COWARD! GO!!” Roars the Queen again, her voice becoming hoarse from screaming like only a Goddess can. Zeus, face down in the marble, just lies there and lets the bits and pieces of his throne room pelt him in the back. He just wants to die… and he knows he’s going to. To put a fight against a force large enough to break through the gates of Olympus would be futile. It’s all over now… all over… “AAAAAAAAAAHHH!!!” All three Gods of Olympus lift their heads straight up and turn towards the door, only to nearly piss themselves at the sight of their elite guard flying through the door and landing on the floor back first. His body is shining with a hint of light blue covering his entirety. Icicles cover his arms and the rest of his extremities. Hera gasps in horror and turns away, unable to bring herself to look at one of her guards frozen to death. Ares squeals like a little girl and turns away as well, shivering with fear as though HE was coated in ice. Zeus inches his way over to the deceased, iced over guard and looks up at the door. “Uh oh…” The God of Thunder mumbles to himself. He can feel the incoming trouble… it’s so close… almost like it’s… “WELL, HELLO!” The Olympians turn towards the door once more, and instead of one of their guards, it’s a man in a long black leather trench coat and shades. His jet-black hair is tied behind his head in a ponytail. A cocky, sinister smile sits upon his face like a gremlin taunting the Olympians. Upon his shoulder, though… sits of all things… a rocket launcher! He comes striding into the room coolly, undaunted by the acts that have transpired. Hera is speechless, while Ares jumps to his feet, wielding his spear with fury. “YOU BASTARD! I WILL KILL YO-“ “Hold it right there, cock vacuum” The man spits, affected not by Ares’ threat. “If you wish to continue your pitiful existence in this place, you’ll sit down.” “Never!” Ares smacks his long tower shield with the head of his spear and charges forth at the man, with spear out-stretched. The man in the coat merely rolls his eyes beneath his shades and raises the rocket launcher to meet Ares’ path. He puts a shaded eye up to the aiming scope, and waits until Ares gets a little closer… BOOM!! The rocket launcher roars to life, deafening all but the man himself, and a bright flash of blue light comes blazing from the barrel. It forms into a ball upon leaving the launcher and goes crashing into Ares. The force of the blow takes him off his feet and sends him hurling high onto the marble wall. Upon impacting with the wall, his body begins to freeze, making it so the God is STUCK to the wall. He struggles with all his might against the force of the cold ice, but in the end, it’s a losing battle and the God of War is now a frozen Popsicle. Hera gasps in horror, her eyes bugging out of her head. Zeus just lays his head on the marble ground, ready for it all. “You fucking MONSTER!” Shrieks Hera, her voice high and shrill. “Why are you doing this?!” “Wouldn’t you like to know?” Sneers the assailant, lowering the rocket launcher. “Yes, I would!” The man shakes his head and continues giving the Olympians that smirk, loving every moment of their apparent torture. He sighs and nods his head, now feeling compelled to tell them of the tirade. “It’s all very simple, Hera,” The man exclaims. “First off, my name is Dorn Tracer, and I am an Astronomer.” “Astronomer? Doesn’t that mean you’re the follower of some small-scale religion bent on worshipping the stares?” Inquires Hera, with a bit of malice in her voice. Dorn growls angrily at her spiteful nature, but nods. “Yes, we do worship the stars. And it’s not a small-scale religion, we were just forced into recession by the likes of YOU and Christians! You murdered our people just because what they believed made more sense!” “You’re foolish!” The Queen cries, not believing a word of it. “We Olympians did no such thing! The people just had no faith in you!” “LIE! My people were forced into secret because the major religions feared the people would see the True Way of things! It’s only right,” Retorts Dorn, unrelenting in his divine verbal assault. “It’s unfair! It’s not right! And I’m here to set everyone on the right path!” He raises the rocket launcher and aims it at Hera, a look of sinister bloodlust in his eyes behind the dark shades. The Queen Goddess gasps in horror and attempts to crawl away, but Dorn sends a searing ball of ice her way with a roar from the rocket launcher. It smashes into her helpless body and sends her spiraling into the wall beside Ares. The expression on her face is forever frozen in horror. “Now, Zeus…” Dorn Tracer turns to the God of Thunder, who’s sitting up with a look of disdain on his face. “Shall we make a deal?” “What kind of deal…?” Asks Zeus, will no real enthusiasm. Dorn lowers the rocket launcher and stands it up on the floor, leaning on it with his elbow. “Despite being a dead religion, you must denounce the fact you ever existed and all the Tiamats to use Olympus as an Earth base,” Offers Dorn, despite the fact it’s a one-sided deal. “If you refuse, I’m afraid you’ll be a thunder-flavored Popsicle for the rest of existence. It’s your choice.” Zeus lowers his head and closes his eyes, knowing the choice he has to make. He doesn’t know if it’s the RIGHT decision, but he has to do it. He stands up, picks up his fallen lightning bolt off the ground, and slumps towards Dorn, who’s grinning ear to ear. “I know I’m going to forever regret this, but it’s the choice to make…” Zeus says, murmuring beneath his breath. He takes the lightning bolt and extends it to Dorn, offering it to him like it’s the key to Olympus. Dorn nods his head and reaches out for the lightning bolt. “Good Zeus, I knew you’d-“ Suddenly, the God of Thunder jerks the lightning bolt back and raises it in the air, a look of fury on his aged face. Dorn leaps away from his rocket launcher in surprise just as Zeus sends the bolt screaming towards the Astronomer. It goes crashing into the marble floor, exploding the material and creating a large crater. Zeus raises his arms over his head and yells with all his might. “OLYMPUS LIVES!!!!!!!!!!” Dorn, however, has regained his composure and leaps over the crater towards the rocket launcher. With his leg outstretched, his dropkicks the God of Thunder, sending him staggering back onto his throne. Dorn whips the rocket launcher off the ground and aims it at Zeus, stopping only to speak. “No… it doesn’t.” And with that, before the Sky God can react, The Astronomer sends a barrage of ice shots at the throne. They all go crashing right into Zeus, each one spreading a layer of ice over his body. The ice soon becomes so thick from the launcher shots that Zeus disappears within the icy prison. Tracer, exhausted from holding the raging weapon, drops the launcher on the floor then strides over to the block of ice. He puts his ear against it and knocks his knuckles against it, looking for signs of life. When none make themselves apparent, Dorn leans his back against the ice and lets out a low, sinister sounding laugh. “One down… many more to go…” [End.]
