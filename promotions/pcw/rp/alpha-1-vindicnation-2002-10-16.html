Date: 10.16.02 “Heh, good morning.” Morning, a theme in which I always had hated to hear as a kid, school always calling you back at 8:00 when you just hit that good dream. Now that I’m older, the saying means you get another chance to prove people wrong. “Wonder what the Billboard says.” That was the voice of a woman, whose voice was so silky, so smooth, I loved to hear it so. Its been nearly two months since I looked back into that standing list printed on every music website in the country. It told you whether or not you happened to make it in the business, and make it so that I was the very damn best in the business for nine consecutive weeks. Lying next to a burning candle, wax burning and dripping down into a canister as the smell of savannah filled my lungs. I wanted to lie here for eternity, but its not to be. As long as I am a player on this earth, rest will not keep me down. Not in a million years. As I slept, the warm tinged feel of warm water dripped against my back, her clear tipped fingernails running circles in a true spiral. A smile grew along my face, just about my teeth digging into my bottom lip. “Says that you’re #12, 91, 000 copies sold this week, bringing your twelve week total to 8.5 million copies sold.” She stated. “Better than Nelly’s debut.” I snicker, always knew I’d be better than that and well its been proven by fan bases. Quietly, she continues to bring it to my attention of how I’m good selling rap artist. The wax continued to drop, a clanking sound hitting the canister holding the droppings. The second album, there is your problem. Most second albums are considered flops or brought back to make the established star ever more popular. No one person can have five straight #1 albums, yet DMX is trying to go for it. It’s quite the accomplishment, but when you win a battle royal participated in by every single man and woman on the roster, its ten times better and a title on the line is making the pot even sweeter. The woman sighs, before whispering to my deaf ears. “Let’s see how fate plays with your mind, Dante…” (-| a l p h a |-) Title: Alpha :: Verse ISubtitle: Vindication Smoke was gathering in the room, the small war table. A single cackle could be heard, before many voices joined back into the room. The smoked dimmed, as a burning cigar happened to be put out quickly. Only a single row of men lined the oval shaped table. Each of them holding down a significant spot in the organization, figment of their lives since they could remember. The emblem illuminates in the very middle of the right wall, shining with a spiked border and crystal shaped lettering. One man sitting on the very end of the table leans back, stringing away on his pencil drawn mustache. His round, droopy eyes hang a bit, held up under small round bags of lost sleep. His small frame is only equal in mind power and ruthless aggression. Orlando Riley was once a small claims broker in New York, back when the city knew what it needed to generate money. Organized crime had been rampant since the 30s, and Riley had always dreamed of sometime becoming a big time gangster, pushing around stooges and shaking squealers. Whenever he wanted a piece of the action, his boss was always there to hold him back from getting a large head. He hated when the topic arose about him becoming a Mafioso, down right mugged him to the cold brim of his jacket cuffs. To the right of him, fiddling with his fingers as he continued to pat his baldhead for good luck, an Irish charm appears on his neck. The large, yet soft man doesn’t look the hard killer type when you stare into his blue piercing eyes. But don’t take him for granted, as those eyes may be added towards a growing list of charms. Timothy O’Rourke had been a main time hustler back in Ireland, happening to slang whatever product had hit the streets currently at the time. The headman in charge brought him over towards the states, to keep him away from Interpol, and Scottish Yard. Hounding him had been the difficult part, his wide trade of weapon mastery made it sure he was a hot commodity around the world. News pieces, magazine clippings, even a cover on the Ire Times. Big man spent a lot of money to bring him into the fold, and he planned to continue to add towards his lucky charms, one dead man at a time. Towards the inside of the two men lies an even larger man, the rest of them on the opposite side lawyers and brain men willing to put their lives on the line if they’re top dollar falls out. The main one, secluded to the far end of the table was the one emitting cigar smoke to puff out of his lips. The Mafioso boss loomed heavy in height, sharp green eyes and a knack for getting into the most devious, and incriminating schemes to ever hit the city of New York. Reno Enders had been mainly lowbrow, under the zestful eye of Maggio Leone`, and the Leone’s during the late eighties to early nineties. By the solemn death of his predecessor, Enders had rose to the occasion, taking under a few men under his wing, much as Leone` had done with him. One of them was Dante Jackson, a lone up and comer, trying to get his pockets a bit fatter, and his gun skills a bit. Once Jackson was forced into wrestling thanks to a dying breath from Ender’s grandfather, he reluctantly stepped into the ring. Since then, Enders has been cutting at Jackson, waiting for the correct moment to come back and show the puppet who the master is. These men happened to compose…The Enders Crime Family “Gentlemen,” Enders blew away Cuban cigar smoke into the sky, ever man at the table catching a whiff of it. His old frame made him look like the bulky Kingpin from Spiderman comics. He wore a white designer suit, hefty enough to suit the cold front hitting New York as we speak. “It is time that we got rid of the pesky problem known as “The Lyrical Messiah”. O’Rourke eyes lit up like a Christmas tree, thinking that he’d finally be able to put a bullet in the Messiah once and for all. “Boss, you’ve been gunning for this man for years.” One lawyer in an outburst told him. “You can’t seem to take that man down.” Another reported. “Hush, you can’t see the picture in front of you can ya?” he asked the both of them, puffing away while enjoying the taste of nicotine burying around in his mouth. “He’s lost his mind, that one part of his sanity is slipping by the minute.” “True.” The young hit man whispered, tugging on his necklace before raising a wily eye towards Orlando. “And you, scalp. You seriously thinking to get the boys on des one?” his accent growled as he shifted most of his body weight onto his shoulders, leaning onto the table. The small Riley kept quiet, tapping a pen along the wooden table to keep his mind away from O’Rourke’s scintillating comments made him keep away from the big man. Slowly, the restraints of fear began to unhook as Riley began to mutter, “You’ll still fail,” before chuckling reassured. “Why do you laugh?” the bodily movement from O’Rourke’s posture changed from looking at the yes men and giving the small man on his left a glaring eye. “And you find something funny, mick?” Orlando shot him a quick glaze, before turning into Reno. “When are we going to get this Jackson feller anyway?” “Soon,” Enders tipped a few dead ashes into the ashtray. “Very soon.” Quietly in short breaths, Riley looked back down at the newspaper folded against his lap. There bolded up in the headline it read, “MAN SEEKS VINDICATION”. He swallowed hard, before getting up from the table, wanting to exit the room. O’Rourke looked upward, sensing that the man walking away from the scene happened to irk him a bit. “Where’s ya going mick? Pussy out?” “Not that, just a bit of business came up.” He turned his back towards the hit man; placing on his burgundy leather coat hanging on a coat rack positioned a few inches from the nearest door. He began to whistle the tune of “Late Last Night” by The Strokes. Obviously, his very own sense of grip on pop culture hadn’t changed. As Riley shut the door behind him, Enders began to puff once more on his cigar, eying his prized possession on his very left. “O’Rourke, come by later. As for the rest of you boys and girls,” he got up from his chair and began to pace around, eyeing the New York Skyline. “Work is needed to be done, so you better be prepared for Thursday.” Enders shrouded the curtain, before placing his body back into the chair. He puffed more, before smiling to himself. To rebuild an empire, it takes a nation to bring it back. I rested my head back, leaning up against a weight room wall. Inside of here, small miniscule men continue to pump their muscles, wanting to be the next small heavyweight, or bodybuilder. I was here for one thing; just something to prove myself. Vindication. The last time I entered one of these small parties, something called a cluster-f*ck around the circle in PCW, I was eliminated early. Way before I could prove something to not only the people, but myself as well. The Gym is a figure of gaining that vindication. For many, this place is a place where you happen to notice things about yourself, the full sense of whether you gain or lose strength. It’s somewhat of a mirage, your actual ability. Call it a hunch or whatever, but it may be another trial period for me to go through. “Dante?” A voice calls out towards me, as the crunches begin to take more of a toil along my abs. Inside my head, the rheumatic thumping of Nas, “The Lost Tapes”, to be exact continue to make sure that I keep pace with myself. Slowly, I can see a figure hover over me, somewhat of a reminder to my mind. Bishop. Now, Malik Bishop was the man I’d known since childhood, basically my brother if my mother and father decided to have another child back when I was only pre-years old. He was muscular, about 6’3” and two hundred sixteen pounds. It may be New York, or something that brings my past back, but the bald headed player, as he called himself in high school was back. “I thought I killed you, and Enders.” “Can’t keep a good man down can you?” He wore a Muslim Wave Cap, fitted along his baldhead to go along with a leather black jacket and a white sweater underneath. His small-rimmed glasses lay bridged on his nose. Bishop resembled Tupac in a way. He had that rebellious spirit, brought out after a swift change in his heart. I brought it out. “So, wonderin’ how I made it?” he asks me where I strain back, living more upward efore falling back halfway, and then lifting back up once more. “Not really,” I told him in a gasp. “Just wonderin’ why are you coming to me? What happened to that friend of yours, Enders?”“Oh, he’s still alive.” I stopped in mid-point, before letting my eyes catch a look of my former best friend. The cold shaky feeling in his eyes, told me whether he was lying or not. This time however, he was dead serious. “Damn.” It was all I could mutter. Bishop shifted his ground, lowering into a squat position and his left knee hitting the carpet. “After the car flipped over, he broke a few bones, punctured his lung, and needed a blood transfusion. You didn’t hear the phone calls from the hospital?” “You knew about them!” Bishop chuckled, “Well yeah, I mean I was right there.” “Ugh,” I sighed before pushing my body up from along the wall. “No need to tell me if you were giving him sponge baths or not.” He shifted over, coming in front of my path. I looked down at him, figuring him to be nothing more than a pushover now. Back when I was a runt in Brooklyn, I was scrawny about 6’0” 150 pounds of food stacked upon food. Now, it’s different. Glaring back, he took a shot. And missed. Oh, you want your style back? Don’t ya? Next to me, I can see a horde of people standing around the both of us, as I swing and he ducks backwards wanting to take my hand and convert it so that he can get the advantage. In a way, he may be bigger, stronger, but I’m smarter. You…rrragh! I duck back, roaring with all my might as I throw a decent punch, catching him in the stomach. He doubles over, looking as if he was going to puke right in front of me. He had given me confidence with that stupid look on his face. Soon then, his fist became clinched once more and then he swung. Owwww! I shrieked in obvious pain, nursing a probable knot on my face from the blow as I reeled back. He smirked, knowing he had the full advantage over me. He threw another punch; I ducked this time before he kicked me in the gut. I doubled over in pain, a teary eye starting to flow. He continues to laugh, and joked at me. Look at him cry, look at the bitch cry! With a tear in my eye, I couldn’t let the people down and there’s no way in the world I’m losing a fight. I’ve lost once before, nursed a knot and a bruise on my face. Bloody was my bottom lip. Now it was time to end this. Big boy, fuck you. One last punch was thrown and he fell back, snapping his head against the pavement. Small pieces of pebbles crash and crack under his frame. I smile, tipping my blood up with my index finger. Turning away would have been that odd sense. The song, “One Last Breath” by Creed blasts through my head, bringing me back to my main perceptive. “So what are you thinking of now?” The thing I’m thinking of? Revenge.
