The peril of night. Part 5

21 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 21/05/02 They both stood on opposed sides, good versus the forces of evil. Darkness against light and individuality against dictatorship personified they locked glares, each as deadly as the other, but the glare of one would soon only be a memory and the glorification of the other banished their very existence. As if frozen within the glacier of battle they watched one another, their every moves monitored by their opponent, both observant, for one mistake and the other would surely triumph in what would be a monumental battle. “Shall we begin?” Asked Anna in a now far more sincere tone than before, showing the true grit that she held and what better time than this to show her true powers and colours, as when her life was on the line, like many others she would fight in the only way she knew and do as much as she was capable of to keep her soul fro m being snatched away by the dark force that now faced her. As she says this, the figure simply draws one hand before her and moves its fingers, as if to invite her to take the first blow. She simply scoffs at the cocky attitude of this and readies for the battle that is imminent. With the invitation Anna launches her first assault upon this being, she darts toward the taller and larger black figure, with her arms like pistons pumping toward the chest of the fiend. First with her left and then with her right she flailed, yet she did it with such a skill that she hit the being as it simply stood there and took the blows. With Anna’s last punch landing a hit to the jaw of the being it leapt back, as if it were a dog scolded, with a grunt it came darting back toward her like a mad dog. As the being kicked and wailed at her like a banshee, she simply attempted to dodge the seemingly perfectly placed blows, she managed to avoid most, although with what seemed like a speed so quick that it made it invisible to her eyes, the being slid behind her and delivered a fist to her lower back. Anna screamed in total agony, as she slumped onto one knee the being then walked before her and spoke once again. “Surrender yet?” Said the creature, as its unforgiving eyes loomed above her in shadow and the haze of perspiration that bled from her skin and trickled into her eyes. She growled in a violent manner and then leapt to her feet. “Not by a long shot, I am not afraid of you!” The consonant prose trickled from her tongue, as if it were liquid gold, gliding with beauty down a soft ivory slide. However, the words echoed from the jagged rocks, like the jagged lies, which they were. For she knew, as well as he that they were such lies and that such lies could not be hidden under a voice even with the sharpness of hers and her emotive covering. Still she could not hide what she felt and he could see it, he could smell the fear within the vapour of her breath and taste it from the perspiration that she wept. Showing true fighting spirit still and living the lies that she spoke, Anna drew her dueling knives from her pocket, as she did the diamond edged silver blades danced like the manic grins on the face of insanity across the crimson red haze of the night. Her hair now danced in the fury of the wind and her movements, like a warlord readying for a great battle. The figure smirked at her, with a hint of admiration for such bravery. “Such brave souls shall make great additions to my collection.” Said the figure, in a somewhat confident tone. Despite Anna’s desire to win, she knew that the being was not false in sensing victory and she knew, as did he that the fight would last for little longer and that soon he would have succeeded in taking her soul away. However, like the true warrior she had become, Anna simply shrugged off her falsifying conscience and readied to continue the battle, which she had started and intended to finish. With a deafening scream, she dived forth back into the fray that raged like a forest fire, tearing through life and shedding blood on the field of battle. As the beast seemed to conjure a staff of energy from thin air, the white pole-like beam seemed to set within his sweaty palms, as the fight raged on. Anna backed away slowly, each step caused a grating of her heel against the trembling earth, with the blink of an eye she fell. Almost as if the wheel of time itself slowed, she plummeted with terrifyingly slow speed to the ground, her hands cut as she slammed into the heap of glass beneath her. A shriek tore through the air, like a heated blade through ice. Wasting no time to ending her existence, the fiend slashed wildly with the rod, and as it plunged toward her head and hope seemed forlorn, a light shone through from the heavens and gave her the strength. With newfound hope and a glorious lunge of desperation, from her pockets she drew her dueling fork and plunged the silver, glistening blade into the pole, deflecting the blow. With the fiend in shock, Anna turned the tables with a swift and agile sweep of her left leg, flooring the creature and halting the fight, as it slammed head first into a pot hole several meters from her. Anna clambered to her feet, not showing the fatigue that loomed over her like a virus, she tried not to show it, but it seemed to ooze from her body like the white flag of surrender, that she knew would soon be raised high. As she stood there valiantly, now drawing her second dagger to continue the skirmished brawl, she gulped as the ground began to quake and the beast began to moan and writhe on the floor, as if in a state of self-induced agony. She simply stood desperately lunging to take in the oxygen around her, in what little time she had left, she wanted to appreciate more and more as the sand fell through the glass of time, which would sound the fanfare of her demise. However, as Anna stood she began to realize that this demise was more real than she thought. Never before had she seen anything like it. The beast grew fur and the claws of a behemoth. It’s body instantaneously tripled in size, with the ground now creaking and cracking beneath the colossal weight. Never before had she tasted the essence of fear within the air, but now all scents were snatched away by the blustering wind, all except for her reception of a smell called terror. The perspiration of this seemingly endless war against fate froze upon her face, as she now realized that fate had won and she had lost.
