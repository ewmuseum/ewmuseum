The Other Way Around

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 26.11.02 (#2 FF) “It’s only a dream…..only a dream…..” Abner finds himself awake, uncomfortably sprawled out in a pool of sweat. The damp white sheets of the hotel bed are no longer a comfort to him. With the back of his hand, Abner reaches up and attempts to rub the sweat out of his eyes to no avail. The salty liquid burns into his already aching eyes, and Abner’s become id fed up with it all. He’s fed up with his discomfort both physically, at the moment, and at his life in general. The hick feels alone and out of his league in such a strange and bizarre place. A Holiday Inn, and, well, the city in general, isn’t his preferred environment. The rough, black-blistering texture of the hardened surface of his old bed was more comfortable, he thought to himself. Back at the Detention 29 facility in Vermont, he felt almost at home….. Abner’s stomach is tight, his muscles tense, and his nerves aware of even the slightest of motions: evidently, he’s having a physical and mental breakdown. And what is it caused by? Fear. Fear, honest, terrifying fear, is induced into his system via this awful dream. But not just any dream, a nightmare….. As the unsettled Abner lies his head back down onto the comfort of his pillow, his eyes, though heavy, refuse to close. It’s as if he’s forced to hold a thousand tons of pain on his face, for what seems like an eternity. But what’s the use in trying to fight it? That only seems to make it worse. And besides, he can’t seem to win anyway. So slowly, ever so slowly, Abner slides back down beneath the covers, and in thoughtlessly clearing his mind, somehow the hick manages to drift back to sleep…... …..And the dream starts again….. The bell rings. The two opponents circle each other; round and round they go, as death lingers in the air. Few words can be used to describe this blatant madness. Competition turned mad; corrupted souls: these two dangerous elements have combined to bring these men on a one-way trip to…..fate. And yet, they brought it upon themselves, and of their own free will, and for that, no one feels bad for them now. But out of guilt, Abner anyway, feels bad for himself. Guilty that he had let his hickish pride push him this forward, forward into something that at heart he didn’t really, in his own words, “Give a horse’s dick” about. Cornelius Greyhend, “Corn” on the other hand feels nothing…..no will, no intention, no cause, but only to do what he “has to do”…..and for him, that’s the end of it. But there’s something different here. You see, in real life, it wasn’t…..it wasn’t quite like this. No, not by a long-shot. You see, when it really happened, there were spectators. Tens…..dozens…..no, close to a hundred of them: a hundred members of Third Party lined up in four distinct rows, and on all sides of the “ring”. A hundred drunken, enlightened, and “drunk-lightened” (as Abner called them) lined up to watch a “good fight”. But here, there was no one standing, cheering, eagerly awaited the match to begin. And in real life, the results would end up having a huge implication on the lives of these people: and the results would be final. But no, here it was different. Here there were no spectators, no human qualities of any type……no signs of life. You see, here, in the depths of hell, there was only darkness; darkness and nothing more. In the “real” hell there are no flames; it’s all just a simple fairytale. Anything giving off light, almost a holy symbol of life, is nonexistent in this void of darkness and eternal suffering…..or something like that. Anyway….. And so it began the same way: darkness. You see, in real life, just like in the dream now, when the “bell” actually rung (or in this case, when the gunshot from the hick’s shotgun rang out), things really did go black. And so now the two were completely parallel…..well, in a physical sense that is. But in this dream, as compared to when it really happened, the emotional pain is worse. When the fight between Abner and Corn had actually occurred, Abner had no idea about what was to come (both physically and legally)….and if he did, he certainly wouldn’t have fought in that match. But at the time, Abner was optimistic, overconfident, and even a little cocky. He was the best wrestler (well brawler that is) in the club at that time, and so, was inevitably favored greatly over his so-called “pacifist” opponent. But who cares about the odds anyway? You know what I mean? In Super Bowl 36, the Rams had the Pats beat, did they not? Yeah, right. Sometimes the numbers really do lie…..Well anyway, this is how it was with Abner. When the fight started, just after the bell rang, and just before taking his first shot at Corn, everything went black. His whole body momentarily went into an unnatural stasis. “Shit!” And what was wrong? Poison. Someone had poisoned Abner. Evidently, he was now under the influence of an (at least temporarily) blinding substance. And as a consequence, Cornelius of course had his way with Abner. His body was simply laid out for the picking. But worst of all, Corn took it a slow. He inflicted Abner with a number of calculated “maneuvers”. His favorite: a slow, painful death. Instead of laying it all in at once, Corn took it slowly. And slowly, piece by piece, punch by punch, and kick by kick, Abner was bring ripped apart. The pain in those few moments, the swarm of screaming nerves that swam down his spine and through his aching body, was unheard of. It was undoubtedly unparallel to any other physical, or even mental, single feeling or emotion that Abad ever felt in his whole life……even including the incidents that occurred during his stay as a “guest” of D-29…… By the matches end, a gruesome nine minutes later, Abner was in lying in shambles. By the time his vision would have came back to him, it proved useless, for he had passed out well before that time. And when he finally came to, senses and all, he wished he was still blind, because the sight, as you can imagine, was horrendous. The club was destroyed. The club was physically, literally in the process of being destroyed. The clubhouse of Third Party was now engulfed in a whirlwind of flames. And worst of all, no one was in sight. But somehow, just like in every other incident in his life, Abner had made it out. But this time, Abner really was lucky to have escaped alive. Crawling through the dust and smoke of the burning shed, he barely managed to reach the doorway. Luckily for Abner, the doorway had already collapsed in flames, and besides for a few empty cans of beer, his path to freedom was unblocked. So he barely escaped in time, and turned just to in time get one last look at the flaming pile of wood as the “clubhouse” began to cave in. And then, it finally it did…… And up until this moment, that was last thing Abner experienced when he was a “free”man. You see, the next morning he woke up in a hospital bed, and he was not alone. Two young law officers were peering over his bed….. He was charged with arson, attempted-manslaughter and was fined tens of thousands of dollars, as he was held responsible for the destruction of private property. Curiously enough, though convicted, Abner never really was responsible for any form of “manslaughter”. He obviously wasn’t capable of pulling off such a feat after the end of that particular match! But no matter, his punishment was just, for he had gotten away with a great many deal of things before. But one regret that Abner had, one that he never really got over, was the fact that he never really found out exactly what had happened. You see, he never found out who started the fire, how they went about setting it, and whether it was intentional or not. And the truth be known, Abner never even knew the man who he allegedly tried to kill (some guy named “Jim”…..Jim something or other….)…..But evidently, this was the guy who owned the shed…..the shed that Third Party decided to “barrow”….. And so, that’s how Abner got to meet his friends at Detention 29, a federal-class state prison, and, ironically enough, where he learned to fight. And then, with a flicker of life, Abner’s eyes came open. With a nervous shake, Abner rose out of bed and tried to catch his breath. Though he only reached the beginning of the dream, that’s all that ever happened anyway. Things would just go like that, and it would continue on like that for what seemed like hours. But now, finally, it was over. Or was it? There was still unfinished business. Cornelius Greyhend. Corn would have to pay for his mistake..... Corn had caused a harvest load of trouble….. After Abner’s four-year stay at D-29, neither sight nor sound of any friend, family or foe had crossed Abner’s sensory path. Now, at almost 23, Abner was alone. And he had Corn to thank for it all. All that Abner can remember that was connected to either Third Party or Corn at his stay, was a rumor in D-29; one that spread like wild fire over the entire facility. Apparently, a member of Third Party, now an official criminal organization, had come forward and confessed his crimes in order to receive protection…..a week later, he turned up missing. Not good, Abner thought. Most folks claim that Corn, still around, had complete control of the Party, and under his “guidance”, no none, no one except for Abner that is, ever left it…..ever….. But things were different now, Abner’s vengeful spirit was still rotting away where he left it: in some prison, stuck out in the middle of nowhere. He was a changed man now, a new man. And revenge was not on his mind, If they crossed pass, yes, Abner would certainly give Corn a piece of his mind (and then some!), but Abner wouldn’t go looking for trouble……No, for now anyway, just a new start at life will leave him content….. With that, Abner got out of bed and prepared to check out of his room. A road to wrestling superstardom was in his path, and Abner was all go. PCW tryouts would be held soon, and he had more than a fair chance at succeeding…. So exiting the room, Abner turns back and looks at the sweat-stains of the one-time clean hotel bed, and thought to himself, “War is hell…..or is it the other way around?”
