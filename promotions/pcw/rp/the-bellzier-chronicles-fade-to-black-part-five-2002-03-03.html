The Bellzier Chronicles: Fade to Black [Part Five]

3 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 3, 2002 GoodLuck SupermarketOakland, CA Doan nodded slowly, the memory returning to her. “Ah yes, I do remember that. As I recall, Tom and his other friends ran immediately away from the store. The only difference was that the other boys came back.” “I was scared,” Tom said quickly. He found the strength to somehow look Doan straight in the eye, unwavering now. “I think I was so young that I didn’t know how to deal with the accident. I should have stayed and apologized to you. I’m sorry. If there’s anything I can do to help pay for a new one…” Doan laughed, which surprised Tom. In the few years that he had known her as a child, he had never seen her do anything but scowl and yell. Her laughter, however, seemed as golden as the morning sun. He had to allow himself to smile. Doan looked over at Mai, still chuckling. “It’s been over fifteen years, and now he wants to pay for the aquarium? My, what valor. What about all of those white rabbit candies Nyoc would give you and the boys for behaving in the store? You must have eaten at least a hundred of them.” “I’d be sure to include interest with the payment,” Tom replied, a bit of a snicker in his voice. Doan and Mai laughed, Tom as well, knowing that his charm might be able to get him out of another jam once more. “So, the young man has finally revealed himself,” Doan said, now crossing her arms. “What have you been up to all of these years while you were hiding away?” “I went to school, got a job…I live in San Francisco now actually…” “San Francisco?” She said with a bit of surprise. “Your parents told me you lived far away, but San Francisco is quite a ways from here, isn’t it? Isn’t it hard to see your parents?” “It gets busy around the household sometimes,” Tom said, “so I don’t always get a chance to come visit my parents. Plus, I’m always on the road traveling…” “Traveling? Traveling where?” Tom was a bit hesitant to explain his occupation to yet another person. Although he loved his line of work, just telling people that he was a professional wrestler seemed a bit comical to him. It was not a profession that was looked upon as a respectable vocation, unless you were a 13 – 17 year old male that happened to be a huge “The Rock” or “Triple H” fan. Professional wrestling was a phenomenon, but it was by far the most outrageous sport in the world. “Tom’s a professional wrestler,” Mai said, not allowing Tom to respond for himself. Tom looked over at Mai for a moment, wanting to just walk out of the door right now. He wanted to somehow break the news gently, but now it was out in the air, and it sounded absolutely ridiculous. “A wrestler?” Doan asked confused. “What is that?” Mai smiled over at Tom, who had an amused look on his face. He was actually glad that she didn’t know what wrestling was, because people who did often had preconceived prejudices about the sport. Unfortunately for Tom, he had the unenviable job of trying to explain his profession to her. Mai must have seen how uncomfortable Tom looked at preparing to make his explanation, so she stepped in and explained it for him. She told her all about the physical aspects of wrestling and how they traveled all over the country and the world, performing to crowds, all for the pursuit of being a champion. When the explanation was over, Doan looked at Tom with a different expression now. An expression of surprise. “So, that is a wrestler…” “That would be me.” “I remember seeing a show about this wrestling,” Doan replied. “I saw big men, fighting and talking very loudly…is that you?” “I do fight, but I try not to take myself too seriously when I talk,” Tom said. “The talking aspect doesn’t quite fit me very well. From what I’ve experienced thus far in my career, it’s nothing more than an argument who is better by words. I’d rather prove my excellence in the ring. Whenever I talk, it’s usually just jokes or making fun of my opponent.” Tom remembered times when he’d actually catch wrestlers in the backstage areas rehearsing what they would say to the crowd during their promos. They’d put on the meanest face they could and would stare into the mirror, puffing themselves up to be the baddest person they could possibly be. Tom would try to keep a straight face while he was in the locker room; he would gather up his belongings, tie up his boots, and head out as quickly as possible. Once he exited the locker room, he could do nothing but burst into laughter. He knew that the mad man that the crowd saw spluttering words around and talking trash would not be the same man that would step into the ring and fight the good fight. It was all a front – only Tom didn’t try to fool the fans. Why insult their intelligence? That was when he decided that he would go for a pure comedy approach and instead of being the ultra serious personality that a lot of the guys in the wrestling world had. “Do you enjoy fighting?” she asked. He paused, trying to analyze the question. At first it seemed like a moral question, but then he realized what the woman had been through. She had a first hand account of the Vietnam war; to see someone fighting for a living probably seemed like the most ridiculous vocation to her, considering she witnessed people fighting for their lives. Tom replied, after a bit of hesitation, “I don’t enjoy it. It’s something that I’ve grown accustomed to, and it’s my job. I can honestly say that I’ve developed a passion for wrestling.” It was an honest enough response – one that could warrant many other questions, but it answered the most pertinent. “Then why do you do it?” Silence… Tom blinked for a moment, not quite sure how he should answer. “I’m not sure. I grew up watching them on television…wrestlers, that is. They were superstars, men above men…I suppose I wanted to be like them. I never really thought I’d become one of them…I was content on just watching it on television.” “But now you wrestle…for money.” “I do.” Tom stated. He wanted to say more; he wanted to tell her how gratifying it made him to entertain so many people and how it boosted his own confidence, but he knew it would have meant nothing to her. She would only be concerned with his health and how it kept him away from his family – that’s all that mattered. Tom agreed to a degree, but his career was his career. Nothing would ever be able to change that. “I don’t know,” Doan said, looking at Tom now as if he had made the biggest mistake in his life. “I don’t see why you are in wrestling.” Tom shrugged. It was a gesture that was often a sign of defeat, but Tom didn’t care anymore. He didn’t feel like justifying himself to her any longer, and he highly doubted that he would be able to change her mind about anything. “I suppose it was destiny…” Mai watched her mother make a face – a face that signaled resignation. Perhaps she had been trying to sway Tom away from his chosen career – if so, she didn’t make much of an effort. She began to walk away, heading back for the table to check receipts, Tom reasoned. He sighed to himself, deciding that the formalities to end the conversation had basically been thrown out of the window and it would end as abruptly as it had begun. Tom walked away from the table, heading for the back area where he knew there was a door up to his aunt’s apartment, above the store. As he walked past the cashiering table, he was stopped by Mai who had an apologetic look on her face. Tom had a good feeling what she was going to say. “Tom! Wait!” He turned around, a bit unwilling to discuss any matters further. “What is it, Mai?” “Look, I know my mother was being unfair to you…” “No, she has a right to her opinion,” Tom replied, shrugging once more. “I have to respect that. If she doesn’t approve with what I do with my life, then she can think that way.” “She disrespected you, and you’re insulted,” Mai said. “I can see it in your face.” “My face? Are you a mind reader now, Mai? My face is not showing anything. I just don’t feel like justifying my life to everyone, that’s all.” Mai looked a bit hurt at Tom’s harsh response. Tom knew that she just wanted to apologize, but he didn’t have time to accept anything. He needed to speak to his aunt, and he had been side tracked. Breathe… Tom closed his eyes, taking a deep breath. Suddenly his mind was swirling, and he realizing that he was becoming angry…a bad sign. When he became angry, his mind was disillusioned and his judgment was clouded. There was no need to snap at Mai like this… “Look…Mai, I’m sorry…” But Mai was no longer listening. She was slowly walking back to the cashiering table, where her mother had gone back to counting receipts. Tom paused for a moment, realizing that he might have cut short a new friendship. His anger had once again caused him to be short sighted. Tom just hoped that this one mistake wouldn’t come back to haunt him. Tom turned around, heading back to his course for his aunt’s apartment. He walked past the herbs section, which had all sorts of asian medicines sitting on the store shelves, promising relief to countless numbers of ailments. As he walked past the shelves, he noticed that he was quickly coming upon the deli section of the store. This was the area that prepared all of the fresh meats and fish that were put out for display during the day. The smell of cold cuts and meat was awfully strong in the area, and Tom could not help and stop at the sight. That was when he spotted a man cleaning up the chop block where they prepared all of the selections for packaging. He wore a white apron, which was now stained a dark red from the day’s work. He was placing old meats in the trash when Tom approached him. “Mr. Cai! Attention!” The man looked up suddenly, then a broad smile came upon his face. He patted Tom on the arm, replying, “Tom! What are you doing in here? How are you?” Tom looked over at his shoulder, which was now stained with blood. He wiped it away, wiping some of it back onto Mr. Cai. “Too many questions, so little time, Mr. Cai.” He smirked, apparently appreciating the humor. “I guess we could begin with ‘What are you doing here.’ And how many times do I have to tell you, call me Terrence.” Terrence Cai – one of Tom’s greatest mentors. He was there when Tom had first decided that he wanted to be a wrestler, and was there to help him train for some of his first matches. Terrence was an expert when it came to self-defense and the art of karate. Yes, it was a stereotypical story of a person of asian descent who was proficient at the martial arts, but his talent was nothing to be joked about. One issue that should have been clear was that he was by no means a fighter. One phrase that was often coined by those involved with martial arts is that it was never used for offense, only defense. It was an important aspect of Tom’s training that he often neglected – sometimes a good offense equated into a good defense, although Tom preferred the latter. “I’d call you Master Cai, if you wouldn’t berate me every single time that I did,” Tom replied, finding a chair to sit on. “I’m not the master of anything but myself,” Cai replied. “You of all people should have realized that by now. I was the first person to explain that to you.” Cai grabbed a towel, slowly cleaning off his hands. “So, what are you up to nowadays? Last time I heard from you, you were teaming up with some guy in tag team…” “Has it been that long?” Cai nodded. “You used to call me every week, updating me on your progress. Now I’m lucky to even get a postcard.” Tom felt a bit guilty inside, biting his lower lip. “I hadn’t realized how much time had passed since we had last spoken. So much has changed since I last talked to you. That partner ended up being one of the greatest moves of my career. It really spurned on more success.” “It brings a tear to my eye when I realize how creative you’ve become,” Cai said, taking a seat alongside with Tom. “Reminds me of the times you’d be creative with your kicks and punches as well.” Tom shook his head. “You’re not going to bring this up again, are you?” “I suppose I don’t have to…” “…Thank you…” Tom was extremely embarrassed of some of his first workouts with Cai, in which he basically pretended like he knew how to throw an effective punch and kick. He had basically shown what he learned from watching movies and television, and it showed immediately. Cai would never let him forget the everlasting embarrassment. “Confidence is a good ally…overconfidence can be an invisible foe.” Tom lowered his head, as the quiet scolding hurt him a bit. “I understand that. I just think that we have a great chance to come out victorious.” “I’m sure you do. You’re very talented, Tom. But never forget – as good as you are, there will always be someone better. Always.” “I don’t think I need to be reminded of that,” Tom said, smiling at his own misfortune. “But it was good to see you again, Cai. We’ll have to keep in touch more often.” “Please do,” Cai replied. “Where are you headed off to now?” “I want to see my aunt actually. Is she home?” “I believe so,” Cai responded, nodding his head. “When you go up there, tell her I have her dinner ready.” “No problem.” Tom headed away from the deli, stepping into the back door which led up a flight of stairs into the apartments above. As Tom stared up the spiral of stairs, he remembered times when he would run up and down this same flight, tumbling around and having fun. The racket he made would often prompt his aunt to scold him for his insolence. Those were the days.
