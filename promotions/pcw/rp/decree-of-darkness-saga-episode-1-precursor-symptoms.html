Decree of Darkness Saga: Episode 1 – Precursor Symptoms

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 23 of 2002 A.D. (Excerpt from the memoirs of Bruce ‘Violence Jack’ Shanahan; dated August 25, 2002, days removed from VJ’s last appearance in PCW and final entry before his institutionalization) This burden has become too much to bear. With the loss of my Extreme title, all I can see is the overwhelming darkness as it bears down on my disgraced carcass….for with that fatal blow to Flaymin Aymin, a carcass is all I am inside and disgrace is all I embody. A shattered, decrepit husk of a once proud soldier of the slums who has degenerated into a downtrodden and mere silhouette of former glory. Even now I can hear the mirthful cackle of that loathsome Nyarlathotep as he reigns from atop his pungent throne amidst the plane of Kadath, ripe with glee at my in-ring breakdown and the shower of criticism that has showered down in the aftermath like a stinging rain. The industry’s most outspoken critics and my most supreme detractors have withheld an array of condemnation in recent weeks; my success has prompted such action and I knew they were virtually bursting at the seams to unleash the critique and the mocking assessment they passed down in the newsletters and columns. Their hatred for me is high, and with my every victory comes the obligated tongue-in-cheek compliment from their ranks; though it originates, undoubtedly, from forked tongues. They all know of my nature, my revulsion toward their elite social circles. And they detest me just like I detest them. Fucking know-it-all smarks…. Existence has become empty. There is little to hope for, little to look forward to. Life is a charade; it offers the merciful escape that is death, then mockingly draws it just outside your straining grasp. I have become the Reaper’s court jester. There is little else holding them back now. I can feel them spiraling from the depths of space, wrenching away at my soul and clawing at my sanity. Oh that Nyarlathotep! Even now I hear his hellish voice radiate through the abyss, whispering abominations into my ears. God, I just want to cut my ears off and spare myself that accursed horror! I want to tear out my fucking eyes and not have to stare into those hideous gulfs where the darkness lurks, shut out the images channeled to me through the powers of that incarnate evil Crawling Chaos! The blasphemous obelisks and the unnatural terrors bowed in profane worship surrounding them while chanting in alien tongues beneath skies of most uncanny and spectral hues, the monstrous fiends perched atop blackened thrones as they feast on the blood-soaked flesh of unnamable sacrifices…..God just it end! Lord have mercy and dispatch the angel of death, expel the wickedness and….. Wait, there’s something at the window…..a mellow and steady tapping beyond the drapes. A light breeze has begun to wisp through the drawn-up window through the screen, carrying a foul and putrid stench like that of death. I’m going to pull up the drapes, and face whatever lies beyond. I have little other choice…… (NOTE: Handwriting becomes nearly incoherent until the end of the entry, obviously signaling the madness setting in on the mind of Shanahan) A throbbing, probing eyeball blazing with the flames of Hell’s deepest depths glares through the glass, a twitching pupil enclosed by an iris of pale and sickly yellow. It leaks with noxious pus that trickles down the cadaverous cheek and jaw line to spill into a curled maw, which appears to be drawn up in a feeble grin. They’ve sent their vile minions across the continuum with the intent of destroying their greatest threats. The prophecies are coming to pass, the Cleansing is near at hand. That grin from beyond the glass…..that wicked, malicious grin…… (Account of the events of September 1, 2002 from the statements of Doctor Houghton and various hospital records) “I am not insane! Goddammit I’m not mad! They did this to me! They were the ones speakin’ through my body, it’s all part of their plan! Can’t you hear me?! I’m not insane!! Beyond the padded door and the shatter-proof glass, shrieking madly and squirming fiercely as if in the clutches of some imperceptible demonic talons, lurked the shirking mass of a stocky cult-market celebrity; Bruce Shanahan, known otherwise as ‘Violence Jack’, was locked in the throes of delirium. In the adjoining administrative office of Greensburgh Sanitarium, peering at a green-tinted monitor glowing with the unsightly image of the crazed Boston resident, gawked two members of the perplexed psychiatry team of Primetime Championship Wrestling’s medical staff. Dr. Samsanov Okoye, donned in a tweed coat and slacks, adjusted his glasses before shaking his head befuddled. “We diagnosed Mr. Shanahan several months ago, just after his signing onto Primetime Championship Wrestling. I can’t believe he’s back, I thought it was a fleeting phase….” His associate narrowed his eyes momentarily, crossing arms about his chest. “What was fleeting, Sam?” Okoye exhaled deeply, reaching for a stack of files propped up by an arched ebony paperweight and metallic thermos. He thumbed briefly through the manilla folders and drew out one scrawled across the upper tab with SHANAHAN, BRUCE. He shoved the flimsy object toward his colleague, turning down the audio of the monitor to mute the frenzied screams of Shanahan. As he spoke, Doctor Houghton skimmed through the contents of the file. “A few months back Mr. Shanahan was diagnosed as being afflicted with paranoid schizophrenia…..delusions of persecution and acute night terrors, terrible visions so frightening and realistic that the effects had a dire effect on his daily routine, his occupation and his career. At the time he was a virtual unknown in professional wrestling beyond a few regional companies, and had surprisingly advanced in the 2nd Global Title Tournament to a match with Drake Maxwell…..one of PCW’s ‘golden boys’ at the time. The management decided Mr. Shanahan had talent and wanted him in tip-top shape to give Maxwell a good contest, and to give the fans a quality showing before he fell to the heralded PCW superstar….” Houghton’s eyes darted quickly over the scrawled handwriting of the affidavit, though he was more focused on the words of his senior colleague than the forms themselves. “We gave Mr. Shanahan a prescription to deal with the illness.…and he went on to defeat Drake Maxwell in what is still considered a monumental upset. Eventually Bruce did lose, and after that occurred he was offered a contract to PCW. Again, our services came into play.” “And so he was cured for those months?” “Alas, no. In truth, the medicine we prescribed was ineffective. It did nothing to quell the night terrors or delusions disturbing Mr. Shanahan and he claimed it only bolstered the effects of the dreams, made them more vivid and dynamic. Thankfully he somehow repressed any urges for months, until this recent breakdown. The illness is not recurring…..it simply never went away.” “What about this diagnosis of intermittent explosive disorder?” asked Houghton, pointing a scrawled and nearly illegible mark made near the bottom of the page. “Was this treated?” Okoye grinned widely, exposing sparkling canines. “We were instructed not to treat that particular illness. The office staff told us that part of Shanahan’s success was attributed to his rage, and that suppressing that fury would only be detrimental to their investment.” “So Mr. Shanahan has suffered through his anger because of PCW’s money worries?!” exclaimed Houghton in shock. “It’s not our place to make that decision…..at least not when we’re being paid to overlook it. I did what I was paid to do, I treated the illness in question and was instructed that treating the other present disorder would be of potential harm not only to the funds of our employer but to the career of the patient.” “And you didn’t tell the patient, didn’t ask his opinion?” demanded Houghton in shock. “You simply took for granted that he valued a paycheck more than his own sanity and emotional stability?!” Okoye stalked away with nary a glance at his accuser. He stuffed his hands into his broad pockets. “Please send the guards to Mr. Shanahan’s cell, Doctor Houghton,” instructed Okoye flatly. “I’m going to speak to him about this latest breakdown.” The heavy metal door clanged shut in his wake, the younger associate glaring in anger at the elder Okoye. * * * The doctor adjusted his wire-rim glasses, looking down on the pallid, sweat-drenched visage of Bruce Shanahan as he shuddered in the confines of the straitjacket enveloping his brawny mass. His breathing came in erratic, desperate gasps and his eyes imparted a begging plea for salvation that he couldn’t utter through words in his present state. Okoye felt pity for Shanahan, regardless of the crude wrath the Boston native had shown the doctor in the past. “I’m sorry, Bruce,” soothes the doctor, setting a hand on the shuddering patient’s shoulder. “We tried….I did what I could…..” Bruce was almost catatonic with dread, unresponsive to stimuli from the doctor as he directed a thread-like beam of light into the idle Shanahan’s eyes. Try as he could, he could elicit no response from the quaking PCW prospect and let out a long, exasperated sigh before turning to leave. He cast one final doleful glance at the disoriented and silent ring warrior before ordering the door closed and bolted by the guards. “I’m sorry, Bruce. I really am…..”
