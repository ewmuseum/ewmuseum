Voyage to the Gold: Part I

2 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 2, 2002 Certain streets have an atmosphere of their own, a sort of universal fame and the particular affection of their citizens. One of such streets is the street that Jack Valentine & Johnny Heart live on, and the jest: 'If anyone else had a Heartbreaker on their street it would be a little slice of heaven' is the jocular expression of the municipal pride. However, it is not the street that they live on that they are concerned with. It is the street that leads to the unknown, the street that leads to a championship. The street leading to the unknown will be revealed in due time. This scene opens up back on the street that Jack Valentine & Johnny Heart live on…Wacker Drive in Chicago, Illinois. There was a part of it where one could see as many as five big cafes in a resplendent row. That evening The Heartbreakers strolled into one of them. It was by no means full. It looked deserted, in fact, festal and over-lighted, but cheerful. The wonderful street was distinctly cold, especially at this time of night. They were both feeling anxious, yet empty without any hint of recognition or reward. They went in and sat down A gay bar across the street was closing. Everybody, high and low, was anxious to have the last fling. Companies of masks with linked arms and whooping like red Indians swept the streets in crazy rushes while gusts of cold mistral swayed the lights as far as the eye could reach. There was a touch of bedlam in all this. Jack Valentine & Johnny Heart looked at the people across the street in disgust, although they themselves are accused of being homosexual. Perhaps it was that which made them feel empty, since they were not in any other way in harmony with the bedlam element of life. They hated the very people they were accused to be. But they was not sad. They were merely in a state of sobriety. They had just returned from a heart-felt battle in a Cincinnati, against 19 other participants. Their eyes were still full of distress, the memory of their experiences, lawful and lawless, which had their charm and their thrill; for they had startled them a little and had amused them considerably. But they had left them untouched. They did not succeed in their mission. They planned to walk out, one of them Extreme champion. They did not work as a team, and although Jack Valentine almost made in all the way, they were not happy. Indeed that match was another men's adventure, not theirs. Except for a little habit of responsibility which they had acquired they had not matured them. They were as young as before. Inconceivably young---still beautifully unthinking---infinitely receptive. One may believe that they were not thinking of Miyagawa and Syko and their fight to rise once again in the tag team ranks. Why should they? You don't want to think of things which you meet every day in the rumors and news. They don’t want to hear about a tag team that people are saying are ten times better than them. All they care about is their title shot at Supremacy. They had even been approached by several magazines that have requested their beautiful faces for a cover shot. But they were not interested. Apparently, they didn‘t even care about their looks right now...if you could believe that. All they wanted was gold, pure and true…the tag team championship. On the front page of the illustrated paper they saw lying on a table near them, they looked picturesque enough, seated on a red, white and blue float, two big strong men with a look of politically greatness to them---and all around him a landscape of red white and blue streamers. They caught The Heartbreakers eye on that spiritedly composed scene. It was these very men who possessed the small pieces of gold that represented so much to The Heartbreakers. However, just then some of the men from outside invaded the café, dancing hand in hand in a single file led by a burly man with a cardboard nose. He gambolled in wildly and behind him twenty others perhaps, mostly wearing pink and purple, holding each other by the hand and winding in and out between the chairs and tables, to the disgust of Jack and Johnny. They were people of the openly-gay sort, wearing costumes and scantily disgusting clothing. Coming in behind them there was a girl in a black dress sewn over with gold half moons, very high in the neck and very short in the skirt. Most of the gay clients of the cafe didn't even look up from their gay games and excitement. However, Jack and Johnny, probably the only straight men in there gazed right at the girl. The girl costumed, wore a small black velvet mask. What made her daintiness join that obviously gay lot they couldn’t imagine. Her uncovered mouth and chin suggested refined prettiness. The girl seemed to distract Jack and Johnny’s minds from the gold for a mere second before they finally realized that it was the gold they should be worrying about, not the girl. In the back of both of their minds though, they were thinking about that black dress. She filed past their table; noticing perhaps their fixed gaze and throwing her body forward out of the wriggling gay-chain shot out at them a slender tongue like a pink dart. They were not prepared for this, not even to the extent of an appreciative before she wriggled and hopped away. But having been thus distinguished, they could do no less than follow her with their eyes to the door where the gay-chain of hands being broken all the costumes were trying to get out at once. Two large gentlemen then entered and sat next to the woman. She gave them each a kiss to the disgust of Jack and Johnny. The men glanced over at Jack and Johnny and noticed them staring at their table. They stood up and stormed over to Jack and Johnny. “What the hell are you looking at faggots,” one of the large men said. Jack and Johnny both stood up, wearing their pink clothing and still looking peculiarly like two of the gay bar partiers. “Do you know who you’re talking to?” Johnny said, hovering over both of the other men. “Looks like we’re talking to a couple of queers who think they can waltz in here and start hitting on straight men,” the other large man said. “Queers? You’re calling US queers?!?” Jack responded. “Take a look in the mirror,” one of the men said. “That pink clothing, the pretty boy haircuts…you guys have to be gay.” “Well, here’s a newsflash for you, we’re not GAY!” Johnny screamed. “And it’s all about THIS PINK!” Jack shortly shouted thereafter. The four men began brawling in the bar. The gays were in the back screaming like little girls in fear and the woman was trying to break this all up. Jack and Johnny, being professionals, got the best of the two instigators, however their spirits were broken. If they could let something as miniscule as a little name calling distract them from thinking about the match, then how on Earth would they have a chance against Politically Incorrect, let alone Syko and Miyagawa?
