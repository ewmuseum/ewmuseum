Not Another PCW Promo

2 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/02/02 Somewhere... stuff happens.... The End . .. … …. ….. What? Oh, that’s not a good RP? Oh, it’s not an RP. What do you want from me? Porno? I don’t have any, talk to Aymin. Oh, a promo. Why? Ah, hell, you know I don’t like to do those things. Fine. Fine! FINE! The camera fades, showing a white brick wall. The room is empty and cold, almost as a hospital in its sterility. The End stands leaning against the wall, with his arms crossed and head aimed to the floor. He is in his standard ring attire. Interviewing The End is Cal Seaver in his brown three-piece suit; he looks like a child standing next to the tall, well-toned PCW star. Cal Seaver:'The End, you're facing Stormbringer at PCW’s April pay-per-view, Spring Fling. Thoughts?' The End does not speak at first. His head does not move, but his voice level compensates. 'It is quite odd,' The End begins, 'that someone such as myself, who has been consistently winning matches since March Madness, would be paired up in Spring Fling with a man who has consistently lost matches since March Madness. Look at our records. I’ve really only been booked on maybe one card per week, but then look whom I have defeated… Darkstar, Clyde, The Rush, Chet King…” Seaver interrupts The End’s break in speech. The End still does not turn from the floor. Cal Seaver:“Quite an impressive list of victories.” 'Yes…” The End replies in less of a statement than an aside. “Now look at Stormbringer’s record… he’s lost to Clyde, Badge… he couldn’t even finish his match with Charlie Loc. And he went to a no-contest with the recently fired Drake Maxwell.” Cal Seaver:“Didn’t you interfere in that match?” The End commands Cal Seaver to, “Shut up.” “Why can’t we,” The End begins, turning his head away from Cal Seaver, “take it as tournament rules. Clyde beat Stormbringer; I beat Clyde. Can’t we take it on faith that I would beat Stormbringer, and then allow me to get on with my career, and do something meaningful like contest a freaking title?” A shrug from Seaver’s shoulders shows his lack of initiation with the backstage events and the thoughts and meetings of the bookers. The End seems to sense this, and turns his head to Seaver. Cal Seaver:'I don’t know.' 'Stupid questions from a stupid man,” is the insult The End levies upon the much smaller Seaver, who must take the abuse. Now you know why I hate to give interviews and promos.…' The End walks away, saying, “If I didn’t hate you, Seaver, I’d wish you a good day.” Seaver is stirred uneasily, and decides now is the best time to end the on-camera time. Cal Seaver:'... Okay. This is Cal Seaver, signing off.' The camera opens in the middle of a television studio. The backdrop is an orange wall and there is an audience that the camera pans away from to move to the stage, which has a telecounter on it marking dollars. Standing on stage with a microphone, The End waits for the camera to get in position. A quick shot of various WWF janitors like Crash Holly, Funaki, Bob Holly, and Mike Awesome manning the phones is shown before the camera moves to a stationary shot of The End. He is dressed in a black suit, white shirt, and red tie, though he wears his mask. The stage has a back wall that says, 'Stormbringer's Kids.' 'Welcome,' The End begins, 'and thank you for being a part of this special event.' The audience applauds as prompted off-camera by the stage director. 'As you know,” The End continues, “there are certain persons that can't for the life of them succeed. Try as they may, no good comes to them and they need the guidance of bitter enemies to do anything right. Such is the case of my opponent at Spring Fling, Stormbringer.” A picture of Bobby Stormbringer is placed on the video feed. The picture is also put on the back wall beside the “Stormbringer’s Kids” logo. The feed comes back to The End. 'Stormbringer’s trying to wrestle matches as if he’s thinking that this is just another match, and he’ll easily defeat me, but we all know that I have put the fear of God in him. Why else would a normally successful wrestler be on a losing streak? Or to question even further, perhaps Bobby Stormbringer wanted to be fired. He was relieved and ecstatic that he would not have to face me at the pay-per-view, knowing that I would defeat him. “And it's getting to be a nuisance, PCW brass telling me to cut promos against him to make things look good. But I hate these things. So I'll make it easy on Stormbringer.' The camera switches to a shot of the telecounter. The End speaks off-camera. 'If there is any one who would like Stormbringer to beat me at Spring Fling, then this telecounter is for you. Call in now and make a donation to the 'Bribe The End to Throw the Match Fund'. My target is $1 million dollars, and I won't stop until I get there.' The camera changes to a view of more WWF janitors, Rhyno, Billy Kidman, APA, and Godfather manning the phones this time. 'Operators are standing by waiting to take your calls, place your donations now, and when we reach the target, I will gladly no-show the pay-per-view, allowing Stormbringer to win by default. In the meantime, here are two terrier/pug hybrid breed dogs dancing the Charleston on a wire ten feet above the ground.' *Time Compression: 5 Hours* 'Well, I've only made $5,000, that coming from the estate of Bobby Stormbringer. Thanks for the money, but this isn't enough, so Stormbringer, expect me at Spring Fling ready to wrestle. I'm getting tired of talking to you people, and I'm out of mind-numbingly inane acts to show you, so get the hell out. The show is over.' Out in the middle of Times Square in New York City, The End has set up a kiosk opposite the MTV studio where they shoot TRL. It's simple, with several cups labeled 'The End' and more cups labeled 'Stormbringer.' The End stops random people. The first is a middle-aged accountant in a bland brown suit, his head balding and a rather large mole hanging off of the left side of his face. 'Excuse me, sir,' The End says, 'I need you to participate in a little taste test.' The accountant doesn't pay much attention. Accountant:'I'm in a hurry.' The End grabs the accountant by the arm, yanks him back to the kiosk, and shoves the two cups at him. 'Drink the damn drinks,' The End commands him in a deep voice. The accountant tastes the cup flavored 'The End.' It is pleasing. Accountant:'Cool, smooth, fruit-flavored. Very nice.' The accountant drinks the second cup labeled 'Stormbringer.' His face cringes and he spits the drink out, pouring the rest on the ground. Accountant:'This tastes like sh*t!' 'That's exactly what Stormbringer is full of,' The End says with a slight smirk. The accountant grabs two more cups of 'The End' and washes the bad taste out of his mouth. He walks off gagging. The End turns to the camera. 'Stormbringer doesn't seem to be that bad of a guy,' he says. 'This just seems to be the sort of thing I'm required to say whenever I have to do one of these promos. God, I hate these things.' The End leaves the kiosk and walks off, the camera following his movements from a stationary position before fading out. The End is marching in front of the White House with a picket sign, shouting for all to hear. Passersby are looking at The End with perplexed expressions. Secret Service agents line the inside of the White House gates, just in case The End tries something. The End holds his sign high above his head and marches back and forth in a 16-foot-perimeter ellipse, shouting, 'Stormbringer for President! Stormbringer for President! Stormbringer for President!' The two White House Secret Service agents escort The End off the premises. The camera fades in during a church session. At the podium, speaking before the congregation is The End. Many people sit in the benches, listening eagerly to what The End is saying. The camera starts wide then zooms in close up to The End. The End addresses the camera. 'Welcome, children,' says The End in a wise old voice, 'to the Church of Stormbringer.' Meanwhile, in the background, a voice that sounds somewhat like The Rush’s says, “Let me back in! It’s my church!” As quickly as the scene faded in, it fades out. The End is in the same white room as he began, only the room is darker, and his wrestling outfit can hardly be made out. His voice is the only identifying feature. The End breathes heavily, and speaks in a dark voice... nah, on second thought, he speaks in a deep, though not dark voice. 'I would hope, Stormbringer,” says The End; “I would hope that you wouldn’t take this whole ‘firing incident seriously. We do have our match scheduled for the pay-per-view, that tool Healey doesn’t have the presence of mind to edit the card. So you and I shall still do battle. Even if you’re fired, and I have to fly you to the arena and pay for your ticket to get in, we will be having our match.” 'I've given you quite a bit of advertisement in this promo, but that’s just because you seem unable to do that yourself with your recent... dismissal. You can thank me after I kill you at Spring Fling….” The camera fades out for the last time.
