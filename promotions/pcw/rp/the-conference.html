The Conference…

Date: Sunday the 16th... (Follow on from Another Kind Of Plague) Sunday evening, February 16th: 5: 30 PM. Cal climbed the stairs and knocked on the door.The Guards had let him through without much trouble, and judging from the countless production vans and reporters parked outside the house’s gates, he had been the first allowed to enter. He looked down at his watch again and waited a moment, humming a distant tune under his breath and a patient glint lingering in the deep recesses of his eyes. His stance was abruptly interrupted as footsteps from beyond the door quickly became audible. He straightened his suit and then, with a sudden thrust of air, the colossal slabs of wood were pulled inward and the curious visage of PCW’s The Pharaoh protruded from The Palace’s interior. “Cal,” He said with a sigh, “You’re early.” Seaver grinned hopefully as Titu led him into the mansion, the wondrous ornaments and statues lining the halls striking him with a sort of awe. With some effort he composed himself and broke into question, the only thing he was really any good at. “So what’s this all about, Titu?” he began, “I got a call from Harold early this morning saying you had some stuff to say to the public. Are you gonna let me in on the secret, or what?” Pharaoh heaved a sigh, his eyes rolling back into his skull. “Have you never heard the phrase, ‘curiosity killed the cat’, Cal?” Seaver broke into a wide grin, ‘Well I’m no cat, Titu; you know that as well as anyone!’ ‘…I do?’ queried Pharaoh, cocking an eyebrow. A moment of uneasy silence ensued. ‘Cummon, Pharaoh, what’s it all about?’ Cal said at last, ‘With all those vans out there it’s gotta be something important! Are you retiring?’ Pharaoh chuckled. ‘Not for a while, Cal, not for a while. Now come this way, the conference hall is just up ahead.’ Titu led the inquisitive reported through an archway at the end of the lobby and continued through many more until they came towards what Seaver could only make out as a tall brass doorway, two giant statues of Ibis-headed humanoids guarding either side. ‘What’re those, Titu?’ Cal stuttered, clearly a little uneasy around the colossal stone figures. With a smile and a pat on the back, Pharaoh informed Cal that the two sculptures were that of the God Thoth, a deity of ancient Egypt and the most knowledgeable of the other Egyptian celestials. ‘I see. So why have you got them on either side of this door?’ Throughout life, Cal had always desired answers. It was one of his many character traits, and, according to a few people, one of his many character flaws. Pharaoh looked at him with a pearly smile, and then returned his gaze towards the two monstrous figurines adorning the entryway. ‘Because, Cal, this is the conference hall! Inside, much knowledge is gained and expressed! And so what more fitting than the God Thoth to guard this room, I ask?’ Pharaoh took a step away from the reporter and strode up to the huge brass entrance. He grasped the two bronze knockers on the front and, with much effort, prized the interlocking doors open. The room inside was vast. Expensive-looking chairs span out around it in great rows of black leather, and towards the far back stood a stage and a tall stone podium. ‘Jesus H. Chri--…’ ‘Do not utter that name under this roof, Seaver!!’ Pharaoh snapped, ‘The Gods are watching us right now, and they are not forgiving beings, I can tell you!’ Cal took a step back, grasping at his shirt collar, a little overpowered by the sudden outburst and clearly shaken by the threat. ‘All right, I’m sorry.’ Pharaoh looked about the conference hall and nodded. ‘Everything is as it should be… Excellent, the guards have done a brilliant job! Please, take your seat in front, Cal; the others should start piling in at any moment.’ Titu pointed Seaver out a seat near the podium and left, leaving the shell-shocked reporter alone in the giant hall. He gulped, surveying the ominous vestibule and set off with careful footsteps towards his seat.Each step echoed and even his gentle breathing seemed to resonate off the conference hall’s walls as he made his way across the sparkling marble floor.After that display he couldn’t wait to get out of here. The place was cosy from what he’d seen so far, but it was more than weird. Something about The Palace spooked him, and Titu’s anger-filled words hadn’t helped him come to terms with what the hell it was that he found so strange about the place. He sat down, made himself comfy, and waited… Seconds turned into minutes and minutes lasted far too long.By the time everyone had assembled in the conference room, Cal was nodding over. Someone beside him nudged him methodically in the ribs rendering his eyes to spring open in perplexity. ‘Wake up! He’s starting to talk!’ Cal shook his head this way and that, his eyes still sleepy, and reached into his jacket pocket for his notepad. Up on the stage, Pharaoh now stood overlooking the crowd, a stern expression quickly gathering upon his face. Surprisingly, the whole room was almost full, and this came as a shock to many when they considered the hall’s dimensions. What could be on the Egyptian warrior’s mind that required him to gather so many reporters into the same room? It was all quite odd… ‘Friends, wrestling critics, and Cal Seaver, you must all be wondering why I asked you to come here today--…’ A buzz of voices clouded the room as reporter after reporter after reporter stood from their seats and began to fire queries this way and that. Pharaoh sighed, and turned to the Palace Guard beside him. ‘Bernard, sort them out, will you?’ Bernard Johnson stood to attention and walked off stage. A few moments passed before ten or fifteen guards, including Johnson, rushed the reporters, driving some of them back out of the hall. A dead silence passed over the gawking multitude. ‘As I was saying: you’re all probably wondering why I gathered such a mass of reporters here today, right? Well, my friends, I’d like to express my feelings on the matter of those damned false-idol worshipping do-gooders who visit my home at least twice a week in an attempt to flood my brain with absolute nonsense!’ The crowd, including PCW reporter Cal Seaver, looked on in bafflement. ‘I’m a one-religion man! Please, get it into your skulls that I don’t want to be apart of your damned stupendous beliefs! I like my religion as it is; I like my Gods as they are, and nothing, nothing in this world is going to change that!’ Pharaoh pauses, looking out over the sea of perplexed faces. ‘Thank you. Thank is all.’ With a stride in his step, Titu stormed off stage leaving the crowd of reporters extremely annoyed. With so much of a mass of correspondents called into The Palace, they all thought the conference would have probably involved Pharaoh’s thoughts on Clyde, his match for Tuesday night, and his wife’s condition. But alas no, not on this fine Sunday evening. Twenty minutes later, the mansion was empty of all journalists.That was aside from one man, PCW reporter Cal Seaver. After the disappointing announcement, Seaver had found Xansiv in the lobby again and, being the ever-intrusive person he was, he had gone about asking some more questions. ‘What was that all about, Titu? I don’t get it--…’ Pharaoh frowned, ‘All that had to be said was said, Cal.’ ‘But--!’ And Seaver found himself outside The Palace, the Guards dragging him down the pebbled path…
