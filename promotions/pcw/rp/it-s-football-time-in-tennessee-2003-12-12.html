It's Football Time in Tennessee!

12 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12-12-03 RP 2 Turmoil Jan. 14 It has been told that baseball is America’s favorite past time. In years past, that was as true as the Holy Bible. After mother’s homemade apple pie is gone, aunts, uncles, and cousins would gather in the backyard and play a game of baseball. Father’s old catcher’s mitt and Brother’s brand new glove would be taken out of the closets and before the afternoon is done, both gloves would have at least one more wrinkle. The wrinkle would leave its mark of that game. Teresa has no such memories. Sure Mama Tomas was famous for her homemade pies no matter what flavor. At the Tomas Farm just outside of Nashville, the game of choice was always football. No, I don’t mean soccer. Good old fashioned American Football. That one sport that seems to be taking popularity over baseball more every few years. When Teresa was growing up, family get-togethers meant the whole family would gather at the Farm. Mama Tomas’s three sisters, two brothers and their kids, Captain Tomas and his two younger brothers and sister with their kids all together made about thirty including husbands and wives. While Teresa, her brother Eric and the cousins played in the hay filled barn, the women would gather in the kitchen like a house full of hens and gossip while preparing the huge meal, and the men filled the den drinking beer and watching football. Tennessee didn’t have a National Football Team at the time for the state’s favorite team was the University of Tennessee’s Vols. During college football season, the highlight of Saturdays was where the Vols would play and their stats for the year. The rest of the year, the men would discuss their favorite NFL teams and make bets on the game. When Mama Tomas hollered towards the barn, “TIME TO EAT!” You knew that you better head inside or the best of the food would be gone. Not everyone would sit at the table. Most of the men would take their plates and return to the den leaving the women and some of the kids to sit at the dinning table. These were times of innocence that left lasting memories. After the meal was over and the dishes were cleaned, everyone would go out in the large backyard. Teresa’s brother Eric would grab the football while the kids form their teams. The main rule was always that adults couldn’t tackle the younger kids. Most everyone participated and the games continued till nearly dark. Now most American football fans know the Titans won their chance at the AFC playoffs. The game was one of the most intense games this narrator has ever watched. First quarter Titans dominated. Second, Pittsburgh caught up and the remainder of the game, both teams were at each other’s heels till the final second when Tennessee made the final kick in overtime that won the game. Can the Titans continue as victorious against Oakland? We’ll find out next week. It’s been a few years since the Tomas family had a family reunion. So far everyone except for Captain Tomas is still alive. Teresa’s midnight purple step side (not the monster truck) creeps up the old gravel driveway leading to the house where she grew up in. Just as Mama Tomas, nothing has changed with the farm except the hired farm help each year. Traffic on the interstate was awful so she’s a bit late. Teresa can see dozen or so cars and truck parked around the side of the farmhouse. The smell of turkey fills the two-story barn shaped house. As Teresa steps through the front screen door, she shuts it quietly trying not to let it slam. She can hear Uncle JR and Jeff in the den talking about their pick up trucks while Mama Tomas laughs with Aunt Betty and Aunt Violet in the kitchen. Teresa makes a right turn across from the den to the living room where a handful of her cousin sit talking about growing up and what they done in high school. “Teresa, I’m surprised you decided to come. With your career and all, I figured you’d be too busy.” Her brother Eric stands as he speaks with a hint of resentment in his voice. During the first months of her pro wrestling career after the Indy days, Eric served as her manager. Things became rocky and he got involved with the girlfriend of her then rival Ariz Ona. After Teresa fired him, he left with her and has held a chip on his shoulder ever since. Today is the first she’s seen him since NTICW. “Don’t tell me you still mad Eric. Business is business and I had to do what I had to do.” Eric walks toward Teresa. She doesn’t move neither does he intimidate her. He may be a well built man, but he fought like a girl. Eric stops just inches from Teresa. He looks down at her for he is a good six-foot to her 5’7. “You think you hot shit don’t you Teresa? You’re in the top fed now and even the TV Champ? Don’t let that belt make your head swell little sister. You’ll lose that belt as quick as you won it.” Teresa takes a step forward. If they were the same height, they would be nose to nose. Her baby blues looks up to her brother’s. “You’re jealous. You’re jealous that your little sister can kick ass better than you can. And, you’re still pissed that you’re not riding my coattail anymore.” Eric reaches his hand back to smack Teresa’s face, before his hand touches her jaw, she grabs his wrist. He makes another attempt with his other hand. She ducks and gives his gut her elbow. Eric moans loud enough to get everyone’s attention. Mama Tomas and the women urge the Uncles to break up the fight, but they simply stand in the doorway watching. “Some things never change. Just let the kids fight it out like they used to do in the backyard.” Uncle Jeff told the women. The adults watch in silence as the cousins cheer Teresa on. Eric slams into a bookcase causing books to fall all over the floor. Teresa waits till Eric stands then dropkicks his knees causing them to go down. While Eric is down Teresa grabs his right arm, twists it and turns so that he is on his stomach with his arm twisted. Then she sits down so she's kneeling facing away from her brother. She smiles at her watching family and grabs Eric’s leg and pulls it back. Southern Comfort. “As you were saying dear brother?” Teresa pulls at his leg causing her brother more pain. Eric screams. “Let me go Teresa! You cheater.” “I’m not a cheater, you’re just a 30 year old baby. Now scream like a bitch or I’m not moving.” Eric begins tapping the hardwood floor. His stubbornness refuses to let him give to her crazy demands. “You’re freaking crazy. Just let me go.” “Scream like a bitch.” Teresa takes a deep breath and pulls harder. “NO!” “Yes.” One last pull at his leg and.. “AHHHHHH!!!!” Eric screams So much for reliving childhood memories.
