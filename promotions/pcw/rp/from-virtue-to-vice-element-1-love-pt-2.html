From Virtue to Vice: Element 1 - Love (Pt. 2)

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 20 of 2003 A.D 8:01 p.m... 8:02 p.m... 8:03 p.m... The minutes crawled by as Desmond Muldoon continued to eagerly watch the clock, awaiting the muffled hum of the ’99 Porsche to pull into the gravel driveway of the remote Vermont lodge, signaling the arrival of Krissy from town. After Desmond’s awakening the past night, and his subsequent lapse into terror-stricken insanity immediately following, his devout other had stood by his side for sleepless hours until remnants of reality had finally replenished his pallid features. After assuring her he was alright, she had hurriedly departed for nearby Rutland in hopes of bringing back help in the form of doctors to evaluate his current state. He had chosen to stay behind, wholly doubtful of his ability to maintain rationality if he indeed were to fall asleep again. The night visions had gradually become more vivid and dramatically more macabre over the past week before culminating this horrid night, and what they tended to lack in duration they made up for in their ghastly imagery. As a result his sleep the past 24 hours (especially) had been severely afflicted, and he lacked confidence that he could remain awake during the lengthy drive back into Rutland. He could still see the sneering face of Bruce Shanahan, the evil man he had once met when he was still sane and rational, and those abominations skulking in the blackened realm behind him. And he wasn’t about to gaze into those aberrant sockets again. He had finally dared drift back into slumber in the waning morning hours, having awakened only an hour previous to now with his stirrings accompanied by the glow of the setting sun. To his alarm, Krissy had yet to return to the rented cottage. He had doggedly launched into prayer over the past hour for the drone of the import’s tell-tale motor to break the eerie silence of the Vermont hillside, where the rhythmic warble of the whippoorwills continued to orchestrate from the surrounding wilderness. Muldoon shifted his eyes from the red glow of the clock to the lighter shade of orange painting the western horizon. His thoughts drifted back to the circumstances surrounding this, their second honeymoon, and he quickly tore his mind away from the scene which had first awakened him to the truth. It was in the past, and he wouldn’t dwell on it anymore. This is why we came here, he thought somberly with eyes on the red-orange aura hovering in the west, for scenes like this. To enjoy them together. To enjoy...each other again... He watched in silence as the distant radiance deteriorated into gloom of darkness. Clusters of snow dumped on the region fell from the limbs of the neighboring pines and gentle northern winds whistled in their primal glee. All the while, the chorus of the whippoorwills persisted.... * * * “Hello, Rutland Township Police Department, Sergeant Ambrose speakin’.” In the background, the clock turned to 9:08. Muldoon had began to shiver, though whether it was mainly from fear for his wife’s whereabouts or the chill that had brusquely crept into the air in the past few minutes was anyone’s guess. “Hello...I’m staying at the Blue Enoch Lodge, and my wife went out early this morning to visit the doctor in town. I’m really worried because she hasn’t come back yet, and she hasn’t called or anything. I’m really worried...” There was an awkward silence on the other end followed by inaudible mumbling between two or more individuals. The officer let out a gruff cough, mark of a long-time smoker, before replying. “Actually, sir, we had Dr. Harmon, the chief pharmacist down at Dr. Wagner’s office, come in earlier about similar business. Said a woman obviously from out of town came in askin’ for someone to accompany her back to ‘er rented cottage. She was nearly hysterical, and they tried ‘un help her before she left....” “She was obviously worried about me...” Muldoon replied. “No, sir. According to the doc, it had nothing to do with you. It was her own safety that had her in a panic. This roused an inquisitive expression on the face of Desmond. He nervously massaged his left bicep, just barely keeping his voice from quivering. “What...what do you mean?” “Doc said she was ramblin’ about things she done saw on the road before gettin’ ‘ere. Weird stuff alongside the road, felt some sort o’ ‘evil presence’ or whatever durin’ the drive inta town.” Desmond immediately felt irate. “Why didn’t you keep her there dammit if she was so scared??!” “Couldn’t, doc tried to keep ‘er here a while but she shrieked about being afraid to leave you there by yaself. She jumped in her car and left...” “Then could you please search for her? At least check to see if she’s still around anywhere?” “Doc said she left his office around noon. That was nine hours ago, mister...” “Muldoon,” barked the impatient financier. “Desmond Muldoon.” “Yes, Mr. Muldoon. Anyhow we’ll check ‘round, see if anyone’s seen ‘er. And we’ll get back to ya.” “Thanks officer.” Muldoon hung up the phone before collapsing onto the couch, staring warily at the stagnant white ceiling and lulled sleepily by the harmonious ticking of the owl-themed wall-clock and the curious song of those denizens of the woodlands. * * * Muldoon’s eyes fluttered and a fatigued yawn escaped his lips, turning his exhausted mass over on the couch. A brisk gust of wind caressed his exposed arms and face, prompting the banker to draw himself up erect. He purses his eyelids for a moment, a solitary tear drop forming as a result of the pressure and collecting at the corner of his eye. A stifled gasp gurgled in the bowels of his throat at the sight stretching before him, this one warping not his sanity but his fragile temperament. He was outside, standing at the base of an obscene altar where a curious monolithic spire loomed against a background of torches set about the area in an ellipse. Decorated in blasphemous runes and crude, abyss-forged glyphs, the monolith was fashioned into a deformed ankh. And tied to that ankh, bound in ropes and gagged to muffle her screams, squirmed his beloved wife. Stripped totally naked, the ropes gnawed against her struggling body and drew blood which coursed down her shapely form in twisted streams over her rising breasts and down her shivering legs. An impassive chorus rose and fell, formed by a collective body of robed figures around the altar clutching torches. The monosyllabic mantra had no English foundation, but matched that of the disembodied chant he had heard whispered earlier in the week in his dark dreams. “Ph-nglui mglw’nafh Cthulhu R’lyeh wgah’nagl fhtagn” “Krissy!” Desmond shrieked, storming the altar toward his darling wife. Torches lining the edge of the upper level of the multi-tiered altar exploded in a raging inferno, inducing Muldoon into halting and bringing his arms up to shield his face from the flames. Gradually the heat died away and Muldoon opened his eyes, blinking several times to whisk away the haziness of his pressure-invoked tears. Before he stood a man robed much like the rest, but donned in ceremonial dress invoking one to believe he held a greater importance than those enclosing the area. The fabric of his robes glossed and adorned with the markings scrawled about the altar, his appearance was almost stately and regal in comparison to his chanting peers. A chuckle emanated from the shadows of the hood. “Desmond,” the voice declared. “’Tis a pleasure to see you again. I trust you have been well?” Muldoon’s eyes narrowed at the sound of the voice. Though it was not marked by the accent he had remembered, the tembre and tone were unmistakable. “Apart from a few things, I’ve been quite peachy. And you....how have you fared as a godless exile, Bruce?” With cuffs lined in gold and silver trim, two brawny arms rose to lower the hood. The uncannily benevolent eyes of Bruce Shanahan came into view and met those of Muldoon. His normally sinister smirk, now marked with civility, met the angry young banker. “You still refuse to accept the truth, Desmond? You still won’t recognize your true masters and renounce the vile beliefs you’ve been brainwashed and conditioned into accommodating?” “Only one of us brainwashed is you, you fucking maniac,” snarled Muldoon as looked back at his horrified spouse chained to the ankh. “Release Krissy, Bruce. She has nothing to do with this mess.” His demand was met with subdued laughter. “Oh contraire. She has everything to do with this, Mr. Muldoon.” He stalked up the rocky red steps to stroke her flesh, the young woman doing all she could to cower away from the evil of Shanahan. “We all have our flaws, Desmond. They are what make us mortal, the little attributes bestowed on us by your God as his own little prank. Most of society refer to these things as virtues. I call them faults.” “You fucking psychopath....” “In order to achieve immortality,” persisted Shanahan, “one must learn to overcome these flaws. Mercy must become cruelty, honor must evolve into dishonor. These things aren’t merits, they are burdens. They’re flaws designed to keep men in line and lead away from defiance. Your God hates defiance. My masters encourage it.” “None of this has anything to do with Krissy!” roared Muldoon. “Release her!” “You just don’t understand, Desmond. All that intelligence and still you can’t see the light?” “I don’t see any light, or a point for that matter. You’re a damned maniac. This isn’t going to bring anyone back, Bruce, don’t you see that?! No matter how many people you kill, no matter how many lives you destroy, no matter how many confused soul-searchers you convert into this ‘new faith’...none of it will change the past!” Shanahan seemed to pause, lowering his hand from Krissy’s quivering arm and diverting his gaze into the sky. Mist flowed from his mouth and into the air, fading into the darkness as silence dominated the long moment. “I have come to accept the past. I am a machine now. To fulfill Their decree is my new ambition.” “You’re not fooling anyone. You’ve always been driven by that hateful vendetta you have against all of mankind. The anger over your upbringing, your hardships, your losses….it has always fueled you. Don’t think for one fucking second you can convince me otherwise you sick son of a bitch! All of this is your way of retaliating! Two wrongs don’t make a right!” “Miserable little worm,” breathed Bruce. “So near-sighted. You just can’t grasp the big picture.” He turned again to Muldoon, his eyes take on the same blazing gleam that always accompanied them. “Your love for this woman will be your ultimate fault. You are only an example of this wretched, debauched race and its failings. I grow sick of you and your presence. The next time we meet....I won’t tolerate you nearly this long. Farewell, Desmond Muldoon. I trust you now know where to find me. And then my prophecy will come to pass....” Muldoon pounced forward to attack his wife’s merciless captor. As he did so, a smile crossed the New Englander’s face and everything faded to blackness. * * * Muldoon awakened from his ‘nap’ and hurriedly sprinted to the window. As he knew he would, he spied a great bonfire spewing flame on the hill. He thought he could detect faint chant of multiple voices as one, and the hushed screams of a woman. It was Judgment Day. Desmond would take Krissy back, and Bruce would pay for hurting his wife.....
