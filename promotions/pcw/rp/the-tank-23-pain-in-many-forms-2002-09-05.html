The Tank 23 - Pain in Many Forms

5 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 5, 2002 ﻿*********The Tank —23—********* Pain in Many Forms I’ve never seen that look before.The way it seems I sucked the world out of your eyesand the only things left are contempt and myself. You could kill me a thousand timesand each one would be suicide. ~Leigh Donovan Saturday, August 31 - Day 58 “Why the hell does the Warden want to see me?” Snow asks in the midst of hisconfusion as he stares at the surprisingly colorful face of Geoffery Rankin. The pair are standingjust outside the main gate to Unit B, which Snow has just passed through and is now in theprocess of being handcuffed by one of the smaller guards in the Tank. “He doesn’t,” Rankin, the Unit B manger, answers with a slick smile coming over his youthfulface. “But you said...” “I said what I had to so you could get out of there without some of the nastier prisoners stickinga knife in your back,” Rankin tells the incarcerated star. “Listen, it’s been a stressful situation inUnit B since visitation privileges were revoked - if those motherfuckers in there know that youhave a visitor, while they’re not allowed to have visitors, they’ll turn on you.” “Wait a minute,” Snow says, holding a cuffed hand up to Rankin to slow down the conversation. “You mean, I’ve got a visitor? A visitor that I’m allowed to see?” “Provided that you don’t tell any of the other prisoners,” Rankin nods. “I think thecircumstances are extraordinary enough.” “So Krista’s here, right?” Snow asks, not even waiting for an answer before he continues. “Do Iget to see her without the glass?” “Of course,” Rankin tells Snow as they begin to walk. “This is going to be completelyconfidential - no one’s going to know about it. I’m taking you to Jenny Vegas’ office.” “Krista’s in there?” Snow raises a curious eyebrow. “Why is she in Vegas’ office?” “Like I told you before,” Rankin shrugs as they take a left toward the segregated staff section ofthe prison - a heavy iron gate, guarded by four machine-gun toting men, separates the two areas,“Krista has grown attached to Jenny Vegas. You have to understand how traumatic this entireexperience has been for Krista. In Jenny, she’s found someone to trust, and those people are fewand far between for her right now.” Snow scowls - his opinion of Jenny Vegas isn’t a high one. To him, she’s just eye candy to keepthe prisoners calm. He’s sure that since she’s come to work as an anger management councilor,the male-on-male rate of rape in the Tank has dropped. Seeing a woman from time to time helpsscratch homosexuality off a lot of prisoner’s lists of options. But to Snow, that’s Jenny’s onlypurpose, and the worst part is, she doesn’t even know it. “Jenny Vegas...” Snow smirks and rolls his eyes as one of the guards unlocks the heavy irongate, permitting the pair, and the guard following for Rankin’s protection, entrance. As they pass the Warden’s office, Snow can almost sense the authority radiating from behind thedoor. He hasn’t seen the Warden yet, and until the day of his parole hearing in a few months, hehopes he never does. Finally, they stop at the door of Jenny Vegas’ office and Geoffery Rankin knocks lightly on theheavy wooden door. A moment, later, Jenny Vegas herself opens the door with a pleasant smilewhich Rankin returns - Snow’s face remains cold and hard. Before permitting Snow entrance, Vegas steps out of the office and closes the door behind her. “Hello gentlemen,” She says before nodding hello. She knows Snow quite well as the man whosits at the back of her Saturday Anger Management Meeting and says nothing, even when spokento. “How is she?” Snow asks immediately. Jenny sighs, “Better.” “Let me see her then,” Snow says anxiously. “Jason,” Jenny says without motioning to the door, “you have to be prepared for how Krista mayreact to you.” “I saw her last week,” Snow shrugs, his eyes beginning to burn. “Yes,” Jenny nods, “and she wasn’t very talkative then. She’s getting better, but you have tounderstand, she’s been having a very hard time dealing with men. When you walk in there, youhave to show compassion toward her. You can’t at any time, let her confuse you with the type ofmen she’s afraid of - and that group includes just about every male with a pulse, excludingfamily.” “What’s the problem then?” Snow asks, getting a little upset. “I’m her brother-in-law. Hell, I’mthe closest thing to a father she’s ever had.” “I know, Jason,” Jennifer says in a hushed tone, hoping to lead Snow by her example. “You justhave to make sure that she doesn’t feel threatened by you at any time. You’re one of the fewmen who she doesn’t fear the sight of right now.” Snow’s heart breaks in his eyes, “How can it be that bad?” “She’s nineteen, Snow,” Jenny says quietly and finally reaching for the brass door knob. “Herentire world just changed, literally. She’s living in Los Angeles, which is a far cry from NewBrunswick Canada, and she’s just been raped. So suddenly, she doesn’t know anyone and hermost memorable experience is a horrible one. You’re the closest thing she has to a friend rightnow... and you’re in here.” His heart already a victim, Snow’s soul burns for the young girl in the room beyond the door - ifonly he weren’t here, locked up like the animal he is, maybe he could comfort Krista. If heweren’t in here, none of this would ever have happened. “Lets go in,” Rankin says, nodding to the door. “Not you,” Jenny says sternly to Rankin. “No men.” Geoffery takes a step back and respects the situation as Snow and Jenny Vegas step inside thesmall, well-organized office. Each book is arranged perfectly in the shelf and there’s a neatstack of papers on the desk. The room is decorated by plantlife and seems to be arranged in away to make both its occupant and visitors as comfortable as possible. It reminds Snow of home- his wife decorates in such a way. Sitting on the far side of Jenny’s desk is Krista, her big brown eyes fixed on Snow and a weaksmile spread across her face. “Hey,” she says, widening her smile. Snow suddenly becomes aware, and ashamed, of his handcuffs as Krista rises from her seat,steps around the desk, and gives him a warm hug. From Vegas’ description of her, he imaginedher moping about and crying all day. Instead, he finds her more vibrant than he’d imagined andseeming somewhat happy. Then again, he knows, trauma is not exterior. “For a while there, I didn’t think they were going to let me see you,” Krista says, only letting goof Snow’s rugged body enough to look up at him. “Something about your Unit not allowedvisitors.” Snow nods, “a mob boss got killed.” The thought of it brings a faint breeze of joy to Snow’smind - Hattoni’s death was a sweet sort of revenge. “Anyway,” Krista sighs, finally letting go. “I’m just really glad to see you.” “Me too,” Snow says as he takes a seat on the near side - Krista returns to the far. “I’ve beenworried.” Krista nods but says nothing. Behind Snow, Jenny Vegas crouches in the corner and watches theinteraction intensely - though she may not be a wonderful psychiatrist, she has developed apersonal attachment to Krista, as most people who meet her do. Finally, Krista speaks. “Haley turned a year old last month,” she says pleasantly. “I know,” Snow says, lowering his head. His daughters anniversary of life, and he missed it. Hemisses everything in here. “I was up for the party,” Krista smiles sweetly, reaching out across the desk for Snow’s hand. Amonth ago, Snow thinks, he was so angry with Krista for her drug use. Now, all that anger isgone - replaced by guilt. “She’s starting to talk now. She misses you.” “She’ll probably forget me,” Snow says quietly, lowering his head. Fatherhood hasn’t come easyto the young man, despite endless good intentions. “She won’t.” “I’ve been here for two months,” Snow says, trying to be realistic. “That’s a sixth of her life. And it’s not like I was around a whole lot before that, with work and all.” “She won’t forget you,” Krista says sternly, squeezing Snow’s cuffed hand. “You’re her father.” “I wonder if she knows that.” Snow pulls his hands away from Krista with a sigh and runs hisfingers through his long blond hair. He can’t look at her right now. They’re both hurting more than they’re willing to show the other, even though they know theirdisguises are useless with each other. “You’ll be home soon enough,” Krista assures Snow with a hopeful smile. Snow stays silent. Suddenly, Jenny Vegas takes her feet and announces, “Everything seems to be going well, so Ithink I’ll just leave you two alone.” Snow is grateful for the gesture. As soon as Vegas is gone, he asks Krista, “Have you told yoursister? You know, about...” Krista shakes her head and lowers her eyes. As much as she’d hoped the conversation wouldsteer clear of this subject, she knows that’s not possible. “I don’t know how she’d handle it. After a miscarriage and you being in prison... this would just be something else for her toswallow.” Snow nods, realizing that Krista is able to handle these types of situations much better than heever could. His wife, her sister, has already been through so much in the last few months. Theentire family has been emotionally butchered. News of yet another cut can wait. “What about you?” Snow finally asks. “How are you handling it?” Krista swallows hard while tears glass over her large brown eyes. “I’m doing ok,” she says,shaking up a smile. “Jenny’s been really nice to me. We talk every day.” Snow nods, finally realizing how much good Jenny Vegas has done for his vulnerable sister-in-law. “Maybe it’s not such a bad idea that you stick around LA for a while longer. Just makesure you find a safe neighborhood to stay in - there’s plenty of money in your account.” Snowreaches out for her little hand again. When Snow stepped into the millions of dollars that camealong with being a superstar pro wrestler, as well as some wise investments and a stint as one ofTyler Nelson’s closest friends, one of the first things he did was start an account for Krista - onethat would make sure she would always be taken care of. “It is,” Krista says, wishing she hadn’t ventured out of the safe neighborhood on that horriblenight. “I’m sorry, Krista,” Snow says as he leans forward and lowers his head. “I mean, if I weren’t inhere... I could have done something. I could have protected you.” Tears run from Krista’s eyes down her pretty cheeks, yet she smiles to comfort her brother-in-law. “You can’t, Jason. Sometimes, you just can’t protect me, no matter how big and strongyou think you are. That doesn’t make me think less of you, Jason.” She gives his hand a squeeze and he knows she’s right. Still, he’d do anything to protect her -he’d kill for this girl, and in a way, Doug Hattoni’s death proved that. “I wish I could though,”he tells her. “I know,” she nods. “And you can, sometimes. When I’m in trouble, I’ll always run to you -you know that. That’s the way it’s always been. But things aren’t exactly the way they used tobe, Jason. There are times when I’m just going to have to fend for myself.” “I can still be there for vengeance though,” Snow says as a vicious light enters his blue eyes. “Not always,” Krista answers, “and not this time. The guy killed himself, Jason - there’s no oneleft to blame here.” Snow gives Krista a suspicious smile and squeezes her hand tightly. “Things aren’t always asthey seem.” Krista’s face loses all color. “What do you mean?” “I mean,” Snow sighs while leaning closer, “I’ve already taken care of something in here. People have paid for what happened to you.” He really couldn’t help but tell her - it is, afterall,the only thing that brings any joy to this situation. Doug Hattoni, the only man left with anyresponsibility for Krista’s rape has been killed, and Snow played a big part in his death, handinghim over to the Nazis. “You took care of something?” Krista asks quietly. “In here?” Snow only smiles warmly at her, trying to convey as much information that is safe to givethrough his eyes. Krista, however, takes a more verbal approach. “Jason,” she says sternly, “what do you knowthat I don’t? How could you possibly do something in here that had to do with what happened tome out there!?” Snow’s eyes grow wide with shock - she put it together. She knows now the source of his guilt -in an indirect way, not only was Doug Hattoni responsible for her rape, but Snow is too. If he’d never asked Hattoni to have someone follow her, none of this would ever have happened. “What do you know, Jason!?” she repeats, only this time, more harshly. “How was someonein here responsible for what happened to me? How!?” Suddenly, her eyes burst into angry tears as she forms conclusions in her head - she can tell byhis silence that no explanation he can form would please her. Could it be that he did somethingthat angered another prisoner enough to have him make the call to have her raped, she wonders? She doesn’t know the answer, but she knows it’s bad. Snow’s silence professes his guilt. “Krista...” he says, but finds no other words to follow her name. She holds up a hand to stop him, “I don’t want you here any more.” She turns her head towardthe wall, no longer wanting an explanation - if the explanation were too horrible, she knows shewouldn’t be able to handle it. Instead, she shuts him away from her completely. “I don’t wantto see you,” she tells him coldly. “I don’t know if I ever want to see you again.” *** From the Office of the Warden “The worst pain is the pain of guilt,” says the Warden. “It’s a pain that you know you inflictedyourself. You think back, going over and over the thousands of ways you could have handled acertain situation differently. But in the end, you didn’t, and you have to deal with the guilt ofyour actions. Having a conscience isn’t all it’s cracked up to be - most of these criminals arelucky, in a strange way...” *** “So who’s the fucking cowboy going to be?” Snow says to a team of C.O’s as he paces thelength of the closed bars in his completely destroyed cell. The C.O’s stand on the other side ofthe bars each have the nightsticks out, though they hope to solve this problem through words. Snow’s words, however, are extremely confrontational, “Come on! The door’s fucking open! You want me out, come take me!” “Snow!” shouts Benji Graham, the lead officer. “Come out quietly, boy! Don’t do anythingstupider than you’ve already done!” He’s referring to the fact that Snow has completely trashedhis cell, including his own belongings and the belongings of his cellmate. “You want to know about stupid?” Snow says wildly, pointing at the guards. “The stupid one isgoing to be whichever one of you fuckers grows a set of balls and tries to take me to the fuckinghole!” Snow’s not thinking logically by now - he hasn’t been since his meeting with Krista. Pacing back and forth in his cell, with his blond hair and beard looking unkept and wild, heresembles a caged lion more than ever. That’s exactly how the C.O’s are treating him as well. “Snow!” Graham shouts firmly. “If you don’t come out of there peacefully, I swear to Jesus andall the saints that you’ll fucking regret it!” “You going to make good on that promise old man?” Snow asks with a sickening smile on hisface. He’s wearing no shirt at the moment as it’s lying in shreds beside the overturned andbroken bunks along with all of the rest of his, and his cellmate’s, clothing. He’s gained a lot of weight in the time he’s been in the Tank, as can be expected with his all-meat diet and endless time spent in the gym to pass the hours. His muscular upper body iswhat’s intimidating most of the guards from playing hero and stepping into his cell. “Snow!” a new voice suddenly bellows - it’s Geoffery Rankin. “What the fuck is going on here,Snow!?” “Stay out of this, Rankin!” Snow says sharply, pointing a warning finger in Rankin’s direction. The young Unit B manager turns to Benji Graham, “What the hell has gotten into him!?” “I haven’t got a fucking clue!” Graham responds. “We looked up and he was tossing fuckingshoes over the balcony railing!” Rankin shakes his head, “Jesus Christ!” He then turns back to Snow and asks, “What the fuck isthis all about, anyway!?” Snow doesn’t answer. He simply continues to stalk back and forth in his cell, glaring out atRankin and the C.O’s through his wild blue eyes. Rankin sighs and shakes his head - he has no choice but to give the command, “Take him out,and ship him to the hole.” “Now we’re talking,” Snow says, his wild blue eyes lighting up as he prepares himself forcombat. “Who’s first!?” Benji Graham slides the bar-gate open and one of the larger, and younger, C.O’s enters Snow’scell cautiously. Like the lion he is, Snow pounces immediately, grabbing the young C.O by thecollar and slamming him hard into the concrete wall. He leans his face in close to the C.O,glaring at him within an inch of the young man’s face. And then, it’s all over. Benji Graham, instead of clubbing Snow with his nightstick, elects to usethe small can of mace that he carries in his right hip pocket. The mace, upon impact, sendsSnow peddling backward where he trips over the rubble that used to be his bunk and falls flat onhis back, screaming in agony. The one drawback of Graham’s decision is that his own C.O, the young man brave enough to befirst into the cell, is also lying on the floor and clutching his face - his face and Snow’s face wereonly inches apart at the time the mace was used. To Graham, it was necessary - Snow would have gotten extremely violent and made good on hispromises had he been given the opportunity. Mace ended the situation quickly. “Get him to thehole,” commands Graham. Obediently, a pair of C.O’s rush in to cuff the screaming lion - atboth the wrists and the ankles. After a few more C.O’s enter, they drag Snow to his feet andbegin pulling him out of his cell and toward the hole. Behind the wild rage, Snow doesn’t care much about being taken to the hole, where he’ll spendthe next day or two in complete seclusion. It just doesn’t matter... In his cell, he’ll do nothingbut be eaten away by guilt. The teeth of guilt will be no sharper in the hole. He won’t be attending Jenny Vegas’ Anger Management Session tonight.
