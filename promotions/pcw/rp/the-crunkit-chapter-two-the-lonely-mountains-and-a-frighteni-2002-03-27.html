The Crunkit Chapter Two: The Lonely Mountains and A Frightening Encounter

27 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/27/02 The scene of this adventure in the tales of Rushbo Baggins, the most famous Crunkit of all time, on a lonely road, where that very same Crunkit and his three dwarf companions, Coren Oakenshield, Trevin, and Sethin can be seen on their small brown ponies, meandering down the road at a rather slow speed, not in a particular hurry to reach camp tonight. Visibly missing however is the man who dwarfs all of them, Juandalf the Wizard. Coren: Now where has Juandalf gotten off to? Crazy old bastard probably is off smoking his pipe again? He needs to lay off the weed. Rushbo: Wait a sec, that means that if he’s gone then, I’m left with all white people!! Ahhhhh!! Sethin: Dude, for a thief you’re pretty loud and non sneaky. Rushbo mutters to himself, now lagging a bit behind the other riders. After the party has been silent for a few minutes while the ponies do some brisk galloping, the silence is broken as the group returns to a slow trot. Rushbo: Umm… excuse me but where the hell are we going? White people are not exactly the greatest directions followers in the world, and you might be leading me off into Mordor or some place like that. Coren: How ‘bout this, one more white person joke and I will shove your head so far up your ass that you have to wear yourself as a hat? Rushbo: Try it. I’ve got Johnny Cochran on the other line. I’ll dial him up and cha-ching! Besides, haven’t you ever heard of the Crunkit fighting spirit? Trevin: That’s like how some people say that the Irish are tough when really they’re just drunk all the time so no one can tell the difference anyway? As the group continues their feeble quarrels they stop at the side of some large buildings, which provides shade from the late morning sun, which is beginning to rise in the sky. Coren: Here it is, our home. Rushbo: Damn that big ass house? Sethin: You see the one huge house in the middle of the houses? The one with beautiful carvings and those beautiful ponies in its stables. Rushbo: Yeah? Trevin: Yeah, our house is a little shack behind that, but sometimes we get to see into their bedrooms and it looks really cool. Rushbo sighs, realizing that this is going to be a long trip, having to deal with more white people than he can shake a stick at. Rushbo: Can we just keep going? He gets no immediate answer, but none of the party moves an inch. Rushbo looks around, but sees no trouble, so he asks again. Rushbo: Hey, white people; were you not listening or something? I asked you if we were gonna keep going or if you were gonna stand around here all day staring at the road like Phil Collins was standing on it or something. Coren: This is the end. Rushbo: This is the end? That’s the most pathetic journey I’ve ever heard of! Sethin: Dude, the end of Crunkit lands. Rushbo: Well, here it is, the end of Crunkit lands. Okay, let’s go. Coren: No goodbye? Rushbo: Okay, goodbye Crunkit lands. Can we go now? Coren: You don’t even care about your homelands? Rushbo: Jesus Christ, just because you white people love Wisconsin more than life itself is no reason to jump on my ass. The four small people set off once again, their small brown ponies trotting down the dirt road towards their next destination. Through the day they passed many more places. After exiting the Crunkit lands, they saw a dwarf here or an elf there, but the people became scarcer and scarcer as they rode farther and farther. They saw no more inns; in fact they saw no more buildings at all. The roads also became steeper and steeper with every mile. However, not far in the distance were dark gloomy hills, littered with dark trees. Even ruins of old castles lay on some of the hills, with an evil look about them. Rushbo: It looks like Halloween come early around here, huh guys? Rushbo laughs softly to himself, while he can get no answer from the other members. The wet and cold fog that is rolling in does nothing to help the mood that everything has been going downhill ever since they left the Crunkit lands. The dwarves still made no noise; they just continued to jog up the hills with their ponies, ever going on. Rushbo: Man, I wish I could be in my hold right now. I could be eating some nice chicken, some cornbread, maybe some collared greens. All of the dwarves just shake their heads. Rushbo decides that he’ll just stop trying all together. As the four continue to ride, the darkness of the night and the fog both roll in creepily, making for almost impossible conditions to ride in, yet Rushbo’s companions press on once more. Rushbo, cold wet and tired continues on but slower than ever. Finally, the group can take no more and decides to settle down for the night. They find a clearing and pitch little people tents that are just cute as a button; it just makes you wanna pinch their little dwarf bottoms. Sorry about that, I swear Steven Smith will not be typing for me anymore. Rushbo: So this is where we’re staying? Great, out of the cold wet damp air on a pony onto the cold wet hard ground to sleep. Boy, this trip gets more fun every hour. Coren: Burglar Baggins, if you don’t want to continue on this journey and you are going to keep complaining you can turn around tomorrow. It was of your choice that you came on this trip. Rushbo: No it wasn’t, I just was afraid of saying no to three white people. Coren turns back towards an area in the center of the clearing and begins attempting to make a fire. After a few futile attempts, finally Coren gets a puny fire started, which all four of the men sit by, trying to warm themselves, though being soaked to the bone. Sethin: Why did you come with us Rushbo? Rushbo: Well I suppose I wanna see the world. It’s kinda boring staying in Crunkiton you’re whole life. Suddenly, Coren dives and covers Rushbo’s mouth with his hand, glancing around. He waits for a minute and finally lets go, sitting back down. Rushbo: What was that all about? Trevin: Orcs, dude. Coren: Burglar, go check out the scene near that fire over there. We need lookout if we’re to live through the night. Rushbo: Oh hell no! I’ve seen the movies. The black guy goes off to fight the evildoers, and he ends up with a 3-inch hook through his body! Coren: Burglar, you are our only hope. Rushbo: I knew you were going to say that. I guess I’ll go but if I die, you better feel absolutely terrible! Reluctantly, Rushbo sets off towards the fire on the other side of the wood. It burns brightly and Rushbo can feel the warmth. Strangely, he can also hear singing from both figures, which doesn’t fit the characteristics of a troll. The words of the singing can’t be made out, but it is obviously some sort of troll chant. However, as Rushbo nears the fire, the warmth calms and soothes him, as he fades off into a sleep… Awakened by a thrashing sound, Rushbo spring up, right into a giant troll. The monster is about 7 feet tall and 500 pounds, but most disturbing of all, in place of where a normal monster face would be is the face of none other than Phil Collins. Troll: I can feel it comin’ in the aaaair toniiiiight, hold on. Rushbo: Holy Crunk! AAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAGH! All of a sudden, out of nowhere comes a flash of light. After the flash, nothing has really changed, but of from behind a tree steps Juandalf the Wizard! Rushbo: What’s with the flash? Juandalf: You get your entrance, I get mine. Besides, just stepping from behind a tree is so lackluster. With a wave of his staff and a pointing of his hands, Juandalf fells one of the trolls. Rushbo: No special effects or anything? Juandalf: Wizardry is not all glamour and glitter you know. As the other two trolls try to escape, they do not notice that the sun began to rise, and as the fingers of sun’s rays reached the earth, the two trolls turned suddenly to stone. Rushbo: That was disturbing. Juandalf ignores him and goes to a large burlap sack. He unties the bag and out tumbles Rush’s three dwarf companions. Rushbo: What were you guys doing here? Coren: We came to look for you, oh master burglar. Rushbo: Why? Sethin: You were out like the whole night.Rushbo: Oh yeah I fell asleep, sorry about that. Coren: You will have to prove yourself thief. I do not trust your skills to steal from Smaug. Rushbo: Thanks for the vote of confidence. The three dwarves head back towards their camp, as Rushbo dejectedly walks back as well. Juandalf walks besides him though. Juandalf: Do not worry, my little friend. If I didn’t think you were worthy, then I wouldn’t have brought you. Rush: I was the only black person around that you knew. Juandalf: True but I trust you. Rushbo: That makes one. Juandalf: They will come around, don’t worry. Rushbo: I hope so. As the two figures walk off in the early morning sun, the stone figures of the trolls glisten in the sunlight. “ I can feeeel it comin’ in the air tonnnnnnnnight, hold oooon.”
