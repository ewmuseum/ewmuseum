England in the winter

15 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 15/02/03 Her eyes roved aimlessly, taking in the beautiful collage of colours that enveloped her. The rolling green of the hills, the purple heather and sandy rock falling down into the gushing, clear stream. The Yorkshire Dales were perfect at this time of the year if you could withstand the howling winds and freezing cold. No rain fell today, which was a novelty to the sheep that roamed the hills freely. Just once, she thought, she would love to push a sheep down the hill and into the stream, just for the comic effect. She wasn’t a cruel person; she just got bored easily and had let her mind wander. Funny things happen when you do that, especially for the imaginative types such as Lacuna Debris. She headed on down the mud-trodden path, her heavy black walking boots carving footprints into the deep sludge. The wind was picking up, a sure sign that the rain was on its way. This apprehension, coupled with the layers of thick black clouds above her head, instructed her to find her way back to the car, yet she did not know how long she had been walking. It would surely take her a long time to find the black Rolls Royce on the single road in the middle of nowhere. She reached the bottom of the valley as the rain began to fall. She reached up and pulled her grey hat down further over her head and sheltered under a withered tree from the torrent of water. It offered little protection. “Typical!” She screamed “Bloody Typical!” Uttering another curse towards the sky, she pulled a black plastic bag from her walker rucksack and settled down on it. She should probably sit out the storm, the wind was strong and would flush out the clouds in little time at all. It was strange that the sheep did nothing about the weather. One had slowly wandered down the valley slope opposite her and seemed to be approaching her. “Hi!” She motioned to the sheep “Here sheepy sheepy sheep! Come on! Over here!” If it was possible for sheep to glare, then this one did. It was a look that could have turned a butcher vegetarian, as if to say ‘gerr owf my land!’ She knew sheep could not glare, or put on bad Cornish accents. She knew a lot of things, yet chose to ignore them. Doctors said that it was her over active imagination, but she chose to ignore that as well. There was no limit to the amount of things the human brain could ignore to make it self feel better. She had been renowned for her imagination back in the UWF. She would dream about arguing with herself and wake up in such a state. Thank God for Brook, she thought, he had always been around for her. “Brook! Bloody hell I forgot about Brook!” She jumped to her feet and whisked the plastic bag from the ground, shoving it into her pocket. One last glare at the sheep would be sufficient, before setting off to the car. Brook was waiting in the car and she had to get back there as soon as possible. Today was a bad day to take a walk because it was also the day when the inspectors were coming to inspect the damage done to one of her garden sheds. Of course, Lacuna’s garden sheds were not your average sized things, rather the size of a luxury cottage. Lacuna liked big things, big things were a sign of power and status, and she liked that too. What had caused this evaded her, she had made herself and her family were far from made of money, well, before they died. This shed in particular was the closest to the fenced boundary of her land. It had been set alight by some pesky kids, apparently protesting against the class difference. She detested the narrow-mindedness of such fools. It was surely hypocritical how they would complain about the rich for living such lovely, expensive lifestyles and, if put into the same situation, they would do the exact same thing. This had been how the conflict had escalated. Lacuna, aroused from her sleep by a shaken Brook, visited said shed and, I quote, told them to ‘take a bath, get a haircut and stop with the ape impressions’. At this point, she was sure that the one who climbed down from a tree made a very ape-like grunt. Fire: the cleanser, she thought as she battled uphill into the torrent of ice-cold raindrops and biting wind. She reached into the deep pockets of her black and purple waterproof walking jacket and removed a pair of stylish sunglasses. She pulled her raven black hair out of the way and placed the sunglasses on. She was well aware of the fact the sun was hidden behind thick black clouds, but she had to find some way of stopping the rain from contacting with her deep brown eyes. “Fire.” She began, resisting the urge to shout ‘I am the lord of hellfire and I bring you…’ as a prefix “What I could do with some warmth right now.” The wind rattled her head and the cold began to battle with her senses. She could feel herself going numb, but realisation hit her hard. She was close, she recognised the gnarled, twisted, bare wood of a tree growing outwards, across the valley and she knew instantly that she was roughly ten minutes hard walk from the small farm road, currently holding the sleek black Rolls Royce and Brook, its driver. The walk had become harder, the land was steeper as she approached the lip of the valley and the wind had picked up to some ungodly speed. Her hair flailed viciously from underneath her hat and the classy sunglasses were marked from the driving rain. She was wondering what the inspectors would say, there was little left to say now. She hardly cared about the money, it was just a pleasant formality to someone as rich as her, she just wanted to know how soon it would be until she moved the contractors in to build a new ‘shed’ and whether legal actions against those idiotic kids would be worthwhile. Her house in England was her favourite, not just as she was English but also because it was a perfect setting for thinking and relaxing. It may be cold and draughty, but they were minor details. It was also the biggest of the three, quickly followed by her French home and then her American dwelling. It was also the first she brought when the money began to roll in, which should have created some sort of nostalgic feeling, but she felt lacking in that emotion. She had been for a long time; the death of her parents took more of an effect that she had expected, and more than she can even understand at the present time. Still, she felt she owed it to them to make a success of herself now they were gone. This was exactly why she had to get a new ‘shed’. It wasn’t as an extravagant way of showing off her mounds of cash; just a small way of saying ‘look, I’ve made myself, you can do it too’. She was nice like that. Well, sometimes. And now, the car was in sight. It hit her every time she saw it; the sleek and slender beauty of the long black car, the smooth curves and the intriguing darkness. The sides were tainted with the deep brown of the mud path; she would have to get that cleaned they returned home. As if on queue, the balding and overweight figure of Brook climbed gingerly out of the vehicle and opened the rear door so she could climb in. Lacuna began to jog across the road, which was fast becoming a pool of muddy water in the torrential gales sweeping across the Dales. The mud on her shoes slowed her down slightly, and it became harder and harder to move them. The numbing sensation was agony, but she had reached the car. “Welcome, Ma’am” Motioned Brook, waving his right arm in way of greetings “Brook, what have I told you about calling me Ma’am?” She removes her hat and lets her raven black hair flop down over her face gently “I believe you said I shouldn’t do it and if I did you would kick me in the shins” He winces, Lacuna never gives up on her word. So she kicked him in the shins. Hard. Her right walking boot cannoned back from his shin with the force of one of those battle cannon things and she sat down on the luscious leather interior feeling most happy with herself. Anyway, she would create a nasty reputation if she went back on her word wouldn’t she? “You know that was only out of friendship, Brook? I wouldn’t have had to do that if you had remembered to call me Lacuna. Were friends aren’t we?” “Yes, we are Lacuna, the fact you pay my wages makes it all the more bearable” he smiles despite the pain. Surprisingly to some, this was a traditional Lacuna trait, friendship didn’t come naturally to her after all. It had to be worked at, and her work was bringing pain.
