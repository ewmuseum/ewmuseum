Long Road To Gold

12 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/12/04 Part One: Cold Hard Advice It was a hard night to get any sleep. I'd lost a lot recently that I had worked for in a very short period of time. I had spent years wrestling all over the globe to earn respect, championships, and money. In my second year of wrestling, I'd worked in Mexico for a measly 25 bucks a night and a free room in the boss's basement. One night he had a bit too much tequila, and words and blood was shed. I haven't worked South of the border since. But because I stood up to the promoter I earned my respect among the boys. Night after night I would outclass men in the ring and on the mic. Night after night I'd go out with the boys, and have some drinks and hang out with the love of my life during the day. Friends were great. She was great. But I wanted to be a success. The nights rolled on and I realized that while I was a top star of the local promotion, I was merely spinning my wheels in the mud in the big picture. So I changed and I lost friends. I lost her . . . I wouldn't get close with a woman for more than a night. Friends were good for a quick poker game backstage, and nothing more. I dedicated myself to my work, and becoming the best I could be. Now five years later I am in the big times. Many had the goods, and made claim that they were the best thing since sliced bread. But none actually had the nerve to sacrifice what it took to be the best. I'd taken PCW by storm. I made my stance known. I wrestled and talked my way into a title match and won. Before I could continue to recollect I felt a very large hand on my shoulder. I looked up and saw a pair of eyes and smiling teeth staring back at me. I jumped up out of my bed, narrowly missing the top bunk that hovered over it. I was in a small cell and wouldn't have much room to fight. The man moved in closer and I raised my hands, fists clenched. The man moved in closer before sitting on my bed. The light from a barred window shone down on his black bald head. He was dressed in baggy clothing, and looked like a stereotype of a modern African American. He looked up at me and shook his head with laughter. 'I ain't tryin’ to mess with ya' boy. I'm just bored and need some conversation.', his huge voice boomed through the cell and surprised me. I lowered my hands and walked toward the uncomfortable bed. I sat on the edge and as far away from the man as possible. 'So whatcha' in for white boy?', he questioned while I looked at the wall. 'This girl I was about to sleep with turned out to be a man, and . . . I beat her ass..well his ass.' As I finished my sentence a look of confusion spread across his face, and then laughter boomed from his monstrous frame. He slapped me on the back before asking another question. 'Ain't you a wrestler? The Bust or something?' I had a smile on my face until I heard the words 'The Bust.” I tried not to get angered as I didn't need another fine to be added onto my record. 'It's THE BUZZ, and yeah I'm a wrestler. World Champion a few weeks back.' 'And why ain't you now? You look like you can take care of yourself pretty good for a white boy' I leaned back against a post on the bed that held the top bunk up. Before I could reply the man spoke again. 'I see I hit a touchy subject . . . let meh’ ask you something tho'. How'd you get that blood on yo' neck? Did that man get some licks in too?'. The man started laughing some while he awaited an answer. 'No, I woke up with it. Some pyscho nailed me with a belt and a fish hook. But to answer your question I gave the belt up. I didn't like how I won it. I'll be honest with ya' man. I'm an asshole and probably always will be. I had to make myself this way to make it to where I am in the business. I've lost my fair share of great women and friends along the way because of it. But the one thing I'm not is dependent on others to do my work for me. So . . . I gave it up and made a pronouncement that I wanted to earn it by myself.' I stood up and walks to the bars. I grabbed hold of them and looked out into the dark. They were cold enough to send a chill down my spine. In the background, I heard the man stand up and I turned to see him leaning against a wall. 'So what brings you to this lovely cell here tonight?' The man let out a quick laugh before answering. 'It's funny you should ask that. . .You see I lure white boys into a false sense of security by asking them questions. Then . . . ' He walked closer toward me with a sadistic look on his face. 'Then..I rape them . . . then . . . I kill em'!' As soon as I began to prepare myself for a fight he began to laugh again. 'I'm just fuckin with ya' white boy! I'm not in here for anything. I'm an author and I'm doin' research for my latest book. For the last three weeks I've been coming to this cell and talking to the guys that came in and out of here. Seeing what makes them tick. What goes on in the mind of a criminal. I can see you aren't a criminal at all. A troubled man with some priorities out of place, maybe.' He sat down on the bed again, and looked at me. 'I used to be like you when I was younger. Though you had to be cold and heartless to get anywhere in life. And I used to be a criminal myself. I'd sell death to kids, and pussy to the unfaithful husbands that passed by in their minivans. Then one day..BOOM! I found myself in a cell much like this. And I began to question what I was doing in life. It all was just a thought until they slapped me with a 10-year jail sentence for running a prostitution service and possession of drug paraphenillia.' The man laid back on the bed and continued to talk as he stared at the top bunk. I leaned against the bars and paid close attention to what he had to say. 'Yeah, so I began to write things. Letters, poems, songs, stories it didn't matter what. And you know what I discovered? I discovered I had a talent that didn't involved being cold or ruthless. I could write, and when I got out five years ago I pursued it as a career. I've had a best-selling autobiography so far, and now I'm taking an in depth look into the minds of criminals. It's a really interesting subject as you can see.' I looked at the man confused, and didn't say anything. 'I know you don't believe that you don't have to be a complete asshole to be respected and succeed, but you're young. You'll figure it out . . . white boy. If you'll excuse me, I need some sleep since I was awake all last night. Guy before you was a rapist . . . psh and you thought I was scary.' I slumped down against the bars and thought about what the man had said. I agreed with him whole heartedly, but there is a huge difference between being a low life out on the streets and being an asshole in the wrestling business. Either way you have to look out for the most important person. Yourself . . . still maybe even I could change . . . Part Two: 7-11 and Change. I pulled into the parking lot of a 7-11 convenient store thinking about the advice I'd received from the man the night before. I had been thinking about it ever since I was released from the jail cell. I stepped out of the car into a windy cool air. I shivered slightly before walking toward the building, which looked like it'd been built thirty years ago. White paint was peeling off the side of the building, and one of the doors seemed to not shut completely. I reached for the door and opened it. I noticed a middle aged portly woman sitting behind the counter. She wore the standard clerk uniform with her hair in a pony tail. She was smoking a cigarette and looking at a hunting magazine as I began to walk through the small aisles of the store. I didn't know what city I was in, but judging by the generic brand of potato chips, candy, and . . . what was this . . . pickled eggs. AHH I WAS IN HICKSVILLE, USA! I quickly moved toward the soda-pop, and looked for Coca Cola. I grabbed the last one on the shelf and was walking toward the counter when I noticed a price tag that read $5.99. I heard the case from which I grabbed the coke close as I made this discovery, almost like a symbolic gunshot to my wallet. 'What in the hell are they smokin' round here?' I mumbled to myself as I continued for the counter. I placed the Coca Cola on the counter, and the woman was motionless. She kept thumbing through her magazine as I cleared my throat to catch her attention. 'Hey there mind giving me some service?' The woman pushed a button on the register and held her hand out. She spoke in a southern drawl as she said. 'That'll be $6.35, sir.' 'Don't you think that's a bit steep for Coke-A-Freakin Cola?' 'Sir, you don't have to buy our Coke or walk your fine lil' tushy out the door. Just because you're on television does not mean you get special treatment!' I did not know whether I was more afraid about the fact that a middle-aged woman called my tushy fine, or the cockroach corpses that lined the floor behind the counter. 'Are you sure the Coke is clean? I mean I'm not gonna' be sippin' any roach juice now am I?' 'Sir, I do not appreciate your sarcastic comments. $6.35 or I will call the sheriff to remove you!' 'Geez, calm down ok.' I reached in my pocket and pulled my wallet out. I opened it and thumbed through it for the money. I handed her a ten and waited for my change. She pulled it out of the cash drawer, and I walked out with my now treasured Coca Cola bottle. As I walked out of the store, a young man brushed past me. As the wind was blowing his black leather jacket back, I noticed a gun tucked in his blue jeans. I thought nothing of it, and walked to my car. I was wondering why should I try and be nicer to the world when I just got ripped off by the 7-11 lady. I walked around the side of my Corvette, and was reaching for the door handle when I heard a gunshot. My body froze for a second as I heard a man yelling at the clerk. “I WANT ALL YOUR FUCKIN’ MONEY, BITCH!” I looked through the glass door, and saw the man who’d brush past me with a gun pointed at the woman behind the counter. I began to get in my car when a sudden rush of chivalry swept me. I slowly moved back toward the building and began to walk, with my back pushed against the building, toward the door. “HURRY IT UP FAT ASS!” I heard the woman crying and scrambling through her door through the slightly opened door as I reached the edge of the shut door. I thought about what I would do for a second before looking down at the bottle of Coke in my hand. I slowly crept to the slightly opened door, and took a deep breath as I watched the man approach the counter with his gun still drawn. I had my game plan, I would sneak up behind him and pretend as if I was a cop with my gun pushed into his back. As I swung the door open, and began to sprint toward him it didn’t quite go like that. He turned quickly and pointed his gun at me. “SHIT!”, I yelled as I saw the man turning. I had to make a quick choice, and I did what any man would do in my position. I threw the unopened bottle of Coca Cola directly at the man’s head, and knocked him unconscious. I couldn’t believe the scene as it unfolded in front of me, and began to think of possible headlines. “Professional Wrestler KO’s Crime with Coca Cola.” I looked at the fallen man and saw he was about 23 years old. I looked back at the lady behind the counter who was shaken with fear. I walked toward the body of the man, and picked up the gun that laid beside him. I shook my head before picking it up, and walking toward the counter. I sat the gun down and flashed a smile at the woman. “If he wakes up. Shoot him in the leg.” “Thank..tha..” “No need for thanks, beautiful. Just call the police, and forget this ever happened.” I walked out of the store once again, but not before picking my Coca Cola off the floor. I’d spent way too much money to leave that bad boy behind. I thought about what I’d just done. I’d saved the women’s life, and stopped a robbery. Maybe this change thing could work after all . . . nahhhhh. I doubt I could ever redeem myself as a human being, but with this World Title Tournament . . . maybe being a great world champion isn’t such an unlikely goal after all. I got in my car which had turned from black to a dusty grey from the wind swept sand. I began back down the road, and realized that the road back to the title belt was a long one. But, hell, no one else gunning for the big shiny belt had the wrestling stylings known as Buzzoresu. Heh, it was as good as mine.
