Abnormal Conduct

29 June 2002

Date: 6.29.02 (OOC: Not my best...but it’ll work. I have a whole week to gain some information into my thick skull; then, I will try to get back up to my regular roleplaying pace. Take care everybody, and good luck at G&G in your matches. See you next week.:) ) 6.29.02706 pm.Lexington, Kentucky-- No substance has been more avidly sought than gold. A symbol of value for ages, it has been a beacon for explorers and adventurers and a lure for thieves and conquerors. Since the prehistoric day when someone first picked up a shining nugget of gold, this rare metal has been a lure. The first uses of gold may well have been practical. Primitive humans could shape or work it with stones as their only tools. When Christopher Columbus reached the West Indies, he found natives fishing with gold hooks. Similar hooks were in use in remote parts of Colombia in the 20th century. Gold achieved value as its rarity became known. Because of its color, gold was associated with the sun-gods of pagan religions, who represented sovereignty, wisdom, and justice. Eagerly sought, gold was made into idols, offered to the gods, and used lavishly to decorate temples, such as those of the Egyptian sun-god Re. The royal metal was also the adornment of kings; royal treasures of the past have been among the most exciting discoveries of archaeologists. And much gold has also been retrieved from sunken treasure ships. The search for gold has been a major force in history. Men have fought wars and conquered empires to obtain it. Now, quantity of gold lay in men’s way to be dubbed the ‘greatest.’ To prevail among all others insinuates a champion...and a victor with pure glory. -- Along a landscape of vast region, trees and mailboxes mark their spot on the side of Walnut Grove Avenue in Lexington, Kentucky. An elderly woman walks her feline on a leash...yes, feline...as it hikes its leg up and marks its territory on a yellow fire hydrant. What a strange cat....Kris Burden peers from the top of a hotel room sporting his usual garb. Closing the blinds, he takes a seat on the side of his white hotel room mattress. Laying back, he opens the book in which all his writings are stored. -- June 23, 1999 Today I was walking...and I found a couple of kids running. I know I wouldn’t usually start out an entry like this, but it was kind of awkward for a few grungy kids to be running alongside a park bench. I looked up from my resting place on a park side bench to find the boys running to the end of what seemed to be a rainbow. The rain had just let up and the boys were very intrigued about getting there. I listened to their conversation as I walked behind them. “So...John...how do you really know that there is gold at the end of the rainbow?” the first boy said, almost out of breath from the running. “Well, Ben, I’ve read about it. You know, sometimes you can be so stupid,” John said with a push to the other boy. “Whatever...I just want to feed mama tonight,” Ben says to John with a depressed look. I don’t know what caught my eye about it...but I knew something was up. By the look of their faces when we reached the end of the rainbow. A group walked up to the boys, all dressed with draping clothes...sagging off of their bodies. One man began to yell out things about how he said he was going to get the boys back for what they had stolen from his car. I watched with curiosity as the man walked up to the boys, pulling out a silver object...and jamming it straight into the forehead of the first boy...then into the next, quicker than I’ve ever seen. Then, he walked away like it was nothing giving members of his gang a high five. I was shocked...I didn’t know what to do. I guess a couple of old ladies seen the murder and called the police as I just walked away. This really has no significance to anything, but I’ve never seen a murder before in my life. The boys were only seeking gold and glory...but received pain and murder. Obviously, from what I have gathered from it...the boys stole to feed their family...They believed a myth that was not true. I’m not crazy. -- Heh...back when my so-called disorder was bad. I don’t even remember writing that...June, 1999...I was hooked on a drug called purple mesc or mescaline. I remember...that must have brought these strange stories about. What intrigues me now, though...is that this can very well resemble tomorrow night’s outcome. Trigger stole his chance of a one-time match by costing a double disqualification...now he must pay the price for this. Then, who knows? The world will probably fuck me out of this, too. End
