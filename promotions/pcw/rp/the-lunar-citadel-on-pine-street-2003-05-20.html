The Lunar Citadel On Pine Street

20 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5 - 20 - 03 Howling like a banshee in the deepest of agony, the wind blows and whistles through the swaying trees of the surrounding forest. The obsidian stone of the strange structure darkens like a dying light in the dismal gray of the sky, as if connected with the atmosphere itself. But yet the building stands tall against the whipping elements, towering like a sentinel above the swooping grass. A massive wooden door of medieval style construction creaks and groans in the wind, but never budges an inch. A carved moon sits on each side of the door, actually etched out of the wood with a gaping hole left behind. Nothing shines from within… it’s like a door to another dimension… a darker one… In the distance, on down the road running parallel to the front of the building, a pair of lights burst forth from the foggy vapor. The sound of a steady rumbling motor breaks through the screaming winds and is soon accompanied by what appears to be a black Jeep Cherokee Sport. The fog tints the windows and the rapid movements of the windshield wipers prevent anything from being seen within. The Jeep turns onto the rocky path leading towards the obsidian structure and comes to a halt about twenty feet from the door. The vehicle’s engine never dies, however, and the headlights never cease their piecing light. The driver’s side door swings open and a figure wearing a long black jacket with attached hood quickly steps out, slams the door, and darts towards the door. The figure bangs on the smooth exterior of the door roughly, getting a pounding echo from within. A few seconds elapse before a winded voice speaks from within, ‘Who’s there…?’ ‘A loyal follower, Magister. Please allow me passage.’ There’s only the sound of wind for a moment before the raspy voice returns, with the slight hint of suspicion hanging in its tone, ‘I’m afraid that won’t do, my son. Please just le-‘ ‘Pridie Kalends Iune,’ The figure suddenly chimes in with a rushed tone. There’s more silence on the other side, further prolonged this time, before the mighty doors of the structure groan as if near death and slowly begin to sink into the building’s interior. When the gaping hole being created widens enough, the figure steps into the structure. Upon reaching the inside, the doors suddenly crash shut behind the figure, somewhat surprising it. ‘Thank you…’ A light flares on the ceiling and the entirety of the dark room is illuminated, revealing a truly strange visage. The walls are painted with unknown depictions of violence, beast versus man being the major subject. A statue of gold stands like a mighty sentinel against the far wall of the rectangular room, and appears to be a dragon of some sort. It’s surrounded by fiery red torches and a black altar sitting only 5 feet in front of it. The floor is pitch black sans the numerous white and yellow dots that speckle it. A red carpet runs from the middle of the door to the altar, being the only thing of color on the floor. It’s truly an amazing room… and the figure doesn’t seem to be surprised a bit. He throws back his hood and the reason for his calm demeanor is revealed: It’s Dorn Tracer. ‘Magna cum laude, Magister,’ The Astronomer says with a grin on his face. He removes his wind-swept jacket and hooks it on a set of iron hooks adjacent to the door. ‘I had forgotten about the password…’ A man wearing a strange red and blue rosary brushes past Dorn from behind and turns around to meet him, the strange inverted crescent on his head nearly stabbing the Television champion, ‘Forgiven, child of the stars. The harsh attacks on our Citadel from the blasphemous Christian beasts have intensified, and we cannot take chances.’ Dorn’s grin quickly drops into an angry frown and the Astronomer scoffs, ‘So the bastards still haven’t given up? What a fucking surprise. Leave it to the Bible thumpers to pester a peaceful place of religion.’ He shakes his head and stares at the carpet for a moment before sighing deeply and looking back up at the strangely dressed man. ‘I’ve come for the Goddess’ divine guidance, Magister. I’m afraid the pure levels of infidelity are stifling my communication with her elsewhere.’ ‘I understand. Please, tell me of your dilemma,’ The old man cracks, his voice raspy with age and strain. ‘Besides the ever-typical oppression of the majority media and propaganda?’ The old man nods and tries to stifle a grin, amused by Dorn’s comments. ‘Of course.’ ‘I feel as though that my connection with the Goddess is growing wary, and it is clouding my judgment,’ The Astronomer retorts, casting his gaze downwards. ‘I have fallen under the never-ending curse of a cloaked figure named “The Druid”, who has been taunting me and tormenting me for days with no apparent sign of end! Am I being punished, Magister? Has the great Goddess En’Flarim forsaken me??’ His desperate, pleading tone receives a quaint grin from the Magister, who places a hand on Dorn’s slightly quivering shoulder. ‘Now, now child… I doubt that is the case. It is the month of Orion’s victory, and the Goddess is naturally very vengeful. Therefore, certain events might not completely go your way, for the Dragon Goddess’ eyes are elsewhere,’ The Magister tries to reassure Dorn, but he gets a skeptical look from the Astronomer. ‘I don’t understand…’ The man of Astrology places his arm around Tracer and begins to slowly lead him along the carpet towards the altar, still trying to consol him, ‘Do not blame yourself. You know full well of the events that transpired all those eons ago between Orion and En’Flarim. They still disturb her majestic person to this day, so she cannot concern herself with mere followers such as you and I.’ ‘But I am the Lunis Superia, and her personal warrior here! She’s had such a close hand in my affairs, I refuse to believe she’s given up on me…’ The Astronomer seems to be getting a little angry, but it’s not entirely clear as to WHAT he’s getting angry at. The Magister smiles and shakes his head, patting Dorn on the shoulder. ‘She has faith in you. The Goddess loves her children. Just as we love her and all she’s done for us. ‘ They reach the altar and Dorn slams his hands down on it, staring down at the clean, smooth surface with a look of distress on his face, ‘I feel as though I’m drowning in a river of blackness, though. This Druid ordeal is weighing on my mind greatly and the lack of En’Flarim’s presence only worsens it. I fear as though…’ The Magister leans a closer to listen as Dorn’s voice trails off. ‘… as though I might fail…’ The priest of Astrology gasps, but quickly regains his composure and steps out in front of Dorn on the other side of the altar, ‘You cannot say such a thing! Think of how unhappy En’Flarim were to be if she were to hear you speak of failure under her banner!’ ‘I know, Magister… I know… please…’ Dorn starts to trail off, causing the Magister to learn closer to hear him. ‘… leave us. I want a moment with the Goddess.’ Surprised by Dorn’s request, the Magister gives him a funny look for a moment. When Dorn doesn’t respond nor look up, the priest of Astrology nods and walks towards the door in the corner, leading farther into the citadel. He stops upon reaching the door and looks back at Dorn Tracer with a sympathetic look. ‘Poor child…’ [End.]
