Accusations, part 4.

13 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 13th June 2003 :: Suddenly Sivasian looked up. All hope was gone, he thought, he thought he didn’t have a chance. He couldn’t possibly beat this man, beat these 60,000 peacekeepers. Beat the most powerful country in the world. His old country, the USSR may have been able. People may have been able to help him, such as Ivan Stanislav, but even he had given up in beating the Americans. He had, Sivasian honestly thought, sold out completely. But then, they were entirely different people. Ivan hated the USA because they were not communist, they were the complete opposite, they funded anti communist movements in their and foreign countries whereas Sivasian hated them because they had, he felt, killed his parents. Had the Society of Order and Logic really pulled it through? Could they help him get somewhere into finding the killers? He wasn’t, to be hugely honest again, that bothered. He wanted success now, in a direct link with finding the killers. If he could win gold, beat that bitch Tessa Windsor Green, and capture a title, more money and fame would head his way, paving the way for expensive research to be done into it. Once he found the killers of his parents, he thought, he could finally life his live how he wanted, and in peace. Sivasian smirked slightly, in a twisted time of anger, everything had gone, he was done, dusted, in the dark about what to do. How to pull the greatest sniper EVER out of getting killed and being shot against his own will right now. He laughed out loud. He couldn’t help himself. He laughed and laughed until his stomach hurt, until it felt as if someone had been punching his sides. Before he could finish, he felt a sharp pain in the back of his head. A deep pole had been smashed across his head to knock him out. But it wasn’t enough, he had thick skin and it didn’t knock him out. In a horror like movie, he looked towards the man questioning him and smiled, as blood filled his guns and overflowed from his mouth and starting to slowly drip down onto the wooden table. The man behind the desk didn’t break. He didn’t care, he was colder than steel. Worse than a mother in law, by far. He crossed his arms, took a sip of his steaming coffee and cracked his neck slightly. He stared eye for eye with Sivasian, before another harsh shot hit his skull, and he slumped across the table. The man behind the desk asking questions nodded to the security guard, and both walked out the room, pleased they had several hours before a certain sniper would be awake again. :: :: Sivasian woke with two things annoying him immediately. A splitting headache and a huge pain in his head and gums. Pain he was used to, but the headache wasn’t pure pain, it was a nuisance pain. He looked around, a sharp and long painful blare of white light shone straight into his eyes. He could feel the sweat pouring down his face. Surely this was wrong, the way the Americans treated prisoners. He wanted to get up and beat every American in the room. The detective smuggly walked into the room and placed his coffee down on the table. :: Detective: ‘Now Sivasian, it appears that the new relations between ex soviet states, Russia and the USA has got you into a little bit of bother.’ :: Sivasian sighed heavily to himself. Some other reason for a beating, he thought. He didn’t care too much now. It felt like hell, he hadn’t now eaten or drank in hours, and his lips were becoming cracked in the huge heat. :: Detective: ‘Not so much bother really, more a lifeline. For you see apparently we can’t just hold and charge you like we would do to the normal American, or Korean, or…’ Krikorian: ‘I get the point.’ :: The detective smirked. For the first time this cocky, son of a bitch sniper as he saw him. As a guilty man. A man who could only produce the sort of shot that would have killed that American. He shook his head towards Sivasian, then lurched forward. Sivasian had no strength with which to defend himself. The detective hooked his hand under his chin and held his head up. He spoke aggressively and spat a little into the face of the Soviet Sniper. :: Detective: ‘Listen here. Don’t you get cocky. I’m giving you a lifeline, a way out of here, and don’t you give me any back chat whatsoever. You want to get out of here don’t you? Is that what you want?’ :: Sivasian shrugged a little, and spat some blood out onto the already dirty floor. :: Krikorian: ‘Right now, I’d love a drink. ‘ :: A sharp slap took the smirk from Sivasian’s face, and the cracked and dry lips started to bleep. :: Detective ‘I’ve got your attention now maybe? Good, now listen. You have a court appearance. A leading man in the American army over here will listen to your case and maybe you’ll be let free. What’s that? Can we provide you with a lawyer? No, no we can’t. That’s the whole weak link in this deal, you have to find a lawyer wanting to work for you for free. I also suggest, Sivasian, that you find a good one, we’re very, very harsh about these things.’ :: The detective assumed he’d won, and smirked. However, Sivasian knew someone. An old friend. He hadn’t seen him for some 15 years. This man had wanted to marry Sivasian’s wife, tried to marry her ahead of Sivasian. She had shown true love for her sniping husband, however, and married Sivasian. Krikorian assumed that they were still friends. Sivasian had lent him some skills in sniping, and his lawyer friend had said that he ‘owned him one, whenever he was in trouble.’ Sivasian truly assumed that at this moment he was in trouble, bad trouble. ::
