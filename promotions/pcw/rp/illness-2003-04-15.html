Illness

15 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-15-03 The pale contrast of white on white, almost gray, but that wouldn’t do justice to my situation. Lost time, second, minutes, even hours or days sometimes forbid me from moving forward to what I’d mostly like to consider my ultimate goal. Why even try? Why put forth the effort? Every effort I put forth is met with strict and total loss, each and every day, as I walk. The lone road chosen for me, by me, suits me like a poorly tailored suit at times, never feeling quite right. I used to transcend everything that was just and fair in my field, now I feel as if though all of that is overlooked. Effort, however minimal it may seem, apparently is futile at this point. Predetermination is the key, for if one has been chosen before another, prior to combat, regardless of effort, the outcome lays in the hands of others. So I sit here, shaded white on white, in anonymity waiting, waiting for the day when things feel right again, when I believe in my heart that it matters what people do, when once again, effort is looked upon as essential, not just content to make pretty an already gorgeous requiem of history and reputation. Lights flash wildly as A.J. Trinidad sits patiently at a fold out wooden table. Seated around him on the stage are a few PCW public relations gurus, a clump of a man dressed in a Target suit acting as security, and Amira, A.J.’s lone reason for moving forward at this point. A collective of sports reports scurry to their seat, anticipation seems to flood the room as well as excitement, everywhere but in a bubble around Trinidad that quells most calmly lacking peace of mind, but permeating an all around loss of motivation. “Mr. Trinidad!” a reporter seated in the first row shouts out underneath a cheesy, thick mustache. A.J. nods in his direction giving him permission to carry on. Slumped down in his chair, donning completely opaque sunglass from the audience’s viewpoint, A.J. looks as disinterested as could possibly be. “Can I get your feelings on the 4 corners tag match tonight on Tuesday Turmoil?” the reporter questions after standing to make himself the focus of attention. A.J. pauses for a moment, cocks his head back, then offers a trite grin in the man’s direction. “I’m sure that merely by me being present here today, will certainly seal my fate in that match.” A.J. responds in a monotone voice. “Appearances such as these, effort around the organization, these things have lost merit in my eyes. Those who do the least, in the present tense, get rewarded the most, I am incapable of hiding my heart under my sleeve, I’m incapable of not showing up, I’m incapable of not successfully achieving everything I need to put forth to win. Yet………” A.J. trails off as a stocky man in a black suit from down the table shoots him a glare. “Yet…… yet what?” The reporter prompts A.J. along to finish his statement. “Yet, I don’t know,” A.J. admits. “Things happen for reasons that are beyond myself, and most definitely beyond you reporters and media types. I am merely a puppet in the biggest scheme of things and the puppeteer can be cruel at times, this much I’ve figured out in my short tenure here in PCW. I refuse to think, no wait, I know beyond a shadow of a doubt, that I have been outworked thus far in PCW, I know, where I stand in the eyes of my peers! But what I don’t know, and doubt I never will, is the supremacy of my “over” acquaintances, where their loyalties lie, with whom, and for what reasons. In it’s purest form, my job to me is a game, something I love doing, but motivating factors are often times torn away from pawns such as me as easy as taking candy from a small child.” “Moving on,” a female reporter dressed in a navy blue business suit says as she stands up from her seat. “The Pay Per View this Sunday, an actual 4 corner singles match pitting three former DWO champions against one another, then throwing former PCW star Semore Smoke into the fray, does this pose a threat to you?” “The only threat to me, is the one that I pose to myself.” A.J. answers. “People come and go in this business. Before two weeks ago, I only understood why people came in, but now, I’m beginning to finally understand why people “Go”. Should I win the match at the PPV?” A.J. asks more so to himself than anyone else in the room. “Of course I should. I know I’m the most talented man in the ring. I’ve been through more in one life time than these other three men could ever hope to achieve collectively, but sadly, that’s not the point here.” “How is it not the point?” the woman asks again. “The best man wins right? That’s how things are supposed to work.” “You, ma’am, might just be the brightest media member I have ever met,” A.J. says with a smug half-smile. “And you’re completely right, that’s how things are supposed to work. Unfortunately, it doesn’t. For me, this is far past winning and losing, hell, I’ve probably lost more matches in my career than I’ve won, as a matter of fact, I’d be my life on it. The game is bigger now than it has ever been in the past, with more players, more people feeling the power to smite the man who they deem unworthy, regardless of effort. Reputation is far more relevant today than ever before, and it’s a two way street. “Mr. Trinidad, Buster Mackon, I used to cover DWO issues back in the day,” a sloppily dressed man with an obvious wig perched atop his head mentions as he stands. “What has happened to the A.J. Trinidad I knew only a year ago? The man who’s moxie and machismo was rivaled by nobody in the business? The man who had fun at all cost, never got down spirited about anything, ever?” “Ah, yes, Buster, I do remember you.” A.J. addresses. “Unfortunately, the bite can be taken out of the dog, so to speak.” “So you are saying that PCW has completely broken your will to compete?” Buster asks again. “No, PCW is a fed based on reputation and honor. It is a goal of just about anyone competing today to get into PCW or it’s equivalent federations. In baseball, hitters go into slumps, nobody can explain it, it just happens, and I’m not saying that PCW is in a slump, hell, it may be me, but there is something amiss lately, something I can not quite figure out. My heart is still here, but PCW lacks the ability to see that right now for some reason. My effort is still here, in PCW, but it goes unnoticed, again, for unexplained reasons. I love being here, in PCW, but reciprocation has never felt more distant. I guess I should just………just………..just………” A.J. trails off as a blank look comes to his face. “Mr. Trinidad?” Buster asks again. “A.J.?” Trinidad merely goes from slouching to slumping, his head rests calmly on the table in front of him as the cameras in the media room flash wildly and a commotion begins to arise. “That’s all, that’s enough,” shouts the PCW PR rep. “Get him out of here.” Two gentlemen rise to A.J.’s side picking him up and carrying him out of the press conference as a woman’s voice in the back rises up, “someone call an ambulance!”
