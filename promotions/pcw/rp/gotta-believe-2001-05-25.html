Gotta Believe

25 May 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5-25-01 [While in New Jersey, Phantom Phive member, Ryan ‘Streak’ Stephens was asked by a church group to speak on how to become a model citizen and how drugs could effect your life, etc. After doing so the church group asked their youth director if Stephens could go paintballing with them. The director thought it would be a fine idea and Stephens graciously excepted the invitation.] [Fade In] [The glaring sun is beating down upon the scene below, penetrating the already hot camouflage of the people standing in a circle near a barrel fort of some sort, on the edge of some woods. The people are virtually all dressed in army fatigues. They’re wearing paintball masks, which constantly fog because of the glaring sun, and moisture in the air. All of the youth group and Stephens carry paintball guns, and huddle up discussing the game scenario with another man wearing camouflage and an orange bandana signifying that he’s the referee.] [The referee goes on and on about how the game would be capture the flag, 8 on 8, first to nab the other teams flag and successfully take it back to their own teams barrel fort would prevail. When he finished 8 kids begin walking away from the barrel fort through the nearby woods where they would find their starting place. Stephens huddled his team up, and told them that two would stay in the fort, two would go right, to would go up the middle, and he and, he pointed to the smallest kid on his team, Marvin was his name, would go left. Of course all of the kids agreed with Ryan’s plan as soon as the referee blue his whistle Ryan and the little kid began running left.] [Stephens goes in front of the kid so as to lead him, and tries to run slow enough for the kid to keep up, but Stephens is just so competitive. He ends up a little further than the kid does, behind a thin piece of board connecting two trees, when they’re first fired upon from directly ahead. The kid seems kind of scared, and is nestled amongst a bunch of thin bushes, but isn’t receiving any fire probably because the opposition isn’t aware he’s there because he’s scared to lift his head. Stephens urges the kid closer to him.] Streak: Hey Marvin, see that tree right here to my left, the pine tree, think you can get here and give me some fire on these guys. [Marvin doesn’t answer.] Streak: Don’t be scared Marvin, it’s no big deal, they don’t even really hurt very much. [Marvin is about twelve and he’s not buying it. His remains buried amongst the bushes. Stephens fires a few shots at the opposition who have taken a bunker about 50 feet in front of him. He thin crawls back to the bushes where Marvin is.] Streak: Come on Marvin let’s go get them. Marvin: I’m scared Mr. Stephens. Streak: Don’t be scared Marvin, you can’t be scared. I’m in a profession where I face men bigger than me nearly every time I step into the ring, and I have to fight these men with my fists, not with paintballs that don’t even hurt that bad. [Marvin is not convinced.] Streak: Take for instance this Tuesday Marvin. I have to face a man who is two inches taller, thirty-four pounds bigger, and five years younger. He’s probably a better athlete than I am Marvin, and you know what? He’s probably stronger than me, and maybe even faster than me. He’s all the things to me that those guys ahead of us are to you Marvin. Marvin: I don’t understand where you’re going with this Mr. Stephens. Streak: What I am trying to say is, I’m not scared of Rush Mason and you shouldn’t be scared of those guys in front of us just because they’re stronger or faster than you. Marvin, all you have to have is a big heart, and a smart brain and more times than not you can out work, or out think your opponent in order to prevail. I for one intend to do both to Rush Mason, and you Marvin, should help me do it to these guys in front of us. [The shots in front of them have ceased, and Stephens figures they’re moving up to where he was earlier because they think he and Marvin have pulled back, or gone to help another flank. His competitiveness won’t let him just give up.] Streak: I think they’re moving up on us Marvin. It’s fixing to be war in this area, and you know how I was just talking about being smart Marvin? Marvin: Yes. Streak: Well thinking that we won’t get shot laying here amongst these little bushes isn’t that smart so what do you say we slide around and get on that bridge right there to our right? [Marvin still seems to terrified to move, intimidated by the little balls whizzing through the air.] Streak: We can’t just lay here and give up Marvin or we’re sure to get shot. In the ring, I’m not just going to lay there while Rush Mason pins me am I? [Marvin shakes his head ‘no’.] Streak: Battle Marvin, that’s what this is, and that is what it is every time I step into the PCW ring; A ring in which I will challenge myself week in and week out. I am going to lose a few matches here and there along with all my sweat and blood, but if I’m smart Marvin, and if I have more heart than my opponent, then no matter what I’m going to succeed. But if I just lay there and give up then nobody will respect me, and I’ll be a loser. Marvin: I don’t want to be a loser! Streak: I know you don’t Marvin so let’s make a run for that bridge. [Stephens points at the little bridge over a tiny little creek that separates the middle of the paintball field with the left side. Marvin nods and the two stand up and make a dash for the bridge. WHOOSH, WHOOSH. Paintballs slice through the air, coming from the area in which Streak was earlier. One paintball crashes firmly into Stephens knee and he throws his hands above his head, gun and all, and yells.] Streak: I’m out! [Upon doing this we hear various yells coming from what seems to be three individuals camped out at the board between the two trees. Marvin; however, makes it to the bridge without being hit. He looks at Stephens with a scared look in his eye, as Stephens walks off the field. Stephens clinches his fist and smiles at Marvin.] Marvin: Believe in yourself Marvin, just believe. [Marvin most likely feels let down by Stephens, betrayed even, but Stephens smiles smugly. He knows that one day when Marvin is a little bit older he’ll appreciate what he’s done for him, he’ll appreciate the lesson he taught him on the paintball field.] [Stephens continues to walk off the field as the other boys converge on Marvin who is ducked down peeking through one of the cracks on the bridge. Stephens doesn’t even look back as we fade to black.]
