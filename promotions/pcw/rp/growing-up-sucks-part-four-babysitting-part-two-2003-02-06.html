Growing Up Sucks; Part Four. Babysitting; Part Two.

6 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 6th, 2003. Responsibility…yep, still on this thing on growing up. It puts a lot of pressure on people sometimes. They know what they are needed to do, and yet, sometimes they don’t want to. But it’s all a part of growing up. Learn to live with it. Jackson is trying, anyway. Give him some credit at least. It was a dark, cold night on the outside world, but it was a cool, eerie, and yet, so normal feeling dwelling inside of the overly large white tank; a diabolical science lab. The sounds of crickets chirping outside can be faintly heard as several instances of muffled screams are heard in the yonder. Jackson Rust confidently paces back and forth on the polished, white tiled floors, as he pats his white science jacket, placing an sadistic grin upon his unshaven face. His hair was a mess atop his head, as it hangs out all ways. “Are they ready?” he impatiently asks to an unknown listener. The muffled screams are heard once more, before the usual Jackson Rust entourage, the feisty Kimberly, and incompetent Al, the normal guy, Steve, and the unseen to the eyes, the sadistic Narrator walk slowly to where Rust is stood. Three children hang upside down from their ankles in the hands of Al, Steve, and the glove of the Narrator, taped and tied up. Al nods, “They are, master.” “All’s in order, milord,” Kimberly reassures Jackson. Rust brings his arms to his chest, and purposely taps his fingers on the opposing hand, a wry smile creasing his ruggedly handsome face. “Excellent, minions. Now, you know what to do. Do it.” Rust nods in command. Al replies with a simple shrug, before marching off in the opposite direction, holding the struggling Christa by the ankles, as she kicks and tries to scream, before managing to escape the mask that prevented her voice. “Stop your bitching, infidel. You will soon be sacrificed for all of the right reasons!” he enthusiastically shouts, before easily trudging up a metallic stairwell, making his way to the top as the young girl continue her shouting and resistance. “And that time is now, young one,” he says with a confident smirk adorning his face. “What are you doing to me?” she hurriedly questions her holder. “Do you take baths, little girl?” he quizzically asks her. She nods as if saying “yes”. “Well, I suppose you like them steaming hot. And this is what you will be getting!” he shouts, before walking to the command controls on the stairwell, and tapping on several rounded buttons, causing the stairwell to be pushed back, as a shroud of smoke rises up, revealing a boiling chamber of hot water. Christa’s eyes widen immediately. “No!” she screams, trying to get her small voice out, as her siblings, Alex and Evan look to her upside down from below the stairwell. “Yes!” Al screams once more, before simply shrugging, and tossing the young girl by her feet into the boiling vat of water. She lands headfirst upon the steaming liquid, and immediately screams after emerging back to surface, promptly before sinking to the bottom of the vat. Jackson nods in approval as he glares up at his henchman. “Superb.” Her soul was a tortured one. Her hallowed screams will forever haunt the empty halls of the lab. She was the first to meet her maker, and her demise. “Who’s next?” Jackson’s masculine voice echoes throughout the lab. The glove hanging the youngest, Evan, sways forward, as the young boy’s tears drop to the floor; Alex sobbing for his brother’s soon-to-be death. “This mortal is, milord,” the booming voice of the Narrator replied with no emotion whatsoever. “Bring in the dingoes!” shouts Rust, stroking his reddish hair back down, trying to neatly present himself. With his words ringing through the lab, an automatic door swiftly glides open on its hinges, allowing three no-named henchmen pushing a moderately large cage into the main sector. Scratching can be heard from beneath the silk covering that rested on the cage, as growls, and guttural sounds also accompanied the fear factor. “Open,” commands Rust with one word, and a snap of his fingers. The three men whom pushed the cage quickly exchange glances toward one another, before tugging on the silk robe, easily revealing what lies beneath. Rabid, starved dingoes. “No! No! Please Mister, please don’t!” screams Evan as he dangles precariously above the solid flooring. Rust, a sinister smirk on his face, signals to feed the small youngster to the dingoes, by simply pointing to the cage. The Narrator’s glove more than happily carries the small, light child, and simply tosses him up and over the boundaries the cage sets upon the savage animals. “Help!” Evan screams, as he has caught grasp of the cage walls, and is now hanging on for his life, as the menacing animals below leap up, trying to at least nip at the small boy’s heels. “Help!” he yells out once more, but none could help. “Off, bastard!” shouts Rust, startling the little boy, causing him to lose grip, and plummet downward. As he hits the floor, the loud thump follows, before the starved animals leap atop of his small body, and begin to dissect, and rip apart his body. He was four years old. He was the second sibling emotionally, mentally, and physically tortured at the hands of Jackson Rust. He was never to be heard from again. Good riddance. Alex now is breaking down, as he cries into his hands upside down, still in the firm grip of Kimberly. “Release the boy,” commands Rust. Kimberly, without question, quickly nods, and rotates Alex over, and plants him on his feet. He looks around for a moment. “Are you…go-gonna…kill me?” he asks, his lips trembling in fear. Rust smirks, walking to the boy. He calmly looks down at him, still adorning his wry smile. “Are…are you?” he asks once more. “But of course, child. I just needed to do it personally,” he replies, as if it were no big deal. Alex gasps in shock as Jackson delivers one fatal blow, punching the small boy square in the throat, causing him to fly back several feet, before lay dead upon the floor…blood trailing from his mouth. “It’s over.” Jackson Rust: BAH! What the fuck was that?! I AM NO EVIL SUPERGENIUS! Al: You gotta admit, it could straighten the kids out. Jackson Rust: Yeah, but c’mon! My last promo said those things weren’t going to happen. Al: Oh…my mistake. *smirks* Jackson Rust: Jesus Christ, you desecrated my promos! Novel format? What the hell were you thinking? Quality is not something you find in my promos, asshole. Bah! Ladies and gentlemen…I now bring to you, the original promo. Enjoy. The scene (re)opens back up into Jackson Rust’s Louisianan house, sitting peacefully around the Bourbon Street action, but, alas, it is still inside of the home where Al and Steve are collapsed on either side of the long sofa, and Kimberly is wearily trying to sleep, adjusting herself on Jackson’s chest restlessly. The three children: Alex, Christa, and Evan are all about the living room, yelling at each other, much to the dismay of the usual entourage. Evan: Stupid! Alex: Don’t call me stupid, stupid. Christa: You’re both stupid! Jackson snarls, as he carefully picks the light Kimberly off of him, and places her comfortably upon the couch he was resting in, before wobbling to the three little fu—rascals. Jackson Rust: Kids…you’ve been yelling at each other all night… Alex: Because Evan’s stupid! Jackson Rust: *sighs* He’s not stupid. Go to bed. Christa: No! You go to bed! Jackson slaps his forehead. Jackson Rust: I have been trying to…*glances at his watch* For the past eighteen hours, actually. Just go to bed, and we’ll discuss who’s stupid in the morning… Evan: No! Alex is stupid! Stupid, stupid, stupid~! Jackson Rust: Whoa, little man. You’re too young to be abusing the almighty tilde… Evan: Huh? Jackson Rust: Never mind… Evan: Okay. Jackson Rust: Now please…sleep. Alex: No! Jackson Rust: RAR~! Christa: Tessa does that! Jackson Rust: Lemme rephrase, then…RAR~!© Evan: Pooh. Jackson Rust: Now…I’m going to ask you one last time, because this is for your own good…go to bed. Christa: We don’t want to! Jackson Rust: *sighs* Rust calmly walks away before his face has an angered expression, facing away from the kids as he walks to the nearby wall, and flips up the light switch, illuminating the near-black room. Al: *wakes up* No! The chickens are drowning! The chickens I say~! Jackson looks at the awakening Al quizzically. Jackson Rust: What? Al sits up immediately and looks around confused and embarrassed. Al: The he— Narrator: Helicopter. I saw it too. Al: Huh? Oh…yes, censorship. Jackson Rust: What was with the chickens drowning? Al: Err…uh, that’s really not an issue here. Jackson Rust: Whatever…but, in case you were contemplating on thinking of asking of why we awoken you, it’s because the kids won’t sleep. Want to help put them to bed? Al: Not real— Jackson Rust: Great! Al: Huh? Narrator: You’re helping. Al: Don’t you ever sleep? Narrator: And miss and opportunity to continue tormenting you? Hell no. Al: Dam— Narrator: Damocles. Al: Jesus Christ, stop censoring! You said hel— Narrator: Hello to you, too. Al: Grr! Kimberly: *wakes up and falls from sofa* Umm, yeah. Why is everyone awake at…late? Jackson Rust: Kids won’t sleep. Kimberly: Tell them to go to sleep. Jackson Rust: I already did. Asked them, told them…woke you guys up. Kimberly: Let me have a try. Al: Your funeral. She shrugs as she walks over where the three kids are verbally dismantling each other with as high as third grade insults. She coughs, getting their attention. Kimberly: Bed…now. Otherwise, Tessa and Chris will have your heads on their mantles. Good night. She turns around and walks back to Rust, as the kids immediately run to their designated rooms, and no…they don’t pay rent. Jackson Rust: How the--…err, huh? Kimberly: I have power… Jackson Rust: Der…uh…teach me? Kimberly: It’s all about the threats. Don’t take too long explaining yourself, and make “throat slitting” motions with your thumb, as you talk to them. Jackson Rust: Oh. A lesson well noted. Growing up isn’t so hard. Narrator: Not yet anyway. Jackson Rust: That means it will be hard, right? Narrator: Bingo. Jackson Rust: Shit. The scene abruptly fades out.
