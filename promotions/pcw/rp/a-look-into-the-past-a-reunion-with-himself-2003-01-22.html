A Look Into The Past: 'A Reunion With Himself'

22 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1 - 22 - 03 The rundown, shit box of a house creaks and groans like a tortured spirit against the cold, evening wind. A layer of gray dust piles upon the carpet-less, wooden floor and drab walls. The wallpaper is faded and indistinguishable from age while the floor is cracked and dirty with missing boards in a few spots. And that’s just the front room. The hallways are bare from wall to wall. Doors leading to unknown rooms are closed and locked, forever abandoned by whoever used to own the home. The kitchen, from white tile floor to green top counters, is bare, dusty, and stagnant with old air. The refrigerator has been removed along with the stove, dishwasher, and whatever appliances once sat upon these now sad surfaces. The scene is drab and depressing, for lack of a better way to put it. The home has obviously been abandoned for a couple of years, with no new owners since. What does it matter? If home is where the heart is, then this heart must have stopped working. Even the windows are fogged and dusty, making the visage of the outside world very difficult to see. The air in the house is heavy and dirty, due to the ventilation being shut off and the winds of the outside world creeping in through loose bricks and wood. But why does it matter? Who could honestly care? One man does. The creaking front door of the house slowly opens, letting in a long stream of light that snakes across the wooden floor into the next room. As the door opens more and more, a great white light finds its way in and illuminates the front room. A black silhouette resembling a human stands in the gap between the outside world and the aging house, frozen in motion. After a moment or so of rigid surveying, the figure steps in… “Been a long time…” He mumbles to himself in a low, dark voice. “The place has changed from the hole it was before.” He steps into the front room and his features become illuminated now that he’s out of the blaring light: A black leather jacket, baggy fit blue jeans, long jet-black hair tied in a ponytail with a rubber band, and shades. The look on his face explains a lot and nothing at the same time; the place disgusts him. “Seems like the termites have finally finished eating the floor from beneath my feet, as I suspected they would,” The unknown man growls, gazing down at the aged wooden floor. “I was hoping they’d condemn the place and sell the material for scraps.” The man shuts the door behind him, causing a cloud of dust to rise from the floor like a transparent geyser. He continues on into the eastern hallway, his boots tromping against the weak wooden floor. Two doors pass him by and he doesn’t turn a glance. The wooden portal at the end of the dark hallway seems to be what interests him. The corridor gets darker as he moves along, due to no windows being nearby. Upon reaching it, he stands in front of it and merely stares. He reaches out and grabs the brass handle, but doesn’t twist it. Something is bothering him… something isn’t right… A pained look crosses the man’s face and he looks at the ceiling. “Ugh… I remember…” ------------ “I DON’T CARE, SHARON!” “But Dorn, he’s just a boy!! Let him make his own decisions!! It won’t hurt anything!” “I WON’T HAVE IT!!! FUCK YOU!” I can still hear them arguing… back and forth for hours… “DORN! Listen! I know you hate the fact that he wasn’t to join something like that, but if you continue to shelter him away from what he wants, he’ll shy away from EVERYTHING!” “You can’t fucking tell me what’s good for my boy, Sharon!” “Dorn, please!” It’s horrible… I can’t take it. Why are they fighting? What did I do? “YOU DON’T KNOW SHIT, USELESS BITCH!! I RAISED THE KID AND I KNOW WHAT HE WANTS!!” “If you know what he wants, then why doesn’t he want to be a baseball player? Why doesn’t he want to work in the policeman business with you?! HUH?! WHY DOESN’T HE?!” Please… make it stop… “FUCK YOU!!” SLAM! The sound of a wooden door crashing against its hinges with a massive amount of force races through the house. A quick, patterned sound also comes racing through the house, followed by the sound of… tears. Sorrow. Rage. Why are they like this…? How come mom and dad always fight now…? A young man, maybe 14, sits lonely in his room in a red chair. Seated at a white wooden desk, he stares at a blank piece of paper as a lamp blares light and heat down on his head. His room is dark and indistinguishable, despite the light. The only thing visible is the young man, staring at the paper, cradling his head in his hand…------------ “Heh… a lot of bad memories.” The man turns the knob and steps inside, expecting to see an empty, dusty room. But instead, the room is caked in blackness… all except for a single corner. And in that corner, sits that white desk, with that same child from the man’s memories seated in the same red chair. And guess what’s on the desk? A piece of paper. “What the…?!” The man exclaims to himself, wondering what’s going on. His surprise is not ironic, but the feeling washing over his body is truly surreal. It gets worse… the kid speaks. To mom and dad… from your loving son… “What’s going on?” The strange figure asks himself, giving the child a very blank, confused look as he watches him write and talk to himself. I’m sorry I’ve caused so much trouble. I didn’t know what to do with my life and I thought it was the right path to take. You two fight every night, and every night I have to cry myself to sleep like a little baby, hoping it’ll go away. I don’t know why you hate me so much, Dad, but it doesn’t matter… “Oh no…” The man suddenly remembers EXACTLY what’s going on. In fact, he knows exactly what the kid is going to say next, and says it with him in complete unison. “I’m just going to go away.” I’m just going to go away… “This can’t be! Why this again?!” Suddenly, the door bursts open behind the man and a crying, middle-aged woman with blonde hair and frumpy house clothes comes storming in. The young man drops his pencil and turns in surprise to the intruder, wondering what’s going on. Mom? What’s wrong?! “Mom… Mom? Is that really you?” The man says to himself, overcome with utter shock at the appearance of a figure from his memories. “Son, I’m so sorry you had to hear that…” Mom? It’s all right! Why are you crying? “I remember why…” “Oh Dorn, it’s all just too much!! Your father left us again, probably gone out to drink and get himself arrested again! Dorn, we can’t afford to bail him out again if he hurts someone!” The mother cries out, holding her face in sorrow. Small, gray tears pour from the crevices of her hands as she cries her heart out, trying to make it all go away. The child stays up and hugs his mother, feeling a tear of his own trail down his smooth cheek. Mom… it’s ok… I’m sorry, this is all because of me… “NO! Don’t say that, Dorn! Your father is just angry because life is going so wrong for the two of us. It’s not your fault… please don’t think that…” “But I did, Mom… you two always fought about what I chose to do with my life. After awhile I stopped thinking that, but at first I did…” Mom… please… The middle-aged mother of the young man nods and breaks away from the sorrow-filled embrace. She stares deep into the eyes of her grief-stricken son, who’s beginning to lose control of his emotions. His mother gives him a soft, tear-stained kiss on the cheek and turns to leave the room. The man, however, doesn’t want her to go. He reaches out to her, cries out to her! ”Mom! Wait! Hold on!” But she walks right through him… “MOM! Come back! Can’t you hear me…” His voice drowns off as he feels his heart sink into the pit of his stomach. The mother disappears through the door and the man turns back to the child, who continues to write as tears flow from his innocent eyes. I can’t stand to see you cry anymore, Mom. Every night, you have to lie in bed alone, wasting away and staring at the wall just because of me. I’m sorry Dad is so unhappy with me… I’ve really tried to make him proud. But nothing works, and I’m hurting you and the entire family by just being here. The pressure is too much… I can’t stand it anymore. “WAIT!” The man cries out upon hearing the young man’s words. He rushes over to the desk and stands over the young man, shouting down at him. “It’ll all be better!!” But it’s no use. It’s like the kid doesn’t even hear him. He just continues to write, bawling his poor heart out onto the paper. It’s all over for me. I can’t stand it anymore. I wish I would have died on that hospital bed… maybe you two would be happier. I’m so sorry. By the time you get this… your troubles will be over. Your son, Dorn. “CAN’T YOU HEAR ME?!” And with that, the man swipes a hand towards the boy, trying to get his attention. But instead, his hand goes right THROUGH the boy, getting a startled reaction out of the man. The child then sets the pencil down on the desk, and disappears into the blackness. The white desk is the only thing left. The man, overcome with shock, takes a seat in the ominous table and covers his face with his hands, trying to hold back the feelings of rage, confusion, and sadness. “No… this can’t be! I remember… I remember all of it,” He cries, trying to console himself. “I was going to commit suicide because I thought my parents hated each other because of me.” Parents will be parents, my warrior… The man lowers his hands quickly and darts his head around, looking for En’Flarim. His fruitless search just angers him and he smashes his fist upon the desk, gritting his teeth. “HOW WOULD YOU KNOW?!” I watch over the Universe as a whole, if you remember. “But you don’t know what we feel! The pain we go through!” Indeed I do, Lunis Superia, but you will learn to overcome that pain. He shakes his head and sighs, knowing full well that the Goddess is right. The pain of losing his mother to the rage of lightning… his father abandoning the family… his brother dying at a young age… it was all too much. But as En’Flarim said, he’ll learn to overcome it. The man gives a slight nod and sighs once more. “I understand… but it’s just so hard. I became an Astronomer to thank the stars for saving my life, and it ended up ruining a lot of it!” Do not blame your choice of following, mortal. It is Fate, and not even I can control a force so vast. “Whatever…” He grunts under his breath, a little skeptical of the whole situation involving the Goddess and his quest. He turns back to the desk and notices a picture frame sitting on the corner. He picks it up and gasps at the sight of it. “Mom…?” In the picture, it’s his mother with him at a young age, sitting together under a tree. He remembers that day… it was a couple days before the force of nature took her life for no reason. At the sight of the picture and the thought of it all… he sheds a tear… a single, solid, gray tear. [End.]
