005 - The Scarlett Dress; Act II, Scene I

4 September 2002

Date: 09-04-02 Howard Johnson Motel RoomBaton Rouge, LA 9:40 am He walked down that narrow hallway, some twenty feet in length or so how his eyes measured it. Taking quiet steps, he wanted everything to be perfect. Explaining perfection happened to be quite a daunting task by anyone since they couldn't happen to call themselves perfect, nor even superior in a way. He scratched his chin, blinked and holding on to his brown coffee mug. That stench or in his case, aroma happened to be enough to wake up a few people. 'What the fuck do you have in that mug!?' The man smiled to himself, continuing to take the mug along the sweeping hall. A peach colored carpet with twenty-two doors, eleven on each side of the narrow walkway. His eyes were blurry, as in a blind man walking through the valley of light. Starring down, he happened to stay silent, until April Jackson happened to step into the fray. 'Nine-forty-two in the morning and this idiot wakes me up!' She whispered to herself, full well knowing that something is definitely up. The bellhop happened to continue his strut as April had her hair tied with a rubber band, a white pajama top with blue sparkles vertically coming down, with a pair of sandals on her feet. Her eyes are light brown, with no make up on. Its a beauty secret, she doesn't wear makeup at all, except maybe the occasional addition of lipstick. Eyeing the bell hop, she folds her arms along her chest, an impatient gesture on her part as the bell hop eyes her with a glee in his eyes. 'What the hell...' April mumbled to herself before snatching the mug out of the hands from the bell hop. He chuckles surprisingly, before before forced out of the hallway as April shut the door behind her. 'Stupid bellhop.' she muttered to herself, before plopping onto the bed. At twenty-four, she happened to be getting by on her looks, very little intelligence, book wise. But street intelligence happened to run full in her line of work. On her dresser happened to be a telephone, plugged into the wall with the copper cord wrapped around the base, a wallet containing false identification, three-hundred and nineteen dollars, along with a few credit cards she happened to swipe off a few guys she met only nights earlier. She hadn't tried to remember what happened that night at the mayor's mansion, letting the thoughts often slip her mind. But she was always brought back to them, whether or not she'd hope it to be her own likeness of them. She never had a family, abandoned when she was in her young years and thrown away for adoption. She was your typical street daughter, so to speak.A knock on the hotel room door caused her to shake away the perils of her lifestyle and answer it. 'If this is that damn bellboy...'she muttered under her breath, before pulling the door open, finding a man with a gray mustache and shapely brunette hair standing in front of her. 'What do you want?' she asked strongly, rubbing through her hair. The man wearing a black suit with red tie happened to lean against the wall, holding a manila folder in his hand. 'Ms. Jackson, I'm Peter Evans from the Magnolia Police Department. We have reason you are to be in question for the murder of Mayor Tyrell some two weeks ago.' he told her, tapping the manila folder with his right index finger. She struggled to hold her own ground, 'So?' 'So, it means we need to take you into custody. Just to ask you a few questions, that's all.' he reminded her, in that sense that what he was saying needed a voice of reason. 'For murder, I didn't kill anybody.' 'I never said you did.' 'But still,' she began to roll her eyes. 'Even if I was there, you'd be the only person to really prove it. He wrote my name on a piece of paper, so what? You aren't finding any prints of mine on him.' Evans nodded, the fingerprints turned up nothing on the woman, yet what he was wondering is that why was she there in the first place. 'Can I question you right now 'mam?' 'You don't have a search warrant, so no you may not.' she happened to be inside of the Magnolia district plenty of times, almost calling it a home of sorts. The detective knew for a certain fact that he couldn't be inside of the room without serving a warrant, but he insisted on coming in. From behind him, Kendra James happened to step forward wearing a blue business suit and her brown hair wrapped in a bun. She held out the warrant papers, before pushing herself into the small apartment. 'Stupid bitch.' she muttered, before sitting at a stationary. 'All right, Ms. Jackson,'Evans began. 'I know you have been a resident of New Orleans for the past nine years, so I'm not going to bring charges on that up. You were inside of the room where the Mayor was murdered right?' 'Yes.' April nodded. 'Can you tell us what happened?' April placed her right hand under her chin, playing smart. 'Well, from my viewpoint the room was cut into darkness at about 1:30 that night. Me and the mayor fooled around, before I heard a loud snap and he fell to the floor. I began screaming at it, before two silenced gunshots rang out. I went out of the door, covered in his blood. Ugh, what a mess.' She began to tear up a bit, Peter grabbing his handkerchief and handing it to her, cleaning the tears. Kendra sighed, before clearing her throat, insisting that Evans began once more. 'And that's all you know?' 'Yeah, to a point.' she told him, before lowering her head. Kendra got up from the chair, and whispered into the ear of Evans. 'I'll call for the press conference, you finish up with her.' 'Fine.' he nodded back. The assistant D.A. happened to step out of the room, her blue heels clacking against the carpet floor. She had a hate in her eye, yet didn't feel like condemning people that she only felt needed so. A hot shot in her own right, James became only the second female assistant D.A., and was a shoe in for the main job. Back in the room, Evans jotted down the testimonial of April into a small black notepad, before shaking hands with her and leaving away from the scene. As the door closed behind him, April couldn't hold back her tears. 'Why was I so wasted that night?' she asked herself, before slumping on the bed, crying her eyes out. 'Stupid, that woman was right.' she yelled out. 'I'm so stupid...I fell for it.' She whimpered along, before dressing herself in a pair of black sweatpants and University of Tulane T-shirt. These were the daylight hours, only supposed to fit against her other 'normal' life, she thought to herself. For whatever reason it might have been, she shrouded herself from the incident, but Peter Evans still for some reason didn't believe her testimonial was accurate. Three bullet holes were found in the Mayor's head, and a few more on the wall where she happened to leave from. Someone wanted her dead, he was sure of it. * * * Waltz Hotel RoomNew Orleans, LA 8:59 pm It was only nine pm in the evening, yet here I am lying in my bed, staring up at the ceiling. I was beat, no doubt. Working out from six in the morning, to about an hour ago happened to burn me out. But I needed the pain, because it only brought some satisfaction in me. Getting up that early was horrendously ill fated for me, looking at the sunrise and the birds squawking me awake only brought some sense of sleepiness. My mind and body were worn out. Even after pumping iron, running for miles, I still would have to face the facts sooner or later. Ms. James had made a promise to herself that she would bury me before the end of this tournament, making good on her threats. I would bet good money that she would take me out of this tournament and take me right to Louisiana State Supreme Court. But I had other plans in my head. I knew I wasn't going to drop out for anyone, and the only way that I'd be out of this tournament is if I lost. Believe me, I didn't plan on losing, I had enough of that before. So what do I do now? Do I talk to this Ms. James and straighten her out? Do I work out a deal? No, she wouldn't take it, and it would still make me the guilty party, not wanting to fess up to the crime and do the time. She was determined to try harder than any opponent placed against me, to beat me into obscurity. She was going to enjoy it, for a fact, but I was innocent in this predicament, so I had nothing to worry about. I was innocent, right? Right? ...Settle down; I could remember a voice screeching out in the distance, howling in the room as the giggles of a drunken woman happened to ring in my ears. There were only the girl, myself, the mayor, and the bodyguard in the room. He stepped out, fearing for something happened to be up with it. I could only wonder what could happen now, as he tossed her on the bed... ...I had unfinished business with Tyrell, only finding out that the man was to be the mayor of the city. He struggled with her, pinning her arms on the bed, before smacking her around a bit... ...Looking down in my pants pocket, I could see a Beretta nine millimeter flashing, glowing in the darkness while he as busy with the hooker on the bed. I could only glance at it a bit, while she started to realize that he was the drunken incumbent male, and she was his prey... ...I couldn't let him do this... ...Pulling him off, he only looked at me with an odd instance of 'What are you doing?' I only happened to just grunt and throw him onto the floor, punching away, before someone entered the room and fired three shots, one grazing my shoulder, but hitting him in the back of his head. The second, point blank sntaching him off of my body as another bullet went inside. He slumped to the floor, with his eyes recoiling back as the third shot hit the wall, into the stationary desk... ...A shot to my face, before the prints happened to be left on the ground, wiped clean with myself only being unconscious for a few minutes. I stirred awake, looking at the rotting body of the mayor, and the girl realizing that he was dead, screaming out of the room. I followed her, sneaking off into a hotel room, making the mistake... ...That night brought a burden on my shoulder, the same I hadn't seen since March one year ago. It was fufilling that someone had stopped him, before he raped that woman and held me to keep quiet about it, but he died. He didn't need to be dead, but they figured it so he needed to be... ...How could I be so skewed... I woke up in a cold sweat, the blankets given to me by the maid had been strewn around the couch in disarray. I took a deep breath, but nothing could calm me down right now. It had to come so fast I couldn't hold my breath. James was right, and I was wrong. I was the part that killed Franklin Tyrell. The sins of that night, I went back on a promise that I made to myself that I wouldn't go to jail again. A promise that I made to Tracy that her husband would be not known as a jailbird in our marriage. That was the last nail in the coffin, my very own crucifixion. My head fell back on the pillow that night, wanting to sleep from something I couldn't. ...I killed him. I am a murderer... I shook out of it, before I continued to breath in and out of rotation. Something happened to figure inside of me, something I'm not quite sure but something had to give. Defeated, I was not, but I was coming closer towards that. Would I come longer away from this? I got up from the bed and looked over at a black notepad Desmond happened to give me earlier in the day. It was a small scouting report on Andy Murry, Quo and myself opponent and a mystery partner of his choice at the end of this week. It said he was quick, nimble and a superior striker. Do not doubt his abilities, nor speak. Well shit, I don't doubt anyone, but I have to win this one, not only for me but for the people that believe in me. I was confident that I was going to prevail because its my job. I wouldn't have it any other way. I'm Dante Jackson, and I'll do what I do best. Win!
