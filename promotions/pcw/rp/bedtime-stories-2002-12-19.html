Bedtime Stories

19 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 19/12/02 'What's the matter hun?' inquired Judith to her husband Colin. 'What do you think?' he snapped. Judith knew that it was his drink problem as usual that was causing his sleepless nights, causing the physical abuse that he was unleashing on her and little Jake. 'We didn't finish the story last night' spoke Jake to his stepfather, not looking up from his coco pops. Colin smashed his fist down against the table, a large amount of milk splashing from Jakes bowl of cereal all over the surface. 'It's reading those bastard stories every night that's making me lose sleep! You can ready can't ya? You 7 for God's sakes!' 'I'm 6' squeaked Jake. 'That's still old enough!' 'Calm down Colin' spoke Judith softly. 'Shut it Judith..... just....' Colin spoke in between each deep breath. '...Stop, just stop.' Judith smoothed over the bruise underneath her eye that had swelled up considerably since last night. 'Get ready for school Jake' 'Bus doesn't come for another 20 minutes yet.' 'Do as your told!' snapped Colin. Jake, young as he was, amazingly would show no fear to his stepfather. Most of it was due to the fact that he still missed his original father, Simon, who read to him every single night. Colin never read to him though, and when he did he never read the stories right. He'd always skip the best bits. 'No, I haven't finished my...' 'NOW!' Jake remained in his seat and didn't budge until his mother gave him the final chance to escape the house today without being physically abused. 'Run along now sweetie, it won't hurt to be early.' * * * * * * * * * *Colin came in home from work late that night as usual, drunk to the bone and barely able to stand. Judith feared for another night of domestic violence. Colin slowly stammered in through the hallway and into the living room, where Judith tensely pretended to be watching the television, avoiding Colin's attention as best as she could. 'Who's the boy talking to?' asked Colin. She hated it when he called him 'The boy'. Her and Simon had named him Jake, not 'The boy'. However right now was not the time to correct Colin in his drunken state. 'What do you mean hun?' 'He's talking to someone upstairs, he having friends sleep over again? This isn't a damn youth hostel.' 'No ones sleeping up here.' 'Oh for God' sakes. the last thing I need is a crazy ass step son'. Jake stormed out of the living room and up the stairs. Judith prayed that no violence would come of the repercussions of Colin hearing voices from his inebriated state. Surely it had to be because he was drunk, Jake couldn't actually be talking to anyone, could he? * * * * * 'What's going on?' asked Colin standing in Jake's doorway. 'Nothing' Jake replied softly from underneath his bed clothes. 'Who were you talking to, yourself? Listen here boy I won't have a step son who's a frickin' looney and-' 'I was talking to Gideon.' '......... who?' Jake pointed with his index finger out towards a small chair in the corner of his room. 'He says you should stop reading me stories, and start reading that to me.' Colin looked in the corner at the thick dusted over book resting on a small wooden chair that he himself had painted blue 2 years ago. He walked over and slowly lifted it up. On the cover, in a gold old fashioned font style read the words. 'The Tibetan Book Of The Dead' 'WHERE DID YOU GET THIS?!' Colin shouted in horror. 'Gideon gave it to me!' 'Bullshit!' 'Don't swear!' 'Shut the fuck up! I will not allow you to have some twisted imaginary friend!' 'He's not imaginary!' 'He is so!' 'Ask him yourself!' 'What?!' 'He's right behind you!' Colin stopped dead, his heart skipped at least 3 beats and a cold sweat began to form under his armpits and across his forehead immediately. 'Jake....... don't mess around' 'I'm not, he's smiling at you now' Colin sobered up immediately, his drunken state replaced with a fearful one. 'He's waving now Colin. Waving right at you.' 'Jake, please' 'He says your a bad man, and you should read to me at night' 'Jake, your scaring daddy' 'Gideon said it's alright to scare people, he says fear makes us a stronger or a weaker person, he says either way it's good for you because you find out what kind of a person you really are.' 'Jake....' 'He says you're a weak one though, a weak useless man. He doesn't need to sense it on you.... because...' 'I can smell it on you Jake' came a deep mature voice. The voice of Gideon. 'Oh dear God' whispered Jake. A dark liquid spreading across his trousers as he wet himself. 'Ah, now we have quite the predicament on our hands. The last time the boy did that, he told me you hurt him.' 'I-I-It's not true...' 'He says you ask him to touch your, well, you know what I'm talking about Colin, and when he doesn't, you hurt him, he said you hurt him really badly. So bad....that you make him cry.' 'I, no, he's....no' 'Aren't you going to turn around Colin?' 'N-N-No' 'Turn around Colin' 'No' Colin sobbed. 'Turn around now' 'N-' 'Now Colin' 'NO'!' 'Turn around and face that of which you fear Colin!' 'No!' 'DO IT NOW!' Sharply and swiftly Coin turned to face The Shade Gideon Poe. As he did a bolt of lightning crashed from outside, making Colin stumble backwards and cry out in horror. 'Hi Colin' 'Nooooooooo!' * * * * * * * * 'More pancakes Jake?' asked Colin. 'Sure' replied Jake. 'You two seem to be getting on pretty well' beamed Judith. 'Yeah' Colin mumbled, his face pale, and a thin white streak of hair had formed in his hair from the fear of seeing The Avatar Of Death last night. 'Will you read to me tonight Colin?' 'Y-Yeah, sure.' Colin mumbled again. All the while, Judith merely smiled. Convinced she did right in marrying Colin. Not realising that it was The Sinister Paladin himself who had to fight Colin from his domestic reign of terror on Judith and Jake. Yet, this meant nothing to The Shade, all this was, was nothing more than a small feeling of purity and sanity which he knew would soon pass.
