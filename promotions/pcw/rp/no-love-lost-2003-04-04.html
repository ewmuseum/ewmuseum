No love lost

4 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/04/03 March 31st, 2003. “I’m taking five minutes out from training. You know, one disadvantage of this body of mine is that no matter how hard you work out, or how many electrodes you attach to your stomach because the Home Shopping channels say it can zap the fat out of you, or even a matter of liposuction and other drastic medical treatments… I’m carrying extra body weight of a small child in my system that doesn’t slot into place unless I’m in a form that terrifies most of who see it. But I digress, this my diary, so I may as well move onto something a little more relevant. Gaz’s hotel room right now looks like a hundred of me just went through it. Lamps, mirrors, beds… everything’s smashed, slashed or broken into pieces. The one thing Gaz is pretty quiet right now. I’m not exactly sure what’s going on inside his head. I tried to listen in on his thoughts and all I can hear is a mixture of sobbing from his lips and one word inside his head. “Natalia.” Needless to say, his loss is my gain. Wait, I wrote that wrong. I’m not interested in Natalia. No, no, no, I’m talking about all the suffering Gaz is going through right now. His tears are like an all-you-can-eat buffet for me. Remember how I told you earlier that I absorb the negative emotions of humans? Well Gaz is giving me a feast here. Every time he speaks or thinks her name, it makes him cry even more. I feed on that. Every time he looks around the hotel room and sees something… the novelty French Maid’s uniform I bought for him to give to her as a joke, he presses his teeth together and grabs his clothes, pulling at them like it was Natalia pulling at his own heart. I feed on that. And what’s worse is I can make out a few thoughts of his. EVERYTHING reminds him of Natalia and reminds him that they aren’t together any longer because of a prank that went awry. I can’t recall any time I’ve cried since I became a vampire, but I did it enough as a human. And right now, I know he’ll have a throbbing headache and raw marks around his eyes where he’s been rubbing the tears away. The psychological pain right now is greater than pain we experienced the time we were catapulted across the stage by Skitzo into a plate of glass, or the time that both Gaz and I were buried underneath the rubble of the Front Office’s scaffolding in that No Ladders match. And I feed on that. Now I think about it, he reminds me of the time I lost my first girlfriend. I probably cried like this. Bawling, wailing like a child who broke his favourite toy. It was so long ago now that I can barely remember. But this is the state Gaz has been reduced to, a whimpering little boy. Strange that I felt no emotion like that on the date that I had with Teresa Tomas. But either way, I’d better stop writing. As little as I care for Gaz’s emotional stability, I’ve had my fill for today. And at this point, I need a partner who is well-motivated. Time to go share my experiences. After all, misery loves company.” Nerezza replaced the cap on his pen and pushed himself away from the oak table. The vampire’s bony hands tucked the bookmark in place and closed up the leather-bound book. There was still an audible wailing coming from next door and Nerezza had to restore Gaz’s spirits before he lost his mind along with his opportunity to become Cruiserweight or Continental champions. Picking himself off the chair and adjusting the collar of his coat, Nerezza inhaled deeply, more out of habit than need. Marching out of his room, he turned to lock the door behind him, then looked to his right to see Gaz’s door. There was a “Do Not Disturb” sign on the handle, which wasn’t enough to deter Nerezza. However, the sound of breaking objects inside was enough to make him think twice. “Gaz?” CRASH! Nerezza pushed his body up against the door, taking the handle in his right hand and wedging a boot against the bottom. Counting to three, he yanked down on the polished brass and used his body weight to swing the door open. Greeting his eyes was the sight of a broken man. Gaz’s room was an extension of himself. In his bloodstained hand was a half-smashed vase, the contents of which were on the soaked carpet, crushed underfoot and mashed into the fabric. The wallpaper, beds and tables bore deep scratch marks from the weapon, even the windows looked like they had been scratched against with it. Then there was Gaz himself. His dark green suit was cut up with strips of fabric hanging off. His tie dangled loosely around his neck, about to slip off. His usual spiky green hair hung over his face, dripping with perspiration. The dye had almost washed out, leaving his locks a muddy, soggy brown and green. A broken man, indeed. He sat on his bed, head hung like a scorned puppy looking at the carpet. “..what…?” he mumbled with a desert-dry throat. “It’s a nice decoration job. Although we have to move out tomorrow. I’d say a splash of paint could do here or there.” Nerezza grinned, trying to raise a smile on Gaz’s face. “I’m not in the mood for jokes.” Gaz grunted as another tear rolled down his beetroot-red face, wiping it away on a soaking sleeve. “I wish I could sympathize… but I don’t quite have the chemicals in the brain for it. But don’t worry, it’s a small world. You’re bound to meet her again at some point.” Nerezza harshly replied. “Sounds like you have experience…” Gaz trailed off, half-heartedly. “Well… let me take you back. Back before the days of the DWA. Back before we even met. Believe it or not, I was once in love. I doubt I’d be able to even tell you if I was in love these days, it’s a feeling I can’t experience any longer. But just like a certain food you’ve not had in a long time, you can still remember the taste, even if your tongue hasn’t graced it in forever.” Nerezza began, staring at the jagged wallpaper as though it was displaying his past. “I was 15, maybe 14. She was the daughter of a priest. Long, auburn hair, an angelic face, a smile that could even make a demon cry a tear in awe. Of course, she also had the 3-size quality of a model. Her only problem was the one thing I’ve always hated.” “And that would be…?” Gaz interrupted. Nerezza heaved a heavy sigh as his eyebrows lowered, facing his past. “Religion. It’s odd how a 2000 year old fairytale that was mainly used as a means of discipline in the Middle Ages has become such a strict way of life for the gullible. Thoughts of Hell and so on force them to become better people amongst only their elitist eyes. Religion in all it’s forms is merely a convenient excuse. Just as Kannon abused his faith in his early days of this company to sneak to the top, her Father used is as a restraint, a leash around her neck, to ensure she didn’t become too close to …as he described me when he thought I couldn’t hear him once… that devil on Earth.” Nerezza’s voice was starting to show a little more resentment and anger. “Rowdy childhood?” Gaz chirped with a little curiosity. “In my teens, I was still following my childhood obsession with vampires and it showed on my outward appearance. He didn’t like the idea of his daughter being with something that looked so evil on the outside, despite the fact I respected every single rule of her religion. But in the end, like a good little brainwashed girl, she started to follow his law and… well, suddenly the reasons she was attracted to me for in the first place – the fact that I was bound by no laws of behavior or dress code or anything – became the reasons she had to stop seeing me. Ironic, isn’t it? No reason was needed to begin our relationship, but one was needed to end it.” Nerezza stroked his fingers down the side of his face. Traces of a beard brushed harshly against his leather gloves and made a rough scraping. “Rezz…” Gaz growled. “Is this going anywhere? Your effort to cheer me up isn’t quite working, Squire.” “Hold on, I’m coming to it.” the vampire said, trying to draw a little patience from his partner. “Well, just a little bit after you and I left NTICW, I met up with her again, under strange circumstances.” *-* Flashback. Late August, 2002. “I had gone to feed while you were booking the plane tickets. My travels took me to the docks, where I wound up finishing off some drunk who had staggered into the wrong lane. As I left the confines of that little cramped space, blood was still trickling down my chin like a red beard. Then someone called out to me from behind. I thought it was to be my lucky night, but then… oh, how fate can rip a man apart… “Matt?” Came the voice. I froze up. If there had been a witness to the murder I’d just done, I had to make sure that they were silenced too. And then I wondered why they called me by a name that I had long since forgotten and forsaken. All these thoughts swimming around in my head in the time it took for me to spin around in the opposite direction and lock eyes with… those glacial spheres… that autumn hair… it was her. “Matt… Jay? Is that you?” she said again. Guilt swamped me, for the first time since I was first turned into a vampire. “It can’t be… Louise?” I stammered. Now grown from the teenager I knew once, she was a woman in every sense of the word. Her hair was cropped to her chin, framed by two long strokes around the face that dangled pleasantly. Her body was still striking, especially with my mind playing seductive tricks on me as she stood there in a long overcoat that clung to her figure and whispered kinky suggestions of what may lie underneath. “Oh my goodness! I thought it was you! I just had to see!” she cried. It hurt me when she kept her distance while saying all this, but then again, you don’t approach someone when they have a mouth full of stained teeth. It’s not too attractive. “What’s that on your mouth?” “Oh… I… I… got into a fight with someone. Nothing serious.” I swear, if I was Pinocchio, I could have touched her from where I was standing with my nose. “For Heaven’s sakes, it’s practically gushing out! Let me take a look at it!” she frowned. She always had that mothering attitude, but I never minded. She finally closed the gap between us and came up to me, investigating my face. I did my best to try and hide my fangs from her. Heh… ironic that despite me being the vampire and her supposedly being the damsel in distress, I was almost helpless to move. “Funny… I don’t see a cut anywhere.” “Yeah, like I said… nothing serious. It’s right at the back where you can’t reach it. Besides, you wouldn’t really be able to heal it.” I said, trying to back away. I’ll NEVER forget the confident smirk she gave me then. “Try me. I’m a fully classified Nurse now. I’m with the Navy. We’re just stopping here for some medical supplies, then going back to sea.” she smiled, like a little schoolgirl who got a sticker of a gold star on her work. “Hmm… an angel of mercy.” I muttered. “What was that?” she said accusingly, poking me in the ribs playfully. “I said: A change, her at sea…” How odd… we were like polar opposites. After we had broken up, she had gone on to college and learned to heal injuries, taking in masses of hand-written knowledge and applying them to people in an altruistic way. I, however, sat back, squandered my time learning how to hurt people, how to pull muscles, how to knock the wind out of them. For every second I had spent watching someone explain how to take a man off their feet and mirroring them so I could inflict that discomfort on others, she learned how to treat them with kindness and nurse them back to health. The angel and the demon… how poetically disgusting. “So what have you been doing since…” her voice trailed off as she realized what would have come next. Since we broke up. Since she said I wasn’t suitable for her. Since hypocrisy struck. Since she listened to Daddy like a good little puppet. I gave her no lies that time. “I’m a professional wrestler.” “…wrestler? You mean, like Big Daddy and Giant Haystacks on the TV?” she gulped, half scared and half in a disapproving way. “Yeah… but like you can see, I’m not as fat as them. Have you ever seen NTICW on TV?” I asked her, perhaps seeing if I held any place in her life. “I don’t watch TV anymore. I haven’t the time. It’s just sailing around. I think I’ll probably end up buried at sea. So what are you, still pretending to be a vampire?” Her words cut into me like a knife. Not only was I actually a vampire now, but there was a mocking tone that couldn’t be overlooked. Still Daddy’s puppet, no doubt, obeying the little dance he taught her about how Matt Jay was a silly little boy. “Actually… yes. Sort of.” I swallowed, fearing the reaction. And my fears were well-placed. “Playing wrestling and vampires… no wonder we broke up.” … I’ve never spoken with the devil. I don’t do any satanic things and I have no idea what he’d say or talk like. But in my mind, I doubted any words he could say would wound me as deeply as that sentence. “…That was harsh.” “Yeah, well… I just wanted someone who was more in touch with reality, someone more normal. The hypocrisy fell from those scarlet lips like a waterfall of falsehood. How could someone raised around the fanatical adult version of the boogieman be so quick to question my life? For me, there was the proof that I WAS what I was. For her, the millennium-old warning of “If you aren’t a good person, God will get you and send you to Hell when you die” was her entire life… with a few convenient additions and amendments where necessary. “Anyway, I’ve got to go. I was only passing through this way as a shortcut to the Pharmacy. “…Louise… just do me one small favour…” I whimpered pathetically. “What?” “…Give me the hug you never gave me when we broke up. One last hug… for closure. Please.” “…” “…please…” I looked up at her with genuine pain in my eyes and heart. I wanted that final hug, just to end it. To close things on a good note between us. She gave in, and leaned into me, resting her head on the armor I wore on my chest to stop would-be vampire hunters ramming anything into my heart. Not that it had stopped her words already cutting it to pieces. She wrapped her arms around my stomach and squeezed. For a moment, we both became ten years younger and relived the days gone by… I enveloped her in my arms… for a second, I felt almost human again. “…Louise… you’re still a Christian, right?” “Yeah… still one of the big guy’s army.” “May I make a confession…?” “This isn’t the right time.” “Please, listen to my sins.” I whispered in her ear. She stood, still embracing me. “I’ve had… thoughts of murder. Images of violence for so long, a need for blood.” “That’s just wrestler talk… I know you people have to look big and tough.” “No… seriously. I’m… not what you think.” “I don’t understand.” “I really AM a vampire.” She suddenly started to giggle. “Don’t be such a kid. You know there’s no such thing as…” And she pulled her head away from my chest and stared into two red orbs, each punctuated by a solitary black dot and divided by an upturned nose. “And now that all is right… I can send an angel to Heaven where she belongs.” If nothing else, she died in the arms of someone who truly loved her, even if at the same time, he hated what she had become. I reached through her abdomen, plowed underneath her ribcage and literally broke her heart. Just like she’d broken mine. But I couldn’t drink from her. I’d said goodbye to the girl I loved, and killed the woman I’d learned to hate. The most I could to for her was pick the body up and set it down gently into the water. That was when the last trace of Matt Jay was killed… and the being called Nascosto Nerezza finally assumed his place in this body. In a surreal, romantic way… Matt died with her. Present day. “The body was never recovered.” Nerezza shrugged. “That’s when I stopped caring for anything to do with humans.” “Wonderful, Squire…” Gaz sighed. “But tell me one thing. What does that have to do with Natalia?” Nerezza pushed his palms to his thighs and leaned forwards, lifting himself off the bed and yanking his collar straight. He grasped the door handle and turned to face his partner. “Simply this… love and God are both the same. They work in very mysterious ways.” “…is that it?” “Well… either that, or… you’ve now got a wife. Natalia’s now just a ghost from your past. So to Hell… or Heaven… with her.”
