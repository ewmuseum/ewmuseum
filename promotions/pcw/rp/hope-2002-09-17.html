Hope?

17 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 17th of Sept 2002 Why I hate you: A brief reminder. Eight months ago a youthful and gifted wrestler by the name of Titu Xansiv entered a federation named Primetime Championship Wrestling. To say that the Egyptian was of an unwavering shape of mind would be untruthful, but to say that he was better off then than he would be two diminutive weeks later would not. Titu’s fragile mind was unfortunately warped. Not by his own self, not even by the world he thought he was trapped within, but by a being – some may even go to the lengths of calling him a God – by the name of Akira Akiyama. This character, this monster…this God, took the young man under his wing and forged him in a furnace of deceit, calling him a divine creation and ushering his mind into a circumstance where Titu believed he was indeed a deity. It is thankful that the naïve Xansiv unfolded to be more intelligent than he first looked. Only a true madman, he decided, would justly believe him/herself to be some sort of immortal in the elemental hierarchy of living-beings. And so he returned… Akira was banishing people to his personal void and now, Xansiv knew, was the time to strike… Pharaoh set out on his own personal mission and stopped Akiyama from destroying the mortal known as Frisco. Titu saved the Argentinean diplomat’s life and for it he got no thanks, but he did receive what he wanted most - he felt that he’d broken free from Akiyama’s vice-like grip. Shortly after, however, the pain began to grow. Akiyama killed Titu’s best friend and former bodyguard Carmoonz and continued his demonic attack by assaulting, kidnapping and torturing his wife Isiy. The pain grew, and grew, and grew. The disgust grew, and grew, and grew. The hate grew, and grew, and grew. The God and the mortal went to war. The God and the mortal battled. And the mere mortal won. The God…was not best pleased. In the past, all of his nemeses had fallen. All of his enemies had been beaten. All of his foes had been sent to their own secluded hell. All had been banished… aside from him… And ever since that fateful day, when the young Egyptian warrior beat him, the blood has boiled within the veins of the Neo Void God and now the metaphorical bomb has blown… Akiyama has once again taken an interest in Titu Xansiv. Costing him matches, attacking him physically, pestering his mind with non-physical assaults… Akiyama has shown his true colours once again and only The Pharaoh can be blamed for the monster’s rage… And thus the battle draws nearer…again… And so we open up in the confines of the Xansiv mansion. Within the dark domain of The Pharaoh’s mind, the wheels of thought turn upon each other and attempt to process the realism behind Akiyama’s attack on Titu into his fragile brain. A fire burns behind him in a rich stone mantelpiece and a glass of fine white wine sits, unmoving, in his right hand. The door of the chamber opens slowly as Isiy Xansiv’s beautiful face looms into the darkness, an expression of concern about it. “Titu?” she murmurs as her pale blue eyes gaze about the dark recesses of the study. “Yes?” he utters, yet his eyes remain focussed on the blank wall in front of him. “You’ve been in here almost all day, honey. You’re not still worried about him are you?” Akiyama – his eternal torment. At one point he thought Xavier Kannon was the one who fitted the bill. But no, now he realizes that it is Akiyama who has always acted as the proverbial thorn in his side – the one who caused him his pain. “No.” His answer was more than blank. She felt that it had no real stability. She pushed the door open a little further and entered. Her eyes immediately fell upon the crystal wineglass in his hand. Absent-mindedly, he raised it to his mouth and took a sip. “It is him, isn’t it?” Isiy began. “Look, you have to stop worrying about work so much!” Titu chuckled, humourlessly. “You don’t understand!” he bellowed. “Did you see how he handled me on Fusion?!” “That doesn’t matter,” she sighed. “He’s no better than you, and you know it!” “No better, you say?” Pharaoh laughed again, and once more, just like the last, no touch of humour was involved in his snigger. “He’s after me again, Isiy!” She watched as he gulped, his eyes still fixed on the wall. “After me, AGAIN! And what can I do this time, huh? I can’t do jack, because he’s more powerful than he’s ever been, and I am simply a…weak mortal!” Isiy frowned. “Listen to yourself, Titu! Listen to what you’re saying! You’ve beaten him before! You defeated him for the TV title, and you defended it against him months ago, too! You can beat him, Titu, and do you know why?” He said nothing; only the soft wavering of his gentle breathing etched its way past the deafening sound of absolute silence. Isiy’s brow furrowed. “Because you already know him! You already know what he’s like in the ring, and outside it, my love!” “You don’t understand--…” “Oh, but I do! He had me trapped too, Titu. He punished me for your sins. I was more frightened than you’ll ever be of him. And yet I still managed to stand strong and cling to hope.” She sighed. “Titu, you must do the same!” Xansiv’s eyelids clenched together. He knew what he had to accomplish. He knew that he had to take this win – this match – by storm. And he wouldn’t be alone. El Diablo would be there to aid him, and for Akiyama’s evil Pharaoh would punish the Void God. He rose from his seat. “Then I must do what I have to do.” He said. Isiy smiled. Hope had come to him. “Turmoil shall see The Pharaoh’s return. Titu Xansiv is back, and Akiyama is his first priority!” he murmured, nodding to himself methodically. And in the darkness his heart felt warmth. After all, you can’t spell Hopeless without Hope…
