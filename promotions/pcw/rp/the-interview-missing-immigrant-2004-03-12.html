The Interview/Missing Immigrant

12 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12th March 2004 A sudden chill swoops over Eric Sagar’s country ranch. It sits in the piney woods just south of Memphis. The scenery around it is a perfect selection box of hills doing roly-polys, a few carefully placed splashes of trees and it is all being hugged by an emerald carpet of playful shreds of grass. It’s all peaceful and natural. It is the complete opposite to a city life. Along the hills a river shilly-shallies across the tops and dives down into a valley. The sun shines silver sparkles off of the glowing river that glistens in glee. Animals like rabbits scuttle around and be scamps in this, nature’s playground. The only sign of industrialisation is the ranch itself. The ranch is embraced by seemingly endless white fences that span out for acres, inside it there are three buildings. One big long rectangle for Sagar and any visitors he may have; it is a big white rectangular building, inside it is furbished finely - fit for a king. The walls are coated in a light convivially lemon paint. Many rooms. More than suitable for living in. The other two buildings were the worker’s bunks and the animal’s stables. Not much different between the two. Most of the workers are of ethnicity, uneducated and ridiculously underpaid and mistreated. At the moment everyone is out, being used for profit, snorting and grunting out of sheer displeasure. Animals are spread out across the ostensibly endless acres of Eric Sagar’s farm, when they are not needed for profit they jump and skip around their confined playground, but jealously leads them to look over the walls to see their cousins roaming free, on the greener grass than that in the ranch’s confides. Two workers by the ranch’s entrance two scruffy black workers take a few seconds out between the pig sty and the entrance gate. One has skin as dark as the night, his hair is a fuzzy ball of thick black chords going haywire in the shape of a hedge, he is of an average build with a head twice the size of his neck. In fact his head is really quite large in comparison to the rest of his body. He looks like one of the Corinthian figures. He has a white shirt that is coated in oil stains and denim dungarees, which are tatty, dirty and one of the brace straps have snapped. He is relaxed against the pig sty’s fence. His head bowed forward, standing like a drunken letter “t”. The man he is talking to has hazelnut skin. He is of big, bulky build, around 6’7”. He has his shoulders hunched and his fists dragging like a Gorilla. He is dressed in a scruffy black T-shirt and scruffy blue jeans. “Not much dun’ be happenin’.” the larger one oinks. “I know.” “But then again, there aint usually stuff dun be happenin’.” “I know.” The pigs look at the two workers with an understanding glare. Then they just leave them on their own. The silence is broken when a white S.U.V. starts to drive up the road towards the ranch. “Look, there be a purdy lookin’ car.” The larger one snorts in amazement. “I know.” “It must be that thurr journalist for Mr. Sagar.” “I know.” The car drives up to the gate, out of the tinted window a beautiful black haired young lady pops her head out. “Can I come through to see Mr. Sagar?” she asks politely in an innocent English midlands accent. The small one with a big head smiles vacantly as the larger one says “Why, of course ma’am. Go right ahead. His is that thurr big buildin’.” “Thank you.” She replies as she drives. She drives up to the entrance of Sagar’s residence and steps out of her car. She is as striking as a Hollywood star. Her syrupy, wavy, long black hair gracefully cascades down to her shoulders and encircles her diamond-shaped face. Her smooth, clear complexion and high cheek bones are complimented perfectly by her milky white skin. Her eyebrows formed to perfection - slightly arched and they are a shade of thought-provoking brown, they display her emotions by moving up and down as she responds to the world at her feet. Her large blue eyes are so deep you could drown a painless death in them. Her nose bows with grace to portray her as a sweet little girl and it could make the devil smile when she talks. She has a petite mouth summarised by inviting lips that she accentuates with a profound maroon lipstick. She lights taps on the door of Sagar’s ranch house. As she waits for someone to answer the door she hears the slight noise of unholy grinding from within the house, it’s got a rhythm. Grind, grind, grind, grind. It sounds like rods of iron being scraped across a human skull. It is a very unpleasant din. The door opens, “The Reverend” Eric Sagar answers. He is wearing a dusty grey suit, ironed in immaculate condition and a matching tie that looks like a kipper squashed to his chest. An ordinary white shirt. “Good afternoon.” Sagar’s false smile lifts the excess skin his wrinkly Bulldog like face. “Hello, I am Bethany McCarthy from ‘Christians in the Spotlight magazine’, I have booked an—“ “An interview; yes; I am aware. Come through.” Sagar waddles on ahead of Bethany, as he gets further into the room the grinding noise gets louder and louder. Sagar leads her to a proud old dining table, the hot chocolate coloured wood shines underneath the twinkling chandelier that sparkles over the whole room. “Take a seat Mrs. McCarthy.” Sagar commands dominantly as he points to a seat round the dining table. “Thank you.” She sits down in the seat Sagar was pointing at. Sagar waddles over to the opposite side of the table and sits in the chair across from her. “I’m sorry I’m a bit late, traffic has been awful, with Police looking for that abducted Asian immigrant” “I understand.” He says as he fiddles with something in his suit pocket. “Now, Mrs McCarthy. You may start the interview.” He pulls out a silver box from his suit pocket; the box has an intricate design. He flips it open to display five very fat Cuban cigars. “Care for one?” “I don’t smoke.” “Not even cigars?” “No.” “Oh.” Sagar seemed slightly shocked by her response. The old, fat, preacher pulls out one of the fat Cigars, cuts it and puts it in his gritty purple lips. The cigar looks like it could burst out of Sagar’s evil lips – they are bulging them to their limits. “So. Questions?” Sagar hints in a rude tone. “Oh, yes.” She is very apologetic “I’m sorry.” “Just ask the questions.” He barks impatiently. “Right. So, Mr. Sagar. You recently got back from an event in Shanghai, how was that?” “Well, Shanghai itself is an awful place, full of poverty and scum and I was glad to get out of that pit.” Sagar growls in disgust. “Full of chi—“ “I’m sure it wasn’t that bad.” “It was.” Sagar responds instantly. “Well what about what happened in Shanghai?” She asks, trying to change the subject to avoid a potential racist remark. “Ah yes. Shanghai’ed. My client Propaganda beat Chris Sloboda to within an inch of his life. He made him bleed and he won, fair and square.” Sagar boasts. “But wasn’t the match recalled?” “Technicality. All-in-all, propaganda was the true winner of that bout. He beat Boda and then was the most glorious moment in PCW’s history, old and new. The formation of the stable that will prove to be the most dominant force in the existence of this industry. It will mount up like a giant and just squash PCW, with ease! We may be called the Primetime Players, but when we crush them, there will be no “playing” about it.” Sagar gloats and sits back in relaxation like a pig in mud. “Do you feel that any of the past stable in PCW are better than the Primetime Players?” She asks as Sagar’s claims are rather bizarre. “Not at all.” Sagar is confident. “Not even Media?” “Nope.” “The Red Army?” “No.” “Ultimate Authority?” “No.” “Do you ever fear being over-confident?” Bethany asks, rather concerned by Sagar’s over-assurance. “Never, because I know God is in our corner, Sister McCarthy, and I, my client Propaganda and the lord will overcome all evils in the world today.” Eric Sagar scoffs. “Now when you and your client first appeared in THW your reason was to destroy the whole wrestling industry, is that still your aim?” “Of course. Now have you noticed Propaganda’s history? He has been in THW, OSW and PWC. Have you noticed how all three of them fell at our knees? That’s not coincidence. That’s the power of God translated through the irrefutable strength of The Power Himself, Propaganda!” Eric Sagar preaches whilst puffing on his cigar. The room is slowly getting constricted by thick, smog of smoke. Yet that awful grinding noise is still conflicting the silence. “Now, you’ve spent a few weeks out of the States, how does it feel to come back to your home state, Tennessee?” Bethany asks. “Yes. Everywhere else is full of scum.” Sagar puts it plainly. Not knowing what to do Bethany looks from side to side. “Ummm… Care to elaborate?” “No. Next question.” Succumbing to Sagar’s stubborn nature, she moves on. “Now, you are one of the most popular preachers in the world, today, but your methods have been deemed, a tad… traditional.” “Traditional? What do you mean? I still believe in Jesus.” He cackles at his own joke. “Well, you have been accused of being… racist.” She stutters unsurely. “Racist? HA! POLLYCOCK!” He scoffs. “I employ whites AND negroes. And I’m all for equality Mrs. McCarthy, I am, that’s why I let my negroes work longer so they can earn as much as the whites.” “That’s not--” “Yes it is. Next question.” “Right… okay.” She is a bit dumfounded. “Where is your client, Propaganda?” “Brother Propaganda is upstairs, working on a new training method which we have developed.” Eric Sagar states. “Ah, so that is what that grinding noise is. What is your new training method?” She enquires innocently. “Top secret.” Sagar barks. “I understand.” “I don’t need you snooping around…” “Okay.” “…Because I know your sort…” “I’m sorry. It was just a question.” “…Yes and it better stay like that!” Sagar yaps like an angry dog. “I’m sorry.” Bethany is obviously discomforted by the Eric’s strong defence and dismisses it as just trying to have an edge over the competition. “Now this week your client faces Inphino Blitz, wont this be a bit of a challenge considering Blitz’s sheer talent. “Excuse me, Propaganda got seeded, not him. Therefore Propaganda is OFFICIALY the better wrestler.” Sagar boasts. “Yes, but…” “No, buts, there is no disputing about it.” Sagar is as stubborn as a mule. “Oh, uh…” Bethany can feel this interview is going to be a disaster. “Yes…” Sagar beckons her to continue her questioning. “Well, actually, Mr. Sagar…” “Yes?” “I, um, I am pretty short of time. I have an important emergency to attend to.” She stutters, obviously making it up as she goes along. “Oh yes?” Eric Sagar cackles like a sly fox. “It can’t be as important as an interview with me, can it?” “I’m afraid it is… My, job, yes, my job is at risk. My apologies.” “It’s okay.” Eric Sagar says patronisingly “You’re not a very good reporter anyway.” “Uh. Yeah. Bye, thanks for your time.” She says as she quickly sweeps her stuff off the table, clutches them to her chest and heads swiftly to the door. “Show yourself out.” The fat preacher rudely commands. Bethany barges the door open, gets outside and shudders. “Weirdo.” Meanwhile inside Eric Sagar’s ranch house he is making his way up the stairs, on to damp, undecorated corridors, it has obviously not been changed since Sagar moved in a few months back, He then approaches one door. Its haunting nature is daunting, a dim, dark, iron door. It looks like a dungeon door with a window that is barred up. Like something in a dungeon. Sagar opens the door, as soon as you enters the door, the noise of grinding stops; it was coming from this room, then the smell jumps into your nose and rips at your nasal hairs. It smells awful; it smells like blood, sweat and tears. Sagar glances in the room with little dark. All that can be seen is Propaganda; he is in tatty jeans and a white vest, with an Iron bar in his hand. He has blood all over him. His glasses have blood dripping off them, his white best is drenched in it and as are his jeans. “Good work, Brother Propaganda.” Eric Sagar says as a huge, evil grin lifts his face. He looks ecstatic. “Thank you, Brother Sagar.” An awful, quiet nose can be heard. It sounds like an out of tune siren. “Shut up!” Sagar shouts. The noise continues. “SHUT UP!” The noise continues. “BROTHER PROPAGANDA.” Propaganda takes a deep breath, grabs the iron bar with both hands, lifts it above his head then swings at the wall. But on the wall is Dong Zou, the abducted Chinese immigrant. He is naked except for a pair of wide fronts, which are drenched in blood. As is Dong, he is cut to pieces, he is hanging from two chains that are holding his skinny little body up, he looks like he is about to die. There are rows of cuts dripping down from him. He is crying. The iron bar swings straight into Dong’s jaw. It flies out of place and starts to bounce around like a space hopper. It’s a disgusting sight, Dong is like a five or six long noodles hanging off a wall, drenched in crimson paint. “This will teach these pesky immigrants to stop plaguing over to our country.” Sagar looks at the virtually dead Dong Zou. “Scum!” he shouts at the weeping immigrant. “I will make him fell the wrath of God, Brother Sagar. Just like Blitz.” Propaganda demands as he grabs the bar again for another strike at Dong Zou.
