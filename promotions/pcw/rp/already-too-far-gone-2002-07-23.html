Already too far gone

23 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 23, 2002 The fog horn blew again as the ship motored along its journey. I steadied myself by hanging onto the side rail, looking out into the night sky. The air smelled salty, causing me to let loose some sneezes of ULTIMATE PAIN AND FURY~!, although some of it had to do with the cold chill on the ship's side decks. A cool breeze was coming in from the north, and I bundled myself up to shield me. I had not seen either Thinh Alexander nor Robert Yarney, although I knew they were on the ship. We had come seperately, knowing that there were still some ill feelings towards one another. I didn't want to hurt them. They had been my comrades through the hell I've been through in PCW the past few weeks. Without them, I don't know if I'd still be the relatively sane person I am now. Pssh. DarkStar and Duke might damn well be dead now. An overstatement? Hardly. While I remained as a vigil for all that was a part of the Crusader code, now was not the time for me to go gallavanting into the sunset like the Lone Ranger in his last episode. No, the Crusader Code was gone. The other crusaders can hold it up much better than I could now. I can't remain true to it when I have a battle to fight inside of that ring. 'Cold night for you to be strolling outside isn't it?' I turned around to see Raymond Burris coming up behind me. He had a heavy overcoat on and he was smoking his now signature cigarette. He was only forty-two years old, yet he looked twenty-something. Must be the cigar. 'What the hell are you doing here?' I asked in shock. 'This vessel is supposed to be for wrestlers and press only.' 'Supposed to be?' He mocked, sagging a sheepish grin. 'I thought you were a veteran of the business. Money can buy a lot of things, Tom. Even a ticket on the famed Wrestle Vessel.' He took another puff of his cigar and laughed. 'Wrestle Vessel. Now who was the genius that figured THAT one out? Must have been that Phantom Booker guy, huh?' 'What do you want?' He stared at me, a serious look crossing his face. He didn't like my tone of voice apparently. 'Why don't you just sit there and enjoy yourself? I'm not here to play mind tricks with you or somehow cost you a shot at your precious title. I'm here to just keep you company.' I shrugged him off, heading back down the aisle. 'I need your company, or your money.' 'Then who do you need?' He shouted from a distance. 'Thinh? Robert? Who the hell are you going to interact with for the remainder of this trip?' He had a point. I planned on just training in my room, coming out to get some grub every now and then, and staying in my room the majority of the time. It was going to be a tough week, but I'd be damned if I was going to talk to Burris the whole time. 'You can't hide forever, you know? One of these days you have to find a new friend. One that won't feed you to the wolves.' Low blow, buddy. Low blow. 'What is that supposed to mean?' I growled back. My face tensed up as the memories returned. 'Are you going to sit there and try to have me relive RPG3 again? Huh? Are you going to have me spill my feelings about how my best friends betrayed me in the middle of the most important event of my life?' Burris remained calm the entire time, never getting nervous. He was in the entertainment business after all. He was used to stars erupting in front of his scrawny little face. 'You went through a lot, Tom. Most people wouldn't have gotten over that.' 'Well I have.' 'Have you?' He answered back quickly. His eyes stared into mine, trying to find answers that weren't there. 'I see you, and I see talent and a future. But I also see a man that doesn't know whether he's coming or going. Whether he knows who he really is anymore.' I snickered. 'You want me to sit here and debate my growth as a person? I've seen enough shrinks to tell you that I'll never get over what happened at RPG3 that month. All of the shrinks told me that I would get over it, that I need to 'move on.' Well where the hell do you think I got the idea to create the Crusaders in the first place? I needed partners and I got them.' 'And then you dumped them.' I stuck my finger in his face. 'I did NOT dump them. It was your incessant badgering that made me drop myself from the group in the first place.' 'Look at yourself,' He replied, disgust riddled in his voice. 'Like a child. You ever watch TV anymore? All I see are Duke Williams and Dark Age promos ridiculing you every two seconds. They don't know what the hell you're talking about anymore, and honestly, neither do I. I don't know if I'm watching a Legend Inc. promo or the latest edition of Comic Zone cartoons.' 'And now we're back where we started...' He took a deep breath and tried to calm down. 'Listen, I'm not here to preach to you. I'm here to help you. You dropped the Crusaders because you HAD to. A bigger decision awaits you, but we won't know the aftermath of that until the PPV. Now, take this...' He handed me a slip of paper. I was about to open it, but he didn't let me. 'If the PPV goes down like I think it will, you'll need to call that number. Now, try to get some sleep. We'll talk about this in the morning.' He stalked away, disappearing behind a bulkhead. As I opened the slip of paper, I saw a cell phone number and a name. I whispered, 'Now this is EXTREME.'
