Jim the Truck Driver Part Uno

23 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9-23-02 RP 2 Turmoil24th On this rainy Sunday afternoon, not much entertaining has happened. The sky remained cloudy as the rain came and went all day. The dull down-mood aura lingered thick in the September air. By dusk, there will be a cool breeze in the air, but the still dull aura will not have left. Teresa sits alone in a booth at a local Iowa Waffle House. Her mother informed her over the phone earlier that Florida Gators really put a hurting on Tennessee Volunteers. The news didn’t help matters any. It simply added to the list of downers of the long dreary day. College Football is almost as huge in Tennessee as the NFL is to the entire US. One of the biggest rivals of the Vols is the Florida Gators. They rank along with Georgia Bulldogs. These two games are the biggest of the SEC season. The rain looks like beads beating against the Waffle House windows. The sound is a tune all it’s own almost soothing. Teresa sips on a cup of hot coffee when a blue Peterbuilt catches her eye. Her favorite color next to dark purple is blue and to her, this blue big rig is the prettiest one she’s seen in quite a while. Teresa watches as the rig is being parked and a dark haired man steps out. His hair is shoulder length and a light brown suede cowboy hat on top his head. The hat has a ‘redneck’ fold in it. Over his chest, he wears a blue flannel sleeveless shirt. To match, he also wears faded blue jeans, a leather belt with a large buckle, and green snakeskin boots. Teresa turns her head to take another sip of her coffee. That cowboy just had to remind her of Jack with the exception of a few differences. Jack would never wear flannel unless it was over a tee shirt and unbuttoned. Jack’s boots were silver snakeskin and his hat was always black. As for the jeans, as long as they were Wranglers. Teresa turned her head to take another sip of her coffee. She gives the truck driving Mexican cowboy little less thought until someone sat down in front of her. She sets her coffee on the table looking up to see the same truck driver’s brown eyes looking straight at her. Excuse me, are you a wrestler? You look like one I seen on TV the other night. If I’m correct, you are Teresa Tomas. The cowboy driver takes off his brown hat and places it beside him on the bench seat. He appears to make himself quite comfortable sitting at Teresa’s table. A red haired waitress approaches and takes his order, then leaves. Teresa watches the man with a raised eyebrow. She is a bit surprised at his sudden approach. What do you think? Am I Teresa? Teresa says this with a smile. If he’s going to join her, he’s going to entertain her. The man nods not appearing to be the least bit intimidated by her. I don’t think Miss. I know. You are Teresa. I am Jim. Jim extends a hand for a handshake. Teresa glances at his hand a moment the hold out hers for the shake. She smiles already being amused. Well Jim, nice to meet a fan such as yourself. Sorry I don’t have a pen for an autograph. The red haired waitress brings Jim his eggs, toast and sausage with coffee. He smiles to the young woman thanking her then begins to eat. Teresa continues to sip her coffee watching him trying to figure him out. It’s all right. Just to say I sat down with you at the Waffle House is good enough. Teresa laughs quietly and glances out the window. The rain has let up some. She moves her baby blues back to this cowboy. She brings her arms on top of the table and leans a bit closer to him in means of getting his attention. I’ll tell ya what. Give me a ride in that nice looking Peterbuilt you have and I’ll make sure you get special VIP ticket for Turmoil Tuesday. Jim drops his fork and looks up from his plate only to meet her eyes waiting for a response. His dark brown eyes light up. Not everyday he gets a free ticket for anything worth going to. Sure thing. I’ll be more than happy to give you a ride. Teresa smiles and sits back in her seat. After Jim had finished his meal, Teresa grabs his receipt and scoots off the seat. I got the bill. You just get the truck ready and I’ll be right out. Why thank you Miss Tomas! The excited truck driver rushes out of the Waffle House and prepares for his riding partner. He grabs an empty bag and gathers all the loose wrappers and such into it. The now full trash bag is then thrown in a nearby trash can. He then starts the engine just in time for Teresa to open the passenger side door. Before Jim can show her how to get in, she is sitting in the seat and shutting the door. Well, you got this baby fired up, now let’s get rollin’. That a big 10-4 lil’ buddy. Jim puts the truck in reverse and the duo heads out into the highway giving Teresa her joy ride through Iowa.
