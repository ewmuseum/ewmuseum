A date with destiny...and midgets Part 1 FF#2

7 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/5/03 Justice Inc #9 It had been over a hour and nothing. It was slow tonight. Justice Incorporated had already cemented their stature in the fair city of New York and only the bravest of criminals dared to soil the streets. Of course, there was a super villain here and there who occasionally tried their luck, but that was just because they stood more of a chance than the average garden variety villain. It didn’t take them very long to call it quits. What was the point, the last few jobs had been freebies anyhow, assurances by their agent Lusty Linda had made them believe this was a correct way to put their business over. He had pointed out that this was miss-advertising and that the company would require pavement, but Linda was insistent that this be the case. It figures, Blind Rage thought to himself. Typical porn business methods. The team were in what they so liberally called the ‘living quarters’. Blind Rage sat on a chair, with his tree trunk-like legs resting upon the dusty, worn out desk in front of him. Warrior lay on the ground staring up at the ceiling, with a pillow behind his head, trying to ignore the rat nibbling on the toe-end of his boot. Despite protests, Lusty Linda was also working as the secretary, which she thought was a little too much hard work and for some reason sexist. Blind Rage had made her fill the role as a penance, since it was her idea to do a few freebies. The desk was as old and worn as the rest of the ‘furniture’ in the room. Badger Man had perched himself on top of the cabinet in the far corner of the room, absently tapping a pencil on the corner of the wooden cabinet. The Handbag Warrior had took to floating in the other corner of the room, furiously searching inside the bottomless pit of his enchanted handbag. When the call came through on the phone, all four members of the team jumped and looked at Linda in anticipation. However, she continued to file her nails absently. After a second or so she sensed that everybody was looking at her and looked around confused. “What?” she asked, looking at each of them individually. “ANSWER THE DAMN PHONE!” The superheroes yelled in unison, startling Linda so much, she fell off her chair and hit the floor with a thud. Quickly, Warrior rushed over to the phone and hurriedly lifted the receiver, placing it up against his ear. “Is that…uh…is that Justice Inc?” The voice asked, unsure. “Indeed. What is it we can do for you?” Warrior asked. “We have a slight midget problem.” “Well…how big could it have been?” Warrior holds the receiver out in front of him, and places his hand over his mouth in order to stifle his laughter. “Well…huh…I’m in the park and there are about 47 or so midgets causing havoc!” “What kind of havoc?” “Well, just the general kind…one of them took a dump in the water fountain.” “Why son of a…” Warrior composed himself. “We’ll be right there.” Warrior put the receiver back on the stand. The others gathered around him, in anticipation. He cleared his throat. “Seems we have us a midget problem, boys…lets go and kick ass!” The team cheer before rushing out of the door, closely followed by Linda. “What the hell are you doing?” Blind Rage asked, running through the door, instead of opening via the handle. “You’ll need me there. As your public relations agent you’ll need me.” “Why?” “Well the massacre of hundred midget’s ain’t the best way to establish yourself as a superhero business.” ~ “Our plan worked convincingly, Little Willie.” The ‘Outrageous’ Owl-Man said with gusto as he put the receiver of the phone back down. “Get the rest of your robotic men and get your ass down to the park…a massacre is about to take place!” Little Willie sighed. “Yes boss.” He replied, scampering off into the back chamber. “Er…Willie?” Willie poked his head around the corner. “Yeah boss.” “Why have you changed from the white mouse suit, to the purple body suit?” “It’s new gimmick.” “Nothing wearing a purple suit can be described adequately as a gimmick.” “Okay…but I have a slogan.” “Let me hear it.” “Think purple…think Willie.” “Oh dear god I hope them robots are good!”
