The Voice on The Other End

23 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: December 23, 2002 Forty-eight hours, the equivalent of two days, and yet not I couldn’t even manage to put out the slightest of smiles. Sure Christmas was just round the corner and I’d be receiving all sorts of gifts. But even the world could not make up for what I had lost and could never get back. First my grandfather, then my brother, and now two innocent children. Any bit of faith in god that I had left I had lost. I was sure now that there was no such thing as a God, because God would never let such horrible acts to keep taking innocent people who had nothing to do with the evil doings of others. For the first time in my life I had regretted getting into this business, I just wanted to die and die quickly but I would not allow myself to because that would be giving up and that was something I would not lower myself to. I was never one to quit in the past so how could I let go now, I wouldn’t. I had to believe somewhere in my heart and mind that this was all some sick and twisted joke and that somewhere out there Christa and Alex were both still living and just waiting for me to stumble across them and rescue them from the hands of a demon by the name of Shin Yaguu. In an attempt to calm my nerves I collapsed on the bed back at home. Normally I would have gone with Chris back to his apartment, but for the time I just wanted to be alone. I couldn’t stand to let Chris see me in such distress knowing that it my distress would eat away at him as well and leave him feeling no better than I. There had to be some spectrum of hope. I waltzed around my bedroom back and forth not saying anything, just whispering silent prayers to myself of hope. They couldn’t die, not before me; they were much too young and innocent. Finally feeling the weight of the depression I was going through at the moment, I collapsed on the bed closing my eyes hoping that I would never have to wake up again to such a horror although I knew in the back of my mind I would. I would have to live with the fact that innocent children died all because I want to compete in this sport. Sure it brought me recognition and fame that one could dream of and it introduced me to the man of my dreams, but it also took the innocent souls of the beloved children I mothered without having them come from my womb. Laying my head on the feather pillow resting underneath my head, I tilted the direction of my head slightly to the side and spotted the pictures of my angels. Each one of them had the biggest smile I had even seen. Even in the hardest of times when their mother had taken off and their father had died, they somehow managed to keep a smile on their faces. I was sure that even during their final moments on this earth that smile still remained on their faces, even if they were aware of their fatal fate. They can’t be dead. There has to be some way they made it out alive. Maybe this is just one sick twisted plan to get inside of my head. It has to be. They can’t be dead, they just can’t be… The hope continued to burn a hole in my mind as I tried to erase the words and the picture that remained etched in my mind of what took place Sunday night. A hangman’s gallow, Alex’s head, and then a clunk that shattered the silence and broke every last bit of happiness I had. I shrieked and reached out grabbing the photos of the children, cradling them in my arms as I proceeded to cry once more. Hope was gone and I had let yet another person down. It wasn’t just one person I let down though. It was two people, two people who had looked up to me as if I were god himself. My crying was interrupted by the sudden ring of the telephone. Never before had I been so happy in my life to hear a phone ring. I jumped up from my bed thinking that perhaps there was some hope after all. In my bare feet I ran down the carpeted stairs that were beside the door that led to my room. I prayed that whoever it was would have patience enough to wait just a few more seconds and whisper into my ears that they had found Christa and Alex and that everything was fine. Just the simple ring of a telephone had planted the idea in my head there was still hope. Finally reaching my destination, I reached out to the phone snatching it before the person on the other end could hang up and before the answering machine could steal the call. I took a deep breath before answering knowing that this phone call would determine the rest of my evening. I would either spend it in joy or hang up the phone in an even greater depression knowing that with each minute the hope of Christa and Alex still breathing would be worn down until it was eventually determined that they were gone and nothing that I did could bring them back. “Hello.” I answered the phone as the rhythm of my heart quickened as the anticipation grew greater. Who was on the other line and would they bring the news that I had been waiting for? “Tessa?” The voice on the other line answered in a familiar tone. It was a high pitched voice that certainly belonged to a female. But what female beside my mother called me? Certainly no one I could remember.“Who is this?” I questioned, knowing that I had heard this voice before in my life but I just couldn’t trace it back to a face no matter how hard I tried. “This is Alison.” The voice responded calmly, expecting me to know just who it belonged to. “I don’t want to buy your shit so don’t even try to push yourself and your shit on me.” Was my response, figuring that it was another one of those telemarketers who act like your best friends until they push a load a junk you paid good money for, and then ditch you like you’re yesterday’s garbage. I went to hang up the phone but before I could the voice shouted out, “Tessa wait!” I pulled the phone back to my ears thinking that perhaps I was wrong. Maybe it was some cop who had rescued Christa and Alex from the clutches of Shin Yaguu. “What?” I questioned, twirling my finger around the telephones cord nervously. “You don’t remember me do you?” She said, her voice growing quiet as if she were ashamed of herself. “No I don’t but could you hurry this up. I’m really not in the mood for guessing games.” I said getting sick of having to hold back the tears so that whoever this person was would not read into my emotions and want to play teddy bear to me. If anyone was going to comfort me it was Chris, not some random person that I knew nothing about. “This is Alison, Christa and Alex’s mom.” The voice answered in an even quieter tone than before. “Oh my…” Before I could even finish the sentence I had to gulp back the tears that were getting ready to explode at any moment. Sure she hadn’t been there for the kids in nearly three years, but it was still hard to face her knowing that for seven years of Alex’s life and five years of Christa’s she played a significant role in their life and I felt guilty knowing that it was my fault that her children were missing. I sunk into the couch knowing that this would be a long conversation. This would become one of those conversations that seemed to last a life time. I close my eyes listening to the words I expected to hear knowing that things would get worse from here, much worse than I could ever imagine.
