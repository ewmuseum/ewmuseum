What It Means To Have A Father: Starring Clyde and Dark Gimmick

18 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/18/02 We last saw The End speaking with Akiyama and Fate in the Celestial Realm, learning of the course of history. The when everything was said and done, we learn that The End wishes to know about his father. It would turn out that The End’s father, who died on July 4th, 1981, is not his biological father. Though the universal truth will not allow for The End to learn his true father for some time, the search for the father remains ever on his mind. The End never had a father figure growing up, nor does he have one to this day. He has no idea of what having a father, or being a father really means. The following are a series of stories, designed to give a better, complete understanding of what it means to have a father, and what it means to be a father. The first, is an unlikely story, taking place in a father/son focus group…. “Bah!” “Don’t take that tone with me!” The scene opens in a small room. The walls are whitewashed, the formerly white tile floors covered with black shoe-skid marks. The room is a classroom, with the desks arranged in a circle in the middle of the room. The door is at the corner of the room opposite the windows, which give a view of the rooms in the same building directly across from the classroom. Underneath the chalkboard on the right wall by the door there is a snack table with Orange-flavored Hi-C and Ginger Snaps. On the left wall opposite the table, the wall is covered with pictures hung up by an open art class that holds sessions on other days than this: pictures includes a flower vase, a car, a human body in the nude, and the Battle of Gettysburg. A number of fathers and sons sit in the circle, but most notably is the duo of Clyde and his father, Dark Gimmick. Clyde wears the same shirt and pants he wears in the ring. Dark Gimmick looks like Knight Rider in a black leather jacket, pants, and boots. His dark hair covers his face. The Burned Man and Dark Gimmick sit near the door. Wonder why? “Don’t interrupt, Clyde,” says the leader of the focus group, a Richard Simmons clone, seemingly stepping right out of the “Rockin’ to the Oldies” or whatever ridiculous tape he makes where he wears red shirt and shorts cut way too high for him; his brown hair ‘poofs’ out in all directions. “Let your father speak.” Clyde crosses his arms and slumps down in his chair. Everyone turns to Dark Gimmick, who continues with whatever he was saying. “I try to be there for Clyde,” Dark Gimmick says in a dark, yet somewhat sympathetic voice, “but he won’t let me. He’s a grown man now, but I’m still his father, and the things he does I would like to be kept in the loop.” “And what does your son do?” the focus group leader asks. “A little kidnapping, killing, puppy abuse. The usual he always does.” “What?” Clyde breaks in with, “I’m a wrestler.” “Oh,” the group leader says. “And a part-time postal worker,” Clyde continues. The other father/son pairs move their chairs away from Clyde a distance. “And what do you do now?” the focus group leader asks, addressing Dark Gimmick. “Nothing much,” Dark Gimmick responds. The focus group leader nods intently for ten seconds after that. Dark Gimmick and Clyde have no idea what he’s nodding about. Finally, the focus group leader says something. “I think,” he says, “that there’s just a natural problem with communication. I mean, you can’t really communicate with me, how would you communicate with your son?” Dark Gimmick starts to answer, but he’s cut off. “Let’s allow Michael to talk about his experience last week with his son Douglas.” The father, middle-aged and balding; out of shape, the heavy rug of hair on his chest and underneath his armpits showing from his low-cut wifebeater shirt, has his arm rested on the chair to his right where his son should be seated. “I found out from my ex-wife that my son was smoking crack with some high-school kids. I went over to talk with him, but he wouldn’t listen. So I just… hit him.” The other fathers and sons murmur their disapproval. Dark Gimmick, slumped to this point, sits up and stares into the father, speaking at him as much as to him. 'Would you say you have a good heart?' Dark Gimmick asks of the deadbeat father sitting three seats to his left. The father looks over at Dark Gimmick. “Yes, I would,” the deadbeat father responds. “I’m just a little tired.” Dark Gimmick eyes the deadbeat dad with quiet, reserved hostility. Clyde breaks in. “Bah!” shouts Clyde. Dark Gimmick says, “So, you’d say you have a good heart. Would you like us to remove it and show the group firsthand?” The father nervously replies, “Uh….” The focus group leader breaks in. “Clyde! Please ask your father not to make empty threats like that.” Clyde cracks a very slight smirk and says, “Empty threats?” The father gets up and goes over to the snack table, filling his pockets with as many ginger snaps as he can before leaving the room, not taking Dark Gimmick’s threat lightly. Everyone’s eyes are on the father as he leaves. The focus group leader brings the attention back to the circle. “Okay, Billy,” says the focus group leader to a 13-year-old boy Justin Timberlake clone to his immediate right, “why don’t you tell us about your father.” Billy strokes his curly hair with his right and speaks to everyone though looking down at the floor, hunched forward in his chair. His voice is paranoid and anxious, and his mannerisms are not one of a completely “normal” person. Billy’s father just left the room. “My dad, man…” Billy begins, rocking back and forth. “My dad just won’t leave me alone. I’m always just mindin’ my own business, hangin’ with my friends and doin’ what us young people do. And he’s getting all nervous and saying stuff like ‘You shouldn’t hang out with those people, they’re no good.’ They’re better to me than he ever was! They give me the support and family my dad always seemed too busy to do. And he wasn’t even busy, he was out doin’ thing he shouldn’t be doin’ anyway. D’you know he gave me a baseball cap from a strip club as a birthday present? He doesn’t even try. He’s not my father. It’s like it’s too much of an inconvenience to be my dad. He just jumps in whenever he gets visitation, but that’s just some five minute ‘talk’ like they do on television.” {And now, since Mike Frampton is German for the moment – not another multiple personalities gimmick, sheesh! – here’s my best attempt at Billy’s entire monologue in German.} “Mein Vater nur gewonnen’t verläßt mich allein. Ich’m immer nur mindin’ meines eigenes geschäfts, hangin’ mit meinen Freunden und doin’, was uns junge Leute machen. Und er’s erhaltend alle nervöse und sagend, daß Stoff mag ‘Sie haben gesollt’t aus mit den Leuten, sie’betr. kein gut.Sie’betr. besser zu mir hängt, als er war je! Sie geben mir die Stütze und die Familie meines Vater Immer hat zu beschäftigt geschienen zu machen. Und er wasn’t sogar beschäftigt, er war aus doin’ Ding, das er gesollt hat’t ist doin’ sowieso. D’Sie wissen daß er hat gegeben mir eine Baseball Kappe von einem Streifen Klub als ein Geburtstag Geschenk? Er doesn’t sogar Versuch. Er’s nicht mein Vater. Es’s mag es’s zu viel von einer Unbequemlichkeit, mein Vater zu sein. Er springt nur in, wenn er Besichtigung erhält, aber das’s nur einige fünf geringfügige ‘Rede’ mag sie machen im Fernsehen.” Clyde leans over to Billy, looking deep into his eyes. “He angers you?” Clyde asks, expecting Billy’s answer. “No sh*t!” Billy snaps back. “We’ve got a frickin’ genius here!” Clyde lets out a low grunt, then looks out at the rest of the circle. He speaks loudly and openly. “Use fire,” Clyde says simply. There’s a awkward pause, as Clyde is expected by the others in the group to explain his rationale, or rebuke it in favor of something less harsh as what he just said. Clyde instead sits quietly, ignoring all the eyes that are on him. Billy’s father has decided to return. It is at this point that he walks through the door and takes his seat, that seat not as near to his son as the others in the group are. The eyes in the room follow him, but the only truly evil eyes are not those of Clyde or Dark Gimmick, but of his own son. “Welcome back, Chuck,” says the focus group leader. “I’ll have to ask you not to leave in mid-session again.” Dark Gimmick clears his throat in the obvious way to let Clyde know that it’s still his turn to speak. Clyde runs his hand on the burns across his face before continuing. “What?” Dark Gimmick nods his head in the direction of Billy. “Right. Kill him, Billy.” The focus group leader nearly loses it. “WHAT—” he begins, stopping himself. “What… sick freaks… are you two?” Dark Gimmick calmly looks back to the focus group leader. “That’s not very appropriate of you,” he says. “Yes,” Clyde interjects. Dark Gimmick stands and tilts his head to the right. His arms dangle at the floor, and he ever so slowly approaches the focus group leader. “So we’re a little… dark,” says Dark Gimmick. “You’re no better. Look at you, a middle-aged focus group leader, a man that enjoys volunteering at the YMCA just so he can chase naked little boys around the swimming pool locker room. Certainly you are no better than me or my son.” The focus group leader is more than a little flustered. He can’t speak. “This has been quite an informative and revealing session. Compared to you, my son and I are as normal as can be.” Clyde stands up and walks in front of his father, facing Dark Gimmick eye to eye. “You’re right. They are the crazy ones.” Dark Gimmick and Clyde just meet eyes for a few moments, the equivalent of a father/son hug for someone else. “So, son,” Dark Gimmick says to Clyde, with an evil grin on his face, “what would you say to a little father/son bonding?” “Very well,” Clyde answers, managing only a slight grin that is no less evil. “I haven’t killed in a few hours anyway.” Clyde turns around and walks over to Billy. “You have a lighter?” Clyde asks, expecting the answer to be ‘yes’. Billy reaches into his left pocket and pulls out a crack lighter (more intense flame than usual). He gives it to Clyde. Clyde turns his head to Dark Gimmick and nods. Dark Gimmick does the same and exits the room. Clyde then turns his head to Chuck, sitting some seats away from his son. “Use fire,” Clyde says to Billy while looking at Chuck. Dark Gimmick returns with a towel and a plug-in wall lamp. Dark Gimmick plugs the lamp into the outlet beside the door and covers the outlet with the thick towel. Clyde stands on his chair, located directly beneath a fire sprinkler, and uses the lighter to set it off. All the sprinklers in the room begin to gush water onto everything. Everyone in the room is soaked, but too shocked to leave. Clyde climbs down from his chair and walks over to the door, where Dark Gimmick waits for him. Clyde dons the attire Dark Gimmick provides for him. Clyde looks at Billy one more time, repeating the words, “Use fire,” followed by a two second pause. Clyde then adds, “… Like this.” As Clyde and his father leave, Dark Gimmick pushes the lamp over, the exposed bulb cracking and breaking on the floor. The electrical current flows through the filament in the bulb to the water being poured into the room. And excellent conductor, the water sends the electricity all through the water, shocking the people in the room and burning them alive from the inside. Smoke bellows from every orifice, the internal tissues melting away to nothing within the bodies of the victims of Clyde and Dark Gimmick. Screams, deafening, blood-curdling screams emanate from the classroom, a mere bodily reaction to the electric shock. Clyde and Dark Gimmick, a bag of popcorn in hand, watch the sickening display from the window of the first floor classroom. Their bloodlust staed for the moment, Clyde and Dark Gimmick laugh at their latest victims, sharing a moment of, in their own way, pleasant father/son bonding…. The End has been watching his handler write yet ANOTHER RP which in no way features The End within the main story. He shakes his head in disbelief having read the whole thing. The End:“Wait, I’m just checking… aren’t I wrestling against Clyde on Turmoil?” The handler looks back at The End, who is reading over his shoulder at the computer in the campus lab where the handler is working at this moment. Handler:“Yes.” The handler’s friend Fran looks over at him with a confused expression on her face. She is writing a paper for Law & Anthropology. Fran:“Who are you talking to, Sky?” Handler:“No one…” The End ignores Fran and continues. The End:“Then why are you featuring him in an RP for me against him?” The handler scrolls the screen back up to the top to show The End these words that he skipped the first time around. Handler:“The End never had a father figure growing up, nor does he have one to this day. He has no idea of what having a father, or being a father really means. The first, is an unlikely story, taking place in a father/son focus group….” The handler looks back at The End. Handler:“That answer your question?” The End:“I guess….” Handler:“Good… now do you see what you’ve done? This RP ended nearly a full page ago, and you with your dumb@ss questions had to make me drag it out.” The End:“I’m not making you type this…” Handler:“You KNOW I’m compelled to type whatever comes to mind when writing an RP! Now stop talking! This RP is finished!”
