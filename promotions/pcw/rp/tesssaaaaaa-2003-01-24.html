Tesssaaaaaa

24 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: January 24th, 2003 The sun was shinning down on the state of Massachusetts. But with it came no heat, only a third straight day of zero degree weather that would leave you numbed the minute you stepped foot outside the door. But the cold was the least of my concerns. Sunday was coming in two short days and with it would bring the stress of having to render one of my closest friends a loser. Chris or Jackson would have to lose on Sunday and it would be my hands that led one of them to be the less powerful of the two which would surely stir up even more problems. It had been on my mind for so long that I had forgotten I even had a match coming up with Bob Vurdo, but my match with Vurdo was still the least of my concerns, if I could beat him in the past I could surely beat him once again in the future. I sat in the passenger seat of the car as Chris continued down the highway, the kids all gathered together in a back. Quite obviously the length of the drive was starting to eat away at them the way Sundays match would eat at me. “Tessaaaaaaa, Alex is crowding meeeeee.” Christa cried from her seat between both Evan and Alex. “Alex can you move over a bit and give Christa some space?” I asked, turning in back just to see how much space there was between each child. “I’m not crowding her, Evan is!” Alex retaliated with a scream. “Alex just move over a bit then you can have more space for yourself.” I said, not wanting an argument to ensue. “But then I’ll be squished against the door!” He retorted with a pout, biting his lower lip while flashing me his teary blue puppy eyes. “Alex!” I shouted in a curt tone, letting him know that there was no choice in the matter. He would either push over and give his sister some space, or be grounded once he got home. “Fine…” He sighed, pressing the side of his head against the cold window while closing his eyes hoping that any minute now we would pull into the driveway of our home in Massachusetts so that he could get out and stretch his tired legs. I turned back around facing the windshield and the road before us, but before I could relax the high pitched whining of Christa cried out once more. “Tessaaaaaaaaa.” “What now?” I shrieked, wishing just like Alex now that any moment we would pull into the driveway, just so I could have some time to think to myself about the two matches that lay before me in a period of three days. “Are we almost there?” She asked with an innocent smile on her face, knowing that she was doing a good job at getting me aggravated, which seemed to be her goal. “I don’t know. Go to sleep and as soon as you wake up we will be home.” I said, letting my head fall to the side and rest up against the window just like Alex’s. “Tessaaaaaa!” Shouted another voice that was much deeper than any child’s. To no surprise it was Chris’, whose face was buried in a map trying to find out which way to go from here. “Chris just drive! We’ll get there eventually even if we go the wrong way twenty times.” I said in aggravation, tired of all the whining around me. “You wouldn’t snap at Jackson like that.” Chris complained like a child who had been punished unjustly. “Don’t start that now.” I snapped, wishing that this trip would just end so that I could go up in my room and rest, giving me some time to relax before my match with Bob Vurdo later on. “You know it’s true.” He argued back while keeping his eyes fixed on the highway before him. “It is not!” I argued back like a child in preschool. “Tesssaaaaaa.” Two voices from the back cried in a chorus. “It’s going to be a long trip.” I sighed, whispering to myself as the car carrying Chris, Christa, Alex, Evan, and I came closer to it’s destination that seemed be getting further away than closer, and it only made things worse to think that it would be the same way on Sunday, only with two grown men. Oh god it’s going to be a long weekend.
