TPSC - Part III - Visiting Hours

29 May 2002

CAMPBELL The Perfect Storm Chronicles - Part III - Visiting Hours Then.... The Showstopper snickers while he stands and stretches his legs out. Kira folds the papers and sticks them in his back jeans pocket herself. Bobby wraps an arm around her waist and kisses her gently before winking to her. 'I know you love ya some silly boys though.' 'Hehehe. Maybe.' They hug before Bobby walks up the stone set path and into the house. After dropping the papers onto the wood coffee table, Bobby Campbell settles himself onto the genuinely fake leather couch for just a minute. There was still one more important thing he had to tell her. He just couldn't do it right now. Yes... now is the time to go back to his roots, he reasoned. Despite what Kira said to him, right then and there he silently promised himself that from this day forward he would do absolutely anything to protect him and his own. Anything. And now.... * * * --5-27-02, 10:32 pm, last Monday night-- We fade in on the pretty young face of a female hospital receptionist. A receptionist who happens to be standing behind the main station in the hospital lobby and trying awfully hard to look busy when she really wasn't. Not even close. Of course, this is a very good thing. If she was busy, then it would stand to reason that many people would be being rushed in after ending up stabbed, shot, hung, drawn, quartered, crushed, or Gawd knows what else. As it was, she is answering the phone when it doesn't need to be answered, scribbling doodles to make it look like she's taking messages, and telling nurses to go in random directions as if she somehow knew what she was doing. None of these things mattered in the least to Bobby S. Campbell, who startled all nine levels of hell right out of the working class slacker of a peasant when she finally realized he was standing right there in front of her station. 'BWAH-HAA!!' she screamed, unimportant papers flying every which way. The Showstopper couldn't muster up the will to stifle his laugh at the poor woman's plight. Other than this he stands still and calm before her. His flowing red hair acts not quite as wild as usual, pulled straight back from his forehead and hanging straight down just an inch or two past his shoulders as well. Black T-Mac sneakers, tight black jeans, a black and red Orlando Predators Arena Football t-shirt, blue fingerless gloves, and his two constant trademarks make up his attire. His dark silver Steve 'Sting' Borden-like trenchcoat and his lightning-streaked Oakley shades. Don't worry, we can talk about that guy now. He retired. Or so he says. The cute lady looks Mr. Prime Time up and down nervously. These days something about B.S. is unmistakeable. His presence... his aura... his eyes. Even a random and common person could see it. This was not your everyday person. Not anymore. 'M-may I help you?' The lady stammered quickly. B.S. doesn't answer right away. He's too busy looking around. Nurses. the occassional patient. A doctor or two. Two guards just outside, one at least for each floor. Adjacent elevators to the station. The stairs were farther on down the hall, he had already checked them out. He was going to need an escape route, after all. 'Sir?' The lady leans forward and tries again, a little more confidently and a little more loudly this time. 'May I help-' '-Yes.' He interrupts after he finally decided to get around to answering the general annoyance. Silly humans. 'Yes, you can most certainly help me. I have a family member... er... 'staying' here. Am I allowed to see him? Or is the hour too late?' The lady steps to the side and turns. Ah. So that's where the clock was. Obscured by her head. She turns back and smiles that type of infuriating smile that B.S. despises so much. You know. The fake one you have to use in public to prove you're a really nice person? 'Certainly, sir. You'll have to make it short, though. As you can see it's already twenty-five 'til, and visiting hours end at eleven. Your name?' 'Eh?' The sudden question threw Bobby by surprise. 'Oh. Well, it's now Robert Campbell.. but I've had it legally changed, you see.' 'Ahhhh.' She said she understood, basically. Of course she couldn't. Irrelevant, but nevertheless a bit annoying. Why speak when it serves no purpose? 'So you'd have me under my former name. Robert Maxwell Stormbringer. The second.' 'Oh. You mean junior?' There was no doubt by the frightened expression on her face that he was glaring a hole right through her. Between the eyes, to be precise. 'No. I mean what I say and I say what I mean... and I mean the second.' 'Y-yes, I'm sorry.' Bobby snaps his fingers twice. 'You said it yourself, Ann. Time is a-wastin'.' She didn't even seem to notice that Bobby somehow knew her name. Without the help of a name tag, no less. 'Right, right.' She looked through some of those papers that she refused to mess with all night long. 'Who are you looking for..?' His voice already had a hard edge, but now there was cold menace behind it all. 'Randy Stormbringer. He's my... brother.' The blonde-headed Ann looks up at him oddly for the pause. Why would it be so hard to call your brother.. well.. your brother? She shakes the thought away and looks down again to find the room number. Her shift was ending soon, that's all that was really important in her little world. 'Heeyyyy, here it is! Second floor, Room five twenty-two. Burn unit.' A little half-smirk escapes from Bobby's lips. 'Yes. Yes I know. Thank you, Ann. Have a good night.' Bobby left with the conscious thought that Ann was extremely close to the front doors. She would be fine. He slipped quietly and without much fanfare into the second floor. Just like his dream several nights before, no one paid him much mind. The conversation with Ann had been exactly the same. So his suspicions were correct about all this.... at least so far. He came to Randy's door and slowly opened it. The monitors beeped their usual beeps. Everything was clean. Pristine. Everything but Randy, anyway. He was wrapped up. His broken leg put up in a sling. Hard cast. The flowers from his separated wife. A few cards from the lawyer buddies back at work. That was all. Just like he saw it in the dream. He quietly stepped right back outside. He also knew of the fire alarm not even ten yards away from Randy's door. With a satisfied and almost smug look on his face, he slips his new black bat out of his trench and smashes the alarm in without hesitation. Blaring alarms, shouts of alert. No real panic in the air, these sorts of false alarms happen all the time anyway. B.S. would need chaos to do what he needed to do. Twenty minutes? He wouldn't even need half that time to do what was needed to be done. B.S. slips himself back inside Randy's place of solitude and closes the door behind him with the softest of clicks. The scene fades to black because you don't need to see the next part. No really. You don't. TO BE CONTINUED. THIS HAS BEEN A BOTS PRODUCTION. (c) 2002. ALL RIGHTS RESERVED.
