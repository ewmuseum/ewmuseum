Genesis [1]

14 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 14th, 2003 Tap. It seems like wherever I may roam around in the parts of PCW, there is always something, some part or aurora that follows like a plague. Sure, from the moment I set foot here in PCW, its all be a building block of success, pitfalls, and enemies coming my way. But now it seems the norm for everybody from Skylar Green to even his green haired brother Gaz that the revelations of their past actions in federations which are now probably closed at the current point, or still open, breathing without a respirator and on life support. Yes, for every ending to one story, there will forever be a beginning into what actually transpired during the course from alpha to omega. It has been such a strong forthcoming since I’ve been in PCW, a full year of active Wrestling Online membership. Yet, before there was a Primetime Wrestling Academy, or even a Primetime Championship Wrestling for that matter, I started out back when people used to call the Internet Wrestling Organization the crème of the proverbial crop. Men like Titan, an emerging Joey Malone and a blooming Syphon Fission stood out, while men like Evan Levine (not the G.O.D version), Phelen Kell, and the Hardcore Isosceles Trapezoid began to make their final passage through the illustrious and sometimes troubling federation. Sensing a course for the chance, I signed up for the gala, the event so if you can say after Ice Age 2000. That same night Kell and Levine went through each other like Damocles sword to covet the most sought after prize in all of wrestling – the IWO World Heavyweight Championship. Men drooled, almost even killed themselves and tried to even shoot their way to the top. I thought I was ready, but with no experience whatsoever, I seriously took in jeopardy just what was the chance of a rookie showing up almost everybody in attendance in order to make it seem he was the diamond in the rough. Brandon Baller, a man consumed with the ideology that diamonds, gold, and whatever was the namesake that a man needed in order to make himself seem better. I got the notice back in the mail a few days later, as the New Year approached. Nothing rejected without the opening of a tab. So then, I thought about possible names to get me over, enlisting a few of my old New York friends to even more indulge in my chances. Race & Tyler Smith, otherwise known as… The Southside Playaz. Now, they had gotten in, usually off of the tomfoolery of being jocks. They were set to even break off in their very first match against a team who had some odd circumstances to even lead them into the IWO. Yet, the two teams weren’t suited for IWO material, road agents say… “Their ring work is completely lackluster at best.” “So, where are we sending them?” “To Gunner, maybe he’ll know something to do with them.” Boom. They all get sent to former IWO World Heavyweight Champion Gunner “G-Dogg” Smith, who headed up the Internet Wrestling Organization Minor League or IML for short. It was easily thought for both men to even work the mid-card style, or even the upper card. Thus, the first card, they faced off. During the night, I sent in my registration for the IML, sending my work VISA, just about everything and anything I could use in order to get there and be done with it. Then, while trying to even be close and thinking about it, I’m there. The Playaz lost, and I got a job. Right there with my friends Race and Tyler as we formed the original Playaz Inc. I told them I was from Atlana, basically at a bar for my first promo where I did nothing except talk crap about people. Me and Bryan Fury (took a character from the Tekken series and molded it) meshed into a little feud where nothing would be just about the same. We talked, and talked, and talked, until A Cold Day in Hell arrives. This was my first title match and the simple fact that Fury was also in the match made it even sweeter. One problem though: El Bastardo Loco. Now, I can say that Loco is the precursor towards Flaymin Aymin, only difference was him being Mexican. Once there, we faced off in a 10 Man battle royal for the IML Television Title. In my first match, I won and cemented myself as a name. The next Malice which was on a Friday, good time for Gunner to tell me I have to defend the title, and try to go over even further. I wasn’t betrayed or anything, but I was completely off focus that night. And I lost the title without a single word. To Erik Blake. And to say that was the low point of my career, it wasn’t. From then, it became worse. Week, after week, it became more and more frustrating to even operate. I was still under the same guideline of trying to trash talk my way towards the top yet it would never be more successful than it was then.I lost to Kent Anthason. I lost to Scott Styles. I lost to The Deadly Sins. The losses piled up, until after the March Pay Per View event, I left. Gave them my walking papers and served them notice as I left the company. Whether it was going to be on good terms or not, I felt that I wasn’t going to embarrass myself week in and week out. That one seemingly odd ball movement put me under a microscope if I could even compete in the sport – so I took a full year off. And in that time span, I could have come back, took it step by step and won a few matches or titles. Come into PCW with a larger fan fare, but I didn’t. But by April 2002, the rapper and person began to take shape.
