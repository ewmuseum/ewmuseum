Bygone Inscription Chronicles: Prelude

12 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7.12.02 7.12.029:16 pm.Oxford, Mississippi-- The stars in the sky are overlooking the beautiful Mississippi River as I watch from the riverbank. I sit on the edge of the river, skipping stones across the flat current. I can see straight across the river in Oxford, remembering of how it was back in the older times. You can see the carriages led by the horses going down a dirt road. Or some Indians making campground on the side of the bank, with smiles on their faces...the happy times. I don’t smile, though...there’s nothing to smile about when you remember what your mother told you as a child. I can still remember the exact words that she told me when we took our family vacation to Oxford... I can still remember my young voice ringing out in my grandparent’s guestroom as the shots ran through the air. “Mommy, why is everybody fighting the Negroes?” “Kristopher...it’s ok, baby. Everything will be all right. Negroes aren’t the same as us. They’re different, honey. Not just the color of their skin, but the way they speak and their actions. It’s just the way that it’s always been...and some people don’t like them for that.” I didn’t understand...why would a group of people be treated so differently due to the color of their skin? I remember a tear ran down my face as she tucked me into bed, giving me a kiss on the cheek. I wiped it off asking her, “Why do they have to kill them, though?” “Honey, there’s nothing I can do. We just have to sit here and pray that they won’t come in here. Daddy assures us not to mess with the Klan...just don‘t think about it...it‘ll be over soon.” A tear ran down her face as she lay with me in bed, holding my ears from the violent sounds. There’s nothing to be happy about here in the land of the Dixie. Sure, a lot has changed, but I’ll never forget the image I saw that night in my bedroom window. The image of the burning cross...the image of two men hung from branches....and nothing was done about it. I take my glance away from the other side of the bank and shake it off. For some odd reason, I haven’t been able to keep my eyes out of my black journal. It’s like I can’t remember any of my life, except for a few months after Erin’s death...and this puzzles me to an extent. Why is it that I can’t remember my own life? I lived it. I’m living it. There was no stall that I can remember? My life isn’t some computer game that I can stop for a while and then continue back on with it. It’s like I lost my soul the day that my girl died. How it came about was so quick and so fast. My heart just couldn’t keep up with the pace of such a tragic loss. She was the only one who could make me feel all good inside. “Why...why did you have to take the only thing that I cared for away from me?” My own words break the silence with a depressing tone. I felt like yelling and throwing myself at the world with pure rage, but it would do no good. It does no good. I’ve finally realized that there is no reason to stand around and mutilate myself over something that I can not change. “She’s dead, Kris.” There was nothing I could do about it, nor will there ever be a chance for me to do something about it. It’s finally time for me to admit to myself that Erin Saunters is dead and she will not be coming back. She won’t be coming home, this time...I should have realized that 9 years ago. As I stare at the stars in the sky, I lean back and lay on the soft ground, watching the blinking lights of planes move overhead. The sound of the night is overwhelming, with the crickets chirping loudly, and the sound of birds nesting away for tomorrow’s adventures. And now there seems to be another girl in my life. I don’t know why I seem to have a like in her, for I told myself I would never be with another person in my life. Heaven Rayne...the sound of the name gives me shivers down my spine. She just reminds me of my Erin in so many ways... I roll over onto my side and feel the squared shape protruding out from my right pocket. I heave a sigh, seizing into my pocket, dragging out the black journal in which I kept all of my prized memories. It was my treasure...the thing that I valued most in this world. This was the material in which my memories with Erin were stored. Memories in which were stored before...and after she left. This book has been with me for as long as I can remember...since I first learned how to understand writing and put the pen to the paper. So many memoirs....so many pages of information...how I fit so much information into one tiny book amazes me, as I add blank pages to keep fulfilling my life. Generally, I open it up after the days she left me, knowing that I don’t want to commit to memory how it was back when she was with me...the enjoyable days. I don’t want to remember how I felt, because there’s no chance that I will feel that way ever again. “It’s time...” It’s time that I get over my crisis and quit bewailing over this. I’ve mourned over this for 9 years, and it’s time that I divulge to myself what my life was really about. I mean, what could be so bad in this journal that I haven’t already done or seen? Why am I so scared of what my own hand had written...but the thing is...I don’t remember half the shit I wrote. What the fuck is wrong with me? What’s my problem...I’ve had my whole life wasted over this...and too many tears have I tasted. It’s time to let it all come out in the open and for me to go back to it...for me to go back to the reason that I was dubbed, ‘sick’. Silently, I unfasten the little button on the cover and open the beginning of the book...I see the first page, stained with blood...the first page of the journal that I hadn‘t torn out, as I stare at it, fearful of what I just may dig up.
