Love will Find a Way

25 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6-25-02 Love will Find a Way The scene opens to a bright and sunny Monday morning, typical of early summer. Heaven Rayne is still snuggled sleepily under her bright green, yellow, and baby blue comforter. Her kitten, Cloe is nestled on her pillow purring next to Heaven’s face. The sheer, yellow curtains hanging over the large double window allows the fresh morning sun to shine through generously, kissing and warming Heaven’s beautiful face. Being 5 a.m., Heaven is sound asleep until she is awaken by the neon blue telephone ringing next to her on the wooden stand. “RING……………..RING………….” “Ok, Ok, hold your horses…..” Heaven says drowsily as she reaches up to grab the phone. “Hello?” There is a muffled sound on the other end of the line as if someone is rubbing his or her hand across the receiver. Heaven becomes annoyed as she glances at the bold red numbers on the alarm clock stating that it is only five o’clock, and these people are obviously prank calling her. “Hello? UUUUGGGHHHHHHHH!” Heaven slams the telephone down hard on the base and rolls back over. “Meow,” Cloe looks up at Heaven blinking innocently in question to why she has just been using such a harsh tone of voice. Heaven softens and laughs a little, laying her head back on her pillow, and rubbing Cloe’s head, making her close her eyes and purrs again. Heaven tries to go back to sleep when suddenly she hears loud barking. She remembers that the grouchy old man across the street recently bought two full grown Basset Hounds. The two of them sound as if they are in pain, the way that they howl. The suburban neighborhood is usually quiet and calm, especially at 5 a.m. Heaven is again disgruntled. She never gets the chance to sleep in, and the one day she gets the opportunity, it gets ruined. She tries again to get back to sleep, pressing one ear firmly on the feathered pillow, and sticking a finger in the other ear. After a few minutes she falls asleep, but is awaken only three hours later when she hears a lawn mower. Apparently some eager neighbor is getting an early start in grooming his yard. Heaven sits up in her bed and looks around at her bright room. She sighs, realizing that there is no point in trying to sleep any longer. She feels like there is some reason for so many distractions. Maybe she shouldn’t sleep. Heaven feels like there is something wrong, that somebody needs her help. She stands up in her brief, thin, red, cotton spaghetti strap tank top and shorts. She makes her bed, and decides to take a shower and get dressed. Meanwhile the scene is switches from her to outside. Outside the weather is warm and beautiful, however the promise of rain is held in the air, and the sky is beginning to cloud just a little as if a storm is in store. The soft green blades of grass look as though they are dry, and the suburb is filled with rose bushes reaching out thirstily. Walking down the street with a small brown Terrier Pup on a blue leash is a girl who looks to be about eighteen years of age. She is of average height and appears trim and physically fit. Her soft, medium brown hair is thick and long, reaching about two inches from the base of her shoulders. Her complexion isn’t dark, but still far from fair, and her almond shaped and colored eyes seem her most attractive feature. She wears a red t-shirt sporting the words, “Penn State University.” She wears light colored jean shorts, and summer shoes, or “half shoes,” the white tennis shoes with the backs out like a sandal. Her head is down as if she is upset about something serious. The scene switches again to show us a boy looking even more distraught. He is lying on his bed covered by a plain navy blue bedspread. All around him is a room decorated with poor taste, having maroon and navy blue plaid wallpaper. A ceiling fan with white blades dance in a circle above his bed, and a baseball appears in the air, and then the boy’s hand reaches up to catch it, before throwing it back up into the air again. The boy is about eighteen, but he is doing what he has done since he was six, when he is upset. He is lying on his back in bed with his blue baseball cap on front wards, covering his eyes, and tossing his favorite baseball that he caught at his first White Sox game with his dad. He is barefoot, and he sports a plain, white, cotton t-shirt and long jean shorts, past his knees. On the wooden nightstand beside his bed is a silver picture frame holding a snapshot of a lovely young girl smiling like a movie star. As the scene focuses in a little, it is obvious that the girl in the picture is the same girl shown walking the Terrier Pup moments ago. Suddenly, a loud, shattering racket is sounded, and the scene focuses on the window to the left of the boy. The baseball is now on the outside of the broken window, which the boy has had an outrage, and thrown the ball through. His voice is heard. “Damn it, Valerie! Why? I love you so much! Why can’t you just stay here and go to a local college with me, and then settle down like we dreamt? You don’t have to go to Penn St. just because your sister goes there. You are you, and you belong with me! We’ve been together for four years, and I can’t live without you! I can’t deal with this, I don’t know HOW to live without you….Valerie….I….I just can’t….” His voice trails off, as the tears falling from his eyes hidden inside his hat, slide down and choke him up. Even though he is only talking to himself, the words are hard to say, and the emotions are almost too much for him. He picks up a letter from beside him on his bed. It is a letter from the local university to which he applied, and the letter is announcing that he has gotten in. He shakes his head sadly, wishing Valerie would give in and join him. The college isn’t as well known as Penn State, but it still holds many opportunities, and it would work out just fine for Val. She applied to both colleges, and was accepted to both.The scene changes once more, this time back to Heaven Rayne. She is now ready for the day, dressed in a white tube top, white cotton Capri pants, and white flip flops. Her hair is pulled up into a high, lose ponytail, and she sports large silver hoped earrings, and a silver ankle bracelet. While she was in the shower, she saw the images in her head of the sad girl walking the Terrier, and the miserable boy. She feels that these two people are having a love crisis and she needs to help, somehow she feels called to. Having lived as a normal mortal for the first twenty years of her life, Heaven had the advantage of falling in love, and experiencing what it is like. Consequently, Heaven understands how this young boy feels, and she knows she has to make Valerie see the importance of staying with him. Heaven closes her eyes and sees Valerie in her mind again. By this time, Valerie is inside her house, since the temperature has drastically dropped, due to the approaching storm. Valerie’s house is four blocks away from Heaven’s. Heaven teleports to Valerie, and makes herself invisible. Valerie live with her parents in a small white sided house at the edge of the street. Heaven is in Valerie’s bedroom which is much better decorated than her boyfriend’s. The walls are white, with a purple flower border around the top and bottom. Her bed is a silver framed queen size, covered by a darling lavender comforter. The room has many posters and memorabilia hanging here and there, and looks creative. Valerie appears lounging in a dark purple beanbag chair, digging through a large brown shoebox with the words, “Memory Box” written across the side in black marker. A few of the objects from the box are lying on the tan carpet beside the box, having been tossed out as Valerie sifts through. Heaven sees wrist bands from numerous fairs and amusement parks, movie ticket stubs, plastic bottles, and labeled bags of dried rose petals. One of the labels says, “Valentines Day 1998.” Other objects are happy meal toys, pictures, notes and letters, some are handwritten and some typed with special fonts, cards, party souvenirs such as paper hats, and those blow things. There are graduation napkins with, “Class of 2002” printed on the front, and graduation pencils. These are only what has already been looked at and tossed aside. There is still plenty more objects in the box. Heaven stands by, watching as Valerie looks through the box, with tears streaming down her cheeks. Heaven grows bored, and begins glancing around the room. On Valerie’s wide, full length white dresser are two letters of acceptance. One is from the local college and one from Penn State. Heaven realizes Valerie has begun to put things back into the box, and after she is finished, she reaches up and snatches something from her bed, the most recent addition to the box. Before she tosses it in, Heaven steals a quick glance at the small homemade card reading, “Congratulations Val, I love you, Love Ben.” Heaven laughs to herself thinking at least she knows the boy’s name now. Valerie shoves the box under her bed, and leaves to go into her sister’s bedroom. She opens the closet door and looks at the numerous Penn State t-shirts, sweatshirts, and hats. She looks about the rest of the room seeing snapshots of the building and the campus, the friends and the parties. She sighs heavily and walks back to her own bedroom. She lies on her back and talks to herself out loud, just as Ben did earlier, only she doesn’t throw a baseball. “Ben….I love you, I really do, but Penn State is a really big opportunity. My parents have been counting on me to go. Oh god, Baby, how do I make you see that I love you enough to stay here with you, but you’ve got to prove your commitment first, that’s all. You say you love me and want me to stay, but you never elaborate on the future. Are you going to ask me to marry you? How much do I really mean to you? I’ve been with you since the eighth grade, but are you going to want me as a wife? I need to know. I’m not going to miss my big chance to go to Penn State if I don’t know without a doubt that I will have you forever. Ben…how do I say all these things to your face?” Heaven has seen and heard enough. She records in her mind everything Valerie has just said, and she teleports back to Ben’s house. By this time, Ben has fallen asleep. Heaven puts Valerie’s words and voice into Ben’s dream. When Ben wakes up he will know what to do, and he will tell Valerie what she needs to hear. They will live happily ever after. Satisfied, having done all she can do, Heaven goes home. A light drizzle begins outside, and the sky darkens. Heaven didn’t get to sleep in this morning, but after doing her duties, she can rest. The darkness of the sky and the sound of the rain makes it relaxing and cozy. Heaven crawls under her covers. End. .
