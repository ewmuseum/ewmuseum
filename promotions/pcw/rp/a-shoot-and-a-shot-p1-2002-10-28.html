A Shoot And A Shot (p1)

28 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10-28-02 Saturday, October 26th I lost. I did. But am I mad? Not sure really. Not sure of anything these days. My mind’s been quiet… quiet, or dead? Who knows? Who cares? Certainly not me. And why should I? In these past few weeks, since I returned from the hospital, I’ve learned much about myself. I’ve realized that it doesn’t really matter if I get betrayed, for I always have the moment… It all relates to one thing, right? Sarah Mercury—crashing into my life as a manager for my wrestling career. Claiming to simply “want to help.” I don’t believe it for one second. Or do I? Does it matter? Not to me. Hell, things are good now. I’m able to relax a bit; to not worry as much. Of course I’ve felt like this before… I’ve been betrayed before as well. “No. Absolutely not. There is no way in—” “You’re doing it,” she said in a sterner-than-normal tone. Trigger still hadn’t figured out why she was so intent on the growth of his popularity, or ‘personal stock’ as she called it. He didn’t understand why such things could mean anything to anyone. But he kept reminding himself—there are people that kill for fame. It must have some appeal. That was the mindset that he kept as she proposed her plan. He made an attempt to steer the ideas to a different topic, but there was one thing that the Infinite Warrior had learned about Miss. Mercury right off the bat: once she makes up her mind, there’s no going back. “I don’t do television.” “Aren’t you getting a bit ahead of yourself?” she said with a smirk. “This is just a quick commercial shoot… Just to get your name known.” “Yeah—and disgrace it!” His attempts were half-hearted. As a child, Trigger had never become ‘glued to the tube’ as so many of today’s adults called it. He never understood why someone would want to sit in front of a box that told them what to do, while they could be out enjoying the pleasures of self-discovery. One thing Trigger had understood was that others didn’t like being condemned for some higher purpose, so he didn’t make his view known. He knew what a commercial was. An advertisement, a field that became less respectable as time went on. He had seen such advertisements on editions of PCW television shows, and wrote them off as lame. He had no respect for anyone that would partake in them. Yet now this woman, who a month ago had been a complete stranger, was controlling his life—and the first stop was starring in a commercial for some kind of product… What was it again? “—It’s an ad for Coke,” she stated. His eyes grew wide. “The drug?” The efforts to help today’s youth really must be futile, the warrior thought. Sarah responded by doubling back in a fit of laughter. Through giggles, she filled him in. “No, silly! Coca-Cola.” Seeing the blank look in his eyes, she went on. “…The soft drink? You must not get out much, heh.” He stopped himself from interjecting. Let her have her jokes. For some strange reason, he liked seeing her happy. She turned away for a moment. They were in the kitchen of her home, and she opened the refrigerator, and removed something inside, while Trigger sat at a table to read the contents of a folder placed before him. Returning back to him, she snatched the folder from his grasp, and set down a soda can. Never the one to back down from new things, he cracked it open and brought it to his mouth, where he carefully took a sip. She cracked a smile, but he didn’t know why. “I don’t like it,” he exclaimed after a moment. “It tastes so… unnatural.” “Oh well,” Sarah replied, “I don’t think you’ll have to drink it during the shoot.” She didn’t think he’d mind a small lie. “Just go with the flow,” she said, and then added, “You could definitely use the money.” He cringed. Trigger felt guilty doing something for the sole purpose of his benefit. Of course, he was just doing it for Sarah… did that count? The Infinite Warrior had made a decision. He would do the shoot… but he would make certain that it would not violate his morals. “I won’t be doing anything bad, will I?” Another laugh. “Of course not! I’m doubt you’ll even speak.” Another lie.
