Date: Season I, Episode 4 ﻿Location: New York SuburbDate: December 16, 2002Start of Scene Time: 11:45 amShot: Snow’s Home - Interior She set the last of the boxes down in front of the refrigerator and stood up to take a look around. The place was about the same size as the home she shared with her husband, daughter, andsister, up north in Canada; And even though she looked forward to having the opportunity todecorate all over again, she couldn’t help but miss the cozy little New Brunswick home. Then again, she thought, flipping her moderately long brown hair over her shoulder, there wasmore emotional baggage left in Canada than the luggage that they brought with them to theirnew home, located in a nice little suburb in New York City. She didn’t realize that New Yorkhad such a quiet side to it. Just twenty minutes away, she could forget that a place like thisexisted in a city so loud. “Raven,” Snow caught her attention as he through the front door of their new home. “Is thateverything?” He closed the door behind him, confident that there was nothing left. Other thanthe major appliances, everything they owned, or cared to continue owning, was packed snuglyinto boxes that had each been carefully taped by his attention-to-detail wife. “That’s all of it,” she sighed, taking a look around the place. They were in the kitchen, buteverything was still completely bare. If not for the sink, the dishwasher, and the refrigerator, shewould have to decide what room to turn the space into. “I still can’t believe we won’t be livingin Canada...” She turned to him, “I really don’t understand why you want to come down here somuch - you hate cities like this. Especially this city! Why the sudden change of heart?” Snow wasn’t prepared to answer the question head-on. “It’s got some nice spots,” he shrugged,“Like this place.” “It is nice,” she agreed, looking for her box of dinner ware. “Besides,” he quickly thought of an excuse, “I can get to PCW shows more easily from here, andI’ll be able to get to spend more time with you guys.” She gave him a look - he was lying andshe knew it. Wrestling was a dangerous sport, and the heat often flowed outside the ring - that’swhy she had been living in Canada so long while he toured the U.S and the world. He wanted tokeep her away from it. She swallowed her suspicion hard and turned back to her surroundings. “What colour should Iuse in here?” “That’s your department,” he said with a smile that reminded her of old times - very old times. Before the wrestling, the alcohol problems, and ultimately, before the rocky patches that thererelationship had undergone throughout their six years of marriage. That smile took her all theway back to highschool... He was strikingly handsome to her, as well as all other women - that, in fact, was reason formost of their ‘rocky patches’. It was hard to trust a man that she only saw for a weekend or twoout of every month, except for when he was on television, where she was forced to see womenswooning over him. In truth, she didn’t marry him for his looks - she cared very little about thatsort of thing, though she couldn’t help but consider it a bonus. He had changed so much since returning from prison, a place that he refused to speak of evenwhen she insisted that holding back was mentally unhealthy. She remembered the man who leftfor prison, realizing just how different the man that returned was - physically, if not emotionally. Most immediately, he was huge now. He had always been large by every day standards, withtwo hundred and thirty pounds on a frame that was barely over six feet, though he claimed to bea full inch over that mark - she refused to believe it until she saw evidence. Now, he was aphysical monster. The first thing she had to do when he returned was buy him a completely newwardrobe. He gained twenty pounds of solid muscle in the five months that he was gone, andshe still wasn’t quite sure if she liked the change or disliked it. She remembered days when shethought he was too large at two hundred and thirty pounds. She said they looked awkwardwalking down the street together, because at just an inch over five feet, she was a large womanby no standards. She felt the same about the new, larger, version of her husband, but shesuspected that she would feel he was too large to a more intense degree. Two things that she definitely approved of were the absence of the beard and the short hair,though he refused to tell her what made him decide to make the changes. His hair was a fewinches long now - she despised how fast it grew, since she was a fan of short hair. Still, he kepthis face clean shaven, and that was all she could ask for. Suddenly, there came a knock at the door. “Who could that be?” Raven said with a raised eyebrow. “I didn’t tell anyone our address yet.” Snow swallowed hard - he knew exactly who it was. He turned to the door and slowly opened it. Just as he suspected, Johnny ‘Silver’ Rosalini stepped over the threshold, followed by PeteyMoseriole, both of whom Snow had grown to know quite well over the last few weeks. “So this is the Mrs.?” Johnny Silver said, pointing down at Raven, who was staring up from thefloor while attending to a box. “Nice rack.” Raven’s eyes snapped open as wide as they could possibly push themselves - no one had spokento her that way in years. They were all too afraid too. She considered it one of the bestbonuses of being married to Jason Snow - she never had to deal with male idiocy. Johnny had little to worry about from Raven’s eyes, especially with her husband’s bearing downon his like a vice. Even without the beard and hair, Snow’s spirit still made him resemble a lion. “The Spice Rack!” Johnny said, pointing to the open box next to Raven. “My Ma’s in theSpaghetti Sauce business - I know Spice Racks.” Snow glared at Johnny for another momentbefore deciding it was a valid excuse. Raven eyed her husband, unsure of what to think of theircompany. “Raven,” Snow said through clenched teeth. “This is a friend of mine - Johnny Rosalini.” “Oh?” Raven raised an eyebrow. “You’ve never mentioned him.” She turned to Snow. “I’m hurt,” Johnny said with a laugh. “Actually miss, we just met a couple of weeks ago -business associates, with a friendship on the side.” Raven nodded, unable to peel her eyes from the man’s solid unit of a silver comb-over. He waswearing a black jumpsuit, which she thought looked atrocious, especially on a man his age withhis lacking build. “And?” she said to him, finally pulling her eyes from Johnny to the second man. “Peter,” Snow said. “Peter Moseriole.” “You can call me Petey, Ma’am,” he said to her with a nod. He, at least, was a bit moreimpressive that his companion, Johnny Silver. He was somewhere deep into his thirties, but hehad a full head of curly dark hair and was dressed semi-formally in a button-up shirt and blackpants. Petey was deceptively innocent at first meet, Snow knew. He was well-mannered until one gotto know him. Once Petey didn’t care what one thought of him, his other side was madevisible. Even in the two weeks that he’d known Petey, Snow had seen the man do things that heconsidered downright cruel. Raven thought he seemed like a nice man, though she may have been comparing him to hiscompanion, who she didn’t like at all. “Another business associate?” she asked. “Yeah,” Snow nodded. “With all of these lawsuits under way, I figure I’ve got to try to make mymoney work for me as much as I can before I have to throw it all away.” “So,” Raven inquired, “this is kind of like an investors group?” “Yeah,” Johnny Silver shrugged, “Something like that.” *** The mare tires as the familiar trio stumble their way toward the shore - the beach is such abeautiful place, but she knows how ugly it can turn with the wrong stroke of wind. Snow iscarrying O’Reilly on his shoulders, carrying him toward the waterline. “Almost time,” Shena says to him. “I know,” Snow says. “Why’d you bring Raven here?” she asks. “I spent almost half a fucking year in the slammer,” Snow points out, taking office to thequestion. “Pardon me if I want to have my family close by.” “Don’t you think you’re putting them in danger?” Snow closes his eyes. “I’ve got no choice, alright?” he admits. “This thing I’m into - it’sfucking dangerous and I know it. But it’s bigger than me, you know? At least if they’re here,close by, I can protect them. If there in fucking Canada, they’re fair game.” “Do you think something like that could ever happen?” Shena asks. “Do you think it would everget to that point?” “I hope not,” he answers truthfully. “Johnny says they’ll be protected... but the thought of themhaving to be protected scares the fucking shit out of me.” The horse nodded and suddenly stopped in her tracks. “It’s time,” she says. Snow nods, removes the leprechaun from his strong shoulders, and walks out into the oceanwhile the wind begins to howl around him. Soon, the waves engulf him, crushing him beneaththeir pressure. Every breath is a drink and within moments, he dies. *** Location: New York SuburbsDate: December 16, 2002Start of Scene Time: 11:50Shot: Johnny Silver’s Car - Interior “So what’s on today’s schedule?” Snow asked as he took his seat in the back of Johnny’s blackBenz. Since coming to New York, Snow got up every morning and went on some daily missionwith Johnny, although Johnny’s ‘morning’ was usually noon. A few days later, Petey startedcoming along. Usually their day consisted of driving through the city, waiting for phone callsfrom their superiors, who would sometimes send them on some errand. That was rare, Snow hadnoticed. “We’re going out to dinner with some of the boys upstate,” Johnny answered as he started thecar - his eyes were devilish. “After that, I figure will just do some supervising.” Snow nodded. ‘Supervising’ was Johnny’s term for doing next to nothing. Petey lit a cigarette and cracked the window, trying to hide a smile from Snow. “Yeah,” he said,“Dinner. There are some people there who want to meet you.” “People?” Snow was intrigued. He’d been meeting new people almost every day, and he thoughthe’d met everyone employed by the Hattoni family by now. “Yeah,” Johnny said. “Your reputation’s been getting around, especially after you whackedGeorgie. Even if you didn’t get all the fucking details right.” Johnny glared at Snow in therearview mirror, and Snow met his hot gaze - both of them made mistakes on that day. “Heartless Jason Snow,” Petey chuckled, “that’s what they’ve been calling you around the uppercircles.” “For what I did to George?” Snow perked an eyebrow. “Not what you did,” Petey said, “how you fucking did it. I nearly lost my fucking ravioli aftermy first kill. You - you heartless son of a bitch - you didn’t even flinch.” He paused and lookedover his shoulder, “It’s a good trait to have in this business.” “What are you talking about?” Johnny asked as he pulled out of the gravel driveway and onto thenarrow street. “Being heartless,” Petey said, using his hands to speak as he always did. “Fuck that,” Johnny said. “He may be heartless, but that ain’t his fucking nickname.” “What’s the big deal about a nickname?” Snow asked, looking out his window at his newneighbors. “What’s the big deal about a nick name?” Johnny sounded shocked. “Everyone in this businessneeds a nickname. It’s gangster 101. I’m Johnny Silver, Peter here is Petey the Vice.” “I got that name after I broke a guy’s ear drums just from squeezing his fucking head,” laughedPetey - Snow had already heard the story a thousand times. He turned back to Johnny. “Hisname’s already fucking settled,” he said, “Heartless Jason Snow. It’s what the top dogs arecalling him.” “Fuck the top dogs,” said Johnny before correcting himself, “I mean, in this particularcircumstance. His name was decided a long fucking time ago. He’s Jason the Lion. That’swhat Hattoni called him in his letters.” Snow stopped paying attention. “Maybe we can work something out about him being Irish,” suggested Petey. “Nah,” Johnny responded. “It’s probably best that we don’t draw more attention to that thanneed-be.” “You’re probably right,” sighed Petey. *** Location: Upstate New YorkDate: December 16, 2002Start of Scene Time: 12:35Shot: Strip Club - Interior Dinner ended up being eaten at an all-day strip club on the west side of the city. It was a placethat Snow’s companions had eaten often, or at least it seemed that way. Snow noticed thatJohnny and the bouncer exchanged hot glances upon their arrival. “Johnny Silver!” exclaimed a sweaty bald man at a table in the center of the room, seconds afterthey made their entrance. He stood up to greet them and Snow soon saw him hug both Johnnyand Petey - he didn’t really understand all the hugging that went on between cold bloodedkillers, but when it was his turn, he conformed to the Italian custom. “So this is the kid?” the fat man said, wiping his brow. “Heartless as statue, ah?” His roundbelly bounced with a laugh. “We call him the lion,” shrugged Johnny. “Heartless works too,” argued Petey. “I guess the nickname’s undecided as yet,” Johnny’s hard gaze turned to his curly hairedcompanion. “Regardless,” the fat man said in his loud voice, “what you did for the old Don while in the canis something that we only hope that we can one day repay you for. My name is Paul Gouchi.” “Paul the Hawk,” said Johnny, glaring over at Petey. “A nice animal nickname.” Snow nodded, keeping his silence, as was his usual practice around people he was unfamiliarwith. He still wasn’t familiar with all of the customs and formalities that these rulers of theunderworld subjected themselves to, and was in no hurry to break any rules. As Johnny Silvertold him some time ago, no one got fired in this business. Getting fired was a slug in the back ofthe head by one of your closest friends. The four of them sat in the seat, and soon, Snow found his eyes drifting to the stage where threestrippers danced vigorously with each other or against the various poles that were planted alongthe surface. “So Paul,” Johnny said as he attempted to get comfortable in the booth, across from Petey andPaul, “Where’s Joey? Wasn’t he supposed to be here for this?” “Kid got sick last night,” answered Paul. “He’s stuck at the fucking hospital all day.” “That’s what I keep fucking saying about kids!” Johnny burst out. “All they do is shit and throwup - that’s why I stayed single so damn long. I can’t have a kid keeping me from business.” Paul shrugged. “Everyone else has kids.” “Come on guys,” Petey spoke up suddenly. “Can we get down to business here?” Snow’s eyes snapped toward Petey - he hadn’t heard that this meeting was going to haveanything to do with business. “Alright, alright,” Paul answered. “Here’s the deal. A truck’s coming in next week from downsouth, and it’s packing a shitload of bananas.” “Bananas?” Snow spoke up. “Bananas, kid,” Paulie said with a chuckle, “that are laced with the purest fucking heroin that ajunkie could ever dream of.” “So what’s the job exactly?” Johnny asked. “The truckdriver has no fucking idea that he’s transporting narcotics, so your guys have to justget him out of the truck and rough the son of a bitch up a little. All the while, another crew willbreak into the truck from behind.” “How the fuck do we steal a truck load of bananas?” asked Johnny. “That’s the beauty of it,” smiled Paulie. “Only one batch of bananas has the heroin. All theother bananas have blue stickers, but the illegal ones will have red stickers. All you have to dois find the red-stickered bananas and get the fuck out of there. When the driver looks at hismerchandise, he ain’t going to notice a single batch missing.” Johnny chuckled. “So,” Paulie concluded. “Now all you have to do is put the crew together.” “Well,” Johnny said with a smile, “this sounds like a chance for someone to earn his stripes.” All three men turned their dancing gaze to Snow.
