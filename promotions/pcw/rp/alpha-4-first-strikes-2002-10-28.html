Alpha [4]: ...First Strikes...

Date: 28.10.02 He walked down that narrow hallway, some twenty feet in length or so how his eyes measured it. Taking quiet steps, he wanted everything to be perfect. Explaining perfection happened to be quite a daunting task by anyone since they couldn't happen to call themselves perfect, nor even superior in a way. He scratched his chin, blinked and holding on to his brown coffee mug. That stench or in his case, aroma happened to be enough to wake up a few people. 'What the fuck do you have in that mug!?' The man smiled to himself, continuing to take the mug along the sweeping hall. A peach colored carpet with twenty-two doors, eleven on each side of the narrow walkway. His eyes were blurry, as in a blind man walking through the valley of light. Starring down, he happened to stay silent, until Tracy Ellis happened to step into the fray. 'Nine-forty-two in the morning and this idiot wakes me up!' She whispered to herself, full well knowing that something is definitely up. The bellhop happened to continue his strut as Tracy had her hair tied with a rubber band, a white pajama top with blue sparkles vertically coming down, with a pair of sandals on her feet. Her eyes are light brown, with no make up on. Its a beauty secret, she doesn't wear makeup at all, except maybe the occasional addition of lipstick. Eyeing the bell hop, she folds her arms along her chest, an impatient gesture on her part as the bell hop eyes her with a glee in his eyes. 'What the hell...' Tracy mumbled to herself as the bell hop used his force to shove her into the room. He chuckled to himself, obviously on some designer drug pushed out in the streets. 'Mr. Reno shall like this gift.' The bell hop went over towards the phone, a small little button on the side modified for the call downstairs to the kiosk located at the entrance to the hotel. His face changed from the goofy smile towards a serious appeal to him. The other end picked up, a gruff voice answered. 'Better be talking some business.' The bell hop's eyes light up with excitement, 'Boss, I've got great news!' 'You've died and call me from Heaven stupid?' A quiet note of laughter went through the bell hop's mind, before retorting sarcastically, 'T-That's funny, but seriously for a second I've gotten the objective.' Reno took a puff from a Cuban Cigar, and leaned back in his recliner. 'Bring her here stupid, then report back without any further moments in which you happen to prove your stupid characteristics.' 'Oh thank you boss! You know,' he sniffled as if he were about to cry. 'this is the best thing to ever happen to me.' Reno looked at the phone quizzically, shaking his head in disgust with the bell hop's play on words. He longed for power, and he happened to have it. Although the funds didn't come with it, he still had a hold on some people inside of Lower New York City. His pin stripe black suit with matching fedora hat topped off his choice of wear and style. It happened for a strong, built man of forty three, he had to prove something, otherwise he'd become a failure. A statistic. A Nobody. 'Just bring her here and shut the hell up.' He stated harshly to the bell hop. 'And O'Rourke, don't screw this up.' O'Rourke - the bell hop smiled and began to laugh in a hyena-ish style of voice before hanging up the phone gently. He turned his head, looking deep into the eyes of his captive and smiled. He walked up closer towards her, inches away she pressed her feet back, before her best effort of walk, she punched him with her mightest fist. A small thump landed against the cheek of O'Rourke, only making his smile, before backhanding her to the ground. He sipped on his coffee mug and pulled out a bonding rope used to keep logs in place and began wrapping around the arms of Tracy, quiet mumbling and curses flowing out of his mouth as the slight smell of Winterfresh bubble gum happened to highten his tying around her. 'There, that should do it.' he looked and flexed at his proud accomplishment. Picking her up, he dragged her by the arm, jerking her down the narrow hallway with tenants looking on, wondering abou the current situation. His feet rapped upon the steel steps, followed by the clacking of her sandals which made a pitter-patter pattern. O'Rourke still didn't feel like saying much towards her, just doing his job like any other. Smoked bellowed as the two of them busted through the back exit door from the stairwell. Looking deep into his pockets blindly with his hands, he pulled out the keys to a nineteen ninety six Acura, talon blue colored and dirty. It popped with a loud bang towards it, before it sped away, kidnapper taking his hostage without much calculated moments etched into his mind at the moment. He was east bound, on the inner loop of the Montico Freeway, clocked in a fifty five miles per hour. Traffic heavily weighed on his left side, thus he kept the car deep in bedded between a car or two. O'Rourke ignored the glares and occasional honks and upthrust fingers from drivers darting into the middle lanes to get around and kept his eyes firmly on the road ahead, though he did occasionally glance at the speedometer to ensure the needle was on the double nickel. It usually was. Maintaining exactly fifty-five was a point of pride with him. He often thought that if the sppedometer ever broke he could nail fifty-five anyway. He had plenty of pratice. 'And what the hell are you going to do to me?' Tracy questioned her kidnapper with murder in her voice. O'Rourke didn't feel like answering her questions since he didn't feel like doing anything anyway. 'Hello? Is your stupid ass there or not?' She wasn't buckled into her seat belt, as she turned around a caught a peek of the backseat of the car. She stared at a dirty blanket, green in color but fading fast. Under the blanket were two weapons, a twelve-gauge sawed-off pump action shotgun and a Remington Mondel Four Auto Rifle in .30-06 caliber. Both weapons were loaded. Tracy blinked twice, trying to catch her breath. 'What's the need for all the weapons?' 'A little business, I gotta take care of.' O'Rourke kept his eye on the road ahead, not to her silly questions in which the norm would be for her to shut up. He was twenty six, two inches over six feet tall and still had a flat stomach underneath a pair of faded blue jeans and white T-shirt. Soon he took the inner motion, splitting between a couple of cars, Sports Utility Vehicles and darted past, hitting sixty by the time the little maneuver happened to be pulled off. Nothing happened between the moments where O'Rourke and Tracy began to talk, it wasn't his job to talk to her period. As he pulled into an empty parking space right near the sidewalk bumper, he reached into the backseat, pulling out the Remington rifle and checking the gauges to see if the gun is loaded properly. Nodding his head, he moved away from the car, slamming the driver side door before moving towards the passenger side and took out a pair of handcuffs, locking her right wrist to the steering wheel. 'What are you doing?' she asked him, but he turned his back to her and began walking, taking drags from a nicotine cigarette, letting the smoke enter the atmosphere before tossing it on the ground and squashing it with the heel of his snake skin boot. He looked at the motel windows and felt a calmness in his stomach, checking for the number 19. He placed on a small white ball cap, disguising himself as he gently rapped on the door, waiting for the occupant to stand out in broad daylight. An old woman peeped out of the door's peephole and unlatched the lock to the door and jerked it open. O'Rourke stood before her, 'Hi, I'm with General Plumbing...' he gave her a fake name for entry into the small housing room. 'There's been a leak in the cerebellum of your pipes. Care if I, take a look?' The old woman, curly gray hair stood in front of him, as he gave him a smile without her dentures on. 'Sure sonny, g-go ahead.' 'Actually,' he raised the gun to her head, her eyes fixated on the coil pressed tightly on her skull. With a split second, he pulled the trigger and she slumped to the floor, a hole in her skull and sticky crimson flowing. She was dead, and O'Rourke didn't care one single ounce. 'I'll stick around for awhile.' He placed his fingers on the touch tone dial on the cordless phone, ringing up Reno once more. 'Objective complete, housing for the witness.' 'Good, now we have his attention.' Backing out and turning towards a gym, getting ready for round deuce. I should have felt good this morning, Tuesday, it would be the headline that I had cleanly dispatched the opposition, and yesterday I hanged out at the club, enjoying life and getting drunk. The fly in my soup happened to be that where I finish and gain, another steps up to the plate, hungrier and sniffing blood. Everybody was independent, no factions made, or deals cut along the tables to backstabbers. None of that. As I drove down Florence into Normandy on the opposite side this morning for a small breakfast just to get fuel into my already burnt system. It was odd, stepping into the resturant, filled with wrestling reporters, and insight hype men who dished the dirt better than anyone else. You dealt with these people three hundred days of the calendar year, nothing new. These doubters of mine were going to have to realize, that Dante Jackson is a very valuable asset to have in their huddle. Give me the ball and I deliver. Wrestling smarks and bullshit walks. Somehow, I would have to make that point. Professionally, and unobtrusively, of course. Parking the car in a barren and desolate spot in between two compact cars, I shut the door and locked it up, jingling my keys along the index finger. In front of me, the Hays Adams Hotel, walking purposely through the lobby and into a elevator. Whenever you heard the name Bernard Wallace, you knew you were dealing with wrestling royalty. He always stayed in the same suit, a huge job working at the head honcho of Wrestling Premier. Bernard or Bernie for short opened the door, grunted once, and closed it behind the visitor. 'When's it going to get cold down here?' 'Weird weather,' I agreed with him, plopping down in a small arm chair taking in the luxurious style of setting, the sight to the Fourm and naked trees in the middle. Its LA, erything is warm.' 'Like hell it is, nearly froze my ass off trying to cover PCW in Philly last month.' Bernie was a bear of a man. He had been fearsome in his youth; now he was merely fat. The years, however, had hdded no padding to his abrasive personality. He sank into an easy chair and relit the stump of cigar that protruded from his fleshly jowls. 'Breakfast'll be here in a few minutes,' he muttered as he eyed his visitor through the thick smoke. Classical opera played on the radio beside the bed a tad too loud from comfortable conversation. This was a normal precaution. The music would vibrate the window glass and foil any parabolic mikes that might be pointed in this directon by inquisitive souls, such as smark reporters from around the globe. We discussed my future career moves, my damn life. So on and so forth. The knock of the room service waiter came precisely on the hour. After all, this was the Hay Adams. When the white-jacketed waiter had wheeled the serving cart back into the hall and closed the door behind him, Bernie opened his briefcase and extracted a device artfully crafted to look like a portable radio. This device detected the electromagnetic field created by micropphones. Bernie pulled out the antenna, then walked around the toom, paying careful attention to the needle on the dial as he paushed at light switches and electrical outlets, swept the antenna over the flood and slowly down my back and front. 'Shit, you think they wouldn't love getting the things you happen to get huh?' Bernie smiled, he had a good smile. You want to be brought down, have power interfere in your plans and then screw it all up in their faces.'
