Real Life

26 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 26th Febuary 2002 The scene opens in a glacial wonderland, filled with crisp white snow, a clear blue sky which makes you think that someone got a big paintbrush and painted it light blue… and then kind of dry brushed it a little… ok probably not… oh yeah, clear blue sky, alpine trees for miles around, and a small wooden cabin with a glass door all steamed up. With steam pouring out from under it, and just generally seeping out the cabin. The glass door suddenly opens, and Miyagawa walks out, dressed only in a pair of jean shorts and his trademark goggles, and nothing on his feet. He steps barefoot into the snow and looks around for a second; he takes in the sight of the trees, the snow and the clear sky… the sky that looks so blue that… sorry. He looks down at the snow again, and grins, suddenly diving into the snow, and rolling around in it. He eventually pulls himself back up again, and just casually walks back in the cabin, closing the door on the way. A few seconds pass, and it comes to pass that on the request of a few people no Midi is playing, and their thus ye old Pentium Processor hasn’t seized up and died. Again. Sorry, off track, blue skies, snow, and all that other s**t. Right. Dammit where’s the script… ah here it is… Steve turns to Miya and… oh hang on that’s later. The camera zooms in slowly towards the door, and eventually cuts to an internal camera shot. Miyagawa and Steve Jabowski are sitting in the cabin, sweating like crazy… a small metal stove is near the door, with hot coals sitting on it, and it shows the contraption to in fact be a gas stove. Steve reaches over (dressed also in jean shorts, his old wrinkled skin looking like the folds in an elephant’s ass) and picks up a small wooden spoon, scooping what appears to be water out of a bucket and pouring it on the hot coals, as the they sizzle in delight. Miyagawa: Ah this is the life, a small sauna in northern Norway. Steve: InDEED. Miyagawa: It was a good idea to buy a one mile square plot of artic Canada and rename it Norway. It saves the ten hour flight just to get a decent sauna. People have forgotten the difference between the Swedish Sauna and the Norwegian Sauna, and that just isn’t right, is it? Steve: InDEED. Miyagawa looks at Steve who is behaving rather strangely... probably the heat is getting to his brain, which contradicts the idea of Steve not having a brain which has been proved in numerous scientific surveys. Steve is behaving rather strangely because he is staring out into space. Steve: InDEED. Miyagawa: I didn’t say anything. You do remember that I’m supposed to be the crazy one here. It’s in my contract. You’re the relatively normal, yet old, guy. You did read your contract, right? Steve: InDEED. Miyagawa: Stop that, it’s really getting quite annoying. You’re homosexual and you find me mildly attractive don’t you? Steve turns to Miyagawa. Steve: What? Miyagawa: See that snapped you out of it. I was kinda hoping you would say indeed just so I could air it on Turmoil or something. Steve: Indeed. Miyagawa: Not funny. So what’s up... haven’t seen you for a while, and then you invite me out here for a decent sauna, so I figure something must be up. Steve: Well Natalia got me to look into your background. Miyagawa raises an eyebrow is suspicion. Miyagawa: My background? I thought that was all cleared up after the hypnotherapy a couple of weeks back? Steve: Not exactly. The Asylum you think you grew up in never existed, and the first record I can find of you at all is at age sixteen in Anaheim Central Hospital suffering from a serious head trauma following an accident with no I.D. on you. You claimed to remember who you were and you left. Miyagawa: Of course I know who I am, I’m Miyagawa. I’m Isawa Miyagawa. I’ve always been Isawa Miyagawa. I’m a Japo-American and I’m defiantly Isawa Miyagawa. Steve: That I’m about to bring into question. You see, they sent me down the only records they have on you and it states that the name that was sewn into the back of your shirt that they cut from you wasn’t Isawa. Miyagawa: It wasn’t? What the frell was it then? Steve: According to the t-shirt, your name is Alexander. Miyagawa: Alexander? Actually that’s quite a neat name... you can be Alex... or Xander... or even Alexander. That’s quite swell, but it’s not my name. Steve: So I looked up an Alexander Miyagawa being born in the Anaheim area in nineteen seventy five. And I found him... and he’s got the same blood type as you do... Natalia gave me your blood type by the way. Miyagawa: So your night school class? Steve: I’m not studying law at night school. I did do a two week course in detective training and investigative practices. Miyagawa: Sounds like it worked. But is there any cold hard proof; because at the moment it’s all circumstantial. Steve: Do you think that there are two Isawa Miyagawa’s in California... or even the western side of the states? Do you think there is a second Alexander Isawa Miyagawa on this planet? Miyagawa: I can’t be Alexander Isawa Miyagawa... AIM... I sound like an AOL commercial. Get a grip Steve, you have to be wrong, and this is defiantly not funny. Steve: Your father wasn’t listed on the sheet but your mother was... and she’s still alive. There is only one way to find out for certain if this is your real life or not. Miyagawa: But this means... my entire existence is a complete and utter lie. What was that memory they found in the therapy room? Some kind of false memory? Steve: You were quoting lines from a teen slasher film. A pretty bad one too. Miyagawa: Hey, there’s nothing wrong with teen slasher films. Steve: So when do you want to leave? Miyagawa: I want Natalia to come too... if this is really my mother, and then I’m going to want her to meet my wife... I have a mother. This is great... I have family... I’d better get a suit... and a gift of some sort... a car... does she have a driving licence? No, that’s stupid, let’s just start with the flowers... make them nice flowers... make them pretty flowers... flowers for mother... maybe chocolates... good chocolates... Belgian Chocolates... Steve: Woah, calm down there Miyagawa. Let us just finish up here and then we’ll head back to the airport and get back to California. Maybe coming out of this way was a bad idea after all. Miyagawa: You could have waited till after the pay per view... or at least until I kick Maxwell’s and Walden’s combined asses. I’ve got other things I should concentrate on apart from my personal family life. I’ve got to do my best for the fans out there... I can’t disappoint them. Mom will understand... Mom... I have a Mom. He stops for a few seconds. Miyagawa: I have a Mom... and my name is Alexander Miyagawa... Alexander Miyagawa.
