From Virtue to Vice: Element 2 - Courage/Valor (Part 2)

Date: March 10 of 2003 A.D. The door to the rickety old hall creaked shut on rusted hinges, the mark of the structure’s two hundred year lifespan as the community convention center. Very little renovation had been done on the place in its time, a label of the pinnacle craftsmanship seen in its construction period, and it still carried the Victorian aura instilled in its foundation. The echo of footsteps resounded through the antechamber where the town congress converges and the final board member takes his seat, his entrance abruptly met with the thump of a gavel. The head of the board, John Isaac, clasps his hands on the table. “Alright, gentlemen. We’ve coexisted with Shanahan and that demonic little cult he has goin’ on up in that damned house for months now. But lately things have just gotten out of hand, and with several members of our community so badly affected by whatever the Hell they’re doin’ up there…. “There ain’t no reason we should tolerate their filth!” Isaac, perturbed, paused at the interruption. His fingers swept lightly over the hilt of the gavel and he breathed a reluctant sigh. His opening statement fizzled away in his head; there would be no point to iterating it now. “Landon, we’ve barely gotten started…..” “Ain’t no reason to keep goin’, we all know what’s happenin’ up there on that mountain! Dammit, John, haven’t you heard those chants comin’ from that accursed mansion?! Our parents and grandparents sat here and did nothin’ decades ago while those goddamn Wyatts’ ravaged the countryside with their evil projects and killed our neighbors! Why should we have to stand by and endure the same? Why should we allow this town to be stricken wit’ terror and live under the shadow of some group of madmen like our forebears did?!” There was mumbled support in favor of the speaker, Landon York. His story was particularly contemptuous, as it was derived from his own raped lineage; York has lost a great aunt and great uncle during the period of the infamous Wyatts’, both reputedly sacrificed and ceremoniously devoured in cannibalistic fashion by their mad abductors. “Shanahan ain’t even present in that house, Landon!” snapped Isaac. “We’ve been told he took off for Louisiana, deal with his wrestlin’ or some shit. And you know as well as I do that those drones that he controls can hardly support themselves anymore when he’s not around, let alone stage any sort of attack on this community or harm our people when he’s a thousand miles off! The only danger that those freaks present is that they may accidentally start a forest-fire or somethin’ without Bruce to keep ‘em in check. This entire episode is absurd! It’s paranoia! Mass fuckin’ paranoia!” The hall fell deathly silent while Isaac continued his retort. “Those robed freaks have wandered into town in the past, hell they bought food from Jude over at the store for their supplies. They can barely speak coherently anymore!” “Hehe….probably because their minds have been blasted by all that blasphemous ‘scripture’ Bruce feeds ‘em. Too damn busy rememberin’ chants and rituals anymore to have any room for English, or any language for that matter…..” “Nah, they done went and spoke some language to ole’ Jude. But it surely done wasn’t no human language, no sir. That was the language of the Devil!” Isaac sighed reflexively at the uncouth explanation offered, no less the well-known superstition of Randolph Harley and Lana Williamson; the Harley and Williamson households were never inept of tongue when it came to speaking on matters that most men and women were too timid to speak on, especially in regards to witchcraft and equally forbidden knowledge. “We’ve been over this a hundred times!” retorted a rising Jonah Dorsey, a younger and more open-minded counterpart to John Isaac. “Shanahan and that weird group are misguided, weird and their beliefs are totally outrageous. But that’s it! Sure they harbor some sick ideas and we all know by now that they intend on bringing about the end of the world with whatever games they play in that old house. But they’re too incompetent to harm this community. Shanahan is the only one of steady mind occupying that damned manor, and without his guidance those cretins wouldn’t even be able to make toast for themselves! They’re totally blind, deaf and dumb without him!” “What about those two young folk found on Fire Ridge just last week??” protested Harley insistently. “You’re gonna tell me that them maniacs are incapable of murder?!” “That was ruled an animal attack, Randolph!” spat Dorsey, irritated. “It was a couple from New York on second honeymoon at the lodge, went hiking on a warmer day, got lost at night and attacked by hungry coyotes or a mountain lion. They weren’t from around here and they got lost in a bad part of the woods! That’s it!” Williamson, not content with Dorsey and Isaac’s judgment, continued. Her face, contorted and taut with a fear bred into her blood throughout countless generations, leaned forward to stare at her detractors from the length of the table. By the texture of his face and posture, it was obvious she was as touched by the degeneration common to her kind as she was any fear. “I’ve heard tales of what Shanahan reveres, and what he and his followers call forth at night from the shadows....” * * * * The wind gently whistled in the still night, the heavens adorned with that eerily carmine crescent-moon that only a select few had yet noticed among the gallery of stars adorning the night sky. Clouds swiftly blotted out the twinkling heavens momentarily, the brusque westerly winds carrying the clouds in their frigid grasp and casting the countryside in an acute aura of darkness. Stanley Paxton, resident of Jury Road, was out tending the pigs in his backyard before retiring for the evening. He succumbed to drawing his coat closer as a chill swiftly saturated the air, the steady breeze picking up and whipping tree limbs to and fro under its reinforced sway. The wind-chime dangling from the back porch clacked and rattled fervently under the sudden gale, and the pigs launched into a peal of squealing before retreating into the sheltering overhang of their pen. Paxton, confused by the phenomenon, gazed around the snowy yard and cast a wary glimpse at the frost-lined pines flanking his house. He could have sworn he detected an otherworldly piping emanating from the forest, but the source (if any) remained veiled under cloud-shrouded darkness. “Stan?” shouted a voice over the now howling wind. “Are you alright?” Paxton turned his head to face the house, his wife’s concerned face visible through the screen of the second story bedroom window. Gripping his hat, he waved back as a signal. “I’m fine, Karen! But do....do you hear that?” “What?” “It sounds like a flute, or some sort of high-pitched whistle being repeated over n’ over. Sounds like it’s comin’ from the woods, ya don’t hear it?” “I can’t hear much of anything over this wind, Stan! You might be hearing things! May just be the wind passing through the pipes or something!” The outside lights extinguished with a hiss and long sizzle just as the wind began to pick up with even greater force. Paxton cursed gruffly, his feet shuffling through the ankle-deep snow as he strode toward the back sliding door. He could testify as to the piping growing progressively louder and expanding until it surrounded the clearing his house was situated in. Strangely the shrill sound was rather clear and distinct, audible and rich. “Stan! The lights! What is….?” Karen’s voice shrieked over the wailing wind to her husband struggling below. He was lost in the pitch darkness, noise drowned out by the uncannily violent windstorm roaring in his ears. The skies were devoid of luminosity, as was the entire village from his standpoint groping wildly in the backyard. “The wind probably did it, hun!” he bellowed. “Probably a transformer!” Paxton groped about in the dark several more moments before finally stumbling over a plank of loose wood. His hand edged over the top fencing of the hog pen, where even the animals had seemingly silenced. At that moment, the power of the wind dwindled and Stanley was able to release the grip on his flapping cap. The first thing he became conscious of was that though the wind’s power had lessened, the volume of the howling had not. Nor had that of the monotonous piping. At that moment, his wife had maneuvered to the back door through the darkness of the unlit house. She pried open the door and glanced about the blackness. “Stanley, what is this? The wind stopped, but....” “Karen….get back in the house and lock the door....” The light of the crescent moon immersed the yard in a ray of meager light. Paxton trembled precariously, his breathing slowed and heart racing at an abnormally rapid pace. “Why?...” “Get in the house, Karen....and call the sheriff. Now.” She had come to fear that tone in his voice over the years, one invoked only in most dire of consequences. She stepped back warily, slamming the door shut with a slam that was negated by the supernatural howl in the wake of the ghastly winds moments before. Paxton could sense another presence to his left just inside the perimeter of the ovoid clearing. And he could sense likewise to his right and his front, situated in the pen only inches from his own quaking figure. The bearing of these entities he could feel even before the sickly moonlight illuminated their forms, and their identities he could offer guesses to already in light of the paranoia. His suspicions as to their proximity were confirmed; their identities, alas, were far from correct. The last thing Stanley Paxton knew as he stared into that face of embodied nightmare, and felt the others advance from his flanks, was the sanctuary of madness. His deathscream, wracked with unearthly terror, was drowned out by the hellish shrieking of the now-absent winds and the unholy piping of oblivion eternal....
