'The Amazing Role Model' Mike Storm

26 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/26/02 OOC Note: I think this rp was going good, but I cut it a lot shorter than I wanted to, but this is what I get for not starting early on it. Scene: Camera opens and it is focuses on a black television screen. Then the screne comes to life. It is one of the those anti-drug commercials. It shows PCW's 'Amazing' Mike Storm standing in the middle of a wrestling ring. Then it cuts to him drop kicking an unkown wrestler hired for the commercial. Then it shows Storm again in the ring. The it cuts back to Storm hitting the Sky Twisting Moonsault. Then it goes back to Storm. It closes up on his eyes, he looks seriously into the camera. 'Amazing' Mike Storm: I am 'Amazing' Mike Storm a PCW wrestler against drug use. Wrestling is my anti-drug. What is yours? Scene: It goes to a black screen. Then the words my anti-drug is, then there are nine boxes, each box is filled in by one of each of the letters in the word wrestling. Then it has the question, what is your anti-drug? Then the scene cuts to a new commercial. It is a Truth commercial. All of those crazy people with the body bags are dumping them on some body's lawn. Then standing on the side walk watching is Mike Storm. He speaks as this goes on. Mike Storm: I am here on behalf of Truth, and looking to stop child smoking, and smoking in general. I know my business is not exactly the cleanest, it is not the most ethical. I mean that is because we have people like Jon Kano who curse, smoke, and all of this other garbage. We have people like Lance Bell walknig around with illerates who think they are cool. That is okay though, because I am here to provide a positive role model for all. I am here to show that tobacco is wacko if you are a teen. Scene: Camera cuts to that orange screen with The Truth written on it, under that it says tabacco os wacko if you are a teen. The television then is shut off, and the camera pans out to show that we are in a hotel room, and sitting on the bed is 'Amazing' Mike Storm. Next to him is the remote. Mike Storm looks into the camera. Mike Storm: I showed you my two commercials that I recently filmed to show everyone here that PCW is not completely devoid of acceptable role models, of a genuinely moral man around here. I mean we really need it, with people like Jon Kano, claiming wrestlers are not role models. Just because he does not have the will power to not curse out of control. He goes not have the will power to stop smoking. I feel sorry for Kano, picture it, the big goofy kid in school. Taller than it seem natural to be, and more uncoordinated then anyone else in the school. He probably ended up smoking because he thought it was cool, and that the kids around his school who did it would accept him as one of them for it. So he starts it, and like any other kid, there is nothing special about it, he does understand the joy people have in it. He coughs and gets a sickly feeling, but he keeps doing it due to that need to be accepted, and then before you know it he is hooked on the little cancer sticks, adding money to the most organized and methodical form murdering, one of the legal drugs. Mike Storm: Now Kano talks about how no wrestler can be a role model due to promoting violence. You may look at it that way, but I see it to be completely different. I see wrestlnig as an art. You would not know this due to the fact that when you wrestle, you can not pull off moves with the beauty that I can. You can not glide and move gracefully, like I can. Sure a lot of what we do is violent. Sure that is a part of what we do, but why can we not be role models? I mena look through out religions. Every religion obviously has laws agains murder and all, but they also find ways to condone violence. I mean the catholic religion had the crusades. I mean if the pope condones war, and he is obiously considered a role model, then what we do is not so bad. Ever head of a Jihad, the Muslim word for holy war. You see violence is acceptable in times of survival. Violence is okay if you have to do it to survive. The Native American Indians, althought given that stereotype of savagery, but they were very peaceful for the most part. They only were violent when it was the only way for survival, they hunted for food. They knew savagery was wrong, but they did it to survive. Mike Storm: I can go on like this all day. I mean we have had some great American Heroes, who were heroes of war, which is legalized mass murder. People like the great General George Washington, our only unanimously elected president ever. General Ulysses S. Grant. Who also became a president. General Dwight Eisenhower, who was another hero turned president. Those are just a few of our heroes who killed people, or ordered the deaths of people. Hell right now we got The Executioner George W. Bush in the white house. He was the Governor of Texas, the state that had the most Death Penalties I believe. So who says I am not a suitable role model? Mike Storm: Hell, I think I make a great role model. Not just because I donate to charities, and support good causes. Also because I can admit my flaws. I know I am far from perfect. I know there are people have lived a more ethical life, but there are not many of them, I tell you that. Especially around here. I mean our CEO is greedy, there are many corrupt wrestlers who will do what ever it takes to get ahead in the business, I mean hell let us take a look at one of my other opponents, Brock Newbludd. Mike Storm: Brock Newbludd belongs behind bars. No not the bars he takes his little brother to. I mean the cold iron bars that Karl Le Blanc is a resident of. Newbludd allowed the atrocity of Jon Kano moving on to the World Title match, and being allowed into a main event, it is a terrible thing. I mean Brock Newbludd, tonight is just the beginnning. You see after tonight, we are going onto March Madness to see who deserves a shot at the PCW Television Title. I tell you, I am still on a quest to save that title belt. I am still out to bring the division back to greatness. Brock I will handle you tonight. Tonight is not my main focus for you though. Mike Storm: Our real time comes at the pay per view, when it is just me you and the ladder. No Jon Kanos, Lance Bells, Flaymin Aymins, or Miyagawas. Just Mike Storm against Brock Newbludd. The Amazing One versus in the Innovator. Brock I will have my revenge on you. Mike Storm: Then we have Lance Bell. A damn joke. He walks around with a guy names Flava, and JuJu, which sounds very familiar, I mean he is that spanish guy with other guy who sang that song where they say 'There's no escaping this'. Are they the same guy? Well apparently Lance thinks he is cool. Lance things because he hangs out with a bunch of illiterates. Lance, you can hang around with these guys, to make you feel smarter, that is sad. Lance, I believe you had trouble with someone as low as Bob, and I think you may have lost to Jon Kano cleanly, that is just low. Your the rising star champion, no one cares. That title means nothing to the real stars of PCW. That titles means nothing to the fans. It is just something the new guys can fight over to keep them away from the real stars, and keep you guys occupied. In the words of Jeff Marx, it is indeed a jobber title. Mike Storm: All of you are going to realize that I truly am I role model, and more importantly, that I truly am AMAZING! Scene: Camera fades to black.
