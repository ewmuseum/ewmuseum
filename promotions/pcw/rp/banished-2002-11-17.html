Banished

17 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 17th, 2002 The rain tapping the steel roof was to much of a distraction for me, and I wonder why I didn’t stay with her that night instead of going off on my own. Would have been nice for once to have someone who actually cared enough about me to help me along the way and learn something her self. But Safina had her own life to worry about and shouldn’t worry about me. I had to leave this town and more importantly this country. But nothing could stop me from staying, unless they forced me out. But still I had to leave some time. This has been my home for to long, and now I got to leave because I want my life back seems a little odd. But I guess that’s how the world works in Thailand. I decided it was time to leave now and save the fighting till later. No time like the present to leave a place that didn’t want you around them anyway. But still it won’t be easy to just pick up and relocate to some place I have never been. “You are banished from Thailand.” Those words banged though my head, and it seemed to effect everything I said. Depression had set in, the weight of the world was on my shoulders and not one person knew my name like before. National symbol to street trash in one evening is frowned upon in my family. I could still hear my parents now, proving they were right, and that I should have never been taken into the temple and released from Thailand before. Their comments of hatred, rather then sorrow just sent a chill of anger up my spin. “We told you, you would not amount to anything.” “You were a failure from the start.” “I wish we never made this mistake.” Loving parents aren’t they. Doesn’t matter now, my life has been decided for me. Well a small fraction has been decided. Where will I go now, and what will I do. All I have ever been expected to do was compete for something and represent my country. But now that is gone so were do I go from here. Whether the council likes it or not I will still represent this country and my homeland but in what. There was no point in feeling sorry for my self. There would be time for that later, but now I had to start moving. Whether I want to stay here or not, they will be looking for me. I’m a marked man now and some how I don’t think they will come in a peaceful manner. If I learned one thing, its Thailand doesn’t play games with those who have been banished. “Well look who it is. None other then Mr. Street trash himself.” I tried to keep my head down and not look. But of course the bar hopes that were just taking a quick smoke break and not to mention a few blocks walk that they didn’t know they were taking due to the alcohol poisoning, decided it would be best to make sure I knew they were talking to me. I had to look him in the eye. He saw the advantage he had on me the moment he opened his mouth. He and a few of his, I guess friends, decided it would be best to make trouble not only for me, but also for my chances of leaving without a trace. “So it looks like are great representative doesn’t like it here anymore.” My luck the temple issued a warning to all citizens about my little problem, and these foreigners who were not from this country decided to hold me up. Now was not the time and I tried to pass on. I just stepped past, brushing his side as I moved. Without hesitation he turned and took a swing, connecting from behind with a fist. The blood started to flow from my nose, as they stood together in a group and continued to taunt knowing I couldn’t do anything to them with endangering myself. “What’s the matter trash? Can’t you fight back.” Another connection, this time sending me to the pavement, were they continued the beating with solid kicks to my ribs. I had to take the punishment, or risk being jailed which was a lot worse then being thrown from my homeland. “Let that be a lesson to you. Never disrespect this country.” Vile foreigners who believe they can get away with pure violence. The rain-washed down my face and the blood poured from my wounds into the street and washed down to the sewer system. Talk about a way to go out in style. A few broken ribs and what appeared to be enough blood that could keep any human alive was rushing from my veins. “Help…” I cried out, but I was too weak for anyone to hear. I could feel my body starting to fade, hoping for someone to notice me lying in the street. But of course the council has warned everyone to stay clear or risk imprisnionment. My eyes started to fade, my voice could barley hold a ton, and getting up and walking was out of the question I was beat more badly then I thought or had even hopped. Why couldn’t they have just finished the job instead of making me suffer through the pain and torture or no help? I could hold on much longer. Time was against me. Slowly my eyes closed and nothing but the darkness of night and the sounds of rain, patting steel could be heard. Even those sounds cease to exist. ««««««»»»»»» The bright light caught my eyes, and I placed my hand over to cover the light. My first reaction was to shout out, but I could remember a small part of last night and knew I was all right. I finally mustered enough strength to open my eyes and quickly closed them again once I felt the pain on my forehead. The pain throbbed and made it impossible to stand or even open my eyes for that matter. “Your going to be all right,” said a female voice, as a cool damp cloth was placed against my forehead, and for a few seconds the pain released it’s grip and aloud me to re-open my eyes. I blinked a few times to block the lights rays from blinding me in one shot. The familiar white skin and silk hair of Safina was enough to make me grin and add a little happiness into such a dull situation. By the looks of things it was morning, and Safina wore her usual black dress and blue shall. She leaned over the bed and gently tapped my wounds. “See what I mean about going to far.” I didn’t want to admit it, but she was right. Looking back at what I had done, it still amazes me that I am alive. It would have been just fine with me if those boys would have left me in the street to die. I wouldn’t hurt anyone dead, but like this, I am a risk to everyone I talk to. I didn’t recognize this place when I first opened my eyes. I had seen Safina’s entire house and this was not apart of it. “Are we still in Bangkok?” She shook her head in decline and rinsed the cloth out in a bowl of steaming water. I waited for her to tell me where we were but she simply kept pressing the cloth against my skin, trying to clear the dirt from my cuts. The water soaked in and stung for a few seconds sending a sharp pain straight o my brain. I let out a sigh as she continued. “Where are we then?” She stopped and placed the cloth in the bowl. She looked down on me and smiled. She brushed the hair from my eyes and shook her head in disbelief. She too was surprised I was alive, and I think it was getting to her as well. Her smile quickly faded and she just starred down at me. “You are lucky to be alive. I had to take you away from the capitol or you would risk going to jail.” It didn’t matter anymore where I was. Safety was my main concern, and even that didn’t matter as much as my life did. To think, my actions could have caused me my life by people I didn’t even know, but they knew me because of my actions before. It doesn’t make sense to me. How does any of this work in this kind of world? I am the one in trouble, but yet these men getting nothing for their actions. “I tried to warn you that going off on your own isn’t the way to solve your problems.” Again she was right. My desire to move on myself over powered my vision or the success I thought I had. In fact I played right in their hands and made my self, the center of attention. I could blame it this time on the pain I was going through or the emotions the filled my body, but in fact I could only blame it on my actions I decided to take. I was at fault and I am the failure. “My parents were right.” Safina looked down at me with a surprising look. My head tilted so I wouldn’t have to look at her, but the feeling of her presence made it more difficult to admit. My whole life was spent around believing in what I thought was right, and all along I had dropped my own beliefs in the process. I am no representative. I am just another person with some different ideas. “I am a failure.” Safina's eyes widened, “No you’re not. You have had a few problems but not a failing life.” I shook my head and finally had enough strength to sit up and look her in the eyes for once. My face was drained of energy and my body started to shake. Safina grabbed my hand and held it tightly as I started to worry about what was going on. “I need to change. I have been the same way my whole life and I am tired of being treated the same.” I had to leave again. I couldn’t stay here and have her see me like this. I was weak, nothing much more I could say. I could see she was worried as well because usually she leaves or dos something constructive to take her mind of things. I had to keep my self together, not only for my sake but for hers as well. I couldn’t let her see me like this ever again. The sun beamed in through one of the open windows and I couldn’t help but look outside. It was different then what I was use to. No cars making loud noise and no people to endanger my life. No Thailand period. For the first time in as many days there was no fear, no anger, only still calmness. “I’m leaving,” I told Safina just out of no where. She looked back at me and shook her head; “You want this to happen again. I can’t keep saving you every time.” I was insulted by that comment, and she knew it to, but it had to be said. I looked at her with her beautiful face still gleaming in the sunlight. “If I don’t leave, I will wonder what I really could have become.” Somehow I think she understood what I was saying. The worry in her eyes seemed to disappear and she talked calmer. I still wasn’t ready to leave, with the deep cuts and wounds; I still needed to heal first. But it always wondered me, why I left my beliefs in the first place. I was fine before, but all of a sudden everything change. The glass seemed to be empty instead of full. “I’m coming with you.” This surprised me even more. I looked at the window again and watched a few leaves fall from the maple tree that was planted just a few feet from the house. Telling her no would be a wrong choice, so what else could I do? I turned around and faced her, adding a smile and a gentle nod. She smiled back and left the room quietly. I still had much to think about. I laid back down on the bed and tightened my bandages around my head and ribs. I looked up at the ceiling looking for some kind of answer. But this wasn’t something you can get from a spirit. This was a human decision that I would have to make. The choice was up to me, whether I wanted it or not. “I did die back there. Now I can restart my life with a clean slate.” I closed my eyes and slowly drifted away, leaving behind a life that I for sure would not miss but would very well live over again if need be. The breeze added a gentle calmness to the air and I feel fast to sleep. §Fin§
