A Darker Age for Me

5 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 5, 2002 Tom, it was all just a bad dream. Just a bad dream. Maybe you should wake up now and forget about what has happened. You’re a new man. You’re a hero. And so I woke up startled, my eyes glaring side to side, looking for a predator that wasn’t there. My head bobbed back and forth, listening for that sound that would alert me to whoever was coming… “Who’s there?” I demanded, still squinting in the darkness. There was but a light creeping out from under the door, and shadows passed from them from time to time. I realized I was in a bed and that I was stark naked. I covered myself with my blanket, still confused. The door suddenly opened, revealing a beautiful blonde nurse who held a medical tray in her hands. She smiled as she saw me and clicked the light on. I brought my hand to my face to block the light. “Ouch.” “Sorry,” She replied in a soft tone. She walked over to me and placed the medical tray near my lap, checking my IV drip. “How are you feeling?” “Confused,” I replied, shifting uneasily in my bed. “From the looks of it, it seems that I’m in a hospital.” “So you’re not a dumb wrestler jock after all,” She said half jokingly. “Sorry, but everyone gets weird when a celebrity comes in. All the nurses talk.” So it would seem. While maybe the most mild mannered man might have gotten his rocks off at this scene – well, you know, being wildly butt naked in front of a beautiful young nymph, I had other ideas. “Is my girlfriend here?” I suddenly asked, breaking any dirty thoughts that either of us might have had. Oh, I doubt there were any. “We called up a Natalie Dischner. She was the name in your wallet to contact if there was an emergency. Is that who you’re referring to?” “Yeah, that’s her,” I said, feeling relieved. What would she have done had she found me in such a precarious position? Most likely, she would have acted like nothing had happened until we were alone, and then she’d cut off my manhood. No, I’m not joking. “She said she’d be by later. Something about being up to her ears in compost.” Natalie, or Nat as I called her, was a reporter for the local news. She was damn fine at it to, except for the fact that no one else besides me seemed to give a damn. She was always getting the worst stories to cover, and she felt like shit for it. I wish I could have helped her, but there are only so many wrestling “exclusives” to fill her in on. She told me she had been working on a story on reusing old compost in creating a better, cleaner Bay Area. So much for investigative reporting, huh? Here comes more shit. “I’ll have to ready myself for that conversation…” I murmured. The nurse looked back at me with a puzzled look. “What was that?” “Oh, nothing,” I replied, shining back one of my winning smiles. It was that smile which landed so many women time and time again, but I began to see it’s effects were wearing off. Not even the beautiful nurse was incredibly charmed by my own flirtations, and I sighed softly and attended to my blankets. “So what’s with the birthday suit?” She tapped the IV once more, making sure the IV would drip. That’s good to know. “We ran out of gowns. I hope you don’t mind, but it’s only for the night. You’re only here for observation.” The hospital ran out of medical gowns. Nice to see that our fine medical institutions are skimping out on their patients. By the way, wasn’t that a Jaguar I saw the head doctor driving? “Observation for what?” “Well, you took a nasty shot to the cranium,” She said, checking out my medical report. “Apparently one of your wrestling buddies took a glass bottle to your skull. You have a mild concussion, not to mention the fact that you wrestled after that.” Now I remembered. Duke Williams decided to introduce one of his alcoholic beverages to my head when I wasn’t looking and I had to be carted off backstage. They told me not to fight RAV that night, but how could I say no? This guy turned his back on the fans and on all of the heroes in this world. He had to be taught a lesson. Unfortunately, the teacher was away and the substitute was in. I wasn’t the same Legend Inc. that night. “Boy that sucks…” I said, taking a deep breath. As I did, I noticed my heart rate slowing down. Beep beep beep…beep beep…beep beep… “Get some sleep Mr. Lexian.” She walked out of the room and turned off the light, leaving me to my thoughts. I turned my attention back to the heart monitor which beat like a metronome. I had always been fascinated by the little device that my piano teacher would sit in front of me as I played for her. Stay on the beat, Thomas. If you can’t do that, then you’ll never become a world class musician. That was good ol’ Ms. Wright, an eighty year old hag that hounded me for the better part of my adolescence as I strived to be a pianist. Funny how she was right. “Don’t let that ticker of yours stop beating now…” An old man lying in a bed next to me smiled he sat up in bed. He had tubes running into his nose and his skin was graying. Not his hair, but his skin. He was old, and it showed as clear as day. I had to take a step back and remember that respect was something you gave to someone who has earned it. He definitely did. “I’m sorry, am I keeping you up?” He just snorted, or at least tried to laugh. “Hell no. I’m too old to be trying to sleep, kid. When my body wants to sleep, it just sleeps.” I smirked. “Well, I understand that much. Sometimes you can’t mess with mother nature.” “Damn right,” He muttered. His gaze changed to the bandage on the side of my head. “Looks like you took a pretty bad shot to the head, kid. What are you going to do to the guy that did that?” I shrugged. Not because I didn’t want to answer the question, but because I didn’t have an answer for him. He rested his head on his right hand and looked up at the ceiling. “I fought in ‘Nam, you know? I saw some of my best friends get taken down in that damn war, and half of those guys I hadn’t even known until I joined the service. As I watched as they died, I vowed to myself that I would get revenge.” “Did you?” He wouldn’t answer the question. “Kid, until you’ve been in combat, you don’t know squat. Revenge is a dangerous answer to any challenge. Your emotions get in the way and you don’t think straight.” He tapped his head. “This is what keeps you alive, not this…” He made a fist and began punching at his pillow. “Revenge is sometimes the only answer.” I answered his point simply, making my position quite clear. “I know you’ve seen a war; one that I could never understand. But life is funny. Everything is interconnected somehow. While you think fighting might solve everything, I disagree. I think you would do better to try to resolve your problems away from the ring.” My surprise caught me off guard. “How did you know I was a wrestler?” He turned onto his other side and began to try to sleep. “I don’t know one damn person that doesn’t watch wrestling, so I jumped on the bandwagon. You know, I kind of like that Duke Williams guy. He’s just rough and tough.” “Like sand paper.” “Don’t get me wrong. I mean, I don’t think what he did to you was right, but half of that crowd was cheering for him, and the other half hated his guts for doing it.” “Sometimes the good guy doesn’t always win.” “Sometimes the crowd doesn’t see the good guy,” He replied, leaning back towards me. “Now I’m not going to stand up for the other half of that crowd that was cheering him on, but you can’t fight the masses. As long as the crowd wants him to win, he’ll keep doing what he’s doing.” I shook my head. All heroes face a cross roads and wonder what the right thing is. Do you stoop to the level that your opponent has created, or do you raise the bar higher, and force him to play your hand? “I’m just not sure on what I should do,” I said, staring back at my blankets and sheets. “Sometimes I hide behind my superstar and superhero reputation to save me. But when it’s all said and done, we’re just men.” “Ain’t that the fucking truth? But hell, no one said you can’t turn Duke around. Or that Rent-A-What guy. Maybe you just have to beat some sense into them.” “I tried to on Turmoil and look what happened…” I pointed to my head being bandaged up and all of the bruises on my back. “Even Jesus had his ups and downs. Did he ever give up?” I nearly gagged on my tongue. “Are you comparing me to Jesus?” He turned back on his side to sleep. “Are you saying you aren’t here to save PCW?” As I pondered it, he quickly added, “And turn out the light, would you? Makes my eyes all watery and shit…” I leaned over the bed to hit the light switch. Am I the messiah to bring PCW back to side of good? Yeah, I guess I am.
