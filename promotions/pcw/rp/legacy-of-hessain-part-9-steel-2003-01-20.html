Legacy Of Hessain Part 9 - Steel

20 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 20/01/03 Recap: Hessain is in the tunnels of his hometown, which will lead to Magnumek's Bastion. However, Magnumek has enslaved the townsfolk by promising them protection from an invading disease. To escape it, they must become ghostly spirits fused to armour. The remaining six survivors of Coorhagen must now turn on Hessain in accordance with Magnumek's pact... * * * Legacy Of Hessain part 9: Steel I stood before six ghosts - both literally and metaphorically. Magnumek had a cruel sense of irony as the armour that had become one with their spirits was that of the lesser Serafan Knights. It seemed that Magnumek had the idea of rebuilding his army and exercising his authority, particularly by dressing his subordinates in such lowly standards. Clad in his high Serafan General's mail and helmet with his plumed crown, Magnumek was certainly vain and bitter. But enough of their master, I would need to take on six of Coorhagen's swordsmen. Bearing in mind that I was steeped in the same technique and hand as they, this battle would be difficult trying to defeat six of the same sword. I would need to reach deep inside myself and find another of the tricks that the Necroprankster, Morgazius, had given me. What could I use? I knew of no spells, no forms and no disguises that would help me here. 'Hessain - their rage blinds them as yours fuels you. Turn their misery upon them.' A voice spoke inside my head... Morgazius? 'Of course it is is, Squire. In times when the number of enemies is too much, you must touch their sorrow and bring it to their neighbour's face.' 'I... I don't understand' I thought. 'Use the magic of your disguise upon them... INSPIRE HATE' Morgazius leered, before I felt alone once more inside my mind. With no other option, I had to go on his suggestion. Utilizing my magic, I would proceed to cast the disguise spell upon them. Rather than the usual cool blue breeze, a scorching ruby flame ignited and swam into their stomachs. Their eyes glowed like burning embers and they looked among each other with distrust and bloodlust. I knew not which one struck the first blow, but within the blink of an eye, the massacre had begun. Gauntlets and swords, maces and axes, all clattering as the blinding anger overcame the minds of these ghostly warriors. I watched as carefully as I could. Their moves were identical to mine thus far, so I knew precisely what to expect when the spell had worn off. The spell must have taken a portion of my hatred, as I seemed rather amused and content to watch these people behaving as I probably did when I first rose from my crypt and spied my killers. The hacking and slashing went on for minutes until the orchestra of the melee died down to a a solo artist. As the spell wore off him, I laughed heartily, amused at the look on his spectral face as he saw not the nightmares that I had conjured, but the slain and dented armour that belonged to his former cohorts. No longer present within their metal shells, the wounds that were inflicted upon them by their weapons had the same effect as would be expected. Except, however, while the plague would not touch them, they would fall in combat if their armour was pierced, allowing the soul to leak out, or if the armour was pulled from place. 'HA!' I scoffed mercilessly. 'Killing your own kind? Your mind was lost along with your flesh.' 'MONSTER! You tricked me! I saw my son, wandering towards me with open arms and that horrid slime leaking from every hole that his face allowed, begging me to end his pain! And my wife! My dear wife! YOU BASTARD!' he cursed, swinging his sword in desperation and anger. 'You have no heart, wraith. And now, I shall be a plague upon the Serafan, just as Magnumek has been a plague upon this town!' I answered, mocking him and milking every last drop of contempt from him. He shouted in rage and with a face that bore the visage of revenge itself, he charged at me. And just as I had hastily taken Morgazius' offering of rebirth without considering the consequence of living death, this un-named spectral knight had grabbed revenge without thinking of the after effects. A lesson he learned too quickly as his rib cage was opened up and the mail he wore broke under the pressure of my sword. And a lesson that he would never be able to put into practice as his soul dripped like water from his heart, onto the floor and into whatever oblivion he faced. Only an empty, low-ranking officer's uniform of the Serafan served as a temporary gravestone to the desperate and pathetic creature... and now the tunnels beckoned further. * * * Such boredom of marching along these endless labyrinthine passageways would drive a mortal man to distraction and deal insanity to those with the patience to press on as long as I have. But it is hard to corrupt a tune that plays only one instrument. The infinite darkness of these tunnels led to many mishaps on the way out and I was still going on blind luck. The chill of their cavernous emptiness was making me curious as to my whereabouts, for Nosgoth has only one area that the snow falls in. Nosgoth's great mountains to the north... I must have been getting closer... and closer... and clos - ARGH! Uhh... another 'mishap'. Growing less patient, I began to stab and thrust at the cave's walls. The Coorhagen roads were often riddled with pot-holes, so these caves must be very weak. I gave one last heave and my blade broke through. Nothing could have prepared me for what happened next... something I had never before witnessed and experienced for the first time in my life, making my spine freeze and my blood run cold... 'GRAAAAAAH! BLOODY SNOW!' The icy element attacked every nerve ending that still had feeling. It was as thought my skin had been flayed from my body and a sack of salt replaced it. I closed my eyes and swam like a fish to the top, slicing in all directions and screaming wildly. I did more damage to myself with my blade and my mouth closed as soon as the disgusting snow had entered it. Finally, I felt myself no longer surrounded by the overwhelming pain and opened my eyes. My armour had taken most of the damage but still, a few specks of red fell onto the pure white snow around me. Surrounding me on all sides was a gigantic void of white, save for a darkened, black structure in the distance. As I trudged on through the blizzard, his flame red hair began to fade to dull ashen grey with the coating of ice and my skin was slowly turning blue. How humiliating that one of the most feared predators of man may claim sanctuary in places surrounded by such a simple element as cold. Then my heart almost sank as I spied something ahead - a cliff. After all this way, my path was cut off by another natural enemy. How odd that Magnumek would set his home in such a place that was so cut off and alone. Then again, if Skitzrapto had poisoned so many minds in his selfish pity, logic would be ruled out. Not to mention that from the look of Coorhagen, he could easily find life in such a lifeless area. Not to mention that he also creates existence through his magic of forcing metal and soul together as one, although these abominations could not be called life, truly. Regardless, I would have to risk flying across in my bat form. The blizzard would cloud my judgment and my wounds would be carried over, but it was a risk worth taking to get to the knight who had taken the last pride that I possessed as a human. But as I prepared to change, I felt a strange presence, like someone was breathing upon me, then an apparition appeared in the sky. It parted the snow from the sky and made the route ahead clear. In front of me, a mere stone's throw upwards was a huge construction of iron and steel. Spikes littered it's many walls and ramparts and it balanced by impossible means, with the lowest point of the castle curving upwards like a mountain's peak, inverted to make scaling it impossible. And against the sky, I saw Magnumek's face... or rather, his helmet. His ghostly eyes narrowed at the sight of me, then I felt myself being pulled as though the whole mountain had flipped and gravity defied it's masters. Before I could comprehend or work out a single thought, my entire surroundings had changed. I stood at the gates to Magnumek's Bastion and stood in marvel at it's form. The snow was still thicker than ever, and while there was no telling the danger that lay inside, it is true that one cannot ward snow off with a sword. I marched up to the enormous gate and looked for some means of entry. No handles, no smaller doors within it, no crawl spaces, no method. It seemed that Magnumek thought me a fool... or at least wanted to humble me. Doing the only thing I could, I raised my sword and rammed the hilt into the cold metal. The hollow echo resonated throughout the white courtyard and it seemed as though the whole fortress shook. There was another noise from inside and the doors began to swing open and inwards. I pressed against them and as soon as a gap wide enough for me appeared, charged inside. Finally, I was free of the snowfall's soft torment, but perhaps I had foolishly accepted a mixed blessing. As the doors behind me closed, the echo came a thousand times more loud, shaking my soul to it's core and vibrating me brain from it's moorings in my skull. I fell to my knees and covered my head, wondering how many more torments I would have to endure until I would be released from this curse. As the noise faded, I stood to one knee, becoming aware that another noise was replacing the vibrations of the first. It was not a random noise, but had a pattern, a shape and a form. It was a voice and they were words. And those words were: 'Welcome, Hessain... welcome to the home of your hunter!'
