Turmoil Before Turmoil

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01.05.04 (RP#3 TT) Cuzin Ed is in trouble. Not only is his house about to be repossessed by the state, but he’s been imprisoned for drug-trafficking. And without the helpof his best pal...and PCW's resident redneck, Abner...he's finished... After Cloud Nine was abrupty canceled, and the great empire of PCW reduced to ashes, the two, at one time, were seemingly inseperable. But alas, the cousins parted ways. After returning back home, disheartened by the failed Airship Tour (a noble attempt by Abner, in which he almost broke through), they thought it best to call it quits rather than try a different circuit. PCW was their home. And sleeping in someone else’s bed just wouldn’t have felt right. Abner, skilled in all muscle-trades, was able to find sanctuary in the woods, via the logging industry, while fat, lonely and uninspired Cuzin Ed, was not. And so he turned to drugs; and with that, the mess he’s got himself in today... ---------- Abner strolls almost merrily down the lane, wind in his hair. He takes deep, purposefully strides, jingling his change with his hands in his pockets. The cool, breezy wind wisps across his head and over his face, reddening his cheeks and exaggerating the spread of the part in his hair. Sniffing, he wipes a hand across his nose, numbing the fiery cold that has dimmed his senses. He blinks, eyes watering, searching for the perpetrator of the stinging sensation that has manifested itself in his vision. Abner has just arrived in Atlantic City, New Jersey, the home of the PCW. It’s a chilled city night, stars on the horizon, but unavailable to the naked eye, blurred by the pollution of the city’s industrial machine. (Yes, gambling causes smog.) Steam rises far above the sky on all sides, and Abner, not interpreting as most do, this as a sign of his smallness and helplessness in the might and specter of the whole, it calms him, soothingly reminding him that all has not yet been conquered, and that there’s still work to be done. Though he may be big, there’s always a new plane to rise to, and new dreams to aspire toward. He inhales a deep breath of the thick, starchy industrial air-no doubt rimmed with pollutants-and coughs, projecting his air forward, as if to toss it off and force the burden on some other person. The rough metallic sound emitted from his throat reminds him of the almost machine-like buzz of the tools around him, and he cannot help but picture himself as robot: an extension of technology and 'human ingenuity', an extension of industrial might, and the phoniness and dry bitterness, and inhumanness of the world around him. He laughs.This is nothing compared to say, Chicago, and yet, for him, a rural Vermonter, an outsider...it's like hell. Coming to halt by a large, red stop sign, he pulls his dark trench coat up around his body, and buries his head into its protective shade. He reaches into his pocket for a cigarette. Really, he’s not a smoker. And in fact, the only time Abner felt the need was while fishing back in Vermont, where he’d light up a cigar or two…but only to keep the bugs away. However, over the last few months, depressed and uninspired, he’s made a bit of a habit out of it. A bad habit. Lighting up the stick, using the coat as a shield against the wind, his snickers, taking in the irony of the situation. (Until recently leaving prison, he’s never had a driver’s license, and as someone with little respect for such courtesies to begin with, he just happens to stop at stop sign, thinking of its useless significence to someone on foot.) He squints his eyesan stares out into the darknee. With two cold fingers, he pulls the cigarette back, slowing blowing out smoke as he flicks the tip sending ashes falling to the ground. He imagines he’s a dragon or some other mythological beast, blowing flames into the air, the cold night magnifying the intensity of the beam, and the smoke sticking, stuck in the air, as if it could, at any moment, freeze and fall down to the pavement, shattering. Suddenly, he looks up and sees a bright pair of headlights cutting through the mist, coming right towards him. His smiles disappears. He shifts from foot to foot in irritation, and as the vehicle approaches, he tips his head to the side, as if in the process of making a big decision. He shrugs, dropping the cigarette to the cold, wet hardness of the sidewalk below, and stomps it out, dragging his big boots across it. Letting out a little cough, he covers his mouth and averts his eyes as the vehicle pulls up alongside him, and someone steps out of the car. It’s a long, black limousine, with dark, tinted windows…though, with it being dark and all, it would be nearly impossible to make out the insides anyway. Nonetheless, for a brief moment, Abner squints his eyes, trying to peer in, though the man is already out of the car, and he knows who he is. 'Howdy,' the man whispers, stepping up to Abner. Still looking down at his feet, Abner starts with the shiny black shoes and makes his way up the man's legs, looking him over from bottom to top: hislegs, his torso, his neck, before his gaze finally rests on the man’s face, their eyes interlocked. 'Hey,' Abner responds, gruffly, pulling out another cigarette. The man shakes his head, and holds a hand up to Abner. He then goes into his pocket and pulls out one of his own, putting it in Abner’s mouth. Abner goes along with it, albeit unhappily. After accepting the gift, the man pulls out a lighter and lights Abner’s cigarette for him. The redneck nods, appreciatively. 'So,' the man asks, 'how’s life treatin’ ya?' Abner snickers, removing the cigarette from his mouth. 'Ain’t too bad, guess,' he responds, suddenly aware of his hickish accent. And so he tries to take on a more professional tone, adding, 'but I guess, well, could…I mean, I could…be better.' The man nods his head approvingly. 'Yes. Yes, Abner we all could... We could all be better.' Abner shivers, from neither the cold nor the sound of the man’s voice, but at some odd force, a sixth-sense perhaps, warning him of danger. He looks down at his feet. 'Well…,' the man says. 'Well?' Abner asks. 'Are you in, or not?' 'I’m in,' Abner says, raising up his hands defensively. 'Just making sure,' the man says, adding, 'but you know what this means, right?' 'Sure,' Abner grumbles, dropping the cigarette to the ground, stomping it out. The man reaches down and picks up Abner’s trash, sticking it in his pocket. 'Now there’s no reason to pollute.' Abner rolls his eyes, taking in the irony, and ready to light up another cig. But he thinks better of it. He folds his matches up and places away in his pocket, and faces the man, full attention. 'Alright, but if this falls through…is he...is Ed covered?' 'No,' the man shakes his head, sadly. He steps up to Abner and looks the redneck square in the eye. With a bit of urgency in his voice, he whispers, 'You know the gig. All cards on the table, no guarantees.' Abner lets out a sigh. The man continues on. 'But for now, of course, this is top-priority…and I assure you, you’ll be protected.' 'From what?' 'From who?' Abner asks, shying away from the man. The man doesn't bother to respond. And Abner knows why. He then looks back up, and mouths to the man, softyly, 'but I ain’t afraid of nothin’.' 'Indeed, you’re not.' 'And if need be,' Abner reminds him, 'I’d be willin’ to go the whole way…the whole length, just to be sure that he’s okay.' 'You would, my friend, I'm sure of it...you would.' 'But make no mistakes about it,' Abner says, raising his voice, 'I ain’t nobody’s bitch, and I ain’t for hire for no one after my cousin’s free, got it?' The man takes a step back, shielding his face from the redneck’s glare. 'Ah yes, I understand completely.' 'Good.' 'Good.' 'So it’s done?' 'Done… Well, for the most part…' Abner takes another step toward the man, curious now. 'See, as an…um….well, for insurance, we’ll certainly need to keep tabs on you, wherever you’ll go….but I’m sure you wouldn’t mind the company.' 'Oh, so you don’t trust me?' Abner asks, threateningly. 'No. No, no, not at all,' the man takes a step back. 'It’s just one of our policies. ...Eh...business, you see. Covering our own asses. That’s all… You know how it goes.' 'Yeah. I know how it goes.' They both [ause, quiet for a second. 'Just remember, heed caution, and take care of yourself…' the man whispers to Abner, slowly backing away now. 'Yes,' Abner answers, 'and you the same.' The man smiles, lighting up a cigarette of his own. 'Certainly. Certainly I will. Just remember, be quick, be fase, and don’t do anything stupid. Drop-off by two-thirty, and pick-up at five, and have the package in our hands by midnight. And then you’ll get your cut.' 'I don’t want no cut.' The man shrugs. 'Eh…think of it as a bonus.' 'I don’t want no bonus. All I want is for my cousin to have his place back.' 'Don’t worry my dear friend…don’t worry my dear friend, he shall. He certainly shallll. Ed‘s a fine young fellow, and it was a shame to see him get in such trouble…which is why you want to go through with this, and which is why you must come through. Abner,' the man says, putting a sympathetic hand on the wrestler's shoulder, 'he’s depending on you.' 'I know.' 'And the bank…the blasted bank!… Remember, you only have until January 21 to the final payment...or his house, his belongings, his property…it’s mortgaged off to the state... Gone...all gone. 'Yep.' And with that, the two shake hands before parting ways. Abner heads off down the sidewalk in the opposite direction of the man, as the figure unbuttons his black overcoat and gets back into the limo, with the help of another man. Abner politely waves as the limo drives by and heads out of sight, vanishing into the night. 'Asshole,' Abner mutters, spitting a wad of tabacco out of his mouth.
