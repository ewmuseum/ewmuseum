Doom and Deliverance: Two Roads Diverge

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 15 2002 A.D. (Excerpted from the memoirs of Bruce ‘Violence Jack’ Shanahan) Salvation and sainthood. Sin and blasphemy. Virtues from opposites ends of the proverbial spectrum, currently and supposedly epitomized by Tyler Nelson and Xavier Kannon. BAH! What does an ego-driven executive know about sainthood? And to what depths does that repugnant Kannon comprehend the wages of sin? No man in this downtrodden and decrepit company has plumbed the murky reaches of humane integrity and wickedness to the far reaches of which I have! One must be willing to forgo and even sacrifice sanity to gain a conception of true irreverence and the sweetest deliverance. Only when one peers into the most lurid of the abyss and touches the sheerest of the macabre, as I have on more accursed occasions than I wish to speak of, can they truly assert their fulfillment. Beyond this material world lies a universe of spectral wonders beyond the capacity of the common mind, vast worlds of divine antiquity and ravenous evil alike where our natural laws are parodied and beings exterior to the most vivid of imaginations lurk. Indeed when those sinister telepathic emanations are made manifest into the nightmares I have perceived in these dark months of my life…..then a man may truly claim their resolve. Yes, I have seen it. But it is not a claim I relish nor do I taunt others with my (mis)fortune of the experiences. The situation in itself is bipartisan, two-faced in its irony. I have been afforded the opportunity to be enlightened unto knowledge which can help preserve the human race. But, as the drawback, I have been thrust into a world beneath our own where I constantly confront hideous minions of the dark whom shun the light and wage battle with the psychic emissions produced by abhorrent deities imprisoned for crimes against even more powerful masters. I have witnessed the madness echoed forth from the voracious minds of those wicked celestials, and even for me it was nearly too much to bear. I hold the PCW Extreme title. An honor devoid of flair or grace and deficient of the expertise and proficiency typically signified by other higher championships in the same profession. My business is brutality, ruthlessness is my brother-in-arms. I have never claimed to be flashy or a master of artistry, and to imply my kind as such is ludicrous to most. But to some, those defined as struck by lunacy by most I suppose, this style of battle truly is an art. And I am one of those madmen. To me, there is a certain grace to bloodshed. A talent not inherent in all, but a skill honed to perfection by the elite of my caste. I will protect my prize and my honor with all I have. Once again I have entered the havoc that is tournament competition, and already the memories of my past disgrace have returned to haunt me. In this I seek retribution, and in my ‘homeland’ of Primetime Championship Wrestling I vow to continue my rampage through all those claiming to be hardcore; in that I truly am the best in the world and I have the scars, and belt, to prove it. Bruce Blade will be no obstacle; I have fought too many of his sort to fret. Right now, my mind is focused on John Crichton. Like myself, Crichton make a deep impact in the past tournament competition. If he does so once again only time will tell, but for now we have a date on Fusion. Somewhere along the line, Crichton lost the lone trophy that defined him as a star of any accolade in our organization. Worse yet, he lost it at the hands of a lowly greenhorn. Now, like me, I imagine he seeks payback. And if he can’t have it against that wretched Pendragon, the next best thing for the fallen star is to battle the most tenacious maniac in the company. That’s where I come in. Ah, the irony is priceless. Two men of whom made notorious impressions in the past tournament, both now seeking redemption for various injustices, meeting on the field of battle. Like the commercial says, there are some moments money truly can’t buy. Alas for John, that’s where the similarities end. This is my territory. The realm of violence and bloodlust is under edict of the Empire of Extreme, and ruled over by the true God of Extreme himself. The powers-that-be handed him the wrong opportunity to prove himself. And for their wrongful judgment, Crichton must suffer the consequences. Neither of us are saints, and we both are guilty of sin. But, in this game, he who sins the most can, in a weird essence, become a savior……. * * * Bruce Shanahan paced restlessly across the peeling wooden planks of his shoddy, slum-based apartment. In the distance howled the ever-present scream of emergency sirens, drowned out intermittently by the heated cries of the neighboring couple in room 424 and the sporadic bark of the daunting St. Bernard across the avenue. Thoughts raced through his mind on all proverbial cylinders. The fate of Stryker, and his entire unit, now rested in the hands of the High Council. McCarver’s assembly had ended in precisely what he had predicted: the separation of two factions. The Coalition Loyalists, the veritable drones who marched obediently to the whim of the Council’s dictum; and those who retained an adamant distaste for the elders’ methods, the ‘rebels’. Shanahan had taken the side of the ‘rebel’ cause as he had been among the first, even before the recent catastrophes, to be critical of the current Elder regime. He could smell the corruption of those enigmatic leaders, those bastards who in their lack of vindication for their errors instead blamed the upright and honorable of the organization. And now Stryker was in the hotseat, a scapegoat for their crimes. A light tapping emanated from the door, causing Bruce to pause. He narrowed his eyes suspiciously at the flimsy wooden door, inching his way forward while reaching left for the aluminum bat he kept close by. Just weeks ago he had been spontaneously assaulted by a pair of estranged cultists, worshippers of the hideous Chaugnar Faugn, the elephantine ‘Horror From the Hills’, after the Delta Fox unit infiltrated and crushed a vile ceremony in the upper Appalachians. Though he had warded off the attackers, safety had become a much greater concern for the sturdy Boston resident. “Who’s there?” he boomed, hand closing on the tape-wrapped grip of the bat. “It’s Randall,” replied the voice through the barrier. “We need to talk, Bruce.” Shanahan peeked through the peephole for confirmation and, now satisfied, flung open the door. Craven, donned in a bright set of casual clothes, stepped into the room as his old friend locked the door in his wake. He placed a hand on the armrest of Shanahan’s worn-out tan couch, and shifted his gaze onto the bat-wielding apartment owner. “Must you really hold that thing?” quipped Craven with a slight snicker. “Makes me nervous, knowing how you are.” Bruce set the bat down and leaned the far end against the wall, striding across the living room and into the kitchen. He leaned on the chipped partition beside the door that separated kitchen and den and flung open the refrigerator. “Drink?” he gruffly offered, drawing out a Budweiser and casting a glance at Craven. “Thank you, but I must decline…..” “Quit bein’ so damn formal,” grunted Bruce, walking back in and setting himself into a brown recliner. “And take a seat, you’re makin’ me nervous.” Craven chuckled and sunk down into a chair opposite Bruce, who calmly sipped his beer before leaning back with his head resting on crossed arms on the headrest. “So…..what we need to talk about?” Biting his lip a moment, Randall leisurely turned his eyes to the far window. The moon waned above, a glimmering crescent in the clear and starry heavens. His eyes fell on Polaris and he seemed to fall into heavy contemplation for a moment. “Things have been coming apart in the Coalition recently,” he impressed despondently. “There has never been this much strife in the foundation in its long history. I couldn’t make the meeting the other night, but I heard what went on. I know the organization is divided into two factions now: those subservient to the Council and those who challenge them. It’s simply never happened, Bruce, the Council of Elders has never had their authority challenged.” “Maybe not, Randall. But they’ve never f*cked up like they have lately. Then to place the blame for their own mistakes on loyal Coalition members like Marcus doesn’t help their reputation. I don’t trust ’em and I haven’t since I joined your little group. Frankly I’ve been miserable ever since I signed on…..as if the nightmares and visions aren’t bad enough, then I get those cult followers bustin’ in my door because we broke up their insane little rituals. And your pals on the Council ain’t done nothin’ to make us harder for those lunatics to track down.” “Bruce, I don’t like them either. But God put me in charge as a governing member of the organization in this region….He put those men above me, it was all by his will. That’s what I don’t understand….” “Ain’t nothing to understand,” interrupted Bruce after a long swig of his beer. “These chumps ain’t like the guys you’ve had in the past. Something’s fishy and its about time people in this thing woke up and challenged it! It’s just a damn shame it took a string of virtual massacres and mix-ups to open their eyes to it.” “The organization is in shambles, unity-wise,” continued a downcast Craven. “And frankly I don’t know what the society’s future is. And I surely don’t know yet where my allegiance lies…” “Follow your heart, say some more prayers,” muttered Bruce, staring at Craven. It had been years since he had seen his friend so down, so dejected. Bruce knew the Coalition was Randall’s entire livelihood; his life’s passion was to ward the forces of evil from the innocent. He felt as if he was failing, and it was by no fault of his own. “Prayer…heheh….even I have almost begun to think God has forsaken us. They’re winning, Bruce. Cthulhu, Nyarlathotep, that daemon-sultan Azathoth….we’re playing right into their trap….” With that statement, it was obvious they were all in trouble.
