Transition -- The History (Part 1: HWF)

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: not entered *Three weeks ago, while in FBI custody while being investigated for tax evasion; narrated to Sandy Wong over a tape recorder.* Where did this come from? I mean seriously. Look at my whole history. I've never had to endure anything like this before. Sure, the use my eyes were taken from me, but that was a combination of many things. A vengeful-natured opponent, a panick-stricken control freak of a boss, a greedy implant specialist, a corrupt system... that all led to my 'life situation,' there isn't one person I could completely lay at fault for what happened. This is different. Violence Jack's taken everything into his own hands. No one has wronged me as much as him. Some have tried, a few more in recent history than others. Nobody came close. But look at everyone I've had problems with in some way shape or form throughout my career. Hey, I've only been in the business for a little less than two years, that's not a lot to remember. Back in the summer of 2001, I got my start, working in the Hardcore Wrestling Federation based out of New York City. I wrestled a few matches under the name Enigma, there was nothing too big there. They distorted my height, calling me 6'10' when I was just 6'8' always have been. Who did I face over there that was a consistent opponent? No one, really. There was the Undertaker, but he was just a prick. 'You're in my yard,' he'd always say. Some hick with a territorial complex. I could imagine him sitting on his porch with a shotgun, scaring away the Publisher's Clearing House people. Ed McMahon: But you've won $15 miillion! Undertaker: Get off my lawn! 'I'll beat you in a First Blood match!' Those were the first words I heard from his nervous voice. So we do a First Blood match. 15 seconds in, I blocked a punch and lunged in with a hard headbutt, breaking his nose. It was over that fast. He fell out, I'm sure I heard him say 'What the fuck?' I walked out without a scratch. Two days later (he really should've tried to heal) he challenged me to a hardcore match. You'd think a regular person would have learned their lesson. Then again, no one ever said that the average wrestler was a necessarily smart person. So after I walked out to the ring first, he rode his motorcycle down to the ring and went underneath it and threw in every weapon you could think of. And he slid under the bottom rope into the ring, stood up into a gut kick, and an Exploder '99 later, I'm the winner of the match in 7 seconds. He's never been the same since I dropped him on his head. He moves slower, his matches are less interesting. Apparently he has to hold down the younger guys. Or at least that's what all the internet smarks say. Something along the lines of 'Bikerwanker doesn't give clean wins to any rookies.' But they seem to hate Triple H even more. This, however, isn't about them. It's about me. I beat Undertaker's ass, but then again he's never been a threat to anyone. He was so accustomed to the scripted garbage that is the WWE that when he went into a real federation, he expected everyone to be afraid of him and let him win. He still has some big injuries from that term of service. Everyone of those injuries inflicted by myself.I beat him, simple. In the HWF, I wasn't the franchise, I didn't want to be. I saw what it meant: nothing. There were new independent wrestlers and WWE wrestlers walking through the door every day for a one-time show, and they would just saunter out to the ring, grab a microphone, and systematically destroy the English grammar in an attempt to demand a World Title shot. And they got it without working for it. Funny, there were 4 other titles in the federation, but because the president (who called himself 'Scooby' with a straight face) had trouble saying no to new talent coming in, the only title that was ever contested on regular occasion was the World Title. I myself won the Hardcore Title (which was interesting for me because I was ushered into wrestling because of a traditional puroresu wrestling event that I saw, and as such I did not use weapons in my defenses of the belt) and the Tag Team Titles with a guy called 'Krazy Krash.' He was just some guy, really. Young, [18] impulsive, quick to blame everyone but himself for his faults and quick to throw himself at the biggest of opponents just to make a point. He wasn't particularly large or talented himself, which was a problem. Some of the other people in the federation... The Rock joined and quit about 6 times in the course of a month, the same with Steve Austin and Shawn Michaels. They didn't do anything but ask for the World Title because they were big WWE stars. Sure, they lost any actual matches they had in the federation, but hey, there's always setbacks, right? They wanted respect from a fake show that they hadn't earned in a real sport. Rest assured, their asses were beaten good. There was a guy called Lieutenant Arm. He attacked the -- you guessed it -- arms. He left the federation after Chris Jericho (probably the only decent WWE wrestler to come through the federation) made him submit. And this just a few days after Jericho actually double-powerbombed the 350-pound frame of Beast to put him out in a Last Man Standing match. Scooby tried to wrestle, but he wasn't too good at it. About 10 different guys named Xtreme (exact spelling) jobbed to me within a week and were never seen again. Krazy Krash was okay, would've had a chance to learn the business better, possibly hit the bigtime. He took criticism and advice from me and me alone. I carried him to the tag team titles. He won the Intercontinental Title in a Ladder match, but then again his opponent was X-Pac. You can just slap guys from WWE and they'll be down for a 10-count, so that wasn't much of an achievement. It was frustrating to see random people walk in and be pushed over me, even though I was more talented a wrestler and charismatic a personality for them to ever compete with. Things turned around soon. Namely, the HWF closed because the fans got bored of 'Guy walks in and demands World Title shot' booking. They wanted some stability. They wanted a regular cast of wrestlers that they could count on seeing every time they came to a show. That didn't happen, so they left. I left. *Next session: The UWWC*
