The Flashbacks [Chapter Four] - Annoyances

29 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 29.06.02 Surprisingly things had gone very well. Pharaoh had finally, after days of preparation, defeated Duzza and was well on his way to once again becoming the huge superstar he was destined to develop into before he was injured at PCW Decimation. Pharaoh, and his new assistant Tom, whom we all met on Turmoil before Pharaoh’s match, swiftly left The Pyramid arena in Memphis, Tennessee and headed for Titu’s car, which was parked in the garage-style lot outside. Tom, whom Isiy had apparently sent to help Titu in some, currently unknown, sort of way stood with a look if doubt about his small wearily face as The Pharaoh clambered into the car, whilst explaining the different Gods of Egypt… “And Ra, though he is the most powerful god, has the ability to transform into Amun-Ra…” explained Pharaoh. “So what, he can just do that?” asked Tom, whilst straightening his greasy grey suit. “Yes…Amun-Ra is the most powerful of the great Egyptian Gods.” Tom stroked his chin, as though reflecting over the past few minutes in a very near to stylish kind of way. “I see,” he said at length, “Very…interesting.” Tom followed Pharaoh into the car and was motioned, by ‘The New Hope’, to sit next to him. “So where to next, Mr Xansiv?” Pharaoh’s eyes glazed over for a brief moment and then his voice saddened. “Tom, for a while now I’ve been suffering from a series of disturbing visions.” “Oh?” said Tom. He hadn’t been expecting that sort of an answer whatsoever. He moved uncomfortably, feeling that he may not want to hear what was about to be said. “Yes” Pharaoh sighed, “They hit me every now and then when I least expect, which is why we’re going to find an expert…a person who can help me to understand more about these strange occurrences. ” said Titu, whilst fastening his seat belt. “A psyche?” Tom protested, “But Mr Xansiv-” “No!” exclaimed Pharaoh with a frown, “Do I look that idiotic to believe what ever the hell some superstitious fool has to say about my life? No! We’re actually going to see a profession psychiatrist, Tom!” Tom sagged. He felt better that Titu hadn’t gone into great detail about the ‘visions’. He was a no-nonsense kind of guy… he didn’t like to be given details that were, either unnecessary for that moment, or none of his business, to be frank. Tom casually fastened his seatbelt and then caught a glimmer of light shimmer behind him in the rear-view mirror on the right hand side of the car… “But what about him?” Tom pointed to the back of the vehicle where a dark shape was now visibly sat. It lurched in the darkness of the cars rear and miraculously seemed to take up the entire back seat. Though it would seem impossible in the space it occupied the huge beast of a shape moved nervously, knowing that Tom had pointed it out. “What about him?” queried The Pharaoh, in a tone that indicated Pharaoh’s ‘so what’ approach to the huge shape. “Sir, he’s almost seven feet of pure destruction, you saw what he did to Duzza! What if he does that to a passing person in the street?” Tom felt that it was his duty to point out minor things like this to the ignorant Titu Xansiv. Lawsuits were something that Tom had no intention of getting involved in. Pharaoh shook his head to and fro, figuratively knocking Tom’s question away from his ears. “Do not be so foolish, Tom! He did that because I commanded him to, why, by the name of Isis, you are the only thing that went unaccounted for at Turmoil!” stressed Titu. “So what…we’re going to keep him with us, yeah?” Pharaoh quickly turned in utter shock. “Are you mad? Look at him! He’s a monster! Why, he could injure any passing pedestrian!” Tom sighed, finally realizing that whatever he said, no matter what it would or could be, it wouldn’t really mean anything to his new boss. “That’s just…what I said, sir…” said Tom, carefully. “No you didn’t! Don’t you think I’d know such a thing?” Tom opened his mouth to reply, but Pharaoh wasn’t one who was about to be outdone by his personal assistant. “I am Titu Xansiv! A former Pharaoh of all ancient Egypt! Tom, do not doubt me, for I know what I speak of!” Pharaoh grinned, smugly… Tom watched as Pharaoh put the shimmering golden key in the ignition, turned it speedily and smiled, in the way only someone who was quite applied to driving a fancy car and absolutely loved it would grin. He heard a rumble of the engine and then watched as Xansiv put his foot down on the silvery pedal with a stern look. The vehicle sped off into the darkness of the hot and lazy Memphis night. The tiny white diamond shimmered elegantly in the light cast down from the lights high above the office. Doctor Locksmith stared at it with great contemplation. He knew that Titu Xansiv would come back for the beautiful little thing, yet over the past day or so he had become quite attached to the minute gem, himself - it was, after all, an extremely valuable-looking item. He had studied it the previous night and, as he had first perceived, it was a 24-carrot gem, worth a lot more than its weight in gold. He could get an awful lot of money for this beauty, but he was an honest man and if Xansiv came looking for the diamond he’d give it back to the Egyptian warrior of Primetime Championship Wrestling. What he really wanted from The Pharaoh was an explanation for why he had left the hospital without permission. It truly was a mystery. The hospital wasn’t that bad in any case and the wrestler had been treated with great care and around the clock observation. However, now that Locksmith had the diamond he was confident that it wouldn’t be that long before he saw the familiar face of Titu Xansiv in his hospital again. “Excuse me doctor?” exclaimed the snobby middle-aged woman in front of Doctor Locksmith, in a tone that didn’t seem to be a question but sounded more like a command. The Doc snapped out of his trance-like state of mind and began to grip reality again. “Oh, I’m sorry Mrs Richwood!” “Oh, it’s quite alright, Doctor, don’t worry about me at all.” Mrs Richwood had an aptitude for making people who were respectable people look, and feel, inferior. The Doctor immediately noticed the sarcasm in her arrogant voice. “Once again, I apologize, Mrs Richwood.” He blushed. The Doctor opened a draw in his desk and slipped the tiny jewel into a black velvet bag. It was true to say that Doctor Locksmith had begun to become quite entangled in the excitement behind the finding of the diamond. He was a great lover of fiction books and had read many diamond heists and robbery styled tales He knew the velvet bag procedure like the back of his hand - it was practically compulsory. The Doc gently closed the draw and returned his gaze upon Mrs Richwood. For the first time in ten years of treating Mrs Richwood the Doctor suddenly felt superior… “Now, Mrs Richwood…about those haemorrhoids…” he said with a pleasant smile… Pharaoh had just about had enough of these people. Every time he asked for directions to a good psychiatrist he’d get answers such as ‘Suck my dick, asshole!’ And ‘Fuck you’, these insults reminded him of someone – the supposedly controversial, Corey Williams, his next metaphorical ‘roadblock’ in his wrestling quest. Pharaoh sighed as he was insulted once again by a passing citizen. He turned to Tom. “Don’t these people know who I am?” Tom looked around; the place they stood in didn’t do much to back up a comment such as ‘How can people from a place like this actually turn out like they have done?’ Most of the people walked around with struts that would put someone with an extremely over exaggerated prance to shame. Arms flailed, legs wobbled from side to side in strange and exotic fashions. Neither Pharaoh nor Tom had ever seen anything quite as theatrical as this before, and Pharaoh was six thousand years old from Horus’ sake. “I very much doubt it, sir…” Pharaoh tried, once again, decided to ask for directions to the nearest psychiatrist. He walked slowly, and very cautiously towards a youth in his late teens and sternly looked down upon him. The boy wore clothes that neither fitted him nor seemed, in any way, to be of a colour coordinated design whatsoever –certain people may have called him ‘cool’, some of the more inferior minded may have even called him ‘controversial’… “Excuse me--…” Pharaoh was rudely interrupted by the youth. “Man, why don’t you jus’ fuckin’ get outta my face, dude?” Pharaoh sighed and pondered this sentence. Not only was its structure and grammar completely wrong, but also he hadn’t said anything that would usually provoke someone to say something like it. “I’ll try again, shall I?” began Pharaoh, “Do you--…” Once again the boy interrupted. “I don’t care what you have to say, dude…just leave me alone, fucker…” Pharaoh grinded his teeth; he wasn’t a man known for his astonishingly exhausting patience. “Look, you ignorant little piece of shit, I want to know where in the name in Duat I can find a psychiatrist! Do you understand?” The youth was obviously unimpressed. “Man, get outta my face…” he said beginning to turn away from Pharaoh and Tom. Pharaoh was now extremely annoyed, and with good reason too. He grabbed the ignorant youth by the collar of his baggy t-shirt and dragged him up to his feet. Pharaoh grinned nastily as he picked the youth up off the ground and came face to face with him. The teenager was nowhere near as tall as Pharaoh and of no weight that Titu couldn’t easily lift. “W-what ya’ doin’, man?” exclaimed the frantic youth. “I’ll ask you one last time, and slowly so that your brain can take in what I’m saying, ok? Where… is… the… nearest… psychiatrist?” The teen struggled in the air, his pants blowing in the gentle night breeze as he was hoisted further into the air by the angry ‘New Hope’. “Look, man, I dunno, really I don’t! …Honestly!” Pharaoh sighed and shook his head before tossing the boy down to the ground. The teen struggled to his feet and began to run away, but not without shouting some more cowardly insults towards the now departing duo of The Pharaoh and Tom. “I really don’t know what’s happening to the youth of the world these days, Tom…” Tom nodded his head in agreement, “I concur, Mr Xansiv.” “I mean you can’t even get a little bit of respect from a child! A damned child for the love of Seth!” Pharaoh paused to take a long sigh of air, “Well anyway, we must continue our search! Come, Tom, we haven’t much time!” Tom looked at his watch. “Sir, you aren’t booked again until next Tuesday…we’ve got loads time!” Pharaoh turned to Tom with a stern expression protruding his handsome mug. “Tom, we’re going to Lexington, Kentucky for PCW Gold And Glory…” Tom looked on in surprise. “We are?” he asked slowly, raising an eyebrow. Pharaoh nodded. “But, sir, what on earth for?” Pharaoh laughed. “You will see!” Tom looked on doubtfully as Pharaoh began to walk away. “Now come on, we haven’t much time left!” Pharaoh rushed off into the darkness of the street, many of its lampposts casting only a slight speck of dull light down upon the concrete below. Tom followed…still his eyebrow raised. What did Pharaoh have up his sleeve for Gold and Glory? Isiy didn’t quite know why Peter StCross continued to ring the Xansiv premises, but it was beginning to get… weird. She dared not answer the phone, she didn’t know why; she just had a feeling deep down in the pits of her heart that it was a bad idea to answer. He had rung the house many times; many countless time and strenuous hours had passed since he began to ring. He just wasn’t giving up, at all. Another message was left and then ten minutes past. Had he given up at last? Perhaps… Isiy cautiously tiptoed towards the phone as though it was a sleeping dog. She stared at it for some time before deciding that perhaps Peter StCross had finally given up his search for ‘Charles’ in vain. ‘The Charm’ sighed a breath of relief. Maybe some peace and quiet was possible for the next few hours? But she still couldn’t be sure, and the guy had the house address, what if he came looking of ‘Charles’? Whoever this Charles fellow was Mr StCross wanted to speak with him immediately. She wanted Titu to be home with her… She stood and looked down at the answering machine. She slowly reached out pressed a large green button. “You have sixteen new messages.” Stuttered the machine’s pre-recorded voice. ‘BLEEP’ This is the last time I’m calling, Charles, you really do have to give me a ring, we have a lot to discuss…years have gone past since you vanished, and now its time you cleared your name as a run away who couldn’t handle his work! Look, ring me when you get back… Though the man had said that was the last time he’d call the house he had in fact rang another six times. Isiy placed her finger near to her lips and began to gnaw at her nails. Suddenly it hit her, she could ring Titu on his mobile - she remembered seeing him pack it into his loincloth. She turned off the answering machine, but didn’t delete the messages; no doubt Pharaoh would want to listen to them when he got home and picked up the phones receiver. She dialled a number and waited… “Finally!” exclaimed Titu, wearily. He looked up at the huge building in front of him. An illuminated sign read the words ‘Profession Psychiatrist Office’. Pharaoh grinned at Tom, who returned the smile with a nervous one of his own. “Yeah, so…are you going in?” Pharaoh looked back up towards the sign. “I believe so, Tom!” Pharaoh walked toward the double doors of the building. Inside lights blared giving the impression that the windows and glass doors could be compared to stars against a heavy black background of pure darkness. Pharaoh suddenly felt that things were going to be all right. He felt like this place was a refuge from the dark, socially challenged streets of Memphis. The inside of the huge place looked greatly decorated and well kept. Dark maroon leather sofa’s and pictures of great past physiatrists hung against the cream and dark red walls giving the environment a sophisticated appearance. Pharaoh liked sophisticated… “So…you are going to then?” exclaimed Tom. Pharaoh snapped out of the moment. “Well, yes, of course…” ‘RING’ The sound coursed through Pharaoh’s body like ice through a red-hot tube of iron. Pharaoh immediately reached for the pocket inside his royal loincloth and took out his mobile phone. He pressed the enter key and said: “Hello?” There was a long pause. “Oh, Isiy, my darling! How is everything at home?” Pause. “What?” Yet another pause… “But I don’t even know a ‘Charles’!” This time it was a lengthy pause; it lasted minutes and Tom watched with a curious gave as Pharaoh’s brow wrinkled more and more as the words of his gorgeous wife were spoken into his ear. “Come home? But I’m suppose to be setting off to Lexington, Kentucky for Gold and Glo--…” Pause. “Well, yes I know I aren’t booked…” Pause. “But--…” Pharaoh sighed as he listened to Isiy’s words. “Alright…I’ll set off in an hour or so…” Pause. “Well I was going to go to the psychiatrists…” Pause… “Because--…” Pharaoh sagged. “Fine! I’ll come home, right now…” There was a short pause, the shortest of them all. “Yes…goodbye…” Pharaoh pressed the enter key on his silver mobile again and put the mobile phone away. “Well?” asked Tom, breaking the silence like a hammer through thin ice. Pharaoh frowned. “Well looks like we’re setting off back home, apparently a guy’s been bothering Isiy over the phone or something, asking for a guy called Charles…” Pharaoh sighed. “So I don’t get to go to Gold and Glory, or visit the Psychiatrist.” He strained, a tone of spite in his voice… He began to walk away back down the street, away from the psychiatrist’s office, surprisingly. “So…you aren’t going to see the quack?” asked Tom, to which Pharaoh shook his head without a word. Tom thought carefully of what to say… finally he explained: “Well you’ll get to go some other time, sir…don’t worry about it.” Pharaoh didn’t say a word; he was obviously pissed off a great deal. “We haven’t got much time,” said Pharaoh, finally, as they edged closer to the car park where ‘The Jewel’ had parked his car. Tom pondered the sentence. Pharaoh had said that very same thing many times tonight, in his world there never seemed to be enough time. He was a very straightforward person when it came to time. Every minute more that one spent with Titu Xansiv was like another part of a huge puzzle being connected to the tiny part that you already had. He was a man shrouded in much mystery and even Pharaoh, himself, was beginning to get the unsure feeling that he didn’t quite know if he remembered some of his past. It all seemed glazed over… “You say that a lot, sir,” said Tom, referring to his thoughts about the connection between time and Titu… Pharaoh didn’t hear him, though; he was too busy trying to remember where he’d parked the blasted car…
