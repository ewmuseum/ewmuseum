Transition Into Madness: Final Episode (4), Chapter 2

30 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/30 (Excerpted from the memoirs of Bruce Shanahan) To reveal all the covert information I received during my tour of the Miskatonic facility would be unwise; if these memoirs were to somehow come into the wrong hands then I could be held accountable. Wrong hands you may ask? Consider the hysteria that would abound if this sort of information were revealed to the public, with the capacity of being confirmed with a search of the New Miskatonic grounds no less. Even worse, the hordes of insane cultists around the globe who mindlessly worship these macabre deities. In light of these possibilities, I will leave the facts as bare as possible while still making my intended point. My initial briefing on the events shaping the Coalition and its mission, and my own experiences, was scant at best; I was told the operations of the Blackhelm Coalition and its affiliated organizations were conducted under a top secret veil of confidentiality, and that it fell under the category of being on a need-to-know basis. I was told what I needed to know in light of what had happened in the past months, but they were very slick and obviously very cautious about what I was told in that small room. My primary informant was civilian; I say this only because I had picked up hints that this entire little outfit was operating under a heavy government influence and strict supervision by some secretive federal agency. Some of the agents present in that room and throughout my entire tour and ‘initiation’ also possessed a very militaristic air about them, in their swagger and demeanors. Captain Stryker’s subtle hint given in his Land Rover was a major influence on my suspicions; he informed me later that he and his unit only used their official ranks out of past military service, but I could see through his lie. You learn to do things like that on the streets where it becomes something of a necessity to save your life at times. After that conference I was guided through the upper floors of the New Miskatonic University laboratories. I could only crack a smile as I brushed by a plethora of the students who gawked at me in puzzlement. I guess I couldn’t blame them; I’d be curious too seeing a stocky, shaven-head, sunglasses-sporting street thug being guided through their alma mater’s hallways by a crew of officials clad in dark Armani suits with the attention of the vice president. I caught murmurs from those youthful yuppies, some saying I was a government official dressed fairly inconspicuously (I guess in truth I wasn’t that subtle in my dress, but it still was more ordinary than being donned in a $2000 business suit). I got a background on the university’s ‘public’ activities and credentials; I didn’t understand this that much, seeing as I didn’t actually give a f*ck about their student-driven programs for the homeless or the award-winning resource library. Obviously it was only to make my visit look normal in the eyes of the student populace, as I had to go through the normal student-accessed areas to reach the area displaying the true purpose of New Miskatonic across campus. After touring their modest mathematics wing and English building, I was taken across the manicured lawns of the central square on campus and toward the ominous cluster of bare structures built on the far north side of the property; I would have thought them to be massive maintenance sheds otherwise, seeing as they were even more plain then the rest of campus and in a uniform state of disrepair. The latter detail of which I assume is to downplay any possible suspicion as to what their purpose actually is. I read profiles of numerous cosmic antiquities, entities of such immense girth that Meanstreak and Stanislav would look like cruiserweights and deriving of such profoundly archaic origin it would make that antediluvian Nosferatu seem as if he should still be in a stroller or even still within the womb he was spawned from. These may not be the best comparisons, but I believe them to be suitable in light of common perspective. I was also shown a variety of ancient artifact utilized in the ‘arsenal’ of the Coalition and their clandestine affiliates. These otherworldly devices came in a variety of shapes and sizes and were used for different purposes. The star-shaped medallions excavated from areas of interdimensional activity, items I was told were vital in keeping our world shielded from invasion from the dark Limbo of which many of the CCD dwelled. There were the primeval discs with the texture of CD’s constructed by other elder civilizations, plates of a distinctly mystical aura found embedded in bedrock around the globe and which were heralded as the only means of suppressing the various ‘higher’ deities in their Earthly graves. Numerous charms and arcane talismans of such elaborate and mind-numbing fabrication that to produce even one would consume the entire existence of but the most skilled craftsman. I even got to see these primordial relics in action. All my skepticism was laid to rest when I lay eyes on exactly what the Coalition held in those mystical crypts; there was simply no briefing or dossier that could prepare me for the horrors confined in those walls beneath the surface of New Mioskatonic, the nameless things held in check not by the man-made steel itself but the magical artifacts embellishing the walls of their humble prisons. A range of subspecies comprising the CCD underling base, some malformed and bloated with the features of numerous Earthly animal species and others that defied logical explanation; I simply don’t have the words in my vocabulary to relate their ghastly appearance. Some seemed even ethereal in guise, teetering on the brink somewhere between solid physical manifestation and existence on the spiritual plane (and in truth the staff was as stumped as I was, even amidst multiple decades of study). I was shown the nature of that strange paralyzing elixir from my dream of the ravaged PTC Tower which Stryker and his crack team had used to stun my attackers. The whole time I felt the steely gaze of their ‘officials’ beating down on me; I presumed they were being sure I didn’t snoop or look too much into anything and thus disrupt matters. Under this rather unnerving scrutiny I believed it best to simply nod and agree with whatever they told me and dump any questions into the back of my mind. The hospitality offered to me during my stay was admirable from most, though I can’t go without saying that the subtle feelings of hostility radiated from some of the staff diminished the friendly environment; to say I felt like a fish out of water is an understatement and I was subsequently gratified when my ‘tour’ ended and Stryker escorted me from the facilities of New Miskatonic and back to the regional airport outside Rutland. I remember his final words before boarding that plane back to Boston, my last few hours before what I hoped to be my first PCW title reign. “You’re a target now, Bruce. You know things now that a select few do. They see more than you know and when their wrath is unleashed on those they seek, it’s unparalleled to anything mankind has yet to unveil on this planet. God speed.” He handed me an amulet wrapped in tissue, something he instructed me to keep close at all times. It could save my life, or so was his assertion. It had a core of what looked to be shimmering quartz, surrounded by a bronze shell. Keep it close, in my pocket or around my neck, whatever I could. With that the PA system announced my final chance to board, and I left the captain behind as I boarded my flight. * * “Daveylicious with a brutal chair shot! Its gotta be over!” “Is this chump going to job or not? It’s getting late…..” Bruce reclines back in his old beige couch, bits of stuffing dangling from the armrest and cushion on his right. He takes a swig of beer and exhales a deep sigh as his brawny cohort Vince Armond leans over the bare counter that partly separates the ransacked living room and grimy kitchen, with Trevor Jenkins scouring the refrigerator for food. “Bruce, how many f*ckin’ times do ya gotta watch this sh*t?” he quips. “I’ve never seen anyone so damn enthralled by Monday night-style ‘hardcore’ matches!” “Rule one, Vince,” responds Jack with a nonchalant stretch of his arms, “is scout your opponents. Maybe the fact ya didn’t do that is why ya can’t even get a decent job in a competent federation anymore.” Armond crumples his empty beer can and pitches it at Bruce’s head, bouncing it off to clatter on the peeling wooden floorboards. “That’s what I think of your ‘major league’ federation, cueball,” snickers Armond. Trevor spins around, shutting the fridge door with a jab of his elbow. In one hand he clutches a fat pickle, biting through it with a ravenous snap of the jaws. Armond swivels his head to look at Trevor as the crunch of the pickle reverberates between the walls of the modest apartment. “Damn, ya sure that’s any good still? I don’t think Bruce has anything edible left in this apartment except for beer. It’s never a good sign when milk has chunks floating around in it…..” Trevor shrugs, brushing past Armond and trudging into the living room to slump into a worn dark-green recliner. ”Daveylicious wins! What a great match!....” “I think I’d prefer ‘travesty’ to ‘great match’,” snorts Jack, cracking open another beer and pitching the tab over his shoulder. “If it goes that way in Cincinatti tonight I give up on the idea of extreme matches in the States.” “Extreme wrestling in the big leagues,” mutters Trevor, crunching on the remainder of his pickle. “Ain’t that sort of an oxymoron, Bruce?” “There are exceptions,” he replies as Armond chucks another beer can tab at the back of his head. “I’ve seen ‘em wanderin’ here and there, might even get the chance to tango with a couple when they drop my ass in Cincinatti International. If either one of those clowns Frampton or Daveylicious actually escape that airport with the Extreme title then the entire match is a f*ckin’ charade. Either one of those suckers holdin’ a championship with any relevance to ‘hardcore’ is an utter mockery of all the battles I’ve waged in my godd*mn career….” “Jesus Bruce, if ya break out into another those damn death match stories I’m going to kick your ass out tha f*ckin’ window! Heheh.” Trevor chuckles at Armond’s crack in reference to Bruce’s continuous accounts of his overseas battles in the notorious death match circuit. There is a short silence before Armond sets his beady gaze on Shanahan. “So where tha f*ck did ya go yesterday? We stopped by and ya weren’t around. Your lazy ass is always around here when ya don’t have obligations to that damn PCW.” Bruce gawks out the window as the shrill howl of sirens passes. “Had to visit some people, Vince. Complicated sh*t I don’t wanna get into right now.” He glances at a circular clock hanging on the wall over the television and spouts a muddled curse. He draws himself to his feet with assistance from the armrests. “Plane’s headin’ out in an hour. I gotta bounce boys, but chill here all ya want. I’ll be back tomorrow sometime after I get this sh*t over with and pound a few yuppies into the dirt. Bruce walks over to the door and takes a hold of the shaky brass doorknob, snatching up his black travel bag already set beside the door on a stand. Trevor and Vince mutter quiet farewells as he walks out, peering warily at one another as the door slams shut and his footsteps echo down the adjacent stairwell. “He’s not tellin’ us somethin’,” iterates Armond. “Give him some distance,” comes the retort. “Bruce always has a good reason to hide sh*t. And knowing him all these years, whatever it is this time has him spooked….. ” (Excerpted from the memoirs of Bruce Shanahan (short entry) ) The Extreme title division isn’t a place for synthetic sacrifices to the trendy teenybopper culture. It’s not a place for pseudo-rebel lounge-acts like Cowabunga Connection or cardboard cutout wannabe-sitcom personalities of the mold Big Daveylicious fell out of. And I don’t give a f*ck about that lumbering, lobotomized gorilla Jay Sylo or that celestial reject Akiyama. The Rush can take his hypocritical racism and go back to whatever hole in the wall he crawled out of and the Chef can shove the Koran where the sun don’t shine. This is my world. It’s my time. It’s time for the Era of Extreme to begin. It starts tonight with a statement, and it rolls on when I destroy Duzza Sunday night as well. I will become this promotion's purgatory personified. And the fire in which they are all baptized. Welcome to exile, motherf*ckers.
