Don't Expect Me to Lie

31 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/31/02 I lean against the wall of a building, resting on the curb. In front of me, a rusty old Chevy lurks. Stolen of course...I drag off my cigarette, watching the smoke drift free of the confines of the earth. Heavy shades cover my eyes, even on this overcast day but no one seems to notice. From the inside of the car a voice sings over the radio. Cracked and broken, a tormented voice..... One I could most certainly relate to. 'And we continue on with back to back CD Thursday, with Nirvana Unplugged,' states the ever present disk jockey, 'right after a word from our sponsor.' Of course. 'Hey, can I borrow a smoke?' Somewhat dully I blink, and turn to my left. A child sits before me, no older than 13 at the most. Mexican, dirty.....quite the little stereotype ghetto boy. I chuckle a bit. 'Aren't you a little young?' 'Age doesn't matter, we all got habits,' he replies. I look at him through my sunglasses. He's quite right. 'Hm.' Producing a cancer stick, I extend it to him. He takes it from my grasp, pressing it between his lips. 'Light?' At this I nearly crack up, the thought of his body suddenly engulfing in flames running through my mind. 'What's so funny?', he asks me. 'Oh, nothing,' I reply. 'Here.' Reaching into my pocket and producing a lighter, I hold it before the cigarette. A flick....and nothing. Then another. Nothing. 'What's wrong, never used a lighter before?' 'Well, to be honest I've gotten used to spontaneously producing flame. Haven't actually used a lighter in a while, why would I need to?' This is what I should be saying. However, I merely sigh. 'Not in a while.' 'Meh.' Abruptly he snatches the tool from my hand, effortlessly bringing it to life. He then hands it back to me as I grumble. 'I could have done that.' 'Sure. So what's your name?' '.....Clyde.' This time it is he who chuckles. 'What's so funny?', I ask him. 'Dude named Clyde wearing sunglasses when the sun isn't even in sight, listening to Nirvana while sitting outside a building. Man, you nuts.' I merely blink beneath my dark shades. Seemingly noticing my growing anger, he stops. 'Settle down, didn't mean anything by it.' 'I know.' I stare blankly at the radio as the dead man continues to warble. Underneath the bridge The tarp has sprung a leak And the animals I've trapped Have all become my pets Again I chuckle at the irony. It's very, very true. 'You're a weird one.' And again, I look at the boy. 'And you're here speaking to me, close enough to my still running car that I could drag you inside and spirit you off to your grisly demise before anyone could even notice. What does this say about you?' He shrugs and drags off his cig, leaving me to shake my head and look at the ground. It's okay to eat fish Cause they don't have any feelings That's true as well. 'Mister? What are you smiling at?' 'It's okay to eat people, cause they don't have any feelings.' This again is what I should be saying, though I don't. 'Nothing. Nothing at all.' This little one's quite annoying, isn't he? I look to the sky, and it hits me suddenly. A short time ago he would have been dead already. Why isn't he? 'Mister, what's really your deal?' 'Didn't I tell you already? I'm a demon from hell. I'm trying my hardest not to kill you where you sit but you're making it very difficult.' 'What's your deal,' I ask him instead. 'What do you mean?' 'I mean why are you here asking me such things?' 'Because you're funny.' Somewhat wearily I blink again. 'How am I funny?' 'You've got a car running, you could be in it, away and listening to your nerd music in the blink of an eye. But you're here letting me bug you. Why's that, Clyde?' I ponder this. Why in the hell AM I here? 'Why the hell AM I here?' 'I don't know,' sayeth the little boy. 'Maybe you like being bugged.' 'What?' 'Maybe you actually LIKE being irritated. What the hell would you do if no one was around to irritate you? What would you do with yourself?' 'Be happy?' I state sarcastically. My small friend merely shakes his head though. 'You wouldn't be happy, you'd be even more miserable than you are now.' 'What do you mean?' 'I mean you need people around to bug you. If you weren't such a miserable dude you'd probably just shrivel up like a leaf and blow away in the wind.' What else can I be? 'Stupid radio.' Grumbling, I stand and begin to make my way around to my stolen car. Not looking back, I speak to both the child and to no one. 'Maybe you're right,' I say. 'Maybe I do need the irritation to survive....the hatred. It motivates me. And don't we all need motivation sometimes?' All sorts of motivation. Again I chuckle slightly, then open the car door. Sliding in, I continue on to myself. 'But that doesn't make us bad people.' 'Depends, it might make you a REALLY bad person.' Nearly jumping out of my skin, I look to my right to find the little boy sitting in the passenger seat beside me. 'How in the hell!?' 'Take me with you, just down the road.' 'No, get out of my car!' 'This probably isn't even your car.' 'Be that as it may, get out!' 'Come on Clyde, don't be a punkass.' 'Argh!' 'And change the channel, Nirvana sucks.' The kid reaches over and begins to flick the dial on my radio, I smack his hand away. 'Stop that!' 'Dude, come on!' Finally I'm overcome. Glaring at the boy through my shades, my voice rumbles forth. 'Hey kid, want to know a secret?' 'Sure.' For a brief moment there is silence, the song on the radio hitting a temporarily lull along with the sounds of everything around us. The small boy looks at me as I remove my sunglasses....the color drains from his face. I smile as he sees what rested beyond my shades....gaping holes where my eyes should have been. Tiny, pointed teeth gnash around the edges of them, as flame licks impossibly from somewhere behind. 'Curiosity killed the cat.' 'AH!!!!!!' The child screams out, backpedaling across the faded seats. Shutting his eyes tight, he fumbles for the latch, at last finding it and squeezing for all he's worth. The door pops open, sending him sprawling to the pavement. 'I hate kids.' Reaching over and yanking the door shut, I give a wink to the fallen boy with one malformed eye causing him to wobble, then faint dead away. Then in a squeal of tires, I vanish into the eternal darkness.
