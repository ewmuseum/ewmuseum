Welcome to the darkness

6 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 6, 2002 Feel the darkness. It’s all around you. I turned pale as I walked down the dark, abandoned alley. The sound of water dripping from the apartment overhangs was a quiet torture to a man that shouldn’t have been scared. But I was scared. I was so terrified I couldn’t walk two steps without looking behind me. Click. Click. Click. “Who’s there?” I demanded, turning a full 360 degrees and pumping my fists in the air. Sweat trickled down my palms, but I wiped the perspiration away with a frenetic motion. I had been walking about the city all day after leaving the church. The good Father and I had a deep talk about what it meant to be a true hero, and afterwards, he treated me to lunch. I tried to talk to him about life and other world current events, but it didn’t seem right. He looked at me as if I wasn’t a normal person – rather, a man with a destiny that no one else could understand. “Shit.” I pulled my jacket apart as the burning sensation being emitted by the amulet caused me to yell out in pain. It was glowing red, which meant only one thing… Vampires. It sounded ridiculous. Sure, I mean, vampires can’t exist, right? They’re fictional creatures, no more real than Big Foot or the Loch Ness Monster. I guess the average citizen could believe that, but I knew better. I wish I could be naïve like the rest of the world, but sometimes duty overrides all of that. The sound of footsteps above me caught my attention. I stared up into the dark night, and I could see shadows moving about. It was them. I took a hold of the amulet and rubbed it. It was burning hot, warning me of the impending danger, but I didn’t care. I couldn’t get lost in the moment, and the searing heat helped keep me cool. The irony never ceased to amaze me. Darkness was the enemy that I fought. The Dark Age was a power that I couldn’t stand. Tyler Nelson would eventually pay the ultimate price for his crimes, but for now, I would have to fight his cronies. I wouldn’t have to do it alone, not when I had my Crusaders, but even Robert fell to their evil ways. I tried to help Duke Williams. I really did. But he didn’t want my help, even when he was being used and abused by the Dark Age. What the fuck did he want me to do? Sacrifice my life for him? “Lexian…” The voice was sinister and familiar. I knew who it was immediately. Vwwwwwwwwwwosh. He stood in front of me, all six foot four of him. He wore a black coat with blue jeans and no shirt underneath. His shoulder length black hair hung uncombed and wet, probably something some unholy woman would gag and google over. He had that smile that made me want to rip his head off. He hid his fangs well, though. He should – he was over three hundred years old. “Alejandro…” I murmured, taking a step back to ready for any attack. “I haven’t seen you in a long time.” He flashed open his neck where a long scar made it’s way down the base of his neck to his shoulder. “Not since you ripped a gash in my neck the size of a large python. By the way, I’ve never repaid you for that.” I remembered the blow. We were struggling for the amulet during one of his crazed attempts at taking over the world, and he gained possession of it. A vampire with possession of the amulet would be able to walk around in daylight, be immune to holy water, and was basically a walking killing machine. But they weren’t invincible. My pocket knife basically proved that. “You’ll get your chance one of these days.” He took a step forward, easing his fists into a ball. “Maybe sooner than you think.” Just to be different, I took a step forward towards him. He flinched for a second, but then flashed his fangs at me. “So, you’ve gained more of a back bone since the last time we crossed paths. Good. I’m glad. That means I’ll enjoy killing you that much more.” I could feel him trying to enter my mind. His hideous voice was trying to persuade me into giving him the amulet, but I resisted. A normal human might be able to fooled that easily, but the amulet protected me. Poor, powerless little vampire. They feel naked when they can’t roll over your mind with their thoughts. “Nice try,” I snickered. “But to no avail. Or do you forget what the amulet does for me?” He snarled at me, showing his obvious displeasure for the fact that I still had some advantages over him. “It keeps you alive. That’s all it does. But once I get a hold of it, you will pay the ultimate price, Lexian.” I shook my head. “Oh, stop with the melodramatic bullshit. You still aren’t living in the 13th century. Oh never mind, you aren’t that old. I guess that’s why Alexandria is still the Vampire Master of the City, eh?” “You would be wise to restrain your words…” He was trying hard not to rush at me, mostly because he knew he was going to lose. I could feel the anger pulsating throughout his entire body. He wanted to rip me from head to toe. While that would put fear into most people, I felt that same feeling whenever I was around any vampire. It was a basic feeling that most vamps had for the Exeuctioner. After all, it was my job to kill them. “How is the old girl doing anyways? Still good after I roasted her with that torch I hope…” Woooosh. We were now face to face, nose to nose, and I could feel his breath crashing against me own. He was taller than me, so I had to look up at him, but I showed no fear. He could kill me, but not without a fight. Not without risking his own death. That would probably save me tonight. “You speak about Alexandria with a tone of respect,” He hissed, spitting in my face. I wiped the saliva away from me and knocked him back with an elbow to the jaw. He barely budged, only sort of giggled. “You’ve been playing with the other humans for too long,” He remarked, licking away a drop of blood from his lip. “Your fighting skills have deteriorated. Maybe you’ve been fighting muggers and drug dealers for too long.” I shrugged. “I can’t spend all of my time with you vampire losers. Besides, they’ve got more balls than vampires.” “How so?” I looked above into the sky, seeing ten shadows trampling about the rooftops. “How many of your lackeys do you have up there anyways? Ten? Twenty?” He looked at me with disgust. “You’re the Executioner. You tell me.” I paced back and forth, like an astute professor explaining a very difficult topic to a student. “You see, muggers and drug dealers don’t need that many people behind them. That gives them an aura of bravery in a way.” He laughed. “Chivalry in crime? You’re being ridiculous. You fight crime, yet you want to glorify them at the same time?” “I’m just saying, they don’t need lackeys when they get in some trouble.” Alejandro threw his hands in the air, laughing like a hyena. “So is that’s what bothering you? The odds? I could put them back in your favor. Would you like me to tell all of my creatures to leave so we may fight?” “I have no reason to kill you.” He snickered again, this time crossing his arms. “Try to kill me, you mean. Of course, the good Executioner does not kill without reason or provocation. How nice.” “Why are you here?” I demanded. “I was just trying to get home. Don’t you have someone else to be bothering?” “I just wanted to ask you how it felt finally feeling the sharpest pain possible by the Dark Age.” Before I could answer in surprise, he continued, “Oh yes, the vampire world follows your career with great aspiration. After all, you are like a cult figure to us. Like a Charles Manson or Martha Stewart, if you will.” Martha Stewart? “So you saw what happened…” “Oh yes,” He replied. “While, I don’t care too much for this Dark Age, I thought it was humorous that even you, the bringer of light, couldn’t still take their fire away. You even let them hurt one of your fellow crusaders. And what happened on Fusion? Satanic Fusion? You let that happen?” My embarrassment began to fill the entire alleyway. He had just hit a nerve. “I was trying to stop them…” “But it didn’t work,” he interrupted. “Even when you tried to help that Duke Williams person, he just assaulted you. Now you have to be on his team and face the Dark Age again? Who are you kidding Lexian? Just get out of this mess.” “And how do you suppose I should do that?” He grinned, fangs and all. “You’re the Executioner…you figure it out. Until we meet again, and I promise we will, I bid you adieu.” And within a second, he was already gone. I was left cold and alone in an alleyway that I didn’t want to be in. On Turmoil, I’d be on a team I didn’t want to be in. Funny how things work out sometimes.
