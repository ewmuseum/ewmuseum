Welcome To The Pit - Part 2: To Knock Down A Tree

9 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3 - 9 - 03 ‘And over here, you have the Knockdown Training Area…’ This is so ridiculous, The Astronomer thinks with a sigh, trying his best to stay on task. For about twenty minutes now, Copernicus has led Dorn Tracer around a wide-open area around his cabin, showing him the various ‘training areas’. The Knockdown Training Area is just a small grove of trees with a single layer of rubber wrapped around a portion of each trunk. The Top Rope Training Area was a pit dug about a foot into the ground with a wooden pole at each corner. Ropes spanned each pole and acted as makeshift tight ropes. Dorn has found this a little more than ridiculous… ‘So Dorn, what do you think?’ Copernicus inquires, without actually turning and looking at the Astronomer. ‘Quite a primitive setup, but hey, it works!’ Dorn doesn’t really think so, but nevertheless, he had better just go along with it. The guy might smack me over the head with a fucking chair again… Dorn wears the most content smile he can muster up and nods, ‘It’s something else, Copernicus. I can’t wait for our first training session,’ He exclaims, his tone lying like a dog. Dorn would love nothing more than to be in a gym or at the arena, using their equipment and getting himself into shape the human way. Trying to train with trees and pits of mud isn’t exactly his idea of a workout, but apparently, ol’ Copernicus thinks so. ‘So, what’s first?’ Dorn asks, again trying to wear a feasible expression. ‘We’ll try the Knockdown Training Area first,’ The blind man replies, stopping suddenly and causing Dorn to come to a screeching halt. ‘But first, do you want pads or anything? It’s going to be painful…’ Copernicus’ tone hints at truthfulness, but nevertheless, Dorn isn’t worried. He has rammed his shoulder into enough people to know how it feels. Bone against bone doesn’t feel like a pleasant massage, but you just have to get used to it and condition your shoulder to take such things. Dorn shakes his head, declining the use of pads. ‘I’ll be fine,’ Dorn states with confidence in his voice blocking out any sense he might have. Copernicus nods and turns towards the small, singled out grove of trees that acts as the Knockdown Training Area. Signaling Dorn to follow, the two make their way over to it, approaching their first training session. Stopping in front of the grove, Copernicus signals for the Astronomer to halt, then walks up to a tree that seems to stick out from the others. Placing his hand on the rough trunk, he slowly slides it up and down, feeling the texture of the tree and getting a feel for the sturdiness. He then punches the rubber padding lightly, as if testing it. Apparently satisfied, he turns around with a smile on his face and nods. ‘This’ll be good. You ready?’ This should be good… Dorn thinks to himself with a nod. ‘Sure. So, what do I do?’ To Dorn, it’s pretty obvious, but he decides to question the procedure anyway just to make sure Copernicus doesn’t have something else in mind. The blind man suddenly takes on a grim mask of determination and places his hand on the rubber padding, ‘You are to charge the tree and attempt to knock it down with the knockdown shoulder technique.’ At first, Dorn doesn’t really believe Copernicus, thinking it to be a goof. But when he notices the all-serious look on his teacher’s face, his eyebrows rise in surprise and he tilts his head, confused as shit. ‘You can’t be serious. Knock down a TREE?’ The emphasis on the word tree gets a nod of out the blind man, and Dorn turns his gaze to the wooden beast. The trunk isn’t that big around and the tree only stands about 15 feet into the air. It’s probably not even that old, Dorn thinks to himself while giving his strange opponent a look over. The Astronomer turns a questioning gaze to Copernicus once more, and upon seeing his teacher’s nod, he shrugs and backs up. ‘Well, here it goes…’ Before Dorn can even move, however, Copernicus darts behind him and sends him crashing shoulder first into the rubber padding with a harsh push. The Astronomer hits it with a meaty SMACK and rebounds off, stumbling backwards. Copernicus catches him and steadies his wobbly student, ‘Go, Tracer! Hit it with all your might!’ Copernicus’ orders barking in his ear only angers Dorn, but he decides to throw his anger into the tree. He throws off his leather coat and runs at the tree with his shoulder out, ready to ram right into it. His body goes smashing into the padding again and rebounds off once again. ‘Put your back into it, bitch!’ Roars Copernicus, trying to incite Dorn to keep going. The Astronomer obliges by throwing himself at the padding again, then again, and again, and again. Dorn just keeps going, crying out in rage each time he runs at the tree. Copernicus just listens to the smack from each attempt with his arms folded across his chest and a look of solemn concentration on his face. As his student continues to fling himself against the rubber padding around the tree, Copernicus contemplates other ways to possibly use the surroundings to train Dorn. He’ll get tired of the tree before long and his anger will subside. Better think of something else… Copernicus begins to brainstorm further regiments while Dorn throws himself at the harsh rubber-covered bark. After about 10 more minutes, Dorn stops his training and doubles over, breathing heavily and practically raining sweat upon the dirty grass. He stands up, gripping his shoulder, and turns to an amused Copernicus with an enraged look in his eyes, ‘So this is supposed to help me?!? This is bullshit! My shoulder feels like it’s GONE and the entire right side of my body is burning!’ The Astronomer’s enraged attitude only amuses the blind teacher as he cracks a subtle grin. ‘Don’t worry, Dorn. We’ll take a twenty minute break,’ And suddenly, Copernicus’ face turns back to the grim look of determination it once had. ‘But know this, we will be here until you knock that fucking tree down. Even if it takes until this Sunday. If you want to succeed in a Battle Royal, you have to learn to down a large number of people and not wear yourself out. Got it?!’ Dorn doesn’t answer and still wears a look of rage upon his flushed, exhausted face. Copernicus shakes his head and turns to walk away, calling out, ‘Take your break, Dorn. Just be ready when it’s time to go again.’ And with that, Copernicus walks off towards the cabin, leaving the Shooting Star Champion to his thoughts. This could take me forever… I had better find a different way around this. If I can’t knock it down with strength, then I’ll knock it down with intelligence. Stars willing, things will work out completely in my favor… [End.]
