Pulling Strings, The Fourteenth Part Of

9 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 9th, 2003 Jason Cunningham checked his Rolodex. He found the number he wanted under a code he had made up himself. He called it. 'Yeah man.' 'Hey, this is Jason Cunningham. How's it going?' 'Too smooth, dude. What's on ya mind?' 'You seen this morning's 411 review of Fusion and Turmoil?' 'I NEVER read that honkey shit. You know that.' 'Question, what's the street price doing right about now?' 'What d'ya mean?' 'Is it going up or down?' 'Steady, man. Five bucks a pop. Some talk about dropping it down to foru, but nobody wants to do that. Not as much juice for everybody, you know?' 'Any supply problems?' 'Not that I heard.' 'Thanks.' 'Be cool dude.' The man that Cunningham had been talking to, Ben James Affleck -- he had taken to using his full name since that actor became popular -- cradled the telephone and went back to his coffee. The newspaper that he had just told Cunningham he never read was spread on the kitchen table in front of him. The story he had been reading when the phone rang had Cunningham's byline. Star Studded March Madness Gaining Steam, the headline shouted. Recently gone superstar Akira Akiyama, apparently tied to Neo Void ties as well as personal feuds with his own daughter and those gunning for his ultimate big man status was set as the main target of Xavier Kannon. PCW officials aghast. Massive violations of booking between writers backstage had left the wrestlers burned out, possibly dead sluggish for the Pay Per View, hoping to keep in track of the last few rapid fire shows such as 'Shanghai'd 3', 'Decimation' and 'Alliances'. Jackson's little press boy's article sure ain't mentioning his slacking. Ben James poured himself another cup of cofee and lit another cigarette. He glanced out the dirty window at the building across the alley, then resumed his seat at them and grinned at 'Cathy,' he picked up a pencil and began the crossword puzzle. Ben James like corssword puzzle. He had discovered long ago that he could think about other things while he filled in the little squares. Today he had much to think about. At the head of his list was Reno Enders. He knew that Enders had been laundering money through Phillip Harrington's Savings and Loan. What would Enders do now? Enders' operation was taking in almost three million cash a week. About a fourth of that amount went to the West Coast to pay for raw new product, and a big chunk went to salaries and payoffs and other expenses. Still the operation produced a million a week pure profit -- a littleover four million a month -- cash that Reno Enders had to somehow turn into legitimate funds that he hand his immediate cronies could spend and squirrel away. It was certainly a pleasant problem, but a problem nonetheless. It would be interesting to hear Reno's solution. In the year that Ben James had spent working for the organization he had qcquired a tremendous respect for Reno Enders. A sixth-grade dropout, Enders had common sense, superior intelligence and a cat's ability to land on his feet when the unexpected occurred, as it did with a frequency that would have appalled any legitimate businessman. Many of Enders' troubles were caused by the people who worked from him: they got greedy, that became addicted, that liked to strut their stuff in front of the wrong people, they became convinced of their own personal invulnerability. Enders was a natural leader. His judgements were hard to fault. Those people that he concluded were a danger disappeared, quickly and forever. Those errant souls whom he believed trainable he corrected and trusted. Like every dealer, Enders was in a never-ending battle to protect his turf, the street corners and houses where his street dealers sold his product. This was comabt and Enders had a natural aptitude for it. He was ruthless efficiency incarnate. And like every dealer, Enders was in a cash-and-carry businesss that demanded constant vigilance against cheaters and theives. Here too Enders excelled, but he had been blessed with a generous dollop of paranoia and a natural talent for larceny. To Ben James's personal knowledge, poorly advised optimists had attempted to swindle Reno Enders on two occasions. Several of these foolish individulas and received bullets in the brain as souvenirs of their adventure and one had been dismembered with a chain saw while still alive. Low and behold, that Dante Jackson accompained the tag, 'The One That Got Away'. But although Reno Enders had many attributes in common with other successful crack -- ring leaders, he hwas also unique. Enders intuitively understood that the most serious threat to the health of his enterprise was the authorities -- the police, the DEA, the FBI. So he had systematically set about reducing that threat to an acceptable level. He found politicians, cops and druge enfrocement agaents who could be bought and bought them. Consequently Ben James Affleck was in New York undercover instead of riding around Evansville, Indiana, in a patrol car. He wasn't known as Ben James Affleck here though, but as Sammy Z. Writer of Purgatory, 23 Across. Five letters, the last of which is an E. Affleck had arrived in New Yokr a year ago and rented this shithole to live in. After two weeks of hanging around bars, he got a job as a lookout for one of Enders' distributors. He had been doing that for about a month when who should come strolling down the street one rainy Thursday night but his high school baseball buddy from Evansville, Jason Cunningham. He had leveled with Cunningham -- he had no choice: Cunningham knew he has a cop -- and the reporter apparently had kept the secret. Ten months had passed, Ben James was still alive, with all his arms and legs firmly attached, and he was now personally running errands and delivering product for Enders. He was close. Very close. He knew the names of two of the local cops on Reno's list and one of this politicans, but he had no evidence that would stand up in court. It could come. Sooner or later he would get the evidence. If he lived long enough. Dante. Dante was the writer of purgatory. If that fox Reno Enders didn't catch on. Damn that Cunningham anyway. Why did that white boy have to pick today to call? Oh well, if worse came to worst, Jason Cunningham, press relations and all would write him one hell of an obituary.
