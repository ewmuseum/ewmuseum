Never Never Land I: [Part Two]

5 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 5, 2002 Shock is a good word. No, no…something stronger than shock, because shock implies that I wasn’t expecting it. I sat on my buttocks, staring at the most beautiful five inch woman I knew, and there was nothing but contempt in my heart. I could have just slapped her away like a petty fly, but that would have killed her. We can’t have that, now can we? “Tom, you look like you’ve seen a ghost,” Tinkerbell said sweetly. She batted her eyes in a similar fashion as the nurse I hit on earlier, only I knew Tinkerbell had a good right cross. Fairies were funny that way. “I think I have,” I replied, gingerly sliding away. “Bobby…did you have some sort of poison on that spine you hit me with?” “I’m a cactus, not a voodoo medicine man.” Bobby’s humor had little effect on me. I still had a shocked look on my face, and from the curious gaze that Tinkerbell was giving me, she was obviously a bit disturbed as well. “What’s wrong?” She asked, fluttering over my head and onto my right shoulder now. “You’re acting funny.” She then laughed, tossing her head back. “But then again, you’re supposed to be funny, right? I mean, you’re Tom Lexian!” “At least you remember my name.” “Of course I remember your name, silly. How could we forget you?” Tink proudly stamped her foot down, which felt like pins and needles on my shoulder, and began to proclaim, “Tom Lexian…hero of the Lost Boys and of Never Never Land. He bravely fought against Preston Pot and his cronies, only to free Peter Pan and save yet another generation of Lost Boys.” “You did all of that shit?” Bobby asked, a tinge of sarcasm in his voice. I shrugged. “I suppose I did.” “You silly mongoose! Of course you did all of that! You did it all against your will, of course, but you were drunk at the time.” “No I wasn’t!” “You got drunk?” Bobby asked, once more sounding sarcastic. He was getting quite accustomed to sounding that way all of a sudden. “Damn, why can’t you be more like your old self?” Tink shook her head violently. “No, no. We don’t want that Tom Lexian. He was old and grumpy. Well, not really old, but he was grumpy. But he came through in the end, and won back Peter Pan’s freedom through the dance competition! Yay Tom!” The memories were coming back, slowly but surely. I was suppressing them, trying to forget all about my experiences over there. I needed to see a good shrink. “I remember now…” I began to say, looking away at the ceiling. “It’s all starting to come back to me now…” Tink rubbed her hands eagerly. “Yes…Tom, remember all of it. Don’t you remember the beautiful horizon and sun rises?” “Yes…” “The incredible adventures with the Lost boys? The wonderful creatures you came in contact with?” I nodded, feeling somewhat euphoric. It was as if I were on drugs, only, I wasn’t. “Never Never Land was chock full of adventures. It was the most amazing experience in my life.” “We love you, Tom. You’re the best.” “Wait a moment…” I began to say, remembering everything now. “You hit me?” “Who me?” Tink asked, genuinely surprised. “Who me?” Bobby said, being a stupid little cactus. “Not you, you idiot,” I said, shooting a mean look at that worthless cactus manager of mine. “I’m talking to the fairy.” Tinkerbell looked hurt, as if I had said something to offend her. All I did was pronounce her the bearer of bad news in my life. Okay, maybe that was stretching it a bit, but she had brought a lot of misery into my life. “How can you say that?” She replied, depression rising from her voice. “After all we’ve been through. After all we’ve seen.” “Cut the crap,” I sternly advised. “I don’t have time to play games with you. Every time I’ve seen you, something goes wrong and I end up in a really screwed up situation.” She placed her hands on her hip, curling her news in an annoyed fashion. That must have been fairy talk for, “I’m pissed off now.” “We’ve only been in one really screwed up situation together.” “Thank god…” I muttered under my breath. “What was that?” “Oh nothing,” I replied, keeping my comments to myself. Only the good lord knows what kind of trouble I would have gotten myself in to if she had heard the last sentence. It was at this time that Bobby flew from the medical tray he was sitting on and wound up on my lap. Sometimes I despised the fact that he could fly. Birds fly because they have wings. People fly because they have machines. Cacti in a pot are not supposed to fly. Especially large, heavy ones like Bobby. “Let me guess…” Bobby began to say. “You knocked her up, didn’t you?” “What?” I looked up at Tink, who looked furious at the thought of such an event occurring. I tried to placate her anger, knowing full well that a fairy, when angry, can be almost as dangerous as carrying a machete in your pants. “Tink, don’t listen to him. He doesn’t know what he’s talking about. I mean, he’s only a stupid cactus!” “Yeah, I’m so stupid I can carry on an intelligible conversation with actual people.” “Is there a reason why you talk to inanimate objects?” Tink finally asked, after staring at Bobby for quite awhile. She must have been wondering that since she got here. “He’s my…my…” I just couldn’t find the right words to say. Any way I looked at it, I’d either make myself look as crazy as Dennis Rodman. Oh, the horror. “Manager. I’m your manager, you dummy.” “Thanks for clearing that up, Bobby.” I said, actually relieved that he said it instead of me. I just didn’t like the way it sounded when it came out of my mouth. “Okay…” Tink began to say. “That’s fine. I don’t really care, actually. I was just wondering why in the hell you were talking to a plant.” “It’s cool. I was wondering why in the hell Tom was talking to a three inch, flying chick too.” Bobby answered back. Tink and Bobby had a bit of a stare down at this point. It was like watching two kids squabble over who was right and who was wrong. Although, this was more like, who has Tom’s affection more. I suddenly realized that I was baby sitting two kids, only one was hella, hella good looking, while the other was a member of the plant kingdom. Tough choice. “Guys, take it easy on each other…” I said, placing my hand in between the two. Suddenly, a spine flew out from Bobby’s body, almost striking my hand, and zipping past Tink’s head. I drew my hand back quickly, stunned. Tink flew into the air, throwing fairy dust at us both. It tickled my nose. “Stop it! Stop it!” I screamed, as the dust caused my skin to itch. It was a horrible feeling all around, and I wanted it to cease immediately. “Yeah! I can’t take it anymore!” Bobby screamed in unison. A few seconds later, three flowers bloomed out of Bobby’s cellular wall. They were pink, purple, and yellow. They didn’t match his pot at all. “Hmmph!” Tink said defiantly, taking a position back on my shoulder. Poor Bobby had very little to say at this point, except, “You crazy fairy! What did you do to me!?” “I figured you needed a bit of flower in your life. You don’t seem to have much respect for women, so now you can be more feminine to understand where I’m coming from.” I laughed. “Bobby, you look…gorgeous.” “Shut up, Tom.” “Oh, stop it,” I replied. “You look great.” “Don’t you get it? Cacti don’t have flowers. If we were meant to have flowers, I would be sprouting them by myself.” “Actually, some desert cacti do sprout flowers occasionally…” Tink answered. “Whoop de do. Probably gay cacti.” “That wasn’t very politically correct,” I observed. “Take it back, Bobby. I thought we talked about this before. We were going to be equal opportunity insulters, remember?” “Put a sock in it. I have flowers dangling from my manly spines now. I don’t care about being politically correct.” I took Bobby and placed him on my left side on the floor. I figured he wouldn’t be too dangerous there. He continued to sulk and sulk, complaining of back pains because of the flowers. “Oh my god…I think I just broke my spleen!” “You don’t have a spleen.” “Well, if I did, I would have broken it!” I shook my head, not listening to his endless pleas anymore. I turned my attention back to Tink, who looked depressed. She was sitting on my shoulder, rather than standing, and she was deep in thought. “Times change, don’t they?” She moaned. “I guess they do. I’m a different person. I’m not the same Tom Lexian that you met so many months ago.” She smirked. “Not too different. You’re still as stubborn as ever.” “I’ll give you that much. But you realize that I’m just a plain old wrestler. I’m not superman. I never wanted to go to Never Never Land in the first place.” “But we need you, Tom. Things have gone bad again.” “They’re always bad, Tink. I can’t change that every single time.” “Tom, Hook is back to his old tricks again. He’s captured a very sacred ring, and if uses it, all of Never Never Land will be gone forever.” I was disbelieving, to say the least. “How could Never Never Land be gone forever? That’s impossible. That’s Hook’s home. He would never do anything to jeopardize that.” “That’s the whole problem. He doesn’t know what’s at stake. He thinks the ring will make him stronger, but in a different way than he thinks. If he does use it, everything will be destroyed.” I looked away, feeling compassionate but still unwilling to help. “Look Tink, I want to help. I really do. You know I still miss the guys, especially Belly and Bat. They were great. But I’m living a different life now. I’m no longer the good guy you remember. I’ve taken a turn for the worst…I’m evil.” Tink gasped. “You’re a bad guy?” “That’s right. I’m the ultimate bad guy, and I’m ready to take anyone down. Of course, I’m still learning and all, but I’m still bad. I can’t help you, Tink. You’re one of the good guys.” “Tom, I refuse to believe that you’re bad. I can’t believe it. And I’ll prove it to you.” “How are you going to do that? By hitting me again and dragging my ass back to Never Never Land? Sorry toots, but that was back in the day. I’m a new man now. No one is taking me anywhere that I don’t want to go. That’s all. End of story.” CONK! “Oh my god!” Bobby exclaimed. “You just hit Tom! And he’s unconscious!” “You’re a perceptive little thing, aren’t ya?” Tink said, a sadistic smile on her face. “Look, you can’t take him anywhere! He’s got a match this Sunday! A big one, too! He’s going for the World title!” “I’ll get him back before then,” She replied. “Besides, if I don’t, we’ll all be dead anyways. We all have to go sometime.” “Damn, you’re a scary little fairy.” Tink nods enthusiastically. “Well, you can’t take him anywhere without me. I’m his body guard. Look, take me along for the ride. I have to look after my investment.” Tink thinks about it for a moment, and then nods her head. “Okay, fine, you guys are coming. But don’t think that I’m forgetting what you did to me earlier. You pull anything, and your ass is mine.” “Fine by me.” Bobby flies unto Tom’s stomach as Tink carries them off. They disappear into thin air as nurses and police officers file into the corridor.
