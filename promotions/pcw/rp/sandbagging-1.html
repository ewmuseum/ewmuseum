Date: Whenever The desire to be the best... What is it in our human nature that inherently forces us to strive to be the best? Something inside of us drives us to dreams that we may or may not be able to fulfill all for the want of being the best... It is at once a blessing and a curse to reach that goal. On the one hand, it's a matter of being the alpha male, the leader of the pack, the most dominant. Everyone knows and respects you. You can walk into a room and everything will stop, people standing up and applauding you. On the other hand, it makes you a target. Day and night, you will walk down the street wearing armor for fear of being stabbed in the back by your friends and family... or maybe just some random person who decides it's their day to get lucky. To be the best, you must be constantly in defense, never on the attack. For indeed the entire world will be seeking to challenge you, and you, as the best, have no choice but to accept that challenge. Allow me to give different meaning to the words of Hamlet in Shakespeare's play: To be {the best}, or not to be {the best.} Whether tis nobler in the mind to suffer the slings and arrows of outrageous misfortune {that of being left to wither and die in the midcard, or even worse the undercard, while those you feel superior to rise ahead} or to take arms against a sea of troubles {for to be the best means that you shall be the 'hunted' by everyone in the wrestling industry, looking to make a name for themselves, or just to settle some old score}. Indeed, it seems to be a gargantuan trade-off, being the Continental Champion. The right scale is weighted down by glory and power, wealth and fame. The left scale is weighted by the hundreds and thousands of people who will be gunning for you any day of the week; the fans who will bear down upon you in droves for an autograph or some possession of yours to sell on E-Bay; the barrage of flash-bulbs which has uncovered more than a few unwitting epileptics; the endorsement deals, movies and television appearances that combined with wrestling allow you 3 hours of sleep each night... When does it get to be too much? When does dedication turn into blind faith? When do the positives turn into negatives, thus turning a great life into a punishment worse than death? It takes a lot to be the Continental Champion... It takes a lot out of you to be the Continental Champion... Makes you wonder why so many people want it... Either they don't know what they'd be getting into, or the foolhardy rush headlong into their future without stopping to think things through... I speak a lot about the cons of being the Continental Champion, one may wonder why I even agreed to be in the match. Short answer, long answer; both the same. I LIVE FOR SPORT. Don't confuse that with 'I love winning.' That's not why I'm in the game. That's all this really is, the workers don't understand. A game. You can enter at any time, get out at any time. You earn points, you lose points, you may win a prize but it isn't obligated to you... This is a game and I play the game for the fun of it. The thrill of sport, the spirit competition. Two or more wrestlers meeting in the ring and facing off in honorable combat until one is declared the winner. I don't feel the need to win, though I will not give anything less than 200% in a match. I don't require a title of any sort, be it the World Championship or some other strap. The only reason I wrestle for the Continental Title is because it gives me access. Access to what? Silly rabbit, access to the other wrestlers. As the Continental Champion, I won't have to ask for matches, won't have to request sport. The competition will inevitably come to me. Some may not be able to handle it. Some would turn away challengers. Not me. I thrive on the challenge. So much to the degree that should I win the Continental Title it shall be defended every week. No resting on my laurels. The belt has had too many paper champions. *Cough*RUSHMASON*Cough* Why do this? Why do anything? I realize I may have been sounding like a pessimist, or even a nihilist earlier. But that should explain things pretty clearly. I recognize the ups and downs, BUT I SHALL GIVE MY BLOOD, SWEAT, AND TEARS TO PCW AT DECIMATION, AND EVERY OTHER SHOW AFTER THAT UNTIL I STOP BREATHING OR GET RELEASED. And really, you wouldn't ask anything more of me? ... Would you?
