Being Watched - Chapter 2: The Traitor

21 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6 - 21 - 03 Standing before a pseudo-council of six strange men, the man known only as Mr. Jones has his arms folded across his chest and a sneer across his face. His blue Armani suit stretches at the joints and his shades ever so slowly move down the bridge of his nose. He stands tall on a large steel out-cropping that protrudes from the steel walls, towering over everyone else. The expression on his face could curdle milk, so obviously, something strange has gone down. “Did anyone else know of this?” Mr. Jones bellows out to the six other men, his voice booming with venom. Most of them shake their head, except for a cloaked man at the end of the left line. His runs a hand through his wavy red hair then strokes his beard thoughtfully… almost nervously. Mr. Jones notices, “Councilman Redgers??” The cloaked man known as Redgers shakes his head, “I can’t say I knew of it. But I think I might have caught wind of it when the Runners passed through my area. They spoke of ‘redemption through the hero’. It was all very strange.” “And what else did you find out?” “He’s lying!” One of the other men pipes up. Donned in what appears to be tight steel torso armor with black leather pants, the angry looking figure swipes his hand out dismissingly and sneers at Redgers. “You can’t trust someone who used to work for the Runners. He’s fucking lying, Jones!” Redgers looks appalled, “WHAT?! How dare you! You-“ “SILENCE BOTH OF YOU!” Mr. Jones cries out, furious from the altercation. “Your words are harsh, Damien! You have no right to accuse Councilman Redgers of traitorous happenings due to his misty past.” Damien’s face melts into complete shock and he points right at Councilman Redgers, “He was involved with the cosmic fire incident, though! He and the leader of the Runners were plotting to put the High Templar on the throne opposed to having this organization run things. They plotted against us! How could you deny that? The evidence is CRYSTAL FUCKING CLEAR!” A murmur arises from within the meeting. The other four men begin to discuss things amongst themselves while Redgers and Damien start bickering furiously, the raging tension quickly building in the room. Meanwhile, Mr. Jones just stands at the edge of the out-cropping, looking down at the floor and shaking his head. The murmurs begin to increase in volume and the two feuding councilmen look like they’re going to come to blows. Finally, Mr. Jones speaks up just loud enough for everyone to hear them, but without malice in his voice, “Calm down, everyone!!” The murmurs stop, but the bickering councilmen continue. “You’re a lying cretin, Redgers!!” Damien cries. “You cannot accuse me of anything! The truth of that incident has been lost amongst time and speculation! The High Templar was long dead before that!” “Gentlemen…” Mr. Jones cries out, but they don’t seem to here him. “Are you speaking of High Templar Buyatis, Redgers?” Damien bellows, the volume of his voice slowly decreasing. Mr. Jones sighs, “Gentlemen, please…” “Yes, he died of natural causes at the healthy age of 74! Or is your mind so clouded from conspiracy that you’ve forgotten? HOW CAN A DEAD MAN BE PUT ON A THRONE?!” “YOU-“ “GENTLMEN! SHUT UP!!” Mr. Jones bursts out, his face turning bright red. Instantly, the bickering stops and the two men just stare at him, bewildered. Mr. Jones exhales slowly and regains his composure, “Thank you. Now, this is NOT the situation at hand. We’re trying to reveal the person or persons that thought it was necessary to nearly sabotage Dorn Tracer’s efforts to restore faith in Astrology. Case in point, the steel masked man. I’ve observed this cretin, and he seems to have a vendetta against Tracer. Now normally, I wouldn’t think anything of this, but the recent uprising of the Brotherhood of the Machine has-“ Suddenly, Councilman Redgers chimes in, “Ah, yes! DAMIEN’S group! Why does that not surprise me?” “Hold your tongue, blasphemer!” Damien yells, his voice rising. The bickering looks to begin again, but both men notice the look on Mr. Jones’ face and just resort to mere ‘fixing a conflict of opinion’. “We’ve nothing to do with this! I assure you, Mr. Jones! I’d like to now what their evidence is!” “Well, they-“ Mr. Jones begins. However, another face in the council cuts him off. “Allow me,” The man pipes in, wearing a long dark blue trench coat and matching steel torso armor. His black leather pants and boots gleam from the heavy ceiling lights and his short blonde hair emits its healthy glow upon the concrete floor. He pulls his hands from his coat pockets and folds his arms across his chest. “I may know what Councilman Redgers speaks of.” Mr. Jones nods and the man clears his thought, demanding the attention of the entire room. He steps up out of the line and gives an accusing stare at Damien, who just returns it with a nasty glare, “Councilman Damien, I have been rummaging through the archives of Dorn Tracer’s journey lately and I have stumbled upon something interesting. Something you may wish to explain. May I, Mr. Jones?” “As you wish, General Carns,” Mr. Jones replies. The clean-shaven man known as General Carns nods and pulls out what appears to be a small palm pilot-like device from his coat pocket. He points it at the nearest steel wall and presses a red button. Suddenly, a large motion projection appears and it’s displaying the Steel Masked Man! The council and Mr. Jones watch in confusion as the projection displays the Steel Masked Man about to strike Dorn. As the masked man brings his head back and thrusts it forward, the projection suddenly stops and Carns looks at Mr. Jones, “Observe closely, and you may have the evidence you’re looking for.” “I don’t see anything, General Carns.” Mr. Jones retorts, squinting at the projection. “There’s nothing to see!” Damien shrieks, becoming more and more furious. “This is quite obviously a plot by Redgers and his sect to do away with the Brotherhood of the Machine! We’re still in good standings with the company! I swear it!” General Carns shakes his head, “I beg to differ. Look closely…” He presses the read button again and the projection zooms in on the side of the masked man’s head. Everyone looks for a minute and there’s dead silence for a minute… before Councilman Redgers gasps. “THAT’S THE BROTHERHOOD INSIGNIA! I KNEW IT! THEY’RE BEHIND THIS!” Uproar bursts up from the council as they all begin to speculate and accuse Damien. Plain as day, a half-circle with another inverted half-circle inside of it sits upon the side of the man’s mask. The councilman of the Brotherhood begins to slowly step back, frantic with fright, “I DON’T KNOW WHERE THAT CAME FROM! I SWEAR I DON’T KNOW WHAT THIS IS ABOUT!” “Treason is a VERY serious crime, Councilman Damien. You know what the price is.” General Carns says very bluntly before pulling a silver .45 Desert Eagle from his pocket. Mr. Jones’ hand suddenly underneath his suit as he watches General Carns raise the firearm, ready for anything. The General shakes his head, “It disgusts me to know that some of our own people would betray us just to defend a dead legacy. Goodbye, Damien.” “WAIT, I-GAHHH!!” A thunderous bang fills the room and Councilman Damien suddenly clutches his heart. He staggers back and plummets to the floor, gurgling noises escaping his throat and blood pouring from his nose. General Carns pockets his firearm and turns towards Mr. Jones, “The traitor has been executed for high treason against us.” “I see that. Call the guards and have this mess cleaned up. Council adjourned…” Mr. Jones replies, staring at the dead body of Councilman Damien. The other councilmen begin to slowly make their way towards the door quietly. Even Redgers is silent. General Carns stays behind and walks up to Mr. Jones with a sneer on his face. “He had to die sometime.” “I’d like to know why you executed him without my permission.” Carns scoffs, “He was a traitor.” “On the contrary,” Mr. Jones retorts, a frown quickly forming on his face, “You know and I know that that symbol was wrong. It was backwards and the angle of the projection was not at fault. What’s going on?” “I don’t know what you’re-“ “Don’t play games with me, Carns.” Jones spits, his lips curling into an angry sneer. “The Brotherhood is going to be pissed, having lost their representation in our council. And I’m sure the Runners will support them. The last thing we need right now is a civil war due to someone framing Councilman Damien and then YOU executing him. When they learn that it was done without my consent or without a trial, they’ll be furious. And I’m beginning to think someone WANTS that to happen. Don’t make me believe it’s YOUR people wanting something to gain from this, Carns.” Carns stares at Mr. Jones for a minute before shaking his head, “No, sir.” “We’ll speak of this later. Just watch yourself in the meantime, Carns.” At that, Mr. Jones steps down from the out-cropping and walks towards the door, not uttering another word. General Carns watches him go and sneers when Jones leaves. [End.]
