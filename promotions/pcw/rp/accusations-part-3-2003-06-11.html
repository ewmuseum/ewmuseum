Accusations Part 3

11 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11th June 2003 [ ] – Sivasian speaking directly to the reader, his thoughts. :: :: - Action, and reflection on other stuff from a narrator Krikorian: ‘’ – Someone speaking :: Sivasian just stood, or rather sat, and stared ahead. Ahead towards the nothingness that was the wall. The dirty, skanky, green mouldy walls of that interviewing room. He couldn’t feel anything, but then again, did he want to feel anything at all? It was a question that ran through him. He couldn’t imagine what he should say in response to the line said earlier to him. Amazed, he simply couldn’t say anything. He wanted to say something to charm maybe, maybe something that will make them think differently, but he couldn’t. He simply could not force anything to come from his dry throat. He would normally have said something by now, he thought. He ran his hand through his hair. He was hot, sweaty through the nervousness. Like he had woken up, try that. Ever woken up in the middle of the night and just stared ahead, wondering who the hell you are, and in what condition your life is, as you feel uncomfortably hot, but then cold at the same place from the rooms in your house? Hell, Sivasian felt three times that bad, and he hadn’t woken up, he was stuck in an investigations centre all along. The detective, a mean looking man, but still young and maybe vunerable, just stared too. He wanted to make Sivasian say something, something to put the charges back down onto him, something to make them stand up and accuse him with. If he got some form of confession from Sivasian, he could be home with his family within a week, and be holidaying with his wife in the Caribbean in two. Like a game of chess, both men holding ground, sacrificing nothing, not giving a hint or trace to their opponent. However, as Sivasian sat for what seemed seconds to him, five minutes passed in the room. The only sound was a hushed cough from a guard standing at the door. Suddenly, the detective moved his knight, and spoke. :: Detective: ‘Sir, this may seen a slight, shall we say shock, but trust me. You’re not as such yet under any form of imprisonment on this, however, as I said, it all points towards you. You’re rifle, you’re skill, you’re in the country, you can see where we’re coming from, right?’ :: Sivasian still sat, he barely heard it at all. It took seconds to settle in to his body what the detective was saying. He didn’t respond immediately, how could he? In fact, thinking about it, he really disagreed. He couldn’t see their point of view, for they had been wrong. He hadn’t killed anyone in Iraq, he hadn’t shot a man in anger for 10 or more years. Sure, he practiced with his gun for the first hours of his mornings at home, but had never shot anyone. A rabbit he had shot when he practiced back in his old hometown 5 years ago, his only previous visit to his homeland since his parents had been killed, but a human? Never again, for he had an incident which horrified and shattered his fragile mind. He hadn’t been able to be as effective since then. He didn’t want to run the incident through his mind again as he sat there in the investigation room. It sat there, like a present on Christmas Eve, he wanted to open it, to relive it, but he held off. He would cry and collapse, as he had done night after night after. The army majors thought he was weak, but they didn’t understand, they weren’t human, were they? No, they never could be. Finally, Sivasian managed to croak something out from his throat, it hurt, as his throat felt dry, but he made it come out, he forced it to come out. :: Krikorian: ‘Please, I did nothing.’ :: The smug detective smirked slightly towards Sivasian. He knew he had his mind, he had Sivasian just as he wanted him. Matt Jackson may well have been able to hold off, act the hero, but that was petty. In reality it was probably just a little game he had played with friends for a joke, but this, hell, this was serious. He was the medieval man with his head on the block, his head ready to be chopped off, to be plunged into the dark velvet fabricated basket, to be killed. American army leaders had no time to listen to countries pleads, they had no time for individuals to beg, if you were thought to have killed a man under no order, out of war, you were to be killed. Killing, save for the phone calls to the capital to tell of the killing, a lot of paperwork was saved in the progress. :: Detective: ‘You’re wrong, aren’t you Sivasian. You told your mind you didn’t kill that man, but you did. You did damn it, didn’t YOU!’ :: The detective forcefully slams his hand down onto the small wooden table. It creaks, it’s low budget, they don’t want to spend millions furbishing a place where ‘dishonourable’ men, like Sivasian right now, come. Sivasian looked into the detective’s eyes, and they drew his soul from him. The eyes were nothing, there was nothing there, like Sivasian felt. This man had little or no emotions, imagine a computer program or robot on the television, no emotions; surely this was how the man was. Sivasian, while water filled his eyes, had a flash of his two sons and his wife, and dropped from the small wooden chair onto the floor. He had no strength, the emotional forces had taken his physical strength. Using his last ounce of strength, he stared once more into those black, space eyes. He pleaded through just his eyes, before trying to convert it to words. :: Krikorian: ‘Please, I’ve got kids and a wife, please, let me go, give me a quarter to phone them, please.’ :: He trailed off, he was tired, and the detective picked up on it. He tried to look as though he cared, as though he could feel the pain inside Sivasian’s head and heart. Truth be told he didn’t care. He got top dollar for making people like Sivasian admit they were wrong, and he sure wanting the additional one day bonus if he could push things in by the midnight of the day. Sivasian, on the other hand, felt hot, bothered and generally in trouble. ::
