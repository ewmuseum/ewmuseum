Transition Into Madness; Episode 1, Chapter 2

11 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 11, 2002 Jack cast a quick glance at the elevator shaft, doors open and with no elevator beyond. For that matter, the cable dangled before him with no elevator attached. The tip was frayed, as if chewed or torn off. The thought crossed Jack’s mind, but he quickly dismissed it with a shake of the head. “F*ck no,” he grumbled as he stuffed the loaded handgun into his ample side pocket. “That’s thick steel cable for God’s sake. It’d take something with a lot of power to shred that thing…..” He arose and hurriedly walked to the staircase, clutching the grip of the gun in his pocket. Behind him, he swore he detected a low growl; he disregarded it, the sound having come from the exterior of the structure beyond the doors. He was still uncertain what he was scouring this decrepit, lifeless tower for, yet some unseen force goaded him ever forward. Scaling the staircase brought yet more scenes of revulsion; more bodies lay collapsed on the staircase, some crumpled in positions implying their futile attempt to fend off their phantom attackers. VJ, with a look of dismay spreading across his normally solemn/angry face, meticulously stepped over the bodies while taking heed of his ominous surroundings. He ascended up the first three levels without incident before reaching the metal door marked as access to the PCW office floor. He clenched the thin bronze handle and turned it, echoing a rusty creak. With the handle turned, he whipped out the gun and shoved open the door, bringing up the weapon as the main corridor was revealed before him. Office doors stood ajar, posters and notices at the foot of the various bulletin boards set at intervals down the passage. He strode down the hall, appalled to find bullet holes speckled all over the walls. “Damn!” he cried out, catching glimpse of another downed security officer in the doorway of a side office. “Looks like a f*ckin’ warzone in here! Jesus, I slept through all this?!” There was the distant sound of rustling papers and screeching metal down the hell and to the left. He remembered this floor as being a veritable maze from his past two visits, and instincts partly told him to investigate while the other half of his brain told him to high-tail and get the hell out of the building. Neither alternative seemed exceptionally advantageous. “Aight,” he mused aloud. “Stay up here and take my chances, and pray whatever that was isn’t interested in makin’ me its dinner…..or head downstairs and try to stay from whatever the hell I heard prowlin’ outside. Decisions, decisions….” He stopped at the body of the security guard and squatted down, opening the chamber to the revolver once again. He quickly snatched up the weapon set in the now-cold palm of the officer, breaking open the chamber and emptying its contents with a metallic clatter onto the floor. “Guess I’ll gamble taking a stand up here,” he concluded. Eagerly he seized the emptied bullets, slamming three into the trio of empty compartments in the pistol he already clutched. The others found their way into his vacant pocket as he arose, a dire smirk concealing the trepidation rising in his heart as he drew back the hammer and cocked the firearm. “Daddy’s home,” he murmured somberly before moving slowly down the hallway. Glancing into offices he saw more corpses, office staff and security alike. Some of the entrances showed signs of being initially barricaded, but obviously their attempts at fortification proved ultimately fruitless. Jack peered around the corner, again spotting a cross-section of adjoining hallways. With a frown he stepped to the edge of the circular passage and perked his ears, picking up more of the screeching. However he was distracted yet again by a rumble emanating from an office he had passed. He gradually swung his head back, the rumbling originating an office on the left side. “Aww, sh*t,” he groaned, wiping a bead of sweat from his brow. “What now?!” Cautiously he pulled himself up and crossed through the ovular-shaped room, moving in the general direction the harsh screeches had radiated from. He grasped the revolver, the grip now slippery with sweat from his trembling hands. Finally he reached outside the office of his intention, the paper rustling and screeches now being joined by a low-end growl. Jack inhaled and exhaled as he pressed his eyes together in a silent prayer. “Ah hell, Bruce, what ya nervous about?” he thought to himself. “Probably just some people blockading their office….right?” He poked his head around the corner and sidestepped inside. He nearly staggered backwards in shock of what towered before him. In the corner of the room, at nearly 7 feet tall, stood a hulking beast composed of what he thought to be transparent ooze; a brawny giant of see-through jelly with shimmering talons on side-mounted arms and a gaping maw where its abdomen should have been. It had no visible eyes, but somehow it instantly sensed VJ’s presence and turned to face him with a deep growl. Without hesitation VJ dropped a step back, pulled up the firearm in his hands and squeezed off two shots directed at the head (if one could call it that) of the looming fiend. The projectile ripped thorough the flesh(?) of the monstrosity and continued out and into the far wall. The beast continued to sway back and forth, its scrawny arms whipping around in the air. “It’s useless, Jack!” The statement somehow reverberated through his mind, presumably through some form of telepathy as the creature had made no sound. VJ backed off, firing off another pair of shots that ruptured the jelly-like beast and continued onward through the wall. It still appeared unaffected, shambling toward him on three legs as the plasma composing its lucid form swelled and reformed to cover the gap. “Human technology is just a bore,” iterated the voice again. “Save yourself the trouble and the dignity…all your struggle is in vain. And I’m not the only one wishing to share in your demise….” At that moment Jack spun around to see another pair of the malevolent atrocities barge into the room, one through the door and the other smashing an entryway through the plaster wall. Jack cursed and backed against the far window, faintly detecting the whir of a helicopter somewhere above as the beasts approached. “I guess this is it,” sneered Jack. “But hell…..I ain’t goin’ down without a fight!” He reached out and snatched a tube of fluorescent lighting, which one could only assume was present as a forthcoming replacement to the lighting above. He beckoned to the original of the demons and leaned back with the tube. At that point the whir reached deafening volume, and VJ glanced out the window. His eyes widened, his grip loosening on the glass tube as a jet-black chopper lowered until hovered horizontal to the window. “Bruce!” bellowed a voice from outside. “Get down!” Instantaneously, VJ hit the floor and a barrage of gunfire tore through the wall. He could make out the wailing of what appeared to be a gatling gun mounted on the chopper, the jelly-things stumbling backwards under the torrent of fire. The chopper swung right as a gaunt figure garbed in black with a cable attached to their back swept in through the window, smashing out what glass remained in the pane. He grabbed Jack by the waist and gripped him in a tight waistlock. “Hold on to your ass,” commanded a gruff voice before VJ found himself and his rescuer soaring out of the window, moments later shooting skyward with help of a winch and landing in a heap on the roof. Jack grunted and collapsed to the roof, recapturing the breath that has rushed from his body under the powerful ‘hug’ of his redeemer. “Catch your breath quick, Bruce,” uttered the same voice with urgency. “Not out of the woods yet.” A couple moments later he struggled to his feet, bumping against a pipe still billowing smoke from the basement boiler of the building. He grimaced a second before looking up, making temporary eye contact with his presumed rescuer, a light-haired man of no more 27 years old with a solemn look crossing his youthful visage. He was clothed in a thick foam bodysuit, a discarded helmet laying at his side. And in one hand he clutched what appeared to be a modified AK-47. “Don’t know if that gun will do ya much good,” said VJ without moving his position. “That bastard downstairs didn’t seem to give two sh*ts about having four nine millimeter bullets tear through ‘em….” “Ah you’re good to go now it seems,” his rescuer interrupted grimly before tossing a double-edged blade resembling a battleaxe in his direction. VJ picked it up, surprised to find that despite its size it weighed very little. “There, use that,” instructed his ally who refused to take his eyes off the stairwell. “You got a gun and ya give me a f*ckin’ axe?? Are you a loon?!......” “Trust me on this one, Bruce!” his rescuer shouted, maintaining his calm demeanor. “That will do you a lot more good than this gun will me!” “WHAT?!” His response was drowned out by the whirring of the chopper again as it ascended to zoom over the besieged PTC HQ tower. He glanced up into a spotlight just as it glided over the roof. Two more figures dropped down via ziplines, both clutching firearms with silver blades attached to their belts like the one VJ now held. He scowled at them before looking back at his rescuer. “Why the hell do you jokers get guns and all I get is a damned hatchet?! We’re not cuttin’ down any f*ckin’ trees here!” The duo, both still masked with their heads covered over by helmets, ignored his cry and took up arms beside the original of their crew. “About five seconds,” said the first to the newcomers. “The bastards are on their way up….” The chopper inclined and went into an abrupt climb before turning to circle again. The spotlight drifted over the stairwell entrance just as the metal door exploded outwards, giving in to a rush of the three beasts that had attacked Jack below. “F*ck, I hate these ones….” mumbled one of the two to just drop down. “Ugliest sons of b*tches I’ve ever seen…..” Gunfire exploded from the barrels of the trio’s respected weapons, the beasts stumbling momentarily before going back into full charge, their speed about equal to that of the average human being. “The elixir!” shouted the original, the one VJ took as the chief. “Pull it!” One of his subordinates lowered his weapon and dug into a pouch dangling from his belt. The figure withdrew a thick vial, cast a glance toward the leader made a skyward gesture. He cast it overhead, the bottle peaking above the enraged beasts who had gathered into a cluster under the hammering fire. The leader of the trio raised his gun, firing off a quick shot overhead that shattered the beaker and sent its contents plummeting to Earth in a shower, splashing down all over the frenetic brutes. “GO!” bellowed the leader, dropping his gun with a clatter and drawing what Jack distinguished as a curved scimitar from a sheath suspended from his belt. Following orders, the duo did likewise and joined their comrade in rushing the dazed fiends. One fell to its knees (again subject to interpretation if they could be called ‘knees’) only to be cut down by one of the sword-wielding trio. Jack watched in astonishment as the beast fell limply to its side and began to dissolve, emitting a rancid stench that turned his stomach. The other two met similar fates, collapsing and liquefying into a pool of nauseating slime. The chopper lowered onto the roof, propellers slowing until it came to a complete rest. Another duo bounded from the helicopter, donned in similar attire as they compatriots. They removed their helmets and visors, breaking into a conversation which VJ leaned in closer to listen in on. They paused and the leader turned his steely gaze in Jack’s direction. “I thank you for your cooperation, Mr. Shanahan,” he replied with an air of sincere gratitude, shaking VJ’s hand. “At the same time, I apologize for the fate of your employer company. I wish I could assure you we know the fate of your coworkers, unfortunately we don’t have that information as of yet.” “This is bizarre,” said Jack, running his hand over his shaven head. “What the hell were those?.....” “The Migu-Nurath,” calmly replied an Asian member of the group, thrusting the short trident into a holster on his belt. “A subspecies of minions serving a yet unknown deity which we have failed to detect. Instrumental in what happened to this city, along with other species forming the bulk of their ‘army’. Harmless to your corporeal form, yet prolonged physical contact results in a rather protracted and painful death. A form of alien black magic of sorts, granted unto them by their diabolical masters.” VJ stared spell-bound at the quintet of warriors, one of whom wiped a streak of blood (if you could call it that) from his weapon with a white handkerchief. “Wait a damn minute, this is f*cked up…..bullets didn’t make those things even twitch, but a freakin’ knife dissolved it?! What the f*ck are those things made of? Silver?!” One of them, a short blond female, laughed. “Silver?? BWAHAHAHA!! Mr. Shanahan, we ain’t hunting freakin’ werewolves here! These precious blades here are blessed in the confines of the Vatican and soaked for forty days and forty nights in holy water. “What the hell happened here?” demands Jack, gesturing to the crumbling buildings in awed astonishment. “Earlier today They attacked…..all around the Earth,” the Asian male replied in a quiet, melancholy tone. “You were obviously unconscious, sleeping or something apparently. Anarchy, pandemonium everywhere. Some of us are already referring to it as the Apocalypse. In just the few short hours since it happened, we of the Blackhelm Coalition already suspect one-third of the world’s population was annihilated by the revived Old Ones and their mindless followers.” “Entire cities have already been cleansed of life by those beasts, and in only a matter of hours,” quipped the decorated commander of the underground regiment in a caustic tone. “Those who escaped the onslaught managed to flee to the countryside, and have fortified themselves in isolated outposts set up in the past by Coalition teams for just such an emergency. They’re safe as long as the protection holds up, which is thankfully indefinite. But even so, provisions will eventually run out, and they’ll inevitably face either starvation or death at the hands of the deities and their underlings.” “This is ludicrous,” grumbled Jack, his clenched fist whitening. “Possibly,” smirked the leader again. “But then again, sometimes truth is stranger than fiction. And if this is crazy, just wait, Bruce…..” * * The phone rang, abruptly snapping Jack from his bizarre dream. In a daze he arose from the bed and staggered toward the blaring phone, bracing himself against the stand with one hand while snatching up the receiver with the other. “Yeah?” he mumbled, still in a tired stupor. “Bruce, salutations to you,” greeted the elderly voice on the other end. “I apologize for calling you this early in the day, but I insist that you come down here to Philadelphia at once!” VJ stumbled exhaustedly toward the bare window, casting his eyes out on the grimy street. Damn….maybe things would be better if that dream was real…. “Jack, are you there?” VJ breathed with a forlorn sigh. “Reginald, do you realize I have a rather big match tomorrow? I don’t have time to fly down to Philly just for you to feed me more of your delirious mumbo-jumbo!” “I realize this is a bit hasty, and I apologize to do it on such short notice,” apologized McCarver. “But some vital information about your case has come up, and I was notified only minutes ago. Please come down to my office as soon as possible. I’ll book the tickets myself if need be…” “Nah, don’t worry about it,” mumbled Jack. “I’ll be down there later this evening sometime. But this better be important. It’s bad enough I’m having dreams about the Apocalypse, now this…..” “Just trust me. It will be worth the flight. I’ll see you then.” Jack hung up the phone and ambled back to bed, collapsing onto the springy cushion.
