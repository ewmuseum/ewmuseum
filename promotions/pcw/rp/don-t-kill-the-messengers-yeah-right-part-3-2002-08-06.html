Don't kill the messengers?...Yeah, right.(Part 3)

6 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 6, 2002 [Fast forward a few hours from arrival. The sun has fully set leaving a blanket of darkness in Lubbock, with lights beaming in from several buildings near the downtown core, and a cool breeze has begun to blow in from the North West. We head in to a seedy bar near the south end of the city. The place looks like…well...shit. Neon lights on the sign have burnt out. A piece of plywood currently acts like a shop window, and the door is resting on a slant…barely. We head inside the bar now. The bar smells like piss, the usual compliment of bar flies are predominant, and several people are passed out already onto free standing tables a few feet away from the bar, and near the main entrance. A few feet toward the back of the bar, we see a pair of pool tables, and we see Eddie Mandrake there, cleaning house on the table. There are seven balls remaining on the table, six of them solid, and the eight ball. Eddie winds up…and takes a shot. The sound of it resounds throughout the bar, and the eight-ball goes into the corner pocket with authority. Eddie is still wearing the stuff he wore when he got off the plane.] Eddie: Well…That’s three games my friend. Pay up that fifty you owe me. Pool Player: Ah, (Mumbling). [The bar’s resident pool player pays him the fifty. As the next “victim” puts the money into the table, and the balls file out into the tray. Eddie racks the balls up, and sets them on the table. The opponent grabs his cue. All have seen much action, as most are bent, and some tips have been knocked off. The opponent eventually picks one. Grabs the Cue ball, and breaks with extreme power.] Eddie: …That is a nice break. Opponent: Thanks. But I’m not done yet. [Both the seven ball, and the eleven ball have been sunk.] Opponent: I shall choose solids. [The opponent now takes Eddie to the cleaners. The one ball to the six ball, are sunk with relative ease. However, when the opponent reaches the eight ball...he scratches, thus ending the game.] Eddie: HA! YOU SUCK BUDDY! Opponent: Do you realize who you are talking to? I am a [BEEEEEP] Bounty Hunter! Eddie: And you’re drunk. Maybe we should go outside, huh? Opponent: I’m sober, damn it! [Eddie takes a sniff of the breath of this “opponent.”] Eddie: Yup. Drunk. [Eddie takes a quick swing onto the jaw of his opponent with a mighty punch. He then drags the opponent out the back door. Out in the back, it is a well lit area with a dumpster full of garbage. Eddie takes the opponent and places him onto a pile of boxes near the dumpster.] Eddie: You [BEEEEEP]. You know that announcing publicly you are a bounty hunter is against the Code! Opponent: And what do you know of the Code? Eddie: Because I am a bounty hunter myself. Edward Mandrake. Opponent: Oh. Name’s Hank Quaid. I operate out of Abilene. Eddie: Then what are you doing here? Abilene is a good drive away from here. Quaid: Well…I’m on my way to Vegas. I got a tip on a good score there. It is really a race to get to Vegas right now. Why aren’t you going? Eddie: I’m trying to give it up for a new career. Quaid: Apparently, a high ranking government official is headed to Vegas to open a new IRS Taxation Center. Bounty Hunters from all over the U.S. are headed to Vegas to claim the $10,000,000. Eddie: $10,000,000?? Holy [BEEP]! Quaid: My car broke down and is getting fixed at a service center at the edge of town. Eddie: When is it supposed to take place? Quaid: Three days. Eddie: I wish you good luck on your hunt. [Quaid looks at Eddie puzzled.] Quaid: You mean you aren’t going? I’d gladly give you a ride. Eddie: No man. I’m going legit. Besides, I can give out pain and punishment in my new profession. Take it easy. [Eddie turns and heads back into the bar, and out the other side. Quaid is left on the boxes, pondering. Eddie heads out of the bar and into his rental car.] Eddie (Thinking): $10,000,000. Damn, now I wish I didn’t take this wrestling job. [BEEP]. $10,000,000! [Eddie starts the car, reverses out of his parking spot, and drives off back to the hotel.]
