When Gods become bored, they play...POOL?!

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: A time not long past. (The following took place before the events of Tuesday Turmoil,September 24th, 2002.) It had been a dull and dreary day in the Heavens. The Egyptian Gods had each remarked that a day such as this hadn’t occurred in their private realm in some time, and then each commented that that statement couldn’t possibly be true since time had no real meaning here. At present the Gods find themselves preoccupied with utter boredom and as time passes Seth, the Egyptian God of basically all things bad, turns his watchful gaze towards the glistening all-seeing-crystal sat upon its ancient podium. With a sigh he leans forwards and coughs politely, catching a few of the other Gods’ attentions… Seth: “WHY IS IT THAT WE ARE GODS, THE ALL MIGHTY OF MORTALS, AND YET OUR FOLLOWER, TITU XANSIV, FINDS MUCH MORE THAN WE TO DO WITH HIS HOURS?” As the God’s words are formed in the vacuum of space and non-time the vigilant ears of Isis spring into life. The beautiful yet haunting deity glides forth from the back of the pillar-flooded hall and cocks a thin eyebrow. Isis: “Xansiv finds things to do in his realm because time actually exists there. And besides look at his house! As if he can’t find something to do.” Isis hovers a smooth hand gracefully over the crystal, her dark skin glistening in the light from outside the chamber. Moments pass before the all-seeing globe becomes cloudy and a new image appears. It shows a room, tall and shady where a long thin green table sits unmoved. A smile forms on the God’s face… Isis: “Yet I feel a trip to visit our friend…err…follower would lighten our spirits, don’t you think, Amun-Ra?” The echo of a cane upon stone flags fills the room. Before long the tall, slim figure of an aged man etches forth through the gathering crowd of immortals. His long hands find their way to the top of his walking aid as he comes to a stop in front of Isis, smiling knowingly. Amun-Ra: “And I, Isis, feel your motives for wishing to see young Titu are far from true. But, I must confess, the image I see does strike me with curiosity. What is that thing, there?” Amun-Ra, high ruler of the other Egyptian deities, points towards the green-skinned table. With a thin-lipped grin Isis says… “Pool.” The rising Californian sun brushes through the thin gaps in the curtains as the stirring Titu Xansiv is woken early. There’s a distinct sound in the air and Pharaoh immediately (well after some yawning and what not) realizes it is in fact the clash of billiard balls. Rising from his bed, slightly confused, Titu manages to trip and fall over a pair of Sphinx-headed slippers and crawl to the room’s door. Far off, in one of the many rooms of The Palace, a conversation is taking place, and though it would appear to be hard to hear the words are so crisp and clear that everything said is heard without much trouble at all. “LOOK, I DID POT IT… I SWEAR DOWN!” “Like Duat you did! You were obviously manipulating the game…again!” “BAH! I WISH YOU’D BOTH JUST SHUT UP ARGUING!” “Ha! And that’s coming from somegod like you?!” Pharaoh scratches his head, obviously confused, and rubbing sleep from his eyes looks back at the peacefully sleeping Isiy. He edges out of the bedroom and into the winding hallway beyond where he leans over the banister and continues to listen in on the conversation. “You’re all acting like mortals…” “Even me?” “Even you!” “TECHNICALLY WE WERE ALL MORTALS ONCE. ” “Oh, go and eat some birdseed!” “HOW TERRIBLY RUDE!” “Come now, come now! Everyone just settle down!” Titu rubs his eyes and shakes his head to and fro, still not fully awake. He can’t quite believe what’s going on downstairs in his own home. He has intruders lurking somewhere in his house and he’s the only one who can get them the Duat out! And so with a nod of his head he picks up the nearest and heaviest thing he can find, which turns out to be a novelty coffee mug shaped as a bear barrel, and ventures downstairs. As he reaches the bottom he steps out into the colossal lounge and creeps methodically across the room, daring not to make a noise so as not to disturb the trespassers. Following their voices, he comes at last to a stop outside the games room and pushing a vigilant ear against the door, he listens in… “Now come on, let’s all play nicely, shall we?” Outside the door, Pharaoh’s brow furrows. The Pharaoh: “Play nicely?!” “Well, he potted his balls while I wasn’t even looking!” Pharaoh’s eyes widen. “STOP COMPLAINING, YOU COULD EASILY TAKE ADVANTAGE NOW!” His eyes widening even further, Pharaoh shakes off any thoughts he has on what the culprits within the room could be doing and decides that now is his time to strike. The Pharaoh: “Right, that is it! The Gods know what the Duat those burglars are doing in there, but I’m putting an end to this right now!” His eyes fall upon the beer-barrel coffee mug in his hand… The Pharaoh: “Or at least try…” Like a getaway steamroller, or perhaps a train or even a yacht, (yachts, after all, can be fairly dangerous if left alone on speedily moving waters) the door is dramatically kicked open with the aid of Titu’s foot. A battle cry follows abruptly as he sprints into the games room, bearing only the mug in his hand as a weapon, and wearing only his black evening loincloth. He stops hastily, looks around the room and, as realization sinks in, sighs. Horus: “Ah, Titu. We meet again at last.” Amun-Ra: “Well met, I must say!” A moment of shocked silence fills the air before at length Titu says… “What the Duat are you all doing here?” The slim figure of Isis stands from her chair and seductively edges towards the Egyptian, smiling sweetly and sexily all the while. The other deities gathered in the large games room watch as she points in the pool table’s direction and grins mockingly, the disgruntled Pharaoh looking onward. Isis: “We were divulging in a game of pool, Titu…” The Pharaoh: What the heck for? You’re Gods! Haven’t you got fates to be controlling, or something?!” Seth: “YOU, TITU XANSIV, ARE THE ONLY FATE WE ACTUALLY HAVE TO MANIPULATE THESE DAYS. FAITH IN US IS LONG GONE, AND BESIDES…WE WERE DREAFULLY BORED IN THE HEAVENS.” Xansiv shakes his head and pears about the gloomy room. The Egyptian Gods each casually sit upon chairs surrounding the pool table, but far away enough from the green-skinned game to give the Gods playing room to take their shots. At present Titu recognized Anubis and Thoth as the two arguing deities enrolled in the game. His gaze then falls on the billiards (classic red and yellow versions) and finds that each God has four balls remaining, not counting the black. He sighs. The Pharaoh: “I really can’t believe this! I mean, yeah, sure, you give me the guidance I need to make it through the day but do you really have to crash at my house and hog my damned pool table?!” Oblivious of the cynicism in his voice there is a general reply of nods and mumbles in approval of Xansiv’s words. Titu’s face grows red with anger. The Pharaoh: “Bah!” He storms out of the room, huffs a long breath and then slams the door behind him as he leaves, the echo of its impact on the well-crafted frame flooding The Palace like the Nile bursting its banks. Feeling the need to calm the Gods’ enraged only follower, Thoth shouts after him… Thoth: “IT’S A VERY NICE POOL TABLE, MIND YOU…” The others glare. Thoth: “WHAT?!” “HAHAHA! I DO BELIEVE YOU’VE JUST BEEN SEVEN-BALLED, HORUS!” “Bah! If only you could play without altering the rules…literally, eh Thoth?! ” Pharaoh winces. The Pharaoh: “Still in there, are they?” Isiy sighs. Isiy: “I’m afraid so.” It’d been hours since the Gods first arrived at the house and already they’d seemed to settle down considerably within the confines of the games room. Laughter had filled the Xansiv mansion for most of the day so far and after hours of Gods having a good time at his expense Titu was just about at his wits end. Well, he thinks, they aren’t sleeping over, that’s for sure! “I might ask Titu if I can get myself a drink of his finest wine soon.” “ASK FOR ME, TOO, HORUS.” “But of course…” Isiy watched in concern as the vein in Titu’s forehead throbs hastily. At length he harshly throws his newspaper to the carpeted floor and growls. The Pharaoh: “Right! That…is…IT!” Pharaoh rises from his armchair in the lounge as laughter from the games room once again envelops The Palace’s entire interior. The Egyptian walks across the room’s length and out into a thin hallway which leads off into the distance. Isiy follows at his heel with a look of concern about her beautiful face as the voices of the Gods grows ever louder, and subsequently ever nearer. Eventually Titu comes to the games room’s door and scowls. The Pharaoh: “Enough is e-bloody-nough!” With those words Pharaoh opens the door, looks about at the gawking Gods and with a grin walks towards Thoth, the Egyptian God of Knowledge, and rips the tall, wooden cue from his feathered hands. The ibis-headed deity looks on in shock as Titu leans upon it with his full weight and waves a hand absent-mindedly as he speaks. The Pharaoh: “Ok, this is how it is: Thoth, you seem to be having quite the outing here at my pool room, and so I challenge you to a match that will decide whether or not you and the other Gods gathered in this room get the Duat out of my house. All that remains now is your answer” Titu smirks. The Pharaoh: “So, do you accept my challenge?” Though his beak enables him to do little but grin, Thoth, for once, smiles out of his own accord. He looks down at the green-skinned pool table, which today he has become accustomed to playing upon, and nods his knowledge-filled skull. Thoth: “YOU REALIZE THAT YOU’LL BE BEATEN, THOUGH?” The Pharaoh: “As true as that may be, I’m still willing to put up a fight.” Nodding, Thoth looks around at the other Egyptian Gods sat about the table. All gathered seem to commend Titu for his nobility, many smiling visages littering the gaming area. Thoth: “VERY WELL, TITU. ALL THAT REMAINS NOW IS WHETHER YOU WOULD YOU LIKE TO BREAK OR NOT?” With a wave of his hand, Thoth renders the balls gathered in the underbelly of the pool table to emerge from within and form a perfectly stacked triangle of billiards. Grinning, Pharaoh places the white ball on its appropriate spot and lowers his torso into position to break the formation. The Pharaoh: “I think that maybe I shall…” With great force he draws back the cue and smashes the white ball straight through Thoth’s perfect arrangement. Circling the table the God of knowledge watches as two yellows fly into their metaphorical homes, and winces. Thoth: “DRAT! A NICE BREAK, NEVERTHELESS, TITU.” Xansiv positions himself into the best angle possible and once again brings his cue back… The Pharaoh: “I know…” Hours had past since the game began and now the two duelling players were drawn at evens. Thoth had three red balls remaining on the green surface of the classically designed pool table and Titu had three yellows. A bead of sweat falls from the brow of Titu Xansiv. Thoth was on a roll…again. In the last few shots he’d managed to pot most of his remaining balls, and now with only three left upon the table it looked as though the Gods would be staying at The Palace for a little while longer. Thoth: “GOOD GAME, TITU; SHAME YOU HAD NO CHANCE OF DEFEATING ME IN THE FIRST PLACE.” The Gods squawks a chuckle. The Pharaoh: “You’ll find that I’m usually classed as the underdog in everything I do, Thoth. Yet I always seem to manage to defeat the odds and throw people’s opinions back in their faces.” Thoth’s eyes gleamed. Thoth: “IS THAT SO? WELL ON THIS OCCASION I REGRET TO INFORM YOU THAT YOU WON’T BEAT ME, AND OUR STAY AT THE PALACE WILL BE LONGER LIVED THAN YOU FIRST HOPED.” Sat around the table, the other Gods frown and shake their heads, obviously disagreeable with Thoth’s words. The God of Egyptian knowledge turns to them after Amun-Ra coughs politely in an attempt to catch his attention. Amun-Ra: “There is no need to be so cocky, Thoth.” Isis: “Indeed.” Thoth sneers, polishes the end of his cue with the blue chalk and looks down at the billiards lingering. With a smile he draws back his instrument of play and clatters the white into one of his reds. Unsurprisingly it rolls into the designated pocket. Thoth grins as the white ball mysteriously rolls backwards in a strange manner, hits a yellow, which relying on logical know-how would have had little chance of hitting in regular situations, and pots that one too! Sensing foul-play Pharaoh’s brow furrows as he blows up a row with the Gods watching. The Pharaoh: “WHAT THE DUAT?! You cannot be serious?! He manipulated the game for the love of…well…Horus!” Ignoring his anger-filled words, Horus, Egyptian God of the sky and protector of the dynasty, nods pleasantly and points two fingers in Xansiv’s direction. Horus: “That’s me, kid.” Amun-Ra pays his fellow God a strange look. Amun-Ra: “Getting back to the point, Titu…You may be right, perhaps he just did alter the game; but Thoth, being a God, is allowed to manipulate it…and besides, what are you really going to do about it?” A thought occurs in Xansiv’s brain. He smirks as it is processed thoroughly and then ushers the other Gods to be silent so as to speak a few choice words. The Pharaoh: “Fair enough. Let the game prolong.” Thoth couldn’t quite believe that he’d been beaten. He really couldn’t. Titu had managed to counter every one of his cheating ways with deception-filled tactics of his own. The Gods had laughed at Thoth when Titu pulled his cue from his feathered fingers as he took shots, as Titu knocked him slightly when he was trying to cut the ball finely, as Titu placed his red balls in front of Thoth’s yellows so the God had little chance of potting anything. Xansiv truly had been sly, and for that Thoth paid him great respect. Once again the Gods find themselves in The Heavens. The day had been acceptably fun-filled and a relaxing few weeks of doing absolutely nothing was all they needed to unwind after Thoth had slaughtered them all at the game the mortals called ‘pool’. At length Seth looks around the ivory temple and, after hours of the question praying upon his mind, finally utters what he’s wanted to say for some time. Seth: “SO WHY DON’T WE GO DOWN TO EARTH MORE OFTEN?” Thoth nods in concurrence. Thoth: “YES, WHY DON’T WE?” Amun-Ra’s eyes grow wide. Amun-Ra: “Don’t you remember Godsdamn Jesus?!” Seth: “AH, POINT TAKEN…”
