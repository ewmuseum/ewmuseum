006 - The Scarlett Dress; Act II, Scene II

6 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09-06-02 Magnoila Police StationNew Orleans, LA 10:00 am The bonanza of reporters from all over the state happened to shock Kendra James herself didn't realize that the numbers would be of this magnitude. Reporters lined up, bunched against one another, all vying for a good spot to find the truth. It was a moment of anticpation as one of the wrestlers in the local wrestling tournament had happened to be in scandal. A scandal that possibly well could bring the abrupt end of that wrestler's career. There was a podium set up inside of the room, the spacious crowd was tense with excitement. Standing there was Kendra James and a representative of the tournament, wishing that this was good publicity. The backdoor to the room happened to open, showing the solum face of Peter Evans as he began to make his way to the podium. One of the reporters looked over towards another and snickered, 'He looks like he'll bring down the industry with that smirk.' 'His cold gaze could probably bring down Big Allan Steele,' the other happened to joke. 'I'll tell you this, I'd hate to be the guy he's trying to ruin.' 'What's the deal though? Since when does the media go after men of this nature?' The reporter shrugged his shoulders, 'Seems like he's a celebrity - a wrestler. He's trying to put this away open and shut.' 'Something of this magnitude could ruin a life.' 'Yeah, no kidding.' Their attention happened to turn to faces towards the speaker, Peter Evans. He wore his black suit, white collar shirt underneath and a red polka dot tie to match. All of that couldn't match the sad, expressionless face of his. 'Thanks for your participation in this matter. Although I wish it were under better circumstances that I could come towards you today, this is the best platform. We are stuggling to find justice in the murder of Franklin Tyrell, mayor of Magnolia, and I wish to find the guilty party and bring him to where he or she needs to be.' The arm of one reporter happened to stand out of the crowd, interrupting the conference. 'Detective evans, we all understand the general conception that evidence is to support the charge. These are very serious charges you are placing on a person. What is your evidence?' 'Our most damning evidence is from a young woman who has pin-pointed whom the murderer happens to be,' Peter answered. 'But the woman is a prostitute along the streets, is she not?' Peter stopped himself short, unsure of how to answer the question at hand. He wasn't expect the brash claim to be spoken by the press at this certain moment in time. 'I'm sorry, but that information is being withheld...' The reporter pressed on, being aggressive about the questioning. 'I have reports that infact, a April Jackson happens to be found inside of the mayor's mansion at the time of the crime. She has been a known prostitute of our streets, and it has been known for a fairly good number of years.' 'I will not confirm, or deny those reports, but...' 'So the question reminds still,' the reporter interrupted Evans once again. 'How can we take the word of a prostitute, a woman known on the streets to see and believe a lot of things to claim that she saw who murdered Franklin Tyrell? She could have been flat out lying, just to save herself you know?' Peter slammed his fist along the podium, startling the reporter from more questioning. That slam happened to silence the entire congregation, as the conference was in a standstill. His face was caught in flush red anger, not hiding anything from the crowd. 'Dante Jackson, the man whom the woman happened to point out, is guilty.' He pointed out defiantly. 'I am willing to put my reputation on the line, to prove it to you all that he is, infact, the guilty party. A man's first duty is to love and honor thy neighbor, and Mr. Jackson did not do this. In his brutality, he snuck three bullets into the frame of Franklin Tyrell and killed him. 'Mr. Jackson is a very popular and intelligent individual...' Detective Evans laughed hard as he proceeded to castigate the man in charge. 'Mr. Jackson is very popular with a minority of the population, steaming from any social background. He is a wrestler and a musician. Not one of your doctors, or lawyers, or educators, but a wrestler. He does something that is not even considered, a form of entertainment. He has no sense of the word decency as his stage shows have been known to hype violence, promote sexual behavior, and deviate conduct. He lives a lie everyday, and god knows for certain his skeletons haven't felt it as well.' Detective Evans shook his head and stalked off, ending the conference abruptly. There entire sporting and reporting press were in a buzz. Reporters moved away from the scene, shaking their heads in disbelief. They didn't believe what this man had to say. Kendra James stood up from her seat and began to smile. 'Well Barry, I believe you got what you came here to get. The press doesn't believe Detective Evans, or what he has to say.' Barry Gordon, commissioner of the tournament was young, forty-three with golden locks, charming looks and a knack for finding talent. He had put together the tournament, to prove he was the best talent advisor in history. 'His cause is well known, but he's treading in waters he surely will drown in.' 'He's in a bit of pressure.' Kendra replied. 'It'll be good once he can settle back down to petty items again. It eats at him for failing only once at something holding his tenure and it cost him his chance for a better life. Now I've done my part in the operation, is Jackson the culprit?' Barry chuckled as he grasped his chin. 'I don't know, and I'd rather not want to find out. He's doing wonders for this city, bringing in tourists and generating money. If any investigation should be conceived, it is how he can stay on top of his respective fields. Good day Ms. James. It was a pleasure.' Kendra just smirked at him. 'No, the pleasure was all yours.' * * * Waltz Hotel RoomNew Orleans, LA 11:15 am I had been awake for at least three hours, but I sat in bed as if it were a few minutes past my bedtime. I didn't care that the birds were singing outside or if the sun was shining, I couldn't go outside and face the press. No doubt Detective Evans had done his best in swaying the crowd that I was a criminal, a fraud, and that I was doing nothing more than being a contumacious prick who needed to be reprimanded. The worst part of the entire ordeal happened to be I wasn't sure if he was being truthful of it. I just buried myself in the blankets and hoped it happened to be a dream of sorts, just a dream. I felt a slight pull on my skin, showing that this was no dream. Usually when you pinch someone from sleep, they wake up. I wished it happened to me. Ring Ring Ring! Peeking out of the little airhole I had happen to create, I reached out and grabbed my cellphone, buzzing with activity and indistinct. If it were one nosy reporter, click, the phone would go dead. If it was a salesman trying to sell me something, then she'd get a verbal tongue lashing, before hanging up. Fortunately for my nerves, it was Desmond. 'Dante?' Desmond asked. 'Here,' I answered out with a craven tone of animosity. 'You find a good hotel room?' he questioned. 'The press is swarming all over New Orleans looking for you. They want a statement.' 'I'm sure of that already Desmond, thanks for telling me,' I told him with a low tone. 'Are they screwing with my head?' There was a pause on the other end of the line, telling me that something had to be wrong, or atleast something had been given out new. Towards the contrary, Desmond didn't hold things back form me, as he was the most up front man other than my pops. 'They follow you on this one,' Desmond replied. 'The press didn't believe one cent of the old codger's story. Their story happened to come from a woman whose seen a lot of things, her being a hooker anyway. I guess they feel sympathy for you, and the past transgressions against you.' 'I saved her Desmond.' I could almost feel Desmond's voice cripple when I told him that. 'Who, the hooker?' 'That's right,' I replied. 'The mayor tried to rape her and in my best interest, I saved her.' 'But that's impossible,' Desmond said, shock enveloping every word. 'You saved a woman who maybe was drunk and now since she saw you in the room, she happens to visualize a lot of things. Even a murder. 'She did, and she told the truth.' Desmond was one of those men who'd you think would know everything before his correct age. He'd seen everything, just about from death to the Superbowl. But for once, he was quiet, something I hadn't even known from him. 'I just…I just don't believe it,' Desmond stammered. 'You can't have done what you said you've done.' 'I was there for a reason, just some mayoral trip with my aunt who happens to live in New Orleans. It was one because she got shitty times. He barged in, and I stayed to watch the entire thing.' 'No…' Desmond said, trailing off. He was destroyed, and broken hearted at the same time. In the farthest regions of my mind, I wondered if he would just hang up right there and then and never speak to me. It spoke volumes of how a friendship could come so often to not be broken by another's tragedy. 'Did they print you?' 'I don't remember,' I told him honestly. 'I was in there, minding my own business when the lights went out. I could see the sillouette of Tyrell standing over the woman, so I tackled him to the ground. Next thing I know, I get hit over the head…' 'Dante, you didn't kill him. Its not possible.' He was being seriously ludicrous. How could he bring it upon himself to find the truth, that I did for some reason or another saved this woman's life, by taking another? It didn't suit fit with him, and I knew he didn't. Everytime he spoke a single word, he trembled with fear and tried to shake it off as if it wasn't there. I knew it was, he was scared. Scared for what'll happen to me. 'Desmond, I know it might be difficult coming to terms with…' 'Dante, I'm reminding you that you didn't kill Tyrell,' Desmond repeated. 'They could have found the prints, we can get a fingerprint test and it'll all come out. For the positive I hope.' I just sighed in discomfort, knowing that any type of test would prove something of my innocence, or guilt. No matter how a test turns out, the truth is known in my mind. 'Desmond, I don't need a test…' 'You're not really thinking straight are you? Can you truthfully even say that you did this? This would obliterate your life, and what about Tracy here? Have you thought about her?' 'I haven't told her,' I truthfully admitted. 'Sure, she's heard of it by now, but I'm trying to avoid her at all costs. I'm not going to ruin my life thanks to this.' 'And when she does, what is she going to think?' 'I…I don't know,' I stammered, still confused about the situation. 'Upset doesn't even define where she happens to take this. She's been known to throw huge fits, and I don't want to be caught in one. She might not want to hear my side of the story.' 'Look Dante, I've never even tried to run how your life shall turn out But I'll tell you this, for a bit of advice. Get out of the country right now, so that the press won't even being to sniff you. Get away and clear your mind, before this all boils over into a huge battle for your credibility.' 'I'm not going anyway, Desmond.' I told him straight towards the point. 'I'm not going to run away from things now, I haven't done that ever in my life.' If it sounded like I was giving myself defeatist idealisms, it's because I was. 'Dante, I'm going to get you through this,' Desmond pledged. 'Call Tracy and explain it to her nice and calmly. I'll pull a few strings from my side of the deal. We're going to get through this.' I wanted to tell him not to worry about me, to leave himself out of this and let it all rely upon me. But the phone began to buzz before I could tell him. Now, I was alone, once again in my bedroom. Forced to make a call I will come close to regretting for the rest of my life. From one other end of the spectrum to the other, I could only let my head rest in my palms. Looking back at a picture of my mom and dad, raising me - the addition of both him and her, it was depressing. He hadn't done anything to launch us into trouble, but I had done that. I was no good son, just the bad seed who came to realize his pain in the latter portion of life. The numbers began to dial themselves as I visualized just how Tracy would react, back in Houston. She could drive over here and confront me, but the press would catch a whiff of that if it came straight from the White House itself. My heart pounded faster and faster, anxious for her sweet voice to come on the phone and let me stop all of this and slow things down. And then, the air on the other end swept up and she was on. Okay, I thought to myself. Time to be truthful. 'Hello?' A very broken and worn out voice said. 'Hey,' I returned, trying to be as normal as I could. 'What are you doing?' There was the slight pause, instantly recognizing who was on the phone with her. I could only wonder if she wanted to talk to me or not, dissolving our relationship and throwing our child towards her parents. My god, I couldn't let that happen. But when you're upset, you don't blame yourself for the actions, you point the finger at someone else. 'What do you want?' She knew. The whole story unfurled on television and she had to sit there and eat up every word. Without hearing my voice of reason. 'Tracy, I don't know what to tell you…' 'Dante, I haven't made up my mind yet. Even when you rescued me during the Wrestling God tournament, I didn't say a word. I know how stories are manipulated in the press. I'm a part of the media you know...' A slight glimmer of hope appeared to me. 'Tray, you know that I would never try to hurt you…' She knew that much was true, I'd never try to make her apart of my mess, and all I wanted was for us to be together. Not to faulter like Sean Combs and Jennifer Lopez during his trial. But she hadn't made up the part of her mind whether I was a liar, or truthfully speaking. She thought the press was lying, letting me have one good chance of reestablishing my case... 'Just tell me one thing, Dante. Tell me that you didn't break our promise. Tell me you didn't.' I paused and sighed. All of a sudden, my life was crashing all around me, and there would be nothing that I could do to save it. Could I tell her the truth, and then she'd distant herself away from me. If I lied, then she'd know it for a certain fact. We'd been together since Junior High School, so she knows. 'I love you, and Simmone.' And the phone died in my hands.
