Succumbing to Shadows: Chapter 2 – Liberation Avowed

Date: February 3rd of 2003 A.D. Ah, the memories.... The doctor adjusted his wire-rim glasses, looking down on the pallid, sweat-drenched visage of Bruce Shanahan as he shuddered in the confines of the straitjacket enveloping his brawny mass. His breathing came in erratic, desperate gasps and his eyes imparted a begging plea for salvation that he couldn’t utter through words in his present state. Okoye felt pity for Shanahan, regardless of the crude wrath the Boston native had shown the doctor in the past. “I’m sorry, Bruce,” soothes the doctor, setting a hand on the shuddering patient’s shoulder. “We tried….I did what I could…..” Bruce was almost catatonic with dread, unresponsive to stimuli from the doctor as he directed a thread-like beam of light into the idle Shanahan’s eyes. Try as he could, he could elicit no response from the quaking PCW prospect and let out a long, exasperated sigh before turning to leave. He cast one final doleful glance at the disoriented and silent ring warrior before ordering the door closed and bolted by the guards. “I’m sorry, Bruce. I really am…..” -------------------------------------- (Account of the events of September 3, 2002 from the statements of Doctor Houghton and various hospital records) “Please bring the patient forth.” A pair of burly guards grasped the straitjacket-bound New Englander by the underarms, dragging him across the threshold and propping him up at the bare table. He blinked unresponsively, a foot tapping repeatedly on the floor opposite his languid, hunched form. “Thank you. Please leave us.” The duo, clad in simple white shirts bearing the institution’s symbol, nodded and left the sullen chamber with nary a word. The heavy, reinforced door slammed abruptly and clicked as the bolt lock slid into place, a standard precaution when dealing directly with Greensburgh’s more dangerous inmates. Bruce Shanahan had become detached and withdrawn since the night of the 1st, unresponsive to all but the most direct of stimuli and in a near state of catatonia. His complete change of behavior had prompted worry from his employers and the staff of Greensburgh State Mental Institution, but nevertheless the staff wasn’t taking chances. Doctor Samsanov Okoye adjusted the thin spectacles resting on the bridge of his nose. He casually perused the flaccid Shanahan, whose appearance was almost zombie-like. He used but the sparest amount of energy to hold himself upright, and that trait alone gave the vague depiction he was alive and not a propped up corpse. “Mr. Shanahan, how do you feel today?” Okoye’s lone response was another involuntary blink. He bit his lip and leaned forward, trying to peer into the glazed visage of his dismal patient. Shanahan appeared to gaze at the floor. “When the police arrived at your residence several days ago, you were raving about demons, gods and some disembodied eye outside your apartment window. You rendered two officers from the Boston P.D. unconscious before you were subdued and put under a sedative. I’m asking you to please try and describe what you think you saw. Your account two days ago was virtually incoherent and you did nothing but mumble about something you called……” Okoye shuffles quickly through his notes as Shanahan wavers in his chair. …….ah, the ‘Great Old Ones’ you said. You claimed this ‘eye’ was hovering outside your third-story apartment window as some messenger of your doom, sent by these ‘Great Old Ones’. Now, Bruce….please understand that I’m here as your friend. I want to help you, and part of that means that we need to communicate. You see what I’m saying?” Shanahan’s eyes darted to the white-coat-garbed physician. Okoye, attempting to establish a bond, cracked a smile and arose from his chair. He paced to the east side of the room and sighed. “No one else that night saw this little floating monstrosity of yours outside your window, or anywhere else around the building for that matter. So how can you substantiate that claim, Bruce? Can you?” “Yes.” Okoye arched an inquisitive eyebrow at the monotone reply. “I see. Well, please do so. Do you have evidence?” Shanahan retained his silence. Okoye merely shrugged and shook his head. “Now as I see it, you’ve fabricated this whole ‘Great Old Ones’ charade to deal with the stresses of life. It’s a natural human reaction to trauma, especially for one with the oppressive life you’ve endured. You’ve hallucinated, perceived grandiose illusions engineered by your ego and repressed memories....” “Alas, Dr. Okoye, I fear you are incorrect yet again. Just like you were incorrect six months ago when you diagnosed me as being a victim of acute night terrors.” Okoye paused mid-step. The melancholic tone was gone from Shanahan’s voice and had been replaced with an air of menacing calm, prompting Okoye to rotate his head to look upon his patient now sitting erect and attentive. It spooked the doctor that Bruce had altered so quickly from a catatonic personality to the keen and actively subdued interest displayed in his voice and body language. That was to say nothing of the confidential informatoin in his file. Samsanov sincerely felt a sensation of dread creep into the atmosphere as his patient uttered those bitter words. “I don’t know what you’re talking about....” “I have....sources. Ones that fools like you dare not call into question.” “Bruce, I don’t know....” “There was nothing Earthly scientific about it, doc, no psychology or any of that other bullshit you quacks feed. It was a game, Doctor Okoye. A game that became a recruitment drive.” “Let’s be reasonable for a moment, Mr. Shanahan....” Dr. Houghton is abruptly cut off by a curt scoff by his straightjacket-laden patient. “Sometimes reason fails, my good doctor, it becomes inapplicable,” smirks Shanahan with an evil leer. “Not every phenomenon functions with the luxury of reason. I have seen that light. When shall you see the same?” The doctor pursed his lips, staring in bewilderment at the awkwardly twisted face of Bruce Shanahan. “You fucks like to aptly place logic on every unexplainable element your precious sciences can’t clarify,” continued Shanahan venomously on the spell-bound doctor. “Strange lights in the sky, defied laws of physics, the disappearances of entire civilizations…..heheh. You peddle your ‘rationale’ to the weak, solace-craving public like a drug. But ironically, in your refusal to acknowledge what you don’t understand….the only ones who are lost on the concept of reason are yourselves, the educated.” Shanahan chuckled, a glimmer of madness twinkling in his eyes as he leaned back in his chair. His sardonic smirk was abruptly exchanged with a menacing sneer. “I’ve spent too long defending people like you, guarded the lives of too many who have deserved to see their lives destroyed. The most worthy of life have had it extinguished for the greed of your kind. They’ve died on account of mankind’s avarice and ruthlessness. For governments and corporations! And now, I swear by my hand that they shall all pay for their sins. They will find atonement by my judgment!” The doctor clasped his hands behind his back, turning away from the demented glare of his patient. His eyes fell now on the portrait adorning the west wall, a panorama of bright hues to detract from that maddening sparkle in the eye of the crazed Shanahan. “And just how do you propose to do that, Mr. Shanahan?” His query was met with a somewhat stifled and almost giddy snicker. “Humanity already has one foot in the grave, dear doctor,” posed Shanahan cryptically. “I merely need to give that little nudge to put the second one in alongside it. That is my objective.” Though Okoye couldn’t see the look in the eyes of Bruce Shanahan, he instinctually knew what would blaze in those sockets. And he thanked the forces of Heaven he was spared that madness. “I fear that is impossible,” the doctor iterated matter-of-factly. “Until you overcome this affliction, you must remain under our care. It is hard to tell how you may react if we were to release you back into the world while you harbor these cravings for vengeance.” “You have no choice in the matter, doctor.” The doctor couldn’t help but suppress a wide grin at this revelation. “Oh really?” he scoffed. “I, the head physician at this facility, have no say in whether or not you go free at your leisure?” The smile Bruce expressed in retort was morbid and not subtly perverse. “Tomorrow afternoon at 4 pm. At that time, I will be released. That has been affirmed.” Normally Okoye would have retaliated with laughter and dismissed the mad lunatic. But something he couldn’t explain brought fear to his heart and caused a cold sweat to break out on his brow. He could only stare at his sinister subject with skepticism. And to his chagrin, for some reason even that was declining..... (Excerpted from the memoirs of Bruce Shanahan) Today was one of those rare days I opted to stomach television. I can’t explain why I felt the urge to suddenly expose myself to the despicable clothing ads and ridiculous propaganda plaguing the airwaves which I’ve avoided for weeks now, but something propelled me to switch on that dusty contraption and watch something. As I did, it dawned on me at that moment that PCW had local programming syndicated at that time, and things began to make sense. I see it as yet another heads-up from the Old Ones. Duke Williams, that blubbering old redneck, had the gall to run me down on network television. Alas things never change when they should, and they do when they should not. When I was terminated during my initial run in the company, Williams was an outspoken ignoramus. I make my return over three months later, and Williams is still an outspoken ignoramus. I’ve been directed to simply send Duke Williams into purgatory. For the glory of the Great Old Ones I have aimed to become an unfeeling machine, but Williams was an annoying twit that got under my skin even before I was committed to the Greensburgh asylum and old habits tend to die hard. Most of my directives these days come without malice, without passion, and without any excessive pride. But what happens in the ring on Turmoil will be far from without passion, pride or malice (pardon the pun). It will be crucifixion. Put quite simply, Williams is a stubborn, fucking stooge that needs to be exterminated. Somewhere along the line the drunken bastard has picked up tales that I don’t know how to wrestle nor fight. Apparently he and his sources have conveniently forgotten my rather well-documented history in the Japanese circuit. It’s been a while since I unleashed my ‘training’ in the warzones of the death-match circuit. But no means have I forgotten. It’s sort of like riding a bike. You learn, you don’t do it for a while, maybe your skills deteriorate. But when that big-mouthed show-off down the block challenges you to a race, it all comes flooding back and you smoke the snotty motherfucker. There may be a fine line between outracing a child on a 10-speed and dismembering some liquored-up yokel, but the logic isn’t much different. After I send Williams plummeting into the tortuous bowels of Hell, I’ll move on to do likewise on Friday. What conscious wrath still lingering in my soul will be wrought on Duke Williams. One of us will be ‘Hell-Bound’, but there won’t be any whiskey involved. At least not short of me using it as lighter fluid to saturate his maggot-ridden corpse on a funeral pyre before setting it ablaze. If that bumpkin wants to go out in a ‘blaze of glory’ then he can be obliged. We can spread the man’s ashes across cattle country, and his soul can rot in servitude to Shub-Niggurath for eternity. More than the half-wit deserves. I only pray Skylar Green and the other heathens in Friday’s main event are watching the monitors come Turmoil. The Old Ones have grown restless. And a demonstration of their power has become necessary.....
