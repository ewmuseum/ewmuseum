Old Flames New Names

22 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1-22-03 Old Flames New Names This little story begins in Teresa’s early years. The most crucial time in a child’s life isn’t just the first five years, but also those short lived years of high school. The decisions a teen makes during these four years can affect him or her for the rest of their life. Some choose band over a sport. A few of these may develop into famous musicians. A young girl decides to loose her virginity to her high school sweetheart of five months. Nine months later she has a baby. Three months after that, the sweetheart whom she gave her body and being to crushes her future dreams of a happy family with a break up and a new girlfriend. A boy has done well academically all his life. Socially he is well liked and plays football for his school. During practice he injured to the point he can never play again. He begins to believe there is no use in making the grade if he can’t play instead of planning a new goal. Before the end of the school year, his grades begin to fall and soon drop out. Five yeas later he is busting butt in a job he hates and the pay barely gets him by. Enough about everyone else. We have these teens and then there is Teresa Tomas. The badest tomboy on campus with a figure most girls envied her over. The young Tomas had her share of fling and swings one of which she is sure she won’t forget. The time is the present. Teresa sits alone in a coffee shop. She glances at her watch and continues to watch the door. A tall woman enters followed by two cops and an old man. A week before, she had received a phone call from an old friend that she knew in high school. His name was William. Together, they watched each other’s backs as well as had their own slug outs. He was the average Joe in their group of friends while Teresa was just one of the guys so to speak. On graduation day, William announced to the group that he was enlisting in the Navy. He would return in a few years as a Navy Seal. That night during the graduation party, William and Teresa shared more than memories together. As their bodies met and heated emotions rose, they created a memory that would be etched in their thoughts for years to come. No one to this day knows what happened that night except Teresa and William. The next day he said his farewells and with his belongings thrown in the bed of his Dodge pick up, William left to serve his country. He never returned. William had sailed the seas, done his duty as a Navy Seal and met a woman across seas who he later married and had a baby girl named Kristie. One hellish night, their home fire consuming both his wife and his 2 yr. old child. Depression consumed William for years and finally he has learned to cope with reality and move on with his life. Today Teresa will meet her old friend for the first time in almost 10 years. The woman who had entered a few minutes ago suddenly sits in an empty chair in front of Teresa. Startled, Teresa jumps slightly and gives the woman a strange look. The woman has a dark complexion and shoulder length brown hair which is straight and slightly curled at the ends. She wears stylish jeans and a dark green long sleeved shirt. Her make up is almost too obvious and when she spoke, her voice was surprisingly deeper than most women were. “Hello Teresa. It seems time has changed the both of us.” This only adds more confusion to Teresa. She has never seen this woman in her life and out of the blue, she just sits down and starts talking. The redneck doesn’t speak. The questions seem to be seen in her eyes. The woman seems to understand and tries to give a reassuring smile. A waitress approach and the woman orders a black coffee. Teresa continues to stare at the strange woman not really knowing if she should say anything at all. The woman continues. She appears to be a bit nervous. “My name is Whitney. Don’t you recognize me?” Teresa raises an eyebrow and adjusts in her chair. “I’m sorry, but I have no clue who you are.” Whitney smiles and licks her lips trying not to laugh. Her coffee is set in front of her and she picks it up with both hands. “It’s me Tomas. William. Remember our phone call last night? I told you I’ve made some changes in my life and I thought it’d be best to show you instead of tell you.” Naturally William didn’t expect Teresa to be fully comfortable in this situation. Watching her reaction, he can see the color leave her face as she simply sat there motionless for a moment. Then finally she spoke in a low voice. “Well this is definitely a surprise. Do you dress like this all the time?” “Yes, the operation and the works. I had my name changed to Whitney after the recovery.” Whitney watches as the expected mixed emotions speak out across Teresa’s face. He has no time to brace himself when Teresa finally explodes with clenched fists slamming against the table. This of course causes everyone’s attention. “WHY IN THE HELL DID YOU COME BACK? You and me back in the day, we were tight. When you called, I expected us to ya know, catch up on our lives. Hell, I was even planning on taking you to Shanghaied with me. Now, I don’t even know who I’m talking to.” Teresa stands to leave but Whitney grabs her arm tightly to stop her. “Teresa, it’s still the same me. I just look different. What we had back then, what we shared, I still haven’t forgotten. I knew you wouldn’t be comfortable seeing me again, but the truth is, I ran into the Personnel Director of your company and she told me how close friends you were. After some talk, she convinced me to give you a call. She said it would be good to ease your mind from your title match Sunday. I never knew you became a wrestler.” Teresa looks to the woman that used to be one of her best guy friends. After a couple minutes of silence, Teresa’s face softens. She combs her fingers through her sandy blonde hair and takes in a deep breath. “I’m sorry. This isn’t your fault. That Personnel Director will get what’s coming to her. Mark my word.”
