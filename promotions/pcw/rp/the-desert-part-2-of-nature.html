The Desert (Part 2 of 'Nature')

2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/9/03 Dear Journal, The sweltering sun above a desert says more in an image then any words can. Birds circle above their pray in order to make sure of it’s death and the cactuses stand proudly through the sand. The sand, mountains stacked upon mountains of itself and the wind which blows that sand makes the area be believably in some place like Libya or Egypt, but it wasn’t stated which, or if it was either of those. Let’s take the desert into thought: You see it as a hot, painful experience to go through. The sun beats down upon your face, and yes I did rip that off 'Kashmir', and you become so thirsty your throat is dry and your eyes are watering with pain. It doesn’t sound exactly like the best honeymoon place, but a good honeymoon place wouldn’t be here if a bad honeymoon place wasn’t. The desert is hot, and not very pleasant, but thing of it this way: Without the pain, you wouldn’t have pleasure. I recall seeing a Beavis and Butthead episode where Butthead said that, but it was more unintelligent, like 'Things wouldn’t rock if things didn’t suck.' It’s true, and quite simple. Pinch yourself, then wait. Don’t you feel good afterwards? Doesn’t the pain fade from your mind and your grateful to be 'normal' again? Even though we think everything like a pessimist, just be in total pain and you’ll really like to be in normal health. Once you are in normal health, you think nothing of it. I read this in a Stephen King book, I can’t recall which: 'When you have a grain of sand in your eye the only thing you think of is the sand. Once it washes away and falls out, you don’t call where the sand dropped and only feel good that your eye doesn’t hurt.' I don’t think that’s EXACTLY what was said but it was close enough. We kick back in our Lazy Boys, drink cokes, eat chips, and our complaining your TV isn’t getting a good reception of 'Joe Millionaire'. People in Africa are starving to death. Ribs jutting out, not fat on their bodies, nothing to be happy of. Yet, when they get an iota of food they go nuts and are very grateful of it. We argue about how the mash potatoes aren’t warm enough. Be grateful you have what you have, and live a life with more luxury then others. Respect those who don’t have what you do and be generous. I admit I try my best to be nice but sometimes I am ungrateful for what I have. Be grateful. Eddy shut his journal over the knocking at his door. He stood up and walked over to door and opened it. The man was tall in front of his. Dark black hair, round glasses, a thick bushy mustache with a blue polo shirt and black jeans. Eddy didn’t recognize him at first, and still didn’t. 'Hello.' 'Hi. Do I know you?' 'Yes.' 'How?' 'I’m Max Greenwald, and you haven’t paid your full amount for this house.' 'What? Yes I did.' 'No, you didn’t. You have until this Tuesday to come up with the money or you have to evicted and move all your stuff out of the house by 6:00 PM.' 'That’s crazy!', screamed Eddy. 'I paid full amount a whole year ago. Tell me other wise.' 'You were off by $3,000.' 'Show me.' Max sighed and pulled out some paper. Eddy did notice and remember he was off by $3,000 dollars and said he’d pay back later. Eddy froze. 'You’re right…But sir, I don’t get my next paycheck from PCW until 10:000 PM Tuesday. I’ll be gone all of Tuesday anyway because I have a match.' 'Not my problem', said Max, turning. 'I hope to see the money or you out of here.' 'But…' Max walked away and into his Cadillac, as Eddy saw him drive off. '3,000 dollars…How do I come up with that by Tuesday?' Eddy shut the door and sat down on his blue couch, thinking about it, in confusion on what to do. Then Eddy remembered that, in his luxury, he was grateful to be in the spot he was. Not starving over in Africa, and so he never thought bad about the situation again.
