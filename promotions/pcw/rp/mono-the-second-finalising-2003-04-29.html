Mono:- The Second:- Finalising

29 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 29/04/03 The room was cold and damp, the window had been blown open and the gusting winds were picking up, dragging with them the smallest particles of rain, which soaked lightly into leather furniture and purple decorations of the small room. The room itself was not overly small, but in comparison to the rest of the house it was. It was generally used for housing things she couldn’t find room for, that or when she didn’t want to be found. Lacuna was settled in a dark corner, her raven black hair was settled over her face as she stared deeply into the ground. What she was about to do went against every single molecule of her being. It placed all of her morals into dispute, plunging her reputation to the ground. She was sure she knew where Jade was, and if she needed to delve deeply into the soft underbelly of the cities crime circuit, then she would do so. Things were not the same without Jade around, she had taught Lacuna a lot, and just letting it all go again would bring back all the pain and suffering that being alone all those years had created. She didn’t want that; she couldn’t take it again, so she devised a plan and now, in the small room where nobody ever checked, she would finalise the details. She was going to do this the hard way, because there wasn’t another option. She flicked back her hair and pulled out a small blueprint map from her back pocket, placing it on the ground neatly. Her thin fingered hands roved across the marked pathways and air ducts until they stopped on a large room around the second floor. She banged her fist angrily into the marked room and screwed up the map, forcing it back into her pocket roughly. “I better be right, I better be bloody well right.” she muttered to herself quietly, her elegant head shaking slightly from side to side with each word. There was no room for error with this plan, she had no escape route or second action, it all had to go well or she would be left with nothing, nothing at all. Swiftly, she drew a bag from her shoulder and opened it, pulling out ‘Steve’, her fabled shotgun. “This is it, Steve, your debut. Do me proud, please God do me proud.” and she loaded Steve for the first ever time. Steve had seen plenty of action in the past, she had used it to put Haley Hilton in hospital and to beat up numerous cronies and enemies, yet she had never loaded it, for a weapon so bloodstained and rusted you would not have expected this, but it was fully true. Steve had never been loaded although Lacuna was well capable of doing so. She didn’t want to fire the weapon, if she intended to actually do so then she would have used something much more subtle, but she wanted some weight for her words, as well as to crack over the back of someone’s skull if needs be. If things went badly then at least she had an option, well, if she could get away with it. She had been taught to shoot as a child by her father, yet another memory she was angrily trying to cram back into her head at the moment, because if she became emotional then she knew more than any body that she would probably just sit and think it over, getting more and more wound up as the day continues. She needed to get this done. She stood up, brushing back her hair and smiling wryly. She had never done anything illegal alone before. Maybe the Jade effect was not as good as it seemed at the time, but it had definitely brought about a change in the way she was acting, and it was a much more enjoyable side for Lacuna. Sometimes she sat back and thought about the rule abiding, class-orientated old Lacuna and then she thought about her first true friend. Bad influence or not, Jade had clearly changed Lacuna, and Lacuna liked it too much to let it be forgotten. Crossing the room clumsily, she eventually managed to shut the window. The rain was getting heavier now, and the patterns formed by the droplets hitting the window enticed her to stare, but she resisted. Jade needed saving, and she had convinced herself of it. She picked up the bag and slammed the door closed loudly. It was time. Brook was still watching as she left the front door, draped in a black leather coat that had once belonged to Jade, and climbed into the driving seat of the silver Mercedes convertible that had been brought for her by Jade, even if Lacuna’s money was used. The black bag was clearly visible over her shoulder and Brook winced, for he knew that inside it was Steve the shotgun. That was the only thing she had ever kept in the bag, and now he knew something was up. “She’s going to get herself bloody killed, I have to follow her, what would her parents think…” Brook closed the rain-splattered window and grabbed a long brown coat from a peg behind him. In it were the keys to his Land Rover, brought by Lacuna for his fortieth birthday. He closed the cloakroom door and headed out into the main hall with its immaculate marble flooring and beautiful works of art lining the neat walls. This was what she was leaving behind for that lower class fool, the common criminal. She was ruining her reputation and maybe her career. Maybe even her life. Slowly she rolled up the hood of the convertible Mercedes against the rain and started the engine of the car, which rattled excitedly, as if it knew something big was going down. Or was it? She could be wrong; she might not even be there. If she wasn’t then the consequences didn’t even bear thinking about. She was going in alone, against an army of Jade’s worst enemies and bodyguards in a building she scarcely knew. It was like a suicide mission, and the odds were even worse than that. The car jolted forwards, spraying wet gravel up from behind it as the rear tires skidded slightly in the rain. It was the time now, and she slid on the purple lenses of her driving sunglasses and put her foot down, rapidly accelerating through the gears as she began to reach the end of the driveway. The huge black gates were opening already, and were fully open by the time she had forced her way out onto the country roads. She had no time for music; she barely had time to think, as she was so deep in helplessness now that all she could do was go with the flow. Her body had taken over, she couldn’t even grin let alone change the course of her thoughts. It was like an autopilot embedded deeply into her mind, she had no control, she just had to let fate take her winding course and make something good out of it. This was something Jade would do, not her. And she couldn’t make head or tale of it. The Land Rover gripped the tarmac well, and Brook was staying at a steady distance behind the speeding Mercedes. The rain-splattered windshield gave a somewhat distorted view of the car ahead, but he was sure what of what he needed to do. “Keep close, Brook, but not too close…”he muttered to himself over the roaring sound of the engine as he raced across the twisting country road. They were approaching the freeway now, and he would be sure that if he were seen then, then she wouldn’t be able to stop at all. A thought struck him. “She will stop if she sees me, surely. I must catch up!” The roaring of the engine picked up again as he pressed down heavily on the accelerator. If he didn’t catch her soon then he wouldn’t be able to stop her at all, and then she may end up in trouble, maybe even dead. The speeding Land Rover was not difficult to see in a rear view mirror, neither was the red-faced figure of Brook driving it, and Lacuna cursed herself for her stupidity. Of course he would have seen a problem, now she had to have him involved. She pressed down harder on the accelerator and moved away from the tailing vehicle. “He couldn’t keep his bloody nose out, stupid fool. I cant let him get involved, not now. It’s too late” And the gap between the two grew, the speed catching the eye of many casual travellers as they raced down the small country road and pulled out onto the bustling freeway. She had lost Brook, and now only one thing stood in the way of her quest to save Jade. The problem is, she doesn’t even know what that thing was. The sky grew darker, the lights flared orange, but this had to be done. Sleep could wait, Jade had to be saved.
