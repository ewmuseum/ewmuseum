Mind's Eye: New Outlook

24 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/24/03 It was hot that day. It was always hot. At least once the sun got high enough in the sky. The nights in Baghdad were mild enough, but once the sun rose and it got to 90 degrees on a good day within the tight city, an outsider had to wonder when Hell decided to expand its real estate ventures. The troops had mostly left the city, save for some skeleton crews. Guarding the oil wells was a more important objective to the American occupation force. There were those troops who remained in the city to keep the looters and armed gangs from taking over the city, but they were only allowed to use their guns in self-defense. Many places in the city were still in ruin. The targeted attacks on strategic Iraqi military installations did their work, stone and metal alike strewn around the street. These were the scenes that weren’t shown on the news. The Iraqis are all put to work by the American occupational force while the military deals with the removal of all Bath party members. Meanwhile, the clean-up effort from the war isn’t anywhere near started, let alone showing results. Despite the arms market in the center of the city, despite the civilian, Kurdish, and US patrols lurking everywhere, despite the incredible heat (which wasn’t helping much given his apparel), Skylar still walked through the city; the deserted part of the city. Here there were the remnants of the first Gulf War. The burnt out husks of buildings showed that there hadn’t been a clean-up for a while. Skylar knew about this place, how no one stayed around there with all the bad memories; how Saddam made people move into the troops’ quarters of his military installations so that when the US bombed them, United States forces could be called on killing civilians. Skylar meant no disrespect to the memory of the innocents who lost their lives here. He just needed a place to walk out in the open and think. {Here I am. Triple Threat match. The PCW World Title on the line.} Skylar half envisioned the layout of the debris in his mind’s eye. He had grown accustomed over time to sensing obstacles in his path when there weren’t thousands of screaming people to distract him, or an opponent trying to pummel him. He could clearly “see” where there were hunks of brick that he was to step around, over, or under as he made his way through the area. Many buildings lay crumbled to the ground. The “voices” of the dead ring out in this place. The sun hits it in an eerie way, such that everything has a shadow cast upon it, even with the bright, hot sun already high in the sky given the early hour. {My best chance yet. It was something I wanted to do since I had you by my side. With you I thought I could do anything.} The rest of Skylar’s attention was devoted to pure imagination. He pictured himself holding the PCW World Title over his head, won cleanly in a hard-fought battle with the top two villains in PCW, Totally Tubular Tommy and The Pharaoh. The air was still dry, yet thick and hot. Skylar was clad casually in a red T-shirt that was already sweaty across the chest, and some black denim jeans by RocaWear. What made the sun bad, though, was the fact that he still wore his white coat. He had received it as a gift from Sandy Wong after performing so well in PCW Alliances, making it to the last three men involved. Circumstances were such that his making it as far as he did was quite impressive, and Skylar was the simple type. He’d graciously accept a simple token as a present, unlike some others who require Cadillac Escalades or houses as gifts. It was a simple white jacket, given to him by Sandy Wong. She gave it to him, and thus it was perfect. {I’m in the main event. It’s not a cluster, just myself and two other men. These are the best odds I’ve had for winning that title. If I’m ever going to win the World Title, now is that time.} A couple of weeks ago, he had it altered, made into a white trenchcoat. It was weighted clothing, 60 pounds to be exact, mostly around the shoulders and upper back. He never wore it during weigh-ins, so he is still counted an official weight of around 280 pounds. But he wore the coat during training, and walking around. It helps him for muscle strength and training, especially in the legs and back. The sleeves could be weighted too, with little effort, if he wanted to train harder for arm strength and speed with his punches. It was the first time he had changed anything that Sandy had given him since she passed away. {But I still feel so empty.} Skylar finds a cool spot underneath a fallen brick wall angled at 70 degrees, about 5 feet off the ground at its highest point. He bends down and sits in the shadow of the wall, bringing his knees up to his chest and wrapping his arms around them. He’d finally gotten a chance to be by himself. The hotel in Baghdad (which wasn’t as great as they made it out to be) was so low on rooms that Skylar had to share a room with Sivasian Krikorian. That Ukrainian was not a quiet sleeper. He snored, coughed, tossed the whole night; a few times he talked in his sleep. From the previous night to much of his waking hours this morning, Skylar had been in press conferences, photo shoots, autograph signings (which didn’t actually take that long because Skylar just uses his initials)… never a free moment. Plenty of dull ones, though. {I almost don’t want this win now.} He just sat there in the shade, by himself. “Why not?” Skylar turned his head to his right, as Sandy Wong sat down next to him. She wore the same tan business jacket/skirt combination that she had on that night when Violence Jack beat her to death. She looked a lot better, though. Indeed, she is as exactly as Skylar remembered her. She was pictured in his mind’s eye, after all. “You wanted this, and now is your own moment to shine. Not you and many other people. Just you.” Skylar shook his head. {This was supposed to be something that we would experience and celebrate together.} Sandy smiled at Skylar, her surprisingly round (for an Asian woman) eyes narrowing by the movement of the muscles in her face. “I’m still here. In spirit. Don’t let that hold you back.” Skylar shrugged his shoulders, burying his chin into his forearms. A desert breeze kicked up and the air flow meanders through the alleys of the city. The bangs on Sandy’s short black hair whisked in front of her eyes. {It just doesn’t feel the same without you being physically here. If you were, I’d be resting up and not sitting in a blown up building, talking with a figment of my imagination.} Sandy put her arms around Skylar, hugging him firmly over the arms. Skylar felt her body against his, the comfortable warmth despite the heat. “It’s not your imagination, Skylar. It’s more than that.” Skylar sat up straight and reached his hands up to her face, gently touching her cheeks. Sandy reacted with a closing of her eyes, exhaling and placing her hands over his wrists. {I never could imagine a tactile sensation… are you real?} Sandy turned away, facing off to the side, hesitant to respond for a moment. Skylar tilted his head to the right, wondering what’s going on. But a moment passed before she looked back at Skylar and smiled again. “I can’t tell you what’s going on at the moment, but just know that I’m watching over you and wishing you well. Don’t let anything hold you back. I want you to go in there with the intention to win. If you do or don’t, that won’t matter to me as long as you try your best. That’s all I want.” Skylar nodded his head at Sandy’s request. “And do me a favor?” {Anything.} Sandy looked up to the sky. Skylar followed suit, envisioning a clear blue sky above them. “The girls are going to contact you, soon. Talk to them. Try to get them back.” Skylar looked back at Sandy. {But, they already lost a home. Three times. I don’t want to risk them going through that again if someone else decides to attack the family I attempt to build. I lost you; I lost them. I don’t know if I’m ready to start again.} Sandy shook her head, taking Skylar’s left hand into both of hers. “They want to try again. They want a home. A family life. And I want you to give them one.” Skylar withdrew his hand, turning slightly away from Sandy. {I don’t think I can. I’m not an expert on these things.} Sandy gently turned Skylar’s face back to hers with her right hand. “Neither are they, but they’re willing to try again. Couldn’t you? I know you’ve lost more than one family in your lifetime, but you can’t stop trying. If you stop trying, you lose hope. And when you lose hope, what do you have to live for?” Sandy’s eyes went to the ground in front of Skylar, the gap of about three inches between the two. “The girls have lost their families three times, but they haven’t given up hope. They’re just children, and they’re naïve, but they still have hope. They have to if they want to survive. You have to if you want to survive.” Tears started to form in Skylar’s eyes. {But I don’t want to do it without you. You’re the only one I ever wanted to do such a thing with. Now that you’re gone… I can’t do it without you.} Sandy put her arms around Skylar’s neck and gave him a hug and a light kiss on his cheek. “You have to, Sky. Life goes on. So must you. Win the PCW World Title, get another place to live, and try again with the girls. I promise, you’ll feel better. You can if you want to. You just have to trust me. You always did before. Please trust me now.” Closing his eyes, Skylar nodded to Sandy. The tears streamed down his face and fall to the ground. They hit the rock upon which Skylar sits but they didn’t splatter and splash like water does when it impacts the ground. His tears pooled up in the exact spot that they land and stayed for a few seconds, before the dry heat in the air evaporates the small amounts of water. “All things change. If you allow yourself to be happy, the tears of the past won’t even hurt. It’ll just be a passing thought. Don’t focus on the past, Sky. Look to the future.” Releasing Skylar, Sandy stood up. “I’m sorry, but I have to go now. Remember what I said.” Skylar opened his blank eyes and looked up at Sandy. Sandy, however, is no longer there. Wiping his face dry, Skylar came out from under his quiet, dark rock of sad memories and stepped into the bright sunlight. It was so bright and hot that Skylar had shield his head with his hands. The entire shadow seemed to lift from the deserted section of the city. {I won’t forget. I’ll never forget.} Breathing in deeply, Skylar changed his direction. He doesn’t walked south-southeast, the direction of everything he lost and left behind; he walked north in the direction of the National Stadium. He walked north toward his match and his opponents. He walked north, into the future. Into his destiny. Into a new day and a new outlook. The Supremacy main event was his to win. And he wanted it now, more than ever.
