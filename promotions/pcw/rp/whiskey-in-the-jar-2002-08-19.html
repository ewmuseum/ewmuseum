Whiskey in the jar...

19 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 19th of August, 2002 The sound of crickets chirping merrily in the grassy fields further south was the only noise that he could hear at present. As he walked on the gravel path towards the bar, a fairly local looking establishment, it crunched under his weight sending out a deep echo throughout the valley. As he came to the bar’s door he hesitated and then reframed, bucking up his courage, and then pushed the entrance inwards. The sweltering heat was nothing compared to the sunburnt air outside, which was unusual since it was fairly late at night. To his knowledge it was highly expected that the people inside would shun him as a sort of automatic reaction but never had he prepared for the stern glares of the Montana locals. He clearly felt uneasy, even with his ceremonial helm upon his skull… He walked towards the bar tender. The man was in mid-polish of a large pint glass when he finally realized someone was standing behind the counter, waiting to be served. With a generous smile, considering that this man was both unusual looking and not a local whatsoever, he approached him with caution. “What can I get ya’, stranger?” he said, an untrusting gleam in his eye. The man looked on behind the bar tender at the many bottles of liquor he had to offer. He wasn’t actually in the mood for anything like that at the moment, however, and simply asked: “Pint of Stella, friend.” The man wandered towards the backroom where he picked out a well-polished pint glass and brought it back to the counter. “New to these parts, huh?” he said, pulling down on the lever just below his chest. The sound of the alcohol flowing into its new home filled the stranger’s ears, it seemed to surpass even the sound of snooker balls clattering together and remarkably also the sound of the countless conversations going on within the pub. “Kind of. I’m not staying for long, though. To be honest I just came here to get away from the city life,” he sighed. “I needed to just get away for a while.” “I see,” the pint came to its head and was pushed under Pharaoh’s nose. “$3, if you’d be so kind, sir.” Reaching into his loincloth he produced the required 3 dollars and handed them over, his gaze catching the pool table in the second room of the bar. “It’s just good to get away sometimes, you know? From the wife, and from the work, and from… enemies,” he trailed and the bar tender noticed. “You in a bit of trouble, or somethin’?” Titu shook his head, smiling. “Nah, I guess it just comes with the job.” “Oh,” the man said, eyes following Xansiv’s gaze towards the pool table. “Play pool, do ya’?” He shook off the comment with a wave of his hand. “No, not really. I’ve played before, but…I’m not that great to be frank.” The bar tender swiftly resumed his polishing of the glass he’d placed down on the counter. “Feel free to have a game, mind you.” He said absent-mindedly. Pharaoh didn’t really listen to what the man said. He took a sip of the pint and savoured the taste for a moment…it quickly past. Taking another mouthful he put the glass down on the counter, making sure he situated it on top of a mat and walked towards a stool by the bar’s door. “It’s nice weather up in these parts… very nice.” He said, pulling the chair towards the counter. He was never that great at making conversation but at least he could give out some decent insults when needed, unlike a certain Duke Williams. He sat down as the bar tender eyed him up in awe. This guy had just turned up out of the blue, just like that. He wasn’t your regular ‘city guy’ either, that was plain to see from his clothing. He was different, but he couldn’t quite put his finger on the reason why. He seemed to radiate status. He looked like an important guy, but one who was modest about his stature in the metaphorical hierarchy of life. The barman nodded and slipped his watchful eyes away from the man and back to the pint glass in his hand. “Yeah. It’s nice up here in the summer, but in the winter it gets real cold. Terrible weather in the winder, really; but nice around this time of the year.” He said at length. There was a pause. One of the ones that occurred when there was nothing left to say. The long dreaded ones that nobody liked… the deeply hated anxious silence. “So,” the bar tender ventured at last. “Where ya’ from? Don’t mind me asking but your clothes--…” “Yeah, I get that a lot, friend. I was born in Egypt and I’ve taken on many beliefs over the past years…I dress like this because, well,” he paused. “Well, it’s what my ancestors would have worn, so I’m honouring them by dressing in this.” The barman stared as though in a deeply methodical trance. The stranger really WASN’T your regular city guy. The man sat in front of him was unique. In all the years he’d owned this bar he’d never come across a person like this - like nothing the Montana locals had ever witnessed. Smart, well spoken… and… Egyptian? Something wasn’t right with that equation, he considered. But this guy seemed like a really down-to-earth type of person, yet look at what he was wearing - the loincloth, the golden grown, and the jewellery. Was this guy all together…up stairs? He wondered. Pharaoh noticed the man’s stare and coughed politely. The barman snapped out of his thoughts immediately and shook his head. “Sorry about that,” he coughed. “Egypt, eh? Nice place, I’ve heard.” Pharaoh took a gulp of the Stella. “Used to be better.” He said. “Well I’ve never been, so I can’t really comment.” The pint was dwindling away now. In fact only half a pint was left. In a way it was like the time he had until Tuesday Turmoil arrived. In half a day he’d be facing off against the likes of Akiyama, Chris Chase and Duke Williams. It would mark the end of his three match-winning streak, and in actual fact, he didn’t wish it to end at all. But in situations like this one you had to look at it from the realistic point of view. Come this Tuesday the streak would be over. There was no other way of thinking about it. Frisco would get involved in the bout and cost him the win. The Argentinean dictator would do just about anything to get in his mind, including costing him his matches. To the Hall of Fame member it was all a mind game, a sick, sadistic mind game… He raised the glass back up to his mouth and downed the remaining fluid; slowly the froth at the bottom dissolved into a thin white liquid and then there was nothing left… “I’ll have anoth--…” He stopped himself in mid-sentence. “Actually I’ll have something a little stronger. Give me a shot of whiskey.” The bartender edged towards the back of the counter and produced a large bottle of unnamed brown liquid. He poured out a tiny shot glass and handed it to Pharaoh with a smile. “I’ll pay for them all together a bit later on, if it’s Ok?” The man behind the bar nodded, “Yeah, sure.” Pharaoh took it back and it wasn’t long before the heat built up inside his body, his throat burned like a volcano and his tongue begged to differ that the throat got the worst of it. “I didn’t catch your name, by the way.” Pharaoh stuttered as the full effect of the whiskey kicked in. “It’s Harvey…Harvey Nash.” Pharaoh extended his hand in the gesture of a handshake. Nash forwarded his and shook Titu’s without a second thought. “Titu Xansiv,” Pharaoh said. “Pleased to make your acquaintance.” Harvey smiled. Across the room the snooker balls clattered again and the sound of one being pocketed rung out as the Nash once again began to clean the already gleaming pint glass. Titu knew that he hardly had a chance, but he wasn’t one to give up without a fight. Turmoil may mark the end of his streak but it wouldn’t mark the end of his progress to the top of PCW. He looked up at Harvey and asked for another shot of whiskey. Drinking it he wondered why he’d ever come here. It wasn’t that he didn’t like the bar - saying that would be lying! But he couldn’t quite remember… He appeared to get on with the owner, Harvey Nash, and the locals hadn’t thrown anything at him yet… (A fairly good sign in its own right that the Montana natives already residing in the bar weren’t shunning him as much anymore.) All in all, though, he just couldn’t quite place his finger on the reason why he’d come to the bar on that night – that warm summer night… It was some time before Titu Xansiv finally finished his drinking session in the Montana pub. After that he wandered back to his hotel room, awaiting Tuesday to come. Awaiting the time when he had to step up to the challenge and show the world what he was made of. Deep down he knew he wouldn’t win. Deep down he knew his time had come. But deep down he knew he could face up to the others in the match and give them a good run for their money. He’d already beaten Akiyama… and Duke Williams, he realized, was nothing in comparison to him. He may have been a veteran, but he wasn’t what the new age of PCW would call a ‘prospect’. Chris Chase was another matter. A matter he’d take care of later. But for now all he wished to do was relax and think nothing more of the match. Even if it was the one he feared most deep inside.
