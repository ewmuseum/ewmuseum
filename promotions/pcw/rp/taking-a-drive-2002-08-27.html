Taking a drive

27 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8-27-02 RP2T Nothing is more unoriginal than the typical Canada versus United States in today’s wrestling entertainment. No doubt patriotism always tends to cause adrenaline to soar through the audience’s veins. A sense of pride for one’s country can be a strong thing. A new fed brings Teresa a sense of new beginnings for her career. Nothing pleases her more than to hear the roar of the thousands of ticket buying fans echoing through the arena and into her mind. Especially making a flip off the top rope before landing on her opponent. A lot has happened to her career wise these past few months, but she will not dwell on that now, not anymore. That chapter in her life has come to a close and now a new one has begun and is being written as we speak. Somewhere in Michigan is where Teresa is at now. Her and Jack have taken a plane leaving all their vehicles in Tennessee. They have taken a cab to an auto renal company and are sharing a new model Chevy Silverado pick up. The truck is silver with nothing extra about it. To Teresa, it looks as if it came straight from a car lot, which it probably has. The wheels surprisingly are made from Goodyear with hardly any wear to them whatsoever. The two friends are taking a site seeing trip through Detroit. The city has a cool breeze and the sky is clear with every star shinning brightly. Playing low is the old melodies of a Classic Rock station, a type of music both Teresa and Jack can agree on. Classic Rock is all he will listen to while Teresa finds a likeness to nearly all types of music. “Don’t you ever get tired of listening to the same songs all the time?” Teresa asks Jack while looking out the window of the passer side. The big city is no stranger to her. In her career, she has seen many cities. As they pass a strip full of clubs and sport bars, she is reminded of Scott and how they used to jump from one joint to the other just to see how each differed from the other. A light sigh escapes her lungs and she turns her head to look out the windshield ahead. Suddenly it hits her how she really does miss him. In the meantime, Jack is doing a bit of sight seeing him self. He is in the driver seat finding the seat non-too comfortable. His right wrist rests loosely over the top of the steering wheel while holding a Camel cigarette in between the fingers of his left hand. He turns his head just in time to see her sigh. “What are you thinking about Teresa?” “Nothing you’d be interested in. Let’s skip the clubs tonight and just get a bite to eat at McDonald’s” “If you’re nervous about your new fed, don’t be. I’ve been watching you since you went out on your own. Don’t give up ok? The Teresa Jane Tomas I know don’t give up.” Teresa gives Jack a surprised look, then smiles as she sits back in her seat. Jack and Scott haven’t been on good terms since Jack took off to Texas a few years ago. When he came back, he expected Teresa to greet him with open arms but instead she had moved on with her life in Scott Taylor’s arms. Enough said. “Thanks for the encouragement Jack. Let’s find a liquor store after we get some food and head back to the hotel. I don’t feel like much of a party animal tonight.” Jack makes a turn into the McDonald’s parking lot and slows down in the drive through line. About three cars are in front of them. He turns his head toward Teresa and arches an eyebrow at her recent request. Food, hard drink and hotels lead to other hard things and Jack is simply the average man thinking average manly things. Teresa doesn’t even have to look at him to know what he’s thinking. She has known him long enough; she can almost read his mind. “Don’t even go there Jack Slayter. Once I start getting sleepy tonight, you will go to your own half of the hotel room. If I wake up and find you in my bed, I will beat the living hell out of you and send you on a one way ticket back to Texas. Is that understood?” Now Teresa looks to Jack giving him a cold stern look. She may be woman and she may be a bit vulnerable at this moment in time, but she doesn’t take shit from anyone lightly. Jack nods before pulling the truck to the menu then order two burgers, fries and Cokes. Soon the two are back on the road searching for a liquor store. The stereo is still playing the same Classic Rock as it has been all night. The time is around 9:30pm. Over the radio an old song from the 1970’s called American Woman by Guess Who/Bauchman Turner Overdrive filled the Silverado’s cab. Teresa glances to the bag of burgers and fries sitting between her and Jack, then the two Coca-Cola’s resting in the cup holders by the gearshift. Suddenly something hits her: not literally, but in her mind. What’s more American than hamburgers and Coca-Cola, and McDonald’s? “Hey Jack?” “Yes Babe?” “Don’t call me Babe.” “Alright Darlin’ what is it?” Teresa sighs We’re going to Canada for Turmoil right? “Yep.” “With all this American Pride floating about. Let’s say we bring some over the border? It’s the American thing to do right? Everyone seems to do it every time their fed jumps the boarder. “Ok, so what would make you any different from everyone else?” “ Well, of all the ‘American Badasses’ running around showing out, is there one female amongst them?” “Nope.” “And there’s no pride like Tennessee pride. Am I right?” “Well..” “You know I’m right. Come college football season, you’ll see more Tennessee Orange than any other SEC teams.” “True. Your point is?” “Jack Hun, you are looking at the Ultimate American Woman” Teresa grins. Somewhat proud of her new idea. Jack parks the truck in their hotel parking area. While she was going off at the mouth, he had made it through a drive through liquor store and purchased a sack full of intoxicating goodies. “ Woman, you’ve lost it.” “Yeah, that’s what I’ve been told.”
