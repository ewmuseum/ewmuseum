Behind the Ego of Jason Snow: Family and Relationships, Chapter 4 - Absence of Comfort

Date: not entered 'Wow,' Krista says softly, as she looks around the small room. She finds herself surrounded by Snow's the physical presentation of Snow's wrestling accomplishments. Gold and Bronze plaques line the walls, trophies are neatly set along the floor - in bunches in the corners, and there are three replica PCW Heavyweight Championship Belts are hung on the wall opposite the door. 'This is all yours?' she asks, turning to Snow, who lightly touches a framed certificate on the wall that reads 'SIW North American Champion - July 29, 2000'. Snow nods and then points to a bare spot on the wall next to his three replica PCW Championships, 'I'm hoping to add one there soon.' Krista knows that he's talking about something to represent a Wrestling God 3 championship. She also knows, that he's now using wrestling and his success in an attempt to ignore his real life. ...A real life that saw his daughter removed from his here in his New Brunswick home this morning before he returned from Los Angeles... 'What's this?' his young sister-in-law asks, kneeling down beside an old cedar chest in one corner of the small room. 'Just some old junk,' Snow shrugs, 'Family things, I guess - your sister insisted I keep them.' Krista rolls her eyes - she knows Snow would never hang on to family keep-sakes without prompting from her sister. Slowly, Krista opens the old creeking chest and finds a wealth of Snow's family history. Photos from the late eighteen hundreds of fishermen posing beside their boats, newspaper articles about current events in the tiny village of Backwater, and then... 'A gun!?!' Krista exclaims. Snow's eyes narrow, 'Forgot that was in there.' He kneels down beside the younger girl and takes the antique hand pistol from her. 'I don't like having guns in the house,' Krista says quietly. Snow knows what she's referring to. Only a few days ago, she saw her brother-in-law snap in a way she never thought he would. She never dreamed that he would one day turn on her. Every time she thinks of it, she feels a sharp pain in her scalp - remembering how monstrous he looked when he grabbed a fistful of her hair and tossed her against the wall. She wishes she could forget that day. She's long since forgiven him, but forgetting the incident is impossible. Snow wishes it never happened. He wishes none of this happened. Secretly, he hopes that he will wake up in his Los Angeles hotel bed and realize that it has all been a horrible nightmare. He wishes that he hadn't perminantly damaged his relationship with Krista. He wishes that his stepmother-in-law hadn't hidden his wife away, filling her head with what he believes are obvious lies. And above all else, he wishes that he could wake up, still expecting to be the father of a baby boy. Snow needs to change the subject, 'Have you settled in?' he asks as he puts the gun back into the chest and closes it. 'Pretty much,' Krista sighs, 'I haven't gotten all of my stuff yet, though.' Her eyes lower to the floor - she understands why Snow insisted that she live at his home over the summer, instead of with her parents, but she can't help but feel embarrassed about the situation. Since marrying her sister, Snow has been more of a parent to Krista than either of her biological pair. Still, there is always a distance between Snow and those he cares about - both physically and emotionally. 'We'll buy you more stuff,' says Snow, rationing the girl a small, gentle smile. Krista knows that she can always count on Snow financially. Krista, much like Snow's wife, wishes that Snow's emotions weren't so green. He sees no difference between affection and money. Krista would love to make him see that difference - 'Would you like to go shopping with me?' she asks, her eyes lighting up. Snow doesn't understand why should would want his presence. He's no good in a mall - he can't tell the difference between a Gucci bag and a garbage bag. What help could he possibly be to her? Snow may not understand the reasons, but he does realize that she would like him to go with her, 'Sure,' he says warmly, 'I'll bring the car around.' 'Oh!' Krista says, her eyes wide with shock, 'I can't go today...' her voice trails off. Snow already knows her reasons, but she feels obligated to explain, 'I haven't seen Billy since... well... you know.' He does know. The last time she saw her boyfriend, Snow was violently tossing him out of the house - a reaction after catching the pair of young lovers in bed together. 'Oh...' Snow says quietly, unsure of what he should add. He doesn't want Krista going with Billy, but he realizes he can't stop her. Just because she's living in his home now doesn't officially make him her father, despite the fact that he's been the closest thng to a father she's had in years. But she's twenty years old - there's nothing he can do, no matter who she wants to date. And who knows? Maybe Billy's not such a bad person after all... then again, first impressions last a lifetime. ... ... ... His teeth clench as his young sister-in-law climbs onto the back of a Chevy pick-up truck, lined with young adults - only a few years younger than Snow. But he missed out on that part of life. He can smell the alcohol that they're hiding from his sight. Jason Snow knows all too well about alcohol, though he never enjoyed it in the same sense that they do. By the time he was their age - late teens, early twenties - alcohol had already become a demon. Now, at the age of twenty-four, he's defeated those demons with the help of a prison term and a stay at the Betty Ford clinic. The truck pulls away, leaving Snow watching from the front lawn. He doesn't let his eyes leave Billy's face as it fades into the distance - Krista waving beside him. For a moment, he wishes he'd been harder on Billy that morning he found him in bed with Krista. In Snow's eyes, Billy's no good for her - not good enough for her. Nothing is right. Krista's leaving - quite probably in love with a man who'll take her no where. She's throwing her life away for him, in Snow's dark opinion. She's already given so much.... His mind drifts to the thought of his wife, Krista's sister - where is she? Why hasn't she called? And his infant daughter, Haley, is gone too - how can he argue that the tiny girl be taken to her mother, who must be yearning for the young girl after the loss of their second child. But for Snow, their absence not only leaves an empty house, but also no where to turn for comfort.... except maybe... He turns back toward the house and goes inside to find the gun.
