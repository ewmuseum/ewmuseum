Date: not entered SKKKRRREEEEEE!!! The missile payload streaks from out of the sky, heading toward the Big man in the toga… FWOOOOOOOOOM!! “Give up, fool! You cannot compete with my superior brain and awesome weaponry!! Kneel before your steely DOOM!!” The metallic voice ricochets around the once quiet street outside the Metropolis Café and Beanery. The scene is dirty with soot and asphalt scorched as we rejoin our Big and Bold hero, Big Alan Steele. The once perfect art deco of the businesses have blistered, probably from the explosions that have carved out craters in the street itself. A heaving Big Al wipes his brow with his forearm, failing to remove the dirt and grime that have accumulated. With a defiant posture, he juts out his lantern-like jaw and looks up into the eyes of his adversary. Or more appropriately, looks up into what could be passed for eyes. Sensors and camera equipment more like it. About 25 feet up into the air, the gleaming mechanical menace shadowing out the sun behind it – Big Duce, the mechanical menace, piloted by the mad Dr. Destroyo. “I shall not rest until your inhuman machine of destruction lies in ruin at the feet of I, Big Alan Steele!! So come, cowardly villain encased in steel, whilst I have but only a flesh encasement of flesh… and by the gods I’ll give you what for!! Hi-Keeba!!” Giant gears whirr, the huge arm of the mech swinging around in a quick fluid motion. Steele’s eyes widen as a compartment slides open, another group of missiles primed for attack. “THEN YOU DIE!!!” The group of missiles scream from their confinements at once, all trailing a hard arc through the sky at Big Al. Steele leaps at the exact point of impact, the successive concussive blasts propelling him through the downtown street lined with palm trees. AS ALL STORIES, it started when he was young. No one knows exactly when or exactly why, but his first childhood memories weren’t really memories at all. They were pain. Whenever childhood reared itself inside his mind, true physical pain wracked his body. And it felt good. Mother was your typical mom, seemingly chased right out of a 60’s sitcom. She stayed at home with her only little boy. She cooked. She cleaned. And she loved her little boy. He was smart as a whip. He loved to follow her around the house and cling to the apron, trying to help out in any way he could, and making those messes that only children do in their assistance. But nagging worried dogged her. He was extremely quiet. He didn’t make friends. Playing outside was out of the question. And what about the bruises? The cuts. The emergency trips to the hospital? My god… what is he doing to himself? What is happening to her little angel? She knew, of course. But the she couldn’t bear the answers. BIG AL GRABBED a mailbox and ripped it from the ground. Hoping that Uncle Sam would forgive his felony of tampering with US Mail, he hurled it forward with all his might, hoping his hiding spot had served him well enough. The mailbox flew true, but at the last second the mechanical arm swung around, batting the box away like a mere child’s toy. There was a small click from the top of the mech as Steele stole a glance upwards, seeing the Vulcan cannon snap into place. Big Al was running again, the heavy gunfire riddling the car he was behind, making it look like it was melting in the hot sun. Big Al swore an oath to Zeus as he lifted a huge manhole cover and held it in front of him. Heavy fire slammed into the heavy iron, and Steele could feel himself sliding backward on the pavement, his sandals not providing the needed traction. The metallic laughter of the man in the mech echoed across the empty street. “Give up!! You cannot hold out there forever!!” Though Big Al couldn’t see it, he could hear, and feel, the mech’s body trying to negotiate around in the tight quarters. Then the heavy footfalls as the mech began forward. “Now, I shall send you to HELL!!” The Vulcan cannon stopped firing, and an intense wave of heat and flame flew around the manhole cover. Steele grimaces in pain as the manhole cover grows red hot to the touch. “Hi-Keeba!!” Flames rushed by the hole in the ground, just as Steele’s sandals disappeared below the street’s surface. FATHER WAS A disciplinarian, and a harsh one at that. He would come home late, reeking of liquor and irate at his little brat. He would learn soon enough that anything could draw Father’s ire. Dawdling about. Running around. Being too quiet. Speaking too loud. It seemed that Father even could see behind his eyes to see what he was thinking, and even that was used as excuse enough for discipline. The cellar was where it took place. Beneath the prim and proper household was Father’s workroom, a damp place that was converted from a basement. Water still dripped from the walls if it rained enough that day. The dank was stifling in its omnipresence. The floor was little more than hard-packed dirt, covered with sawdust and metal shavings. The walls hung row upon row of tools. Then there were the tools of discipline. Each night, Father would drag his little brat downstairs. He never knew when it started. And at night, it still occurs in his nightmares. Sometimes it was something new. And on good nights it was something tolerable. But most nights, it was the chair. Strapped down into the creaky, homemade device, he would be offered something to bite down upon to keep from splitting his young teeth at such an early age. Dentists cost money, after all. “COME OUT YOU coward, or I’ll level this whole town to flush you out!!” To punctuate his statement, a hatch in the back opened up and yet another cache of missiles are revealed. With a whoosh, they arced high in the air, coming down randomly in the town with minor explosions. “Big Alan Steele!! Come out and face your demise!!” AS quietly as possible, Big Al creeps out from a sewer grate, careful to avoid detection. The great monstrosity Big Duce, is scanning the other side of the street. His massive musculature tensing, he gets down and hefts a fallen streetlamp, holding it across his body. As the mechanical menace starts to swing around, weapons at the ready, Steele roars out and rushes the metal beast. Before Big Duce can react, Steele labs the long, metal pole through the legs of the automaton, running around in an attempt to trip it up… “Oooooooof!!” The air rushes out of Steele’s lungs as the pole stops. He nearly runs through the bar, bending it slightly, as the armored legs don’t even budge. The cockpit whirls around on the waist actuator, and Steele, as he gasps for breath, could swear the mech is grinning at him. “You fool! This war machine weighs well over 30 tons!!! You might as well move a mountain!” The legs buzz to life, and the mech steps backward, the leg furthest from Steele moving back. Big Al, still hanging over the streetlight, is flicked away like a flea, careening across the street and shattering through a plate glass storefront and crashing into the back. The world becomes unsteady, as our hero grunts and tries to ignore his protesting body and crawl away. A snick from an arm unit, and another cadre of missiles flies forth… FWA-BOOOOM!!! YOU CANNOT SUCCEED without discipline, Father used to say. Discipline is what made this country great, Father used to say. Discipline is life, Father used to say. Without discipline and sacrifice, human beings could not survive in a hostile world. He drove his point home differently each night. One night it was a leather belt, buckle side down, of course. Another a small hammer. Sometimes, if feeling particularly authoritative, a two by four. But the one constant was the pain, excruciating and complete. As Father explained, discipline can only be brought about by pain. And discipline is life. Life is pain. The boy never cried out. Tears would streak his young face, but never a sound went passed his lips. Father would continue each night until the overwhelming pain brought him into unconsciousness. In the beginning, it was usually a quick process. But as time went on, the sessions grew longer and longer. He began eschewing the mouthpiece, savoring the grind of tooth against tooth. The taste of his own blood became delicious. Father would look on in amazement as the he absorbed blow after blow, each hit harder and harder. And he would smile. The human body is wondrous in its design. We are designed to process so many differing sensory inputs it truly is a marvel of engineering. And with each input, there are a series of processes and responses that our bodies go through. Pain is merely one of those sensations, yet an extremely important one. Pain tells us something is wrong, and where problem is located. Without pain, we would be subject to any number of dangerous maladies because we did not experience a sensation telling us to protect ourselves. But extended period of pain are also detrimental. Our bodies then respond with powerful, self-produced opiates called endorphins that mask the pain and, along with an influx of epinephrine, stimulate our bodies into action to remove ourselves from a situation. It is this response that allows injured men to continue during periods of extreme stresses. And it’s addictive. Our bodies begin to crave the rush, the natural high. It’s why athletes compete, pushing themselves to the limit. It’s why extreme sports flourish and why amusement parks are everywhere. People love the rush. But our bodies need balance. If there is too much of something, we adapt. Thus, it takes more and more to create the desired effect. ‘WHERE, BY ZEUS’ BEARD, is he?’ wonders our hero, Big Alan Steele. He struggles up out of the rubble of the building, fortunately surviving the blast with only a ringing in his head and a few minor bruises. Steele mutters another swear to the gods of Olympia and makes a mad dash behind the row of buildings, through the alley. The rhythmic thumping of the robomech, Big Duce, can be heard at the opposite end of the alleyway. Big Al desperately looks around for anything that could be of some use against the mechanical monster, but so far, all his efforts have been for naught. It’s too big, too string, and surprisingly fast. Its weapons were deadly and accurate, putting him on the defensive the entire time. A sudden surge of hopelessness engulfs our hero, just as the whine of jet engines alerts him to yet another horrifying fact. The mech has jump jet boosters. And it’s flying. “By the gods!!” “You are correct Alan!! I am the god! I AM THE GOD!! BWAHAHAHAHAHA!!!” “Come down and face me man to man, coward,” snarls Big Al at the advancing machine, “And we shall see who the superior warrior TRULY is, Destroyo!!!” “But don’t you see?! We already do!! Victory is to the one best prepared, as well as all the physicality you so care for!! I merely have used my massive intellect to construct an armor that surpasses any nightmare you can dream!!” Reaching the main street, Big Al screeches to a halt and changes direction, running directly at the flying mech. Weapons fire all at once, but Steele has come under its arc of destruction, running underneath. The mech labors to control its flight, boosters firing and trying to turn. Steele crouches and brings a large chuck of building over his head. “Hi-Keeba!!” A great heave and the piece of rubble flies true. It stuffs itself into the main booster, an explosion from the backlog of pressure sending the mech back to the ground in a hurry. The machine manages to land on what you would call its feet, but small explosions and electrical flares indicate that the leg actuators have sustained heavy damage. Steele manages a small grin of satisfaction, just before the world spins out of control and upside down. The mech has spun around, dragging its arms low, smacking the Greek demi-god hard and sending him somersaulting across the street. AS THE YEARS WENT on and the beatings continued, he began to enjoy the responses. He body would take the pain and release the endorphins. His skin would tingle. His breathing became rapid. Somewhere at the base of his brain, he could feel it spread out through the blood. A new feeling would take the place of the pain, one he wasn’t sure he could identify. Happiness. The sessions would go longer and longer. It would take more and more to reach that place he enjoyed so much. His happiness would be longer in coming. He would beg Father for more, who would look oddly at him but oblige. Blows rained down, but the desired effect would not come. The boy would scream for more, pleading to find the happiness that only their sessions could bring out. Father would get a scared look at his son, mutter something about his sickness, and then strike harder. Longer. Faster. More. Then one day, he couldn’t reach that place anymore. Father couldn’t last long enough. He would tire and give up, leaving him strapped and crying for more. Just one more. Use the sledgehammer, I can take it. Please don’t go upstairs!! We’re not finished!! Father! I need you!! FATHER!!! “WOW, THAT’S sooooo sad.” A concussion blast shakes the room, small pieces of plaster falling from the ceiling of the Metropolis Café. Big Alan Steele’s crime-fighting partner, The Masochist, has to cover his cup of coffee to avoid the falling dust getting in the steaming, black liquid. He gets a solemn look and replies to the buxom redhead his side, the only other soul still in the café. “Yeah, it is sad. Very sad.” “Uh, I don’t mean to be a bother and all,” the woman replies, nervously biting at her cuticle, “but since YOU ARE the superhero and all, maybe you should get that Big Duce guy?” “What, him? Nah. I don’t tangle with the small fries. I’ll just let Big Al… my SIDEKICK… handle this one.” “Oh, right,” she sighs. “I’m surprised he let that guy do this much damage. I might have to do some re-training with him.” “Yeah, don’t let him get so soft! Anyways… I’m so sorry about what your father did to you. But at least you got a superpower out of it.” “What, me?” The Masochist rears back in confusion, then smiles. “Oh! No, no. no. You have it all wrong. I was talking about Dr. Destroyo, the guy who created that Big Duce mech out there.” “But you’re The Masochist… and I thought that’s how…” “No, I got my superpowers from drinking too much tap water. You see, my town was one of the first to use fluorine in the water supply, and, well, it got into my bones and organs and all, creating a hardening effect that allows me to sustain amazing amounts of damage,” The Masochist explains, rather smugly “And that’s why I’M the superhero, and Big Al is the sidekick. They determined that though he was in the tip-top percentile of humans in strength, you have to be statistically beyond a human range.” “Oh…” “Yeah. I guess I just got used to it, and enjoyed the endorphin kick myself, ya know?” SMASH!! Big Al somersaults wildly through the picture window of the café, sprawling out at the feet of The Masochist and the girl he was hitting on. Steele is laying on his back, eyes rolling around in his head. The Masochist takes a nervous look at the redhead, and tries to sound like he’s scolding Big Al, but not really succeeding. “Hey! You almost spilled my coffee! Be more careful, Al!” Steele shakes his head, his eyes slowly coming back into focus on The Masochist. A desperate smile crosses the Big man’s face. “There you are!! Now, finally, we can rid this world of the mechanical menace that is Destroyo and his Big Duce!!” The Masochist rubs his fingernails on his t-shirt, buffing them smugly, much to the delight of the redhead. “Well, okay Alan. I’ll bail you out this time but… URK!” Big Al hauls The Masochist off bodily by the collar, kicking his way through the rest of the broken glass. The masochist squirms in Steele grip, trying to make the telephone gesture with his hands while looking back at the woman. “Call me!” he croaks. “HI-KEEBA~!!!” “WELL, ALL’S WELL that ends well, eh boys?” We re-rejoin our Big and Bold hero, Big Alan Steele, within the plush confines of Stately Steele Manor. A weary and filthy Big Al looks over as his trusted manager, Sue Saksnussum, and beams, his expression saying it all. The Masochist stands next to the Big Man, back, acrid smoke streaming from his charred body. “So,” Sue presses, “How’d it go?” “Fantastic!!” exclaims the bohunkular one, “A superb training exercise for the two of us!! Duke Williams shall see a finely tuned Big Alan Steele as a partner come Tuesday!!” “Yeah,” growls the smoking Masochist, “Great. Superb. Wonderful. Lovely.” Sue ignores the sarcastic Masochist, focusing on Big Al, “I have to admit to being really curious. I mean… how the hell did you beat that thing?! It was freakin’ huge!!” Steele flashes that infectious, child-like smile of his, “Teamwork saved the day!!” “He threw me at a street light. When I tried to grab at the wire to stop myself, it snapped, and I fell back INTO Big Duce. The result was a live wire… and with the mech acting as a ground…” The Masochist grumbles. “Great idea, Al!” Big Al’s grin somehow gets bigger and brighter. The Masochist frowns. “Yeah, but that girl was, like, mine for the taking! I got her number and everything! She was so impressed with my superhero certification card.” Steele nods sagely, “Yes, yon laminated cardboard pieces have been referred to as ‘chick magnets’ by many of my associates.” “Yeah, well, I can’t call her now!! Not after what happened!” “Oh stop it,” Sue scolds, “You guys did GREAT out there! She has to be impressed with you, even if what Big Al did was a tiny bit embarrassing.” “No!” The Masochist whines, “You don’t get it!! The electricity fired the paper I wrote it on!! I don’t HAVE her number anymore! And chicks NEVER call you first… some sorta dating rule.” Sue rubs her chin for a moment, searching her mind for a solution. “Yeah. You’re pretty much screwed.” Big Al wraps a grimy arm around The Masochist, pulling him in tight enough to crush the air out of his lungs. “Cheer up, partner!! We hath defeated a great and powerful evil today!! Truly, this is a victory for GOOD!! The gods are undoubtedly pleased with you, and I shall PERSONALLY ask Aphrodite for some attention to you.” “Oh no!” The Masochist pleads, “No blind dates!!” Big Al gives the Masochist a big thumbs up. “But love IS blind! Hi-Keeba!”
