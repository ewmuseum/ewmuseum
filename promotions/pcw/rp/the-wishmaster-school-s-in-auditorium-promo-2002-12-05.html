The Wishmaster --- School's In (Auditorium Promo)

5 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/5/02 We open with The End standing in a school auditorium, a simple lecture hall. The kids, high school 10th and 11th graders, smoke their cigarettes and drink their liquor, shoot up heroin and sniff crack. Ah, yes, a ghetto school in Houston. Darius Johnson asked The End to come to his school for the day, and The End is taking time off from preparing for his match for the Continental Title to do so. Actually, by being at a school, this promo automatically counters The HIT and Rush Mason, but that's not his intention, it's mine. *Insert evil laugh here.* The End brought along Dante Jackson since he's the hometown guy and since they're teaming up on Adrenaline. And so in the auditorium/lecture hall, The End is going to be giving the Physics teacher's lecture, with his own little spin on it, thus turning this into a PCW promo at the same time. The End:'Black holes are some of the more mystifying objects in the universe in my mind. Especially one in particular, an earthbound phenomenon. The infamous Black Hole of Suck. The Black Hole of Suck was first described in detail by Clyde, current PCW World Champion. It is a freak occurence whenever anyone other than myself gets into a ring and wrestles. I'll have to brave it when I face Tim Walden, The HIT, Rush Mason, and Totally Tubular Tommy to become Continental Champion, but I'm willing to face the abyss of talent, charisma, and success in order to achieve my goals. But let's talk about black holes in general. 'Their properties stay true to Newtonian and quantum mechanical theories while deviating substantially from them. That fact withstanding, black hole study may be very easy or extremely difficult depending on how one chooses to analyze one. I shall describe a few of the methods of the detection of black holes, what conditions and situations would be observable upon astronomically close-ranged study, and then I’ll talk a little about what conditions may occur beyond the horizon. Before I begin, let me assure you all that I have no idea how to give a scientific lecture, so forgive me for the occasional “slips” in which I may break format, such as not giving a thorough description of technical terms. I also remind you that this will be tested since your Physics teacher is a prick.' This comment gets a laugh from the students. The End:'There are many different ways that one can detect black holes. One way would be to use an X-ray telescope. A common misconception among those who realize that X-rays may be used to locate black holes is that the X-rays are ejected from the black hole itself. True, black holes do eject matter (due to its Hawking radiation, which I shall discuss later), but the X-rays do not come from within the black hole, rather, the X-rays are emitted from the accretion disk of the black hole. The accretion disk is a cloud of gas that surrounds the black hole. It is comprised of all of the matter and energy being sucked into the black hole. 'Another misconception is that everything that will ever fall into a black hole will “fall” straight into it. Studies of all the black holes that have been detected by humans show that the vast majority of them rotate on an axis. Only a handful are non-rotating, or stationary, holes. So, knowing that the black hole spins, it would make logical sense that the objects sucked into the black hole would spin as well. The accretion disk is the visible disk of matter spinning into the black hole. As it spirals inward, the individual atoms increase in speed and collide with each other. With the increase in speed, the collisions become more violent and occur more often, raising the temperature until the gas becomes so hot that it emits X-ray radiation. 'Trying to locate X-rays, and thus the accretion disk, would require that the black hole have some significant angular momentum, or rotation. If the black hole has little or no rotation, then there will be no accretion disk. The probability of a black hole with no angular momentum is very small, so most black holes will have an accretion disk, “fed” by stellar wind, visible gas and light taken from a neighboring star.' The End decides that this is very boring, and hurries to the end of the lecture so he can add in his own bits to it. The End:'Another way to detect the black holes would be to measure its Hawking radiation. Hawking radiation is a quantum mechanicl effect, in which black holes radiate by emitting photons with wavelengths roughly equal to the size of the black hole. The radiation has a blackbody spectrum of a certain temperature. If the Hawking temperature becomes greater than the energy of the rest mass of a particular type of particle, then that particle, its antiparticle, and photons will be radiated from the black hole. The Hawking quantum mechanical effect is how matter can escape a black hole; however, this process is random, and only possilbe through a quantum mechanical effect. That temperature is inversely proportional to the mass of the black hole, so Hawking radiation would not be a good way to search for larger black holes; it may, however, work for smaller ones. Hawking radiation also affects a black hole’s luminosity, which is inversely proportional to the square of the mass of the black hole. Luminosity is quite small for larger black holes, but for holes having masses less than one solar mass, luminosity is then substantially increased (since the square of any number less than one results in a smaller number). 'The last method of detecting a black hole that I shall discuss is the gravitational “lensing” effect. Any mass can bend light, but greater distortions require greater masses. Black holes have very high gravitational potentials: the higher the potential, the greater the distortion. The lensing effect compresses the image radially, with nearer objects compressed more than farther objects. The lens bends the image around the black hole, and may even duplicate the image if the gravitational potential is high enough. Making the lens easier to detect is the fact that any light coming within 1.5 Schwarzschild radii [the Schwarzschild radius is the radius of the black hole itself] of the black hole will fall into the black hole, meaning that no images can appear in that region. The lensing effect is one of the best ways a black hole can be detected.' The End shakes his head at the nonsense he had to present to the students. But he's at the end and may now insert his own parts to this drab lecture. The End:'Once a black hole is located, one may set about studying it. Fortunately, many properties of a black hole may be observed from afar. Thanks to both Newtonian and relativistic theories [Einstein], humans can safely compute the mass of a black hole and the gravitational pull at certain orbital circumferences above the black hole’s horizon. Some properties of the black hole, such as the Schwarzschild surface, may be simply observed (in fact, one sees the entire Schwarzschild surface). However, one cannot be truly certain about what one believes until he/she tests that belief. Assuming that humans have the capability of near-light speed travel, let the reader imagine some arbitrary starship, like the U.S.S. Enterprise (copyright lawyers rejoice!) that is about to study a black hole. Having done the preliminary calculations and measurements from Earth, the crew ventures to explore the black hole as closely as possible without endangering the ship. Lacking robotic probes to luanch toward the hole, the Enterprise crew remains at a safe distance of 100 billion kilometers and instead launches a small shuttle (approximately 10 meters long by 5 meters wide) carrying Rush Mason, Totally Tubular Tommy, Tim Walden, and The HIT.' And now we get to the part of the promo that has anything to do with anything. The End:'The four intrepid adventurers transmit information about their fall and the gravitational conditions back to the Enterprise via a laser directed at the starship. During their fall, Totally Tubular Tommy records that the shuttle falls relatively slowly at the beginning of its descent, and then speeds up the closer it gets to the horizon, also known as the Schwarzschild surface. Tim Walden reports information about the strength of the gravitational pull by black hole. The HIT sends the information back to the Enterprise. The closer the shuttle gets to the horizon, the tidal effects become more pronounced. Rush Mason is the black guy, he's there to fill the minority quota. Sad, but true.' The kids laugh at the expense of Rush Mason. The End:'At first, moving becomes slightly harder. Then, the tidal force stretches the men’s bodies apart. The HIT signals back to the Enterprise, “The tidal force is too strong and the horizon is quickly approaching. We must pull out now!” 'Hearing this, Captain Skylar Green (wink wink), sends back a message saying, “Negative! Maintain present course!” Within 20 seconds, the shuttle disappears into the black hole, and Tim Walden, The HIT, and Totally Tubular Tommy have effectively disappeared from the universe. Oh yeah, Rush Mason disappeared, as well. I keep forgetting about him, maybe it's his overall lack of importance to anyone and everyone. 'Back on the Enterprise, the entire crew is celebrating. The senior officer, Sandy Wong, exclaims, “We now have the data we need to confirm the theories of gravitational relativity,” to which Skylar Green responds, “Yes, but more importantly, we’ve rid the universe of Rush Mason, Totally Tubular Tommy, The HIT, and Tim Walden! Good job, everyone, it worked! Let’s go get a taco!'' Those three are really getting hammered in this promo... The End:'Lastly, I shall speculate on what may be occurring at the singularity, using our doomed quartet as the subjects in a few scenarios I have pictured. Scenario 1: Before they even reach the singularity, they are ripped apart, down to their subatomic particles, by the tidal forces due to the overwhelmingly strong gravity. The remainder of the men joins a giant sentient ball of mass at the center of the singularity, whose goal is to swallow the entire universe. Scenario 2: The remnants of the men and their shuttle reach the singularity. There, they are sent through a wormhole and are reassembled shortly before exiting a white hole in a different galaxy, where other forms of intelligent life exist, and they all happen to speak perfect English. The shuttle runs into a living ship named Moire, piloted by a symbiotic organism named... Pilot. The four humans, a former intergalactic space criminal turned faith healer and scientist, a blue-skinned white-haired female humanoid that sleeps with pretty much ANYTHING, a short money-grubbing alcoholic alien, an alien fighter with burte strength and the ability to knock out organic organisms with his tongue, and a weird human with half of his face missing and existing as pure energy, are on the run from an evil, evil, very evil, evil evil alien known as Scorpius. If the reader hasn’t figured it out yet, I’m giving the basic plot of Farscape, and the only reason John Crichton wrestled. Scenario 3: Walden, HIT, Rush Mason and Totally Tubular Tommy travel into what I shall describe as “hypertime,” the phrase used most famously in DC Comic's special 'The Kingdom.' Scenario 4: HIT, Rush Mason, Walden, and Totally Tubular Tommy exit the ear of Jerry Lewis during a Jerry’s Kids special. Scenario 5: The four men travel back to the prehistoric time and change the course of history by killing all creatures save for microorganisms. A super-intelligent race of amoeba develops. Scenario 7: HIT, Totally Tubular Tommy, Rush Mason, and Walden are actually the aliens that are abducting and anally probing the citizens of the world in a sick, twisted form of payback. I could list millions of possible speculative scenarios, all of which would sound quite absurd, but since no one knows what happens in the singularity, discrediting me would be pointless. I am reminded of the inspirational words uittered during one of just many McDonald’s commercials. “Hey, it could happen.” When exploring the possibilities inside the singularity of a black hole, the aforementioned phrase is not a terrible thing to keep in mind. I thank the listener for taking the time to hear me out. I am officially out of things to say. Have a great day.' The End leaves the stage to obliged applause, and the students hope they get tested on the many possible deaths for Rush Mason, The HIT, Totally Tubular Tommy, and Tim Walden.
