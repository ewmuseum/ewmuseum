Winding

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 27.05.03 (RP#2 TT) Abner loves the NRA. Ever since he became a member-at the ripe old age of eighteen (he applied through the mail back when he was in prison)-he has never missed a meeting. He always managed to keep a rifle or shotgun on hand (you know, in case of emergencies), and up until his latter prison days, this was a fairly easy task. But now, with a well-regulated legal system, it’s “damn near difficult”, as Abner says, for a “Felon Fella” to carry a gun. And so driving down the muddy back roads of Vermont, his heart sinks into his chest. He puts on the break to slow down and read a small sign in front of him: “NRA Meeting Today”. He sighs and looks down at his floor. Between the gas and brake, where the usual stock of a deer-rifle or sidearm once laid, is nothing but a pile of empty beer bottles. He shrugs. “Well, at least I still have that…” But without the live ammo, the great combo of booze and bullets just ain’t the same. “What’s this world coming to?” Abner asks himself. Shrugging it off, he steps back on the gas and heads on off down the road. “There’s no sense in crying over spilt .30/.30 shells”, he assures himself. And of course, he is right, Dwelling on the pass never got him anywhere-or anyone else for that matter-and he might as well give up. He’s had his fight with the law, and the law won. He appealed to various people and institutions using a wide array resources, but these tactics just aren’t enough. After serving his two years in the prison confines of D-2-9, few would here his words. “Damn hippie liberal sons-a-bitches!” Abner steps on it and the car jerks forward at an ungodly rate. But pulling up to a street sign, he remembers that an other ticket (or D.W.I. for that matter) will do him in for good, and so he slows down. “Eh, there’s no good reason for that crap, an’ I sure as Hell don’t want to go through that shit again!” His big red Ford slows down and he pulls into a short, narrow drive. “Better cool off for a while”, Abner thinks out-loud. The truck comes to a full stop and the engine dies down. Getting out of his truck, Abner’s heavy boots crunch against the aged and broken gravel of the ground. The old, small parking area is falling apart, literally crumbling beneath the feet of this [admittedly] big man. He tucks in his shirt, sucks in his stomach (away from PCW, he’s started to develop a beer-belly), and straightens out his hat. He cocks his head to the side, makes an nauseating noise, and spits out a tight, small package of cheap wet tobacco. Now he’s ready to go in. “Howdy”, he roughly, but politely tips his hat to a passerby. Making his way up the steps of the diner entry, Abner is overcome by an old series of memories dating back to his childhood. This was the first restaurant he had even ate at, and as far as he could remember, up until he was a teen, also, the only restaurant he had ever ate at (with Abner’s family’s poor income and all). Kicking open the door, Abner steps up onto the dusty wooden floor. It’s just what you’d expect of a small, small-town diner: four or five, rickety round wooden tables, a long, wooden countertop with a handful of red-seated stools in front of it, and a quiet crowd of a dozen or so people. “…Afternoon” a fellow redneck attired in plaid salutes. “Howdy” Abner replies. A young, long-haired teen with a U.S./Peace Flag brushes by him. Abner grabs him by the shoulders and gently tosses him out the door. “What the-“ but it’s of no use. The chunky-faced owner, clad in a stained, sweaty apron, simply laughs, the rounded top of his tummy rumbling, as Abner slams the door shut. Another local teen, grasping a “No War on Iraq” sign steps up to him, accidentally making eye-contact, and then tries to fade away, but meeting the same fate as the last. “Hehe…” Abner laughs under his breath. The owner/cook smiles, tugging his roughly shaved chin as Abner plops himself down in an unoccupied stool toward the front. And only a moment later, a smiling waitress, probably in her mid-thirties, dashed in makeup, comes and lays a neat black menu in front of him. A moment later, bending over his shoulder, the women takes his order, and waltzes away smiling, and heads toward the back. Woof. Woof. A local man’s young hunting hound rushes across the floor behind Abner’s back, chasing at some invisible, unattainable object. “Ah, shaddup you ol’ coon-dog “ Abner snaps at the dog. The frightened pet, pride hurt, puts his tail between his legs and retreats to the back of the diner. Lowering his head, it hides between the legs of its master. “Pussy” The master states, nor in anger or in shame, but simply stating a simple fact. The dog gives him a cock-eyed, confused look before lying down at his feet. “Hehehe” Antoher man in the back laughs out, apparently having found humor in the situation. Abner shrugs, grunts and then turns back to the cup of coffee in front of him. And a few minutes later, the food comes. Eggs, toast and sausage…Abner’s favorite meal. “Now if I only had a beer” Abner thinks to himself, knowing full well that alcoholic beverages are not allowed on the premise. But hey, it couldn’t hurt. And so pulling out a small bottle of scotch, he uncaps the lid and adds a shot into his [mostly empty] coffee cup. He lifts it up and chugs down the drink in one large, smooth gulp. “Ah!” He wipes off his lips with his coat sleeve. “There, no harm done.” He pushes the cup aside and shoves his plate forward. Standing up from the stool he straightens out his jacket, and pulls out his wallet. Leaving a twenty-dollar bill on the table in front of him, he tips his hat to the chef and makes for the door. Moments later, sitting back in his truck, he finds himself driving back on down the road, not really knowing what he’s doing, or how he got there. “Time flies bah so damn fast” Abner thinks “and there’s no way for us to catch up”. So how, in the events of our conversation, did we go from the business of hunting rifles, handguns and NRA, to a small-town diner located out in the middle of nowhere? I don’t really know. And just like Abner’s life; he doesn’t really know. But for that matter, does anyone? “Heh…fuck it!” Abner pulls out a can of beer from beneath his seat, opens it, and quickly chugs it down. “All this thinkin’ and wonderin’ bullshit…it don’t get you nowhere…and gives you kind of a headache, too”. Abner shrugs, and reaches under his seat, and pulls out another Budweiser.
