The Task at Hand Part 2

20 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/20/2002 'Yeah, I'll have the grilled chicken sandwich with everything on it, fries, and a Pepsi with my meal. Start me off with a Jack and Coke right now, though,' says Jon Kano, PCW's REAL LEGEND, as he sits at a table on the patio of a little cafe in downtown Amsterdam. Kano, dressed in a black-on-black Versace suit and wearing golden framed sun glasses, watches the traffic of cars and people go past him. 'Here you are, sir, your meal should be done in about ten minutes,' states the possibly gay waiter with a slight lisp in his voice as he sets the previously ordered drink in front of Kano. 'Bob Vurdo, let me count the waves. I usually respect those who hold titles in PCW, but I will make an exception in your case. First you hold the Extreme Championship, which in itself has a stigma of jobberness. If you were any kind of honorable champion, you would do your best to bring some credibility to your title, but no. You just go along doing your thing, but not the championship thing. Case in point, when I was PCW Continental Champion, I was the first man to bring credibility to that respective title. Now I could go on and on about my past accomplishments, but this isn't history class. Normally, I wouldn't give a damn about another title, I have had my share of the gold. But this title means something to me, it will put me in a very elite club of people here in PCW who have held four separate titles. And if there is one thing I like, it is having another credential in my already legendary career,' lauds Kano. A young man of about fifteen years of age walks past Kano and does a double take. He smiles and cautiously approaches Kano. 'Mr. Kano, sir, I can't tell you what a huge fan I am of yours,' the teen says with a hint of caution, excitement, and fear in his voice. 'Well, son, thats good to hear. Would you like an autograph or something,' asks The Man who Rocketh the Mosteth. 'Mr. Kano, that would make my day,' responds the teen with an upbeat tone. Jon Kano whips out a pen and a piece of paper and begins to scribble his signature on the piece of paper and hands the paper over to the kid. 'Thank you, Mr. Kano,' the teen says to Kano. 'No problem, kid, now I got a little business to take care of here,' Kano responds. The kid walks away from Kano with a certain spring in his step. 'Ahhhhh, fans,' shutters Kano quietly. 'Now Vurdo may be a competent wrestler, but he is no Jon Kano, and he is no LEGEND. Now these Extreme matches are brutal and bloody, not really my style, not my fortay, if you will. I am more of a technically sound, proficient power wrestler. I hardly ever use weapons. I guess with all of this said, I am at a gross disadvantage in this match, but I am like yeast, I always rise to the occassion. I was never supposed to get this far as I have in my career, and on Friday night I show everyone, especially Bob Vurdo, the PCW Extreme Champion, why I am what REAL LEGENDS ARE MADE OF!!!!!' exclaims the future PCW Extreme Champion, Jon Kano.
