Tales of the Golden Toe: Tale of El Romantico (Finale)

28 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/28/02 Tales of the Golden Toe: The Golden Heart as passed and the Golden Toe has come back again. The Golden Toe is the old Juan Borro, the fun loving foreign soccer player who did his all to help others. Through the tales of the Golden Toe Juan gives us insight into present experiences with his life and duties and encounters with people. The Golden Toe has seen his calling, and that calling is to do good be good, and be a power for good. No force will stop Juan’s GOAL~! Time: 11: 31 P.M Location: Barcelona, SpainScene: Hotel Final Part “People always say the past in there to help you. A person also say the past is there to haunt you. Which one is it? Can it be both? In order to be helped you have to be haunted first. Does it make sense? Or does it not?” The scene opens with Juan Borro sleeping in his bed. It’s been a long day and Juan is tired and just wants some rest. Juan lays his head down in his pillow wand looks up at the chipped white painted ceiling above him. He then looks at the bed his body is lying on. He wonders how many people’s bodies have ever graced this bed. He also wonders if he is the most significant person to sleep in it. Who knows? We don’t know why Juan is thinking of this, but maybe it has something to do with the past. You’d think Juan wouldn’t worry about rekindling the past of someone else, when it was so hard for his to remember his own past. His past ruined a part of his life; maybe he doesn’t want the same to happen to someone else? Or maybe he’s just curious? Curiosity can kill. Juan thinks of all of these things as he sits there. He then tries to play detective by putting the things he’s learned together. Maybe if he sorts it all out it could make some type of sense, or at least he hopes it would. First, Juan saw him in person, after hearing about him mysterious power and passion for love. He saw the strength of the man first hand, and knows this is more than just a normal man. Secondly, Juan went up to the director of the Underground Fighting League El Romantico fought in and asked for some background. He was told El Romantico’s family had all died, so he was on his own at a very young age. Then, at Friday Fusion El Romantico appeared mysteriously in Juan’s dressing room, leaving a poor young woman breathless after his passionate assault. He then went on to tell Juan to stay away. But later that night he attacked Timothy Walden, in a kind of defense fro Juan. Juan was left skeptical at the actions. Lastly, Juan went to a fortuneteller in need of more background or truth. Some of which was handed out about an amazing El Romantico show back in the 1800’s where the same exact man was present. All of this adds up meaning a young mysterious man with no family, tries to hold back his past and help aid Juan in an attempt of asking Juan to stay out of it. But he still has enormous strength and is older than he really seems. Juan then thinks up o a few questions in his head that also come up. A lot of these questions need answering and Juan is yet to know where to go for them. The questions being: Does El Romantico have a real name, and if so what is it and where does he live? Is El Romantico the real thing or just a modern day copycat of an old powerhouse lover? Maybe the whole thing is just a big gimmick. Does El Romantico have a family or is he abandoned the same way Juan had been as a child? What makes El Romantico so strong and such a great lover? What’s so special about him to begin with? Is El Romantico real? If he is then how does he disappear every now and then? If El Diablo was the fortuneteller Juan last spoke too then why did he give Juan that essential clue? Is the clue the truth, or is it just a big lie? If it’s the truth why would El Romantico want Juan to find out his past. When the two first met he said he would be there whenever Juan needed him? How can that be, and why isn’t he there when Juan needs him? And lastly, what does the white rose symbolize for El Romantico? Juan wished El Romantico could just stop being the lame fairy he is, and confront Juan like the man he is. If he was such a man and supposed great lover and warrior than he would have enough balls to ring that door bell right now and come in. As Juan drops his head to his pillow, something unexpected happened, THE DOORBELL RINGS. As the bell ring Juan jumps out of his seat and falls into the corner. He takes a deep breath knowing its El Romantico. Juan doesn’t want to show it, but deep down inside he’s scared, knowing if El Romantico can hear his thought that he must be some kind of force from nature. Juan slowly approaches the door and opens it, with his heart pounding a hundred miles per hour and the hair sticking up all over his arms. As he swings the door open and looks who stand in front of it, it is none other then an employee of the hotel. Juan takes a huge gasp of relief as the employee has a bottle of wine in his hand. “Are you all right sir, you look kind of tired?” The worker comments on as Juan is taking heavy breaths. “I’m fine, is there anything I can do for you?” Juan asks still a bit out of breath. “Well I have some Port wine here. It says you ordered a bit earlier. So here it is.” The employee says as he hands Juan the bottle. “Oh yes! I can’t believe I forgot.” Juan says as he accepts the bottle and hits himself for being such an idiot. The employee waives goodbye and Juan closes the door with the bottle in hand. As Juan turns around with a smile in his face, the smile suddenly turns into horror. EL ROMANTICO is standing right in front of Juan, and has a sick smile stretched across his face. “I see you called for me?” El Romantico asks. “How did you get in here?” Juan asks frantically. “I already told you. Whenever you want me I’ll be here.” El Romantico answers in a simple manner. Juan pauses not knowing what to say. “Well that should answer one of your many questions.” El Romantico says as he lets out a holler of a laugh. “How about answering the rest for me?” Juan demands. “That will all happen soon enough just be patent.” El Romantico respond. Before Juan can speak again dust is thrown in his face and when his eyes are opened El Romantico is gone, with the same dumb white rose, left behind. Juan can’t help but be angry. When will Juan find out the answer? He wants to know now and wants to know it all? The Golden Heart Tales are not to be written or sold by anyone but Golden Heart Tales Production. Copyright 2002.
