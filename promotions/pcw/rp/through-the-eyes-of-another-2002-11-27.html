Through the Eyes of Another

27 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 27th, 2002 So, there I stood – clad in my normal (and expensive, remember!) Armani three-piece suit – just underneath the flora hanging down from the awning above the entrance of the Amarillo Hilton Hotel. Pacing around nervously, I whipped out a comb and brushed my jet-black hair back quickly, checking my Rolex every so often. No, I didn’t have a meeting with or anything; I was just freakin’ bored. I spotted some minimum-wage hotel bellhop and opened my mouth to speak to – rather, at - him. “Hey, you,” I commanded. “Get some goddamn fans out here, I’m sweating like a pig thanks to this fucking heat.” One would question my sanity standing around with no real aim in the midday Texas sun clad in a suit, but it was all part of the job – I did mention looking important, did I not? The bellhop passed by me, on his way to park someone ten times more important than him’s car, no doubt. I was going to let him go about his meandering, but I figured I’d cure this boredom – and find an excuse to head inside the hotel. “Hey, Bobby, Joey, whatever your name is.” He turned around at me, and managed to stutter out a response. “Y-yes, sir?” “Is the guy in Room 203 up yet?” He gulped, and adjusted the collar of his maroon uniform. He was probably pretty damn uncomfortable – not like I gave two shits. “Uh…yeah, I think so.” Well, good. “Thanks. Take this, and don’t fuck around with anyone’s car. A lowlife punk life yourself couldn’t afford to reimburse a vehicle like the people own here.” I reached in my pocket, and threw a 20 on the ground in front of him. His eyes lit up. “Geez, th-“ he started to reply. “Piss off, it’s chump change to me.” I chuckled as I pushed my way through glass revolving doors, entering the fortunately air-conditioned lobby of the Hilton. I peered up at the golden chandeliers looming down from the Roman-inspired architecture of the ceiling. The crystal of the bulbs shimmered softly in the hotel, creating a nice little atmosphere that any average Joe would find breathtaking for just a hotel lobby. Who cares. I’d seen better. I made eye contact with some tramp behind the counter, and gave her a wink, causing her to blush. “Hi, Mister Anderson! Your client is up in Room 203, you can go see him if you wish,” she called out at me cheerfully. Stupid, cheery tramp. “Thanks, toots.” I replied, kindly. At least she has a nice ass. I walked over to the elevators, dragging my slightly muddy shoes on the burgundy carpet as I did so. I pressed the up button, and it illuminated immediately. Ding! One of the four shafts, to the right of me, opened up, and I stepped in. Not bad luck, I thought to myself. I hit the 'two' button – hey, I’m too good for walking up one flight of stairs – and stood patiently as the doors closed. Thank god there weren’t any Texan hicks in there trying to talk to me. I stepped out as the doors opened on the second floor, and immediately found my destination – Room 203. I accompanied my knocking with a nice, loud, attention-grabbing shout. “WAKE UP, BRO! It’s urgent!” “Ugh…hold on,” replied a voice from inside. Stupid burnout. Hurry the hell up. And finally, the door clicked open. Standing before me was my latest client, Tyler Valentine. “What’s wrong?” he asked me calmly. I guess feigning urgency worked to wake him up. “Hey, buddy! Rick Anderson here, your new agent! Now that you’re here in Primetime, I’ll be handling your business stuff from now on, you know, promotional appearances, sponsorships, the whole deal,” I explained to him. Translation: I’ll be making some money off you. He sighed. “What happened to Tom?” “He was only your agent for your time in RUSH,” I said. “Now that you’re here with us in PCW, Mister Kannon sent me to work with you and some of the other guys, some guy named Elia, another dude named Harris, I think…” I crossed my fingers behind my back. “And, by the way, I’m really sorry that this was on such short notice. We didn’t want to inconvenience you at all!” He showed no sign of acknowledgement, and simply opened his door completely ajar. “Come in,” he asked, quietly. I obliged, happily (yeah, right), and followed him in. Ho…ly…shit. This place is a fucking dump. There were soda cans, plates of barely eaten food, and silverware on the dressers and, hell, even one on top of the television. Clothes – all either white shirts or pairs of blue jeans – were strewn about the floor, almost completely hiding the forest green carpet from sight. “Hey, man…where’s the maids when you need ‘em?” I asked, possibly with the fakest chuckle I’d ever managed to bullshit out of me. “Heh.” Stupid stars. Always acting like they’re better than everyone else. “So, look, Tyler, is it, right? Now that you’re under PCW contract, you’re going to have go out and do a lot more promotional appearances, and sponsorships…” “I don’t do sponsorships. I don’t do promoting,” was all I got as a reply. What the fuck? What kind of shitface was this guy? “Uhh, well…” I replied, thinking of something to say, “if Mister Kannon says so, you DO need to go out…it’s all in the name of the business, you know.” “Fuck the business. I don’t care for PCW, and I certainly don’t care for Kannon.” What a toad. “Sir…uh…” Time for a subject change… “What do you care about?” “The fa-“ he began to respond, almost as though it were a trained response. But he stopped. He paused to think about it for a moment, and replied shortly after. “Nothing. I just don’t care about anything.” What kind of answer was that!? Nothing!? What the hell is wrong with this guy? “Nothing at all?” “Nothing.” I bit my lower lip, successfully containing the frustration that was quickly building up inside of me. He looked at me with apathetic eyes, and I tried to keep on my bullshit visage of happiness, but I decided I couldn’t deal with this anymore. “Well, look…I’ll get back to you with something you can do, I’m sure there’s something easy you can promote…do some crap with kids for a couple hours, eh?” “Okay.” “Bye!” Okay, my ass. I left, glad I didn’t have to deal with that depressing ass anymore. --------------------------------------- Shutting the door behind him, I let out a deep sigh. I’m sick of agents. I’m sick of the RUSH guys. I’m sick of this whole damn business in general. Hell, I’m just plain sick of people. I wanna sleep. And with that, I went back into my little shell, turned off the lamp, and I went back to sleep, where I could be free from all the people and things that make me miserable. Goodnight.
