Miyagawa Kicks

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11th June 2002 GMT (but the 10th in EST) The modern form of the homosapien. No huge black monolith can save you now, as we find him in the natural habitat... the kitchen. This is Sunday night, the evening that the PCW Sabotage show would have previously aired... of course now it’s merely one of those crappy one hour long house show things with Precious Peter Love beating off a strong challenge of Boca del Clyde every single week. But hey, that’s the magic of television, isn’t it? Anyway, Sunday Evening, the kitchen. Miyagawa is leaving on a Jet Plane... Don’t know when I’ll be back again... Leaving on a Je-.. well, whatever. He’s leaving to get his ass to Baltimore in the morning, and so therefore this is the last time that he will see his dearest Natalia until the following Saturday. And how is he spending this important evening? He’s staring into the fridge. Intently too, with some degree of focus. Natalia walks in wearing one of her flowery dresses and stops to look at the Japo-American staring into the fridge with his right hand holding the door open at arm’s length. She asks the obvious question. Natalia: What are you looking for, dear? Miyagawa doesn’t look away from the fridge, but his answer is forthcoming and not exactly to the point as one would hope. Miyagawa: Dunno. Just looking. Natalia approaches him and looks over his shoulder into the fridge. There is a bottle of milk and a piece of cheese that looks older than Hulk Hogan. The rest of the fridge is empty. Natalia: I think I had best do some shopping while you’re away on business, you know? Miyagawa: I’ve been thinking. Natalia: About the shopping? Miyagawa: About our baby. Natalia: And you were staring holes out of the fridge while thinking about it? Miyagawa: Well... yes. Don’t all men go to stare at the fridge to think? Natalia shakes her head in a definite “NO!” action. Natalia: Not in Russia they don’t. But then again, we don’t exactly have much use for fridges in Russia unless we want to keep our food warm. Miyagawa: Oh. Natalia sighs. Natalia: That was a joke you know. You Americans have no sense of humour. Now Boris, he had a sense of humour. He could laugh for the mother land, but you Americans... nothing. You don’t even find France going out of the World Cup amusing. Miyagawa: Heh, don’t try that one, I happen to like soccer, and thus I find that completely hilarious. Natalia looks at the side of Miyagawa’s head and runs her hand through his hair. Miyagawa reaches up and touches her hand before shutting the fridge and turning to her. He looks at her with soul in his eyes. Miyagawa: I love you. You know that, right? Natalia smiles warmly as she hears that. Miyagawa suddenly crouches and puts his head against Natalia’s enlarged belly before speaking to it. Miyagawa: And I love you too. Oh! Natalia: I felt that one, it’s kicking. Natalia holds Miyagawa’s hands to her belly to feel the baby kick as they share this tender moment. Miyagawa: I think this one’s going to be a fighter, just like his mother. Natalia: Bah, just like her father, you mean. Miyagawa grins and ignores the beginning of another “It’s a boy” “No, it’s a girl” argument... they’d had it before, and he had come to the decision that why bother to argue over something that neither person can change and they’ll find out in six weeks anyway. Miyagawa: I was thinking. Natalia: So you said. Miyagawa: About something else this time. Well... it’s related. Natalia: Go on then, tell me about it. Miyagawa: Well... men get maternity leave now. I was thinking of asking Nelson if I could have a couple months off after the baby arrived... you know, so we can get settled in. Natalia: What about your Ricky Steamboat impression? Miyagawa: I think I can leave the Wrestlemania Four impression for a couple of years. It’s not like it would break my heart to not be on the road for a couple of months. I can’t see how dirty nappies and sleepless nights can be any worse then putting up with some of the stuff that goes on at shows. Natalia: Wait till a couple of weeks of the dirty nappies has gone by and you’ll be desperate to get out of here and go back to work. I know I will. Miyagawa: Huh? But I don’t want you at ringside. Ever. It’s too dangerous. I’m not one of these people who like the idea of taking someone down to the ring, they could get hurt and I don’t want it happening to you, or the baby. You don’t know what some sick freak who’s trying to make a name for himself could try. Natalia sees the concern in Miyagawa’s eyes and backs down from the confrontation. Natalia: Ok, I won’t come to ringside. But I expect to get decent seats for when your in state and at all pay per views, ok? Miyagawa stands up again and shakes Natalia by the hand. Miyagawa: Deal. So where do you want to go for dinner tonight. Natalia: Don’t know, you choose. I think it’s your turn anyway. Miyagawa: How about somewhere special? Tell you what, you go get dressed up and I’ll pull some strings. Natalia looks at him flatly and cross. Natalia: What? I’m not dressed up already? Miyagawa looks back at her completely gob-smacked when she suddenly backhands him gently into the chest. Natalia: You Americans, no sense of humour at all. And with that Natalia, leaves the kitchen and Miyagawa grabs a phone probably with the intent to pull some of those aforementioned “strings”. And that is where we leave this one as we fade to black.
