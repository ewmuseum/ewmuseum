The Tank 8 - Death and Realization

17 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 17, 2002 Tuesday, July 16 - Day 11 “These fucking niggers,”scowls Harvey Blake as he watches a young black man stalk away through his lone eye. “They’re all the fucking same.” Like most inmates of Peter J. Tankerton maximum security prison, Harvey Blake is part of the work program. He makes a small amount of money to spend a few hours per day serving dinner in the cafeteria. He hates the job, however, because he’s forced to deal with - and be nice to - people of every race that are incarcerated in the Tank. “Harv,” says one of his co-workers, a young white man with short cut blond hair, “I’m going to take the trash back. Can you handle this by yourself for a while.” “Fuck you, Kenny!” Harvey responds, “I’m the boss around here and you should know by now that I take the trash back. You handle these fucking monkeys.” With that, Harvey Blake picks up a garbage bag and heads toward the back of the kitchen. The trash is always taken to a fairly large room behind the kitchen where city garbage men pick it up every day - escorted by armed guards. But before they come, there’s that split second where the man who takes the trash out can actually stand in the doorway at the back of the room and breath some fresh air - enjoy some sunshine. Unit B prisoners aren’t aloud outside like prisoners from most other Units, so for Harvey Blake, this is the best time of his day. After dropping the large garbage bag off in it’s designated spot, Harvey pushes open the heavy steel doors and takes a seat on the pavement outside. What a wonderful day, he thinks. The sun is shining - gleaming off the barbwire laced fence in the distance. He can see the watchtower from here and he knows the sniper that sits in the tower can see him - but no one’s ever gotten in trouble before for taking a short break in the sunshine. He can’t help but break out in a slight sweat under the hot California sun. He rubs his balding head and rubs the sweat off his face by stretching his white wifebeater tank top. “Nice day, isn’t it?” asks a voice from behind, sending shock through Harvey Blake’s body. He snaps his head around and finds the other occupant of the room is none other than Bruno Gasperoni. “I used to work the kitchen way back when. I think everyone who’s ever worked in here used to sneak back to get a minute outside. Well, at least those of us from Unit B.” “What the fuck are you doing back here?” Blake asks sternly, getting to the point. “The Hatt asked me to pay you a visit,” says Bruno as he steps out from a shadow and into the sunlight that blasts through the open doorway. “He did, did he?” Blake says with a chuckle. “Yeah,” Bruno says as he steps closer and leans on the open doorway. “One last job, you know. After you’re dead, I’m going to be moving up to management.” “Well,” Harvey sighs calmly, “lets get this over with then.” Blake stands up and lets the heavy door fall shut behind him. A moment later, he spots a small piece of sharp metal wrapped in a rag sitting in Bruno’s right hand, “Nice of you to come prepared.” Suddenly, old Bruno lashes out with the metal object, but his old body isn’t as quick as it used to be and Harvey is easily able to dodge the attempt. Blake, an oldschool type of fighter, raises his fists and begins to circle around Bruno. Bruno takes another swipe... another... another... “Shit,” Blake says with a smile, “you really are an old man, aren’t you?” Twenty years ago, ten years ago, probably even five years ago - Harvey Blake would already have been dead. But now, at fifty-six years of age, Bruno’s body doesn’t work the way it used to. Well into his forties, Harvey Blake is no spring chicken, but the ten years between their ages makes all the difference in the world. Bruno is quickly realizing that he’s no match for the one-eyed Nazi. After one more swipe, Blake grabs Bruno’s wrist with his right and sends a right fist crashing in with his left. Bruno stumbles back, dropping the knife-like object in the process. Harvey Blake moves in like a wolf, smiling at the scent of blood. In desperation, Bruno lunges out at Blake with his fists flailing, but his old body is too slow to do any damage. Harvey effortlessly dodges the attack and slams Bruno down to the concrete. “See what you get?” Blake says as he begins firing kicks into Bruno’s old midsection. The large Italian begins to spit blood. “See what you get when you fuck with me Bruno? Same thing as everyone else. I don’t give two fucks who you work for oldtimer! I’m Harvey Blake! You hear me cocksucker!? I’m HARVEY BLAKE!!!” Bruno lies motionless on the cold concrete floor with blood trickling from his mouth as a result of broken ribs and the possibility of many other internal injuries. Still, his massive chest heaves with life - not for long. Harvey scurries off in search of the knife-like object that Bruno earlier dropped. As soon as he finds it, he steps back over to Bruno. The dominant animal, Harvey stands over his victim and smiles. A second later, he slits the old man’s throat. *** From the Office of the Warden “Despite all attempts,” the Warden begins, “the fountain of youth has never been found. And age is a funny thing isn’t it? It’s the greatest disease known to man. It starts at a different age for everyone. Some start to feel it at thirty, some even at twenty-five - others can go all the way to fifty without ever being bothered by it. But the thing about age is... sooner or later, it’ll get us all.” *** “Don’t be so fucking smug!” sneers Geoffery Rankin, Unit B manager as he stares across his cluttered desk at Doug Hattoni. “I know you fucking did it, Doug - or had someone do it for you!” “I don’t know what you’re talking about.” “Fuck you, Doug - fuck you! You know damn well what I’m talking about! Two Italians start a brawl on the main floor and a minute later we find Boris Valinov - your enemy - dead in his cell!?” Rankin scowls at the old mobster, “You’re not getting away with this one, Doug. I’m going to find a way to pin this on you, and you’ll be stuck in solitary for the rest of your fucking life!” “Do what you want,” Doug says with a smile. “You won’t find anything. I had nothing to do with this.” “Don’t treat me like a fool!” exclaims Rankin. “You know you did this you sick bastard! You think we’re not going to put it all together? With Boris being killed almost simultaneously with the discovery of Bruno Gasperoni’s body?” Doug’s face turns pale, “Bruno’s body?” His voice shakes, “Bruno’s... dead?” Very quickly, young Geoffery Rankin realizes that this is the first Doug has heard of the news. “Shit, Doug - you didn’t know?” His voice is almost empathetic. “How?” Doug says with a trembling voice, “How did it happen?” “We were hoping you could tell us,” says Rankin, shaking his head and leaning back in his chair. “But if you don’t know...” “You found his body?” “City garbage men found it,” says Rankin, unsure of how to touch on the gruesome details of the story. “This afternoon, when they came to pick up the garbage. They accidentally tore open one of the bags and...” “Bruno’s body...” Doug hesitates, holding back tears, “was in the bag?” “Well,” Rankin says closing his eyes, “half of it. They found the other half later. Those garbage bags come from all over the Tank and no one knows Bruno’s last whereabouts. We have no idea where it came from. With lunch being served, prisoners are usually never where they’re supposed to be.” Doug puts his head down - he knows what happened. He sent Bruno out to kill Harvey Blake, but Blake got the upper hand. And now, Bruno has paid with his life. Doug Hattoni silently mourns the loss of his employee, and more importantly, his best friend. “You really knew nothing about this?” Rankin asks - he already knows the answer, and it makes him doubt his first thought that Doug was behind Boris Valinov’s murder. Doug responds with a low headshake as he’s unable to control the tears any longer. “If I may make a request...” he says quietly. Rankin nods, indicating that he’s open to anything. “I would like to attend the funeral,” says Hattoni, looking up at Rankin through tear-filled eyes. “Funeral?” Rankin sounds surprised. “Bruno’s been in prison for almost thirty years - do you really think they’re going to have a funeral.” Through tears, Doug smiles at Rankin. Doug knows that Bruno is considered a legend in underground circles of the Italian mafia. If not for Bruno Gasperoni, plenty of high ranking mobsters would be rotting in prison today. Instead, Bruno refused to testify against them and was sentenced to prison for the rest of his life - a martyr. “Well,” Doug sighs through his smile, “you know what they say about the mafia, Mr. Rankin - they never forget.” *** From the Office of the Warden “I’ve never liked the thought of ‘inconsecutive’ sentences,” says the Warden. “It’s like giving these guys a break. But these days, it’s becoming more and more the norm in the world of celebrities. Their lawyers will argue that hundreds, maybe even thousands, of people are working simply because their client is working. And that’s true. So you’ve got Robert Downey jr. leaving prison on the weekends to shoot a movie, and now...” The Warden sighs as he takes a seat behind his desk, “You’ve got Jason Snow leaving the Tank - a maximum security prison, mind you - every few days to put his criminal ass on television. Is that what our country is coming to?” *** Wednesday, July 17 - Day 12 “I caught Turmoil last night - well, the rerun this morning, actually,” begins Doug Hattoni as Jason Snow struts into the mobster’s cell just before noon. “Well done.” Last night on Turmoil, Snow turned on his partner, and friend, Rent-a-Villain with a vicious attack. Snow says nothing. He simply takes a seat on the bottom bunk of the cell - Bruno’s bunk - and notes that many of Bruno’s belongings have been moved from the cell. “Bruno’s been killed,” says Doug, stifling Snow’s curiosity. “Killed? By who?” “Fucking Nazis,” sneers Doug, circling the question as always. “I’ve never liked the bastards, but I’ve never had reason to do anything about it. Harvey Blake killed Bruno yesterday - the one eyed cocksucker. This does, of course, mean war.” “Blake’s still alive?” “Bruno was old, God rest his soul,” sighs Doug. “I should never have sent him after Blake. I should have sent you. But I wasn’t confident that you could get a job of that magnitude done so early. However, after seeing the way you killed Boris Valinov, I now see that I was mistaken.” Snow is shocked, “Wait a minute - I didn’t kill Boris.” “Huh?” Doug asks, lying comfortably on his top bunk, staring up at the ceiling. “I gave him some lumps, but I didn’t kill him - at least, I don’t think I did...” Snow says, wondering if maybe Boris died from the injuries later on. “Kid,” Doug says, sitting up, “Boris was found in his cell Monday night, before you left, with his throat slit.” Snow’s eyes grow wide - he didn’t even bring a weapon to Boris’ cell. “The question is,” Doug continues, “if you didn’t kill him - who did?”
