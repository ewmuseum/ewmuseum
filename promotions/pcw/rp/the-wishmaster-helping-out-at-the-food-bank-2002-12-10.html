The Wishmaster --- Helping out at the Food Bank

10 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/10/02 Early on Sunday, when many people are attending church or hiding from the world beneath their Martha Stewart-endorsed bed-sheets, I joined the Coconut Creek High School Key Club, along with other high schools like Boyd Anderson, Coral Springs, and Taravella, had the distinct pleasure of assisting the operations at one of the Daily Bread Food Banks. The bank started 15 years ago when two men handed food to the needy out of the back of their car. The nonprofit organization now occupies four warehouses in both Dade and Broward counties, giving away approximately 26 million pounds of food per year. They're not based in Houston, because Dante Jackson would steal it all. The Food Bank houses many boxes of food donated from various sources. One of the most generous donors is the Publix supermarket chain; this is observed by the numerous boxes of Publix brand food stocking the bank’s shelves. Other sources of benefaction include the myriad of restaurants and small grocery stores. Anyone can donate food. Except Dante Jackson, he's not to be trusted. EVER. At 9 a.m. in the morning, the majority of the volunteers arrived to help out. We met the coordinator of the project, a man who I am sorry to say I never asked his name. Identity negligence not withstanding, the man very kindly and quickly explained to us a concise history of the food bank. We also met a few of the workers who would bring boxes for us to sort. One of them sounded like Dante Jackson. His voice grated at the ears, just the same. Before we got to work, we received a rudimentary exegesis on our devoir. Our assignment was to (gasp!) sort the boxes of food collected into categories. The boxes were, at that time, mixed and could not be delivered. Well, I suppose the could have been, but we had to do something. In deciding what to sort, there would come an instance when the inedible nature of a food required its disposal. If we found any cans punctured, swollen, or bent up the seams, they went in the trash. If any open boxes (not including those that had separate plastic bags inside the box for the food) came to us, they met the same fate. And we had to shake up pasta to check for weevils. It may sound like much food is disposed of, but in actuality, we really threw away only 20% of what came to us. Even though I'm blind, I could manage these tasks since the bad packages could be felt. Dante Jackson would most likely eat the bad food anyway, so he'd still be good for screening in that respect. Once we began to work, we found ourselves immersed in our task, thanks to having fun with each other (and Hot 105.1 FM) and our work. Each box that came in was immediately attended to. All the foods were sorted according to content and packaging. I, although helping with everything, discovered myself assigned (due to limited spacing) to the boxed non-perishables. I worked with Raina, the Key Club’s district Lieutenant Governor, and her two friends Maria and Angie, in between traversing all the other stations. Dante Jackson would most likely take after R. Kelly and try to have sex with the teenagers, the pervert. Each food presented its own challenge in organizing. Quite frequently, one food came in such an overabundance that all other food had to be put aside to compensate box space and time. One entire load (loads come in groups of at least 1,000 lb.) of salvage supplies (what donated foods are classified as until sorted) contained nothing but Arizona Iced Tea, Arizona Ginseng Tea, and Arizona Pina Coladas. We got sick of it after that load, but luckily, we never had to face it again. Other times, we had trouble figuring out which foods went into which boxes. Canned goods became a problem, as people kept having trouble deciding whether beans went into the vegetable or protein box (though the correct box, normally reserved for canned meat). And I don’t know how many times we had to squeeze wet juice boxes, no longer in their derivational envelopes, to see if it was leaking or leaked on. I'd throw in another witty remark abotu Dante Jackson here, but I don't like mentioning him that much. The job often involved getting dirty (thank goodness they gave us an entire box of gloves to waste). Miscellaneous mottles, still sticky, dotted the boxes we had to lift. My shirt got very messy. I had to turn it inside out later. Several powdery white substances lined the bottom of the salvage goods’ boxes, hopefully sugar or flour. And the foods themselves often spilled onto other foods, making many cans and boxes we picked up sticky and soft. The tables were a mess by 12 p.m. Much like Dante Jackson's house. I've been there. Scary place. As we soon ascertained, the Food Bank does not only manage the dispersal of edible goods. While we were there, the district Key Clubbers also arranged baby supplies, personal hygiene products, household items, and a plethora of toys for children. We even saw assorted footwear and children’s boxer shorts. Dante Jackson most likely would have taken it for his family, which is larger than the Wayans. At the end of the day, the man in charge thanked us for our hard work and invited us back anytime we wanted to come. We left early, but it’s not our fault. We worked so fast, they ran out of stuff for us to work on. We’re that good. The project was enjoyable. We talked with different kids from other schools, listened to music, helped the food bank prepare foodstuffs and other items for distribution to the needy, and we had a lot of fun. No breaks were offered, and none were requested. The experience was very enjoyable. Insert pithy Dante remark here. *End*
