Give me my life back!

12 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 12, 2002 Vilas re-entered the training grounds, and immediately took to the center of the darkened arena. He sat in the darkness and waited, knowing full well that the council would return in a few short minutes from their sleep and take this facility by storm. Some light seeped through the open spaces in the closed windows, just enough to see the floor and the circle that enclosed the fighting area. Vilas stood in the center and waited. They have no other choice. They will give me back what is right fully mine, and then, and only then will I be able to finally rid myself of this wrong doing. They are no leaders, but simply users to put them self over bigger in this world. For years many have fallen to this so-called enlightened men, but that will son very well change. Vilas closed his eyes and waited in his moist clothing, hopping someone would come soon to finally re-pay the years took off his life. Thoughts ran through his mind, waiting for an answer, hopping for the key or the grip on the moment. This was no easy task, and changing the decision made by one of the high council was not an easy task to achieve. But still, he had to try and win back what was his. Give him the freedom to move where we wanted to, and train where he wished. I have goals, desires, needs and wants. Shouldn’t I be aloud to go through with them all? I still have much to learn, and quiet frankly, I don’t think I can learn anything here. The world has to many possible answers, keys and journeys I want to attempt. These men will not keep me back, just because they want what is best for Thailand. The fact is they want me back because of what I do for this temple and them as a group. The words played through his thoughts over and over again. He could still remember the day his parents brought him her, believing he was pure evil and needed to be taught right and brought up with honor. The contract was made and signed by his parents and all Vilas could do was stand back and watch his life be decided for him. “Your son will be gratefully welcomed here. Don’t worry, he will learn to appreciate everything that we stand for.” Cunning words, but of course they would never sink in. He could never be forced into their beliefs because that is what they are. Other people’s own ideas and thoughts, and Vilas wanted nothing to do with others ideas besides his own. He would not change just because he was told to. “He will receive the best training, and when he is complete, he will represent Thailand and be a glorious figure in this world!” How dare they decided my future, my destiny and my fate. They always wondered what drove me to not believing in a higher power; well that gives them the answer. Always having my decisions made for me puts me in a position that I don’t want to be a part of. Those fools think they can change me, when in reality they are no different from the slaves they own. Vilas eyes shoot open, and his body began to shake with fear. His life was in his own hands, and there was no way to prove him wrong. It was just a matter of time now, but of course time was always against him in every shape and form. It always wants to cut short the window of opportunity and take any chance at a life away. Talk about unfair, how does one go about pleading for his life, when he only has a few minutes to spare? Simply make them listen is all I had to do. Easier said then done, but I am trained enough, I am strong enough and dammit I am brave enough, They will see it my way, or there will be no end in sight. Chances are good just as they are bad, but luck has no need in this game. Only truth and the right to move on in life and become something greater. This isn’t just a game; it’s my own existence at stake. Vilas’s looks up as the torches that line the walls begin to egnight into large bursts of flames, shooting out from dragoon’s mouths as they sit on the wall tilt to the side. This was the time for the council to get their own meditation time in, away from the students who barley ever did see the people in charge. One by one, in lines of two, men dressed in robes walked out from a door that swung open like all the rest. Their heads were down in prayer as they made their way around the circle in which Vilas was standing in. They still hadn’t noticed his presence This is it right here. There is no turning back now. One way in, one way out. The circle closed in on Vilas, and very quietly Vilas made a turn, trying to find a way out if he needed to free himself. In one swift motion all the council raised their heads and starred Vilas down. He looked back at them all and studied each other, trying to find something that may look like an edge. But this battle was all mental and no physical. “Who dare disturb are spirits,” cried out one of the councilmen looking at Vilas. Vilas looked back at him, and approached the councilman still starring at him with widening eyes. He looked the man square in the face and pushed the breathe out of his lungs and into the mans face. The water still drip from his clothing as he stands in a pool of water, not making a move. “GIVE ME BACK MY LIFE,” Vilas shouted, starling the councilman and pushed him back a few steps. Vilas was here for business and not their rituals. And councilman tried to rejoin the circle but Vilas blocked his progression forward. He simply kept walking forward ever so slowly, pushing the man back farther and farther away from the others. His eyes stayed locked as the anticipation started to grow. “You dare keep me from what is mine.” Vilas pushed his finger into the councilman’s chest, pushing him back more. “You think you can own me like a toy.” The man pulled his arms up to block any attack that may come. “I should rip you to shreds for what you have done to me.” The councilman fell to his knees and begged for forgiveness. His hands prayed while the rest of the council watched on as their leader gave into this little man telling them what to do. The leader started to weep, as Vilas came closer and closer. Vilas brought his fist up and held it, ready to strike at any moment. “I’m sorry Mr. Lavagna, but you can not leave.” Vilas was shocked to hear this man say this in his situation. Vilas picked the council leader up and pushed him up against the wall that they had backtracked to. Their faces collided together as Vilas came close to whisper in the leader ear. “Give… me… what… is… mine…” The leader shock his head in decline and simply walked away, brushing by Vilas moving back into the circle. Vilas’s emotions began to build past the boiling point. His heart rate jumped and his pulse raced as his mind went over and over with ideas. Vilas let out a roar and quickly started to rush the leader, knocking him over, breaking the council’s traditions. “GIVE ME BACK MY LIFE NOW.” Vilas got up and aloud the council leader to get up as well. His breath pushed out his body while the leader brushed the dirt off his robes. The anger on his face was enough to tell the story for years. He looked up at Vilas and both had the same expression on their face of sheer anger. The leader pointed towards the large doors that Vilas first entered. “You want your life back, then have it.” Vilas let a sigh out and turned around and headed for the doors. “But know that if you leave this temple you will be banished for ever. No longer will Thailand see you as one of there own, but rather and lowly immigrant.” Vilas headed towards the doors and watched as they swung open and made a large crash and echo against the walls. He peered down the hall and then looked back at the leader who was still angered that he was losing one of his former students. “I would rather be a lowly immigrant then master that couldn’t beat a student.” Vilas let a final smile out before walking through the large doors. The leader kept his eyes on Vilas, waiting for him to change his mind. The large doors closed and the opportunity was gone. ______________________________________________________________________________________________ “I don’t believe you done that,” she said as they both stood by the fire, listening to him recall his story. He walked away from her and cleared his mind of any distractions. She looked into the fire and also looked for the right answer. “I know what I must do, and staying here is not the option that will get me anywhere.” He went over to the coats and grabbed his, now dry from hanging for many hours. He rapped it around his body and looked over at her still starring in the fire. “All those years spent training, gone because they wouldn’t give you what is yours.” She looked up just in time to watch him nod in agreement. “Where will you go now,” she asked, walking over to him and helping him get packed up again. “I am going to do what ever my life wants. No more do I obey someone else, but now I make my own choices and decide my fate.” He turned the doorknob and opened the door into the poring rain. He walked out and the water gently hit his body and washed down to the gorund. He stepped out of her yard and continued to walk towards the nearest town. “Will you ever return.” He stopped and looked back and smiled. “If there is a competition, you can bet I will be there.” With that he continued on his way. She smiled and closed the door, locking it behind her and shouting out any attempt to re-enter.
