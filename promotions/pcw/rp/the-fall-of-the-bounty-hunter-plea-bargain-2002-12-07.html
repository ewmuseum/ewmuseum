The fall of the Bounty Hunter - Plea Bargain

7 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: December 7, 2002 [We begin at Pearson international airport after Alliances. Seeing the fall of his team, and literally going the rest of the match by himself, Eddie Mandrake, is a little sore, and is limping. We head to gate ninety-one in Terminal Two, where we see Eddie, sporting a fresh bandage on his head, and limping off the plane with his bag. He goes down the hall seeing everyone in the departures concourse ready to leave on their trips to wherever they may end up. Then, Eddie makes a left turn leading to an escalator going down into the customs area of the arrivals level. Eddie is the first in line at booth number three where there is a female customs officer. He presents his identification to the officer, the officer looks at the Ontario drivers license and sees a man with slicked back hair and glasses who appears to be Eddie, but she looks up to see the spiky haired, and five o’clock shadowed who is Eddie.] Officer: Mr. Mandrake, the police and your lawyer are waiting for you after you get your bags. Do you have anything to declare? Eddie: Nope. Officer: Move along, NEXT! [Eddie moves from the booth to the arrivals baggage carrousels. He sees his bag almost immediately and removes it from the carrousel. He bolts out the door, to see his lawyer, Sean Putz, and two police officers at the exit door.] Eddie: Arraignment time. Putz: Actually no... We’ve arranged a meeting with the Crown Attorney, Arraignment’s tomorrow. How was the match? Eddie: You didn’t see Alliances? I kicked ass! I eliminated at least ten people during the match! God! Putz: Sorry, man. I’m not a wrestling fan. We should go to see what the Crown has against us, and see if they have any sort of offer or plea bargain for us. Eddie: Alright then. Let’s book. [All of them at that moment leave the airport and get into a taxi. That taxi, oddly enough has a police escort. The maze of roads that lead from Terminal two of the airport, essentially lead into a dead end. They lead right into rush hour traffic. On the weave of roads that connect highways 401, and 427, it is a vein of metal that creeps along slowly. Eddie, in the left seat in the taxi looks out the window to view the scope of the airport lands. The giant Molson brewery, and the many commercial complexes and hotels that surround the area, litter the Metropolitan Toronto landscape. The people in the cab remain silent as they try to carve their way through the vein of metal. Eventually, they hit somewhat open highway. The 427, the western roadway that leads to the lakeshore and the other highways, and major roadways that stream into the downtown core. The taxi, which contains Eddie and his lawyer, traverses the 427 and goes onto the Queen Elizabeth Way that is the major roadway that essentially connects Toronto to the Canada/U.S. border in Niagara Falls. Here it’s mainly industrial complexes littering the lakeshore. Skipping ahead another twenty minutes, we FINALLY arrive at the Crown Attorney’s office at the University Avenue Courthouse. The courthouse itself is in somewhat of a bad position, considering that it is right next to Toronto City Hall, The Eaton Centre, several hospitals, and a few subway stations. A Perfect area for a criminal to wreak havoc. Inside the courthouse itself, it is very quiet, and very…well…white. Every wall that still doesn’t have faux wood-panelling from the seventies has been painted the simple dirty white from the cheap paint that the government uses. They walk through the halls of justice, heading for the office of the Crown Attorney. They quickly enter the office, and see the attorney sitting at his desk behind a giant bookshelf containing many texts of law. The man, who doesn’t look intimidating at all, is writing something on a sheet of paper. He looks up to see the defendant, and his counsel.] Attorney: Mr. Mandrake I presume? Eddie: Yeah. Attorney: Please, have a seat. [Eddie and Sean take a seat in front of the attorney’s desk.] Attorney: Okay…I’ll get right to the point. Mr. Putz, your client committed assault and battery in front of twenty-five witnesses. Putz: All evidence is circumstantial at this point. Attorney: Also, we have video of the act being committed and official medical records plus reports from the on hand EMTs and officers. [The attorney tosses a file to Sean; Sean quickly opens it and looks to see a few legal documents. The attorney has a seat in his comfortable leather chair.] Attorney: You know how this city feels about you and your kind. I’ll get a conviction with my eyes closed. All that you would have to do…is tell us all of your compatriots are in the Greater Toronto Area, and even more so, in Ontario… Putz: I have to speak with my client regarding this. Attorney: Okay…Let’s see…I’ll give you…one day, and only one day. After that, I’ll find you and seek out the maximum penalty, I’ll see to it. [The stern, yet someone dead face of the attorney stares into Eddie’s eyes. The youthful and ever increasingly angered face of Eddie stares into the attorney. Both men, looking for weaknesses in the other, but both finding nothing.] Putz: Eddie…Let’s go. [Both men get up and walk out headed back to the taxi. Putz can barely keep up with Eddie.] Putz: EDDIE! Eddie: WHAT THE FUCK YOU WANT?! Putz: They have a case that is pretty much bullet-proof. Eddie: What are my options? Putz: Go to trial and plead guilty. Cut a deal. Eddie: What’s in the deal? Putz: About one hundred hours of Anger Management Classes. [Eddie’s eyes grow big with amazement.] Eddie: That’s all? Putz: Yup. My advice would be to cut the deal. However, the final decision is yours. Sleep on it. You must be tired. [Sean and Eddie walk back outside and enter the taxi and it speeds off as we fade to black. But return about five seconds later back to day light, the Next Day. Sean walks into the office of the crown attorney. The attorney sits there going through more paper work. Sean quickly speaks.] Sean: What are your terms? Attorney: One hundred hours Anger Management Classes to take place on job, $20,000 fine. Sean: Done. I’ll let Eddie know of the terms of it later. Attorney: Did Eddie agree to this? Sean: He did. I’ll see to it personally that he makes it to his first on site AMS on Tuesday. Attorney: If he doesn’t show…You’ll be held responsible. [Sean’s eyes widen at his thought of going to jail, as he walks out and we fade to black.]
