Internal Exile

23 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"How long has it been? Two years… three years? What day is it? Living in exile does strange things to you… particularly if you’re not interested in the outside world. For me… the outside world holds very little. What has that world given me? What has it bestowed upon me? Nothing. I’m an internal exile…" The sky was clear on this Siberian summer day, it was a warm day, roughly sixty degrees. It hadn’t been this warm for weeks. The gray landscape, which one Russian Bear called home, spread out for miles, as far as the eye could see. A small forest permeated one avenue out of the wasteland. Far in the distance, and opposite of it, smoke rose from a small cabin, which could have been mistaken for virtually anything. And before us, trudging heavily towards an unknown location was Ivan Stanislav. The grizzled, massive Soviet soldier, who once stood before a crowd of thousands and commanded such attention, quietly walked through the melting snow. With a fuzzy Ushanka on his head, an overcoat, and gray pants and boots, the Russian Bear appeared just that: Russian. His beard had grown long, hanging far off of his chin and whipping left and right with the wind currents. The battle of color in the beard had been fought, and neither gray nor black had won, but rather a pristine yet poignant white. A stark symbol of the changes the man had gone through. Humming a song deep in his resonant, barrel chest, Ivan Stanislav walked towards a large, thawed lake with a dock. Snow crunched and was pulverized under his tremendous weight and while he was hunched over walking, he still appeared as a giant amidst the quiet of the world. Upon his arrival at the dock, the Russian Bear dropped a bag he was holding and rose to his full height of over seven feet. Inhaling deeply, he took in the smell of his Motherland and smiled. "Though all things change… my Motherland always smells the same…" With a fluid motion, Stanislav tore open his jacket and let it fall to the ground. Beneath the wool garment, Stanislav wore nothing on his chest. His pants here long and black, reaching down to his boots, and then were tucked inside of them. A pair of red suspenders rose up from his pants and slung over his pale shoulders, down his back and locked their jaws into the waist of his pants, ensuring their security. His chest was as much of a barrel as always. On his right shoulder blade was a huge, nasty, pockmarked scar. Over his heart, the tattoo of the Soviet hammer and sickle. He threw off his Ushanka, revealing a head of silver hair. Ivan flexed his arms and stretched his neck. Leaning down, the Bear untied his boots and kicked them off, and then threw off his socks. Cracking his neck, Stanislav thundered down the dock and dove into the cold water, eliciting a massive splash from the still, cool water. It was so quiet… so peaceful here… Stanislav swam to the other side of the lake, and then turned around and started to backstroke back the other way. His skin turned a blue sheen, but he did not shiver. Crystals of ice formed on his soggy beard, and his hair became matted as he swam quietly. "Sometimes, I wonder what is going on in the world. What are the governments like… what of the Terrorists? How is Communism… or Capitalism? Sometimes… I even think of wrestling…" ---------------------------------------------- Ivan Stanislav’s cabin had served as his home for over two years… and a fine home it had been. With a small kitchenette, a study, bedroom, living room, an attic, and one extra room, it had served him well. Of course, various modifications had to be made. All the doorways were increased in height and the floorboards were reinforced to support his weight. All the chairs were made bigger, by Ivan’s hand, and the tables’ height was increased and its length was shortened. There was no use having any extra table space for any visitors. And so, The Russian Bear sat at his table and sipped on some soup. The wind howled outside now as darkness blotted out his world, leaving him in his quiet cabin alone with a steaming pot of soup and a soft fire. A lantern hung from the ceiling, casting erratic light across the wooden household. There was no use wasting the generators power. Rising from his table, having finished his soup, Stanislav glanced to a door across the dining area. He licked his lips and wiped his mouth with a napkin, and then dropped the crumpled mass to the table. Dressed in a similar manner as before, he now wore a black tank top to match his black pants and red suspenders. Ivan placed his hands on his hips and looked around. It was so quiet. Alexei used to visit… months ago perhaps? He would bring Ivan letters from fans of his old wrestling career… or from Russians that missed their hero. He threw them away. Ivan scoffed at the thought of returning, or to think that people had still thought of him. Adventuring out into America had proven how sad of a life this man had lived… and the folly of his ultimate goals. He had lost everything doing that: his heart, his soul, his country, and his dignity. Ivan had turned away Alexei angrily. He had nothing outside of his four walls… nothing at all. Yet he still stared at the door across the way. Ivan walked slowly toward the door; his heavy footsteps caused the floorboards to groan under his tremendous weight. Reaching for the doorknob, he gripped it and paused. Inhaling deep and thinking hard, he closed his eyes and opened the door. Inside was darkness, a room without windows or any noticeable form of illumination. Ivan walked inside and shut the door, engulfing himself in darkness. Then, he lit a match and put it to a lantern above his head, and the room was made visible. All around Ivan hung pictures, plaques, medals, and honors of various deeds and events in his life. A far table stood which held trophies and objects of his past. Whirling around, Ivan gazed upon them, having not entered this room for over a year. - A picture of a young Ivan, clean-shaven in a Soviet uniform in Afghanistan, towering over his comrades-in-arms in a group photo. - Ivan in the hospital after being shot in the shoulder and a woman sitting at his bedside… his mother? - A picture of Ivan standing in front of the Kremlin, saluting it. - Ivan shaking hands with Alexei Ruslan in Red Square. - Ivan locked up with Tyler Nelson in the wrestling ring. - A roaring Stanislav walking down the entryway and pointing at some unknown opponent. - A picture of Ivan, holding the PCW World Title over his head, with Alexei Ruslan holding another PCW World Title over his head, cocky grins on their faces. - Ivan stomping on an opponent in the OSW. - Stanislav chasing a penguin down a hallway with a look of both fear and rage in his eyes. He gazed around and paused at one picture in particular: A picture of Tempest, winking at the camera playfully. He took a step forward and touched the glass of the picture. Beside it, encased in another small frame, was a red feather. Taking a step backwards, Ivan closed his eyes and spun around slowly… the sounds of his past thundered in his head. -gunshots- “I am the Russian Bear!! The PCW is history!!” “Always… always Think Red!!” “United forever in friendship and labor…” “You cannot stop what you cannot harm!!” “Jeremy Howard… your days are numbered!” “Trashcan Man is a disgrace to the proletariat!” “Nyet!!” “DYAAHAAHAA!!" Ivan froze and his eyes shot open. Glancing around quickly, he swallowed hard, staring again at the picture of Tempest. And then blew the light out… engulfing himself in darkness again.
