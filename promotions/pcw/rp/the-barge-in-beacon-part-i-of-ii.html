The 'Barge In' Beacon (Part I of II)

Date: 29.11.02 (#7 FF) Making his way down the hotel staircase, Abner feels a bit of pride and accomplishment, and deservedly so. This one time ex-con, former cult leader and well-known trouble maker has managed to turn his life around. From the streets, to primetime television, Abner has come a long, long way. But not long enough. Though, he’s thankful to be a PCW wrestler…..and by all means, he truly is, Abner still wouldn’t feel quite right going home with a less tonight. He’s content to be on the roster, but he’s itching to be at the top of it. You see, to Abner, his life thus far has meant nothing: a meaningless void of this and that, and spiced up here and there by the other thing. Overall, though he’s overcame, he knows that deep down inside there is still more potential. He didn’t think he was good enough to be a professional wrestler, but PCW, the greatest wrestling federation in the world, saw it. And he still doesn’t quite believe that he’s world champion caliber, but hey, maybe someone else sees it? And if someone else does see it, than there’s always hope. And where there’s hope, there’s a will, and where there’s will, there’s a way. And Kai is the way. Kai is the doorway standing between Abner and his career. His career in professional wrestling relies on starting off on the right foot. A nice boost in the beginning most certainly leads to an at least somewhat satisfying career. But Abner’s not interested in a “somewhat satisfying” career, no….. That’s horseshit! ‘Cause Abner’s in for the “real deal”. And whether he actually makes it or not, he’ll be damned if he doesn’t at least give it a good try. By-Jesus Christ, there ain’t no one out there who’s gonna stop this son-va-bitch from getting’ where he wants to go! All them other P-C-Dub trucks, they can just get on outta rud! With fierce determination, and an paralleled drive in his heavy heart, Abner wishes with his life to rise up from the lows of society and sit upon the mountain. You see it’s not just PCW, it’s life. They said I’d never make it. They said I was too dumb…..said I was too jumpy…..said I wasn’t born of the right blood…..and they said I don’t have it in me…..well, here you me world, you can carry on your everyday business just as please, and just like ya always done, but never, nu-uh, not ever again will you trample over this Goddamn man! No sir, I may be part of the world, but it don’t control me. Society thinks I’m stupid, they branded me as an outcast…..so be it, ‘cause I don’t care, I know I’m ain’t…..I know I’m…..I’m not…..Abner buttons up the front of his vest as he heads out the door. It may be El Paso, Texas, but it’s still chilly. Taking a left, heading down the molded sidewalk of civilization, Abner feels alone. You see, back in Vermont, back in the woods…..the wilderness, he felt peace. But nom, not here. With all the traffic, people, industry…..pollution of every type: physical, mental and even spiritual, Abner could not stand “civilization”. To him, civilization is quite the opposite term of what he’d use to describe it. No, it’s not “civilization”……it ain’t “civilization” ‘cause it sure as hell ain’t civilized! And his point is well taken. In the wilderness, there are many more laws; laws mind you, that go unbroken. You can but a new thermometer, but you can’t change the whether. You can buy hiking gear, but you can’t build a mountain. But, very unfortunately, today is a time when you certainly can “buy” a mountain though. And what a shame. Yes sir, human beings, what nasty, nasty creatures. They think animals are barbaric? Well just look at them. Do animals get in groups of hundreds of thousands, march up and shoot one another? No! No, but we do….. He pulls his red and black vest in tighter around his body, but not for physical comfort, no, for mental. This old plad hunting jacket reminds him of his childhood years spent walking, riding and living in the old Green Mountains of Vermont. Yes, those were the day. But they’re over now, and he’s got to get focused…..other, much more tangible threats, such as that of Kai, come to mind. Picking up his stride, Abner continues on down the street. He takes in a deep breath of “fresh” air as attempts so neutralize the various odors of the city. But no, he can’t, and instead he is only rewarded for his efforts by watery eyes and a bad, bas taste on the end of his tongue. In this smoggy atmosphere, his dazzling green eyes have lost their glow. They seem almost a dirty, slimy brown now, and in Abner’s mind, probably blend in with the agents that bleed out beneath his feet. Looking down, he has further reason to believe this assumption. The putrid smell of an open sewer duct forces Abner’s nerves to kick in as they manually direct him to the other side of the sidewalk. Chemical agents, and who knows how many, are down there. But ah well, after living with it for your whole life, Abner’s sure that the inhabitants are probably used to it by now. Which of course they are. But I ain’t….. His thick, brown steeled-toes crunch against various wastes materials as the PCWer unconsciously begins to drag his feet. His dirty blue jeans, for some reason, seem somewhat clean today. Maybe he washed them? After the drinking incident Abner had had earlier, maybe he was forced (for obvious reasons) to get the laundry done. Finally noticing his lagging footwear, Abner purposely picks them up now, carefully putting one foot in front of the other. Picking up the pace, Abner moves further and further down the mysterious streets of El Paso, and further and further away from the comfort of his hotel. And then, like some kind of biblical revelation, it dawned on him. He had no clue where he was going. Originally planning on taking a short walk, Abner had veered considerably off track and decided that it was best to stop. So Abner, with his good sense of direction, stops, turns and scans the horizon. More familiar with the woods than the city, navigation will not be easy for him. When stumps and ferns are your normal landmarks, hotels and Laundromats all kind of look the same. So the city, in a blur, is quite a puzzle to Abner’s mind. Shrugging, he decides that “this way’s as good as any”, and takes another left. He doesn’t really care about whether or not he gets lost here though, because he could always signal a taxi, and at any given moment be on his way back to the hotel…..or arena….. Yes, yes….Goddamn Kai……my very good friend, I will certainly be seeing you tonight….. And suddenly, while aimlessly parading down the streets of El Paso, a hand beckons for him….. It’s beacon. A beacon of hope, a beacon of light, a beacon of….alcohol. Yes! To see his hickish delight, a bar seemingly pops out of nowhere and saves the day for Abner. A small wooden place, carved like a log cabin, a big pink, neon sign above the doorway reads “The Barge-In”. Yee-haw! Wasting no time, the eager young wrestler rushes forward toward his source of delight. His newly found friend is certainly welcomed at this stage in Abner’s life: for what better a time to drink than right before your first match as a professional wrestler? Exactly! Ah yeah….here we go….. Abner makes his way up the steps, throws open the door and dives through the entranceway.“Howdy.” A Polite young cowboy tips his hat in Abner’s general driection. Receiving a dozen or so similar responses, Abner looks around the room. Peering intently into their faces Abner notices that they all kind of look the same. About fifteen or so white males, all wearing various colored cowboy hats, and all wearing leather cowboy boots. Abner, slightly unnerved about the present situation, does his best to fit in. Letting out a mellow dramatic “howdy…..”, the wrestler takes his seat. A couple chuckles ring out from the crowd as they notice his Yankee accent. Taking a stool nearest the first man to speak to him, Abner felt a little more comfortable. Lighten up…..yer here for some Goddamn alcohol, and ‘sides, the frigin Civil War’s been over for over a hundred years….. “So what’s your name, son?” The first man asks. He sports a tight black mustache, short brown hair and seems as little darker skinned than the rest. “Abner.” “Abner, eh? Well Abner who?” “Abner….” “No, I mean your last name boy…..don’t cha have a last name?” Abner shrugs, and as if to avoid the situation, motions for the bar tender. An upity little man with a lot of energy immediately comes rushing forward. He’s wearing a white apron with a small blue nametag: “Jasper”. “Howdy, partner” Jasper lets out, “What can I do for ya, son?” “Shot a whiskey, please.” Abner politely replies to the offer. “Certainly, sir.” Bowing, the man steps away and then ducks underneath the counter. Emerging a few moments later, he has returned with a clear shot glass in his hand. Setting the small bottle of potion in front of the customer, he again, bows and then turns to leave. “Thanks.” As always, Abner toasts to his future, lifts the potion into the air, and downs it in a single gulp. “Whoa, better take it easy there partner…..” “Huh?” Pointing, “That there whiskey, ‘taint water you know.” And again, Abner shrugs it off, but then, turning back to face the man, seems to have changed his opinion a bit.“So, what’s your name?” “Jack, Jack Welder…..pleasure to meet ya, Yankee….” “Nice to meet you too…..” ---------------------------------------- And then, before too long, the two are engaged in quite an elaborate conversation…. And Abner, apparently forgetting about his match up, has decided to drink the afternoon away….. To Be Continued…..
