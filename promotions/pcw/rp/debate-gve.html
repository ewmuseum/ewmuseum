Debate - GvE.

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Sun. - Mar. 31, 2002 The musty darkness seems to swirl around as if a corporeal form in and of its own. The air is thick and cold with a kind of metallic flavor, able to be rolled on the palate to completely be absolved in the absoluteness of the black. A voice resonates, echoing through the murk and giving one the impression of small, enclosed confines. Voice: “So, it seems we have been granted time together by fate. I propose we make the best of this and engage in some lively conversation.” The voice, rich and deep, bound together with the kind off wisdom that all men should hope to achieve at some point along the mortal coil. Another Voice: “indeed, it seems as it is so. I accept your proposal of conversational pleasantries. However, perhaps we should take it a step further and exercise our minds in order to stave off the kinds of maladies that could befall the fragile human mind in such conditions as ours.” The second voice, tinged with a British flavor, also manages to convey a deep understanding of the human condition. One can imagine the owner of the first voice smiling at the counter-proposal, a single eyebrow raised in intrigue. Voice 1: “By exercise you mean a debate? Excellent. I concur as I can already feel myself succumbing to the horrors of consequence. Shall I determine the topic?” Voice 2: “Please, be my guest.” Wood grates on mental for the briefest of moments, and a silence punctures the still air. Voice 1: “Let us debate the existence and good and evil in the world: is the dichotomous relationship merely a societal construct or an absolute?” Voice 2: “It is an absolute. You are either good or you are evil.” Voice 1: “But in the world we know of, there can be no absolutes. The cold hand of science has taught us that so well.” Voice 2: “Indeed, but we are debating about ethical and possibly heavenly edicts. Can science prove or disprove the very intentions stored away in my soul?” Voice 1: “Indeed, it is a foamy discussion. But it is the society that creates the moral code that one must adhere to, for what is considered apt in one can certainly be construed and demonic in another.” Voice 2: “Ah, but that very hand of science which you previously referred to has researched this topic in depth, and it has been shown that the definitions of ‘good’ and of ‘evil’ vary little from world communities. While the actions themselves may be abhorred, the intentions behind them are often quite honorable.” Voice 1: “You speak of the Zambezi cannibal tribes?” Voice 2: “I do.” Voice 1: (huff) “Preposterous. Throughout the ages the path to evil has been paved with somewhat honorable intentions. Why, even genocide can be rigorously defended as a good intent, but it would be doubtful to find a scholar willing to take up the position that genocide is an act of pure intent. A society that deems this a tolerable act falls into the spectrum of evil.” Voice2: “You take my point too far. I was merely illustrating that intent can be honorable in the face of what may seem a act of evil. In the case of the Zambezi’s, devouring the flesh of an already fallen adversary is a traditional and religious ceremony.” Voice 1: “Ah, we get to religion, do we? The ultimate purveyor of value and moral code in the world.” Voice 2: “And the fact that all religions pose forth a set of code all remaining true to each other helps to sustain the argument that good and evil are inherent.” Voice 1: “How so? Have we not seen religion used in horrible ways to mask the cruel and vile acts of man as often as redeeming them?” Voice 2: “Manipulated men trying to follow the edicts of their leaders, who they believe to be conduits to their gods. One cannot fault the masses, but the evil in the hearts of the men at the top.” Voice 1: “But if good and evil is to be absolute, then how can this be? Surely, the knowledge of what is considered good is ingrained in all of us.” Voice 2: “It is, but the human mind is, at best, malleable to those capable of human chess. They become pawns in the grand game of the evil leaders, further lending to my point that they believe their actions good and persist. Though they may commit terrible crimes against humanity, they have been manipulated into believing their cause just.” Voice 1: “But would that not be a society view then? It is the morals imposed by the society they belong to that set the table from which they dine.” Voice 2: “I don’t believe so due to the fact that it is the evil of the leaders themselves and their skills of cultivating their broods mindset.” A minute of silence. Voice 1: “I cannot agree. There are always shades in between.” Voice 2: “Only when viewed through the prism of the human mind. In divinity, it is either good or bad. But let’s leave it at that, and agree to disagree. I would hate to have personal conflict come between us in this time of crisis.” Voice 1: “Splendidly argued, my friend.” Voice 2: “It was your wry insight that pushed me, my friend.” Another minute of silence. Voice 2: “I have to admit, I would be more inclined to take up your position on said matter if we weren’t tied up in the back of an eighteen wheeler, captured by a villainous monster bent on forcing us to create a weapon of mass destruction capable of destroying the sun.” Voice 1: “I believe I felt that in your voice. Still, it allowed us a moment of respite from the chilling reality of our situation. Even now I can again feel the cold grip of fear wrapping its fingers around my throat, making even breathing difficult.” Voice 2: “Indeed, and I find it difficult to believe that the muscular fellow in Greek finery shall prove a match to the fiend which demands our science. My rationality is leaving me quickly, my companion, and I wish you the best as I descend into madness.” The rear door of the trailer cracks open slowly, as we rejoin our Big and Bold hero, Big Alan Steele, his face streaked with grime, blood, and a sort of bluish ichor that cannot be described into words. The two scientists in white coats, one with a white beard, the other bald as can be, strapped to creaky wooden chairs squint their eyes against the harsh intrusion of sunlight, straining their heads back. Steele grins at them. Big Alan Steele: “Greetings, noble sirs, tis I, Big Alan Steele! I am truly sorry for the wait, but this half-man half-lizard abomination that is an affront to the very divinities I worship, those of Mount Olympus, is proving to be quite the challenge, indeed!! I have found his strength equal to that of many hearty warriors and his cunning in battle equally impressive. Though I have no reason to believe that he shall be able to best this man-god, I feel tis my duty as a protector of San Jose to offer my humblest and most sincere apologies at the length of your capture!” Voice 1: (bald scientist) “Oh no, none required! Though we cannot say that our comfort hasn’t been compromised by the situation, I’m sure I speak for my colleague here that we understand you are doing your very best to remedy the problem. I am sure that your efforts have proven more successful than that of the local law enforcement…” BAS: “Well, they do their best with their limited capabilities…” A slimy green claw reaches from behind Steele and grips him around the face, ripping him away from the door, slamming it shut, the sound ringing through the trailer and hurting one’s ears. The sounds of fierce battle pick up, the sound stilted by the metal of the trailer. A moment or two passes… Voice 2: (bearded scientist) “Nice chap, that Alan Steele.” Voice 1: “Yes. He does effort to regain our freedom in a most polite manner. Rare these days.” Voice 2: “Not like that crude and self-serving campaigner Superman. I can’t tell you how wrong the papers are about him…” Voice 1: “And Batman!! What an ego-centric maniac! He made me feel like it was MY fault I was captured!” Voice 2: “So, how many times has it been for you?” Voice 1: “Five. You?” Voice 2: “Seventeen.” Voice 1: “Incredible. My condolences.” Voice 2: “Well, I have been in the nuclear science business longer than you. I have experience. Don’t worry, you’ll get there. It comes with the life style.” A minute of silence inside as the sounds of battle increase outside. Voice 1: “The fear is returning, perhaps another debate?” Voice 2: “Surely! What shall we discuss?” Sound from outside, as Big Al delivers a death-blow… BAS: “HI-KEEBA~!”
