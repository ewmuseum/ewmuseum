The Myth of the Highlander -- Blood Gulch -- Chapter One

18 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/18/02 The Myth of the Highlander -- Blood Gulch -- Chapter OneCirca: Pre-AD 500 McGlocklan lied in bed, his neck sore from the weeks upon weeks of Valentine’s “Flashbacks,” his eyes twitching and rolling in their sockets as he struggled with a dream. A thick curtain of fog rested on a bloodstained knoll, scattered bodies littered the damp green soil. A younger looking McGlocklan sat behind a stone barricade, flaming pots of oil sailing overhead and raining down upon his brothers in arms. He reached for his side and rested his hand on the hilt of his sword. Withdrawing the blade, he stood and pointed toward the catapults, still launching great barrels of oil. Within moments, an onslaught of arrows soared down from the second tier of the barricade, piercing men operating the slings. Battle pipes sounded in the background as a legion of dragoons, horse-backed riders, rode across the hazy field. The thunderous clapping of hooves advanced forth, trampling carcasses and kicking stone as they charged across the clearing. Lifting his fingers to his mouth, he whistled between his teeth and called for his horse. A black stallion ran forth. McGlocklan caught a rein with his paw and hurdled atop the saddle, his claymore still held tightly in his hand. Trotting by, he extended his arm and swung forth, connecting with an infantryman’s throat. With a gurgling noise, the soldier dropped to his knees, his head rolling off of his shoulders. A bolt of electricity consumed the corpse’s body and engulfed McGlocklan’s body. Filled to the brim with a surge of power, he whipped his wrist off to the side, cleaning fluid off the blade. “Immortal…” he thought out loud, taking in the power. “Ayden!” A mounted soldier with long, flowing ginger hair turned his head towards Caleb and drove his spurs into his horse’s sides. The gray animal sped over and rode along side the Stallion. “Aye?” McGlocklan cocked his head backward. Ayden turned his head and watched on as several slain warriors sat up, pulling arrows from their chests and running hands across sealed wounds. Shaking his head he sighed, “M’mertul?” McGlocklan nodded. “Aye.” “Wellup, bahck ah go,” he groaned and pulled a wide U-turn with his beast. McGlocklan whistled in a high-pitched tone, gaining the attention of the other dragoons. Nodding, they pulled back on their reins and followed Ayden in another assault. “Fiiiyaaahhh!” A call rang out through the skies as an arrow pierced the black stallion in the side, throwing McGlocklan to the ground. Staggering around, the horse whinnied and took off, the arrow still lodged in its side. “Farkin…” he breathed as he rolled to a stop, the back of his head throbbing. He climbed to his feet, and lifted his weapon from off the floor, the handle slipping with moisture from the ground. Narrowing his eyes, he studied the terrain, and listened for anything suspicious. An arrow through his throat would prove just as lethal as a decapitation. Someone… “Is that the leader?” “I’d assume so…” “Well…what do we do?” “Wait for orders…” “Alright, but I—” A grotesque gurgle ensued, as the soldier vomited blood, a spear straight through his chest. His friend looked at him and winced as a fountain of red splashed across his chain mail boots. He unsheathed his sword and turned to scout the area, only to be met across the face vicious slash. Staggering back, he collapsed to the ground as his head, split in two, was removed with another hack. Energy surged from the body, the souls of slain warriors, and entered McGlocklan with a jolt. With a snapping of his knuckles, he stepped over the bodies and advanced forward, his golden eyes gleaming in intensity. Fifty yards ahead was the opposition’s barricade. Running his tongue across his teeth, clearing the salt off his lips, he slowly worked his way, stopping every so often to scan the territory. Curious… very curious… other than those two dead, there was no sign of any others. It couldn’t be over so quickly… He stepped over a small wall of stone and cocked his head around as the sounds of strings tightening filled his ears. Ambushed. “Aiiiimmm…” called a voice. “FIYAH!” Working purely on instinct, the young McGlocklan made an X with his arms and shielded his neck as a swishing of wind whisked by. Grimacing, fifty arrows pierced his arms and his chest. With a last gasp of air leaving his body, he collapsed to the floor, the cheers of archers haunting him.
