My Own Personal Hell

7 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/7/03 A fortress surrounded by water and trees off in the middle of nowhere. It may have sounded like paradise but once you got inside you would find yourself wishing to get out so that you can save yourself and your sanity. For this house was not where the happy roamed, it was a place for all those who had lost their mind, an asylum dare you say. Why my mind had brought me to this place I’ll never know but I figured that why I was here I might as well explore my surroundings and make myself at home. Sitting down on one of the few seats they had around here, a wooden bench, I made sure to provide myself with comfort as I stretched my legs lengthwise across the bench and leaned back making it a temporary nest. As I did this a short and rather plump woman with disheveled black hair decided that it was time she entertained herself and the way she would be to invite me into her little game. “Why are you sitting there?” The woman questioned in a quiet whisper indicating that perhaps she was a passive person, not looking for a fight but before I could come to a conclusion I found her raising her voice up to an even louder tone. “THAT IS MY BENCH!” She screamed out so that half the building could hear her proclamation. “Relax; there is plenty of room here for the both of us.” I replied, being careful to not raise my voice and cause a minor riot. I hated fights and I wanted to avoid having one with this woman at all costs, especially if she was as psychotic as she looked. “You don’t seem to be understanding, THIS IS MY BENCH!” She shouted out at me once more, as she looked me into the eyes the way my mother often did when she wanted to intimidate me into listening to her words. Instead of giving into the woman though I simply pushed my legs off the bench and patted my hands on the empty seat indicating to her that she could sit down beside me on the bench and make us both happy. “Don’t worry, I promise you I won’t bite or anything. In fact, I’m quite the friendly type.” I said to her in a half joking manner, hoping that this would lighten her mood and convince her that sharing the bench was just as good as having it to herself. “You better hope the horse and his mate don’t catch you on this bench.” She warned me with both her facial expressions and fingers as if this one line was supposed to scare me off, but instead it merely made me laugh inside my mind and question the sanity of this woman. “The horse and his mate? Heh never heard of either of them.” I chuckled slightly, as I stood up from the bench letting the woman have her seat so that she wouldn’t have a stroke and continue to go about this horse and mate. “I can hear them coming, you better hide.” The woman said, as she looked off to the right and fell silent as if she were trying to hear something important. “Hear them?” I questioned not being able to hear these noises that the woman claimed she was hearing. Perhaps this was my confirmation that the woman was indeed as insane as she looked. Before the woman could answer my question I found myself standing before a horse and a man who I had noticed as a man who had appeared on PCW television one time, a man who was considered a jobber among us, a man by the name of Alan Ackart. I couldn’t help but to snicker aloud at the man who had been raped by the horse who still followed him around to this day. “What are you laughing at?” The man whom I considered lower than me snapped, as if her were the king around these parts of the woods and I were his servant. “Nothing his jobberness.” I giggled like a school girl does about her secret crush, causing him to grow only angrier. “Jobber you say? Is that what you’ll become once Dorn Tracer defeats you this Tuesday right in the middle of the ring?” He shot back with a comeback of his own which only caused me to break into an even bigger chorus of giggles. “If you say so, after all who would know better than being a jobber than you?” I smirked feeling a certain satisfaction in my witty cruelness. Usually I would never find the heart to do such a thing, but when it came to Alan Ackart I could make an exception considering he was the man who told half the roster he could beat them without bothering to try. “That’s it, don’t make me sick my horse on you!” He threatened with a tug on the reigns that he led his horse with. “Whatever you say King Jobber.” I grinned before sarcastically bowing before him. “That’s it, get her boy!” He commanded the horse as if it were a dog with the pointing of his index finger in my direction. Surely enough like an obedient puppy the horse lurked forward in my direction backing me into a corner with both Ackart and the psychotic woman watching on joyfully. “FUCK! I should have known better than to mess with a psychopath and her jobber friend.” I muttered to myself as I closed my eyes hoping that the horse would get it’s job over as quickly as possible so that I wouldn’t not have to suffer for very long, I closed my eyes as it moved in closer and closer and closer until…. AHHHHHHHH!!!! I screamed out and shot my baby blue eyes open. It was three in the morning and I was inside my bedroom tucked under the pink cotton sheets covering my bed. Thankfully it was just a nightmare. I sighed and thank the lord that I didn’t have to live in such a place, a place called hell to a person like me.
