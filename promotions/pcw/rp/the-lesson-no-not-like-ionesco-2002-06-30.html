The Lesson (no, not like Ionesco)

30 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/30/02 In the Red Dragon casino in Las Vegas (enjoying much business after Rush Hour 2), The End is going around with PCW interviewer PFIK. The End just doesn’t seem to care much about dressing up for the place. He’s just got on some jeans and a denim coat. At least he’s not wearing his mask. He looks a little like a lighter-skinned Morris Chestnut without the mask, the women find it a shame that he insists on wearing it. PFIK wears a standard penguin tuxedo very well, and is clean-faced this time, but still wearing the same goggle-glasses he always does. he’s never been in a casino before, and it shows in his excitement. PFIK:“Hey, so what are we going to do first?” End:'First, you need to learn the rules.' PFIK:“I know the rules for the games.” End:'No, you know what they've told you. But that's only half of it.' They walk past a blackjack table. End:'Take this game, for instance. The odds are always in favor of the house. That's regardless of the rules. But there's certain things you can do to bring things back in your favor.' PFIK:'Like what?' End:'First of all, sit down at the table.' PFIK sits in a center seat. End:'No. Sit at either end of the table. You'll have slightly better odds that way.' PFIK moves. End:'Now, cash in.' PFIK gets some chips for his money. He nearly sets them down on the table to begin with. The End yanks him out of his seat. End:'You see. You don't know anything. Sure, there's rules to a game. There's also the rules to life. Life's a game, as well, but much different.' The End turns PFIK back to the table. End:'Rule #1 -- Just because you can get into the game right away, that may not be the best time.' PFIK turns back to the table. The dealer draws 21 right away. PFIK:“Hey, you kept me from losing my money. How did you know that?” End:'It's always best to wait to make sure that everything's run its cycle before you try to join. Get in in the middle of something, and you could get in over your head.' PFIK:“Can I get in now?” End:'Fine.' PFIK sits down to the table. After a few minutes of play, he wins $200. End:'You started with $500 and won $200. Now, what do you do?' PFIK:“I'm on a roll. Bet it all?” End:'Of course not! You won enough, leave the table.' PFIK takes his money and gets up. End:'You have to know when to stay in the game and win to get out.' PFIK:“But I was doing well.” End:'Yes, but all the good cards had cycled to the bottom of the deck by that point, and in a situation like that, the dealer's usually the first person to get to them.' PFIK:“So I'd have a better chance of losing than winning?” End:'Exactly. Gamble when you think you can win, but know when to get out of the game on top.' PFIK:“But what if I start losing first? I stay in the game and try to make it back?” End:'No. You cut your losses and move on.” PFIK goes to another table and starts winning again, this time at roulette (and we all know how much of a killer that game is). A flock of women gather around him. PFIK starts paying a lot of attention to them. Before he can do much, The End pulls him aside. PFIK:“What now?” End:“Ditch the women.” PFIK:“Why?” End:“There are only two kinds of women in this casino -- leeches and plants. Leeches want you for the money you’re winning. Plants want you to lose the money you’re winning. None of them will do you any good, so get rid of them.” PFIK cashes out of the game and goes away. On his walk, he picks up some alcohol from a passing woman. The End is quick to take this away from him. PFIK:“I’m allowed!” End:“You think for a second that this casino’s not trying to give you liquor so your judgment will be impaired once you try to play the games by their rules? Liquor, women... they’re distractions. If you must have them, have them later. It doesn’t help anything right now. PFIK:“Okay.” The End claps his hands together. End:“So, let's go take out a loan.' PFIK:“What?” The End meets with the casino's proprietor. (Forgive the lack of details about his office, but if we showed it to you we’d have to pay to use the office. Instead, we’ll just show you Joe Pesci, playing the owner. He seems to be stuck in his fake hair and leather biker outfit that he wore in My Cousin Vinny. It’s a terrible movie, but he seemed to be please with the way he looked, at least.) Owner:“Ah, End, would you like another loan?” End:'Yes, the usual amount and payback deadline is fine.' Owner:'Very well, $300,000 due back in one week.' The proprietor cuts The End a check. Owner:“It is nice doing business with you again.” End:'Likewise.' On the way out, PFIK looks dumbfounded. PFIK:“You can just walk in and ask for that much money?” End:'Of course.' PFIK:“So what're we going to do with it?” End:'Sit on it.' PFIK:“What?” End:'You think something this big I'd take a chance losing on some stupid gamble the house has the odds in?' The End takes PFIK aside. End:'I don't get paid much from wrestling. Never have. I don't even ask for much money. Know why?' PFIK:“Why?” End:'Because of this. Get a 1K, 2K, or 3K loan from a casino, right? Do that at about 20 big casinos, and put it all into a number of different accounts, so I may have maybe $2 million of money that's not even mine in the bank.' PFIK:“Yeah?” End:'Take advantage of daily compoun interest, and by the end of the week, I pay back all of those loans...' PFIK:'And you keep the money you make on the interest?' End:'Exactly. It's still a gamble, everything is. But it's the safest possible gamble I can make. I change the rules. I change the game. I do things my way and come out winning in the end. And besides, it helps to build my reputation.' PFIK:“Which is?” End:'That I ALWAYS pay back what I owe others. [EDITOR’S NOTE: And just guess what this is about.]' PFIK:“Well, I’m gonna go now. I think I’ve got it.” End:'Go ahead. And don't forget what you've learned.' The End leaves the casino. He turned the interview with PFIK into a lesson that the intern will not soon forget. The End did mask his own beliefs in his statements, but whether PFIK picked up on it is up to him. Sunday, June 30. Gold & Glory. The End and El Diablo meet in singles competition, and when the battle is over, only one man shall stand victorious. The End? He’d be a standard cruiserweight, if not for his lack of sight and height. He’s much stronger than he looks, and could easily toss El Diablo with one arm. El Diablo? He’s had much more wrestling experience than The End. He knows more tricks than The End. This, coupled with his characteristic unpredictability, makes him a dangerous man. Who will win?
