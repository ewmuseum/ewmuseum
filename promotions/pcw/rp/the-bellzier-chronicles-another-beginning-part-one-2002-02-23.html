The Bellzier Chronicles: Another Beginning [Part One]

23 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 23, 2002 Denny’sSan Francisco, CA2:45 am Ka-chunk. Ka-chunk. “Damn Jehovah’s witnesses always bugging me…” I love to murmur under my breath. It makes me feel tougher in a way. Although one would have to ask themselves how you could feel tough when you’re dragging wet and muddy shoes through the restroom of a Denny’s restaurant. Ah, the scene is set. Already you realize what a horrible day I was having. Let me recap it for you. I received a call early this morning, and when I say early this morning, I meant before ten o’clock. The phone was ringing off the hook, and when it’s that early, it sounds like there’s a bell going off inside of your head. As you can imagine, my brain felt like it was on fire and I nearly punched a hole through the phone. What stopped me, you ask? I am not a wasteful man, and I believe that it was a forty dollar phone from Radioshack. Yes, I said Radioshack. I bundled myself in my blankets, pulling a few off from my Natalie, who groaned loudly and tried to pry some from my hands. Of course, I’m the strong wrestler and she was but a lowly girlfriend, so the outcome was fairly obvious – she won. So I’m stumbling out of my bedroom, cold and still reeling from only three hours of sleep the night before, and that damn phone is still ringing. “I’ll be right there,” I muttered under my breath. The phone rings again. “Did you hear what I said? I said I’ll be right there….” Bbbbbbriiiiiing. “Dammit…” Bbbbbbriiiiiing. “I’m almost there…” Bbbbbbriiiiiing. “Argh!” I grabbed the phone with a ferocity I wasn’t even known for in the ring, and angrily growled into it. “Yeah, what the hell do you want?” “And a good morning to you too.” I wasn’t in the mood for pleasantries, and god knows the morning wasn’t the time to be polite. “Are you one of those Jehovah witnesses? Listen, I told you before, I’m not joining and you can keep your free subscription to Jesus: The Magazine.” A sigh could be heard on the other line. The voice sounded tired. “I heard you were a tough one to please in the morning. I guess I should listen to my boss more often.” “That’s great. Be sure to give your boss my regards,” I began to say as I put the phone down to hang up. “I wouldn’t do that if I were you…” My interest was peaked for a moment, and I brought the phone back to my ear. “Okay, you have one minute. Who are you?” “I’m a representative from Primetime Championship Wrestling. You surely remember us…” I did. I was recruited to join PCW when I was but a pup in DWF, just starting to break out on my own and finding where I fit in this crazy wrestling world. I was traveling around the states, seeing towns and cities I had never known even existed, and I loved every second of it. And then I got a call. I was sitting on the tour bus, catching up on world news by reading a newspaper (A wrestler travels so much, sometimes the most obvious details of current events escapes him or her), and one of the roadies handed me a cell phone. He told me someone from another promotion was calling for me. I was surprised, for one, because I didn’t think anyone thought very high of me yet. I was only two months into my DWF stay – I wasn’t what you would consider a star. “I do remember PCW,” I replied. “I remember Phantom Booker, or so he called himself at the time, trying to get me to join. He was quite persuasive actually.” “Yes, yes. He even had that great plan for you and your other DWF cronies to join you in the god awful stable. What was it called again? Cream Team?” “Dream team,” I snapped back. Sure, it was corny, but we were a force. Legend Inc, Mike Extreme, Caged Explosion and Jeremy Howard in the same stable? How could anyone in PCW even fathom to stop us? “Whatever your name was. It’s not like it matters. You guys lasted about as long as it takes a leaf to fall from a tree. By the time it was all said and done, you and Mike Extreme left within three weeks, Caged Explosion was jobbed to hell, and the only survivor worthy of your team was Howard.” “I was undefeated.” “I beg your pardon?” “I was undefeated,” I stated again. “It’s quite simple actually. No one in PCW ever defeated me, and in fact, I was well on my way to winning the tag straps with Mike Extreme. Hell, he was even holding the Extreme title when we left.” “Things have changed quite a bit since your departure.” He referred to the roster of PCW as well as my own age. I wasn’t getting much younger, although at twenty-six years of age, I’m hardly an old geezer. It just so happens that hair in my family tends to gray early – like, before you even hit thirty. “If you’re trying to make a crack joke about my age, you can stop right now. I’m in better shape than I was before.” The voice chuckled. “I’m sure you are. Look, I’m not here to insult you, just to give you some information. We heard you were interested in making a return.” I snorted. “Who told you that?” “We have sources. Besides, you’ve been on the shelf for a couple of months now. I’m sure you’re just dying to get back into the ring.” Perhaps some of what he said was true, but I wasn’t about to admit that to him. “I might be inclined to make a return…” “Good. We’ll have a representative fax over some documents to you and you can look them over for yourself.” I paused for a moment. “Oh, and Tom?” “Yeah?” “It’s good to have you back.” Click. The scene shifts back to the bathroom at the Denny’s restaurant. Back from a flashback that was a lot longer than I had made it out to be, but suffice it to say, Natalie wasn’t so happy about the fact that I was getting back into wrestling. In some ways I understand her reasoning, and other times I just can’t help but shake my head. She knew exactly what she was getting into when she met me. She was but a reporter for the local news station, doing a cover story on the burgeoning wrestling scene in the city, and I just happened to be the local jobber that just got his head handed back to him by the world champion. Yeah, I guess I can say it was love at first sight. Wrestling puts food on the table. It’s that simple. Nat always made the comment that she thought I could do anything else that I wanted on the mere fact that I always put one hundred percent into whatever I was doing. But what I wanted to do was wrestle, and no one knew this better than Nat. The past few months that I was “on the shelf” had been blissful, albeit, fairly uneventful. I would wake up, clean around the house, and cook dinner for Nat when she came home from a long day at work. Some old school’ers would say that our roles were reversed, but I had no problem tending to the house. But when you have wrestling in your blood, you can’t just take it out. It’s a part of you, just like your mind, body and soul. She didn’t say much when I announced my intention to come out of retirement, but I expected as much. So here I am, fresh out of a meeting with PCW officials, and I’m soaking wet. So much for the Bay Area and it’s 24/7 beautiful weather. The tiles of the floor were a beautiful cream color. Now they were darkened brown with the mud I had just trudged in. I would never be able to understand the logic of placing light colored tiles in a room that you absolutely know is going to get dirty. Gazing down at my shoes, my once perfect black Armani shoes were stained to hell. You couldn’t even tell they were black. The mud reached half way up to my knees, which was a splattered mural of brown all the way down. I looked into the mirror, placing my glasses on the sink’s surface, and rubbed my eyes. There growing pinkish on the sides, telling me that I needed to go to sleep. At the tender age of twenty-six, one finds that listening to the body’s subtle messages often leads to a healthier life. I ran my hand through my graying hair and wiped away some rain drops that had taken residence on my cheeks. It was nearing three a.m. in the morning, and I was still thirty minutes away from home. Nat would probably be asleep already. I sighed, picking up my glasses, and heading into a nearby stall. I pushed the door open with my right arm and locked it behind me. As I sat down to the sweet sound of a toilet cover crunching underneath me, I heard a voice call out from the stall next to me. “Hello there, mate. What are you in for?” I shook my head, thinking to myself what luck I had to choose the one stall that had the resident weirdo in the stall next to me. I didn’t respond. “Mate? You there?” “If you’d excuse me, I’d like to have some peace and quiet right now.” My reply was curt, but I tried to tickle it with as much politeness as I could. Hopefully he would be able to take the hint. He didn’t. “You’re having a bad day. I can tell.” “You have no idea.” “Sure I do. I can see just from looking at those pretty shoes you have on there. Although, they look rather disgusting at the moment.” “It’s raining cats and dogs out there,” I said, taking a deep sigh. “And I’m still so far from home.” “You sound like you could use a friend.” I scoffed. “And who would that be? You? The guy who talks to stall next to him when he’s taking a crap? Thanks but no thanks. I’d much rather just be left alone.” “Wouldn’t we all? Unfortunately, things often don’t turn out the way we want them to. I learned that a long time ago. So what line of work forces you to be such an emotionally detached individual?” I wanted to scream, but I just didn’t have the energy to do so. Something about this guy told me that he knew more about me that he was putting on. Maybe it was a premonition…maybe it was stupid intuition. “I’m a wrestler,” I stated flatly. “Ah, entertainers should some of the most respected individuals in our society. How can you have nothing but respect for people that put the entertainment of others before their own sanity? It’s just amazing to me.” “I’m an athlete, not an entertainer.” “Some would argue that the two are in the same. But I’m not going to debate that with you. After all, I’m sure you love your work.” “Yeah,” I mumbled back. I shook my head at the pointless conversation and pulled out my cell phone, wanting to give Nat a call. It was pretty late, but I didn’t see a point in continuing this dialogue. I dialed our apartment number and the phone rang until our answering machine. That’s odd. Why isn’t Nat picking up? Where could she be…
