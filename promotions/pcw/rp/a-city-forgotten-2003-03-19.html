A City Forgotten

19 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3 - 19 - 03 Pasted against the gloomy, gray backdrop of dark clouds and a white sun, the cityscape is a little more than a mere shadow in the wind. The demolished skyscrapers crumble and chip into the wind, their steel frames falling apart and the cement beginning to crumble away. The buildings resemble stationary ghouls, frozen forever in time in their destroyed state. It’s almost like time has stopped in this dismal place, where happiness and love is void and chaos swirls about the city like a raging vortex, sucking in the remainder of life that crawls about the streets and consuming the pitiful infrastructure system. The surrounding Earth is no better, in fact, it’s worse. The topsoil is a shade of deathly gray, occasionally conjuring up into a deadly ghoulish whirlwind that sweeps through the ghost city. Trees are completely absent and the barren wasteland stretches out as far as the eye can see in the dismal, shady horizon backed by a shining white sun. Like an image of silent death, the city doesn’t move… doesn’t budge… completely void of life. What is this city? A city long forgotten in the annals of time… A city that was once home to a great and powerful group of people. But now, it’s a mere broken shell of its former self, crushed beneath the unforgiving iron fist of its oppressors. It is a city… long forgotten. The feed switches to the entrance to the city: A large, gray brick wall with a cast iron gate. Beyond is an infinite road surrounded by assorted crumbling buildings and structures. Only a ghostly wind moves to and fro through the infrastructure, casting out a sharp whistling noise and a cool breeze. Not even the street lamps are working. There’s not a living soul in sight, and not a light lit amongst the buildings. A rather creepy sight… Dorn Tracer thinks to himself, wearing a smug look of dismay on his face. It’s amazing what the entire world can accomplish when set on destroying innocent lives… He shakes his head with a heavy sigh and approaches the black, cast iron gate. Rising above the Astronomer like a set of guards, they impose quite a sight. A cold, sharp shiver runs down the spinal column of the Shooting Star Champion, making him shudder, but he knows his quest. Placing a slightly quaking hand on one of the bars, Dorn slowly pushes the gate open. It emits a loud, screeching noise, causing Dorn to grit his teeth and slam his eyes shut. When the glass shattering creaking of the gate ceases, the Astronomer opens his eyes and steps in, almost absent-mindedly. The downright ghastly image of the broken society draws him in like a bee to a honeycomb. The smell, though, could repulse a drug hound and send him scurrying away with fainted yelps. It smells almost like… Death, The smell invades the nostrils of the Astronomer as the word invades his cerebrum, cycling through over and over, reminding Dorn of what he’s going to enter. ‘Better not waste anymore time,’ He exclaims to none other than himself, almost like he’s trying to persuade himself to enter the city of ghostly death. Taking slowly, steady strides along the cracked asphalt, Dorn surveys the landscape around him. His footsteps echo through the streets and his eyes fall upon nothing but rotted, destroyed city. ‘This is just strange. What happened to this place? It reeks of death and looks no better.’ His voice is bloated with confusion, but a deep hint of terror also makes itself apparent. What could have made an entire city deserted? What could cause an entire metropolis to be void of all life? A hurricane? A flood? A volcanic eruption? Or other humans…? Only humans could cause such subtle chaos on each other. Only humans could feel such apathy towards each other, causing them to wipe out an entire city. Reality is like a sword that can cut through anything… and it has surely pierced the concrete walls of this deathly metropolis. Could the inhabitants have been something so detestable that their entire eradication was all but necessary? That could only be true. ‘These people must have been quite hated amongst society,’ The Astronomer deduces, still skeptical as to the cause of the eradication or sudden disappearance. ‘That or they felt the need to leave their city… but I doubt an entire city can migrate and not leave a single soul behind. It’s just not feasible.’ And indeed it’s not. The answer doesn’t lie within the broken structures or destroyed roads. It lies in the statue. The smoldered marble statue, standing about 11 feet in the air, is the entire testament to the tragedy that fell before this city. The statue resembles a humanoid bull type creature, holding a large golden spear. Well, RESEMBLES… it’s been destroyed by some sort of explosion. When Dorn’s wandering eyes fall upon the statue, it all becomes clear… ‘This was a city of Astronomers…’ So that’s it. An entire city wiped clean of life, due to their beliefs. Nothing can explain the rage building with the blackened heart of Dorn Tracer as he stares at the ruined statue of Tartorus. The death must have been massive… and all because they believe in something different. Dorn faces the same crisis. Attack after attack on his being, it’s just becoming more frequent. Specters from the past are now haunting him, like Jonathan Drachen. And it’s all because of his beliefs… All because of his beliefs… [End.]
