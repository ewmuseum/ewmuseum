Date: Before Fusion The surveyor surveyed, naturally. He wasn’t great at it but he did the job, scribbling notes down on some checklist attached to the fake leather backed clipboard seemingly with every move. Lacuna had tried to be nice to him for a while but he wasn’t a particularly nice guy. Since the incident with the zip line and the mud, as well as the insults to his awful pinstripe suit, he had been less than friendly. It turns out that her rather large shed could make for some big income. There wasn’t much in there as most of the equipment was kept within twenty minutes walk of the house, but the décor and the structural damage would amount to a pretty penny or two. Brook passed slowly down the north hallway of the first floor. Most of the oak doors where closed which made the really quite large walkway seem much smaller. He always had trouble finding Lacuna in such a large place. Once, he had worked out that roughly ninety-four percent of the house was unused but this made it no easier as she never stuck to inhabiting the same room, obvious exceptions being her six bedrooms and the two living rooms. Sometimes she may even hide in the kitchen or her sparkling new gymnasium, but this was generally at a push. He scratched at his balding head as he headed down towards the far end of the house. This past week, she had been hiding in there for a while so he decided it would probably be high on the list. Things had changed recently, the air had become thicker and an atmosphere had descended again. It happened quite a lot really. She tended to think too much, that’s what the doctors said anyway, about every possible thing. Whether the lighting was wrong or she had just lost a match to her arch rival and ex tag team partner, she would generally find a room with a view and stare for a couple of days. That was no exaggeration either. Brook gave up, his search proving fruitless in the lower-north area of the house. Little did he know she was on the roof. She had been here before, during the one period of her life when rational thought had taken over her. The whistling of the strong wind through her hair and past her ears, the glow of the cold sun reflecting neatly from the rippling surface of her miniature lake. It was perfect. Last time she came up here, she felt it too. But where was it now? Where was the clear backdrop to her thoughts that aided her so? It evaded her as she did so successfully to those who sought after her. She wasn’t getting bad yet; she knew all too well that she would soon be dealing with that sensation, the pulsing anger and throbbing emotions she was so used to pushing to the back of her over imaginative mind. Yet she could not do that in one of these moods, it just wouldn’t work. But what was she thinking? If only it was that easy to tell. She herself could not separate the strands of thought that entwined her brain. She ran a hand roughly through her flailing hair and stared upwards into the cloudy grey sky. She was only truly alone when isolated on her roof, and she loved it. No traffic rumbled, no grumbling servants, it was perfect. But today that nagging at her brain ruined it, the one she couldn’t place for the life of her. She had felt it the last time she climbed the rickety ladder through the skylight. When she last faced Jade. The last time she had faced Jade, she did more than just beat her, she annihilated her. Three falls, three victories, each sweater than the last as she stepped out of her league into a whole new suit of ‘extreme’ matches. It wasn’t something she had done before, and she did it like some sort of hobby. It was so sweet. But things had changed since, she was kind of pleased that Jade was slowly beginning to put in the effort required to win things; that lazy streak she had been suffering from had seemed to disappear recently, and it had to if she was going to compete, and eventually win, with a fitness freak like Lacuna. Thing is, She didn’t know whether she should hate her or not. There, she had found her reason. It had been staring her in the face for so god damn long that she had to practically fall over it before she worked out what it was. Jade was good, there was no doubt about that, but did she want her to do well, or did she hate her for ruining their run in the tag tournament with her unfitness and general lazy attitude. She just didn’t know.
