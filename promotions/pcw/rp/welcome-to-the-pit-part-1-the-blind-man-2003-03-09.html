Welcome To The Pit - Part 1: The Blind Man

9 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3 - 9 - 03 Sure is dank out here, The Astronomer thinks to himself with a scrunching of his nose. The rancid smells of the moldy, degrading forestland surrounding the Black Marsh are little less than welcoming to Dorn Tracer as he wades his way through the slimy foliage. His black leather pants are already partially caked with mud and slime, while the bottom on his trench coat is rimmed with dark, slick stains. Above his head, the sweet embrace of the sky is interrupted by the scraggly, reaching branches of the gnarled trees. Sounds of insect and amphibian life invade Dorn’s eardrums, causing him to cringe in disgust as he thinks of the nasty little creatures, Why did the damn guy have to hold a training arena in the middle of a fucking shit-filled swamp? The Shooting Star Champion continues to blaze the linear trail before him, seeing nothing but more swamp and more trees in the distance. Being that the forest practically acts as an impenetrable wall around the swamp, the humid air is contained within without escape, causing beads of sweat to slide down Dorn’s face. His breathing is becoming labored and his legs are becoming slower with their long strides. ‘I fucking hate this place…’ He snarls, drawing his eyebrows in and smirking angrily. ‘Who knew there was a fucking swamp outside of Los Angelus?’ Dorn’s question is to no one in particular, but it’s still loud enough for those at least twenty feet away to hear clearly. Dorn can feel the exhausted being to build up within his body. He’s been walking for what seems to be hours, trying to find some old Astronomer’s training shack. En’Flarim had suggested it to him, telling Dorn that it would greatly augment his skills. Never one to pass up such an opportunity, Dorn quickly obliged and began his journey. A couple hours later, he’s still making it. When will it ever end? Dorn asks himself deep within his cranium, truly disturbed by his plight. I hope this fucker had a Mercury of a time getting here, because I sure am! Wearing an expression of pure irritation and grim determination, Dorn continues on. After what seems to be another couple hours, Dorn finds himself at a dead end. The path ends at a clump of trees and there’s no visible way around. Upon noticing this, Dorn stops dead in his tracks and just stares ahead, hoping he’s dreaming. When he approaches it with an outstretched hand, however, he quickly finds that he’s NOT dreaming. Feeling the rage swell within his body, Dorn boots the truck of a tree roughly and leans against it, crossing his arms in a sulking manner. He feels like crying out in a fit of rage and beating the shit out of one of the trees, but he knows that will get him nowhere. Kind of like this fucking journey, Dorn thinks with a scowl. Dorn contemplates going back, not too thrilled about having made his way here and now he’s suddenly at a dead end. He lifts his body off the tree and starts away, mumbling to himself, before a ruffle in the bushes behind him makes its way to his ear. He stops and carefully turns around, curious and suspicious at the same time. Surveying the area with squinting, searching eyes, Dorn attempts to pinpoint the source of the sound. However, what Dorn doesn’t realize is that he’s looking in the completely wrong direction. As he searches around, a sudden rush of wind races across his back and a harsh, metallic object blasts him in the back of the skull. His head burns like napalm and he feels himself plummet to the ground. Dorn’s eyes black out, unable to see the ground racing towards him as his body falls… * * * Dorn awakens to find himself in a ramshackle hut made of tied reeds and a straw roof. Surrounding him are various pieces of old wooden furniture and a nasty red carpet. It looks like a poor man’s forest cabin… even down to the stone fire place. So why is Dorn so surprised by his surroundings? Hanging from the walls are steel chairs, kendo sticks, and a various steel signs. Even worse than that, Dorn notices a caddy bag full of baseball bats in one corner of the little cabin. Thinking himself to be in some Wicca’s torture chamber, he bolts up from whatever he’s laying on and jumps onto the floor. In a fit of panic, he frantically searches for the door and rushes towards it upon seeing it. When he finally reaches it, he extends his hand towards the knob and gets knocked on his ass when the large wooden door flings up and smacks him in the face. He plummets ass first to the ground and sits dumbfounded at the spectacle before him: A man of about 6 feet, maybe 200 pounds. He has shaggy brown hair and wears a tattered brown shirt with matching tattered blue jeans. Both of his eyes are closed and he seems to be in great shape, despite his poor appearance. He cracks a nasty smile at Dorn and shakes his head, “Damn, boy… you’re just getting in the way left and right, ain’t ya?” His voice is as hard as nails and is void of emotion, save maybe sarcasm. “Well then, get on your feet.” When Dorn doesn’t exactly show enough enthusiasm to move, the guy takes a step towards him and holds out a rough, tanned hand. The Shooting Star Champion is reluctant at first, but finally takes the man’s hands and is hoisted to his feet. Dorn brushes himself off and gives the man a puzzled, somewhat frightened look, “Who are you?” “The name is Copernicus, boy. And you are?” The man’s tone comes off as harsh and demanding, but with a hint of humor to it. Dorn takes a step back, somewhat intimidated by this strange individual, and shakes his head. “I’m Dorn Tracer III,” He shoots, his voice trembling somewhat. “Where in the name of Pluto am I?” The man lets out a hearty laugh, his eyes still closed, and holds his stomach to steady himself. Dorn is a little taken back by the spectacle, unable to bring himself to understand what on Earth the man is laughing at. Copernicus just continues to give a deep belly laugh, finding it hard to breath between each one. After a few more seconds, though, his laughter dies down and the man shakes his head, still grinning, “Where do ya think ya are? You’re in my house, in the middle of my swamp.” The man’s smart-ass attitude irritates Dorn somewhat, but the man doesn’t seem to notice. “We got a lot to work on if ya don’t even know you’re standing in my house.” “WE have a lot to work on?” Dorn asks, puzzled by the statement. “What’s this we crap? I don’t even know who you are, aside from your name. I got attacked on in the forest by something, find myself here, and all of a sudden, I’m going to work on something??” The man just gives a stiff nod, “Of course, that’s what ya came for, isn’t it? Didn’t the Dragon Goddess send you?” At the mention of the mighty En’Flarim, Dorn glares at Copernicus with a new light. Had he truly found his way to the place where someone would augment his skills? The pieces of equipment around the cabin certainly testify to that theory, but you never know. “So you’re the guy that’s going to help me train for my contest this coming Sunday?” Dorn hopes so, because he doesn’t have much time. Thankfully, Copernicus nods with acknowledgement. “Aye, I am. The mighty Creator of All came to me in a dream and told me of a brash new warrior that would need my assistance. You are he, yes?” Pretty much assuming that he is, Dorn gives a nod and folds his arms across his chest. “I am the one En’Flarim spoke of. So, this will be my place of training… when shall we begin? What will we be doing?” Dorn’s line of questioning seems to get a grin out of the man, which ultimately confuses The Astronomer. “You’ll see in due time, Dorn. First, I have to show you the complex so you get a basic idea of the horrors to come, know what I mean?” The man’s calm, laughing demeanor unnerves the Shooting Star Champion and he feels a slight shiver of fear run down the course of his spinal cord. “Any questions?” Strangely, and almost embarrassingly, Dorn can only think of one, “Why are your eyes constantly closed?” How stupid… I’m so afraid I can’t even ask a decent question, he probably thinks I’m stupid as stupid as a Christian. Copernicus grins once again and shrugs. “I’m blind, stared at the Sun too long during a session of prayer.” The fact that Copernicus claims to be blind horrifies Dorn, causing the Astronomer to wonder how he could possibly train with a blind man. Copernicus must sense Dorn’s panic, so he continues to grin happily, “But the Tiamats have blessed me with increased senses, allowing me to make up for a lack of sight. No big deal, training will continue as normal.” Dorn Tracer sighs, knowing he’s probably put himself into a hole he can’t climb out of without help from a blind. He gives an acknowledging nod and sighs, “Let’s do it.” Copernicus nods and starts back out the door. Dorn watches as the man seems to move along like he can really see, and that seems to unnerve Dorn even more. “Oh, and by the way…” “Yes?” Dorn asks, fearing the man’s answer after what he’s heard. Copernicus’ grin turns to a sinister-looking smirk, his eyebrows drawn in towards his nose. “Welcome to The Pit…” [End.]
