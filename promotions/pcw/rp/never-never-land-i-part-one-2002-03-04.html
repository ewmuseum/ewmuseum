Never Never Land I: [Part One]

4 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 4, 2002 OOC: I used to write about Never Never Land when I was in the IWO, so if you really want to know some of the back story to the series, just ask me and I'll send you the previous series' involving it. What? None of you want it? Damn. =P “….and I’m really rich. I really am. I’m a big time wrestler, so I got loads of cash in the bank, baby.” I was speaking as smoothly as a baby’s bottom feels, and I felt good doing it. In front of me was a pretty little “thang,” a nurse, to be exact. She had smooth skin, her perfect hair curled up in a bun, and a smile that could make me do anything. Of course, I was being cool and everything, not giving any hints to my wanton desire to get her in the sack. “Gosh, what do you do with all of that money?” She asked, batting her pretty little eyelids. Damn, that really turned me on for some reason. I shrugged confidently, making twisty movements with my hands. “I could just throw it away, or maybe use it as toilet paper. I lot of famous people like to do that, know what I mean?” “That’s really great,” She replied. “But you know what?” “What’s that, sweet ‘thang?’ “ “If you use it as toilet paper, you could stuff it up farther up your ass where your head is. Get away from me, you loser.” She stomped away from me with my hopes and dreams of getting laid running away with her. I just threw my hands in the air, dismayed that my flirting advances didn’t get me as far as I hoped. “Does this mean we’re not going to have sex?” She turned around, giving me the dirtiest look I’ve ever seen. I just gave her a thumbs up. “I love it when they play hard to get,” I whispered to myself, jotting down her name on a pad of paper. “Hard to get? Try not getting at all.” I looked up, only to see a surgical cart slowly making it’s way towards me. On top of the cart were normal surgery tools, but also on the cart was Bobby the Cactus, who was stylin’ and profilin’ like myself. Needless to say, I didn’t like it one bit. “Do you need a scalpel? Maybe a sponge?” Bobby asked lamely. I sighed to myself, placing my hands on my hips and looking to the sky. “My god, why have I been cursed with this cactus as my manager?” “I’ll pretend like I didn’t hear that,” Bobby replied. “What are you doing here?” I asked, beginning to be very annoyed. That was odd, because I almost never got annoyed, unless someone eats my lunch. That actually happens more often than not, and it just pisses me off. You have a number on your receipt – look at it. How can you mistake order number 31 for order number 69? Dammit. “That’s exactly what I was going to ask you. Shouldn’t you be in a drug rehab program, or something?” “I didn’t smoke it, you crack pot cactus!” I shouted at the top of my lungs. As I did, several nurses gave me dirty looks. Most of them were women I had flirted with earlier, so I wasn’t sure if I was getting the looks for my shouting or my annoyance. “What else would you do with a bag full of weed?” “What would YOU do?” I replied, mouth full of sarcasm. I always wanted to ask that of a cactus. Well, no, not really. But it is strangely ironic. “That’s not funny, Tom,” Bobby replied, rippling a few of his spines as a sign that he was beginning to get annoyed. “You know how much I hate other plants, especially ones that get so much hype like marijuana.” I glanced back at the nurses head station, where they were all staring at me intently. It wasn’t a look of seduction, but fear and anxiety. I tried to put myself in their shoes. Here was a man that just tried to flirt with every person in a white skirt in the vicinity, and now he was speaking normally to a cactus on a medical cart. Even I had to arch my eyebrow in question at the image. Realizing that my time here had to come to an end, I placed both of my hands at the end of the cart and began to push. The cart eased forward harshly, not wanting to roll down the slick floors. A screeching noise soon followed, and a drop of sweat furiously rolled down my face. I was damn near nervous. “What are you sweating for?” Bobby asked. “It’s not even five degrees above room temperature in here.” “Bobby, have you ever noticed that everyone gives us weird looks whenever we talk to each other?” I whispered, being barely audible to myself. If Bobby had an eyebrow, he probably would have arched it against me. “Well, let’s review the facts here. I mean, you are talking to a cactus here.” “Yeah, but they can hear you talking. They know I’m not talking to some inanimate object.” “No they can’t.” I stopped the cart suddenly. “What do you mean they can’t hear you? I can hear you just fine.” “Sure you can. But you’re Tom Lexian, remember? Greatest lamer in the Earth and all planetary realms in this dimension? You’re supposed to hear me. Most people can’t. They just aren’t fine tuned to pick up my vibe.” I was shocked, stunned, and horrified. How could I have been so dense? All of this time, I thought other people could hear the on going conversation between myself and my esteemed manager. Now I know where the looks come from. The stares, the jeers…all of it made perfect sense. I collapsed to one knee, leaning my head against the cold, unforgiving steel of the cart. Suddenly that one victory didn’t mean very much anymore. “Tom, get your head off the cart. You don’t know where it’s been. I found it in the OR. For all you know, your head is where someone’s spleen sat.” “How can this be?” I asked, as if a million people were standing around me. “The kid…the kid from the wrestling video store. He could hear you. He was talking and listening and…” I trailed off, trying to find answers. “He was different. You can tell he’s a bit eccentric. Anyone whose that hardcore about something as silly as wrestling is a bit off their rocker.” “Is that what I am?” I said, beginning to spin circles with my finger around my head. “Am I a loon? Am I going insane? Is this the first step to me dialing up Ms. Cleo’s Psychic Hotline each night, just to find out that I have no future? Is it?” I was still staring up at Bobby, but my hands were furiously shaking the cart. “Tom! Tom! Calm down! Stop shaking this cart!” I wasn’t listening anymore. Something inside of me cracked and it wanted out. One second later, I felt a cutting sensation slash into my right arm and I fell back in pain. As I grabbed my right side, I saw a large spine sticking out of my shirt, a tinge of blood trickling out. I pulled it out, gritting my teeth as I did. It hurt a lot. “God damn, that stings…” I moaned, dropping the spine to the ground and getting back to my feet. “I had to do something,” Bobby replied, a bit of sympathy in his voice. “You were going crazy.” Bobby paused for a moment, letting his words sink in. Just as soon as he paused, however, he added in, “You stop caring about me and you’ll get another spine in your arm, or worse, lower…” I cringed as I took my hand off the wound, which was stained red now. I suppose all plants evolve harsher and harsher defenses to fight against the cruelty of humanity. Just when I thought things couldn’t get any worse, though, they did. “Excuse me, sir…” A voice called out from behind me. I spun around, only to come face to face with a police officer. He was about five feet four inches, with skinny shoulders and heavy, heavy bifocals. He squinted at me as he stood there, indicating that he didn’t quite see me so clearly. Lucky for me, I guess. Next to him was the nurse I was hitting on earlier. You know, that chick that didn’t buy that I was some rich and famous wrestler. What a dumb brood. She had about five inches on the poor bastard, which almost made me chuckle, if my fucking arm didn’t hurt so much. “What can I do for you, officer?” I said, trying to cover up my bloodied arm with my left hand. It wasn’t working, though, because the blood was beginning to trickle out of my hand. That brought even more attention to it. I began to wonder if the spine had severed an artery of mine. “You can start by telling me why you have been harassing the good nurses here in the hospital,” He replied, crossing his arms in a stern fashion. Uhm, sorry bro, I’m just not buying the tough guy routine. Maybe there are some tough five foor four inch guys out there, but it’s hard to take someone seriously when they’re being dwarfed by nurses. “I wasn’t harassing anyone,” I said. “Just being curious. I thought they were single because they were all hitting on me…” “That’s fucking bullshit!” The nurse on the right shouted out. She lunged forward, fist cocked back as if she was going to swing a right cross at me, but the police officer stepped in front of her. Unfortunately for him, he got barreled through and knocked to his knees. I bent down to help him off, but he brushed me off. As I stood back up, the chick on the left tried her hand at slapping me, but I caught her hand with relative ease. “I don’t think so, baby,” I said mockingly, dropping her fist. “Even the best wrestlers in the PCW can’t keep me down for long. What makes you think you can?” “Look, this is a private hospital,” The officer said, breaking up my threats to the nurse. “I’m going to have to ask for you to come with me…” “For what?!” I cried out, feeling defensive. He wasn’t taking me anywhere. “You’re being a nuisance to the community, and I don’t feel safe with you roaming the streets.” The officer looked back at the nurses, smiling and giving comforting looks. “Don’t worry ladies, I have this all under control. I’ll check him to see if he’s a registered pervert in any of the fifty states as well. God knows, one of these perverts shows up everyday.” That was the wrong thing to say at the wrong time. I grabbed the officer by his shirt, lifting him a foot into the air. His eyes glazed over in fear, and I could feel his pulse racing through his shirt. I, on the other hand, looked as fierce as a tiger. For the first time, I might have been remotely serious. “I’m not going anywhere with you, buddy boy,” I said angrily. “You’ve got the wrong man, and let me tell you, you’ve fucked with the wrong heel today.” The officer began stuttering and laughing hysterically. “Oh my god…oh my god…look, I must have the wrong guy. Please, you aren’t in any trouble, I swear. Just don’t kill me, for the love of Christ…” Suddenly, just as I thought I had the upper hand, his radio turned on. “Officer Perry, are you there? Over.” “I’m here! I’m here! I need backup! I’m being assaulted!” I gasped, realizing what was happening. I dropped the man on his buttocks, and he groaned as he slammed against the cold, hard floor. I immediately turned around, grabbing the cart and running as fast as I could. Bobby was shouting something at me, but I couldn’t make it out. Everything was happening too quickly. I spun around a corner, and then another, and then another, until I found an empty corridor, and sat down tiredly. I knew the nurses were right on my tail, but they appeared to be gone, for now. I was just beginning to get comfortable. Just beginning to feel good. That’s when she appeared. She fluttered in, landing on my shoulder. She had a black mini-skirt on and a nice mid-drift t-shirt on. And her hair looked perfect, coming down to her shoulders and twirling up in a short curl. Gosh, she was beautiful. “Hello there, stranger. We meet again.” It was Tinkerbell, the first lady of Peter Pan. She was back, and Never Never Land was sure not be far behind. I’m in big trouble now. Obligatory Trash Talk Session Well, here I am, without a world title to my name. I guess I saw it coming, seeing as though I'm not getting any younger, and the youngsters here in the PCW just seem to have a step on me. I'm not worried though. You see, I've been in the big dance before, I've won the titles, and in the end, I will prevail over the injustices that plague this fine promotion. What happened at March Madness...well, it was just a fluke. I made a mistake and I paid for it. Big deal. Boda will have to pay the piper eventually, and one day we will meet up and he will find out what a legend is truly made of. That being said, I turn my attention to Drake Maxwell. A fine man and a fine competitor, but Drake, don't forget that I am still the lame. I have the fans behind me, I have my friends behind me, and come Friday Night Fusion, you'll see my friends in all of their glory. I have plenty of surprises for everyone and I don't plan on disappointing. I wish you the best of luck, Drake. We'll both need it for this match. Until next time, I'll see you in Never Never Land.
