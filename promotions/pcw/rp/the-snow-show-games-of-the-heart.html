Date: Season I, Episode 8 ﻿************************************The Snow ShowSeason I, Episode 8“Games of the Heart”************************************ Sometimes I wonder where we’re supposed to draw the line. As a husband, as a parent, as aman. It seems that every day, we push the boundaries of acceptance a little further and a littlefurther, and then one day, they’re no longer there at all. Maybe that’s what I love so much about Raven. She doesn’t let her boundaries move. She’s asmorally perfect as she was when we were in highschool, and those are the years where mostpeople develop into fuck-ups. That’s where it happened to me, anyway. I try hard in my marriage, you know. It’s just sometimes I see forks in the road and I don’t knowwhich way I should go - so I just fucking stand there. Like the useless piece of shit that I am, Ijust fucking stand there. I get so worried about fucking things up that I don’t maujmke anydecision at all. Then again, I’m worried about fucking things up because that seems to be a pattern with me. When I choose a road, it leads to certain disaster. Maybe both roads do. Maybe I’m like one ofthose fucking idiots on television that just can’t do anything right? Like I’m stuck with tales ona two headed coin. Shit. And all I do is whine. ~ Jason Adam Snow Location: Marty Verrelli’s Apartment - New York CityDate: January 2, 2003Time: 6:30 pm The movie was bad - both Marty and Krista agreed on that as they lounged about his moderatelylarge apartment. Marty was something of a movie buff, so he hated it more than Krista. As the day drifted by, she found herself more and more comfortable with the dark haired youngItalian. And the more she saw of him, the better he looked to her. The way his eyes dancedwhen he spoke to her kept her in awe for most of the time they were together. In the back of hermind, however, she couldn’t help but think of her brother-in-law. “So,” Krista said as she fell back onto the sofa in the living room of the apartment, “how longhave you lived here?” She looked around the place. It was quite large and she found it hard tobelieve that a twenty-two year old, who apparently wasn’t famous, could afford something sonice. He could use a house keeper though, she noted. “About six months,” said Marty as he stepped over to the black fridge in the kitchen. He pulleda beer out. “You want something?” “No,” Krista answered. Snow would kill them both if he caught her drinking. In the U.S, shewasn’t even legal age yet, which was the biggest downfall of them moving south. “I’m contentto just watch a little television.” “Cool,” shrugged Marty as he cracked the can open and came back toward her. He sat acrossfrom her, and couldn’t help but notice her inching toward him. “Do you know what time it is?” Krista asked, but suddenly, she found that Marty was closer -much closer. In fact, he was half on top of her, leaning in for a kiss. His right hand found itsway to her side and was creeping its way up her shirt. For a moment, Krista was surprised. Soon, she regained her composure and squeezed were wayaway from him, before his hand or his lips could make any progress whatsoever. “What are you doing?” she asked with narrow eyes, though her tone wasn’t threatening. “I thought it was pretty obvious,” Marty smiled. Krista couldn’t help but return a smile to him,even though deep down, she did feel a little offended. Another part of her felt flattered, but shecould never give in to anyone so soon after meeting them. “We can’t do that,” she said with a teasing smile. “It’s not like I’m trying to fuck you or anything,” Marty lied through pearly white teeth. “I justwas thinking, you know, we could mess around a little. I really like you and all...” Krista smiled - the young man was charming. “I like you too, Marty,” she said, “but I like totake things slow, you know? Maybe I’m different from most girls down here, but you can takethat or leave it. Besides, are you sure it’s really such a good idea?” “What are you talking about?” Marty asked. “You’re a ‘business associate’ of my brother-in-law,” she said with a smile. “I’m sure you knowhow protective he is of me.” “Snow?” Marty chuckled. “You think I’m afraid of Snow? He’s a great guy and all, Krista, buttrust me - I can deal with the big bad brother-in-law.” Krista almost laughed in his handsome face. “That’s what my last boyfriend said,” Krista told him with dancing eyes. At times, she wassecretly a little proud of her protective brother-in-law. No matter how ‘brave’ the men in her lifeappeared to be, or acted, none stood a chance at coming out of a bad situation with her brother-in-law. It hampered her social life in high school, because most young boys were too afraid ofangering her protector. “And now Jason’s likely going to owe him a handful of millions forwhat happened. I doubt you would be in a position to sue anybody, Mr. Verrelli.” Her eyesflashed playfully, and quite suddenly, Marty realized that the young lady knew all about the‘business’ that he and her brother-in-law were in. “Well,” Marty said, still smiling, “I’m probably not like your other boyfriends. I can handlemyself, Krista - you don’t have to worry about that.” He moved in a second time, and again,found himself rejected by her. “That’s not a green light,” Krista said as she pushed him away. “Just take it slow, Marty. Takeit slow and we’ll see how things work out.” Marty smiled and, despite her warnings, kissed herlightly on the cheek. She returned the smile. *** Location: Snow Residence - New York CityDate: January 2, 2003Time: 7:20 pm “Three fucking minutes before I go over there and rip his fucking spine out through his ass!”Snow said as he whirled around to face his wife. He had been spending the last half hour pacingthe kitchen floor, staring out the window into the street under the surveillance of his wife,Raven. “Language!” she scolded him. “Haley’s not even in the room!” She rolled her eyes - she detested cursing. “I’m sure everything is fine, Jason,” Raven reassured him as she stepped close and rubbed hischest through his white t-shirt. “She can take care of herself.” “Well,” Snow said as he turned back to the window, “she’s not the one who’s in danger of beingthe victim of an unreported homicide. It’s him that I don’t trust!” Snow scowled at theempty street at the end of his driveway. “She said she’d be home by dark, right? Well it wasdark two hours ago! I should drive over there right now and twist his little head off - I’d like to,you know that don’t you?” “And what message will that send to Krista?” Raven said with a sigh. “You said yourself thatit’s good that she’s seeing guys again, as long as she doesn’t rush things.” “I don’t trust this kid, Raven,” Snow warned her. “He’s a f-... he’s a weasel and I know it.” “You worry too much, Jason,” she said. “And honestly, I think it’s you that weshould be worried about. Have you any idea how serious these people that you’re running withare!?” Snow pushed her away and returned to pacing. “No,” he said to her with an eye roll, “that nevercrossed my mind.” “You’re going to have to talk to me about this, Jason!” she said sternly with a raised voice. “You can’t just shrug off the fact that you’re running with the mafia!” “There’s no such thing as the mafia!” Her glare narrowed and her lips pursed. Snow let out a sigh.
