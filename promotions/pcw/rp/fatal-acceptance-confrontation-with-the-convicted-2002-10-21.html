Fatal Acceptance: Confrontation with the Convicted

21 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10.21.02 Front page of the Friday, October 18th edition of the Chicago [I]Tribune[/I]… [font=courier new][B][U]Slipknot Assassin Caught in Chicago![/B][/U]CHICAGO, IL – For two months, a mysterious individual dubbed the “Slipknot Assassin” has run rampant throughout the greater Chicago area, kidnapping and murdering innocent victims without any obvious pattern. Due to the random nature of the grizzly killings, local police had been unable to find a lead to the killer’s whereabouts. At least, not until last Wednesday evening.In a frightening showdown atop Chicago’s biggest landmark, the Sears Tower, a man we now know as Jason Rollins held a young woman captive for nearly two hours while local law enforcement and FBI officials worked together to talk him down. The woman, Lacey Reinking, was the same woman kidnapped by the infamous Slipknot Assassin a month ago, and had been presumed dead. Quite the contrary, Miss Reinking became a focal point of the harrowing rescue on the rooftop of the massive Tower.Senior Official John Dougherty responded to the event, saying, “We’ve been hunting (the Slipknot Assassin) for weeks now, and didn’t expect him to come out in such an obvious manner. We are lucky things turned out the way they did.” Jason Rollins, the killer’s legal name, is now in custody and awaiting trial for numerous First Degree murder charges.Police are thankful that Jason Rollins’ brother, local professional wrestler and friend of the kidnapped victim, Rich Rollins, was on hand Wednesday night. Reportedly, Rich Rollins was allowed to pass police barricade and go to the roof, where he talked his blood relative into releasing his hostage and turn himself in. While Rich was unavailable for comment, local officials are extending him their thanks for…[/font] -Letting loose a disheartened sigh, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins folds the newspaper in half, tucking it under one arm as he nods to the security officer sipping a cup of coffee at his post. Smoothing his long brown hair back with one hand, Rollins scans the surroundings of the small containment area behind the courtroom, the cold gray concrete and thick steel prison bars blending together in the filtered sunlight. His heavy boots resound through the lone hallway like hollow bells, bouncing dully through the cell bars.- [I]My own flesh and blood[/I], he thinks to himself, the bitter taste of anger spreading across his tongue. [I]Thank God the news cameras can’t read my mind, see the things I[/I] really [I]think[/I]. [I]But[/I] he [I]can[/I]. -Putting a hand to his temple, The Renegade suddenly stops in the middle of the narrow cell hall, absently looking down his faded blue jeans and white tee shirt, trying to collect his thoughts. The prison bars blur for a brief second, but quickly come back into focus as the constant pounding in the back of Rollins’ mind begins to fade. Cautiously resuming his stern stride, Rich Rollins wonders if it isn’t fear that causes the pain, rather than complete lack of sleep. After all, it isn’t every day your own brother becomes a homicidal lunatic.- -Jason Rollins sits listlessly against the back wall of his cell, his arms bound tightly behind his back in a stark white straight jacket. The bright orange prison jumpsuit issued to him adorns his legs, and his thick brown hair lays tasseled above his closed eyes. Rollins pauses for a moment in the hall, a flood of emotions filling his entire body as he looks at this poor creature, a product of the same parents that birthed him. Gently grasping one the cell bars, The Renegade takes a deep breath…- Jason: I’m not dead, least not yet. With those boots of yours, it’s a surprise the entire jury can’t hear you coming from the other room. -Rollins reflexively releases the cell in surprise as Jason’s eyes open, those deep blue sockets mirroring his own. A handful of heartbeats pass in silence as both brothers measure one another, the tension thick enough to choke on. With the slightest hint of a smile forming at the corner’s of his mouth, Jason takes notice of the newspaper folded under Rollins’ arm.- Jason: Ah, I see you’ve been keeping up with current events, my dear [I]brother[/I] ([I]he spits the word, contemptuously[/I]). Pray tell, what does the good public say about my moment in the spotlight? -Coldly meeting Jason’s unwavering stare with his own, The Renegade responds with a tone to match.- Renegade: Why don’t you ask Mr. and Mrs. Lombard, the groom’s parents of that couple you murdered in Hannibal. -Rollins grasps the newspaper in a wad and tosses it into the cell. A few pages scatter across the floor, but the majority of the thick volume lands squarely in front of Jason’s confined form, looking him in the face as if mocking his very existence. The cover, Slipknot Assassin Caught in Chicago! jumps out in bold print, causing Jason to recoil as if slapped. Seizing the moment, The Renegade continues.- Renegade: Or how about Lacey Reinking? You almost tossed her off of a one hundred [I]fucking[/I] story building, Jason! You think people are arm-in-arm, demanding for your release? ([I]he motions toward the exit, near the security guard[/I]) That courtroom is gonna be full of people tomorrow, Jason. And I guarantee, every last one of ‘em will be screaming for your blood. Jason: My blood they can have. Lord knows I took enough of theirs. -Jason smirks, relaxing against the concrete wall at his back. His cold blue eyes growing wide, Rich Rollins speaks with a deadly venom dripping from every word.- Renegade: Do you think this is some kind of [I]game[/I]?! You murdered innocent people, Jason! You think you’ll get some cushy life sentence in a fuck-me-in-the-ass Federal prison? Think again, pal. Every member of that jury has sat in their homes for over a month now, watching the news, terrified they might be your next victims. They want you to fry, and with the evidence they have against you, Johnnie Cochran couldn’t save you. Jason: I want a trial, not a circus. Renegade: You’ve already turned this into a nightmare, let alone a circus. Jason: A nightmare for who, you… or me? -The question stops Rollins dead in his tracks, killing the response ready on his lips. An aura of serene calmness exuding from his presence, Jason Rollins licks his lips, carefully studying his brother’s face. A dangerous, glossy sheen fills his eyes as he continues, a quiet animosity building behind his words.- Jason: I watched you for years, Richard. You went off and became a big star, while mom and I forged a living on welfare. You forgot about [I]us[/I] you self-righteous bastard, so don’t come dumping your moral qualms on my shoulders, ‘cause they’re full enough as it is. ([I]he looks around his cell, frowning[/I]) I knew the moment you walked out the door you could have cared less about family. About loyalty. The only thing you’re loyal to is a big fat paycheck with lots of zeros tacked onto the end. Renegade: ([I]frustrated[/I]) Open your eyes, Jason! I went off and pursued my dream, and achieved what you couldn’t. Don’t blame me for being successful, because I tried helping the both of you when I had the money… Jason: Pity mail! ([I]he laughs, a bitter sound[/I]) We didn’t want your goddamned [I]charity[/I], Mr. Superstar. You’d have been better off leaving us alone, instead of bombarding us with useless attempts to buy back our respect. It’s pitiful, Renegade. -His voice speaks volumes in malice at the wrestling title, spurring a collage of feelings to shoot up and down Rich Rollin’s spine. Anger, depression, frustration… rolled into one stinging package, the emotions wreck havoc within The Renegade’s mind, hurting him to the very soul. The hateful rage contained within his brother’s eyes does little to soothe the pain. Taking a few steps back from the prison bars, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins desperately grasps at some semblance of composure, tearing his gaze away from Jason’s penetrating stare.- Renegade: I hope you burn. -And with that hollow phrase still ringing along the empty containment hall, Rich Rollins leaves his brother to his own thoughts, bound and shackled within the prison of his own actions. The heavy thud of his thick boots seem to be sucked into the void of desolation hanging in the musty prison air, not so loud as before. Nodding to the pudgy security guard, Rollins nearly exits the area before hearing a sound from his brother’s cell that would haunt him for years to come.- -The sound of laughter.- ~~ ~~ ~~ Lacey: Honey, are you all right? -Encompassing Rich Rollins with her waiting arms, Lacey Reinking rises from her seat outside the courtroom’s containment sector, her lush blonde hair hanging down between her shoulders in a shimmering ponytail. The Renegade only embraces her, trying to push away the conversation with his brother in favor of holding this beautiful creature. For the most part, it works.- Lacey: I know that must’ve been hard. But that [I]thing[/I] in there… Rich, that isn’t your brother. That’s a shell of what he used to be, a tired and bitter husk of a man that used to care about life. He doesn’t anymore, though… -The words almost choke her up before she speaks them, the memories of her captivity filling her mind. Rollins holds her tighter, returning her caring with his own.- Renegade: He just doesn’t feel anymore, Lace. It’s like screaming at a brick wall… there is no reasoning with him. He’s insane, end of story. Lacey: ([I]pulling back, timidly[/I]) It’s funny you should mention that. Renegade: Why? Lacey: Well… I overheard the defense attorney speaking with a court aid, and they’re pleading insanity. If they can prove it… Renegade: ([I]finishing the thought[/I]) … He’ll be locked in a hospital, not a prison. -They stare at one another a moment, the frightening concept of Jason Rollins being released from a mental institution weighing heavily on their minds. The Renegade just shakes his head, sadly.- Renegade: He needs to be punished, not saved. Too many men and women died because of his hatred for me. Lacey: Christ Rich, that’s a little harsh, don’t you think? I mean, he is your brother. Renegade: ([I]hollowly[/I]) Maybe. But better he be my dead brother than my brother “still at large,” terrorizing the nation. -Lacey watches his hard features, an intense frown furrowing her forehead. Gently rubbing the small of his back, she takes him by the arm, leading him toward the courthouse’s entrance foyer. She speaks softly, trying to lighten his mood.- Lacey: We’ll deal with that when the time comes. But hey, tell me about this PCW venture of yours. You did get the call, right? -Rollins laughs, the first sign of cheerfulness from him in days. Allowing himself to be led along, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins watches lawyers and their aids bustle along the ornate marble hallway, shaking his head in wonder.- Renegade: Yeah, as if there were [I]finally[/I] some good news… “… [I]finally, some[/I] good [I]news[/I].” ~[I]Fin[/I]~
