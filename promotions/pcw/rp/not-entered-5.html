Date: not entered 'Let me tell you about my inferior superior...' Corey stared at the piece of paper placed in his typewriter, those eight words written on it. He finally turned to his left and pulled a cigarette out of an ashtray. He placed the cigarette into his mouth and took a drag before stylishly blowing the smoke out and into the room; it rose to the ceiling until it eventually dissolved into nothingness. 'Let me tell you about my inferior superior...' Corey had written those words in the first thirty seconds, but it had been at least 10 minutes since then. The more he thought about that first sentence the more he liked it, but the more he liked it the harder it became to come up with something to match it. 'Ah, fuck it' he thought. 'I'm Corey Williams damnit, I do what I feel like, I don't think about things! I'm too powerful and talented to do that. That thinking shit is for the average Joe and less blessed wrestlers like Kano.' His concern for keeping up his self-image was usually the true reason for most of his actions, which was strange considering it was his actions that caused his screwed up self-image in the first place. 'Let me tell you about my inferior superior...' He scowled at the words, losing his thoughts to the anger and frustration that often got the better of him, not that he complained; rage is a powerful ally after all. Preventing himself from letting go and doing what he really wanted to, grab the stupid typewriter and throw it against the wall, CCW calmed himself, closed his eyes and then took a deep breath. Having prevented himself from letting go of his anger, Corey dropped the cigarette back in the ashtray and thought about letting go of his thoughts. He tried to clear his mind, he tried to think of nothingness, but just when he gave up, the answer came to him. He didn't know what it was, for to find that out would require thinking, he just knew that the answer, whatever it or the question for that matter was, had come to him. Corey's eyes snapped open and he calmly began to type what he believed was the truth based on the reality of the facts around him: 'Let me tell you about my inferior superior, a man named Jason Snow. You see, the word 'superior' is used to by two types of people, those who use it as an adjective and those who use it as a noun. It took me a long time to figure out which way Jason Snow meant it when he frequently used the word. The reason it took me so long it because I thought I already knew. I thought I understood Snow; he was a talented guy and a fresh face for the fans of PCW. When we formed New Breed together along with Chris Chase and Timo Bolamba, he claimed, as did we that there was no leader, or at least we thought he did. What we didn't know is that each time he used the word 'superior', sometimes right in front of our faces, that he was secretly claiming himself as our leader. But I cannot completely diss Jason Snow, for he was a great man... once. I cannot be sure at which time he stopped being one, the more I think the further back I can think of instances in which his lost greatness had disappeared, times when he hid behind the actions of others; desperate and clinging. Hindsight is indeed 20/20, for when the wool was pulled from my eyes I slapped myself. For the first time in my career I wondered how I could be so stupid, for the first time I doubted myself. But thanks to the guidance of Tyler Nelson I quickly moved past that, for this wasn't my fault, this wasn't about me, this was about Snow. Come to think about, in New Breed everything was ALWAYS about Snow.' Corey leaned back against his chair thoughtfully; again his hand went to the ashtray and pulled out the cigarette. After taking a short drag he turned the cigarette around so that the flaming end was facing him. He squinted at the flame with a look of respect; for if anyone messed around with fire he always got even. That deserved respect in Corey's mind, much like he expected the fans to respect him for beating up Kano on Tuesday and Snow on Friday. As his focus was removed from the flame and returned to the cigarette which encompassed it, Corey couldn’t help but give a short stifled laugh. It had been six months since his last cigarette, a nasty habit he had picked up back in the ‘hood with his homies. The last time he had smoked it was because his Samoan friend Timo Bolamba had gone missing. And now here was, smoking because his Psychiatrist had given a writing task to help him “deal with his problems”, he couldn’t but help notice that his Psychiatrist was of South African descent; it seemed black people had a bad influence on him. Perhaps that’s why he hated them so, for even behind chaos and controversy there must be reason, right? I mean, even Hitler had motives. Corey took a moment to think about Timo, the man who had rebelled against the rebels, and more importantly against Snow. Did he know something? Perhaps he had seen Snow for what he truly was? Perhaps he began to sense Snow’s sinking ship and jumped on board OSW. After all, although Snow did enjoy more success in the next part of his career than in any other, no one could deny that he had lost something. His win over Jeremy Howard was won with the help of Chris and Corey. When he was injured Corey and Chris did not miss a beat, they flourished under the chance of being able to fly without having to worry about not exceeding Snow... They didn’t want to make their LEADER look bad. And when Snow came back? Immediately he assumed the leadership and the belt that Chris had fought so hard for. The only time Snow ever shared that belt was so that he didn’t have to lose it to Big Alan Steele. To put it simply, Jason Snow, the once proud warrior, was holding onto his belt by cheating and HIDING! In Ultimate Authority they don’t hide, they stand tall; it may be because there are six of them but who cares. In the Ultimate Authority they don’t cheat, they annihilate! Again Corey’s thoughts turned to Timo. Had he known what was happening to Snow? ‘Probably not’ thought Corey self-assuringly. After all, although Jason Snow’s ability had began to deteriorate physically then, he was not quite over the hill mentally like he was now. Snow still had a similar attitude; he still had a killer instinct, he just went about achieving his goals differently. But it would be the deterioration of his skills that would eventually lead to his attitude changing, to him joining the dark side. Corey placed the cigarette back in the ashtray with a small cough. “It will be good for you” the Psychiatrist had said, this writing sure was provoking the thought process but it wasn’t doing any good to his lungs. As all the above thoughts circled through his mind, Corey placed his hands on the typewriter and began to type. ”You see, Jason Snow wasn’t exactly easy to get on with, he wasn’t what you’d call a ‘people person’, hell he’s still not. Even though he’s not quite the same guy he’s still the same asshole. He doesn’t exactly get on with anyone, even his friends have to stroke his damn ego just to get him to do something. The difference is that when someone used to call him superior they actually meant it with some degree. Now Snow is nothing but a joke, a comedy figure, part of a “whacky duo”. His constant references to his superiority are jokes as well. It is no longer a description of who he is, it is now just a word. You see in the Ultimate Authority we are all for the evolution of PCW. We, more than anyone else, promote the new era of PCW; the one that we began. But when you take something average to its peak, it has to keep going and eventually die out. This is what is happening to Snow, this is what happened to Kano. Snow is over the hill, past his peak. He has lost that killer instinct that he once possessed. Back in his day Snow was the Killin’ Villain, the Schemin’ Demon... But now he’s the Bridle Idol, the Savior of Behavior! Look, it may seem strange to an observing idiot, but I wasn’t going to let the most controversial stable in PCW history have a sudden morality check. I didn’t want to have to abandon a promo because “it might not be suitable for children of all ages.” Though Snow may have assumed leadership, New Breed was MY baby! No one ever doubted that. After all, it was my principles that New Breed was founded on, my ideas, it was I who represented the New Breed in its most difficult times and it’s most successful, it was I who claimed New Breed as the most successful stable of all time. I was good at claiming things, but Snow was better. It was he who claimed that all of my ideas were his. It was he that claimed to us that New Breed were equals, all the while claiming to the fans that he was the leader. It was Snow who claimed that he was the most successful man in PCW history, just months after I had claimed New Breed as the most successful stable in PCW history. Snow was good at claiming things, fooling people, but bigger than anything else was the way he fooled everyone into believing he was still the man he used to be. That lie has finally be found out, and Snow is now in for a World of punishment. He cannot be successful without his killer instinct, without the ability to take advantage without a second thought. Hell, he has a match against me in just one day and instead of thinking about me, the biggest threat of his life, he’s thinking about the well-being of his wife. He should be worried about the well-being of himself and, more importantly, his career, once I get through him on Friday. I will end Snow’s career, and no one will care. You see, Snow’s retirement is just another small part of the Ultimate Authority’s new era. But if you think the new era is just a roster change, or a few fresh faces, you’re wrong. It’s a plan that will keep PCW on top forever. You see, there are to elements in the new era plan, I have already mentioned them above, those people who use the word ‘superiority’ as a noun and those who use the word as an adjective. Those are the two types of people that occupy PCW. The people who use it as an adjective luckily enough all hang out in one group called the Ultimate Authority. What differs these people from others is that, unlike Snow and Kano, their talent will never deteriorate, never worsen. In fact, we will never even reach our peak because of our unlimited potential. Tyler Nelson is an example of one such being; he has been in PCW for a year and a half now and is still at the top of his game. I do not make many mistakes in my life, but there is one I’m man enough to admit I made, and that’s the fact that for the longest time I thought that Snow was one of us.... The other type of wrestler are the normal wrestlers, the one who go about their careers as normal, they evolve to their peak and then devolve to nothingness. Our use for these types are to make up numbers, after all, superior wrestlers are rare. The new era plan involves the constant flow of these inferior wrestlers going in and out of PCW. We bring them in, evolve them to their peak and then devolve them into nothingness. The problem is that new wrestlers will not evolve unless they have room to evolve to, and old wrestlers will not disappear unless we force them to. Guys like Snow and Kano already have one foot in the grave; it is our job to chuck the other one in. I ended Kano on Turmoil and I will end Snow on Fusion. For they are nothing but pawns we use to gain ourselves more power, to evolve. We let them believe they have power until they have no further use for us, and then we dispose of them quickly. So, Doc, you asked me what Jason Snow and Jon Kano meant to me? My answer to you is: nothing. I have a history with both of them; both have hurt me physically, mentally and even emotionally. Both have done things to make me extremely angry as of late. But the fact is that when I look at them I see nothing but a way to gain more money, power and respect. A way to bring on the new era by getting rid of the old trash lying around. And most certainly a way of evolving, moving past my past and looking towards the future, which gets brighter every minute.” Corey stops typing and looks over his work on evolution and moving forward before patting his typewriter satisfactorily. He was somewhat afraid of these new fandangled computers everyone kept talking about, he didn’t like to see even the slightest fraction power over what he did go into someone else’s hands, let alone a computer. ’That reminds me,’ Corey thought, ’Nelson wanted me to come by his office and talk.’ He had to leave soon in order to make it on time. Corey stared at his piece of writing, unsure what to do with it for the time being. It had certainly helped him, but more, if anything, to realize how little he needed to discuss the past. For Corey was a creature of the future, he believed that in his mind. After much hurried thought, Corey grabbed the piece of paper with one hand, the other reaching for his still alight cigarette in the ashtray. He took the cigarette and pushed the aflame end right into the paper; it began to burn and split. Corey stopped for a moment and scrunched the piece of paper into a ball. He then grabbed the aflame end of the cigarette and again pushed it into the paper, this time it slowly but surely caught fire. Corey threw the flaming piece of paper into a nearby waste paper bin, where it caught fire to even more paper. The flames began to rise, but Corey ignored them, he threw his cigarette into the flames where it disappeared. Corey turned and got up, for the first time we noticed his location, the main office of the CCW Enterprises building. Corey reached the coat rack just by the door; he grabbed his coat off of it and put it on casually. Meanwhile the smoke alarm had just gone off due to the huge flames. Corey turned and opened the door, slowly walking through it but just quickly enough to miss the kicking in of the sprinkler system. Corey’s secretary tried to stop him, tried to point out that his office was more or less on fire, but Corey just continued walking. The conflict, the burnings, the past..... He was leaving it all behind. The camera cuts to a shot of the flames... and we slowly fade out !The End!
