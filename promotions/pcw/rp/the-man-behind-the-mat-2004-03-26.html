The Man Behind The Mat

26 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/26/04 The last fading rays of sunlight beamed off the side of the blue Dodge Viper as it raced along the southern California highway headed toward the city of Santa Barbara. The shadows of the mountains slowly spread across the landscape as the sun began to fall from view. A man with long curly blond hair sat in the driver’s seat. Dark sunglasses covered his eyes, and bits and pieces of jewelry from his ears to his fingers glistened in the diminishing light. The man’s name was Brian Arson. At twenty-eight years old, Brian was financially set for life. Even prior to his successful wrestling career, Brian had plenty of money. His father, James K. Arson, owned one of the largest law firms in Santa Barbara and could have retired years ago, but he loved money and making millions more only made him happier. James spoiled Brian more than anyone could imagine. He was never around to be with him so he thought making up for it by other means would be just as well. Brian’s mother had been killed in a car accident when he was only seven and this left him in the hands of multiple nannies throughout the years of his childhood. Not being taught differently, Brian liked to flaunt his money and was incredibly over confident in himself. This turned many people away from him, but if one thing could be said about him, it was that he was very intelligent, and if one didn’t know this about him, after one meeting, Brian would make sure he did. Picking up speed now, Brian rounded a bend in the road. The hum of the Viper’s engine disrupted the placidity of the coastal mountain range, and as he came around the turn, the tires of the car screeched loudly, running dangerously close to the side of the road, leaving a trail of black rubber marks. Suddenly, from behind the blue speedster flashed the unmistakable blue and red lights of a California highway patrol car. Brian shot a quick glance into his rearview mirror and slammed his fist against the wheel, muttering obscenities under his breath. Nearly a full minute had gone by before Brian’s car came to a complete stop on the narrow shoulder of the road. Brian was nervous, but upon looking at him one would never know. He was always getting himself into tight situations and was blessed with the ability to look completely undisturbed. So when finally the officer was knocking on the driver’s side door, Brian just turned to the man with a smile, his sunglasses covering the uneasiness of his eyes, and slowly rolled down the window. “Good evening, officer.” The patrolman was busy writing and wasn’t paying any attention to Brian. The man finally looked up as if he had just noticed Brian for the first time. “Sir, could I see your license and registration please?” The officer stood there tapping his foot on the ground as Brian reached over to the glove compartment and pulled out some papers and handed them to the officer. The officer looked down at the papers and his eyebrows suddenly rose up as he realized who was driving the car. “Brian Arson? James’ son? I’m sorry about this but I have you down for breaking multiple traffic laws.” He paused a second, twisting his lips. “I’m just trying to do my job, man.” Brian seemed amused by the officer’s sudden change in tone. A grin appeared on his face and he removed his sunglasses. “I know you’re doing your job. You’re doing a great job, but you see, I really am in a hurry, and looking at these roads, you know I wouldn’t have been speeding if I had thought there would be any traffic at all.” Brian sounded so sincere that the officer actually believed him. “I know you want to get home to your family, have a nice dinner, maybe even pop in a late night movie. But you see all you’re doing here is keeping us from these more important commitments. Now, how about we just pretend this never happened and be on our way?” Brian knew he had the man beat and waited patiently for his response. “Yeah, I guess you’re right,” the officer said. He handed Brian back his registration papers and his license and smiled at him. “I really shouldn’t be doing this, but you’ve convinced me.” Brian took the papers and opened the glove compartment to put them back in when suddenly a bag full of a white substance fell out on to the floor of the passenger’s side. This caught the attention of the officer, and he soon realized what was in the bag. Immediately his hand went down to his side, and he stepped back a foot from the car. “Sir, please get out of the vehicle right now!” The officer’s tone changed as he barked orders at Brian. Brian’s face tightened up as he slammed his fist on the wheel again. He slowly stepped out of the car. The officer made him place both hands atop the car, and he unwillingly complied. “You know, this is really nothing. I mean, no one besides you and I know what’s going on here, so what’s the big deal?” Brian shouted over his shoulder, half-amused by what was going on. The officer began reading Brian his Miranda Rights as he patted him down to make sure he wasn’t carrying any sort of weapon. Brian realized that he was not going to be able to talk his way out of the situation at this point, so he began to resort to another means of escape. “This is just going to take forever, bringing me down to the station and everything. You know, if you were to let me go, I could see that you wouldn’t leave this situation empty handed. $100 might change your mind possibly?” Brian was getting a little nervous, but he had been in incidents like this before. “Sir, are you trying to bribe me? Do I really look that pathetic to you?” The half smile and roll of the eyes from Brian seemed to answer that question. “$100 isn’t good enough? How about $500? You know I could get you that money in cash if you just let me go right now.” “You’re not going to be getting out of this Mr. Arson. Now, please, this would go much quicker if you could stop talking.” The officer seemed to be standing firm but the thought of receiving $500 for simply letting the man go didn’t seem all that bad. “Okay, $1500,” Brian blurted out in a last attempt to get the officer to release him. This time, instead of rejecting the offer right away, the policeman paused a moment not saying anything. Brian was nervous and grinding down on his lip so hard that little drops of blood began to form. “I can’t believe I’m doing this,” the officer said as he took his hands off Brian’s shoulders. Brian grinned and turned around and shook the officer’s hand. “You’re doing the right thing sir.” He gave him a little wink and opened his car door. “Hold on just a second, and I’ll get you that check.” He reached into his car and grabbed a checkbook from a side compartment. He wrote the check quickly for $1,500, ripped it out, and handed it to the patrolman. The officer looked at the check once over and placed it carefully into his notebook. Turning around, the man headed to his patrol car without looking back. He was too disgusted with himself to look at Mr. Arson. Brian stepped into his car and adjusted the mirror so he could see the officer. He jammed the key into the ignition and slowly drove off. As soon as the police car was out of sight, the engine of the sports car roared loudly, and Brian Arson sped off, a little smirk across his face…
