Kids, Say No To Saying No

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Turmoil “Kiddies, where are you?” That would be the voice of none other than Rush Mason, recent LOSER at Red October to The End. It would seem that Rush has changed his ways as of his loss from that first line, but we have become pretty accustomed to hearing strange things in Rush Mason promos. “I’ve got all the stuff you can’t get on MLK Boulevard. Another strange thing to hear, but even more strange without any visual, but not for much longer… Rush: This whole kingpin thing should be a lot friggin’ easier. We come up on Rush standing at what seems to be a small stand, no more extravagant than a children’s lemonade stand, but he seems to be sitting there without anything near him, and the sign isn’t visible at the moment due to the camera angle. A Caucasian child no more than seven years old walks past the stand, and Rush yells over to the child. Rush: Hey kid, want some of this awesome stuff I’ve got? The kid looks at Rush, giving him a quick once over, and then responds in a pre-pubescent voice that has the same tone as a songbird’s song, but not exactly the sweet content. “My mommy said not to talk to people who look like the barbershop people or who look like the Green Mile man.” The kid then starts to run off, looking scared as he looks over his shoulder, half expecting the large black man to be running after him holding a 40 or a gat. Rush slumps his face into his palm and rests his elbow on the stand, sighing. Rush: Bon Dieu…it looks so easy on Cops. The camera shot goes up to the sign on the stand, which reads “Goodies” and has the word “Drugs” scribbled out above it. Of course, this would be strange for a normal person, but as before mentioned, Rush Mason isn’t really normal. Rush: Hmm…let’s see, how can I get kids to buy these drugs, er….goodies…? Rush goes into deep thought for ten seconds or so, at the end of which he snaps his fingers, his face lighting up. He races over to his Baby Blue 6.4 Range Rover, which is parked nearby, and begins sorting through thousands of things in the trunk.. Rush: Where did I put the secret weapon? Now, having already been guilty of a felony, one might think that Rush might not sink any lower, but keep in mind that Rush really hates losing, even if he deserves to get his ass kicked approximately ninety-nine percent of the time. However, some sort of ‘secret weapon’ to entice children to buy drugs wouldn’t be totally out of the realm of sensibility for a man who takes pride in having defeated a blind man in a fight once or twice. Rush: Ahh…there we go… We can’t see what Rush is doing, and we fade to the next scene, with small white lettering in the bottom of the screen. 10 Minutes Later… Now we can see Rush standing at his little stand yet again, but this time his ‘secret weapon’ can be seen attached to the stand. Rush has none other than… Multi-Colored Balloons Now, to someone who is not familiar with children, this might seem stupid, but obviously, you are insane. Balloons are the most powerful force in the world to children, they can smell the balloons from miles away, in between burping contests, and can use internal homing devices to get to where they really want to go: wherever there are clowns and/or expensive balloons. Rush: I swear, if this doesn’t work, then I’ll just have to walk around and wait for teenage kids to detect the aroma of weed and follow me around for the next week. As Rush speaks though, two youngish pre-teen boys walk along the street, wearing what would usually be called ‘skater punk style’ clothes. Many intelligent people would call them ‘rags and patches with a small amount of actually cloth in them,’ but then again, if intelligent people influenced fashion, there would be no such thing as elephant pants. “Dude, you catch that awesome new Britney Spears video? Her boobs are awesome.” “I know man, she makes my thing get hard, and it makes me feel all warm inside, kinda li…” Luckily, that’s the last thing we hear, as both the boys glance over at Rush, and look at the stand curiously, losing their train of thought. Rush: Uhh…hey you second-rate offspring of…I mean, hey dudes. Both boys exchange a glance, then cautiously take another step towards Rush, who pumps his fist out of the boys line of sight. “Dude, whatcha got there?” Rush thinks of how to phrase his words so that these young boys will understand the gist of what he’s doing. Rush: We’ll I’ve got some err…video games for you dudes. The two ignores Rush’s terrible attempt at punk lingo, because as Rush says ‘video games’ he makes a weed cigarette motion with his hand to his mouth, and the eyes of the two young boys light up. “Dude…hook us up with some of those video games!” Rush’s face now lights up with a smile too, and he helps the boys pile grams and grams of the crisp green leaves into the boys sweatshirts, pockets, shoes, and everywhere it will fit. “Dude, thanks majorly.” Rush: No problem, youth nimrods…just tell the people that Semore Smoke was doing the community a favor. The two youths nod, then head off to find some way to construct a bong out of a sprite bottle and an old Bic pen. Rush, happy with his work, walks off to his car, as the scene begins to fade out, but not before we get a final nugget of wisdom from the Voodoo Villain… “Isn’t this where my conscience is supposed to kick in or something?”
