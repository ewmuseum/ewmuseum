From Virtue to Vice: Element 1 – Love (Pt 1)

Date: Feb 16 of 2003 A.D. A jagged bolt of lightning cascaded earthward, illuminating rolling hills and evergreen forest as it encompassed the region of northern Vermont, a secluded wilderness area still un-bequeathed by the impending progress of civilization. A peal of thunder immediately pierced the silence of night and shook the cottage, sending the antenna crowning the roof swaying in near harmony to the to the melody of wind-chimes drifting below. Inside the mountain-isolated lodge, banker Desmond Muldoon sat bolt upright in bed. Beads of sweat flowed from his soaked brow and his breathing came forth in erratic, almost pained gasps. His head tilted back as another fork of lightning bathed the room in abrupt mock daylight, highlighting a silken hand as it lightly brushed his perspiration-soaked skin. “Strange time of year for a thunderstorm, dear,” muttered Krissy beneath the blankets. Muldoon’s head lowered and his breathing returned to normal. He brought his own shaking hand up to run through his hair, the effects of the horrid dream still lingering in his mind. Images began to slowly elucidate and flashes of his loathsome vision sparked sporadically in his brain. He grit his teeth, trying to block out the blasphemous impressions emblazoned in his mind. His only solace came when his wife’s smooth hands glided over his trembling shoulders and clasped across his chest. She applied a small kiss on his right cheek, noticing the alarmed twinkle in his eye. “Are you ok hun?” she inquired. She bit her lip, leaning forward to press her weight on his shoulder. “Another bad dream?” “Bad dream doesn’t do something like that justice, Kris,” he replied, drawing his shaky figure out of bed and lurching groggily toward the bathroom door. Another flash of lightning momentarily drenched the interior in a pallid radiance and animated the concerned look tarnishing Krissy’s elegant features. “You’ve been having these ever since we got here on Wednesday,” she remarked, her voice marred with a mix of mild fear and worry. “I don’t like it, Des. It’s spoiling our holiday and I don’t like it one bit.” Then again I didn’t like you fucking half the executive board. The thought came to him involuntarily and accompanied by a flood of images he wished he could block as much as the sinister dreams that had plagued the past week. The solitary holiday with Krissy, their second honeymoon. The youthful financier recognized that breaking nuptials with the vivacious secretary he now called his wife would trigger a positive uproar within the bachelorette community back in Buffalo; he would surely never have trouble getting laid with his appearance, and his lucrative career backing could only entice even more women into his bed if he wanted it. But for some reason even unknown to him, he adored the girl. He exalted her even regardless of that night a month ago when he thought his life was over. He had called home earlier that evening to inform and apologize for that fact he would be working late at the office again. She had told Desmond that she and her husband’s business partner, Tyrone Howard, would be discussing her portfolio and options while he was taking care of more pressing engagements for clients. Love-struck Desmond, naïve to the possibilities, shrugged the matter off and said fine. When he arrived home before he had indicated he would and walked nonchalantly into their bedroom to find her and Ty Howard doing much more than discussing portfolios, Muldoon felt his life was over. He still could remember only fragments of the incident, having thrown open the door to find Howard thrusting into his wife and grabbing for the closest item to attack the traitorous bastard. A broken lamp, accidentally damaged that very morning before he left, found its way into his hate-fueled hands. The last thing he recalled before his rage hit its summit and he blacked out was Krissy’s horrified scream and the lamp splintering over the head of his still awe-struck business partner, shards raining down onto the floor before the first of his own tears followed suit. He was incarcerated for assault and battery, but miraculously found himself released hours later by his rueful spouse as she posted bail. To this day Howard still couldn’t explain why he took her back, especially in light of her melancholy disclosure that Tyrone hadn’t been the first. He assumed it was partly because her intentions had been to help her ambitious hubby’s career, a fact that three more of his ex-partners could attest to her claiming when asked. But he also knew there was something much deeper to it; to take back a cheating wife with full forgiveness was no easy task, albeit her initially good intentions. He still awaited trial for the incident, but all charges had been dropped to a much lesser misdemeanor. Such was the luxury of wealth and the caliber of lawyers it draws. He and Krissy had reconciled and agreed on a second honeymoon, going to a place isolated and remote where they could bond again peacefully and without intervention. This lodge in upstate Vermont had seemed ideal. At the very least, it had seemed ideal until it had seemingly awakened whatever morbid cravings now possessed his mind on a nightly basis. Desmond was snapped from his soliloquy by the gentle embrace of his wife. He instantly forgot the terrible episode that had dominated the past month of his life and wrapped his arms around the warmth of his beloved companion. Regardless of the flashback, he had forgiven her and had again dedicated his life to making their life together a happy one. “I’m really worried,” she informed him with a hint of regret in her tone. “There’s something about this area, Des. I don’t know what it is. I....I didn’t even notice until yesterday....” Almost on cue they both became aware of the far-off warble of what had incited her apprehension the day before. The calling of whippoorwills echoed throughout the night, the rhythm of their call appearing to take on a tangible timing spaced apart by some concealed intent. Most eerie was the fact that birds of their kind didn’t typically become vocal until the dawning of spring. Desmond, however, didn’t want to appear shaken by the phenomenon and thus further rouse panic in his wife, and tried to convey this desire through his laugh. “It’s nothing, hun. I wouldn’t give it a second thought, birds are starnge animals. Just relax, that’s what we’re here to do right?” He planted a firm kiss on her forehead, the leveled his eyes to gaze into hers. They were ripe with dread. “But those calls....they sound so....” Muldoon laughed heartily. “It’s natural, Kris, trust me. It’s just the way they are. Even the locals used to be spooked by them. They have a belief that the whippoorwills are bringers of death, and that their cry is to accompany the breathing of the dying in preparation for stealing their soul off to the afterlife. It’s just local legend and it’s existed for centuries.' By looking into her eyes he realized his little tale had been having the opposite effect of what he wanted at first, but he had been unable to stop himself. He harbored a weakness for corny folklore, in college having read a story about the rustics and their belief about the whippoorwills that haunted the regions of the northeast. 'It's nothing,' he concluded staunchly. 'Let's get back to bed.' * * * She cheated on you, Desmond. Kick the whore straight to the curb! He tossed awkwardly in bed, the singular voice in his head haunting and mocking. C’mon! You really think she fucked those other yuppies just to give your career a jumpstart? You truly believe she didn’t get a charge out of another man’s cock inside of her? Are you mad? His arm swung down to hang over the edge of the double bed, garbled denials hissing from his lips. Oh please, you naïve fool. How oblivious can a man be? Your love for this dime-a-dozen slut is blinding you to the truth. The bitch got off using a few other studs, and then ran back to you like a lost dog when she thought of the money she’d lose leaving you. She doesn’t care about you, she cares about the money. Dollar signs are what keep the whore in line. Your bank account is what commands her loyalty. Anxiety warped Desmond’s features as he struggled in his slumber, his draped arm drawn up to cover his closed eyelids. Face it, chum. You’re the sucker here, the bitch has you where she wants ya. She didn't come crawling back to you....you went crawling back to her. Your love for the harlot is your greatest weakness. You know ya wanted to kill that backstabbing motherfucker when ya caught him pounding that floozy. And why not? A few years in the joint isn’t worth blasting that double-crosser into the depths of Hell? Your priorities are fucked up. An image finally accompanied the voice in Muldoon’s head. Into view in his dream appeared the figure of a stocky, shaven-head man donned in a black robe. The cuffs of the robe were trimmed in gold and silver, and a variety of occult runes were scrawled down the sleeves, back and chest. He wore a smirk, eyes blazing with an unholy passion. Evil emanated from the spiritual figure the likes of which Desmond Muldoon had never seen or felt. Through it all, the banker couldn’t shake a familiarity entrenched in the robed form. Love, especially yours, is a hindrance to common sense. It’s a character flaw, a bad practical joke instilled in every mortal by God's darkest graces. Some of history’s greatest leaders and warriors have fallen because of a blemish in reasoning called love. You’re no different. The background behind the ominous figure was veiled in darkness, and Desmond now saw parts of the darkness begin to shift and warp. It slowly faded into a backdrop that made him yearn for the shadows' return and caused him to question his own sanity, the same blackened kingdom he had envisioned nearly every night for the past week. Behind the orating figure he watched as things flew and walked that should have crawled, and hulking masses of decaying flesh devoured screaming men as human organs and limbs continually formed and dematerialized as ghastly appendages. Beyond these horrors lurked great abominations of whose terribleness he didn’t have the vocabulary to convey. Some of these merely lingered without deliberate purpose and others prowled with the bloodlust of their degenerate minions amongst the blood-drenched soil and jutting ridges dotting the desolate landscape. 'Soon you shall deliver penance for your frailty. The very thing you adore will become the idol of your hatred and curses as you waste away through countless eons at the servitude of the Great Old Ones.' The figure gestured toward the atrocities made flesh behind him. The foul shredding of flesh and dying screams saturated the air, their agony twisting Desmond’s stomach in his slumber. 'We will become your family. And here among us you will stalk eternally, helping to destroy all that exists in the third dimension and becoming forever a pariah to the realms of Heaven and Hell. We await....old friend.” As that final and contemptuous snicker rumbled from the dark man’s throat, his identity finally clicked in the memory of Desmond. They had met only once before his reputation had grounded him in shadow and gloom, and his identity was no secret any longer. Terror-stricken in sleep, Muldoon awakened once more with a fitful scream as his sanity drained. Krissy shuffled through the small cottage to tend to her quivering husband, his eyes as wide as half-dollars and his voice subdued. The only sounds he emitted the rest of the night were an occasional, unprovoked scream of horror, and a single spoken word that was usually accompanied by the former. “Shanahan....” And above it all, the whippoorwills continued their morbid song......
