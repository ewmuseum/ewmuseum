Just the Beginning... Part Two

4 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6-4-02 Just the Beginning...Part Two The scene picks up where it left off in Heaven Rayne’s childhood bed room; the bright pink shades are made clear as Heaven appears, still in front of her window, looking concerned about the people in the brown house across the street. Heaven’s dad is still sitting on her bed, unaware of the chaos going on in the neighborhood. Heaven decides that she had better find more out about what’s going on with these people who she suddenly finds herself being led to help. “Dad, who lives in that brown house there,” she asks pointing her index finger out the window and straight across. “Oh,” her father says placing a hand on his chin and knitting his eyebrows trying to remember. “I know it’s a young couple, I’m not sure what their names are, but I think their last name starts with a “C.” Why do you ask?” “Because, I have this feeling that those people need my help, especially the woman. I don’t know if her husband hit her or what, but I know she was beaten up. She’s hurt, and I need to help her,” says Heaven, “Dad, can you excuse me?” “Sure honey, do whatever you need to do. I’m going to have to get to work anyway, because I’m three hours late. When I get home, you’ll already be gone, and I’m going to miss you. I enjoyed having you home for the week, Sugar. I love you…oh, and good luck with wrestling.” “Thanks, daddy, we had a good week, didn’t we? I’m glad we had our little talk, and now I think I’ll visit home more often.” “I hope so,” says Russ Rayne, his tie swinging to the right as he leans down to kiss Heaven on her forehead. “Bye Honey.” “Bye,” Heaven says quickly and nervously. Any other time she would be thrilled to death about her father going into work late just to say a proper goodbye, and her heart would have been happy. However, at this particular time, she is too worried about helping the lady in the brown house, and she just wants her father leave as quickly as possible, so she can help before it is too late. Heaven hurriedly directs her attention across the street, and her binocular like eyesight comes back to her. The brunette lady is still crying face first into the bare pillow. Her blood has stained the pillow, because the woman’s face is buried so far into it in attempt to muffle her sobs. Heaven hears happy music playing, and silly animated voices singing joyicly along. It sounds as though it is coming from a television set. A child’s voice is heard, giggling as though he or she is without a care in the world. Heaven’s eyes now act more like a video camera than a set of binoculars as her view is expanded to the next room, even though the bedroom door is shut. Her gaze goes from the bedroom where the woman weeps, out the door to a living room. The living room is undersized, but seems spacious because of the small amount of furniture and belongings. The walls all look as though they were once white, but have become a dark yellow color, possibly due to many years of cigarette smoke building up to create thick stains. Along the far back wall there is a plain, dirty, outdated orange couch with cigarette burns up and down the arms and buttons missing on the cushions. To the left of the room are two large windows with no blinds or curtains, over looking an empty unkempt side yard or field. A cheap stand made out of some imitation of wood is placed along the same wall with a grey telephone and an overflowing ashtray sitting on top. The fact that there is an ashtray in the room is amusing, considering the cigarette burns on the couch, not to mention the holes and black stains on the ragged tan carpet. A small adjoined kitchen with a bare refrigerator and bare cabinets is adjacent to the right. In the front of the room is a small cartoon flashing, colored TV set, placed on the floor. A petite toddler of about age five is sitting on the cigarette tainted carpet, laughing along with the cartoons. The little boy, appearing skinny and pale, is wearing only a thin, faded white, cotton, Fruit of the Loom t-shirt which he has obviously outgrown, and he is exposing his little white ‘Hanes His Ways.’ The child’s eyes look hollow with dark purple lines underneath. Heaven feels warm tears of sympathy rolling down her cheeks as she tries to imagine just how long it has been since this little boy has had a decent, hot meal. The poor little kid sitting content with cartoons must have no idea that his mother is hurt. “Where is his father?” Heaven wonders aloud as she continues to tour the house with her eyes. Suddenly, she begins to feel furry run up and down through her blood. There is a woman, sobbing and bleeding in a bedroom, a deprived little boy sitting on a dirty floor all by himself, being entertained by a television, and the father is nowhere to be seen. It occurs to Heaven that the “man of the house” mustn’t be much of a man at all! He is most likely the one who beat up the lady, and he apparently doesn’t provide very well for the family. The child is probably used to living in a violent environment, and is most likely aware of the situation, but because he is so accustomed to it, can easily block it out with cartoons. Heaven knows that she can’t go across the street and say, “I’m an angel, and I’m here to help.” So she must go over there in disguise and be discreet about who she is. She wants to get the child something to eat and comfort his mother, but she doesn’t know how she can do so. Also, even if she helps these people today, the husband is sure to come home and make problems all over again. With that thought, Heaven has solved her own dilemma. What she needs to do is find the husband, and get him help. If she gets him straightened around, the family’s problems will be resolved. Helping this family is the first task Heaven has been called to complete, and she is still a little unsure of exactly how to handle it. The angel who made her aware of her calling told her that she would, in time, understand everything by and by. She said that naturally, the knowledge would come to her. Heaven feels that some of that knowledge is slowly coming to her, and that she should go with her gut feeling with this family. Heaven feels that she doesn’t have to solve everything for the family all in one day. She has made her first step by just realizing that there is a problem. She can go ahead leave her parents’ house to go home. She can go about her daily schedule with wrestling and everything else that occupies her time, but she has been shown this family, to alert her that she needs to help them. Because she is an angel, she can easily travel from the suburban town she lives in, back here to her home town, where this family is, and she can do so as quickly as she needs to. It is a four hour drive between the two places, but if Heaven is doing so for the purposes of her angel work, she can erase distance and appear where she needs to be. Besides, she can’t do anything more until she sees the husband and can start to help him. However, it wouldn’t hurt just to feed the little boy before she leaves town. At least he will have a full belly until she can go any further with the miracles she needs to perform for this family. Heaven hurriedly finishes the packing that she needs to do, and then she loads up her car, so that when she is ready to go home she can just leave. She goes back into her parents’ house and tells her mother what she is going to do. Heaven asks her mother if she can borrow some of her clothes for a disguise. Heaven finds a purple dress and a long black, fancy, women’s overcoat. She puts on black sunglasses, black gloves, and ties a black scarf over her hair which is pinned up underneath to hide its length. Even though it is June, this attire is necessary, because Heaven grew up in this city, and people would otherwise recognize her. She doesn’t want anyone to see her doing things like putting food on doorsteps, because she doesn’t want to be acknowledged. Dressed like January, Heaven walks out of her house and crosses the street. She knows that if she rang the doorbell nobody would answer, and anyhow she doesn’t want the lady in the house to see her. Very quietly, she creeps to the back door, and prepares to open it. She knows that the only person in the living room is the little boy. With her she carries a brown basket. In the basket is a Tupper ware container of heated up left over biscuits and gravy from this morning, a bag of peanut butter cookies, a carton of apple juice, a plastic fork, and a napkin. She opens the back door just a crack, and the little boy doesn’t notice her. She sits the basket down right inside the door, closes it hard to make sure he hears her, and then she walks around to the abandoned side yard, and watches through the window. Once she sees the child get up and look into the basket, she feels satisfied enough to leave. She hurries across the street, so that she doesn’t get caught. Once she is in the house, she changes back into summer clothes, and then she kisses her mother goodbye. With that she is on her way home. fin. I
