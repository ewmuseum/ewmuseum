Pinball Wizard

11 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8-11-02 “When I was a young boy I played the sliver ball….” Growing up in New York City was very different for me because you were taught to be tough and not to take any shit from anyone. Living that way of life had it’s benefits cause I wouldn’t be where I am today with out learning the ways of survival in life. But sometimes, living that way of life can take its toll on a seven-year-old kid. I was a normal kid at my age, but I learned different morals that a normal seven year old kid. Sometimes you just wanted to escape from it all. So a couple of friends of mine introduced me to the arcade down the street from my home in the City. I was never allowed to go to an arcade because I usually worked for my parents. So one day after school my friends took me to the arcade and that’s where I began to fall in love with the game, pinball. The moment I walked into the arcade the Pinball machine immediately pulled me to it. The lights, the music, the excitement pulled me into the game. It was like a drug almost; it pulled me in and kept me addicted to the fun and excitement of it all. My friends went to the rifle range and shot fake deer while I stayed behind playing the sliver ball. The first game I ever played, before Atari, Nintendo and Playstation. It was a baseball themed pinball game. The highest possible score you could get was nine hundred and ninety nine. There were no electronic screens that kept score for you; it was a small counter in the upper left hand corner of the machine. It wasn’t fifty cents either it cost a nickel. Back then that was a lot of money for a seven year old, so I had to spend my money wisely cause at that age I wouldn’t just spend my money on something I didn’t enjoy. I step up to the game. “Take me out to the ballgame” was playing as I stepped in front of the machine. I looked at the spring pulley that would shoot the ball out of the holder and onto the game board. I looked at the buttons that controlled the flippers. Testing it all out to make sure it worked. I was very cautious at first, not knowing would happen. The only time I had seen a pinball machine was on the television when I was watching “Tommy, The Pinball Wizard” the rock-opera based on the song “Pinball Wizard” written by: The Who. I reached into my pocket looking for any amount of change. I pulled out a nickel and two dimes. The nickel was shiny, brand new at the most. The date was 1981 and it was still in good condition. I looked at the nickel in amazement, looking at a small reflection of myself in the coin. The coin began to change my mind I what I should do. I wanted to keep the nickel very badly; it was in mint condition and was beautiful. I then peered my eyes back towards the pinball machine. The music coming from it, the lights flashing, the excitement waiting inside this machine, I felt like the machine was calling for me to play it. I looked back at the coin, then back towards the machine and let out a sigh as I made my decision to use the coin to play the game. I grabbed an old milk carton to stand on as I played the game so I could see. I took one look at the nickel and placed it in the coin slot. Then all the lights on the machine began to light up like crazy. Colors of red, green and blue lit up the area in which I was playing. “Take me out to the ballgame” got louder and the sliver ball appeared out of where it stays and approached the launcher. I pulled the launch stick as far as possible and the sliver ball took off like a rocket. The speed of the ball took me by surprise. I then configured by fingers to the flipper buttons. The ball bounced around off of these metal cut outs of baseball players. It then hit these circle shaped bouncers that lit up every time the ball would hit them. The ball then made it’s way towards the flippers. The ball peered down to the left side and I hit the left flipper button as hard as I could possibly do and the ball shot up back to the top of the board. A dinging noise would sound off every time it would hit something letting me know that I was earning points. The ball shot back down to my area right down the middle of the board. I hit both flipper buttons, missing the sliver ball as it went pass the two flippers. I looked up at the scoreboard that read one hundred and twelve points. Under that was a count of how many more chance I had left, that sign read two. I launched the ball back out onto the game board, the sliver ball when an even faster speed then the first time. It bounced around for a second and shot back down to the area where the flippers were and I hit the ball as hard as I could. The sliver ball shot off the right side flipper back up the board, hit a couple baseball players on it’s way, racking up my point total. The ball shot down the left side again; this time I hit the flipper button missing the sliver ball. I looked up at the point total which now read two hundred ninety-two. There was one more chance for me left on the game. I took a deep breath and launched the ball back up the board. The ball bounced around for a while, hitting baseball players and racking up more points to my total. The ball shot down the left side of the board and this time I was ready, I hit the ball back to the top of the board, the sliver ball bounced back around, racking more points. Sweat was pouring off the top of my forehead and palms began to sweat heavenly. The excitement and the stress of the game was running through my veins, pumping the adrenaline to keep me going, to have me succeed. The ball continued to bounce around and I continued to avoid the ball from ending the game. The third chance in the game seemed like an hour when in reality it was two minutes. After my best attempt to have the absolute best record the ball passed the flippers, ending the game. Disappointment upset my mind and body. I felt I let myself down. I peer up to the scoreboard and see that my final score was five hundred and eighty six. Deep down inside an emotion of happiness fulfills my body now. Knowing that my first time ever playing was a success and a very good display of trying my best. I walk out of the arcade knowing that there will be another day and I will play the game again. That day change my childhood for the rest of my life, whenever I had enough money to spend for the arcade I would go. Every time I walked by the arcade I felt like that the pinball machine was calling me, drawing me into to play the game and the excitement of playing the pinball machine is still the same as when I was a small child. As times changed, I changed and so did the pinball machine itself. But nothing is better then the original. The Who says it the best in their song “Pinball Wizard.” “When I was a young boy I played the sliver ball, ain’t seen nothing like it……”
