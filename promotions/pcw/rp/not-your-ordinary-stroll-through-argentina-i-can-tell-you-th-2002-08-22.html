Not your ordinary stroll through Argentina, I can tell you that!

22 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 22nd of August, 2002 The Pharaoh Chronicles: Volume#1: Adventures of a pharaoh. RP#1 for Third Dawning: Vs Frisco – Tomb Match “Don’t you just hate it when you find that you’re booked for a match that, technically speaking, has two people’s reputations on the line? If I beat Frisco at Third Dawning then that means my standing as a competitor will rise significantly. Whereas, if Frisco wins then I’ll always be dubbed as ‘that guy who was on a rise to fame, but was shown the ropes by a Hall of Fame member and got his ass kicked in the process’. The same thing could be said for my nemesis. If I win then his credibility as a PCW legend will drop, he’ll be thought of as ‘the man who was used to push The Pharaoh to the top’. But from where I stand it seems as though this match isn’t all about Godsdamn status in an organization! It’s about hate, it’s about determination; for the love of Horus it’s about a feud that’s been going on since the last PPV! This Sunday at New Dawning Frisco and I will go at it, one on one, inside a tomb - a place I never thought I’d have the misfortune of competing within. It’s not all that bad since my worst enemy is in there with me, and come on, if there’s anyone who knows more about ancient Egyptian burial tombs than me then I’d like to speak to him or her. The fact of the matter is Sunday’s coming around quickly and I have only a few days to get as much effort into training as I can. Right now, however, I’m over in Argentina. I don’t really think the mind has to wander that far to realize why I’m here, though. You see it all comes down to psychology in the end - Frisco knows that as well as I do, and it’s because of psychology that I’m here. So anyway I’ve gone on too long, haven’t I? I’m going now, as someone famous once said. Ta-Ta…” DATE: 22nd of August 2002TIME: 4: 45 PMLOCATION: Buenos Aires, Argentina. ..::.. Argentina wasn’t so bad. I’d seen better, that’s for sure, but it wasn’t as terrible as I’d first presumed. Who could blame me, though? Frisco was born here, he did live here and he was even running for prime minister of the damn country! Obviously one would naturally assume it’d be a Duat of a lot worse than this. Everywhere I looked groups of cheery tourists soaked up the sun or snapped away with tiny potable cameras at the countless beautiful sights. Trees of exotic breeds lined the pier Isiy and I walked along and the sun didn’t grant us any mercy. I basically got the impression that Argentina was a fairly laidback country, or maybe it was just the fact that this sector was for the tourists, the mainland could have been totally different, then again I’d never know. You see, I didn’t plan on leaving this spot – it was Frisco’s hometown after all, and what better way to annoy him? Isiy and I headed for the beech. When we first arrived yesterday we’d spotted its sandy shore and rolling sea, but unfortunately hadn’t had the time to go and visit it. So today was the day that we’d finally get a chance to soak up the sun, shrivel in the salty waters of the South Atlantic Ocean and irritate Frisco by daring to set foot on his home soil. ..::.. The Pharaoh: I’d have thought this place’d be full of scum like him, but I stand corrected – it isn’t such a bad place at all. ..::.. Isiy stared in disbelief. She was probably wondering why the Duat I’d suddenly changed my tune from being totally against the place to abruptly complimenting it. But after looking around the streets for a while, and taking in the sights and the sounds even I had to admit that I truly did stand corrected. Argentina was a beautiful country, it just had a tendency of producing scum like Frisco...::.. Isiy: You’re so predictable, Titu. I knew you’d say something like that after ten minutes of actually taking in the place. I told you the country wouldn’t be so bad, but no! You couldn’t have that. ‘Frisco was born here,’ you said, ‘so it must be a nasty, disgusting place’. ..::..She sighed. It was true, though; I had said some awful things about the country. I guess you can never judge a book by just reading a few sentences, eh? We walked on as the sound of the ocean’s water rushed up against the sides of the pier. It was almost hypnotic; surge after surge of relaxing sound had a way of ushering the mind into a state of relaxation...::.. The Pharaoh: Well maybe I was wrong, then. ..::.. Good Gods! Had I just done what I thought I had? Admitting I was wrong wasn’t something I went around doing everyday, or at least verbally doing it. I mean right here, where my thoughts are it’s fine, but out there, in the open, it just wasn’t something I did. For once I had to admit, though, that I had judged this place too soon...::.. Isiy: So how long have we got? The Pharaoh: A day or two, then I’ll have to catch the plane for the PPV, I’m afraid. ..::..Isiy’s brow furrowed. A question was coming up, and I knew it’d be related to safety in some way. She was a brilliant woman, but the fact that she was more obsessed with the possibility that the plane might crash, or the breaks may fail, or the Suplex might go wrong rather than the direction of my career was quite annoying at times. It was basically inevitable – something you just couldn’t escape. She folded her arms over her ample bosom in protest....::.. Isiy: Where is it going to be held? I don’t want anything happening to you, you know! I mean last month you went to Jamaica and the time before that it was bloody--… The Pharaoh: Look, it’s only in New Jersey, so stop worrying. And besides I’ll finally get my chance to kick Frisco’s ass once and for all! It’ll be more than worth the journey. Isiy: You know I don’t care about the match, Titu - it’s you that I worry about. If something were to happen to you then I don’t know what I’d do… ..::..She was very caring; the safety factor was the only bloody problem! The sun beat down upon us as we continued towards the end of the pier. Soon enough we’d get a chance to lap up the warmth of the blistering Argentine sun. Another group of tourists moved past us as we slowly edged towards our destination, a light breeze picking up speed as the day wore thin...::.. The Pharaoh: I know, but you can’t expect everything I do, and everywhere I go, and every single trip I take to turn out in disaster. It just isn’t like that at all--… ..::..I suppose after the day was over I’d be eating my words and admitting I was wrong more than once! Damn that Frisco and his army! No sooner had I arrived in his country that he had a dozen armed men tracking me, or so it turned out later on. I stood there, the barrel of an AK-4K looking me in the face. Like anyone else in a situation similar to this, especially those who hadn’t done anything wrong, I asked the obvious. Later I’d wish I hadn’t...::.. The Pharaoh: What’s the meaning of this?! Soldier: I’m placing you under arrest! The Pharaoh: Pretty good English for Argentinean scum like you!! ..::..Don’t you just hate it when you say something you regret later? It was mere seconds after I’d said the words that the end of the gun came crashing into my gut. I hit the floor with my knees and then fell face-first to the concrete, pain surging throughout my frame. It wasn’t as though I’d actually said anything wrong! It’s just that some people take jokes the wrong way. It’s remarkable, really, how some people can take offence to the things people utter. Then again I guess there was a little venom in my voice...::.. The Pharaoh: Surely…that…wasn’t…the…right…way…to…negotiate…with…a…tourist. ..::..Heavy breathing impaired my words. They came out as dry huffs at a time, steady intervals stalling each one from stringing together a steady sentence. I struggled to my feet as Isiy burst into tears. Gods, I hated it when this happened. She was so bleedin’ emotional. It was all all right in the ring - that was acting and she acted well. But this wasn’t something I’d expected! It was obvious Isiy hadn’t seen this coming, too. Who would? The beech was right there, as well; but a few meters away and now I couldn’t get there because of some asshole solider who was placing me under arrest! The embassy of Egypt would not be pleased, to say the least! I hugged Isiy close to my chest and stared the man up and down with disgust...::.. The Pharaoh: Under what means are you arresting me? Soldier: You are not welcome here, Egyptian worm. ..::..And there was me thinking I’d sounded venomous. One thing was certain about the way he spoke to me in comparison to the way I’d spoken to him, however…he wasn’t joking around!..::.. The Pharaoh: I see…and could this have anything to do with a certain homosexual dictator? First name Francisco, second name Cassius? I believe he works for a wrestling organization named Primetime Championship Wrest--… ..::...I was beginning to think sarcasm wasn’t the best way to go about this sort of thing. Once again I dropped to my knees, the pain from the gun’s end being driven into my ribs heaving through my torso. Wearily I got to my feet, looked at the teary-eyed Isiy and then at the man. His stern Argentinean face hadn’t moved a muscle from the second he first pointed the AK-47 at me. Was he for real? I was an enemy of his country, or at least his boss, and he didn’t seem to be enjoying the beating he was putting me through. My eyes led off from his mug to the crowds of people watching, surprise in almost every one of their sets of eyes. Looking a little further back I saw a few more groups of soldiers surrounding the perimeter. Why, and how, did I ever get into this sort of trouble? ..::.. The Pharaoh: Ok, Ok. I can see that the odds are stacked against me, so I’ll go quietly. My wife doesn’t need to be put in jail, though. She’s done nothing wrong to Frisco, or your people! ..::..The soldier’s gaze fell upon my wife. I wondered what he was thinking. She was a pretty little thing; a person I cherished dearly and I knew the looks men gave her off by heart. Yet this guy didn’t even flinch at the sight of her gorgeous body and pretty face. ‘He was a soldier and only a soldier, a man forged in the heat of battle to destroy the enemies of his general!’ were they the only words he lived by? Something wasn’t quite right here. He didn’t seem like one of the typical soldiers Frisco’s other battalions had to offer... ..::.. Soldier: Very well. She must go back to her hotel at once, however! ..::..I sighed and turned to look at her weeping face. A loss to Duke Williams wasn’t half as upsetting as the expression about her soft visage. She was distraught, and I knew it. Frisco’d pay for this abomination; well he would if I managed to free myself from the grip of his Argentineans ‘police’. I couldn’t stand seeing her like this, it almost broke my heart, but I was strong and held to my ways. The patience of the soldier grew thin and so he coughed as politely as he could to catch my attention… Turning I saw him give me a stare that said ‘I still have the end of my gun to move you along, so don’t temp fate.’ …::.. The Pharaoh: You’d better go, then. ..::.. I groaned heavily; after all, this wasn’t at all what I’d expected. I had come here to annoy my foe, but he’d cleverly countered my attack with a more forceful approach of his own. He truly was as smart as people made out, but he still pissed me off. I’m not giving him praise or anything, so don’t even ponder that, but he wasn’t the kind of guy to trifle with unless you were as intelligent as him. I should have seen this coming, really, but as per usual I didn’t…..::.. Isiy: I can’t leave you, though! Not with these…people! ..::..Her loving blue eyes turned to the soldier. Even the puppy dog expression didn’t seem to work on him. He didn’t move, his stern expression staying the same all the while. Realizing this she tried another, more dynamic, advance...::.. Isiy: You can’t arrest him just like that!! I mean, he has to have done something wrong--… Soldier: He isn’t welcome here, and neither are you!! So do as he says and leave or I’ll have you shot! I have orders from El Presidente to shoot you all at first sight, but that’d hardly be fair, no? The Pharaoh: My thoughts exactly…nice to see we agree on something. ..::..Wrong choice of words, perhaps? The man’s face soured, nevertheless, as soon as I mentioned that there could be a chance of a remote similarity between the two of us. Frisco’s influence had obviously been heavily engraved in his mind. Isiy folded her arms, shaking her head in disbelief. ..::.. Isiy: Why don’t you just give us both a chance to leave the country, then? Surely that’d decrease the amount of time you people’ll have to waste on questioning him and stuff? ..::..From my side of things her words certainly didn’t come out as a question. The last few moments of Isiy speaking had sounded more like a demand. Women, I’ll correct myself, women scorn have a tendency to get their own way through how they mouth their words. The soldier didn’t look too impressed. He heaved a long sigh and produced a set of silvery handcuffs, curled them around my wrists and began to escort me away from the area. I went quietly since more trouble and arguing could start a rapid fire of bullets towards my skull – and it wasn’t something that I liked to think about happening to me. I was actually thankful that the man had allowed us to keep our lives; it was quite possible that he could lose his for doing so and there was me thinking all Argentineans were the same. At first I’d thought that Frisco’s influence had been fully driven into his brain, but the fact that he’d spared us made me think otherwise. For now I wasn’t going to argue about the arrest for now…but later was always different matter all together...::.. Isiy: No! You can’t--… Soldier: Someone make sure she gets back to her hotel! ..::..I turned my head and watched as a small battalion of Argentineans milled around my wife and escorted her off the pier. The crowds of hushed people stood watching in shock as an armoured van pulled up and I was forced inside, my head been pushed down so that I didn’t smack my cranium against the metal frame above the sliding doors. Once inside I was forced to sit down on a steel bench that lined the vehicle’s interior, other heavily armed soldiers surrounding me as though I was some sort of escaped maniac...::.. The Pharaoh: So where are we headed? ..::..I laughed nervously and received a glare of annoyance from the other men in the lorry for doing so. At length the soldier from earlier sat down in front of me, steepled his fingers against his forehead as though he’d had a long day and sighed...::... Soldier: You realize that in sparing your worthless life I could be put up for treason, yes? ..::..The engine roared as the van began to move slowly at first, but picking up pace, down the pier. What was this guy talking about? Frisco wasn’t eve in charge of the country yet and he was talking like he’d done something deeply wrong against his country. It occurred to me that perhaps Frisco had more power in Argentina than I’d first thought..::.. The Pharaoh: Frisco isn’t prime minister yet so you haven’t done anything wrong. ..::.. The man sighed. Already I had a feeling I was deeply wrong. The other men in the van didn’t seem to understand English, which I guess was a good thing for me and for the soldier. If they had understood what we were saying then we’d both be dead by now...::.. Soldier: Frisco has his own men, he has his own army, and he has his own air force. I’m a member of his armed forces, in fact I’m a highly ranked member of his armed forces, Titu Xansiv and for not killing you I have dishonoured the code I swore my own life by. I said I’d stick by El Presidente until the end, and now I’ve gone against my own word… I have become a man of treason…a man of disgrace…I am not worthy to be the leader of the troops sat around us. Do you understand?! ..::..I nodded in agreement; at the time it felt more than wise...::.. The Pharaoh: Yes… Soldier: I figure that the only way I can manage to bring myself back up to the rank I’m suppose to be of is to kill you… ..::..I gulped. Thoughts of my life were preparing themselves to flash before my eyes, but I put them on hold until the man finished what he was saying...::.. Soldier: But killing you while your hands are bound and while you’re unarmed would be more cowardly than disobeying one of General Frisco’s orders. I don’t live in cold blood, Titu Xansiv. The only way I can live with myself is to let you go, no? So prepare yourself… ..::..It all happened so quickly - a distant blur in the dim light of the armoured truck. One minute I was bound in the handcuffs the next I had the man’s key in one hand and a long silver knife up against his throat in the other...::.. Soldier: DO AS HE SAYS! DO AS HE SAYS! The Pharaoh: I’ll kill him! Don’t temp me, damn you! Now put down your guns! PUT THEM ALL DOWN!! ..::..Immediately the rest of the guards froze, lowered their guns and watched as I got to my feet. I took off the cuffs and prepared for my daring escape. It was going to be dangerous but if I didn’t flee now I may never have stay alive long enough to see the PPV. The vehicle was rolling on along the streets of Argentina at about 15 miles-per-hour. I’d have to jump and roll, I knew. The driver was concealed behind a thick sheet of plastic; he could hardly hear what the Duat was going on outside the truck never mind what was going on in the room behind him. If he’d have known that an Egyptian maniac holding a knife was threatening the life of an officer then I’d have surely been dead by now. The combatant whose neck I was tempting with the knife’s blade looked up at me and winked… nobody else seemed to notice. I looked about the room frantically and edged slowly towards the back doors. I knew the sliding ones on each side of the van would be of no use to me, and so with the soldier still held under the watchful gaze of the knife I kicked the doors open forcefully. The air pressure nearly sucked me out but I held my ground and let go of the captain, pushing him into some of the men sat around the lorry’s bench. Jumping, I hit the floor and rolled as the van continued forward. Almost immediately I heard the clicking of guns being loaded...::.. “No! No! Target is out of reach! Hold your fire! We don’t want to hit the pedestrians!!” the distant voice of the soldier who’d helped me earlier bellowed in his home tongue. ..::..I looked about. People in the streets were watching in dismay as an escaped convict stared back in their directions. How ever did I get myself into these situations? I jogged out of view down an alley of exotic stores as the van speeded off out of vision...::.. The Pharaoh: Damn that Frisco! ..::..I was a Godsdamn man on the run now...::.. TO BE CONTINUED…
