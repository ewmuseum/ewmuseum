The Punishment

13 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 13th Feb 2004 The whole of the Clarksville, Tennessee are is in turmoil, the media is going crazy. Local catholic priest Father Jerry Flanagan has been accused of molesting one of his choir boys, whose name can’t be mentioned for legal reasons. There is sheer turmoil, Father Flanagan was a much loved member of the Catholic community in Clarksville, and people feel betrayed and shocked. There are serious questions of the morals of Catholicism. After being charged Jerry Flanagan is making his way back to his home with escort from the police, he is without doubt about to be a victim of hate crimes. Flanagan leaves the car which zooms off as soon as he steps out. The entrance of his house is surrounded by hollering journalists and screaming civilians yelling abuse at him. “YOU’RE SCUM!” “Father Flanagan, did you do it?” “KIDDY FIDDLER!” “Mr. Flanagan how do you feel about this public display of hate?” “FUCKING NONCE!” “This is the Clarksville Evening News; can we have a few words?” “YOU’RE GOING TO HELL!” Flanagan bows his bald head in shame and absorbs this bombardment of abuse with a pinch of salt. He takes the walk of shame, taking each step whilst trying to hold back tears. A few weeks ago he was a much loved moment of the community, then one moment of madness. All he did was put a hand on the boys shoulder and he totally overreacted. But the press are making a huge deal of it. Deep down he feels he has done nothing wrong… but he can’t be sure. Are these people right? Is he a nonce? Is he going to hell? He opens his door and steps inside, just in time to avoid an egg that was flying straight at his head. It cracks against the door. He locks the door, bolts it and drops against the door, resting and taking deep breaths. What a hectic few hours. There is still a disapproving riot outside. Anti paedophilia slogans are being shouted loudly. After a few seconds chants start up. He moves into his lounge. He throws himself in a seat, his natural reaction is to pick up the television control and watch the news, but decides it would be a better idea if he didn’t. He just sits, quietly, trying to block out the undeniable hate. Flanagan is a short man, a completely bald head and a grey handlebar moustache. He is rather fat. He is of similar build to his protestant counterpart Eric Sagar. He sits down, the noise is a murmur. But it’s very cold in his house. He shivers a bit and rubs his shoulders with his hands. He hears a whistling. He must have left the window open! He clambers to his feet and heads to the kitchen; sure enough the window is open. Thank god he got to that before any of this vigilante mobs did. He yanks it down shut. As soon as it slams down, he thinks… …“I didn’t leave the window open” He looks around. How was it open? He steps back into the living room. “I always knew there was something wrong with you, Flanagan.” Jerry turns around, in his big comfy arm chair, with a glass of scotch on the rocks is Eric Sagar looking very cosy. He is sat with a big grin on his face. “Other than being catholic of course.” Sagar giggles. “How did you get here, Eric?” Father Flanagan asks. “You’re all the same, you Catholics.” Eric Sagar ignores him totally. “Pretend to be holier since Christ then as soon as an opening comes up you nonce up a child.” “Now, you know…” “A CHILD!” “But, I…” “You can’t make excuse Flanagan. You sexually assaulted a child. You abused your power and trust to attempt to rape him! YOU’RE SICK! I HOPE THEY LOCK YOU UP AND THROW AWAY THE KEY!” Sagar scoffs is disgust. “You’re no angel.” “I’m no homosexual paedophile!” Sagar lashes back. There is an awkward silence as Flanagan paces round the room stressfully rubbing his forehead. “Just get out.” Jerry breaks the silence. “The day I take orders from scum like you is the day someone takes Catholicism seriously!” Flanagan steps forward aggressively in rage, but quickly steps back after he gets his brain in gear. He knows if he lays a hand on Sagar, he would probably cry “rape”. Sagar knew what he was trying to do and stays slouched in chair, not flinching, staying cool and calm. “You deserve to be punished.” Eric Sagar growls. “The lord shall punish you!” “Don’t you think this is punishment enough? Being hounded by the press? Having Vigilante mobs after my blood? Having death threats? Hate mail? I don’t feel safe anymore; I’ve got people breaking into my house. Is that not enough? What else is there to punish me with?” Father Flanagan pleads. And if by instruction a crashing right hook slams to the side of his head sending him hurling to the floor. Behind him stands Propaganda who was virtually silent until the echoing thud of his fist flying into the side of the priest’s face. The priest flops to the floor like a fish out of water, he is motionless, face down on the floor as a pool of blood starts to form under his head. Eric Sagar cackles evilly as he gets up off of his seat and him and the Power Himself leave.
