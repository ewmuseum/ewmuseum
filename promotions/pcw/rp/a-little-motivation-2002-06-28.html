A little motivation

28 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/28/2002 *The scene opens up in a decrepit old gymnasium with dull brick walls and lighting fixtures hanging from the ceiling that look like they haven't been cleaned since the Eisenhower administration. In the center of this gymnasium, is a wrestling ring, where a man of about fifty five years of age is leaning on the ropes talking to a group of young men and women, sitting on the floor of the gym.* Old man: You people need some motivation. You learn and learn as much as I want to teach you on any given day, but I see you losing your motivation. So I put in a call to my most prized student and asked him to come down here and give you some motivation. This student of mine is a world wide wrestling legend. He has wrestled on six different continents, and he has raised the bar time and time again for wrestlers that stand nearly 7 feet tall. He is currently a PCW superstar, and he is the Man who Rocketh the Mosteth. Ladies and Gentlemen, please give respect to this man, Jon Kano. *Kano enters the old gymnasium and stalks toward the ring. Kano is given a standing ovation from the group of students. Kano is dressed in baggy blue jeans, black Doc Martin boots, and a black t-shirt with Gold and Glory's logo across the chest. Kano rolls into the ring and hugs the older man, his former teacher.* Kano: Ladies and gentlemen, I am YOUR REAL legend. I have worked damn hard in this industry for six years, going on seven. Nearly seven years, I poured everything I have in my body into this industry. I bled, I sweated, I broke bones, and I have nearly been killed. And you know what? I would do it all again. That is how great this business is. I sacrifice each day to play this game the way I play. I am not going to bullsh*t any of you. Some of you will go through the training here and will make it to be able to call yourself a professional wrestler. And some of you won't. You are not alone. I remember when I was sitting in those chairs and this man was standing up here giving us living hell. I watched him reduce grown men to tears. These men weren't small, they were 250, 300 pound former linebackers and this man broke them. The training here is the most extensive, and most brutal of any in the industry. I was fortunate enough to make it and I am now one of the biggest superstars in the world. I wrestle for Primetime Championship Wrestling and I wrestle with the very best in the industry. I go out night after night and get to do what I love, entertain. I entertain everyone in the crowd, from the front row all the way to nosebleed section. That is just the kind of stand up individual I am. You all can learn a lot from this man to my right, he knows all there is to know pertaining to professional wrestling. So now I have said my piece, who has questions? *A young, beautiful, petite little blond woman raises her hand.* Kano: Yeah, you, blondie. Blonde: Well what does life on the road do to your personal life? Kano: It sucks. My wife is in Las Vegas at our house doing god-knows-what. I trust her enough to leave her in Vegas, but from time to time she goes on the road with me for awhile. I am on the road 200-270 days out of the year. I hate being away from her, but its money in the bank. If you have a good, solid relationship with whoever then it will survive the career, but if you don't, then the relationship will crumble like a cheap built house in LA during an earthquake. *A large, portly white-trashy looking man raises his hand.* Kano: You, Bang Davenport's inbred cousin, whats your question? Man: Is wrestling as hard as this training? Kano: What the matter? Are you sore? Are you tired? Man: Yeah. Kano: So? Do you think that is an excuse? Do you think the fans in one town care if you are sore because you gave everything you had the night before to some other crowd? Wrestling is tough. It takes a special kind of person to be a professional wrestler. You not only need to be physically gifted and in shape, but to be mentally strong also. Older teacher: Well ladies and gentlemen, class is dismissed for today, I'll see you at eight o'clock tommorrow morning sharp. *All of the class files out of the gym, leaving Kano and his former teacher left alone in the ring.* Teacher: I can't tell you how proud I am of you and your accomplishments, Jon. Kano: Why is that? Teacher: Well you are a living legend, after all, but one thing troubles me. Kano: What is that? Teacher: This Sunday, I think you don't know what could happen. Kano: Big deal, I got a match with 'Controversial' Corey Williams at Gold and Glory. Teacher: Jon, this isn't some ordinary match. It is a last man standing match. Kano: And your point is? Teacher: You and Corey will walk in, and will do your usual thing. You two will beat the living hell out of each other, both of you will probably end up in the hospital due to the extent of your injuries, and the only way one of you can win is to incapacitate your opponent to the point where he can no longer stand. Kano: So it will come down to who is more brutal and heartless to win the match, right? Teacher: You are correct. Kano: So the winner should be someone who has proven time and time again that he will stop at nothing to win and has no reservations about kicking the hell out of anybody and everybody in his way? Teacher: Again you are correct. Kano: So this match is right up my alley. Teacher: Possibly. Kano: What the hell is that supposed to mean? Teacher: I am just saying you need to prepare, and if you do, then you should come up on top. Kano: Should? Let me tell you exactly what is going to happen at Gold and Glory. Corey and I will walk down the ramp, and then we will roll into the ring. The bell will ring, and Corey and I will give it all we have. The action will spill out into the crowd, into the back, in the aisle, and back into the ring. He and I will make each other bleed and sweat. He and I will put on a show like none other. We will make the crowd thirst for more carnage. And finally, Corey will make that one mistake and I will hook him and KANOPLEX HIM STRAIGHT TO HELL. I'll lay on my back for an instant and then get to my feet, with him laying at my feet. The ref will count from 1 to 10 and then history will go down. That page in history will say that on Sunday, June 30, 2002, at Gold and Glory, Jon Kano was the LAST MAN STANDING AND HE FOREVER PROVED HE WAS WHAT REAL LEGEND ARE MADE OF. *The scene fades to black as Kano and the teacher shake hands and Kano exits the old gymnasium.*
