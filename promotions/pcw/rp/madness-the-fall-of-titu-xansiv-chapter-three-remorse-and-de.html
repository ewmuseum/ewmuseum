Madness: The Fall Of Titu Xansiv - Chapter Three: Remorse And Denial...

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Heh Date: March 22nd 2003Location: Cairo, Egypt The road ahead stretched far and wide. The sun, as always, shone down unmercifully upon the already cracked earth and burnt dry whatever remaining moist lingered. The coach - a rickety, aged contraption, and dragged forth by two slim camels - rode onward; its destination, the driver had been told, a vast mansion some miles away… “Where better a place to rest than Egypt, Titu?” murmured Horus. “Yes, what better place than Egypt? Your home…your slaughter.” Mocked Anubis. “Shut up,” whispered Xansiv… At the helm, the man driving tiresomely pulled back his hood and looked up at the baby-blue sky. He sighed. It had been a long day. “You must turn on them for us, it’s your only way out of this sickening predicament.” His passengers, people who he personally deemed strangers to the smouldering land, remained within, hidden from the harsh heat. He spoke to them every now and again, and had thus far learnt very little of their identities, although he could has sworn he'd heard that voice before… Anubis leant forward, his silvery mask stopping but inches away from the grimacing visage of Titu Xansiv. “You know that, right?” “Stop this madness!” he shrieked pitifully. Smirking, Anubis walked onward, “There is no other road…” 'So, what are you two doing here?' he asked at length. There was tense silence for a moment as he waited patiently for an answer. 'Visiting,' came a male voice from behind him. 'Just coming home for a while.' “How can you even call Egypt your home?” He laughed, “Ha! Home…pfft!” The coach juddered suddenly, its front left wheel hitting a pothole in the earth. The driver coughed politely, as though attempting to break the quickly solidifying tension. 'Just visiting, eh? You live here, then?' pausing, he thought over his words, 'You used to live here, no?' he corrected. The air was stifling. Egypt had always been like this, even more so like this when the pharaohs of old had ruled the vast plains. The driver wiped his brow, beads of sweat clinging to his dark skin as he steered the camels to the left. The rough road soon grew smoother as they passed into a quaint town, somewhere close to their initial destination. “Xansiv fell to his knees once more, holding his face in his hands. “Why do you mock me?” “Because, you don’t deserve better.” Titu looked towards Horus for support, but received none. The eagle-masked deity stared down at him, disgust, he knew, probably lingering beneath the tarnished metal. 'Something like that,' said the male voice once more. The man, his face darkened by the shadows inside the coach, peered out at the people gathered in the streets. He let go a remorseful sigh, his eyes tightening as he gazed upon their saddened faces. “Don’t let it get to you,” barked Horus eventually, “They’ll get over it one day.” 'I'm guessing you want to know why they look like that, huh?' said the driver, his voice quite sullen. “You can’t hide forever,” exclaimed the God of the sky. “One day you’ll be found. As unfortunate as it sounds, you must face your fears. Don’t be a coward all your life, Titu.” “A coward?!” scoffed Anubis, “He’s much more than that! You give him too much credit.” Grains of sand brushed past him as he lay at their feet, powerless against their condemning words. “Go…both of you…Go now!” 'It's because death has come to them,' he carried on, emphasising the importance of the word 'death'. 'They don't really know what's going on, but I have an idea.' There was a moment of silence again. 'Moses has risen once more; he's brought about the great tenth plague again! Just as he did years ago!” Silence. 'You know,' the driver began again after a few seconds, 'I think I recognise your voice; you on TV or something?' 'You must have me mistaken.” The male voice exclaimed quickly. 'I am no one important, just a visitor…as I have already said.' “Deceiver!” “Cheat!” “Scoundrel!” 'No, no, I know you! Your voice, it's familiar! You have Egyptian background, no? I can hear it on your tongue, mystery passenger; I know you do. You might sound posh, but you can't trick a trickster!' The man, his tone restless, said: 'Yes, fine, I have an Egyptian background, friend. I am Egyptian, but I wish not to engage in this conversation any longer. Please, just continue to drive…' The driver smiled, almost pleased, 'Touchy subject, huh? You're afraid to face your people, no? You shouldn't be ashamed of your past; you should embrace it.' The man behind him heaved a tiresome sigh. 'I love my country, why can't you see that?' “Murderer…” “Sorry? I meant nothing by it—” The passenger waved a hand dismissively, promptly interrupting the driver: “Just forget about it.” Passing back out of the village, a female voice, obviously that of the second passenger, littered the airwaves. The driver, though it was clearly none of his business, listened in, his expression ever changing as the conversation between the man and women engaged. “Are you all right? You’re sweating!” “You killed them all…” “Every…last…one!” scolded Anubis… “Why did you do it, Titu?” Said Horus, remorsefully, “Why?!” “I’m fine…honestly, I’m just fine. It’s—it’s just the heat,” the man chuckled nervously, “Gods, it’s hot, isn’t it?” “You look awful. Have you slept since the Pay-Per-View?” “Of course I have!” the male voice rumbled, “I’m no idiot, you know!” “I wasn’t implying you were—” “Just shut up, please!” “Just shut up, please!” The man fell back into his seat, sobbing. “Just leave me alone.” The woman shuffled towards him, bringing her arm around his shoulders lovingly. He shook her off. “You don’t understand. I just want to be left alone.” “Nothing more than a tyrant!” Anubis scowled hatefully. “JUST LEAVE ME ALONE!” “Trick yourself all you want!” said the jackal-faced God. “Trick yourself all you damned well want!” And they faded, Xansiv looking after them sadly. He was alone again… She backed away, eyes wide with surprise, as the coach rattled on and Titu curled up into a ball, shaking feverishly. The driver, daring not to intervene such an unhinged display, put this all down to domestic issues… Some people could be that shallow after all.
