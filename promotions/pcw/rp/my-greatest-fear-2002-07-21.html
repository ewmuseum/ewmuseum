My greatest fear?

21 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 21, 2002 I hate flying. Those Wright brothers had no right to try to get us humans into the air. It’s not right. I kicked the seat in front of me, still frustrated over my fear of flying. As I did, the man sitting in front of me turned around to see what all of the fuss was all about. I apologized, reassuring him that it wouldn’t happen again. Now, did I know that for sure? Of course not. Hell, it probably would happen again. “I’m sorry about that, sir,” I replied, trying to give him a smile. It was hard though, because when you feel like shit, being nice isn’t exactly the first instinct. “It was okay the first time, but this was the fifth time..” “I know, I know,” I said, interrupting him, “it won’t happen again. I just have a little fear of flying, that’s all.” He nodded, shrugging a bit. The man looked like he was in his forties, with graying hair and a very apparent receding hair line. This was the sort of guy I made fun of, but I’ve been noticing my own hair beginning to cut backwards. Ten years from now that might be me. “I used to be afraid to fly. Of course, I’m a business man, so usually when you’re thrust into a situation that you don’t want to be in, you get over it.” “What kind of business are you in?’ “Selling carpets,” He replied, seeming very proud of himself. “I have my own business where I go around and sell carpets that my wife designs. We’ve sold over three hundred carpets up to this point.” “Sounds interesting,” I said, lying through my teeth. The truth was, I just wanted this guy to shut up so I could continue with my thoughts. Making small talk wasn’t my greatest strength. He took the hint and turned back to his seat, where I promised myself that I would not kick his seat again for the sake of not having him turn around. I looked over to the seat next to me where a small boy sat strapped in. He had been staring at me ever since his mother placed him next to me. I tried to ignore him. “What’s your name?” The little boy asked me. We had been on the plane for over an hour, yet NOW he decides to talk to me. I licked my bottom lip and nodded my head at him. “Tom,” I answered, immediately looking forward again. Maybe if I made it seem like I didn’t want to talk, he might take the hint. “What kind of name is that?” Annoying, annoying, annoying…. “What’s your name?” I asked, giving him a sarcastic smile. “Billy,” He replied proudly. “My middle name is Herbert and my last name is Jordan. My mommy says I’m the second in my family with this name.” “Billy Herbert Jordan…” I repeated. “You would think they would have figured out the first time with that one…” He looked confused, not understanding what I meant by the comment. There was the beauty with talking with children – they never understood insults. In fact, they took them in stride. You have to love that. “Why don’t you like flying?” The simplest question seemed more than obvious, but then again, people have different reasons for being fearful of anything. Perhaps now I would confront that fear. “I don’t like being in a situation where I’m not in control,” I replied. “When you’re in the air, you have to rely on the pilot, on the plane itself, on the weather going on outside…I want to make sure that everything is okay. I’m afraid when I don’t know what’s going to happen.” “Isn’t that the fun part though?’ “What is?” “Not knowing what’s going to happen.” I shrugged. “You’re pretty smart for such a young kid. How old are you?” “Nine years old and four months.” “Well, that’s pretty old,” I joked. “The truth is, that’s what makes life worth living sometimes. But when you’re not in control, you struggle to find out who you really are.” “Do you ever feel that way?” I leaned in to him. “All the time.” His mother come by a second later, unstrapping him and taking him to the bathroom. I leaned back into my seat and gave a short sigh. Soon I would be at Turmoil, refereeing a match between three of my worst enemies. Am I going to be fair? Should I be fair? Questions, questions, questions. But there was one piece of business I knew I had to take care of, and I would do so before that match began.
