No Humor...Suspense...

13 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/13/02 Over the last few weeks Carey J. Nowski has been expressing anger over nobody truly believing him. He always claims that he’ll get everyone to notice him. He always claims that the world will idolize him once they see his true knowledge. Even his own teammate Stephen Cairns has a very tough time believing his theories. Carey started his wrestling career by searching for a government conspiracy. He had this idea that wrestling was fixed by the government, and he wanted to prove that to the world. So far he hasn’t proved anything. He entered wrestling on a journey for himself. There were no allies to assist him in his journey. It was all up to Carey. He took a trip to New Zealand in order to clear his mind off of wrestling. A cricket fan over in New Zealand invited Carey into a cricket game. This egotistic fan was none other than Stephen Cairns. Stephen picked a fight during the game, and Carey noticed his true rage. Stephen was arrested, but Carey knew he had to have this man as an ally. He bailed him out of jail, and then Misplaced was created. They’re truly Misplaced. These men rarely get along, and they really are different people. You have a jock and a nerd on the same team. It makes for comedy, depression, and many other different feelings between these two men. Carey and Stephen ventured back to the states to start their tag team career in PCW. Carey still had his goal to prove that wrestling is a government sport, but it faded as they started wrestling more. Stephen played a role in convincing Carey to drop the nerdy things and concentrate on wrestling. Carey did so for a while. Lately though we have seen Carey start to back up his old theories. He’s been upset over people not believing his theories. He’s still on his mission, it just took a detour. Carey is alone in a hotel bedroom. It’s silent in the room. Nothing is running, nor is anything making a peep. It’s almost as Carey is silencing the room so he can concentrate. He lies back on the bed and props his head on pillows. His eyes flicker but he keeps awake. Something seems to be nagging Carey once again. He grabs a notepad and pen that is lying on the desk next to the bed. He just writes. He writes, writes, and writes some more. His hand moves slow and smooth along the paper. I’ve had enough of this. If nobody wants to believe me I guess I’ll have to prove them wrong. I let things distract me from my true goals, but now I’m back on track. I’ll continue to wrestle at the fullest, but my focus is back to what I believe in. He pauses for a moment and puts the pen to his mouth. You can see the full concentration in his eyes. Anger, glory, and other expressions appear to cross his face. Once again he picks up on his writing. From the beginning I got into wrestling due to my passion for my work. I let wrestling become my work though. I regret ever doing so. My life has changed drastically, but I want to restore the part that I need. I need my power rush. I need to make people see that I am right. Carey wraps up his writing at that. He tosses the notepad and pen back on the desk, and he once again rests his head on the pillows. Yet again his eyes start to flicker. This time it appears he has cleared his mind completely. A knocking is heard at the hotel door. Carey gets off of the bed to answer the door. He opens it up, but nobody is there. He glances both ways down the hall, but nobody is in sight. Carey shuts the door and lies back down on the bed. Just as he gets completely down, the door knocks again. So once again Carey gets off of the bed to answer the door, but once again nobody is there. Carey grabs his room key and enters out into the hall. The hall is very empty. An eerie quietness can be felt in the hall. It’s almost like Carey is standing in the middle of a horror movie. So Carey walks all the way to the end of the hall until he reaches the elevator. He pushes the down button and it opens immediately. It’s almost as if it’s been waiting for him. Carey pushes the button inside the elevator for the lobby, and he descends down five stories. The door opens to the lobby, and it also is silent. He exits the elevator to look for an employee, but it’s also empty. There’s not an employee behind the desk, or there’s not a tourist rushing to their rooms with a cart full of luggage. It’s just empty. Carey gets a nervous look on his face, and his hand starts to twitch. Sweat is dripping down his face as he glances around the deserted hotel lobby. The paintings all look old, and the color of the room seems faded. He makes his way to the doors that lead out to the city. There has to be people in the city. Something isn’t right. A whole hotel doesn’t go deserted in midday. Someone must be around here somewhere. People don’t just vanish. That can’t happen. There has to be many people running around in the city. There’s always someone running through the streets with their baby, with groceries, or just talking on there cell phone. It’s impossible for everyone to vanish. Carey makes his way to the doors, but his attention is still on the empty counters. It almost seems like everyone just left what they were doing. Coffee cups are placed randomly around the desk, and food is left on the tables at the restaurant. Newspapers and magazines are left opened on there pages. Finally he takes his eyes off of the hotel and enters into the city of Seattle. Downtown Seattle always has people running around in it. Carey stands outside, and worry comes across his face. The city is empty. The wind starts to pick up which blows napkins, newspapers, and other items that would be found in a city. It’s the type of scene where you expect a tumbleweed to come rolling down the middle of the street. There are no people, no cars, no animals, and no action. It’s like a ghost town in the midday Seattle. Carey goes out further into the street to see if any of the stores are open. Nothing appears to be though. The lights are out in all of the buildings, and absolutely nothing can be noticed going on inside. This can’t be a joke anymore. Maybe people could’ve set me up with the hotel thing, but this is no joke. Whole cities aren’t deserted as a joke. That’s impossible to organize. Something terrible has happened. The world must be vanishing. People are being sucked to nothing while they were just innocently carrying on their life activities. There are no frantic mothers, no bullying jerks, no shy men, no nervous businessmen, and worse of all there is nothing. I feel like I’m living in a movie. I need to find out what’s going on. The sky gets darker and darker as Carey walks further into the city. There is still nothing or nobody to be found. It’s like he’s been placed in the middle of a very scary version of hell. As Carey enters the pit of Seattle the sky goes almost black. A huge thunderstorm breaks out. Rain pours down on the empty streets of Seattle, and Carey is standing in the middle of the street. Lightning flashes, thunder roars, and rain beats down on Carey’s personal living hell. Carey makes a break for cover on the side of the rode, but he is shocked by headlights approaching through the thick rain. A very old fashion Cadillac pulls off on the side of the road next to Carey. A window rolls down, and a hand motions Carey into the car. He walks over to the door, and it automatically opens for him. He looks into the car, but it’s to dark to see anyone. Carey is so anxious to get out of the rain and find another person he wastes no time to sit down. The door shuts and the window rolls up. The Cadillac moves and then starts to cruise down the empty Seattle highway. The rain is still pouring to the point where it’s blinding outside. Carey looks across the car to see a black man in all black driving the car. It’s still to dark to make out his exact facial features. The man sits silently and mysteriously until he finally turns his head to Carey. Mysterious Dark Man: “I know how you feel Carey. I feel the same way. I just want people to believe me and you.” Carey gets a look of confusion and fright on his face. This man is very creepy to Carey. Carey J. Nowski: “Who are you, and what’s going on? Why is the city deserted?” The dark man continues to maneuver the car without any trouble in the horrible storm. He just keeps his eyes locked on the road. Mysterious Dark Man: “Oh come on Carey. Lets not play these games. We all know that we’re both correct. Our theories are both correct, but the public doesn’t realize this. That’s why they’re gone. They didn’t see our knowledge of life, so they don’t get to live life. It’s that simple.” Carey’s fright deepens and he starts to shake again. He can tell that this isn’t an ordinary person, if it is a person at all. Carey J. Nowski: “I want you to let me out of now. Please pull the car over.” The mysterious dark man gives off a chuckle. He turns his head to Carey and gives him a deep look. Mysterious Dark Man: “I can’t do that Carey. You’ve caused so much trouble to this world.” Carey goes to open the door, but somebody puts a rag around Carey’s mouth which distracts him. The mysterious dark man gives off another deep laugh as the rag must’ve been drugged. Carey struggles for a moment, but after so long he is out. We see Carey waking up very sweaty. He opens his eyes and looks around him to reveal a hotel room. It was a dream. It was all just a very bad nightmare. None of it ever really happened. Carey takes a look around him, and everything appears to be normal. It’s not all normal though. There is a knock at the door. Carey is startled by the knock, and he stiffens up. His hand starts to shake in fear again that he’s living his nightmare. He grabs a desk lamp to use as a weapon when he answers the door. Carey places himself behind the door, and he opens it with one hand. As the door swings open Carey jumps out in front of it in an attacking position. A frightened shout comes out from the person at the door. Carey drops the lamp to see that it’s his teammate Stephen Cairns. He gives off a very loud sigh of relief as he glances to make sure it really is his teammate. Before he speaks to Stephen he runs to the window overlooking the city. He pulls back the curtain to reveal a busy Seattle city. With a huge sigh of relief he goes to greet Stephen, and fortunately for him Stephen is still there. No vanishing acts this time. Stephen Cairns: “What the hell was that surprise jungle attack back there?” Carey J. Nowski: “It’s…nothing. I just thought you were someone else.” Stephen Cairns: “Yeah, well how about we look through the peep hole before we go attacking our teammates?” Carey J. Nowski: “I had a dream, or at least I think it was a dream. It wasn’t exactly right. I felt like it was trying to tell me something. Everything vanished except for one person, a dark person. I don’t think he wanted me to carry on my conspiracies. That’s exactly why I must! I’ll prove to the world and The Pretty Cool Guys that nobody knows the truth besides me.” Carey again shows anger over his conspiracies. Without continuing the conversation pounces back over to the bed, and he lies down. Stephen sits on the other bed silently. There’s no conversation for a long time…a very long time.
