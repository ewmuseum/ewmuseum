Back to the Bottle

28 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/28/02 The last time we saw the young PCW Champion, he seemed to be going through some internal hardships. He stormed out on the beautiful Tammy Hart, one of the world’s most famous Supermodels. It doesn’t take a genius to notice that his mind doesn’t seem to be on wrestling these days, either. The scene opens inside a bar - not the place where we would expect the recovering alcoholic, Jason Snow. But there he is - sitting on top of a bar stool, staring down at the counter between his elbows. The bartender stands in front of him wiping a glass, giving the young champion a suspicious eye... he recognizes Snow, but brushes off the notion by considering Snow to be just one of his many customers. The bar is quite empty on this rainy night - Snow is the only person we can see, other than the bartender, though we can hear the voices of others. Snow looks much the same as he always has in his PCW tenure. His long blond hair flows down the back of his neck and touches his shoulders - left untied on this night. It’s damp, so we assume that he hasn’t been in the bar for long. He’s wearing his usual jet black leather pants and jacket, both gleaming from the pouring rain outside. And finally, we see his Snake Skin boots... this is a picture of Snow, exactly how he’s always been. The setting, however, is disturbing. Snow and alcohol simply don’t mix, as has been shown many times in the past. The bartender, a rough looking old man with a bald head save for some white stubble on the sides, leans on the bar in front of Snow... Bartender: “Ya miss her, don’t ya?” Snow’s cold blue eyes rise to meet the experienced brown ones of the old bartender... Snow: “What in the he1l would YOU know about the feelings of a Superior Individual?” The old bartender chuckles and leans back, still wiping the large beer mug dry. His smile is somewhat menacing. In his day, one could bet that this particular bartender was quite a tough customer... Bartender: “Mister, I serve the loneliest hearts in America. I’ve seen a million like you.” Snow’s eyes go to “glare mode”... Snow: “Trust me, plebe, you’ve never seen ANYONE like me.” Bartender: “You’d be surprised. ...Wednesday, past midnight, staring at the bar without ordering a single thing yet - I’ve seen a million like you, son. A million. Now, what’ll it be?” The moment of truth... will Snow take that drink? Will he set himself back all of these hard earned months of sobriety... nothing matters to Snow now... almost nothing... Snow: “R-....” He hesitates... Snow: “Rum and Coke.” Now Snow is speaking the bartender’s language. The older man leans forward, sets the beer mug on the table and looks Snow in the eye while scratching his white stubbled chin... Bartender: “Now, when you say Rum and Coke, are you talking about a shot glass rum and coke, or...” The bartender points to the beer mug... Bartender: “A REAL Rum and Coke?” Snow points to the beer mug and the bartender nods. He then reaches down beneath the bar and begins mixing the drink - with noticeably more rum than coke. He hands the mug to Snow who lifts it to his lips... the smell brings him back to old days... Bartender: “You gonna drink that?” Snow has been staring at the drink for some time now... smelling it... finally, he takes a sip... Snow: “OH MY GREATNESS!” The strength of the drink overwhelms the Killin’ Villain, who hasn’t done any serious drinking in quite some time... The bartender chuckles at Snow... Bartender: “Not a drinker, huh?” Snow, having accepted the burning sensation in his throat - remembering it - looks up at the bartender... Snow: “It’s been a while.” Bartender: “That’s what they all say.” Snow smirks and then, in an attempt to show off, he raises the mug to his lips and takes a long, hard, drink from it. For a moment, it looks like he’s going to hold it down easily, and then a bitter look comes over his face as the burning sensation overwhelms him. The bartender laughs again. We know, that in his day - which surprisingly enough were his late teen years - Snow would easily have been able to drink the concoction without any difficulty. But now, as he nears the “ripe old age” of twenty-four, things have changed for him... twenty-four is so young to be old.... Bartender: “You want me to top it off with Coke, son?” Snow: “I’ll have none of that! I’m a Superior Individual! I’m the Killin’ Villain, d@mn it! My stomach can take anything that YOURS or any other local oafs can...” Bartender: “You sure do want to drink that, son. You wanna talk about it?” Snow raises an eye... Snow: “About WHAT, peasant?” Bartender: “The girl! The GIRL! It’s always about a girl!” The bartender stares at Snow curiously... Bartender: “Unless... it’s about a guy...” It takes Snow a few moments to realize what the bartender is implying, but when he does, he rises straight up on his bar stool... Snow: “Are you implying that I, in all of my Greatness, am one of the HOMOSEXUALS!?!??” The old man backs up, chuckling, he holds his hands up defensively... Bartender: “Settle down, tiger. You just never know with young people these days, that’s all. So do you want to talk about her?” Snow smirks, the thought of his estranged wife makes him pick up the mug one more time, and amazingly slams down the entire mug. As the bartender chuckles, and the alcohol starts flowing through Snow’s system, the scene fades to black...
