Robin Hood, Robin Hood, riding through the bronx

27 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 27th November Now the celebrations really start. What better way to celebrate then too… then too… The man known as Arden…was actually called Abner. PCW had phoned through to make the correction within hours of Kai leaving the room. Other then that… everything was and would run smoothly. Time 1245Date November 27th FF#2 The front door buzzer rang loudly. Muffy glanced at the digital clock on the table next to the blaring stereo. One of her one-thirties must have run out of shopping money or finished lunch early. It was only twelve forty five. She lost count on the repetitions and decided ten more humps. It would not kill them. Gena would get downstairs. Two floors below, Gena fumbled for the button under the desk, then turned the page of Time Magazine to find out where the best jewellers in Switzerland were located. She felt something cold against her forehead. Her breath caught in her throat as she looked up. All she could see was the gun. It was blue-black, and close enough so that the conical tips of the bullets were visible in the cylinder. She licked her lips. The voice was soft and relaxed, with the slight sibilance of a Chinese accent. “Do not be afraid. No one is going to hurt you. Stand up and turn around.” She pushed her chair back from the desk and followed instructions, getting a brief glance at the man with the gun. He wore a blue and white Walé Adiyemi beanie, with a hood concealing his face. There was a blur of other concealed faces by the door. “Put your hands behind your back,” he said. When she complied, he taped her wrists together. “Now open your mouth.” She resisted the dryness of the handkerchief only long enough to provoke increased pressure from the gun at the nape of her neck. Another strip of tape held it fast. She was pushed into the open coat closet, then the door closed and she was left standing in the dark. She leaned against the door when she heard the lock turn and allowed herself to slip to the floor. When she was seated, the fear set in and she began to shake. Kai Kua put his gun into his jacket pocket, motioned to his two companions, and began to walk up the carpeted stairs of the health facility. Despite their effort to be quiet, Laszlo Dushan heard their oncoming steps through the half open door of the second floor office, where he sat eating a sandwich. He leaned forward from his desk to see who was there. There was a moment of indecision as Slavic Heroism battled with two years of New York experience. Tradition won. Instead of reaching for the phone, he catapulted out the door on to the landing. “Vat you doink, you guys, here?” he asked in a loud voice. In sweat pants, sneakers, and a sleeveless t-shirt he was brawny and formidable. “Go in the office and lie on the floor,” Kai said. Laszlo sized him up. He was slender, perhaps a hundred and ninety pounds spread over a five-foot-nine-inch frame. Laszlo outweighed him by sixty pounds and was six inches taller. If he whipped their leader, they would turn and run. He threw a short right to his midsection. The reaction was a reflex born of practice and discipline. The first hint of motion of Laszlo’s shoulders triggered a ballet like pirouette. Kai rose on his toes and spun away fro Laszlo’s blow. He raised the heel of his right foot and completed the arc, slamming Laszlo an inch above his belt, over his right kidney. As Laszlo grunted in pain and surprise, Kai used the leverage created by the contact to reverse his direction. Lifting his right foot to shoulder height, he exhaled forcefully as momentum carried him through the blow. His foot struck the side of Laszlo’s head, smashing his jaw and cheekbone, rendering him unconscious. His two companions stood stock still on the stairs, frozen against the driving music from the gymnasium as Laszlo toppled to the ground. “Shit…Kai,” said Alfredo Reyes. “What do we do now?” “Take him in the office and tape his hands and feet,” he replied softly, “and his mouth.” When they came out of the office, Kai let his companions stand for a moment till he was sure they had their composure, then motioned to them and climbed the stairs. Muffy was down to the hard strokes with her class. They lay on their backs, their knees raised, and their feet flat on the floor. They held their hands together behind the napes of their necks and strained their stomach muscles, touching their elbows to their knees. “This is the real test ladies. This weeds out the dilettantes. However, it makes those ugly midriff bulges disappear. Go for it. Good. Hold it. Down. Up. Down slowly. Pay the price. Fifteen more today. Okay Libby?” Libby Beckman was red in the face, her elbows pressed against her thighs. Unable to talk, she nodded in agreement. “One and two, and one and two…” Muffy’s voice stopped. Like Gena, trussed in the closet downstairs, at first all she could see was the gun. “Please listen to me.” Kai said in a calm, even voice. “No one is going to hurt you, or be hurt. We are going to take your money and your jewellery, and we are going to leave. Lie flat on the ground where you are. Just relax. That goes for you too teacher.” He snapped his fingers Paco Nunez, the smallest of his companions, looked around the gym quickly, then trotted the staircase. He returned shortly, shaking his head, “Only a bathtub and stuff.” “In there,” Kai said, pointing at the dressing room. “Its lockers,” Paco said, looking in the door. Kai looked across the prostrate women towards Muffy. His eyes caught the keys strapped to their wrists. “Get those keys,” he said to Paco, “Now, clean out the lockers. Ladies, please take any watches or other jewellery off and put it on the floor next to you.” He nodded at the tall and muscular Reyes and said, “Pick it up.” Libby tried to train her eyes on the ceiling, but they kept straying towards the young man standing over them with the gun, anonymous in his appearance. Dressed in baggy pants, a Nike hooded sweat top and an elaborate white and blue hat, he was fitter and muscular then anyone in the room. He seemed completely at ease, she thought, like a leopard in a tree. She jerked away from a sharp poke in the thigh. “What are you lookin’ at, fatty?” Reyes voice was harsh and tense, with a guttural Hispanic accent. Even through the stocking mask, she could see that his eyes were narrowed with fear. “You keep your eyes on the fuckin’ ceiling!” his hands shook and he smelled of perspiration. Paco, the short man, appeared at the door of the dressing room with a shopping bag. “Okay,” he said “all done.” Kai stood in deep thought. He knew he would never be found out, but would anyone favour his Robin Hood tactic. Rob the rich to help the needy. He began to think of his twisted opponent on Friday and began to laugh quietly. Abner was a redneck, hick and a cult member. Quite a résumé for such a species as adamantly dense as the redneck population tended to be. Poor, uneducated and well he was but a lowly redneck with a speech impediment far worse then Kai’s Chinese scented speech. He would look forward to the competition; it was far from his ‘community project’ as he referred to it as. Kai was not the conventional thief; he viewed himself as parts of a clan, part of the band of merry men, rob the rich to help the needy. “Kai…” Kai was dragged from passive thought, from Abner, from PCW, back to reality. Here he was, the robin hood of New York. The gymnasium was thirty-five feet long and eighteen feet wide. Four cylindrical columns supported the ceiling, edged with ornamental plaster moulding. Kai approached the women and said, “Please get on your hands and knees. Please,” he repeated firmly, motioning them along with the barrel of his gun. “I want you to walk on your hands and knees to the nearest pillar by the stairs.” Following them, he continued, “Now, sit cross-legged in a circle, around the pole. Closer, so you can touch each other.” Suddenly, Adolfo Reyes stepped into the circle and grabbed a blonde woman in her thirties by the hair. “Hold out your hand. Show me your hand,” he said, shaking her head. “Ow.” Reyes shook her again. “Show me.” She extended a slender manicured hand. On the fourth finger was a slim white gold band. He let go of her hair and twisted her wrist sharply enough to make her gasp. Cupped in her palm was a four-carat emerald cut diamond attached to the band. “Gimme that, bitch.” He snarled, pulling savagely at the ring. She began to cry. Helpless and afraid, lost and lonely, as was Abner. Kai would make him a believer on Friday, if he won or lost Abner would remember that Kai Xao Kua existed, and he would remember KXK, for as long as he failed to exist, as the person who… Kai’s moral ethics made him impose himself. “Give him the ring, lady.” Then to Reyes, “Take it and leave her be.” The woman twisted the ring free from her finger and offered it up with trembling hands. “Who else is holding out?” Reyes barked. “Forget it.” Kai said. Donna, the model, edged her hand under her thigh trying to protect a ring worth a few hundred dollars that had been given to her by her father. Reyes drew his hand back to strike her. “How you like I break your fucking nose?” Libby Beckman had never done a thing she considered remotely brave in her life. From somewhere in her throat a stranger’s voice appeared, “If you want to hit someone smaller than you, why don’t you hit me? At least I don’t make a living with my face.” Reyes obliged, slapping her backhand across the cheek. He turned back to Donna and said, “Now, gimme the ring.” White with fear, she passed it to him. “How about another smack fatty?”
