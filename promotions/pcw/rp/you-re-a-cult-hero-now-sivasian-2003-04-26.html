You're a cult hero now, Sivasian?

26 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 26th April ‘Hey honey,’ started Sivasian into his cell phone. His voice sounded harsh, as though he’d thrown back one to many drinks this morning, or afternoon, in fact , without a watch the time wouldn’t matter, or be known. ‘Siv? Where are you?’ his wife starts, sitting at home on the couch, their two youngish children playing on the floor. ‘Listen honey, I’ve got us some money, it’s kinda not what I wanted to do, but anything will do for now…’ he said, keeping his usual cool man appearance. He was, however, slightly worried about being away from his family, he hated lying, and not telling them where he was. ‘Where are you? Tell me Sivasian!’ his wife belted down the phone. She was worried about him, she always was. As though Sivasian was her son, she really cared about him. ‘OK honey, I’ll tell you what I’m doing, but you can’t come here, you’ll put me off,’ He says, sitting on a chair in what appears to be a dressing room. ‘OK, fair deal,’ she replies, where are you? ‘I’m at a television studio,’ he replies. She looks shocked, but pleasantly surprised. ‘You’re selling or getting autographs?’ she asks, ‘better than what I thought, I thought you were gambling, drinking and selling drugs.’ ‘Well, it’s not that bad,’ he replies, fiddling with his hands, ‘but look, I’ll be home tonight with some well earned money. We can go on a nice meal out, take the kids somewhere nice, just , please, don’t come.’ He hung up, feeling better than he had told his wife of his location, and still yet not happy that he clearly made the message she wasn’t welcome here more than obvious. But then, he had a reason. His wife made him embarrassed, at least when he acted. Back in the Ukraine he had offered to help the local school, albeit in an awful state, as most places in the Ukraine where, with their school play. However, his wife came to see him practice, to teach the kids how to act, and he had got awfully embarrassed. He didn’t want it to happen. He was here to relax and he was here to earn money for his family, whatever the result from Friday Fusion. Now he had to refocus on not only performing his best for the director, but now not two opponents, but three opponents, this time adding into the fray Stone Burns, Matt Jackson and James Edwards. These guys were new guys, just like Sivasian, all looking to get a step up in the wrestling world, looking to make an impression. Everyone would be giving it 100%, all they had. ‘Right Sivasian, let’s go,’ came the voice in a weird Russian speaking English accent, ‘we’ve got work to do if you wanna earn your pay.’ Sivasian nodded and stepped out, ‘Who’d have imagined that I would have found such a legend of the Ukraine living 20 minutes away?’ said the director. ‘I had no idea I was classed as a legend though,’ said Sivasian, ‘hopefully they’ll snap up my PCW tee shirts anyway.’ ‘Step forward into the light, adjust your gloves, and say your lines. Then, do the action we discussed, hold the carton right upto the camera, and then say something cheesy.’ Commands the director. ‘Er….sure thing boss,’ replies Sivasian. ‘OK, in three…two…and one…GO!’ shouts the director, sitting behind the camera now. Gulping hard, Sivasian steps forward into what appears to be an indoor make shift kitchen. He gulps again, and turns to face the camera. ‘Hey kids, you want to grow big and bold like me?’ he says, smiling goofishly towards the camera, ‘then drink the right white stuff.’ He opens an old, banged up fridge door and pulls out a carton of milk. Ripping the end off, he leans his head back and takes a gulp, ‘YUM!’ Sivasian sarcastically says, before placing the carton back on the side. ‘This helps me to kill those nasty western men, it helps me kick American ass!’ he shouts, patriotically into the camera. ‘We love you Sivasian! Keep it up!’ mutters the director, as he looks into the camera. ‘Do you want to be able to shoot 100 targets out of 100 with a rifle? Drink up, and we can all destroy the United States! However, we can’t beat the United States if YOU don’t go out and get your parents to buy Vrisklovoviskovi milk! Yes! It’s tasty, and although their may have been bungs thrown around for the right to sell milk in the Ukraine, go and buy this milk!’ Sivasian holds up an oddly coloured and dull carton to the camera, before flashing moderately bright white teeth into the camera. Sivasian holds the smile for a second, before dropping the carton and walking off. ‘Damn it Sivasian,’ says the director, ‘you didn’t adjust your sniping gloves!’ ‘Oh man! Come on, I’ve got other things like a 4 way match this Tuesday to think about above your damn advert.’ Says Sivasian. ‘You want your money or not?’ replies the director. ‘Yes’ replies Sivasian sharply. ‘Then you must redo the scene,’ he says. Sivasian sighs, ‘Damn pressure,’ he mutters, ’I’ll take this out on them Americans.’
