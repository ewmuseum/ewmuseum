Miyagawa? Who?

8 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07/08/02 *Scene Open: The End sitting on a stool in a darkened room. Only The End is seen, wearing full ring attire.* And then, there was a man named Isawa Miyagawa. A quite odd-looking midget he was. Nothing about him made you want to take him seriously. And even he knew it. The man, the joke, Isawa Miyagawa. *A big picture of Miya is put on screen, electronically edited so that he is wearing make-up and a clown suit. A video taken from his World Title match against defending champion Boda is altered. The arena looks like a rodeo stadium, and running up behind him is an angry bull.* Gimmick, you say? Of course, most wrestlers have one. His gimmick, the only one he had for quite some time (which is the gimmick he happens to live by at this moment) was that of being Japo-American. In this, he lays his faith in his skill. He claims to be the best Asian wrestler, the best American wrestler, the best Asian-American wrestler... all in this one gimmick without saying a word. He believes this so much that he even created his own federation just to prove it. *With a smile on his face and a thumbs up, Miyagawa is seen signing the papers to become the owner and president of Puroresu Ultimate Ring Entertainment, PURE.* Never did much of anything, even there. Got beat to a pulp by Kensuke Suzahara (whom he teamed with earlier in his career to win the PCW tag team titles against... some team, they're not important), feuded with Cianan for God knows what reason, and teamed up with Ebola III to win ABSOLUTELY NOTHING. *A picture of Miyagawa and Ebola III, standing tall in the PURE ring holding digitally added Haggar-brand dress belts, since they had no tangible PURE championships to lay claim to.* Of course, it was a long way up from the bottom. He had to endure beatdown after beatdown suffered at the hands of PCW legends like Bad Battio. *We don't need to go into details here. It's as painful for everyone else to watch it as it is painful to Miya to watch it. We'll just leave it at this: Winner - Bad Battio in 6 seconds.* He had to get knocked the hell out time and time again to get what he deserved. Finally, Isawa Miyagawa would finally capture the PCW Cruiserweight Title in a stunning upset (only in that Kid Wonder didn't just kill him before giving him the title). At last, Miyagawa would have PCW success. Wait, no, he would lose the belt shortly after being pummeled into wearing it. *A picture of an unconscious Miyagawa with the Cruiserweight belt draped over his lifeless body.* And all the while, he would tout his Japo-American heritage like he was the harbinger of some master race. But this wasn't the only thing he did. No, he had other halfway 'gimmicks' as well. Take, for instance, the brief 'Urban Samurai' gimmick. *Enter: Forrest Whitaker walks into view with a sword, wearing a red cloak.* No, not you. Go away. *Exit: Forrest Whitaker slinks his shoulders low and sulks, dragging his feet to leave the screen.* Yes, for a short time, Miyagawa stopped trying to be the joke that he is and do something a little more serious. It was not to be, though. He kept to his roots and went back to embarrassing himself on a weekly basis. He even teamed with two equally self-disrespecting wrestlers in Trashcan Man and The Joker. They called this group, Asylum. Completely appropriate, as they all knew. Every one of them needed to be institutionalized. But it's a little better now. Trashcan Man is... somewhere, but away from a wrestling ring (which is the last place he needs to be). I ran into The Joker the other day. Let me tell you, if you go to Wendy's, don't order a pita. He'll have no clue what you're talking about. *In a picture, The Joker's face is placed on the head of a Wendy's employee, shrugging with body language saying, 'How should I know?'* No matter how bad anyone else in the group was, if Miyagawa was in Asylum, it could never be taken seriously. And this was true; throughout the many incarnations of the Asylum, including Darkstar, Jeremy Howard, Gaijin, Bobby Stormbringer, and such, they could never be taken seriously. With Miyagawa as a figurehead, it could never be more than a joke stable. *A clip of a crowd of PCW fans laughing while the Asylum is in the ring. They told a joke, but that's left out of the clip so all you see is them being laughed at.* The walking joke got married. It's weird, he went to Russia (partly not of his own accord with whatever people he got involved with that he shouldn't have. Fact remains that it was at Red October that Miyagawa would be wrestling, but somewhere along the way he met the heroine from Goldeneye. Miya knocked her up and they got married. Miyajoker got a wife, someone to support him and someone to live for, and Natalia got out of Russia. It was a win/win situation for the both of them. *Though no pictures were published, The End has somehow procured a wedding picture of J.I. Miyagawa and Natalia.* Miya's only other experience with Russians involved getting his ass handed to him by Ivan Stanislav, but they're separated by federation lines now, so he's lucky enough that there won't be a repeat. *A picture of Miya getting pinned by Stanislav in the National Pride match.* Then it happened... Miyagawa won the Television Title. I believe he won it from Tim Walden, but it's not important enough for me to check. Come on, it's Walden! The same Walden I beat in my debut match? The same guy who could only get some momentum going for him in his career by taking no-sell pills? Please. Miya's title reign was uneventful to say the least. His challengers were more pathetic than he was. *Another doctored picture shows Miyagawa holding up the PCW Television Title, but the letters are changed to say 'PCW Death to Miya Title.'* A pact made by the Asylum members competing in the March Madness battle royal came to fruition, as everyone helped Miyagawa last in the ring. He was able to get to the second place spot thanks to everyone in that stable working together for the benefit of Miyagawa. Not that anything came of it that night, anyway. Boda, heavily-favored from the start, won the PCW World Title. Of course, feeling sorry for Miya, he let him win it a few days later. Boda had his moment of pity and let Miya hold the PCW World Title. But we know for sure that Miyagawa isn't anywhere near that level. Why? Because he failed to win it again. Not only that, but he struggles to get to that level of competition, let alone stay there. *The clips of the respective matches are shown.* He couldn't get much of anything going for him after he lost the World Title. He teamed a little bit with Syko, but nothing good came from that. As a general rule, anything involving Miya and Syko in the same room together is going to have negative consequences. They didn't even do that well together. The teaming was as ineffective as a square wheel. *Now a picture of Miya and Syko getting beaten up by the Heartbreakers* And that was just before the reset. Look at what has happened since then. Miya's held the Extreme Title (which isn't much of an accomplishment because you have to want to fail in order to miss an opportunity to win the belt). He won it in the School's Out match, which is nothing more than a match to see who is the least pathetic out of the field of competitors. *Footage of Miya winning the Extreme Title* Since losing the Extreme Title to Ellie (which immediately makes him the Hank Gunyon for the New PCW), he's fought for the Continental Title and feel short every time. To think that the only important title in recent months that Miyagawa has won was that of being Xavier Kannon's best man at his wedding. *And then footage of Miya messing up Kannon's wedding.* And now it falls to me, PCW's other Japo-American, to defeat Miyagawa and prove with uselessness once and for all. I've done it before, back at the first Global Title Tournament. I absolutely dominated him in the ring, he didn't score a single offensive move. Just think how easily I can destroy him now with how much I have improved since then. *Dark match pictures of that match are shown on screen.* Miyagawa was once on top of PCW. Now he's all the way back down here in the Cruiserweight Division. And I own the Cruiserweight Division. And once Charlie Loc pulls his head out of the sand and stops lobbying for me to wrestle people like Mark Makonie and Miya (whom I have no issues with), he'll lose the Cruiserweight Title to me. Indeed, I am the King of Kings. A long road, 7 months have I worked to get to where I am now. And nothing and no one is going to stand in my way. Miyagawa, you are but a speedbump on my road to success. Hope I don't crush you too hard.
