War: Prologue

25 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/25/03 Monday, April 21st: 2:18 am. The red door opens and Skylar enters. Casually dressed in a white short-sleeved T-shirt and some jeans, he finally gets back to his hotel room. He lingered at the Conseco Field house for a few hours. Most everyone had bought or acquired their soveniers, autographs, pictures, and wiped up the blood and sweat and the bottles will most certainly be sold on E-Bay pretty soon. He tosses his two dark green Jansport bags on the floor, his ring attire and a few pieces of clothes inside, and slips off his shoes as he makes his way past the beige walls that lead to the small bathroom containing only a basic sink, toilet, and shower, and falls onto the queen-sized bed covered in red sheets with gold lining. The bed is exactly 72 inches long. Skylar is 80 inches long. He turns on his right, the legs pulled up to his body little. It's not the fetal position, more stretched out. {I don't know what's what.} Skylar is exhausted, having mentally taxed himself trying to figure out what was going on with the religious stable in PCW made up of Violence Jack, The Pharaoh, and Dorn Tracer; trying to figure out who or what the druid is; trying to make sense of things in his life. He's historically had things easy and difficult simultaneously. This was another one of those times. With Akiyama on the shelf of his own accord, Skylar was no the most dominant physical force in PCW. But he felt so empty there was really no use in exercising that force. {The post-SF bookings were just released. 6-Man tag match. Myself, Thomas Gordo, and Gaz Green against Bruce Shanahan, Titu Xansiv, and the former Matt Jay. I know how this will turn out. Nerezza is still in the ocean somewhere and there's no one else who even comes closer to deserving or having earned a spot in this match. P.W.A. is only in the match to fulfill their #1 Contendership to the Continental Title, but with Nerezza not present, Gaz can't take the shot on his own.} {This match will be altered. P.W.A. will be taken out of it. Most likely to get their chance next Tuesday, as the Fusion card is already out as well. It will go from a 6-Man Tag Match to a Fourway. Tommy, Jack, Pharaoh, and me.} Skylar didn't trun on the lights, though it doesn't really matter. He wouldn't use them anyway. He closes his eyelids out of a lifelong habit. The synthetic eyes he wears so as not to scare people are made to be left in 24-7, and they are self-cleaned when Skylar blinks or closes his eyes, just as normal. He doesn't even bother undressing, he wants to get up and go later in the morning, when his 7:30am wake-up call comes in. {Jack will want to get a decisive victory over me, something that he lacks. He holds the Continental Championship, which I handed over to him. I didn't want it anymore. Knowing Shanahan, however, that won't be enough for him. He'll want to win the World Title as well. He'll want to hold both belts, and give them to his gods, as if that will mean anything to them. They want humanity to be enslaved (save for Nyarlathotep, who just wants humanity dead). Some easily melted trinkets serves their purpose in no way.} Skylar yawns heavily. He had been up for nearly 34 hours. Training and preparations. The coffin he prepared for Violence Jack had gone to waste that night. Would he get a chance to use it again? Perhaps. Was Friday Fusion's main event that opportunity? There was a good possibility. {The Pharaoh will want to get me back for that freebie attack, no doubt. But as I am now the #1 contender to the World Title with Violence Jack holding sole possession of the Continental Title, and the Pharaoh having been Cruiserweight Champion (and that having dragged his rank down by one), I'm his obstacle to the World Title main challenger's position in the World Title position. The dominance of that title still means a lot to him. He wants to prove that he is a worthy agent for his gods, which really exist off of him alone. They are weak, I can feel it. He needs to sway more believers to his gods to make them grow stronger. Being a World Champion could mean a step in that direction, to him.} {'Ra wants your impudent asses to eat Wheaties and worship him: OR ELSE!'} Skylar settles. He starts to slip off into a much-needed and well-deserved rest. {Tommy has cheated me everytime we've faced each other. This battle will be different. No more cheap trickery. He will lose or I will lose, but it will not be by unsatisfactory means. It's time that a match between us ended decisively. I'll admit that I'm not as angry as I once was, and that anger is the key to harnessing my true fighting ability. That aside, I will not fight lightly. Tommy, if he wants to win, will have to work harder than he ever has, especially if he wants to defeat me........} It was dark out. Not the tranquil dark of night that the spring season usually brings with it once the sun resigns itself to a quiescent slumber and the luminescent moon dances across the skies of the Earth; the dark was extreme. It was painfully aphotic, the unholy water falling from sullen black skies. The heavens were leaking. The heavens were crying. The driving rain dampened normal sound. Kazaki couldn't even hear himself as he sploshed through the puddles of water as he made his way to the Third Street Gym. 'This'd better be worth it,' Kaz muttered to himself, pulling up the collar of his yellow Dick Tracy raincoat, closing it over in neck, ears, and the bottom half of his face. The rain fell upon his body like miniature ice daggers thrown by vengeful angels in the heavens above, hoping to purge the earth of the unjust and evil. Kazaki had always been like that. Pure water burned him. Few in the world had such a condition, it was so rare that there wasn't even beginning to be a cure anytime soon. 'If it's not, I'm doing my hardest to summon up the psychosis.' The psychosis was not something he had summoned, at least not intentionally. The rain kept hitting him, as though trying to push him. A voice in the back of his head even said, 'Go back.' He could hear whispers around him; different voices: male, female, young, old, carefree, stressed, happy, sad, lighthearted, weighted with anguish and pain. All of them telling him-- begging him-- commanding him to turn away. To be patient. Tonight was not his night. Tonight was his night. He would find what he had been seeking, but it would not be what is best. There weren't any cars along this street. It was the daily market section of town anyway, it closed at sundown. No reason to linger around, there was nothing to sell. This wasn't a residential area anyway, no one lived around here. Typically from the hours of 7pm to 6am, this place was a ghost town. The only people you might find are the destitute drug addicts, prostitutes, and homeless former wage-earners, merely looking for a quiet place away from the kids who stay out all night roaming from club to club but always having enough spare moments to stop and laugh at them. Something was pushing Kazaki to move back. Something else was drawing him forward. The gravitational, almost supernatural force pulling him forward was stronger. He had only his conscience telling him to flee. His gut instincts, and something more, something deeper, made him continue. Looking out from the tinted gym windows, two 9 ft. tall blue-scaled winged creatures spied Kazaki as he walked up. The lights were off to reduce visibility inside the gym to nothing. Faint light outside from a streetlamp captured Kazaki's coat, rainboots, and the thin silver ponytail that draped down his back over his coat from the black crown of hair upon his head. One of the creatures squinted its solid red eyes as it pressed its saurian head against the glass, the pointed head placed vertically with the nose and mouth pointing to the floor, drooling through its teeth. The drool hit the floor, crackling and spattering as it dissolved the carpeting from its acidity. 'Aj kxuk kxo edo?' it speaks, more from a gutteral voice box than to actually speak through its foot-long mouth. The horns on its head scraped the glass, making a scratching sound. 'Kxo edo nakx kxo fjosxejaj?' The second saurian creature backed away from the window on its reverse angle legs, crouching low on its knees while the talons dug into the floor. Not clothed, the creature seems to have no anatomical design to show it as male or female. It brought its long arms up to its mouth and licked a thick red substance from its fingers. 'Kxuk cakkco thadw? Amfejjabco.' A humanoid figure stepped closer to the window from the shadows of the empty gym, all the machines and equipment long cleared out; the only prominent architecture in the room being the front counter on the east wall opposite their own positions, behind which the only thing was a pile of dead, half-eaten bodies of homeless individuals. His arms were folded behind his back and he had a way of facing his head down, and looking up through his eyes. 'Indeed, he is the one with the psychosis,' the white-suited man with pale-blue hair said, peering out of the window as Kazaki crossed the street. 'Humans by nature are not impressive specimens, but within this one lies a killing rage inspired by the glorious dreams of Cthulu himself. Our goals would to be served immensely with his assistance.' The claw-licking saurian finished its last few drops of blood. 'A kubo ak, kxod, kxuk no uho dek ke ouk xam?' The white-suited man, a fair-faced Caucasian man of twenty-odd years, senventy-three inches in height and one hundred eighty-two pounds half-turned back to the far saurian, narrowing his eyes. 'No, Gneghi, he lives. Cannot Byakhee think beyond their stomachs for any appreciable time?' Gneghi took offense to the white-suited man's statement, and stalked forward. 'Ak aj dek najo ke adjick u Byakhee.' The saurian at the window turned back and shakes its large head. 'Ted'k--' 'A am dek nehhaot, D'ligo. Kxaj mouk suddoek te udokxadw ke ij. No uho Byakhee!' The white-suited man merely closed his eyes with a smirk and a snicker. 'Byakhee are not invincible. In fact, occassionally, they have a tendency to go boom.' Gneghi, a slender, musclar saurian, swelled up to three times its size around within three seconds before bursting open, its scaly outer-skin and inner organs, tissues, muscles, bones, and blood flying everywhere. The only thing that Gneghi's exploding body didn't cover was the white-suited man. Everything moved around his body. D'ligo, the remaining saurian, was paralyzed, his red eyes wide with fear. The white-suited man turned back to him. 'Shall you speak out of line?' D'ligo simply shakes his head. 'Of course not. Die.' And with that simple verbal command, D'ligo's body was the next to splatter the room. The white-suited man looked to the door, where Kazaki was waiting and knocking. 'You shall serve us well...'
