2 Fast, 2 Furious

23 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 23rd June 2003 A desolate wasteland of sand stretches as far as the eye can see. The radiant sun beats upon its surface with an unforgiving temperature and the natives, in this case the animal kingdom that usually resides upon this hostile terrain takes shelter. A picturesque blue sky looms above with no clouds in sight and the sun hanging like a spotlight continues it’s tormenting, “I’m going to rock…” roars over the silence and the glam rock renditions of Twisted Sister continue to blare. Miles from civilisation, nobody would dare argue and the rock track melodically thumps beat after beat with the occasional chorus of singing. The source of this disturbance, a lone vehicle hurtling down the highway and using the stretch of bare concrete as it’s personal drag strip. The silver bodywork of a classic Shelby GT 500 glistens in the sunlight and the speed it generates makes it blur and distraught like a mirage on this desert plain. The driver changes gear and the dump-valve acknowledges with a snake like hiss and unsurprisingly the automobile launches further forward. The distinctive tune of Twisted Sister ‘I Want To Rock’ fades like an arena PA system but the word arena needs to be used loosely of late for those on the payroll at Primetime Championship Wrestling. The Cruiserweights of this well-established promotion have gone from flying off turnbuckles to literally flying, as have everybody associated with this illustrious wrestling company. The days of roughing it in cheap and shoddy motel rooms have since ceased as have those same days of travelling along monotonous highways in convoys. Temporarily the stars of Primetime Championship Wrestling have swapped the open road for the convection currents of the skies, It’s hard to digest but the individuals of PCW are now the performers of the airs courtesy of the monstrous craft constructed, call it what you will, a blimp, zeppelin or airship, the point is that those overnight journeys along the vast road network of America are gone, even if its only for a month. “I got my very first six-string, boy I can fly…” erupts from inside the observed car. From the wild adrenaline fuelled hit of Twisted Sister to the mainstream cover classic of Bryan Adams, the eighties love of music is certainly apparent thus begging the question of, ‘Who is the driver, Carl Cash?’ The car continues to rip up the highway like as though it were its own private playground and the shifting of gears signalled by the constant hiss of the dump-valves use of the vehicles turbo engine. The driver appears firmly in control but the speed soars to dangerous levels and without doubt has violated the legal speed limit but carefree the person shows no signs of easing his foot off the accelerator peddle. The silver coated Shelby races past a drive-through diner cluttered with it’s own assortment of vehicles, be it modified motorcycles with their uncanny tricycle wheel placement and loaded trucks of a business nature. Not even the enticing smell of sizzling bacon and fresh coffee fazes the driver and the sports car further shifts into petroleum guzzling higher gear, again indicated by the hiss of its dump-valve, a sound one would expect from a leaking gas pipe or factory machinery. “Back in the summer of sixty-nine!” and the Canadian accent of Bryan Adams continues to resound from the stereo system concealed in the trunk, each thump of the bass being more like some hostage beating at their metallic confinement with a series of clenched fists. Far from traffic the automobile continues to hurtle along, occasionally passing the odd car that creeps up in the inside lane. The professionalism of the driving admired by the odd vehicle that passes by and in some cases deemed idiotic by those who don’t share the flow of nitrous oxide running through their veins. Topping speeds of hundred miles per hour the pristine Shelby races past an advertisement billboard splashed with the sports insignia of ‘Nike’ and paying the figureheads of Michael Jordan and Brazil’s Roberto Carlos no-mind the driver fails to notice a stationary squad car belonging to the neighbouring towns police department. Suddenly the blue and white coated police car rolls forward, it’s suspension bouncing as it makes the transition from the soft flooring of the desert to the warm hard concrete of the highway. The blue light atop of the car begins to swing in a circular fashion and the monotone siren blares aggravatingly over the silence of the tranquil backdrop and officially the pursuit has begun, “Those were the best days of my life…” emits from the Shelby’s stereo system and as the guitar rift is about to hit home the song fades and the car itself slows down before crashing to a halt on the side of the road. After several agonising seconds the police car comes within feet of the now stationary Shelby and parks in an orderly fashion behind. Sitting within the squad car appears two uniform clad individuals; one in the thick of the action behind the wheel and his associate in tow, sitting nestled in the passenger seat. The driver’s side door creaks ajar and a large obese gentleman steps out into the intense heat, wiping his brow with the aid of his hat he slowly shuffles forward, these mere actions taking their toll for the officer stands out of breath. Tapping at the tinted pain of glass the officer begins, “Can you step out of the vehicle please?” The metallic door swings open and a single leg tailored in green trousers protrudes, followed by the remaining body parts that make up Primetime Championship Wrestling’s resident Jester and more importantly the man atop of the Continental Title division Gaz Green. The Emerald Menace stands smugly whilst the sweaty officer peers his head into the Shelby, the Jester grinning jovially to himself and with a vane manner he brushes a hand through his spiked mop of dyed green head hair, “Can I help you squire?” retorts the Continental Champion. The officer glances up and his beady eyes stand to attention like daggers before he answers, “This isn’t funny sir and please, do me the common courtesy of losing that ridiculous smile. Now can I see your driving license and car registration?” With a sigh the former Pranks With Attitude member ventures into the glove compartment and retrieves the necessary documentation and scrunched into a bundle within his grasp he hands the paperwork across. “Everything seems in order, have you any idea how fast you were going son?” questions the law enforcer as his hand creeps up to brush the underside of his nose. “Isn’t she a beauty?” smiled the Lord of Laughter. “I mean, I’m sorry officer but I’m kinder late…” tries concluding the Jester but the police officer interrupts, “Late for what exactly?” The Jester stands dumbfounded knowing he’s late for boarding the airship but a flimsy excuse of that magnitude won’t be enough to convince the police officers to overlook the speeding violation. Gasping for an excuse his teeth chatter when suddenly one materialises, “Look, my sick brother needs a blood transfusion and I’m the only one compatible, please, think of my dear brother Skylar who maybe at this very second be knocking on deaths door!” pleads the Conniving Comic with a solemn frown. The officer subtly wags his head with a stiff upper lip and replies, “Ok – You need police escort, you got it!” The Jester smiles half-heartedly knowing if his escort makes it to the airship rendezvous then his cover is blown and a speeding violation will be nothing compared to the list of other convictions that may very well crash down upon a judges desk. The King Midas of Tag rummages within glove compartment once more and removes a small pointy object and nestles it within his velvet business jacket before making his farewell, The officer now behind the wheel of his motor winds down the window on seeing the conceited comedian and acknowledges his presence, “Come on lad we got to get you to that brother of yours!” The Jester sadistically grins and with a flash of his hands and some fancy footwork he kicks his box of tricks beneath the back wheel before concluding, “See you at the finish line fellers!” and with a wink he jumps back into the bucket seats of his sports car and with a screech of his rubber tyres gaining traction on the surface the Jester sped off. Almost a blur at this point the Shelby grows smaller and smaller as it heads off in the distance and the officer realising he has some gaining brings the clutch up and the accelerator down, when suddenly a bang resounds and the engine immediately dies. Both police officers unhook their seatbelts and spring from the vehicle to check on the commotion, “What in the hell is that? Our Mr. Green has some explaining, well after we get the spare out the trunk and straighten this mess out!” yelps the larger officer pointing to the dubious device left by the Jester. “That wouldn’t be a Mr. Gaz Green sir, would it?” murmurs the scrawny officer who had been silent in the passenger seat up to this point as he rubbed his bristled face with an open palm. “Well… Gareth Green but what of it?” “Don’t know much of him but he’s one of those wrestlers, thought he looked familiar, back home the kids watch that rubbish over real shows like Starsky and Hutch!” informs the officer to his colleague. “He’s like a prankster or something, sorry sir, I should’ve been on the ball…” “YOUR DAMN RIGHT YOU SHOULD HAVE!!!!” roars the obese policeman enraged and with a swift curling of his right hand he throws his hat to the sand covered floor in disgust. The scene fades on the glum expression etched along the officer’s face as he sulks from embarrassment.
