The history Sivasian, part 2

16 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 16th May 2003 On that day, a day of adventure already for Sivasian, he had passed a great test. He had fooled a local militia policeman into not taking away any form of his minimal wealth. He shone and felt great. His previously buried sniper rifle was brought out from the snow, and he lined it up against his shoulder. Still, even when you feel great, the snow REALLY bites hard into your skin, more so than in any other country on earth. Sivasian got a tingling feeling down his spine, at how he could survive, but how you’re average Englishman, Frenchman or American wouldn’t survive. And yet, his skin had become almost used to this temperature, ALMOST. For your skin can NOT be prepared for such low temperatures, it lowers your inner core temperature, for it is so cold outside. Your nose becomes a block of crystals inside. He didn’t mind though, the buzz he got from those soldiers, of telling them he was going to crush, mutilate, HURT Americans, made him feel great. He trundled on, waiting for some prey, waiting to get something. Suddenly he dropped, ahead, a deer ran past a tree. He smirked a little, a confident young kid he was definitely. He held it firmly for 4-5 seconds, he placed his head to one side of the rifle and peered through the scope once more. Taking no time, he tracked the deer, he didn’t want to hit it’s leg. He had always been told to cause maximum effect without hurting the animal. One shot to the head, however, would kill instantly, without the horse feeling any pain, ironic thought it may sound. He stopped for seconds, everything seemed to go quiet. He saw the deer shape right, he knew their movements by heart. He waited patiently for it to swing back into place, and then BANG! The trigger was pressed, the buck pressed back into his shoulder. He looked on, and saw the deer drop straight down. Smiling to himself once more, he slung the rifle up onto his shoulder, produced his hunting knife, and headed down towards the deer. **** Sivasian was only 16, but he knew what he wanted. He had been firing for almost all his life, was he regarded as a local hero. He had won every shooting academy prizedraw he had been to, winning hams, money and clothes for his family. Yet, he wanted something else, not to fire at a target, but to fire at a human being, for the killing of his parents had drawn him to a pure hatred, a hatred which built up inside. Sivasian strode towards the army center, a center for which he had yearned to join. It had been three days since his life had turned over to add a 6 where the 5 had once been, and officially allowed him access into the Ukrainian army. He was elated, more so than his uncle and aunt had ever seen him before. But then, they couldn’t tell him. Sivasian didn’t appear to feel pain. He felt physical pain, when he caught his arm on a tree thorn, or fell over, but emotional pain, he had never felt. They knew he wouldn’t react much. In an ironic twist, he was like his ally and comrade in SOCIETY, he felt nothing at that time, except a desire to do well, and highlight a course. Rather than the course of the Society of Order and Logic, he wanted to pay the Americans back, back for the killing of his parents. But it wasn’t that easy, not that easy at all. Sivasian slowly walked in to the large buildings. Military men walked back and forth, and a large military type flying eagle badge was placed upon the wall, with the saying ‘To kill one man, is to fight for Russia’ just below it. Sivasian saw a large sign that said ‘Military trials and sign-ups this way.’. He looked around suspiciously, before walking down the indicated sign way. He saw another sign which read ‘Formal Army’ and an arrow, and another which read ‘Advanced shooting core.’ Thinking for a few seconds, he then started to walk towards it. He slung his gun over his shoulder and marched towards the room, determined to make an impression.
