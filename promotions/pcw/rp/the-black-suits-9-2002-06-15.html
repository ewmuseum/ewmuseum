The Black Suits [9]

15 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 15, 2002 Western Mercy Hospital 9:00 am Dazed, confused...I hate my life. Maybe I should take that back, because one of these days someone 'upstairs' will be listening, and I'll make the fatal mistake of making a request that someone is actually willing to fulfill. My head hurt, my back hurt...hell, even my hair hurt - that's a first. Everything inside of me seemed jumbled up and ready to just pop out of my body. It was almost as if it was rebelling against me, being harsh since I had abused it for the last few days. I would apologize to my body, but how many people do you know sit around and apologize to a large mass of tissue making up your organs? Yes, I was also feeling a bit woozy today and not thinking straight. Must have been the drugs, or worse, maybe I hit my head on the way down. My eyes opened, for what seemed to be the first time in weeks. My first sight was Sean O'Reilly, one of my best friends. I used to call him 'The Impaler,' which was an old joke between him and I dating back to our rough days in the oWa. One of the wrestlers there had changed their name to the Impaler, and made himself look like Marilyn Manson, or one of his flunkies. Most people thought it was stupid, rather than funny. I thought it was hilarious, and decided to steal the name and attack it to Sean O'Reilly. Ah, the memories. Sean was about six foot four, dark black man, with a gentle face. He wore rimmed glasses, a flannel shirt, and light khakis. It was dress up wear for him, as he usually left the house using whatever was lying on the floor. That usually consisted of swears, a t-shirt, or some sort of warm up apparel. He didn't care about what he looked like to other people, and I could respect that. I wasn't quite the stickler for outside opinions, either. Well, except for Nat. Her opinion always mattered. Sean being here was a relief to me. I thought I'd wake up alone and scared, but I was more afraid of being scared. I suppose it came hand in hand with being alone, but I feared that if I was afraid in a strange place, I would most likely go crazy and do something rash. After taking such a vicious fall, I was certain that I would either lose control of my limbs and/or be dead by now. I wasn't dead, that much I was sure off. I wiggled around my toes in bed, and asked Sean to punch my leg to make sure I could feel it. He obliged, and a moment later I was screaming in pain. He always did things too harshly. When I said punch my leg, I didn't mean for him to attempt to break it. Damn big men... That's when the doctor and nurse came in. It was at this time that I was becoming aware that I was in a hospital. I asked them which hospital I was staying at, and they replied, 'Western Mercy Hospital.' Awww, jeez. Western Mercy was located on the border of California and Nevada. I knew that because of all of the infomercials they played on late night television, trying to get more people to come out and give the hospital some business. I guess they lived in a very non-hospital-needy area or something to that effect. I had never known a hospital that needed to 'drum up' business. But like I always say, 'There's a first for everything.' That raised an interesting question on where I had been before this. No, my memory still served me correctly - I remembered everything about The Agency. I remembered Chopsticks, Sourpuss, and of course, Sporty, the big, bad butterfly. They hadn't taken the time to erase my memory, and that was either because they felt they owed me something for saving their sorry asses (Because in all honesty, they were getting the ultimate beat down of their lives), I was so injured that they had to take me to the hospital immediately, or they had just forgotten about it. I wouldn't be against the last choice, considering that they had been pretty busy rigging up the HQ and preparing to blow it all to hell. They must have been a bit emotional about that - Hell, I would have been. Of course, this meant that I really had no idea where the HQ was. For all I knew, they could have dropped me off at a random hospital as far away from their HQ as they could, or the HQ could have been located underneath Western Mercy. I just didn't know. Smart guys, those Agents. I guess they teach you that in government agency school - how to be a sneaky son of a gun. The doctor asked me how I was feeling, and I replied with a shrug. I wasn't sure, since I didn't know the extent of my injuries. He told me I had slightly bruised my spinal cord, which explained the pain I was feeling throughout my body. He said I was lucky that I didn't inflict further damage to the spine, otherwise I was have lost most motor functions in my body. I concurred with him. It was a scary thought to realize how close you were to having one event change the rest of your life. If I hadn't known better, I could have come out of this situation never seeing the outside of a wheelchair, never wrestling again, and never being Tom Lexian again. I brushed the thought from my mind - thinking like that gets me depressed. Other than that, the doctor said I was in very good health, and would most likely be able to leave relatively soon. I asked him who had brought me in, and he told me they just found me outside on a stretcher. Figures - they would be sneaky enough to not be seen doing so. I'm pretty sure there would be some clues on the hospital's cameras, but I wasn't in that much of a hurry to meet up with these guys again. I let it be. After the doctor left, a woman walked in. I thought it was Nat at first, because I couldn't concentrate on the face, and all I knew was that she was attractive. It's a great gift to be able to tell an attractive girl from an unattractive girl, all without being able to see her face. It was a rare talent, I know, but one that I'm sure I could perfect over the years. No, it was not Nat, but another woman from my past. It was Miranda Morehouse, a reporter for Pro Wrestling Magazine, or PWM. She was a very attractive woman. Long legs, smooth skin, black, shoulder length hair, and a face I could wake up to every single morning of the day. She wore a short red skirt, with a white dress shirt, and a black blazer to go over the shirt. Behind her was a camera man, and she held to mics in her hands. I knew exactly what she wanted, and it wasn't to say hello. Miranda was a good reporter, if not anything more than annoying. She was also one of the past acquaintances of mine back in the oWa days, before I jumped over to DWF and now to PCW. She was aggressive, which was always the trademark of a lady that always got her way. I tried to argue with her as much as I could, just for the sake of annoying her. As I said before, one good turn deserves another. I hadn't seen her in many weeks, which surprised me a great deal. I thought with all of the publicity I was getting in the DWF, that she'd be right on my tail trying to get the lead story. I guess even reporters have to take a break. I greeted her with the normal, 'Hey Miranda. What's shakin'?' She, of course, responded by telling me to shut up and listen. Ooooh, I was always keen on foreplay. She explained her whereabouts to me, which ended up being that she was on assignment over in Japan. Japanese wrestling was starting to get very popular over here in the states, and more and more news crews were trying to hoard in on the potential gold mine. I had seen it before, and I thought it was interesting. There was more emphasis on holds there, unlike the power holds and moves with high flying aerial acrobatics here in the States. I guess it was like a step up for Miranda, but I got the feeling that she enjoyed stepping on my toes and following my every move. I made everything seem more exciting, even the most mundane things. I'm sure she liked that form of improvisation, considering she always had to think on her feet as a reporter. She told me that we would be cutting a promo now, and didn't even wait for an answer on whether or not I wanted to do it. That's the way she always was: demanding and completely overbearing. There was a reason that I liked her. But I wasn't feeling very talkative at the moment, and I had to decline her request. She obviously didn't like that, and she never took no as an answer. I'd hate to be going out with the girl - something told me she used the bedroom to relieve her aggression, and she would probably wind up hurting me more badly than I was right now. She continued to demand an interview, and I continued to reject her advances. It was a game of cat and mouse, and I knew that I would win. All I had to do was to press the alert button on my stretcher, and a flood of nurses would come in to clear the room. I didn't do that here, as I'm sure she would use it against me somehow. I told her I was too tired to talk, but I would be happy to write her an exclusive monologue by myself, and send it off to her. She complained at first, but I guess she realized that I would be doing her writing for her, so she agreed. She did tell me, though, that she would be at Super Clash, and that she would have an interview by hook or by crook. I'm not sure if that was a statement or a demand. Didn't matter to me - they were both the same. She left me with a pen and paper - very professional. I placed it at the side of my bed and spoke more with Sean. It seems that he has been working hard trying to repair his marriage. They had divorced several years ago, but I knew that Sean hadn't wanted it. Then again, he was the kind of guy who listened to his wife. If she wanted out of the relationship, he would grant it. That happened before the injury which would force him into retirement. Now he made his living training people and doing odd jobs. He got plenty of money off of disability, so money was not an issue. His son was, however. I'm sure that was his main motivation for picking up on their marriage once more. I knew that he would be able to sweep her off her feet, just as he had done before. The only difference know would be that he would have time to spend with his family, and would not be on the road so much. That immediately made me think of Nat. She was so understanding, even though we've had arguments about my profession before. I guess love does bind all, as I could never stand to be with anyone else but her. I asked Sean about her, and he told me that she was still on assignment, but she was coming home soon and would have a surprise for me. I smiled - I loved it when she surprised me.
