If You Give a Hick a Fishin' Stick

2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05.06.03 A banjo plays quietly in the background as the scene fades in from black. As the music picks up speed, the screen lightens up and a picture comes in to view. A gorgeous lake lies deep in the Southern woods of Georgia. The pristine waters, deep and wide, lap up against the rocky shores as a gentle breeze blows across its top, wrinkling the gray waters. Suddenly, two figures come into view. On the right is a tall bearded man holding a fishing poll in one hand, and a cooler in the other. He’s wearing long rubber boots, tight blue overalls with pockets stuffed to the brim with fishing gear. He sports a small brown hat that reads “American Eagle”. Words appear across the screen, and in white lettering, over his head, reads: “George”. He begins to talk, carrying a gentle southern accent. George: Welcome ladies and gentlemen to a very special edition of Bass Masters. From the left, another fisherman makes his presence known. He’s the shorter of the two, dressed in a black tee shirt, brown cargo shorts (similarly bulging with equipment), a matching hat, and black rubber boots. He reaches up with a hairy arm to adjust his black sunglasses, before wiping the crumbs of some unknown [food?] out his thick black goatee. A word appears on the screen, this time, “Charlie”. Charlie: That’s right. Tonight, we have a special guest! George: A very special guest. Charlie: A very special guest, indeed! George: Why, it’s none other than PCW’s own…Abner! Charlie: That’s right, George, how exciting! George nods, reaching down and lacing up his rubber boots. George: And now, it’s time for a quick word from our sponsor. Charlie: …And when we return, it’s party time! George: Yes, a special party! Charlie: Certainly, a very special party indeed! Charlie nods his head. The screen fades out to a commercial, as the two fishermen make for a small motorboat, parked on the beach behind a large red SUV. As they get in the back of the boat, the scene fades out. (Commercial: A short, graying man dressed in tux, accompanied by a rowdy [stereotypical] redneck, approaches a podium, rifle in hand. Peering out at an unknown audience, he focuses the fierce glow of his beady eyes on a distant object; seemingly attempting to burn a hole threw it. He slams his fist down hard, jolting the stand before speaking. Charlatan Hesston: Good evening my fellow Americans, I’m Charlatan Hesston, and this is my fellow Patriot, Bob Nobody… The redneck steps forward with alcohol noticeably dripping from his chin. He lifts the rifle into the air with one hand, and fires. Hesston begins to speak as pieces of roof reign down from the ceiling and onto his head. Charlatan (smiling): ...And THAT’s what freedom is all about!!! Yes, freedom: the right to bear arms, the right to possess worthless, dangerous material objects that can be used to kill people… Bob interrupts him. Bob: Well actually, I use my for deer huntin’… Charlatan simply waves him off, shoving him aside and putting his mouth to his lips. Charlatan: Shh…be quiet… He smiles back at the camera, as if nothing ever happened. As the camera focuses in, he shoves the redneck away. Charlatan: …Ahahahaha! Silly redneck, what does he know? Why, he doesn’t even have proper healthcare, or the money to properly feed himself, let alone THINK! The redneck scratches his head, shrugs. Charlatan: Yes, my fellow Americans…so just remember our motto: “716 rounds—per-minute before $5.25 an hour”…and you should be all set! Another cheesy smile comes, followed by a phony thumbs up. Suddenly, the redneck whirls around and stares into the camera. Bob: Hey…wait a minute! I just realized my wife is starving, I’m six months behind on bills and my kids go to a school serving almost twice as many kids as the building was originally designed for…sooo….what good is freedom when…. A shocked look comes over Charlatan’s face, nervous sweat emitting from his pores as the camera pans in on the redneck. Bob: Ah well, must be my own fault! He shrugs, as Hesston, with a sigh of relief quickly takes the podium. Charlatan: Ah, must be! With that the camera fades out, the America flag proudly waving in the background.) George: Alright folks, we’re back… Charlie: Yes, we ARE back, and we’re ready to go! George: Yes sir, folks, we’re certainly are ready to go! Charlie: And joining us now from the great PCW…though not as good as WWE, our (TNN’s) own wrestling federation… George shakes his head. George: Nope, not as good as WWE… Charlie: …Abner! The camera pans around to focus in on Abner…who just happens to have his back turned to the camera. A cloud of smoke hovers over his head, as if to represent a redneck’s halo, and in his right hand; a cigar, his left; a Budweiser. Abner nods his head backward in the general direction of the camera before taking another drag of the cigar. Finally, he spins around with a smile on his face, and gives the camera a thumbs up. Charlie: Psst…Ab… The redneck whirls toward Charlie, glaring the fishermen in the eyes. Abner (shaking his fist angrily): Don’t you ever, ever call me that again! Before the shocked man can reply, Abner grins, popping open a can of beer. He downs it in five seconds flat; opens another. George: Uh…yeah…anyway, we, uh, got quite a show for you today! Charlie: That’s right, George. We’re broadcasting live (adding under his breath)…well, minus 48-76 hours…(smiles) from Georgia’s famous Lake Idonwannaleave. It’s the former burial ground of a group of Native Americans, who got forced off their lands! George: That’s right, Charlie! One moment they were here, and then, WHAM, just like that, they’re gone! So they damned up the river, flooding the original village, filled ‘er with fish, and WHAM, here she is, most beautiful lake in the world! Charlie: Damn right, George! God bless America. George: God bless America! Abner raises a brow, shrugging before going back to his beer. Charlie: But of course, before we start this trip… George (excitedly, raising his fists in the air): …the Bass Master, fishin’ tip of the day! Whoo! Charlie? The shorter of the two bends in, sticking his face in the camera. (Nostril shot, Blair Witch style.) Charlie: The Bass Master tip of the day: Remember, when planning your trip, always be safe, and be responsible! Any garbage of wastes products you bring in, you bring out. Keep our environment safe, and conserve our natural resources for the future generations to come. As he says this, the camera pans over to show Abner tossing an empty can into the water. He shrugs. Abner: What? I didn’t do it… George: Yes, that’s right…be sure to plan and be prepared for all kinds of accidents. Charlie: Many, many things can go wrong when your out on the water, and when you’re down for the count, you gotta be ready to face the facts! George: So be prepared! Charlie: And remember, always be responsible and safe on your trips! At this moment, Abner lets out a hellacious belch, and the camera focuses in on him. He smiles, holding a newly found bottle of Jack Daniels out for the audience to study. George and Charlie flash each other a look, considering shutting off the camera. Abner: Cheers! He gulps down the bottle, and tosses it aside. George: Uh…right… And then, Abner pulls out another. Abner: Working on number seven. George: Wonderful… Charlie (ignores them): Well, now I guess it’s time to do what we really came here for.. Abner: Drink? Charlie: …Uh…no… Abner: Drink…because your agent told you to? George: No. Abner: Waste airtime for your promotion because your VP promised you a raise if you did? Charlie and George: No! ….but close… Abner: Well then what then? Charlie: Fish! George: Yes, we’re here to fish. Abner: Oh… Charlie: Uh…yeah…anyway, getting down to business… The camera focuses in on Charlie’s hands as he pulls out a fishing rod from the back of the boat. It’s a long black rod with a brown reel cover with some sort of electronic device on the side. Working delicately with the thin, light wiring, he carefully attaches a sparkling gold lure and lets the bait drop. Holding out in his hand, the camera focuses in on the object. It’s a short red metallic sphere with multiple hooks coming out either end. Charlie: Now what you see here is the RedDevil Max-Fisher 1500. George: Yes sir, right there you saw the Red Devil Max-Fisher 1500! Charlie: Finest lure in the world. George: Finest lure in the world. A smile comes to Charlie’s face as the camera focuses in. With a quick snap of his wrist, he sends the line flying in a curved arch high above the water’s top. A few seconds later, a pool of water jumps up about thirty yards away as the Red Devil submerges itself under the lake. As it settles somewhere down in the murky depths, Charlie lets out a little more line. Abner: And what you see here is a piece of chicken. It’s his turn now. The surprised cameraman jumps a little, forcing the screen to shake a bit as he turns back to Abner. Abner tosses a large red and white striped bucket reading “KFC” over the side of the boat. As large crispy chunks of chicken float along the water, the two elder fishermen scratch their heads in bewilderment. Abner takes a chunk out of a tender breast he’s kept for himself and rips off a greasy shard, stabbing it with the end of his cook. He casts out and takes his seat on the boat, popping open another beer. He sits back and relaxes, not really ready to explain himself. George: Uh…Abner, what was that for? Charlie: Yeah, I mean, really, how do you justify that? Abner: Oh, the hook? Yeah, that’s just a decoy…you know, just incase they don’t come up to the boat and eat the real chicken… Charlie: What? Abner: You know…the chicken I just dumped in? Let them come up to the boat where I can shoot ‘em. Charlie: No! All of a sudden, Abner undoes his hunting coat to reveal a .45 strapped to his shirt. Pulling it out of the holster, he spins it around in his hand cowboy style before focusing in on the piece of chicken. He looks down the barrel of the sidearm, intently watching the fast food. Charlie: Well Abner, I don’t think you’ll be catching many fish that way. Abner: What do you mean? George: Yeah, he’s right. Charlie: I’m right. George: Nope, won’t be cathin’ many fish that way. Abner: Ahh! He waves them off with a hand. Abner: Horseshit! I’ve been troutin’ all my life, and every ol’ Vermont boy knows they like fried chicken! George: They do? Huh, us Georgia folks always though that the only things that eat fried chick… Charlie shuts him up with a sharp elbow to the ribs. He whispers… Charlie: Shh…you’ll never make Grand Wizard that way! George: Oh…sorry. They both cast a nervous smile back to the camera before going on about their business. Charlie: Well Abner, do it your way, but… He’s silenced as a small pool forms around the opposite form of the boat. Suddenly something jumps, and then another. BOOM! BOOM! BOOM! After a few loud bangs, things go back to normal. Momentarily, Abner reaches over the side of the boat and comes back up with a handful of fish. Tossing away the smaller ones, he comes up with a largest fish…a nine-inch Bass. He tosses it over to George. The fishermen doesn’t know exactly what to do, letting the greasy thing slip around his unprepared lap. Abner: See, told ya. George: Thanks…I guess. Charlie takes over for him. Charlie: Well, I guess it’s time for another Bass Master lesson…a lesson in law. See, when you catch an illegal fish-one’s that under the legal weight limit, or in this case, length-it’s only right to throw it back. George: And here at Bass Masters, that’s just what we do. Charlie: That’s what we do. Follow the law. Charlie: So Abner, if you could do the honors. Abner: Nope. Sorry, ain’t gonna happen. Charlie nudges him, talking out of the side of his mouth, away from the camera. Charlie: Abner, we’re on film. Abner: Oh! He pauses for a moment, straightening out his John Deere cap. Abner: PCW rules, y’all, PCW rules! Whoo! He sticks his tongue out, mashing is face against the camera. George and Charlie finally have to drag him off. Charlie: Hahah! Abner’s a real funny guy, isn’t he, George! George (phony laughter): Yeah, real funny! Abner: Anyway, where’s that knife of mine gone to? George (in nervous laughter): What do you need a knife for? Abner: To dress ‘er out, duh! Charlie: Ahh! He throws up his hands in defeat, reaching over to the camera. Charlie: Damn it, I’m shuttin’ ‘er off…guess we’ll just have to edit this part out… The screen goes dead for a second… *All of a sudden, roleplayer, Ryan Emerson shows up and takes a seat in the boat. Ryan: Yeah…so I guess technically, this part wouldn’t really be shown on the show since it got cut and all…but…meh. He shrugs his shoulders. George: What? Ryan: Meh. Ryan disappears.* The screen lights back up, and now, it’s quite obviously later on in the day. The sun has gone down a little further over the lake, and the long shadow cast by the boat begins to mess up the camera lighting, forcing them to change the angle. As the gentle waves slap up against the rocky shores, and the glowing red crest of the sun braids a crimson arch over the horizon, Abner downs another cold one… George: Howdy folks, and welcome back to Bass Masters! Charlie: Yes. Howdy folks, and welcome back. Abner: Goddamn it! Jesus Christ, don’t you two EVER stop repeating each other!?! Charlie: Never. Gorge: No folks, never. Abner nods his head in disgust. But the hosts simply shrug, carrying on about their business. They cast their lines back in to the water. Abner, on the other hand, sits back and pulls out a small green tin from his shirt pocket. The camera focuses in on the object to reveal its identity: “Kodiak Fine-Cut”. It’s chewing tobacco. Greedily pulling out a glob and rolling it into a ball, Abner shoves it into his mouth and begins to chew. His face rolls into a smile of content; reaching down, he pops open another beer. His long and tried face shows signs that he’s probably had a rough afternoon, more than likely, sick and tired of the fishermen… George: Well folks, shouldn’t be long before we catch one. Charlie: No folks, not long at all. Abner: Nope! Not too long, folks! Charlie: … George: … Abner: What? They ignore Abner, and get back to their fishing. Before too long, George gets a bite. George: I think I’ve got one, fellas! Charlie: I…I mean, he, he thinks he’s got one! Abner: No shit… The tall fishermen’s arm muscles tighten, and he purposely leans back, pulling with some strength. His hat falls to the bottom of the boat as he yanks the line in at an odd angle. Pretty soon, he begins to reel in, the fish towing behind. Something large hops across the top of the water, skipping and skidding like a stone. It’s obviously a big one. As Charlie prepares the net and Abner downs another beer, George continues to reel in his catch, putting on the final touches. George: My word, that’s a big one! Charlie: Not bad, George, not bad! George: Not bad, folks, not bad at all! Abner (impersonating Charlie): I’m stupid, hey look at me…I’m stupid! Abner (impersonating George): He’s stupid, look at him, folks, he’s stupid! Ignoring him, the two professional fishermen bend over the boat, slowly removing the line from the fish’s mouth, using the utmost care. After the lure has been removed and the fish is secure, George drops it in the net and Charlie holds it up to the camera. It’s a long, golden, Large Mouth Bass. Thick shiny scales with little black dots and thin brown stripes here and there give the captured fish life, and a long extended jaw gives away its specie. Charlie: She’s a real beauty, folks! George: She’s a real beauty! Abner (peering into a bottle of vodka): Yes she is, boys, yes she is… Charlie: 22-inch Bass, I’m guessin’, ‘bout three-three and a half pounds. George: Three and a half pounds, folks, he says three and a half pounds. George spreads the thinly bloody gash in its neck, exposing the fish’s gills. He peers into the fish’s breathing organ with the camera, forcing it in, as the wide-eyed fish gasps for air. Abner (sympathetically): I know just how you feel buddy, just how ya feel…Why, I remember one time when I went without beer for a whole hour… Charlie roughly jams his finger into the fish’s gills, spreads the bony breathing mechanisms apart, and begins to explain. …Abner drinks another beer… Charlie: This is a mighty fine lookin’ fish here. As you can see by the red gills and deep gashed lines between the…the things…uh…the fish breaths…er…uh…it’s healthy…it swims…and…uh…eats…uh…as you can tell by the gills…um, (scratches head) er…somethin’… George: Yes, that’s right, folks! This fish is lookin’ mighty fine…as you can see by the red gills and deep gashed lines between the…the things…uh…the fish … Abner cuts him short with a quick, painless backhand to the face. George: Ow! George rubs at the sore. Abner: Ah, quit yer bitch n’ moanin’! Charlie: Hey, you cant’ say that here! George: Yeah, you can't say that here. And besides, don’t get mad…we’re just fishermen, not scientists. Charlie: Yeah, you can’t expect us to know what to do after he fish is out of the water, folks…we’re just fishermen, folks, not… A slap for him too. Abner: Huh! Well you don’t got no damn 7-1 super opponent comin' up against you this Friday on Fusion, now do you? Charlie: Nope. Can’t say as I have. Geogre: Nope. Can’t say if I have either, folks, can’t say if I have… Charlie: But we’d rather watch WWE anyway. Abner snickers, waving the comment off the same way as in you’d swat a fly. Abner: Other federations…meh. George: Well anyway, we’re almost outta time folks, and so now, we show you how to… Abner: Dress out a fish? George: No. Abner: Well it’s about….huh? Abner scratches his head before leaning over the side of the boat and spitting out a glob of thick brown tobacco juice. George: No, Ab, ‘course not…why, it’s time for us to show the good folks at home of how to properly release your catch back into the wild! Abner: Release your catch!!! What the hell is wrong with you guys? Charlie (shrugging): It’s what we do. Abner: It’s what you do!?! What you do? I’ll tell ya what we do…well actually, I’ll tell ya what YOU do! You stop bein’ such pussies, and ya give it here, so I can gut ‘er out…save her for dinner…. Now that’s what you do! The two pros break out in laughter, patting Abner on the back. Not amused, Abner shoves them off, quickly taking up a defensive position. Their professional patronizing has finally got the best of him, and the young, vigorous hick is about to explode…redneck style. In a mere 48 hours, he’ll be in the ring with a rising PCW legend, and he has no time to do pointless, worthless promotional stunts for the company, while the rest of the boys are back at home training. Especially not with douches like these two… George: Good one, Abner! Charlie: Yeah, what do you think, we’re some kind of poor redneck like yerself or somethin’? Abner: … George: Why we don’t eat that crap. Charlie: Yeah, and speakin’ of which…you’re damn lucky we didn’t get it on camera….ya know… Abner: What? George: The crap? Charlie: Yes, the crap. George: Ahahaha! Abner here, folks, didn’t know what to do earlier on in the show when he had to take a number two, so the crazy son-of-a-gun… Pausing to laugh for a moment, before continuing, red-faced… George: …Hehehe…I’ll tell you what he did. He pulled down his drawers… Charlie: Pulled ‘em right down. George: …And pretended to take a dump off the back of the boat! AHAHAHAHA!!! They embrace each other in laughter, whilst Abner, shaking his head in disgust, whips out another beer and sets down to work. Charlie: And boy, did that piece of plastic crap look real! George: It looked ever so real, folks, ever so real. Charlie: Yeah, folks, barely could tell…barely could tell… Abner (nervously): Yeah…hehehe…fooled ya…really….really got ya there… Abner turns back to the camera giving it a wink before taking another sip of the beer, ignoring the two professional fishermen, oblivious to their rantings. George: Yeah…Anyway, folks, we’re out of time… Abner (sarcastically): Damn. George: …and so before I go, just remember that when releasing a fish back into the water… He lowers the net, letting the fish feel the moisture of its home… Charlie: Do it slowly. George: And carefully. He slowly lowers the net, making every precaution to be sure that the fish doesn’t simply flip-flop back into the water. George: And voila! Abner: No! Just as the fish drops into the water, Abner outstretched hand drops underneath it, saving the fish. Picking it up out of the water, he brings it to his chest and holds on tightly. He protects his precious dinner from the evil grip of the Bas Masters as they hover around him, slowly closing in. Abner (smiling down at the fish): Got ya! George: Alright, that’ll be enough of you! Charlie: Yeah, that’ll be enough… Abner: No FOLKS, no FOLKS…it wont’ be enough…FOLKS…it won’t… But they can’t take a hint, oblivious to the character attack on themselves. Thinking that Abner’s playing a friendly game, George nods to his partner, and steps forward to intercept the redneck. Abner nods hid head, beckoning him to come closer. He does. With a quick side step, Abner stands side-by-side with the taller fisherman, and gives him a light shove. George: Ugh! SPLASH A lone hat floats to the surface of the water followed several moments later by George’s head. A small fountain of water spurts forth from his mouth as he begins to swim around the other side of the boat, looking for a way to climb back in. But when he reaches the side, putting a hand on the hedge, Abner breaks the grip with a quick stomp of his barn boots, and the fisherman goes flying back into the water from whence he came. He hollers out from somewhere in the water. Geogre: Oh look, Abner, now you’ve got me all wet. Charlie: Yeah, folks, now he’s got him all w… SPLASH Before he can finish the sentence, he too finds himself in the water. George: Boy, Charlie, what’s that smell? Charlie: I don’t know, George, what is that smell? George: And folks, I wonder what I….huh?…Hmm…coulda swore I just felt somethin’ up against the side of my leg…
