Date: Dec.12/02 The parking ticket flapped in the wind as I stood and stared at it . . . it’s waving motion seemed to almost mock me. I couldn’t understand why I had been given this little piece of paper, and I could see the Parking Enforcement Officer just a few cars down from mine . . . ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ I ran up to the portly officer and inquired as to why I had been issued the ticket, and he simply said . . . “You were double parked . . .” With those words I turned to see that my Honda Accord parked, barely over stepping the boundaries of its parking space. In frustration I turned back to the officer and explained that the apartment building’s parking garage had been full, and some idiot had taken my spot. I had forced to park on the street, and in a hurry to run up to my room and use the washroom, I hadn’t parked my car as carefully as I would normally have. Unfortunately, the officer was a cranky, bitter old man who had served as a Parking Enforcement Officer for ten or twenty years, and he was stubborn as a mule. Knowing there was no way out of the ticket, I just reached into my pocket and pulled out a hundred dollar bill and placed it in his hand. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ With my parking ticket problem solved, I jumped into my car and I was on my way to the Haunted House. It was a good hour from my place to the Haunted House, and I only had thirty minutes to get there . . . so obviously I broke yet another traffic rule and sped my way there. Luckily, highway patrol cars were in short supply that day. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ I arrived at the Lenawee County Fairgrounds in Adrian, Michigan with about five minutes to spare until my appointment with destiny. The Haunted House Operator was there waiting for, and upon my arrival he flipped a switch and Haunted House’s power was activated. From the outside, I could hear simulated shrieks of terror, and the haunting wail of ghosts. I could feel my stomach roll over inside me, and a lump formed in my throat. I shot the Operator a look, as if to say . . . “I ain’t goin’ in there!” The Operator put his hand on my shoulder, and almost mockingly asked me if I was ready to go. I tried to calm myself down a little, then took a few steps toward the front door. I got about halfway up the front steps, then froze. The Operator almost had to rip my hand from the railing with a crowbar . . . that’s how tense I was. Finally, with a bit of sweet talking and a slap to the back of the head, the Operator managed to get as far as the front door. My hand was about a millimeter away from the doorknob, when there was a glass-shattering scream which emanated from the other side of the door. WHAT THE HELL WAS THAT!? I quickly pulled my hand away from the doorknob, and took a few cautious steps backwards. As I did so, I bumped into the Operator . . . scared the crap outta me . . . I nearly jumped out of my socks and shoes! The Operator assured me there was nothing to be scared of . . . the scream was just to set the mood before the customers entered the building. I finally shrugged it off, and made my second attempt to enter the Haunted House. As I reached for the knob, the house let out another blood-curdling scream, but this time I managed to grab a hold of the door and swung it open. Unfortunately, I had swung it open a little too hard, and ripped the door off the hinges which snapped the wires from the sensor which activated the scream. I apologized profusely, then entered the Haunted House . . . ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The inside of the house looked old, like it was straight out of the Civil War era. The rooms were all dimly lit, and everything was covered in about an inch of dust. The paintings of old-timey people which hung on the walls had gotten so old, that the paint had begun to fade, and it distorted the faces, making them look demonic. On one painting, I noticed the paint had begun to run . . . but the paint that was dripping was red, and there was no red on the painting . . . IT WAS BLOOD! As I smeared the blood/paint through my fingers, I could feel a tickling sensation in the back of my throat . . . the same sensation you get when you’re about to lose your breakfast, lunch, and supper. I managed to keep my regurgatory problem under control, and continued on through the house. As I made my way up a creaky, spiral staircase, I thought I saw a shadow peeking out around a corner. However, when I reached the top of the stairs, there was not a sole in sight. Even the Operator, how I thought was close by, has disappeared. I WAS ALONE! AND I WAS SCARED! But I had made it this far . . . can’t turn back now . . . The hallway at the top of the staircase split went both to the left and to the right. Smoke floated near the floor in the hallway to the left, and I could see multicolored lights flicker underneath the door at the far end. Weird sci-fi type noises were coming from the room with the colors, so I pretty much figured it was some sort of ‘Mad Scientist Theme’. However, there was not a sound coming from the hallway to the right. There were no funky lights . . . no smoke . . . no nothing. It was just pitch black . . . just like in my dreams. That was where I had to go . . . ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The air was bitterly cold, and reeked of rotting flesh. As I walked down the pitch black hallway, I could hear my heart pounding. I had never forgotten the last time I was in a situation like this, and that’s why I was more cautious then I had been as a child. No more running into walls . . . no more tripping over my own feet . . . I had to do this carefully, cautiously, slowly . . . I had to face my fear while calm, cool, and collected. Suddenly a large, fire breathing clown jumped out in front of me . . . ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Would I freeze in terror . . . Would I run home to mommy . . . Or would I look that clown in the eye, and tell him I wasn’t afraid anymore . . . Find out next time . . . same Michigan time . . . same Michigan channel . . . BOO ~ !
