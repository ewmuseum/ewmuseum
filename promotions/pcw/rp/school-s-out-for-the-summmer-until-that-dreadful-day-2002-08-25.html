School's out for the summmer....until that dreadful day

25 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8-25-02 Getting off the school bus on the last day of school throughout my school life always brought me all the happiness I have ever felt in my life because I knew school was over and I had to no longer put up with the teachers, the homework, the test and all the studying that lies between. I was looking forwarded to road trips with my family, playing baseball on the street and just having fun. But a cloud like no other on those summer days would overshadow all those warm days and this cloud was known as the return of school, the first day of school… I dreaded no day in the year like this one because I knew all the fun and excitement was ripped out of my body only to be replaced with a sour, bitter taste that was left inside of my mouth and a pain the ran throughout my entire body. I was always upset and angry that all the fun had to go away because I had to go back to school. All the family outings, vacations and fun around the block with the kids on my street were washed away by this monsoon known as the return of school. My parents loved it the most cause they wanted to see me get a good education and succeed in life, something I can look back on say “I’m glad they did.” But sometimes I had the feeling they just wanted the fun to end because they loved shopping for new school clothes, school supplies and putting me on that dam yellow bus that drove us to school every morning. Looking back on it I believe my parents looked at it as their vacation away from the me twenty-four seven and it was their direction back into the real world or their original reality of life. As a only child I was always faced with doing things on my own and being a individual but when summer came around I could always let loose, being who I want to be, doing what I want to do the most which was just having some good old fun. I always enjoyed summer trips, barbeques and just being a kid having fun doing things that I always looked forwarded to every summer that went by. But that all changed when school came around because when the first day of school came around and I realized that summer was over, I felt like I had just being knocked out by Muhammad Ali. It usually knocked the wind out of me making me feel like I was out cold and it was always the worst feeling in the world. It made me very depressed and saddened that all that was fun had to come to an abrupt end. Every year my parents realized how mad I was and they would always come into my room telling me how good it is for me, blah, blah, blah. But they would always end every speech with me with the old saying: “All good things come to a end” I hated hearing that because I knew that I was stuck in a school for nine months learning about things and not being able to see my extended family in different parts of the country, no more road trips, no trips to the beach, no barbeques and most importantly no more “summer fun.” Going back to school always gave me that feeling like I was just stung by a bee, a long lasting pain until the stinger is removed from the skin and I always looked at the removal of the stinger as the beginning of summer. The sting itself was always looked upon as the beginning of school, but the only bad thing about this sting was that it was lasting for nine months while the free life was only three months. I begged and pleaded with my parents all the time to take me out of school, but they would always answer with: “It’s for your own good.” I on the other hand always tried away of not going to school; I tried every trick in the book. My most famous way of trying to get out of school was the old sick routine where I wasn’t feeling well and I couldn’t go to school. But my parents would always send me to school unless I had a fever that was to extreme in there terms. So I had a back up plan, I would try the school nurse and see if she would let me go home. That didn’t work either because my parents wouldn’t pick me unless I had a fever. So I was sent back to the classroom, always disappointed. So I need to think of a new idea and I came across a story on the news about home schooling and how your child benefits more from home schooling then regular schooling. I told my parents about the story, I even had them watch the same story on the ten o’clock addition and they sat there watching it with a blank look on there face so I thought there was still hope. The story was over and they both looked at each other, I sweating like a Mongolian monkey not able to reach the banana on the other vine and when they looked each other I thought they were going to turn to me, saying “what a wonderful idea.” But they looked at each other and just laughed getting up from there chairs shaking there heads, laughing anyone one of them said “home school.” I was all out of hope because I knew that at the end of the day I would be going to the regular school that everyone would go to and that all hope was lost. I graduated in the school system I grew up in and walked away with a diploma I’m very proud of. But every year I knew I would be rewarded with the one thing that I looked forward to all year and that was summer vacation. My mother had her saying that “all good things come to a end” but I had mine, “all good things happen to those who wait” and I waited a very long time for summer and it was always worth the wait.
