Mono:- The Fourth:- Repercussions

21 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 21/05/03 And through the dark night, the car sped homewards, pulled through an urge to reach its destination at an unearthly speed. Inside, the car was clogged with a silence so thick and tense that Lacuna felt she was going to choke. She needed to get home, but she was not sure why. The subject could just as easily have been discussed in the speeding Mercedes as it shot like a silver bullet through the darkened country roads. She was so confused, so confused that it hurt. Her deep brown eyes were glued to the rutted tarmac and her hands to the padded wheel in her grip. Jade was still sat in the passenger seat, her white eye gleaming with a purity so deep that it created its own light that seemed to illuminate the area surrounding her. Her eye had been white since Lacuna had met her, and the subject of why had always been neatly dodged around. But this was different; it was so bright and gleaming. It was unnatural. She couldn’t bring up the urge to speak, the words were lost somewhere deep within her throat and all that came out was a gargling noise. Now Lacuna’s eyes were gleaming, but this was a different problem. This was fear. Her friend, the one she had been so afraid to lose, was further away now than she had ever been, even when she had gone AWOL. There was such a gap between them, and it needed to be breached. Lacuna, gathering up all her courage, tired to speak, but yet again her words were intercepted in her throat and all that was voiced was a loud gurgle. “Less speech, more driving” Jade’s voice was, well, not Jade’s voice. It was cold, emotionless, absent. She was not there, what was sitting there was something in the shape of Jade, a doppelganger with its intentions sold on taking over. Okay, maybe now her imagination was running away from her, but she was feeling threatened, and her imagination was her self-defence mechanism. Her thinking had been blocked partially. It doesn’t sound good, like a moment from a bad science fiction film or something, but she could feel it. She knew that it was impossible, and this was what she feared most. She also knew it was impossible for white hair and eyes to glow in the dark to such an extent as were Jade’s. Knowledge was her haven, her safe house if you will, but she was trapped in a mental obscurity while speeding down a dark, winding country road at over one hundred and fifteen miles per hour in a car with little grip on the poor quality road surface. She was trapped, lost without a hope. The organisation of her thought process was slipping, her solace dripping away through the outside-imposed mental block. The only thought that made any sense was that they were closed to her home. This, under intense scrutiny, would not hold up, as she would still have to discover exactly what it was that was causing Jade to act in such a way, as if she were possessed or something. Someone had warned her about this before. Someone close to her, but who is now far away. Someone they both knew so well. “NO! Stop!” Jade didn’t shout, she roared. Her words vibrated from every fabric of the lush interior of the car, crushing against the fragile eardrums of Lacuna who yelped in pain as a sharp shock of electricity jolted through her body. Her hands left the wheel and her head snapped back, flicking hair in all directions as the back of her cranium snapped into the plush headrest. Her eyes closed in a wince against the forces surging through her body. She couldn’t scream; the pain was too intense. Yet the car continued forwards. Her feet left the floor of the car as her body began to crumple inwards, her neck retching forwards towards her rising knees. She was forming the foetal position as the surging pain stopped suddenly to the grunt from the figure of Jade in the passenger seat. Lacuna, her wits beginning to reform, quickly pulled over the car with all the remaining force in her body. Her body didn’t ache, it screamed in agony through each and every of her open sweat pores which, indeed, did pour with sweat. Her raven locks were standing on end, her eyes periodically closed, and her knees were on the verge of completely locking out. But more worrying was the fact Jade was laying unconscious, her head flat on the dashboard, the white streak of her hair fading slightly as time trickled slowly forwards. But that could wait, because all she could do now was sleep. Her body was devoid of all energies, and so she couldn’t resist the slow closing of her eyelids. The bright sun slowly rose over the lip of the green hilled valley, casting long shadows of trees down its grassy slopes. Birds, floating from tree to tree, whistled loudly and with a mighty effort, Lacuna Debris slowly jerked open her eyes and lifted her head gingerly against the burning light. “Jesus, what happened?” she surveyed the car surrounding her as the memories began to come back. Her body was aching, the surging blood bringing new pain with every pulse of her frantic heart. ”Jade? Bloody hell Jade!” She was gone. Roughly, Lacuna shrugged off the protective harness of the seatbelt and struggle to open the door. Every pore of her body yelped with each movement as she thrust open the door to the silver Mercedes convertible and stepped out into the brisk morning air, chilled by the breeze sweeping down the valley. Normally she would have stopped and studied every detail, basking in the early morning light, but now she had a mission. She was searching for Jade again, but this time she was not guarded by dangerous people; she was dangerous herself. “Jade?” she called out, feeling as hopeless as her fragile words sounded amongst the twittering of the early morning birdsong. ”Jade? Are you here? There’s no bloody point in this!” Fiddling around in the deep pockets adorning the sides of the leather jacket she had borrowed from Jade before leaving the house, she found the key and locked the car doors out of habit. It would make little difference, as there was nobody out on the small back roads at the best of times, let alone just after dawn, but it helped her feel secure. She craved security, and Jade had started to change that with her antics, but now all Jade did was put the fear of God into her. “What has become of you, Jade? Why wouldn’t you let me remember the name of the last guy to help you?” Remembrance of the events of last night caused another sharp shock to travel through her body. Jade had instigated some sort of mind block, and Lacuna could remember that she had done it to someone they both knew well. It was… yes; it was the person that introduced them… Lacuna’s friend, what was his name? “By the 9 bloody pits of hell what is his name?” she shouted into yonder wilderness, only the bleating of the far off sheep and the twittering of the birds returning her shouts. She had not seen a sheep out so far this morning and could only presume it was just arriving. Lacuna stopped walking down the poor quality tarmac of the country road and surveyed the hillside. What she saw ahead of her was not exactly what she wanted to see. But it did remind her of something. The sheep was black, and it reminded her of the one person in the known world who could help her. Angelus Sorrow, always the odd one out; Always the black sheep. “He can help me! If Angelus cant help me then nobody can!” she almost shrieked in happiness as she sprinted back to the car, ignoring the pain in her pumping legs from last nights incidents. Sorrow was once a wrestler, but before that he was a close friend of Lacuna because he English, rich and living in America. They were similar in status, and somewhat in personalities. They had learnt the ways of the American culture together, and it was he that introduced Jade to Lacuna. Sorrow was a beast of muscle and brains, yet shackled down by his pessimism and cynicism. He was too dark in his persona to ever get along with Lacuna too well, for he pushed away everybody who did not follow his exact beliefs. He was a loner, deep and dark who rejected Christianity for the exact same reasons as Lacuna later proved to. They were similar in class and background, yet opposites in personality. Anyway, Why would Sorrow know what to do? It was all making sense to Lacuna as she powered back to her car down the country road. He was brainwashed by Jade, this mystical Jade. The one that brutalised her in the car that night. Why she didn’t remember this at the time she didn’t know, but now it could only help. Jade had turned him into a weapon she intended to use to assault her intended targets with in DWO, Chryste and Draven. Sorrow was helpless as he tried to struggle against the brainwashing and eventually broke free of her control, albeit to late as he was injured at the hands of Draven, yet to return to a ring. She had no definite clue of his current being as they had not shared a word since he introduced her to Jade, but she needed to get to her phone. If he had not moved, then she could contact him and get her out of this mess. She didn’t even stop to think about finding Jade. Unlocking the car with the quick press of a small button on the key, she reached inside and ripped her phone from its cradle, jabbing excitedly at the keypad with an anxious finger until she had found his number, pressing send and lifting the small silver phone to her ear, stamping her foot impatiently on the dirty tarmac. The phone rang. And rang. And rang. Angrily, she stamped her foot on the cold ground. It was not like Sorrow and his workforce to ever delay a call; they were like a clock. Time was of the essence in that household, and they savoured every second. Then, suddenly, the ringing stopped. “Hello?” Lacuna’s voice was ringing with excitement, she needed to speak to Sorrow, he was the only answer. The voice on the other end, however, didn’t think so.
