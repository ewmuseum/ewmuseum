A visit to the 'headshrinker.'

30 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/30/2002 ~Legends don't fall. Legends don't die. Legends do not go anywhere. Legends live forever. Sure, the man dies, but his legend lives forever in minds and hearts of those who remember him. Jon Kano will live forever, for he is a walking, talking, living, breathing LEGEND. There is no disputing this fact. If you would ask anyone with any knowledge of the history of PCW if Jon Kano is a legend, they would answer with an affirmative yes. Many have claimed to be a legend, many have added to their already inflated ego by calling themselves a legend, but Jon Kano never called himself a legend until people called him a legend. When he sat down and thought about it, he realized that he truly is, was, and always will be a REAL legend. This Friday, Jon Kano steps back in the ring against a young man in PCW by the name of Venneman Pendragon. This man has already won two titles in his short PCW tenure, but he has never sat foot in the ring with anyone close to being like Jon Kano. There can be no game plan for going against Jon Kano. Jon Kano is a six foot ten, three hundred and four pound, one man wrecking crew. No matter who has been thrown down the pike at him, he has stood tall. He has beaten everyone ever thrown at him....Boda....BEATEN.....Kriegman....BEATEN....Kid Wonder....BEATEN....Stephen Blood...BEATEN....Bang Davenport....BEATEN....Tyler Nelson....BEATEN...Get the idea?~ *The scene opens up in the lavish waiting room of some office in Montreal, Canada. The room is empty, except for Jon Kano sitting on a hard plastic chair, reading the latest issue of Forbes Magazine. Jon Kano actually looks pretty damn good for going through a war last Sunday in a retirement match against 'The King of the New Era' Corey Williams. Jon Kano is dressed to the nines in a pinstriped grey Armani suit with a charcoal black silk tie. He is very in depth in the financial magazine as the receptionist comes from behind her desk.* Receptionist: Mr. Kano, Dr. Brooke will see you now. *Kano places the magazine on the table in front of him, stands up, adjusts his tie, and walks into a room. The psychiatrist is sitting behind his desk, notebook in hand, but raises to shake the hand of 'The Man who Rocketh the Mosteth.' Kano takes a seat across the desk from the 'headshrinker.'* Dr. Brooke: Well, Mr. Kano, I understand you are here for insurance reasons. Kano: Yes. PCW is changing insurance providers and every employee has to get a complete physical and a psychological evaluation. Dr. Brooke: Have you ever been to a 'shrink' before? Kano: I went a few times following my brother's death. The Army told me if I went, I could stay in the army, then after I went, they discharged me. Dr. Brooke: That would be the protocol. Now I am going to do some word association with you, alright? Kano: Fine. Dr. Brooke: Good. Kano: Bad. Dr. Brooke: No, I-- Kano: Yes. Dr. Brooke: I mean--- Kano: Me. Dr. Brooke: Stop that. Kano: Go. *Dr. Brooke reaches into his desk and pulls out a bottle of Advil. He pours two out, sets them on his desk, and downs the rest of the bottle. He then begins to massage his temples.* Dr. Brooke: Lets do something else. Kano: Its not going to be that ink blot test is it? Everything looks like a helicopter to me. Dr. Brooke: No, just some standard questions. How was your childhood? Kano: Great. My parents loved me, and they provided for me as best they could. My brother and me were tight, best buddies. My father was tough on the two of us to do our best, but I dearly love him for giving me the work ethic that I have. Dr. Brooke: Your parents are still alive? Kano: Yes. My mother is retired and my father runs Kano Enterprises when I am on the road. Dr. Brooke: Sounds like a solid family. Tell me about your brother. Kano: My brother was two years my senior and I basically idolized him growing up. He was immensely gifted in sports, he set records in high school for baseball, basketball, and football. I came along and set the new records. It was ironic, he was so much more gifted than me. After he graduated, he enlisted in the army. Two years later, I enlisted. He and I were both demolition specialists. We loved to blow sh*t up, it vented our agression. I was on a special assignment, going to a seminar over new explosives, and my brother died in a freak accident where the bomb he was defusing went off even after the power was cut to it. It sent a piece of metal straight through his chest and into his heart, killing him instantly. Dr. Brooke: How did that make you feel? Kano: What kind of a Mickey Mouse headshrinker college did you go to? How did it make me feel? It made me feel great. I wish it happened earlier that the man that I idolized from a very young age died. I wish everyday I could cry like a baby into my wife's arms at his funeral. Jesus, how stupid are you? Dr. Brooke: I sense some agression. Kano: No shit? Really? Dr. Brooke: I think you may have a disorder. Kano: Everyone does, if you aren't manic depressive, you are bi-polar. If you aren't a schitzo, you are suffering from anxiety attacks. Dr. Brooke: Let me finish. I think you are a violent person with a lot of reserved anger stemming from your hatred of the work of Pablo Picasso. *Kano stands up.* Kano: Oh f*ck this. *Kano leaves the office and enters the hallway and begins to leave by walking down hallway.* Kano: My God, of all the headshrinkers in North America, and I have to get the one who thinks he is Freud. He has a disorder, I'll call it the Vennemann Pendragon syndrome. Or should I call it Lord King Vennemann Pendragon syndrome? Do you see where I am going with this. He says legends fall, well this isn't a Brad Pitt movie. Sh*t, that was 'Legends OF THE Fall.' Well it almost worked. Legends don't fall, they don't die. They give and give and give some more. I have given all I can to PCW and the wrestling industry as a whole. He says I am twenty six and have a broken body. Its true. My body has gone through hell. I have been in too many cage matches, been ran over a couple times, fallen from a balcony twice, went through many tables, and taken entirely way too many chairshots. Why? Because I am a legend. I have a reputation to uphold. My reputation is that every fan in the stands and watching on television is entertained. I keep it simple. I give everything I have every night. The people appreciate it. I will gladly do it over and over and over again. *Kano exits the building and begins to walk down the streets of Montreal, Canada.* Kano: I am going to knock Lord King Pendragon around a little in the ring Friday, just to remind him where I am from, THE OLD SCHOOL. And in the Old School, we do things based on honor and tradition. He is starting to remind me a lot of myself when I was a young buck here, and I am going to stop him from making the mistakes I did. But if he wants to shun my good efforts, then he truly is living up to the Lord moniker. I remember another man who called himself Lord about a year and a half back. His name was Lord Crom Griffin Dagoth, and he exploited PCW to further his own interests, until he got dumped on his ass one day and took his ball home and left. Now, Lord Crom Griffin Dagoth doesn't have half the talent that Pendragon does, but Pendragon has got him topped on ego. So Friday, I will have to give the Lord King his royal ass kicking for the good of him, myself, and most importantly PCW. And that, my friends, is why I am WHAT REAL LEGENDS ARE MADE OF. Now where in the hell did I park? *The scene fades to black as Kano begins to search for his vehicle.* ~Kano, a legend? Definitely. Pendragon, a king? Hell no.~
