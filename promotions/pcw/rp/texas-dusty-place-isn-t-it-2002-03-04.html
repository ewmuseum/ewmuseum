Texas: Dusty Place Isn't It?

4 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4th March We arrive at our destination. It’s a small rundown bar in the middle of absolutely no where... we stand outside the place and it’s literally just dust and rocks around it for as long as the eye can see. You have no idea how this place came to be here in the first place, nor how exactly it still manages to be here since the passing trade can’t be that great. It’s a dusty run down place that looks like a bad gust of wind could knock the entire place down. It has a sign that seriously needs half a gallon of oil to grease up it’s hinges, but it still works displaying “Beer Here!” in brown rusted letters upon it’s dimpled surface. At that point a car pulls up. Not just any car, but a BMW Z3 in British Racing Green Metallic. Or at least it would be if it wasn’t for all the dust covering it. In fact you can see the smears on the windscreen where the windscreen wipers have been used to try and clear the dust off. And for the first time in the BMW’s life (most of it having been lived in nice sunny California...) the hood is up. Yes, don’t faint, but the hood to Miyagawa’s car is actually... UP. It doesn’t take a mathematician to work out why... it’s probably got something to do with the amount of crap being blown about out there. The driver and front passenger doors open and out steps Miyagawa and Steve Jabowski from their respected sides. They close the doors quickly and run to the bar’s entrance way. Miyagawa turns and tries to activate the infrared locking system on his little key ring but nothing happens. He can just about hear him swear through the dust, before he runs back to the car and ends up having the lock the thing manually... all the while his long black woollen coat is getting blown around, the pockets being filled with more and more of this dusty crap. Finally he runs back to the bar, and finds Steve having already gone inside. He steps inside the bar and tussles his hands through his hair to try and get some of the dust out of it. Miyagawa: Jesus Steve, you could have waited for me. He looks at the volume of dust pouring past his face onto the ground and shrugs... which knocks even more of the dust past his face and onto the floor. Finally he looks up and finds that Steve is the only person in the bar apart from himself... and the fat barman who only gets four lines in this promo. Miyagawa walks up to the bar and promptly finds that Steve has already finished his first beer. Miyagawa looks over to the barman. Miyagawa: Can I get a mineral water here. The barman walks over to him. Barman: Now this is Texas. We don’t serve sissies in Texas. It’s either Beer or Whiskey, pick one and then drink it. Miyagawa: Erm... ok, I’ll have one of each then please. Have you got any ice? **looks at the barman** Don’t worry about the ice on second thoughts. The barman pours out a glass of whiskey and then uses the tap to pour out a glass of unknown murky crap that’s apparently labelled beer. Personally if I was drinking in an establishment with that much dust, I would have asked for a glass of mineral water too. I guess I’m just a sissy then. The barman plants the beer down on the table next to the whiskey. Steve: I told him you’d pay for my two beers too. Miyagawa: So how much is that? Barman: That’ll be thirty seven dollars including service charge. Miyagawa: What?! Thirty seven bucks for three beers and a whisky? Barman: Well not really. Its twelve bucks or thirty seven bucks and I won’t call the cops to tell them that you’ve been drinking and driving. Or of course it’s free if I fetch my shotgun and my particularly ugly daughter named Sue-Ellen. Miyagawa: I’m married already. Steve: And I’m unattractive, poor and impotent. Why do you think I’m the only guy on television without an attractive woman on my arm? Miyagawa: What about Boda? Steve: He’s married isn’t he? Miyagawa: Like I said, what about Boda? Steve: That’s low. Miyagawa: Hey, so is getting someone else to do the job for you. Did I stand back and let other people beat up Boda for me. Well did I? Steve: Actually the other guys in Asylum finally showed some stable unity and were doing it for you for a while. Miyagawa: Dammit... ok... well did I actually turn up with them? Steve: You turned up with Jeremy. Miyagawa: Only because Kid Wonder wouldn’t get in the ring! I did nothing out of the ordinary to Boda. Meanwhile Boda, in order to finally get a non tag team title with Hardcore Anthony Cay managed to resort to getting Mexi to enter the match on his behalf and then eliminate me. Me! I deserved to win that match, meanwhile Boda deserved to be locked in the ream team locker-room for an hour. He can’t even decide what nationality he is... is he Czechoslovakian or is he American? Steve: **cough**Japo-America**cough** Miyagawa shrugs and the barman coughs. Miyagawa in his pissed off state accidentally gives the Barman a fifty, which the asshole Barman decides to take as a tip, and in his annoyance with Steve, Miyagawa doesn’t even notice. Miyagawa: There is one major difference between Boda and me. You see Boda rhymes with Skoda and I don’t. Ok, there are two differences, and this one is a really big thing... I would be a credible champion while Boda won’t. Steve: And why is that then? Miyagawa: It’s completely obvious isn’t it? It’s... Because... I’m... Excellent. Steve sits there slack jawed. Not so slack jawed as to have lost the ability to drink however, as he drinks down half his pint of beer before wiping the excess off his lips with the back of his hand. Steve: That’s probably the first legitimate setup you’ve had with that for a while. I’d be proud of that because let’s face it you really do have to work at getting that catchphrase in now, don’t you? Miyagawa: Jesus what the hell have you turned into, my biggest critic or something? I thought Jeff Marx hated me, but you are just really the frickin’ icing on the frickin’ cake you are. I feel that an injustice has been done and you just sit there and disagree with me. Steve: It’s not that. It’s just that you’re complaining like a spoiled brat. Miyagawa turns and goes to say something to Steve when he suddenly stops in mid thought and physically pulls his head back as if in shock at something. He scratches his chin for a second with his finger and then downs the whiskey in the glass. Miyagawa: You know what? Steve: What? Miyagawa: You’re right. This is not a time to complain, it is a time to train and prepare and make right on the thousand promises I have made since I first stepped foot in the ring. And you know what else? Steve: What? Miyagawa: I’m not entirely sure what I just drank was actually whiskey. Let’s go find a real bar... oh and one more thing. Steve: What’s that? Miyagawa: We need to speak to the effects guys, I thought this was meant to be Texas, not frickin’ Dune. And with that the duo get up from the bar, drinks still on the table and make for the door... and the dust outside that always ensues as the cameraman ceases to be... well not really, he just hits the big old “off switch” but if this was something to do with neo void and all that crap, it would have something to do with vanishing and reappearing and tickling your gonads with feathers and all that mumbo jumbo voodoo bullshit.
