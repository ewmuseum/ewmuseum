Suspicions Arise: Section 1 w/ A Step Closer To (Immortality)

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 5 of 2002 A.D. “Red Raptor to nest, Red Raptor to nest. Target acquired. Requesting immediate extraction, I repeat, requesting immediate extraction.” “Well executed, major. Take your team and proceed to the rendezvous point, the extraction unit is airborne. Expect pick-up within min….” “God Almighty!! What the f*ck?! Belfort!! Gregov!” A hideous sound of spine-tingling shrieks mingled with a steady outburst of inhuman cries, deep in pitch and distinctly inhuman yet forming roughly human monosyllables. This was permeated by intermittent bursts of frenzied gunfire and a gut-wrenching shredding sound. A choking voice came over the speakers, the accent carrying a strangled tone as if gargling with some abrasive or thick substance. “Every…where…..help…..” An insidious snarl drowned out the anguished plea and the sound snapped into a wave of sheer static. McCarver, donned in his formal ritualistic dress, clicked the Stop button on the tape recorder. His lips pursed together before his eyes lowered to skim over the tiled floor, the effects of the fateful expedition’s final moments sweeping across the befuddled room of politicians, scholars and soldiers. Several of the Blackhelm Coalition’s ranking officers and committee heads, along with numerous Miskatonic trustees, were scattered about the dim chamber. Also among the dejected audience were the Delta Fox unit, with their civilian ‘guide, Bruce Shanahan, along with a handful of the organization’s other elite military regiments. Major Blake Hawkins broke the eerie silence, removing his thunderbolt-emblazoned officer’s hat and dropping it on the desk before him. “And the meaning of that?” he inquired in a gruff voice, eyes twinkling with a hint of concealed anger. “Its purpose at the present time is to prove a point,” iterated McCarver. “Yet its occurrence is also, in part, the reason for this assembly. What you just heard were the final moments of the ill-fated excursion we sent to Bilboena Isle two days ago. Their mission was to retrieve a valuable relic that had pervaded visions of the Miskatonic psychics for weeks. Presumably they found what they were searching for….but obviously, also encountered quite a bit they were not looking for….” “On yet another glorified suicide mission, “ chimed in Hawkins again. “Another kamikaze run into the heart of a known CCD (Cthulhu Cycle Deities) hotbed, ordered by those bastards on the Council!” “In all defense of the Elders,” retorted McCarver, “intelligence reported that whatever was there had migrated south weeks before. The area was presumed secure…” “Bah!” scoffed Hawkins, slamming his trembling fist on the desk. “Those f*ckers on the Council always presume too much! And because of them, over a dozen good men senselessly lost their lives! Fourteen good soldiers lost their lives in the line of duty for the gain of five faceless sons of bitches who don’t even have the gall to issue any member of this organization orders beyond a letter or a g*ddamned email!” Hawkins epitomized patriotism and military loyalty, and was characterized as the sort to always be among the first of the mourners for any devoted soldier. He had held a strong rapport with Major Cunningham and was loathe towithhold his criticism of the High Council’s decision to ever send Cunningham’s Ukraine-based Red Raptor unit into an area classified as such a ‘hot zone’ of CCD activity. His condemnation had been open before the catastrophe and had expanded tenfold afterward. Even among the Blackhelm rogues he had been deemed a loose cannon. “Just more lambs to the slaughter,” Shanahan thought silently from beyond his traditional mirrored shades. “This is becoming far too common.” “We all know you dislike the maneuverings of the Council,” said McCarver defensively, trying to calm the irate major in order to continue. “But the fact is, they have the authority to issue those commands and they have ever since the charter of this Coalition.” “They’re nothing more than anonymous puppet masters!” exclaimed Hawkins, now drawing himself to his feet in protest. “And they have all of us on strings! Too many have died in these last weeks to not take action! Overthrow the bastards!” A small throng of those sitting around the hot-tempered officer restrained him, his rage consuming and overriding his rationale. McCarver turned away and cleared his throat indignantly as the major lowered back into his seat. Stryker nodded in the major’s direction as a sign of his own more reserved support. The captain likewise glanced back at Shanahan, whose demeanor hadn’t changed since he set foot into the chamber. An impassive stare masked his visible features, and raised a sigh from the Delta Fox commander. Frankly it was hard to tell if Bruce’s mind was on the issue at hand in that humid, tension-ridden room or his next match in PCW. After Shanahan had finally won the prize he had so pursued in that company, Stryker feared the hardcore veteran’s attention would be forever diverted as long as his success continued. And whether the Boston thug realized how invaluable his involvement was or not, Stryker prayed his street-bred comrade would remain loyal. He had a gut feeling the secret society was about to plunge into dark times, and he could think of nary a better man to battle through adversity at the side of then a man with a perseverance-forged will the likes of Bruce Shanahan. “Go on,” rang out an unknown voice, emanating from the section seating the Star Angel unit led by Lieutenant Colonel Neville. “We all know what happened two days ago….why replay it?” “Because it’s distinctly similar to the disaster that befell the Caribbean mission last week….the Tropical Massacre,” resumed McCarver. “Another conversation many of you heard, apart from the unit members themselves who sit here today. Radar and sensors detected no movement, the area was quiet as a grave. Moments later the underwater camera picked up images and the place became a hot zone teeming with star-spawn and those damned reptilian humanoids. Only this attack happened only two days ago, only a few miles off the banks of a small island near Sri Lanka. And it happened to another detachment in the Andes two weeks ago.” “What are you getting at?” boomed Sergeant Fremont from the background, the ‘muscle’ of Star Angel and a man that even rivaled Slade in terms of brawn and sheer size. McCarver cast a glance at one of the Miskatonic professors in attendance, all sitting collectively in the far corner of the room. McCarver clasped his hands and turned away to look on a gloomy mural embellishing the west wall as the nervous professor adjusted his tie before taking the bishop’s place. A thin and slender man with a brow saturated in sweat, he cast a nervous fleeting look at McCarver. “Um…..for those who don’t know me, I’m….um….Professor Maurice Rudyard, chief of the Miskatonic University Physics Department. We have been….uh….doing some studies in regards to the CCD and their projected outposts. And…well….we’ve developed some rather interesting theories about their movements as a whole. Time warps and jump gates, dimensional passages that can only be opened when certain conditions are met….” “I ain’t no scientist, hell I don’t even have a college degree!” shouted Slade, “But, all the science mumbo-jumbo aside, does this mean those things can open doors to wherever the hell they came from and bring in their little lackeys?” “In essence, sir, yes…” Murmurs went up among those in the room, obscuring the words of the anxious professor as he grew gradually silent. “Why the hell weren’t we told about this before?!” “It…it’s still unproven….” “How long have you known this?!” “We’ve been…been researching it for a few months….” In the midst of the surmounting chaos and uproar resulting from the Miskatonic’s scholar’s revelation, Stryker pulled himself into a chair beside the stoic Shanahan, unable to stifle a chuckle at the proceedings which had nearly degenerated into a wide scale blame game. “Bruce…..haven’t gotten so much as a sneer out of you this entire meeting. Anybody home?” The captain snickered as Bruce rotated his shoulders to loosen, then removed his shades casually before shooting a gaze around the room. Professor Rudyard was being bombarded from every cardinal direction with accusations and questions, and McCarvr was attempting to quell the mayhem. “I say people are finally wakin’ up to what I’ve said all along,” he muttered softly. “Unfortunately it took a slaughter like the Tropical Massacre to open their hazed eyes to the truth.” “Ignorance is like a reflex for mankind I think,” Stryker commented. “A safety valve of sorts.” “About time someone breaks the seal on that valve,” sneered Shanahan. “This is the last organization on the planet that should be damned by ignorance…” (Excerpted from the memoirs of Bruce Shanahan) It took innumerous baptisms in blood and a plethora of answered prayers (along with some shattered ones I’m sure) to finally attain what I have attained. After all the gallons of spilt blood, the anguish of defeat and the tattered flesh ripped from my body….the pleas for salvation gone unheard, the seething hatred evoked in my soul, the pain churning in my countless blood-soaked memories….. The crown is mine. Now comes the hard part: restoring dignity and validity back to this tarnished trophy. A prize that should have been worn by seasoned gladiators and hardened radicals now sits disgraced and shamed, stained by such names as pretty Kramer Wild Wing and corporate puppet Reginald Lovecraft. It’s been carried by ex-porn stars the lines of that mockery of a man Flaymin Aymin and misguided ingrates like Big Daveylicious. And it is my duty to restore some of that long lost legitimacy to this parody of a championship. The first major test comes comes tomorrow night. I go into combat with that despicable wretch Aymin at my side, taking on that Nazi f*ck Mark Makonie and his partner/enemy in crime, Duzza……a glorified fatal 4-way packaged as a tag team match. Aside from a few random tags, I don’t foresee much in way of camaraderie or fluent double team maneuvers. As long as Amin keep his word and fulfills his obligations, I leave him be….but the moment he betrays me or even thinks about taking what is mine, I’ll be sure that sorry bastard receives a full Boston-style beatdown. I didn’t ask for this. And I sure don’t want it. The ball is mine, I can feel the glare of the spotlight in my eyes. And if Mark Makonie thinks I’m about to simply relinquish it for him, maybe he needs to throw himself in a f*cking gas chamber. This is how it all starts. I thirst for immortality, the urge is simply insatiable. To make an impact, and have your name someday engraved in the hallowed annals of eternal glory, one needs to make waves and leave a lasting impression. Being the Extreme champion of Primetime Championship Wrestling may not be the key to that door, but it surely is the skeleton key to opening many other doors that will eventually lead down that path. Someday that honor will be mine. But for now, I take it one step at a time. Makonie, Aymin, Duzza....all casualties on my ascent up the proverbial ladder. Time for them to meet their purgatory personified, the fire in which they will be baptized. I’ll take them to the brink of extreme and beyond. A descent into the macabre....
