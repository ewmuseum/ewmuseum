The Death Of Controversy: Moving on to Bigger Things

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: The past, present and FUTURE. Alan Kriegman couldn't do it in the first era. Tyler Nelson couldn't do it in the second. But as we enter the third era of PCW, it seems that Corey Williams has. With the help of love-hate friend Semore Smoke, Williams achieved what many others have tried but never succeeded. Corey Williams finally put a stop to the man who couldn't be stopped; he finally kept Jon Kano down for the count. On Friday Fusion, Jon Kano, an unbeaten legend of the first two eras of PCW, suffered two losses. Firstly, he was beaten at a Pay Per View for the first two times, first to Corey Williams and secondly to Semore Smoke with the help of Corey Williams. Then, once the chinks in Jon Kano's seemingly unbreakable amour had appeared, he was beaten into submission of the thing he most prides himself on, his career. With Kano gone, PCW enters the third era without a 'REAL' legend. As the third Dawning approaches, new legends will be born. It is now time for Corey Williams to put his focus back into becoming one of those legends. ::Our scene opens up in a large banquet hall, with twelve large round wooden table situated all over the red-carpeted room. Sitting at the tables are many important looking men and women. To the back of the room is the stage of the hall, also red-carpeted, it is surrounded by a square of three steps. At the front of the stage sits a rich wooden podium which sports a metal 'CCW Enterprises' logo on its front; a large star with the letters 'C C W' written in it. On the wall behind the stage hang two huge silk posters which hang from the roof and reach all the way to the floor, both of which have the same Charles Foster Kane-esque picture upon them of Corey Williams. It is a scene almost reminiscent of the Nazi campaign rallies in the 1930s. A man in a coat tailed tuxedo steps up to the podium and places his white gloved on either side of it. He jerks his head to flick back his blonde hair, which is slickly combed over, and clears his throat before speaking in a slightly English accent.:: Man: Distinguished ladies and gentlemen, it now gives me great pleasure to introduce you to the man who made tonight happen. The man who has brought us many successes and has guaranteed the happening of even greater successes in the future. A man whose name will become synonymous with the word 'legend'. I present to you, a man who is both the leader of our company and the future leader of his industry. A man who is most certainly the pioneer of the new era. I give you..... Coorrreeey Willllllliammmms!!! ::A thunderous applause fills the hall as the door at the front of the room bursts open and Corey Williams enters the hall in casual fashion. Abandoning his traditional bear fur coat and tight leather pants, we instead see Corey Williams dressed sharply in a Don Armaggio suit and matching black Ray Bans. He walks down the hall flashing that familiar grin left and right and acknowledging his audience, something he doesn't do very often. He steps up onto the stage and shakes hands with the man in the tux before turning towards the microphone attached to the podium.:: Corey: Thank you, Charles. Charles everyone, my new butler and assistant! ::Charles too gets a round of applause as he leaves the stage. Corey waits for the applause to die down before continuing, carrying himself in an almost presidential manner.:: Corey: Distinguished ladies and gentlemen, shareholders and employees of CCW Enterprises, each and every person alive has expectations of the future. This expectation is usually based upon the factors of their past and present, how talented they are, how youthful they are and how intelligent they are. From these three factors we can at least estimate the future of a human being. The future is indeed always in motion, for as a life ends, another begins, and more specifically, when one career dies, another begins. The fact that one happening must rely upon another is a process that we have come to rely upon. But there are people, selfish people, in this World who choose to ignore the truth of the process, who choose to deny others their destiny. One such person is a man I recently had the disprivelage of meeting; Jon Kano. Jon Kano is, or at least WAS... ::Williams gives a small sick grin.:: Corey: ...a man who chose to ignore the fact that when he got up in the morning his bones ached, that he had a wife to look after, that he was past the best years of his career and should do the honorable thing in stepping down and letting fresh new faces aside. He should have done the right thing by himself and disappeared, only to be remembered of fondly on occasion as a great wrestler, instead of staying on and becoming an embarrassment to both our company and our industry. But that was not Jon Kano's style, 'give it all till you die' he said. Gentlemen, fate does not always go our way, for the actions of a few cannot be predicted by the rest of us. But if there is ever a test for true greatness, then MAKING fate come to you after it has turned his back is truly it. Most people would accept the fact that things were not going the way they were meant to, but not a great man like myself. I refused to let one petty man disrupt my destiny. Jon Kano said 'give it all till you die', so I went and sped up that whole 'die' part. I got rid of Jon Kano, I knocked him off the perch so I could claim my rightful position. And now it is time for us to take that position, to begin our stronghold on this industry! ::Applause from the men and women in the crowd fires up again. Williams waits for it to die down and continues, this time a little more cockiness in his tone, no longer seeming so serious, Corey walks around the stage, one hand in his pocket, as he talks with a great passion.:: Corey: Many people doubted this great company when I founded it after the death of my Father. 'What do you even do here?' they asked, they did not share my vision. They did not see the vision of a 100-man strong marketing machine, backed by thousands of investors, all moving towards the same goal. Organizing press conferences, setting up meetings with political leaders, all to promote one man: the ultimate man. The man who assured you that he was the future of this industry, the man who assured you that he could get the job done in the ring. I have proven to everyone that I can indeed do just that, I ended the career of the man that never said die. I shifted the immovable object, I crushed a legacy and made way for my own. Gentleman, the World is our oyster. It wasn't an easy process, but we have done it. It wasn't a direct process and as such was sometimes frustrating, but then again, being a pioneer never was. And now that period is over, we are no longer the pioneers of the New Era Using the foresight that no single man has ever been able to in this business, we have secured our spot as LEADERS of the FUTURE! ::Cheers rings out at this fierce yet positive message and Corey nods a thank you to both sides of the audience before speaking, this time a little more quietly.:: Corey: During my short time under the tutelage of Tyler Nelson, I realized just how much of my Father I had in me. How I was born for business and how the whole 'controversy' thing was nothing more than me rebelling against my Father. Now I realize that not only am I incredibly similar to my Father, but that I am superior to him. He never appreciated the art of subtle manipulation of the public, instead he opted for the more direct approach of corporate destruction; brute force. My Father would have never have had the patience to put together a scheme like this, he wanted to rule today, not tomorrow. What he failed to see is that tomorrow always becomes today, and if you're prepared then you will not only rule today, you will rule the day after that and the day after that. You will rule, WE will rule, like no one else before us! ::The crowd applauds again.:: Corey: Tomorrow has become today; we are witnessing the third dawn. The Sun has risen and it's only gonna get better. Our months and months of planning has built up to this dawning, the dawning of a new era, of a new legend, of a new legacy. With a marketing department working in full cooperation with a living, breathing and highly capable product as well as allies backing us up like Semore Smoke; no one can stop us. Whether it be John Crichton, Jean Luc Picard or even Superman himself! Real or fiction, friend or foe, man or woman, boxers of jockies, we will yield to no one! Today we rule the future, tomorrow we rule the present. First we rule PCW, then this industry, and then THE WORLD! All will bow before us, all will succumb to the New Era! ::As Corey's voice hits a climax the entire crowd give a standing ovation and roar in applause. Corey Williams saunters down the steps with a delirious smile upon his face. He walks down the aisle towards the door, stopping to shake hands with various members of his company. He finally reaches the door, which is opened for him by his butler Charles. Williams thanks Charles before exiting through the door, which is promptly closed by Charles who follows Corey soon after. The scene fades out with a shot of the CCW Enterprises team, a team of people all working towards making Corey Williams the single largest commodity in both professional wrestling and the World. Is Corey Williams telling the truth, has this trivial dispute with Jon Kano just been a means to clear a path? Has Corey William's seemingly flailing career been on track all along? Or is this just a method of propaganda by Corey Williams to enthuse his employees?:: !The End!
