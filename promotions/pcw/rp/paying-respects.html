Paying Respects

2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/03/03 The sun slowly lowers and a light drizzle falls from the heavens. My Dodge Charger splashes through a puddle, as it transports me to a place that I never wanted to visit. I wish that I could have told Kari where I am going, but this would be really hard for her to deal with and I think that I really need to do this alone. A final right off the main road and my vehicle gently coasts into an empty parking lot. My door creaks open and I rise out of my pitch black car. I pull the hood of my Independent sweatshirt up and over my messy black hair. In my right hand is assortment of flowers wrapped up tightly in plastic. 'Here we go,' I say to myself after taking a deep breath to calm my nerves. I begin my journey up a well maintained concrete walkway. With each step I take, I can feel my heartbeat faster and my stomach knot. The pathway is long and full of curves. I look over various tombstones and see fathers, mothers, sisters and brothers all laid to rest. These tributes will never be made for me, but I have seen way too many built for people that I love. The one I have come to see is possibly the worst of them all. I have visited loved ones' final resting places before, but this is different. No words can possibly describe how I feel at this moment because this is not a loved one. This is the loved one. I see a familiar landmark in the form of a massive oak tree. The tree has a fresh new batch of leaves and seems to be flourishing, after its near death in the winter months. Maybe it is a sign from the heavens telling me that times can be tough sometimes, but they will eventually get better. The grave is the second on the left after the large oak and it steadily becomes harder to block out the sadness within me. I look down at the tombstone. 'Kitten McDonald,' I read and I feel my knees become weak. The name rips through me like a hot knife through butter. I cannot stop the tears from building in my ice blue eyes and then streaking down my face, as I lay the flowers against her tombstone. The girl was my world. There was never anyone or anything I cared more about than her. I love Kari, but Kitten was the one. She was to be my very first wife. I rarely let a girl get close enough to where I could let them love me because I could not watch them die, as I remained the same. However, I could not help myself with her. She was all I ever wanted or needed. I struggled to get through each day because there is nothing good to look forward to and then she arrived and my life new joy once more. Each time that I held her in my arms, I felt what heaven must be like. She filled the void in my heart that I never thought would be filled. Kitten made me more than a slayer. She made me human and then, she was taken from me. When she left, my humanity left with her. I became a shell of my former self. I had no remorse, no regret, and no regard for human life. I was no longer a man. I was a machine. The complete opposite of what she liked about me. As I stare at the tombstone, the events of that fateful day speed through my mind at a rate faster than that of light. I can remember it as if it were yesterday. Her and I were relaxing at home when I got a text message from my old ally, Futeki Jr. He was just back from Japan and needed me to come over, so I could pick up something that his family wanted me to have. I was already to go when Kitten told me that she would go because it was on her way to the store. I agreed to the idea. Little did I know what was in store. It was a cold and rainy night, so Kitten wore one of my sweatshirts and I made sue that she had the hood up, so she would not catch a cold. She gave me a kiss and I told her that I loved her, as she walked out the door of our apartment. I drove a Shelby Mustang at the time and I watched it carefully, as it drove past my window. The roar of the engine slowly became quiet and the sound of a horde of motorcycles appeared. I sound of the motorcycles were familiar, so I peeked back out the window and saw them going down the same path as my car. The realisation of what was happening hit me like a ton of bricks. I was being targeted by the local pack of vampires. However, they did not know that Kitten was behind the wheel and not me. I never ran faster in my life, as I rushed to my motorcycle. It took me too kicks to start it and I gave it no time to warm up. I sped through the streets like a bat out of Hell. I finally caught up to them and my worst fear came to life. Her body was damp from the rain and her sparkling, brown eyes were nothing but cold and soulless. I still have nightmares about them to this day. I do not remember anything after that point. The next thing I recall is Futeki Jr. restraining me, before I broke down and wept. I pull my hood down and look up to the sky. I am trying so hard not to crack, but it is so hard. I just want to drop to my knees and cry my eyes out. It hurts so badly. Usually, I can be strong and deal with everything, but I am so weak when matters of the heart are concerned. My defences are giving out and the sadness in my heart is slowly rising to the surface. The feelings are too much for me and finally, I cannot hold back anymore. I lower my head and begin to softly weep. I begin to talk to her grave. 'Kitten, it is me,' I announce. 'I just wanted to stop by and tell you that I am doing fine and that I miss you. For a while, it was really tough for me because you were not there. It took me six hundred years to find my true love. Who would have thought it would have been the cute young girl that I hired to watch my nephew, Bobby? I know that I did not, but I am glad that it did. I miss you more than anything.' I take a deep breath and pull a handkerchief from the pocket of my black, cargo pants. I wipe my nose with it and slide it back into where it came from. 'Well, I am going to change the subject before I get any worse,' I say softly, as I try to relax. 'I started wrestling again. I know how much you enjoyed coming to the ring with me and watching me from ringside. It is odd not having you there, cheering me on... I have a new girlfriend. Her name is Kari and she reminds me so much of you. I like to think that the two of you would have been good friends, if you met, or at least I would hope you would...' I struggle to keep together, as I keep going. 'Bobby is doing well,' I inform her spirit. 'He is staying in England with a friend of mine. I hear from him now and then. My friend says he is doing well in school and actually is beginning to become a decent, young man. It is hard to believe after all the trouble he gave you.' A small smile slips on my face, but it quickly vanishes. 'I have to get going,' I quietly weep. 'I will be here on this day for all eternity because that is how long I have to wait to see you again. I will never make it to Heaven, but if I do, please wait for me...' My emotions become too much for me and I lose control. Tears fall from my eyes like the rain of a hurricane. I am so tired of being strong and it feels good to finally let it all out. I cry at Kitten's grave for several minutes and the drizzle slowly grows to a downpour. The change in weather does not effect me at all. My tears seem to show no signs of slowing, as I feel a petite, warm hand lock with mine. My bright red eyes look to my left and see Kari's shimmering, blue eyes gazing back at me. I wipe a tear off of her soft, right cheek and she embraces me tightly. 'I am so sorry, Kael,' she whispers. 'I know,' I reply. We finally let go of one another, but keep our hands locked. 'How did you get here?' I wonder, as our eyes are locked. 'I never mentioned where I was going.' 'He brought me here,' she replies and points behind me. I turn around slowly and see my old friend standing before me. Futeki Jr. has his bald head bowed, as the rain beats down on his black trenchcoat. I lead Kari over to him by the hand and stand before him. I have not spoken to him since that grave day. 'It has been a long time, Naito,' I quietly utter and he nods in agreement. 'Thank you for coming, my brother.' We shake hands firmly. 'Let's go home,' I say to everyone. With Kari holding my hand and my best friend beside me, I slowly leave this place until next year. Fade to black.
