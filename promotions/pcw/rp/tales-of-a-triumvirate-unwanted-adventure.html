Tales Of A Triumvirate – Unwanted Adventure…

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Recently... [Fade In.] The slap of bear feet upon a marble surface rang out through the dark residence like the toll of the mightiest bell. He turned to his left quickly, beads of clinging sweat finally released and set upon the world, and then turned to his right; he wiped his brow. The echo of voices behind him made him turn back. He strained his eyes to see what was going on and the glimmering candlelight revealed several men sprinting towards him. Perhaps though, they hadn’t seen him in the darkness yet? Whatever the answer, he’d know soon enough. “Titu!” a voice hissed from the shadows. He spun on his heels and looked about frantically. He recognized that voice painstakingly. It was Dorn Tracer, the man who’d idiotically convinced him to go on this little exploration. Why he’d agreed, he’d never know. “Quickly, here!” Dorn spouted, keeping his voice slightly hushed. “Cummon! I’m HERE! …No, NOT there. HERE, you dumbass!” The Egyptian, for the briefest of moment, saw a hand beckoning him into the darkness. He followed, knowing Tracer had found some sort of refuge in the chapel’s interior. Quickly, he ran toward the voice and crouched into the shade, Dorn’s grey face coming into view. “I knew this was a bad idea, Astronomer,” he began, “Thanks to YOUR stupidity, we now have half the bloody Vatican after us!” he sighed, “For the Gods of Egypt’s sakes!” Dorn waved a hand dismissively. “Oh, get over it, Egyptian! We were bored; we decided to go for a walk from the hotel; WE decided to blow this place SKYHIGH! You had as much authority in deciding THIS as I did.” Said Dorn matter-of-factly, shrugging. Titu sighed angrily, “Oh, I can’t believe you’re pinning this mess on me, Tracer! I had NOTHING to do with this! And look at us now—” “Shush!” Dorn hissed suddenly, “I hear voices! I think they’ve found us!” A moment of stunned silence passed. Titu’s heart raced. Dorn kept quiet, listening. Several seconds later, Titu frowned with realization. “You just did that to SHUT me UP, didn’t you?!” Dorn shrugged, “You were getting annoying.” Titu opened his mouth to defend himself when quite suddenly the gentle sound of footsteps nearby treaded past. Looking up at the shadowed figure near to them, Tracer made out a white robe and a greying beard. The man’s brow furrowed as several other priests joined his side. “I can’t see them anywhere! I know they came in here. WE saw them, right?” A general bravado of agreement fills the air. “Keep looking, then…they’re here SOMEWHERE, gentlemen. By God, we’ll have their heads for this!” The men walked away, taking up positions all around the hall. Pharaoh paid Dorn a worried glance, and Dorn returned it. Even more hushed than before, Titu raised an eyebrow and said, “What the Duat do we do now, Astronomer?!” Dorn rubbed his chin. Stubble had already begun to grow back since his last shave, and the satisfying brush of skin against the rough surface filled the air about the Triumvirate members. “We HAVE to take these bastards out. It’ll teach them a lesson for shunning our religions. But HOW is a question yet to be answered, mate.” Dorn rotated his torso in a circular motion so he could get a better look at the gathered priests. “Bastards. They’re as cunning as their ‘devil’ himself. Men of God? Ha! They wouldn’t know God if he stuffed a fire cracker up their ass and lit the fuse.” Dorn buried his head in his hands and rubbed his eyes tiresomely. He was deep in thought, as was the Egyptian sat beside him. Pharaoh was looking on at one of the chandeliers hanging high above the hall. Beneath it stood a trio of searching priests. He followed the rope keeping the massive glorified candle up and saw where it led off. Surprisingly, it was only a few feet away from them, but hidden deep in a blanket of shadow. “Dorn,” Tracer mumbled to himself, “Dorn.” Titu repeated. The Astronomer still mumbled to himself. Pharaoh rolled his eyes, “Dorn!” Tracer heard that one. He turned quickly and scowled, “What?!” Without saying a word, Titu pointed towards the priests, then the chandelier, then the rope and then, moving his finger along the thick piece of twine, the place where the chandelier was kept at bay. Dorn grinned cunningly and patted Xansiv on the shoulder, “Good work, Egyptian.” He said, beaming. His face speedily went pale, his joyous expression faltering. “But HOW in the name of En’Flarim are we going to get across there without them seein’ us?!” He sighed, realizing the plan was a sure fail. Pharaoh, however, wasn’t so overly doubtful and leapt to his feet. Sticking to the darkness, the slimy Egyptian moved along the back wall towards the rope. Dorn watched on, jaw dropped, as Titu reached out, untied the slipknot and grinned at the Astronomer. He mouthed out the words, ‘Get ready to run’. Tracer nodded. Pharaoh let go of the twine and, as though in slow-motion, the rope slipped out of the metal hoop previously housing it, slipped up through the great wooden rafters and met the roof. By this time, the chandelier had already fallen from its home high above the marble floor and loomed over the three now chatting priests. CRASH! It was an immense sound. It ringed out for minutes after the event happened. Upon impact, the other priests in the room diverted their attentions from their searching to the shattered glass lying motionless upon the floor. The metal ring that housed the glinting pieces of glass was bent in two, and beneath it all, three unconscious priests were sprawled. Without a moment too soon, Titu and Dorn sprinted past their collective nemeses and out the room’s door. They breathed. It was all they could do for the moment. And it wasn’t long at all before they heard the patter of feet behind them. “They’re gonna regret this!” Dorn seethed. Titu grabbed his arm, setting off on a run. The corridor ahead was huge and divided into three other corridors. “Just come on, Dorn. We have to get out of here!” He grinded to a halt as he came to the three passages, “By the Gods! Which way do we go now?” Dorn shrugged. “Don’t look at me, I don’t have that good a memory!” Pharaoh threw up his arms in defeat. “Well, this is just BRILLIANT, isn’t it? Any minute now, we’re going to be drawn and quartered by those bloody Neanderthals, and for what? Because YOU dragged us in here on some vigilante mission to blow this place to Khet! By the GODS, I wonder WHY I EVER teamed up with you sometimes!” Dorn scowled. “I THOUGHT WE JOINED FORCES TO DESTROY THIS SORT OF THING! Obviously I was WRONG!” The door behind them burst open and four priests, led by the one who spoke earlier, sprinted out into the narrow passage. Dorn looked at Pharaoh and Titu looked back at him. Together they ran up and through one of the three corridors. They didn’t know what awaited them, but they had no choice. It as either this, or they faced four, perhaps five – since they didn’t have time to thoroughly check – extremely angry Christians. “Where the DUAT are we?” Pharaoh said as they came to a fairly large room consumed solely by a huge bell. Dorn sighed, shaking his head. “By the stars, Titu. What does it LOOK like it?” The Egyptian remained blank-faced. “We’re in the bell tower, idiot.” Dorn frowned, “Oh, and by the way, just have a quick look over there, would you?” Dorn pointed towards a ledge overlooking the church grounds. Titu took in a deep breath as he stared fright-stricken down at the gravel path FAR below. He looked back at Tracer who nodded slowly. “That’s right, Titu, YOU brought us up to the BELL TOWER! There’s NO WAY DOWN but the stairway we JUST ran up. And guess who’s inevitably following us, you dumb fuckin’ bastard?! THE PRIESTS!” Titu frowned scornfully, “HOW DARE YOU BLAME THIS ON ME! T’was you who brought us HERE in the bloody first place!” Dorn waved off the comment, “That’s beside the point—” “That IS the GODSDAMNED point!” Pharaoh bellowed, his voice echoing loudly over the valley surrounding them. Thunder struck suddenly, illuminating the Egyptian’s angered face, and beads of rain began to fall about the grounds below. For the briefest moment, Dorn looked frightened. Then, as the rain pattered gently on the roof above, the pair looked themselves over with annoyance and utter frustration. The tower had a roof, but it had seldom been fixed from previous storms; that was obvious by the sudden amount of water soaking the pair. Pharaoh shook his head and fell to his knees, looking up into the sky. “Foul Gods, why DO you MOCK me so?!” Dorn folded his arms and leant up against the bell, “Because they suck ass?” he sulked. “To Khet with you Astrologer.” Several minutes passed by slowly. “Infidels!” a voice boomed. Pharaoh got back to his feet when the four priests chasing them entered the bell tower. Dorn looked up as one lunged at him and threw a fist almost instantly. But Dorn caught his arm out of instinct and kicked him in the stomach, doubling the man over. At the other side of the tower, one priest ran at Titu, but the clearly frustrated Xansiv caught him in mid-run and, using the man’s own momentum, tossed him OVER THE TOWER’S EDGE! The man screamed out as he plummeted to the ground below. “Murderer!” cried one of the Christians and charged the Pharaoh. But Xansiv back-dropped him onto the concrete where he remained clutching at his back. At the other side of the soaking area, a fairly shocked Dorn Tracer heaved his opponent up to his feet and drove him headfirst into the bell itself. BOOM! The huge steel object rang out ferociously, rendering everyone in the tower to clutch at his ears. As the sound died away, the leading priest was next to challenge The Pharaoh, and in his hand he held a long, thin 2x4. “You’ll pay for trespassing upon these sacred grounds!” he said, gritting his teeth. “But I thought this was God’s house and anyone could come to ask for forgiveness?” Dorn ceased his movements and looked over at Titu with bewilderment. Equally as stunned, the priest loosened his stance and beamed, “But I thought you were… sadists?” Pharaoh chuckled lightly, “Not really,” his expression fell, “We’re much worse.” And with one circular swoop, Titu grabbed the pole from the priest’s hand and clubbed him across the skull with it. The man fell to his knees with confusion and then passed out. “I was getting’ worried for a second there, Titu. I thought you’d turned to the dark side!” bellowed Dorn, an element of thankfulness lingering in his voice. Titu tossed the crude weapon to the floor, ran a hand through his wet hair and began to walk away, “Come, Astrologer, let’s get the Duat out of here.” Titu moved out of the room and down the corridor, Dorn following closely at his heels. “As you say, powerfreak.” Dorn mumbled as they pair attempted to find an exit. “Do we have time to blow this place to the realm of the Gods, Egyptian?” Titu shrugged, “I believe so, Tracer. I believe so.” [Fade Out.]
