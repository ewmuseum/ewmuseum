History: Totally Tubular Tommy

19 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/19/03 It was way back at Alliances when Skylar Green, then known as The End (as some continue to call him today -- in fact, he has yet to get a different locker room sticker or a JumboTron image that says otherwise) helped to retire Heavy Metal Harry. As part of the Celestials, he and Akiyama dominated their cage of the pay-per-view and eliminated many individuals. Heavy Metal Harry was one of them, felled at The End's hand from his Never Again finisher, at that time a Swinging Inverted Russian Legsweep. This, of course, was preceded by a combination spinning leg sweep and running lariat by The End and Akiyama. The attack itself wouldn't have been so bad, save for the way that Heavy Metal Harry's body contorted as it fell, and how Akiyama followed through with the move. While Harry was rotated at the head and crashed into the the mat by his neck, Akiyama fell with the lariat, landing his shoulder across the forehead of Heavy Metal Harry. His resulting injuries included a broken back and a concussion, amongst other things. That did it. It was a single series of moves, ended with an attack from The End that jerked Heavy Metal Harry's neck in such a way to finish his career. Heavy Metal Harry had had the honor of being defeated by the two most dominant wrestlers in Cage 3 of the Alliances melee. Totally Tubular Tommy was beaten in a more humiliating fashion. He had been one of the first of his team, one of the first in the cage, to be defeated. He suffered a submission loss at the hands of PCW's resident cruiserweight jobber at the time, Eddie Mandrake! In a way, Totally Tubular Tommy came out the worse for that match. He had a lot to make up for, getting defeated by a man whose only claim to fame was winning a 'feud' with Scorpion, of all people. It had been a nice run, Cowabunga Connection coming to PCW at a time when OSW was starting up and, it being the 'new thing' at the time, everyone and their mother wanted in on it. Cowabunga Connection came up from NTICW. They weren't a well-known tag team, though Totally Tubular Tommy was a PWA standout. While OSW was having the Most Extreme Team tournament, teams like Samburo, Misplaced, Suicidal Tendencies, Fear Factory, the Refugees, and the International Terrorists. They were a bane to the PCW tag team division. For every useful tag team that came in, there were 3 worthless teams. Teams that came in and didn't do anything. Teams that came in and were gone within the month. PCW barely even had a tag team scene during the first half of last year. The teams were tandems, and tandems that never stayed together longer anyway. Though Cowabunga Connection never reached the tag team titles, they were always considered dark horses. It seemed as though they aspired just enough not to get fired. They considered PCW to be the best place to work, and they wanted to stay. There may have been a Pretty Cool Guys, Politically Incorrect, Akiyama, P.W.A., or even a Duzza and Mark Makonie pairing that would reach the tag team titles before Cowabunga Connection, but they never received the label that many many MANY other teams had been branded. Washouts. They could've won the tag team titles whenever they wanted to. But it seemed as though they didn't want to. Something about their mindsets at the time. They weren't championship-aspiring wrestlers, just wrestlers that were in the business for the fun of it. There was nothing wrong with it, one should have fun with what they do for a living, lest they have no other good reason for doing it. But still, for Cowabunga Connection, it seemed as though there was just one missed opportunity after another. They could win, and win big. But when the big win was most needed and most important for them, someone else was always able to snatch up the victory. Someone with more drive, more focus; more desire to win. Such was the reason for what happened at PCW Alliances 2K2. Akiyama and The End were not tag team wrestlers, and they were not teamed with tag team wrestlers (as Cowabunga Connection were, pinning their hopes for winning on another team that hadn't lived up to its potential -- OSW MET participants Redemption Song). They were singles wrestlers. They had teamed together because they had their own individual goals and aspirations, a clear separation of who was to get what, and they were determined to get it. The Alliances match is hardly a place to just have fun, as there is an entire fed's worth of wrestlers in one match that would injure or even kill you if they could get to the World Title. That is what Cowabunga Connection did not realize going into the match, and that is what ultimately cost Heavy Metal Harry his career at the hands of Akiyama and The End. Totally Tubular Tommy is on his own now, and doing well for himself. Seeing his brother being forced to retire due to injuries without actually accomplishing anything made Tommy get serious, though outwardly it doesn't seem so. He has aspirations, he has a drive now. He has a reason to do well. He wants to have fun, but he also wants to have his time in this sport, this business, be worth something. If he gets injured, made to sit at home for a few weeks or a month. If Tommy is also beaten into retirement (though to be fair to The End and Akiyama, permanently injuring Heavy Metal Harry was never their intention; they weren't going to hold back, however, in a match where they could gain title shots at belts that were for whatever reason inaccessible to them otherwise), he wants to be able to look at his wall and see title belts hanging from a glass-covered disaply case. He wants something to show for his time in wrestling. He wants what he got out of the sport to be worth what he put into it, and, if it so happens, what he sacrificed for the business. In a way, it could be said that Akiyama and Skylar Green are directly responsible with Totally Tubular Tommy's current winning ways. Tommy had never tasted a big match victory, let alone championship belts, in tag team or singles competition until the Cowabunga Connection tag team with his younger brother was forced to dissolve, and he had to strike out on his own. Sure, he didn't win the Versailles match, but Chef determined who would win that. In the Treble Jeopardy match, it was his time to shine. Though it was through underhanded methods, he had surpassed six other Continental Title contenders and won the Continental Championship. It was an impressive victory, which in a way was not marred by he and his brother cheating for him to win the Continental championship. It was impressive, and significant, insofar that Tommy had realized, for perhaps the first time in his career, that he wanted to win and that he was going to win. No matter what the cost. No matter what the method. He was going to win. And like it or not, he has ever since. Whether holding the Continental or now the World Title, Totally Tubular Tommy has repelled contender after contender, and beaten the likes of Akiyama, Clyde, The Pharaoh, Skylar Green, Rent-A-Hero, Venneman Pendragon, and even Xavier Kannon, to do so. Totally Tubular Tommy is the king of the hill. And he will fight to stay there. So it comes to two all-important matches for him to prove now, more than ever, that he is at the top of the ladder and that he won't be going anywhere for a long while to come. On Tuesday Turmoil, he will team with Heavy Metal Harry for the first time in seven months, taking on The Pharaoh and his wife Isiy. All this stems from the Friday Fusion main event two weeks ago, in which during the triple main event (more specifically, Totally Tubular Tommy's matches against The Pharaoh and Skylar Green), Heavy Metal Harry and Isiy Xansiv interfered on behalf of their associative wrestlers against their respective opponents. VP-S signed the match, citing that the managers won't be booked as long as they don't get physically involved. Totally Tubular Tommy has been working to get his brother back in wrestling shape, though with Harry still suffering from his back injuries, how much he will be able to physically compete is a question to be concerned with, indeed. He didn't seem to show much sign of physical handicap when he and Totally Tubular Tommy attacked The Pharaoh in the No DQ submission match between both wrestlers two Fusions past. He even managed some solo offense on The Pharaoh, surprising the PCW veteran. Cowabunga Connection has the edge in this match as they've more experience in tag team situations. Not only that, but now the brothers have something that they never had: a burning desire to succeed. Pharaoh will try to win, and try to protect Isiy; Isiy will try to prove that she can handle herself, but at the same time try not to get seriously injured. Really, it is Cowabunga Connection's match to lose. On to Sunday, at PCW Supremacy. Totally Tubular Tommy must defend his PCW World Title against The Pharaoh and Skylar Green. He has already faced these two men before. He's won and lost against them both, but when it comes to title matches where Tommy is defending, Tommy remains undefeated against both of them. That is where the change is. Tommy wins the big matches now. He stakes his claim and fights for success, and has fun while doing it (rather than having fun at the cost of success). While The Pharaoh and Skylar are caught up in their so-called 'friendly rivalry,' Totally Tubular Tommy is but a third wheel. He wouldn't be involved at all, if not for inserting himself into the rivalry. Indeed, in the past few weeks, he seems to have taken priority for The Pharaoh over Skylar Green. Still, Tommy remains an outside factor in a match that really should only be between The Pharaoh and Skylar Green. That is at once his advantage and his disadvantage. Totally Tubular Tommy can take advantage of the fact that he is the 'outside man' in this match, and stand aside while Skylar and Pharaoh drub each other senseless, picking his time to jump in and cleanly take a victory out from under their noses. At the other end of the match-possibility spectrum, one (especially Tommy himself) must take into account that Skylar Green and The Pharaoh have already demonstrated that they can temporarily get along, if only to try to get Totally Tubular Tommy out of the picture that they might have their own match, or at least their own time within another match, to handle their own score that they must settle. Really, there are a multitude of ways that the World Title match could play out at Supremacy. And the only one who really has anything to lose is Totally Tubular Tommy. The Pharaoh will have the same matches as Totally Tubular Tommy to prepare for leading up to the pay-per-view. For the next week, their fates are intertwined. As for Skylar, he has the somewhat lighter task of dealing with a match against Pharaoh's Triumvirate partner, Dorn Tracer. Tracer has quickly been on the rise in PCW, but this is still a match that most are calling for Skylar to win hands down. So at the pay-per-view, the three titans of PCW will meet in a triple threat match. It will come down to this at the pay-per-view: will Tommy's desire to keep what he has outweigh Skylar and Pharaoh's desire to take it, and will it outweigh Skylar and Pharaoh's desire to get the better of each other? This is truly a hard match to call looking at it on paper; it will be Tommy's toughest World Title defense to date. If he loses, he'll have to go without being the World Champion for as long as it takes him to procure a rematch, something that he may not be able to handle well. If he wins, he'll unarguably solidify himself as PCW's most dominant champion for months to come. The Pharaoh and Skylar Green each have their own thoughts, consequences, and histories going into this match. This is Totally Tubular Tommy's story.
