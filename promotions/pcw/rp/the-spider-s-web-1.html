Date: January blah Back in the apartment and I’ve lost all feeling. The feeling of comfort is replaced by the feeling of loss. As if Davey’s humping isn’t bad enough, Jane’s Addiction doesn’t make things any better. As if the rattling engines of cars aren’t bad enough, Bach’s blah, blah concerto in a blah minor doesn’t improve the situation. The sordid stench of a dead mouse, dead rat, or other dead vermin seeps into the room through the small crack at the bottom of the bathroom door. From the room next door comes the squeaking of springs, up and down, up and down, and I know Davey’s not alone. This is what, these days, is a ‘best’ friend. From the room next to the bathroom, while I’m drinking my second cup of coffee, comes the sound of moaning. Either Mary Shelley’s Frankenstein is alive and has come to take Davey, or Davey is ‘enjoying’ himself. Where Davey works, the Milano restaurant three blocks down, he doesn’t work for money. He doesn’t work to feel any better. To pay off any money he owes me. To pay off what he owes dealers and/ or pimps around town. He works there for the younger girls. He works there for the Yellow’s, black’s, white’s, Hispanic’s, Italians, Spaniards. Can’t work there if you have AIDS or Hepititis C. Skrylosis. Payremagine. They’ve said it themselves: FUCK THESE GIRLS AND YOU WILL NOT GET ANY SEXUALLY TRANSMITTED DISEASE No risk. It’s a guarantee. Davey looks into getting me a job there. Nope, I’m too young. My hair’s too long. My hair’s too short. But all the while I know it’s because of my reputation. Yes, I do have a tendency to fuck around a ‘little’. See: Carla Voncelli Davey’s head chef and he doesn’t know how to make spaghetti bolognaise, topped off with a hint of garlic. Don’t overdo the mint, this ruins the dinner. Davey doesn’t know any of this. Once I tried to get a job at Subway. See: All the above reasons. But there I was ‘too old’. The assistant manager said I was ‘too blonde’. I tell him my hair is brown. He says, “Shhhhh”. “I’m sure you’ll find that job that’s right for you.... It’s company policy not to hire anyone with an.... Unsuitable hairdo.... Where you’ll work with other blondes.... Hey, you can even exchange bottles of peroxide with them....” I say, you’re an asshole. And he asks me to leave. All the while, from inside the kitchen, Tralam’s blue eyes gaze out at me menacingly, her ear to ear grin in full tact. On my third coffee and Davey has been muted. No more springing. Groaning. Thrusting. Grinding. Moulding. Up’s. Down’s. Just silence. Silence for a moment, then from somewhere below a door slams. From somewhere beside that someone screams. From somewhere next door comes the raw slap of flesh. These rehabilitated noise-ophobics. Minutes pass and the door of Davey’s room slams open. Through the open door comes an oozing red liquid. Blood. And inside Davey’s gripping his neck, both hand wrapped around. With one palm rubbing along the red shag carpet he pants heavily. This is the part where I should have done something. But I just stand and watch as Davey manages to say, “Mikey” And it hits me like a thunderbolt.
