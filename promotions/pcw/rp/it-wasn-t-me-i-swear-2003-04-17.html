It wasn't me... I SWEAR!

17 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-17-03 RP 1 Spring Fling “I didn’t pull the trigger. He just barged in my home. I threatened him with the shotgun. He tried to get it out of my hand and I pushed him. When he fell the gun went off and he went out.” Teresa looks up at the standing officer with a straight face. Her story wasn’t entirely true but who would know what really happened? The idiot reporter is lying in his own blood on her kitchen floor. If Mama Tomas didn’t call the cops to check on Teresa, no one would really know the guy was dead. Well, not yet anyway. The officer places his hands on his hips. A doughnut belly dunlaps over his belt buckle and he smells like cheap cologne. His gray hair makes him look fifty something, but his smooth face says mid thirties. A group of medics are preparing to haul off the body and Teresa has yet to flinch. “You do realize this situation isn’t looking good on your part. So far it’s your word against the victim and we all know he’s not speaking. How do we know you didn’t shoot him.” Teresa bites her lower lip. Some fat oversized cop doesn’t intimidate her in the least, but she doesn’t have any plans on staying in a jail cell for the night. Her frustrated voice does little to hide her anger in the officer. “If told you once, I told you a dozen times, I DIDN’T PULL THE FREAKING TRIGGER! You ain’t got shit on me so why don’t you clean up this mess and go hit Krispy Kreame or something. I told you the truth and you can’t prove otherwise.” The zipper of the body bag zips and the medics carry the annoying reporter named Bobbie out of Teresa’s cabin. Still yet, she shows no emotion of this situation. The officer watches Teresa suspiciously. His badge says ‘Earl’. Teresa only sighs wishing everyone would leave. She is also planning what she will tell her lovely mother the next time she speaks to the old woman. Bacon, Teresa’s Jack Russell pads to her side and sits upright. She looks up at the fat cop and cocks her head as if she is trying to figure him out. Teresa pats the dog’s head. All she would have to do is say the word and that half-sized puppy would be chasing ‘Earl’ out of the house. “Anything else Earl? Doughnuts? Coffee?” A grim smirk spreads over Teresa’s face, which shows she’s either fearless or just plain stupid. The two hundred plus pounder crosses his arms over his chest and nods. “In fact, I do Miss Tomas. Do you have a permit to own so many guns? I mean, look at your living room?” Teresa turns around. On her living room wall is a huge rack holding five shotguns and an eight-foot wooden gun cabinet rests in the corner holding more shotguns and a few pistols. The glass door hides nothing what is held behind it. She adjusts in her seat. “Well officer…”
