Warcraft: Tides of Lexian [Part Two]

11 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 11, 2002 This ain’t Never Never Land “ZUG ZUG!” I drew backwards as I watched a four foot, green peon walk past me, carrying on his back a load of very heavy wood. His forehead had very long curves on it, and his hair was graying all around the edges. He walked with a slump, which I originally thought was just from the wood he was carrying, but as I looked around, I noticed all of the little peons had the slump. Perhaps it was genetic. After all, they were not warriors. The building in front of me billowed smoke from its chimney tops. It was made of entirely brick and mortar, fairly crude looking, with the roof looking like it would topple over at any second. In front of the building was an iron forge, which a seven foot orc hammering away at what appeared to be a sword. The hammer he was wielding was bigger than my entire lower body, and it came thundering down on the thin piece of metal. I gritted my teeth as I listened to the metal clang against metal, and I watched as the sweat poured down from his head. He glanced up at me and gave me a horrid look. I don’t think he liked me. I looked over to my left where I huge forest was growing. At least, where it used to be growing. A huge path was being cut down through the dense foliage, and in the distance I could hear the faint sound of axes pounding against the tough bark of the trees. Just as I looked over again, I heard the sound of a person falling to the ground. I looked to my right and saw one of the peons sprawled against the ground, his lumber covering him, and his pick axe lying to his side. “YOU THERE!” Startled, I nearly jumped in the air as I watched a towering orcish grunt make his way towards us. He wore a heavy, metal plated armor, with an axe clanging along his right side against his leg. His hair was swept into a tight pony tail and came all the way to his waist. He picked up the peon with relative ease and brought his face up to his own, leaving the peon’s legs dangling in the air. “What do you think you’re doing?” The grunt demanded. His breath huffed and puffed in the air, and even I could smell it’s stench from where I was standing. “I...I...I…” “You what?” He asked again. “Speak to me with respect or I swear I will kill you where you stand.” “I was carrying the lumber back,” The peon finally said. “But it was so heavy, that it nearly collapsed over my head!” The grunt threw the peon back down to the ground and shook his head in disgust. “I cannot believe your weakness. Here we are, embroiled in a war with the humans, and you’re complaining about carrying wood? What about the warriors? We lay our lives on the line so that you peons can live a life of relative solitude, and this is the thanks we get?” “I’m…I’m sorry…” The peon stammered, as he hurriedly gathered his wood back up. As he did, the grunt bent over and picked up the pick axe that the peon dropped. “A warrior never drops his weapon in battle,” The grunt said, still staring at the axe. “Keep it well guarding, should you ever need it.” The peon nodded and scampered away towards a towering town hall that looked like a miniature castle. The grunt turned his attention to me, grinning and licking his chops. He had about a foot on me, and he was wielding an axe. Odds look good. “And who are you?” The grunt asked. I took a step backwards. “My name’s Tom Lexian…uhm, who are you?” “My name’s Cho’Gor, second in command in Horal,” He replied. “State your business in this base.” I took a deep breath. “It’s a long story. I’m not from this world…” He chuckled and crossed his arms. “That’s what I thought. For a normal human, if I saw you here, I would have killed you without thinking twice. But you didn’t look like most humans. You looked different. And that’s when I remembered what the Ogre-Magi had told me earlier…” “Is this him?” A lumbering Ogre-Magi came walking towards us. It looked old, if a seven foot ogre can really look old, and it had a limp as it walked. It used a cane to stabilize itself while it was walking. If you don’t know what a two headed ogre looks like, let me explain it to you. An ogre consists of one body, two heads, each with their own personality. In your normal, dumb as rocks, run of the mill ogre, these guys constantly fight and are always trying to gain an advantage over the other. This kind of balances out the fact that they possess the fire power to completely destroy a human being. Of course, once they become an Ogre-Magi, the entire story changes. All of a sudden, they’re educated. It’s like they got a college degree and now they’re speaking intelligibly. One of the heads looked at me with a boyish grin. “He looks young.” The other head grimaced. “He is young, but is he the one? What is your name human? And why have you come here?” “Tom Lexian,” I said. “And I don’t know why I’m here. All I know is that I want to go back to where I was.” “He’s brash,” The head said. “Take him inside. We will talk to Horal and discuss what to do with him.” “You guys aren’t taking me anywhere…” I said, taking another step backwards. As I did, I hit a wall. I turned around and noticed a troll standing behind me. He was holding an axe in each head and growled at me. “It would be wise if you followed our directions, human,” Cho’Gor said. “You don’t want to see a troll when he’s upset.” I nodded and followed Cho’Gor into the town base, while the ogre-magi continued to talk amongst themselves. We approach a high wall that had more trolls patrolling the upper perimeter. They were like archers, if they were anything like the video game, and they could assault someone from a distance. The huge doors opened up and we walked through. Immediately, I gushed at the sheer mass amounts of warriors surrounding us. There were four barracks, each resounding with the sounds of armor clashing, sword clanging, and axes hitting the walls. Off to the side were the peons working hard to move precious metals and resources. To the far right I could see a few peons building a new farm. They were having a hard time corralling the pigs in, a supposed staple of orcish horde dinners. “Ha ha!” A grunt bellowed. “Get those pigs into their pens, you worthless peons, or we’ll have YOU for dinner!” “I guess you guys don’t have much respect for the little guy, huh?” I murmured under my breath. “Peons are just as important as the warriors bellowing at them,” Cho’Gor replied. “Just because we ridicule them doesn’t mean that we don’t appreciate how they keep the Horde running.” “You might want to tell them that every once in awhile,” I said, watching the peons hurriedly continue construction. “People work better when they know they’re being appreciated. Otherwise you might have a full scale mutiny on your hands.” “Ha!” The troll laughed. “I’d like to see one of these peons rise up against me. I once met a peon that spoke to me in a tone I didn’t much appreciate. He had an axe lodged in his throat a few seconds later.” “I guess you never learned the benefits of talking things out.” The troll huffed. “Talking gets in the way. An axe speeds things up.” I brushed off the comment, but Cho’Gor could see that I was still a bit upset. “Lighten up, human. Cero has a way of coming off to others as uncaring, but he cares quite a bit about the Horde and it’s politics. You humans are too concerned about feelings and other sentiments that do not matter on the battle field. That is why you lose.” “You would be surprised how resilient the human race can be,” I replied. “I have been,” Cho’Gor remarked. “But it is lucky for you that you are not from this world, otherwise you would be on the wrong side. Take heart, though. You will not perish.” “Thanks.” I didn’t know if I was supposed to thank him or plead for my life. “So, what do you guys do for fun around here?” I asked, trying to keep the conversation lively. Hard to do with towering monsters around you. “With the war, there has been little leisure time here,” Cho’Gor replied. “The humans have been trying to push us out of their territory for quite some time now, attempting to drive us past the Great Sea again. Apparently their mages have built up their strength once more, so we can no longer strike in great numbers.” “Their cowardly spells will be their undoing,” Cero remarked. “I want to go one on one with a mage, and stab him right through the heart with my axe.” “Don’t be a fool,” Cho’Gor snapped back. “He would eat you alive and you’d be snacking on a lightning bolt.” “I can take a lightning bolt.” “Try seven,” Cho’Gor said. “You know that they can fire repeated blows before you even get near him, and by that time you would be fried.” “We’ll see…” “You guys don’t have magic or what?” I asked, feeling a little stupid. I knew exactly what they had, but I just wanted to see how similar this world was to the game. “Our death knights are more than capable of handling themselves in battle,” Cho’Gor said. “But they pale in comparison to the magic that the mages wield. They’ve been studying the magic incantations for centuries, while our knights possess a darker power. We are quite limited in matching them spell for spell.” “But what we lack in light shows and cheap tricks, we make up for in brute strength,” Cero said proudly. “The humans will find that out soon enough, won’t they?” “What do you mean?” I asked. “Tell the human, Cho’Gor,” Cero said, a sinister grin on his face. Cho’Gor looked at me. “This time tomorrow, human, you will be knee deep in blood and gore, for we will be taking the town two miles from here. And rest assured, there will be no one left alive to tell the story.”
