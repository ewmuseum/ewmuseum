The Peril of Night Part 4

7 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07/05/02 She stood there waiting, her decision was made and now two moons had passed since her last meeting with the shrouded figure. She stood upon the grey and dampened pavement, with water trickling into the gutter from the slanting shadow of the curb. Anna’s eyes are flickering insanely, as the streetlight above her also flexes, as if there were some sort of interference with both body and soul. She knew it was coming for her but she did not know when, it seemed to be about the right time for another contact to be made, yet she could not feel the strength of its presence. The time was after midnight and the streets were an emptiness that seemed to hollow the depths of Anna’s soul away, for she began to feel alone and a cold feeling overpasses her. The feeling of isolation, but she for once felt positively about something, for this being could raise the barriers of her lonely lament and drown her sorrows within an ocean of familiar faces that were just like she was, dark, destroyed, tormented and mentally wrecked. For so long she had waited but at last her desires would have paid off and finally she would have someone to call her acquaintance. The simple though of it all invigorated her, although at the same time as her anticipation and anxiety were peaking, so was her concern and fear for what might happen if she had been deceived. Tapping her foot along to the almost regular flickering of the street right, she was clearly distraught and disturbed about something. However, she managed to keep her cool, as she simply stood there waiting for her world to change forever. Anna raised her arm and drew back the blue silken veils that covered her wrists; she then began to chew with savagery upon her nails. The pressure began to rise up inside of her like a surge of lightning crashing into the tree of her willpower and snapping it like a mere twig. She could take it no longer; she began to shake all over. The rhythmic tapping of her foot upon the grey curbstone had now become a raging stomp. The bitting of her nails had become a rabid biting of her wrist, as her eyes began to flicker with even greater speed that before. Then it happened. Anna froze still upon the spot, almost as if a dart of ice had pierced her heart and stopped her from moving; the ice of fear encased her, as it began to become clear. The figure was coming. The fluctuations of the streetlight’s beam became irregular, sparking with tremendous force, as the light hastened, Anna backed away, shielding her eyes and bracing herself for the imminent explosion of it. As she turned her head upward to peer into the light sparks, like a fountain of energy exploded from the heart of the light, fragments of sharp glass were tossed in all directions, a rather large piece glancing Anna’s left shoulder and reeling across the street. She growled in the pain and gritted her teeth, as the light suddenly went out. Anna became filled with terror, as if a parasite had drained the courage from her body she revolved her head hastily looking around her and trying to catch a glimpse of the figure, but it was nowhere. She could feel it’s awesome powers flowing through the very essence of the Oxygen that she inhaled; yet she could not see it. The floor trembled, the rage of the being was obviously only in it’s blossoming stage, as Anna watched the street’s curbstones begin to twist, bend and crack like the tectonic plates of the Earth. Anna was shaken heavily and battered around by the onslaught of tremors and quakes that then caused her to fall. Using no more than her hands and knees to stay upright she growled loudly, trying to keep her head in check, for she feared falling into a confrontation with this darker power. “Stop this! Why are you doing this to me?” She yelled at the top of her voice. The tremors simply continued however and there came no answer to her query. Just then the lamppost that had already had it’s light shattered began to creak. Anna’s eyes grew widely in anticipation of what was about to happen, surely enough as she had foreseen, the metal post began to topple. First the inner electronics were spewed from its core before the curbstone to which it was behind cracked and began to fall apart with the seemingly endless quaking. Then, as the curbstone gave way the lamppost began to fall, the massive post headed for back and was about to hit, when she leapt to her feet and performed a diving forward roll into a pill of shattered glass from the earlier smashed light. She screamed in absolute torment, as like a thousand bees, the glass jabbed into her back and maimed her. Vessels were sliced, her skin was pierced and blood was drawn upon the grey road. She wailed and twisted in torment, as she desperately tried to free herself, but there was too much pain and she was far too weak. “PLEASE I BEG YOU TO STOP HAVE MERCY!” She screamed, as the glass began to dig deeper into her already sliced back, as the glass seemed to seep further into her back a discharged of blood slid down the now upturned pavement. She saw it, pure black in shade it trickled, scented with evil and unspeakable horrors, she grimaced at the sight of it. Yet again there was no reply to her plead, then it hit her, as if the sword of enlightenment had been driven through her heart. She now saw, what she was once too blind to see. She knew that she must take power, for only she could stop this. Against all odds, she tried to fight, slowly attempting to sit up. The shards and spikes still pierced her back, however as if it were no pain or effort at all, Anna gritted her teeth and began to pick the glass out. She scowled and pouted as the pain was hurting her to the extent of living death, yet she persevered and continued, for now the pain was relieved and at last, she was back in control. As the last shard was drawn from her back the blood flow began to stop, she rolled onto her front and slumped to the floor; tired, crippled and weak. She contemplated whether or not she could get up again. With a great heave of every granule of strength and courage that she still held, she pushed herself up and with one final burst she stood upright. She held up her body, one arm sliced and battered, her face and back smothered with blood, standing in a pool of her own bleeding, she simply grimaced. Her hands were graved and sliced, her knees and legs were slashed and her hair was dripping with perspiration and blood. However, despite the physical pain and torment, her heart was still strong, her spirit was alive and like a fire her desire for victory and freedom of her soul remained alive. She would not give in to this tormenting torture, she would fight it. Then from nowhere, once again the being came, as if it had walked up the tapping staircases of hell. It’s glare of terror and hatred locked onto Anna, who still stood anticipating it’s arrival. The being simply raised its hand stomped upon the floor and then the chaos drew to a hault, the twirling helix of insanity that had raged within Anna’s mind in those few moments was now gone, yet it seemed nothing had changed. The creature simply stood there, only paces from Anna, laughing solemnly to itself. Anna’s eyes burned like the raging inferno of her heart, as she glared hatefully at this twisted personified chaos that stood before her. “We thought that you had more sense Anna.” Said the figure in its customary low pitched voice, as it shook its head slowly in casual disbelief. I really thought there was hope to you, I didn’t want it to come to this, but it seems that now it must. The creature simply turns to its side, as Anna breathes heavily listening to every word before replying. “I realized what you want, I am more wise than you think. I will fight for my soul if need be, you shall never get your filthy hands upon it!” Yelled Anna, as she moved into a fighting stance, her eyes glinting with both fear and anxiety, for she knew, as well as he did that she had no chance. She could never win and although she had courage she also held stupidity for now she would put her spirit through an even greater torment. The being simply looks at her in thorough disbelief with its shadowed face, but yet looks pleased at her courage and honour and simply replies to her. “So be it…”
