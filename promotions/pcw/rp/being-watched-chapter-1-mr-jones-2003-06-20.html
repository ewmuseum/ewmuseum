Being Watched - Chapter 1: Mr. Jones

20 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6 - 20 - 03 This coffee is friggin’ cold, Stetson! Sorry, sir! I’ll get you another! Just tend to the computer, Stetson… Aye, sir! The lanky, wily-haired man of young age swivels around in his rotating desk chair and stops himself in front of a huge, brightly lit screen. His entire body is engulfed in light as the huge plasma screen practically irradiates the room with thick beams of white light. Despite the fact that the beams are of pure white, the screen flashes and flickers with a variety of different colors, making for a conglomerate of ROYGBIV. Specifically, it seems as if the various objects and programs displayed upon the huge computer screen are actually motion pictures of different people. There’s no labels, however, nor a clear intent. The man in the blue and white jump suit busily pecks away at a large keyboard, looking up to the screen every now and then to check his progress. “Sir,” He suddenly blurts, still caught up in his flurry of typing, “Subject #28588 seems to have stumbled upon one of our secret beacon areas. The guards tried to sedate him, but he seemed awfully wily. Considered an orange threat. Shall I relay to the Templars?” He then turns for a moment to look at the person to whom he’s speaking. The man has straight, slick black hair and an expensive blue Armani suit with a white undershirt and a rich black tie. His face is completely shaven bar the scruffy goatee and his shoes glitter from basking in the light of the computer screen. He wears a pair of dark shades upon his face and leans against a steel doorsill some 10 feet away from the young man at the computer. He grins and replies, “Don’t bother the uppity killing squad, Stetson. It’s not a dangerous situation and even if he discovers us, we could always expose him to more of the hallucinogenic dreams that brought him to us in the first place. So that’s a big NO to the Templar relay request.” “Affirmative, sir. But the Templars will be expecting a response.” “I don’t give a crap,” The man retorts with a laugh deep in his throat, “Those psychos would get their panties in a bunch over a Catholic telling them to eat wafers. Just pay them no heed. Tell them that Mr. Jones has declared the situation a nix and to deal with it no longer.” Stetson nods and begins typing away. Several more little dialogue boxes appear on the screen, but Stetson is racing right through them as he delivers ‘Mr. Jones’’ message. After blazing through the messy maze of computer dialogues, Stetson returns the screen’s display to the huge collection of motion pictures. He leans back in his chair and sighs, “Things are going to plan, sir, but in way, they’re also not.” Mr. Jones snorts, “Don’t be a fool, kid. What could be wrong? We’re not paying you to be paranoid.” “I understand, it’s just my opinion. Like this guy…” Stetson suddenly leans forward in his chair and rolls himself up to the console. He stands up and points to a slightly blurred out picture of a man wearing a long black coat and shades. “His path thus far has been twisted and strange. The agents we stationed haven’t reported on time because he’s been avoiding them. Or so they say. Not that I’m concerned, sir, but what if he were to find out?” The man known only has Mr. Jones takes a sip of coffee from the mug in his hand and walks up beside Stetson. He places a hand firmly on the back of the chair and looks up at the screen with a pondering eye, squinting from being bombarded by bright light. “I believe we have nothing to fear, Stetson. He’s firmly beneath our grasp. Has ‘Gold’ reported back yet?” “Not yet, sir. But we can expect her report soon.” Mr. Jones nods, “Good deal. I understand your concern, Stetson, but there is no need to worry. He has strayed from the path laid before him, true enough, but as they say… all roads lead to Rome. Understand?” Stetson gives a faint nod and continues pecking away at the keys. He enlarges the target picture and focuses in on it, increasing the resolution to make the picture clearer. It seems as though these two are staring at a still image of Dorn Tracer! Mr. Jones grin and turns away from the screen, calmly sipping some more of his coffee. “We haven’t had an interesting subject like this since the conspiracy known as ‘Orion’, eh Stetson?” The computer agent turns and cocks his head in puzzlement, “What do you mean?” “Don’t play stupid, Stetson,” Mr. Jones barks with a hint of laughter in his voice, “You know perfectly well what I mean. We exploded that Citadel in Utah and called it an act of En’Flarim. Due to a lowering amount of faith, we had to build a hero for the disciples to love. Thus, a lowly man we picked up off the street who faintly believed in Astrology was made into a hero for the ages. The ‘Orion’ scandal, you idiot!” Suddenly, a flash of realization crosses Stetson’s face and he chuckles, “Oh yeah. That was brilliant. Too bad the damned Runners countered it with their version of En’Flarim. It’s almost like a game of chess.” “Good call. The Runners haven’t been a problem recently, though. Our targets are still ignorant to their situations. Especially Dorn. We’ve lead him to such wonderful things and he’s still wrapped up in his thoughts that En’Flarim guided him. If only he knew…” Stetson nods, “If only he knew…” [End.]
