There goes Kano shooting his mouth off again

4 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/4/2002 *The scene opens up on the O'Reilly Factor, the highest rated show on the Fox News Channel. The show has just come back from commercial, and the all the camera shows right now is the incomparable Bill O'Reilly sitting at his desk as he begins his speel on another topic.* Bill O'Reilly: Welcome back, if there has been one thing in the past ten years on television that has exploded in popularity and forced its way into pop culture, it would be professional wrestling. My next guess is in Philadelphia right now and is joining us via closed circuit television. He is currently in the huge wrestling organization known as Primetime Championship Wrestling. He is a multi-time champion and is truly a living legend in the wrestling industry. He is Jon Kano. Mr. Kano, are you there? *Suddenly the screen splits in two, with Bill O'Reilly on the left and Jon Kano, dressed in his traditional PCW propaganda, is on the right.* Kano: Yes, Bill, I am here. How are you this evening? Bill: I am fine, how are you? Kano: I am just gearing up for a match of mine. Bill: Now over the last few years professional wrestling has been taking a lot of heat for young children performing dangerous moves on one another. Kano: Well I think we take a lot of heat for stuff that isn't our fault. We put disclaimers on all of our shows. And where the hell are the parents? If I were to walk in and see my child watching professional wrestling, I would ask them what if they thought it was real or not. Then I would explain that professional wrestlers actually spend years to get their craft perfected. I spent nine months working every day in a wrestling school, then I wrestled in the Orient for two and a half years until I perfected my craft. And even as good as I am, I still make mistakes. Its easy to slip in the ring on a sweat spot or have a rope break. You could have a miscommunication with whomever you are wrestling and both of you could end up laying in a hospital bed at the end of the night. Children should realize that we are entertainers and we spend an amazing amount of time learning to do those moves so that whomever we do those moves on doesn't get hurt badly. Bill: So wrestling is fake? Kano: I didn't say that. Wrestling is very real. We have bumps, bruises, we break bones, and we go through emotional hell. I have broken my arm, broken two vertabra in my back, and had countless concussions. I have wife that I hardly get to see. I am on the road 250 days a year. And I really resent when some fat slob at a bar comes up to me and tells me that what I do for a living is fake. I tell that disgusting fatbody that he wouldn't be able to last thirty seconds in the ring with me. I can entertain mass crowds of people, but all it takes on any given move is just a slight twist of my wrist or something to that effect and I can make that move extremely painful. I hate the way professional wrestling is looked upon, because it is a horrible misconception of our industry. Bill: Do you think wrestling should take any responsibility when children hurt each other using moves seen on television? Kano: Hell no. Like I said before, we put disclaimers on our shows. If children are stupid enough to try and say attempt to execute a Kanoplex on another child, I hope those two children paralyze each other. Bill: Don't you think that is a little rash? Kano: No. I am sick and tired of listening to everyone blame our brand of sports entertainment for an idiot child trying to do a move like a Kanoplex. Bill: For our viewers, what exactly is a Kanoplex? Kano: Well, play my clip. *A scene of Jon Kano executing a Kanoplex on an unspecified opponent is played.* Kano: Now as you see, it takes a tremendous amount of skill, power, and speed to execute that move correctly. Bill: Well, Mr. Kano, we are almost out of time, I will let you rant a little for your match tonight before we move onto our next segment. Kano: Thank you. Tonight, I embark on a mission that I will probably not be able to return from under my own power. I step into the ring with three men who would love to tear me limb from limb. But I will still step into the ring. My music will play, the crowd will cheer, I will walk down the aisle, I will step into the ring, and I will fight to survive. I don't care if I win, because I know I probably won't, but I will survive to fight another day. I'll hit what ever is moving within that ring. If you step into that ring with me tonight, you will probably knock me down, but I will get back up and I will fight you again and again and again until I can fight no more. I will not quit, I will not because I cannot. So tonight I am going to get my ass kicked by three men, and I will probably end up in the hospital by the end of the night. But if I do end up in the hospital, you can bet your last dollar that I will take either Mark Makonie, The Chef, or Corey Williams with me. And I will be back on Fusion, raising hell with my running buddies in the Malice Crew. And why? Because I am what REAL LEGENDS ARE MADE OF. *The scene fades to black as the O'Reilly Factor goes to commercial.*
