Meter Maid Troubles

11 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 11, 2003 A black Saturn comes into view on a semi busy street, pulling up near the sidewalk. With a little maneuvering it is parked by an unoccupied meter, delicately, place itself between two cars, preventing a confrontation with someone due to dinging their car. The engine stops roaring, and out from the driver’s side, Brianna Washington emerges, flipping her hair back as she shuts the door. “Am I forgetting anything?” she asks herself as she takes her black purse from her shoulder and places it on top of the car. She rummages through the interior pulling out her checkbook, and ID. “Everything I need is here and accounted for.” Brianna puts the contents back in her purse and closes it up, walking over onto the sidewalk. She plucks a couple of quarters from her pocket and fills the meter, walking towards the bank. A car full of men pass by and whistle at her in her dark red button up shirt that hangs slightly past her black, well fitted pants. Ignoring the insolence of the ill-mannered men, she continues her walk to the First Star Bank ready to deposit some money. She steps inside the big, well-polished bank and is greeted by the security officer standing alert at the door. “Hello ma’am.” he throws out as she passes by. She can feel his eyes looking her up and down but she keeps cool. “Hello.” she flashes him a kind smile and makes her way towards the roped line, which is pretty long. With every passing minute the line gets shorter and shorter, until Brianna is finally at the top. “Next.” a black female teller yells. Brianna steps forward and walks down to her section. “How may I help you?” the woman asks with a forced smile. “I need to deposit some money into my account.” Brianna places some slips of paper onto the counter and slides it underneath the window. ”I need to see some ID.” replies the woman routinely. Brianna pulls out her ID from the confinements of her purse and slides it under as well. If I can manage to let this money alone for a couple of months, I will have enough to buy some more equipment for my lab. Who knows, I might even expand. I do need more room than what I have available now. Just thinking about it makes my mouth water with anticipation. The scene switches to the outside of the bank, where a meter maid is staring at Brianna’s black Saturn. Brianna exits the bank and sees the woman standing there. She walks over slowly not sure why the person is standing there like that. “May I help you?” “No help yourself. You see this here meter expired exactly fifteen seconds ago and I am going to have to write you a ticket.” “Fifteen seconds? You’re going to write me a ticket for being fifteen seconds short of the meter? That’s ridiculous!” Brianna says incredulously. “What’s ridiculous is you not putting in an extra nickel. That’s ridiculous.” replies the heavyset country sounding woman filling out a ticket to Brianna’s dismay. Brianna leans against her car not willing to fight a meager ticket. That is until she sees a pick up truck pull into the vacated spot behind her. An overweight woman steps out and walks away without feeding the meter. She wobbles towards the bank with her short chunky legs somehow managing to sustain the weight being forced upon them. “Hey May.” She says with restricted breath as she continues on her wobbling ways. This sparks anger in Brianna which causes her to question May, the meter maid. “Did you not see that woman pass up the meter?” “I don’t know what you’re talking about.” May replies matter of factly. “You’ve gotta be kidding me!” May refuses to listen to anything Brianna has to say as she rips the paper from the pad and shoves it towards Brianna. “I refuse to pay this!” “Take it up with a judge.” Brianna stops leaning on the car and takes a step towards May with her hands on her hips. “How are you not going to force that woman to pay for the meter, and penalize me for being a mere fifteen seconds late?” “Miss I don’t like your threatening manner. Take one step closer and I’ll be forced to use my night stick.” The woman reaches behind acting as though she were reaching for the night stick, but Brianna knows she had nothing on her but a pad of paper and pen. “People like you make me sick. You unjustly hurt people and let others just pass on by. I’ll take this ticket but remember what goes around comes around.” Brianna says through gritted teeth as she walks over to the driver’s side of her car. She opens up the door with much vigor ready to get away. “Well how do you think I feel about your kind?” Brianna stops in her tracks looking at the woman with a curious brow. “My kind?” “Yeah, you scrawny folk. You get the best jobs, the best mates, and you don’t have to put up with the criticism us overweight people have to deal with. Life is easy for you and you get all the perks. We on the other hand are expected to just eat and make you feel good.” Brianna stops to soak in all that has been said and it starts to make perfect sense. May must have seen Brianna go into the bank. Instantly she wanted to find a reason to mess with her, and so she was counting down the time for the meter to expire. Then when the overweight woman pulled up she let her go because she was in the same boat as her. “You don’t think I have a tough time? I go to work every week at a place where men don’t think women should be. I get looked over for big matches due to my sex, and size. I don’t get the respect I deserve, yet I still put up with it.” May processes this in her mind and starts to realize Brianna’s plight. “Maybe it was wrong for me to treat you like that, but in all fairness the meter did expire.” “I understand that, and I will pay it, but I hope you learn someday that what you’re doing is wrong. It just puts people into situations such as yourself, in getting treated unfairly. So think about it.” Having spoken her mind, Brianna gets inside of her Saturn and revs up the engine. She takes the ticket and stares at it for several seconds before placing it inside her glove box and pulling out away from the sidewalk and down the street. A ticket…just what I needed when I was stressing over Turmoil. Life just knows how to throw you those curveballs when things are already stressful enough. For Turmoil I am not only teamed with a guy who is having serious issues, but I also have to go against a monster and Violence Jack, the current Continental Champion. I’m going to have to pull out everything I have if I even want to make it out alive. I mean how can I trust Jackson to have a straight head? With all of that stuff he’s going through with Haley, and Makonie, I doubt this match ranks up there. Just another one of those curve balls in a big moment of my life. How great. The scene fades to black as Brianna let’s out a sigh coming to a red light.
