Rude Awakening – Personal – Positive Intentions, Negative Answers

22 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 22, 2002 It was still a bit brisk, with the wind coming in from the north and the sun not being able to stretch across the sky and beam it’s heat down to the surface. Safina walked slower the usually, keeping pace with only her self, as Her and Vilas continued on their way, Safina still gasping about the stories that were almost two decades old. In a way Vilas know he struck a negative cord with her and it was al just a matter of time before she blew up in his face. What was I supposed to do, lie for the rest of my life about something that happened so long ago. I couldn’t just stand back and let all my friends think they know me. It’s wrong to them and it’s wrong to my self. But I sure picked the perfect time to tell her. Her eyes glanced away, looking into windows, or even solid brick so not to look Vilas straight in the eyes and draw attention to her self. But her slow pace and lifeless expression gave away her feelings. She may have been looking for a answer to the moment or a phrase to come back on, but she was left empty with nothing but her own thoughts ringing throughout her head and impairing her judgement. “I had my dreams on my mind Safina,” Vilas whispered softly, making sure not to cause any negative reaction to the situation. “I was just trying to move on with my life and get away from it all.” Safina stopped and looked Vilas straight in the eyes. Her face was still lifeless and her emotions look frail as she stood still, almost like a standing corpse. He could only stand still, feeling guilty for what he had done and what his choices were as a child. But that is what they were and nothing more. Choices made by a child who wanted to survive. I couldn’t have thought my whole life over at the age of eight. To think at that age I had already been abandoned from home, banished from my new home at the temple and was in a war for my own peoples survival, She didn’t understand that I went through more in my short life then she ever did during her time. Was it my fault she didn’t run either? As I see it now, it wasn’t a wrong choice of mine to leave. I did what I had to do, to become someone. To become a competitor, and more important to become a champion of Thailand. “After al these years, and all we have been through, why didn’t you just tell me what was going on,” spoke Safina softly, adding a little expression of sympathy and remorse to her lifeless face. “I would have understand clearer then anyway that you had many problems.” Vilas seems shocked by her composure through this ordeal and quickly made sure he was talking to the same person as he started to. Vilas seemed to be distracted now, not knowing why Safina wasn’t upset made him wonder if she even cared at all. They have been with each other since the beginning and this was no different. But something was different then normal, and something was the same. I could hear the sounds see was making, but the words wear not clear. I spent my whole life being her friend and now she was as close to me as an enemy. It’s odd how one story could rip apart a life long friendship. I wonder how Blassa would take this if I told him. In time he would find out, but unlike Safina he would actually have a reaction. “Why doesn’t this make you anger,” questioned Vilas, looking for more responds then just silence, even though he was famous for giving little answers to questions that took years of planning to ask. “You seem to be taking all these years of lies with an open mind.” Safina started walking again, going to fast and faster till she was almost at a jog, but still she kept her self slow enough for Vilas to keep up. It was almost like she wanted him near, just so she could give back her message any time. Vilas caught up to her and just kept silence as she slowed down to almost a stop, but still kept moving to have an easy escape. “All I have is an open mind. You don’t see the position you put me in do you.” Vilas could only look away, shocked and actually accepting the truth for once. He had left her for dead, and allowed her to fight for his rights. It was now time for Vilas to do the right thing for her. He had came back to confront his fears and now was a better time then any. I knew were I was going now and my life was on track. But it still pains me to think I left my own friends for dead. And now as I look at them they flourish without any hate inside them. Just them alone with there own thoughts and ideas. No free care in the world but their own. “Just answer me something Vilas. Was it worth it?” Vilas thought first before answering, hopping his answer would be enough to give her the right impression. He looked up to the sky, waiting for the answer to fall from the heavens, but to him it would never come. No heaven means no answer, so he was on his own, alone and lost. “If you mean leaving you and Blassa stranded and going after my own path then yes, it was worth it,” responded Vilas, making sure to look Safina in the eyes so see would know everything was true. It’s one of those questions where there is no right or wrong answer. But I still can’t help feel guilty for leaving them and going off on my own. I was gone for so long that everyone thought I was dead. Even now the only person who knows I am alive is Safina. Even Blassa doesn’t know and he has done very well for him self. No I think for now I will just lay low and work out my plains. So far everything is going according to plain. Vilas and Safina started to walk again, now receiving the suns raise and feeling the warmth fill their bodies. Safina was still out of her own body though. She wasn’t ready to here those words, even though they were the truth. Vilas stayed close, ready to answer anything that would pop up. But nothing but silence except for the silent yells of children running passed, and men and women making their way to the markets. It was calmer now then ever. But no distractions mean more time to ponder on the events of the day. Not a good combination for a women who is questioning her ways. “We suffered and died, while you went off on your own little quest. We planned and we fought for are rights as people in this country and you ran off. Tell me where the justice is in that Vilas. Tell me I should forgive you.” She had no reason to forgive me, but I did nothing wrong. I was useless anyway. I was mentally dead in the war and there was no way I would come back. I didn’t enjoy dwelling on the past, but it’s a part of who I am now. I can’t deny that and I can’t escape it. Should she forgive me? Of course not. She is right I did leave without a trace. “You have every right to excuse me Safina. But know this was not my first choice. I was already dead and there was nothing to bring me back. Not you, not Blassa and not even myself could save what were once my strongest beliefs. I died on that field and I am just now regaining life.” It was true what they say. Forgiveness from friends is a never-ending journey. Clearing the air with Safina would take some time, With my friends and her mind, this will be an on-going battle that will last more then any war that Thailand has seen. If I know her the way I did back those many years ago, this will take time. Time in which I don’t have at the moment.
