A story for Jordan X Shabazz: The black people he never sees.

17 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/17/02 'I'm home!' Ronnie calls, walking through the door with his coat buttoned up all the waydespite the sunny and mild 73 degree weather outside. Ronnie slips past the living room to the left and starts up the stairs. A booming, thoughslightly slurred voice stops him in his tracks, shaking the wooden walls. 'Where the f*ck do you think you're going?' the voice demands of an answer. Ronnie slinks back down the stairs with his head in his chest, holding the trenchcoat collarup to his ears. From the living room emerges a mammoth of a man, at 7'0' he towers overRonnie (who doesn't even clear the 6 foot mark), with a hulking muscular physique of 478pounds (that far outclasses Ronnie's 200 pound lightweight build) barely fitting into thejeans and red flannel lumberjack's shirt he wears. His short hair leads down his face to afull brown beard. Upon this man's breath is a heavy stench of alcohol, his nose and eyesred from imbibing large amounts of it. Ronnie slowly looks up at the giant, keepinghimself hidden in the trenchcoat. He ventures to speak lightly, with nervousness evident inthe quivering of his tone and mouth. 'Hey, dad,' Ronnie squeaks. 'What did you say, dumb b*tch?' is the reply. Ronnie shrinks even smaller. 'Sorry, Big Bear.' The man nods his head, his arms crossed over his massive chest. The voice booms 'Where the f*ck were you?' Ronnie can't even look up at him. He stares at the floorboard down to his right and says,'Nowhere.' 'Nowhere...' 'Big Bear' rips off the trenchcoat Ronnie was wearing, revealing a black body suit with agold cross on his chest. In his pocket is a gold mask, and hanging off of his neck is apurple cape. He wore this outift to a local comic convention. Big Bear tosses the ripped coat to the floor and stands back, looking at his son in shockand disgust. 'That f*ggot was you?!' 'You saw?' Ronnie asks, afraid of the answer. 'I saw? 'Course I saw, the whole got-damn world saw! You were a f*cking disgrace outthere! Talking on the news like a damn wimp, and what the f*ck are you wearing?' Ronnie backs up, holding his hands out, waving them in a 'simmer down' motion. 'It's not mine, I just went with a friend and he gave it to me to wear,' Ronnie attempts torationalize. “Was it that f*cking weak cracker sh*t that I told you to stay away from?” Ronnie's 9-year-old little brother, a smaller clean-faced carbon copy of Big Bear namedDylan, walks down the stairs, seeing Ronnie's costume. 'Hey, Ronnie. You look like Redeemer from Spawn with that outfit and mask.' Big Bear looks up at Dylan with evil eyes, the veins in his neck and forehead popping out. 'Who the f*ck?' he shouts at the young boy. Dylan withdraws into himself, staying at his same spot at the middle of the stairs. Heresponds to his father, but weaker. 'Redeemer... he's a character in this comic book called Spawn.' Big Bear clenches his right fist. Without even rearing back, Big Bear throws a hard punchat Ronnie's face, the bone against bone cutting Ronnie with a scar running down the leftcheek. Ronnie recoils a few feet and sways in stance, but he doesn't fall. 'WHAT THE F*CK ARE YOU DOING OUT THERE?!' Yet another hard smack, and Ronnie falls to the floor with a black eye. Big Bear kicksRonnie in the ribs, sending him rolling into the wall. Pinned in place, Ronnie can only tryto cover up as Big Bear stomps down on him. Dylan slips down the stairs past the beating.He sits down in the living room with his mother, and hugs her tight. She covers Dylan'sears so Dylan won't have to hear what Ronnie is going through. Big Bear ceases his attack. Ronnie has bruised ribs (one broken), and he coughs up a fairamount of blood. Bear kneels down beside Ronnie, placing his hand over Ronnie's headand pushing it into the floor. 'You go on television, humiliating yourself? And worse, you do it dressed as a fictionalcharacter? Fiction, fantasy... all it is is a weakness. Be your own man, not someone else.' Bear palms Ronnie's head, pulls him off the floor, and spikes him face-first hard into thewooden floorboards. 'I will not f*cking tolerate weakness!' Ronnie's blood drips out of his mouth as Big Bear gets up and walks into the living room. He holds his hand out to Miranda, holding Dylan. She immediately hands him the phone. Big Bear dials eleven digits and calls the local boxing commision offices. 'Hello? Shut up, listen, why hasn't my son gotten any fights booked yet? Is he notimportant enough? No one's willing to face him?' The voice on the other end is uncooperative in the tone. 'Shut the f*ck up and listen! Look, he's going to be put in a match or I'll drag him to yourevent and have him fight people in the parking lot! What the... Ronnie Mills, dumbass! Geta fight for him or I will kick your f*cking ass, enough of this 'wait for a call' sh*t!' Big Bear clicks off the phone and throws it at the wall. He turns back to Ronnie, still lyingon the ground clutching his ribs. 'You'd better not f*ck this up, boy.' 'Happy Birthday!' cheers the family of Big Bear Mack, not happy, but nervous about theday. Mack does not like to age, and today is his 46th birthday, falling on June 17th thisyear. The five candles on the plain white icing-covered chocolate cake, the grill lighter setto the side with the knife, Big Bear sits at the head of the table with Ronnie, Miranda, andDylan gathered around him. Big Bear growls, 'You know I don't like to celebrate birthdays.' Miranda rubs him on the back, patting off her left hand on her flour-dusted apron, andsays, 'It's good to celebrate these things. It's a nice family moment to spend together.' Miranda turns to Dylan. 'Get Big Bear his present.' Big Bear Mack was born Timothy Michael Mills, but had his name legally changed to BigBear Mack when he entered professional boxing at the age of 15 after dropping out ofschool. He demands not to be called anything but Big Bear, especially from his family. Nothoney, not dad, nothing but Big Bear Mack. Calling him anything else would be anaddress in the familiar, and the treatment in the familiar is a sign of weakness. Beingisolated and impersonal is how one becomes strong, in his mind. Dylan walks behind the counter facing the table and comes back out with acrudely-wrapped box, the green wrapping paper wrinkled with a simple red ribbon holdingit all together. Dylan presents the box to Mack. 'What the hell is this?' Mack says, staring angrily down at the box on the table. 'Thislooks like a badger wrapped it.' Dylan's head drops down at the floor. 'I wrapped it,' he nearly whispers. Big Bear looks down upon his son. 'You wrapped this? You can't even wrap this without f*cking it up? You miserable littlesh*t, you can't do anything right!' Big Bear picks up the fairly good-sized box with one-hand and throws it hard at the wall,breaking the glass inside. He turns to his wife and yells, 'This dumbass didn't wrap everything you got me, did it?' Miranda shakes her head no, backing up against the wall. Big Bear scoops a handful of thecake and stuffs it into his mouth. He reaches out and grabs Miranda by the apron, pullingher close. Mack spits the cake out in her face and shoves her face down into the cake. 'You call that a cake? What'd you make that with, your ass sweat? You could neverf*cking cook before, what made you think you could do it now, you dumb b*tch?' Big Bear clutches Miranda by the back of her neck and flings her to the wall. Mirandalands on the box of broken glass, which pierces through the container and cuts her in thestomach. Big Bear then turns to Ronnie. 'And what did you do?' he booms. Ronnie just stands there, paralyzed. Big Bear grabs the grill lighter, sets the flame, andlocks his free hand around Ronnie's head. Ronnie doesn't even move as Mack puts theflame in Ronnie's right eye. A deafening scream comes from Ronnie as his eye is heated somuch that his lens almost melts. Collapsing to the floor, Ronnie holds his hand over hiseye and flails his body. Mack places his foot on Ronnie's head and pushes down. 'You know I don't like to repeat myself,' he says. Ronnie just shouts. 'In the closet!' Mack steps off of Ronnie and moves to the closet. He jerks open the door and looks downat a pair of neatly shined brown steel-toed boots. A slight smile comes on Mack's face ashe reaches down to take up the boots in his right hand. Walking back into the kitchen, Dylan still standing where he was when Mack yelled athim, and Miranda and Ronnie still down on the floor, Big Bear stands over Ronnie. 'You buy me these?' he asks, with not as harsh a tone as he has displayed. Ronnie looks up with his good eye. 'Yeah,' is as much as he can manage. Mack sits down at his chair and takes off his old boots, stringing the new ones on. Mackstands up stepping in them to make sure they fit. 'Perfect,' says Mack. He stands over Ronnie, a smile on his face. He rears back and kicks Ronnie hard in theribs. 'Dumb f*ck,' he mutters as he begins laying to Ronnie with the steel-toed boots. After afew good kicks, and Miranda starts to get up, he moves over to her and kicks her back tothe ground. Meanwhile, Dylan stands at his same spot by the table. Big Bear ceases his assault on Ronnie and Miranda, turning back to Dylan with abloodlust evident in his eyes. 'You...' he says, stepping over to his 9-year-old son, picking up the sharp 6-inch bladedknife for the cake. Kneeling down, Mack caresses the blade with his hands, then looks up at Dylan. 'You know what has to happen,' he says. Mack grabs Dylan's hand and places the knife into it, folding Dylan's fingers around thehandle. Dylan steps back a foot and holds the knife in his right hand, his left armoutstretched. With his eyes locked with his father's, Dylan places the blade over hismuch-scarred left arm and slices it open. Big Bear sits atop a government building, throwing golf clubs down on cars and at peoplefrom his bag. He is on the edge, and keeps shouting that he will jump off. The policehave been called in to deal with the situation. Officers Sam Rodriguez and CammyHowell are up to talk the man down off the roof, the sunny day totally opposing thesituation at hand. Sam:“Sir, I need you to come off the ledge.” Big Bear:“No!” Rodriguez tries to approach, but Big Bear sees him out of the corner of his eye. Big Bear:“Don’t get f*cking near me! I’ll jump!” Cammy holds Sam back. With a sympathetic tone, Cammy tries to talk down Mack. Cammy:“Sir, why are you up here? Why are you throwing golf clubs down at people? Why doyou want to jump?” Big bear shakes his head, the tears in his eyes blurring his forward vision. He wipes themwith his blood-stained hands. Big Bear:“Why the hell not? I’ve got no more family. I beat my son to death, *holds his hand backat the officers* my wife left me with our son. I’ve got no money and nothing I can do. There’s nothing left to live for.” Cammy steps forward one step. Cammy:“You can rebuild. You can start over.” Big Bear:“I’m nearly 50, you dumb f*ck. There’s no starting over for me, this is it. I’d spend therest of my life in prison, or just die right now.” Cammy steps forward again. Mack knows, but he stays put with his hand still in the golfbag. Cammy:“I won’t lie to you, you probably will go to jail, perhaps for the rest of your life.” Sam, the more callous of the two, smirks with arms folded standing away. Sam:“Nice motivational speech there, Cam.” Cammy doesn’t respond, but shoots her partner a mean “shut up” look. Then she goesback to Mack. Cammy:“But you can still lead as full a life as you possibly can. But what I need is for you not tojump off this building.” Big Bear stands up on the narrow ledge, turning around to Cammy and Sam with his golfbag in hand. Big Bear:“Jump? Why would I do that?” Cammy breathes a premature sigh of relief as Big Bear pulls a single-barreled shotgun outof the golf bag, quickly inserts it into his mouth pointing up toward the brain, and pulls thetrigger....
