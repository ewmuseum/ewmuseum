Drunken Jibberish

5 May 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/5/01 [Fade In] [“AAAAHHHHHHHHH” What’s that awful sound hurting our ears. We’re in a country-western bar in Toronto, Canada of all places. Hey, there are plenty of cowboys in Canada. Lots of famous bull-riders from Alberta. Anyhow, the shot reveals a stage immediately to our left. A band, and all their equipment currently occupies the stage. Also, somewhat to our left, except out in front of the stage is a dance floor where various couples are twirling about, and two-stepping all over the place. The majority of the men are wearing cowboy hats, tight jeans, and button up shirts, while the women tend to have even tighter pants, similar button up shirts, and only a few are sporting hats.] [Surrounding the dance floor is a little ledge in which to rest your drinks, and scattered all around the dance floor and a few separating the dance floor and the stage are round tables, with four stools at each on. To our right is the bar, where a hoard of people crowd around shouting drink orders to three of four bartenders. Lining the walls of the bar, are pool tables. Each pool table features a bright light enclosed in a little shed type thing, donning the Budweiser logo.] [The atmosphere of the bar is typical to most bars. Smoke both from the bands smoke machine, and from the stupid people who haven’t realized smoking can kill you, accompanies the loud noise to make it a rather annoying experience unless you’re picking up a female or drunk as hell. We’re in this Canadian country western bar called Shenanigans because of the latter. No we’re not drunk, but he is…] [The shot now zooms in on Ryan “Streak” Stephens perched atop one of the tall stools near the dance floor. Sitting next to him, is his ever loyal, and understanding wife, (did we mention she’s hot), Dana. Stephens is not wearing the dark Wrangler Blue jeans that most of the men in the club are wearing. He’s wearing rather loose fitting Polo carpenter jeans with the little hammer holding tag hanging off the right pocket, and Polo boots. He’s wearing a nice button up Polo shirt, and a baseball cap. His wife is showing off her cute feature in tight Wrangler jeans, and a low cut shirt.] [He’s staring aimlessly out onto the dance floor, and she has laced her left hand through his right arm, and has rested her hand on his forearm. She pays no attention to the band or the dancers, but rather Ryan. A concern look is draped about her face, and she leans in close so he can hear her speak to him.] Dana: You want to dance some more baby or are you tired? [Perhaps she is unaware of how much he’s had to drink, but he’s not in much condition to dance. Even he is unaware of how drunk he has become. It’s not like him at all. He’s always been the poster boy for doing the right thing, and he very rarely ever drinks, much less to get drunk. Perhaps he’s still dwelling on his upcoming matches with Stryfe and Anna Keyson, and he’s trying to get his mind off of the pressure he has put on himself to succeed. He nods, and tries to stand up.] [His foot gets caught on one of the little bars that connects each leg of the stool together, and he goes pummeling down onto the hard ground of the bar.] Dana: Ohh Nooo! [She exclaims loud enough for a few cowboys to take notice.] [She jumps down off of her stool and rushes, a few steps, over to Ryan’s side. She kneels down, and asks him if he’s okay. He turns and looks her right in the eye, and all of a sudden a wide grin appears, and his white teeth gleam back at her. His usually intense blue eyes, look goofy and innocent. She can’t help, but laugh, but deep down she’s sighing. She shakes her head as if to say, What am I going to do with you.] Dana: Let’s get you up, you need to go home and take a shower. [She grabs him by the hands, and tries to lift him from the cold concrete floor, but to no avail. He’s not small, and he’s not exactly cooperating either. One of the cowboys who heard her yell, and seen Stephens lying on the ground makes his way over to help her pick him up. She lefts go of Stephens’ left hand and the cowboy snags it, and together they lift Stephens from the ground. Stephens still has the goofy grin on his face. He looks at the cowboy and the cowboy back at him most likely just to see what he’s going to do. Dana taps the cowboy on the shoulder. He adverts his attention from Stephens to her.] Dana: Thank you for your help sir. Cowboy: Huh!? [He can’t hear her over the music.] [She raises her voice and repeats her gratitude, he nods and tips his hat to her. He looks back at Stephens who is now all up in his face sniffing him. The cowboy just shakes his head, and turns to go sit down, when all of a sudden Stephens’ lets out a roaring…] Streak: YEEEEEEE HAAAAAAAAAAAA!!! [Stephens bursts out laughing at himself, but the cowboy just stares at Stephens like he’s insane, and he partially is considering his current state of inebriation. Dana wraps her arms around her husband’s waist. She’s now trying to be patient, but she hates when he gets loud and obnoxious. He sometimes does it merely to embarrass her, now is not one of those times, he’s completely unaware of what he’s doing. Have we established that?] Dana: Come on baby, let’s go back to the hotel. [Stephens’ shakes his head and replies.] Streak: Aww come on baby, we haven’t done the Cotton-Eyed Joe yet. Dana: You’re in no condition to do the Cotton-Eyed Joe. [Her frustration is now showing through] For someone who is worried about staying in good shape for his matches coming up, you sure did plaster yourself tonight. [The cowboy has remained nearby, because cowboys when not drunk are usually pretty considerate people.] Cowboy: You need any help getting him out to the car? [He points to a couple of other guys sitting on stools right near the dance floor, their drinks resting on the ledge surrounding the dance flooor.] Cowboy: My friend and I would be happy to help you. [All the while he’s offering to help Stephens staring out towards the dance floor like an autistic child or something, head cocked sideways, eyes staring at nothing in-particular, and yet everything in-particular at the same time. Picture Dustin Hoffman in “Rainman.” Dana looks at him, her frustration written all over her face, and then back at the cowboy. She shakes her head.] Dana: Thanks, I really do appreciate it, but I think I can handle it. I’m so embarrassed. I can’t possibly ask you to help me take him all the way out to the car. [Stephens now seems excited for some reason or another. It becomes apparent he’s actually been listening to the conversation between the cowboy and his wife when he talks.] Streak: Come on baby, let him help you. [He points at the cowboy. His eyebrows rise towards the top of his forehead; he’s into it.] Streak: Look at this man baby, yeah, look at him. He’s so nice, and normal baby. He’s just a normal guy trying to help a drunk F**K like me out. Normal, yeah, that’s awesome. Dana: Fine he can help, but let’s just go baby. You’re just babbling drunken jibberish. [The cowboy goes over to Streak and drapes his arm over his shoulders. Dana keeps her arms around her waist, but Stephens won’t really walk, he’s too busy spouting off a lot of nothing.] Streak: I’m so glad I met a nice real normal person today. I mean here is this totally cool guy who is willing to help you and I out. He’s not some crazy ass psycho whose f*cking out to get the whole world because they mistreated him when he was a kid. He’s not some dark ass personality who doesn’t groom himself, and looks like he worships the devil. And best of all, he’s not some little b*tch with purple hair. Dana: [almost angry] Baby, you don’t know what you’re saying. Granted Stryfe and Keyson aren’t your typical people that you see walking down the street everyday. Hell they may even be a little f*cked up in the head, but quite frankly I’m not sure either of them can help it. They’ve been through a lot in their lives, more than you can imagine, and they’re still out in the real world making money for themselves, doing something they enjoy. [It’s very unlike her to cuss, and Stephens has lost it. He’s saying stuff he’d never say sober. Perhaps he’s saying what he feels, they say you do when you’re drunk, but he’s too politically correct to ever even come close to speaking such things in a normal state of mind. They finally begin making their way towards the door with Stephens, but he continues his endless, obnoxious, drunken verbal banter.] Streak: I’m PCW’s new mother f*cking rookie phenom. I’m going to obtain the respect of all the fans, and then I’m going to rid PCW of all it’s bad men one by f*cking one. I’m PCW’s savior, all these morons running around, disrespecting authority, making their own rules, I’ll take care of them, but first I’m going to take care of PCW’s resident freak shows starting with Stryfe this Tuesday, and Anna Keyson on Friday. [He continues on, as Dana and the cowboy drag him closer and closer to the door. Dana looks at him.] Dana: This is not going to look good if this incident gets on television honey. The kids you love so much aren’t going to respect you, and neither are the fans. You have to be quiet until I get you out of here. [He’s not listening, he continues on, in his verbal tirade.] Streak: Oooh, look at me, I’m chasing a whole lot of nothing into some woods. Or better yet, look at me, I’m the idol of some kid who f*cking killed himself. Wow, aren’t I proud. Dana: [Beyond Annoyed] Baby, SHUT THE HELL UP!!! [He stops rambling on. He looks at her, and then at the cowboy. He smiles at the cowboy, and then says.] Streak: Thanks for being real man, yeah, f*cking yeah! [All of a sudden, Stephens’ eyebrows squint downward, and the skin between them wrinkles up. His eyes bulge from their sockets, and his lips press tightly together into a crooked smirk that is disturbingly goofy. Then he ducks out from under their grasp of him, and takes off towards the dance floor. His wife yells after him, and then begins crying for lack of anything better to do; her frustration has gotten the best of her. The cowboy tries to comfort her, and offers to get Ryan, but she waves his hand in his direction so as to dismiss him.] Dana: Screw him. He can walk home. [The cowboy looks out onto the dance floor, and upon the site of Stephens’ he asks again.] Cowboy: Ma’am, perhaps I should go get him. [She looks up through her salty tears, and the cowboy looks back towards Stephens. He’s doing a dance in which he waves both arms on the left side of his body while bouncing on his right leg, then waves both arms on the right side of his body while bouncing on his left leg, and not to let anyone down waving either arm on their respective side of his body, bouncing on both legs. And all the while he does this dance he screams to where you can hear him over the band.] Streak: STREAK DA DA DEEK DA DA DEEK DA DEEK DA DEEK DA DEEK! [With this both horrendous and hilarious sight we fade out.] [What’s the world coming too? Ryan “Streak” Stephens, one of the most politically correct and morally sound wrestlers ever has drowned his thoughts, his doubts, and his fears with alcohol. His wife, who rarely exhibits the slightest frustration or annoyance by him, is so angry and frustrated she’s in tears. And finally Stephens has said some awful things, that might change how people look at him forever, if they don’t care that he’s drunker than a skunk. Further more, he dropped the F bomb no telling how many times, and Stephens has only been known to cuss in the most intense confrontations. What will be the consequences of his actions? How soon will Dana forgive him? Will he ever stop doing that silly dance, and singing that gay ass song?]
