008 - The Scarlett Dress; Act III, Scene I

16 September 2002

Date: 09.16.02 Magnolia Police DepartmentNew Orleans, LA 2:15 amMonday 'I've got him now.' Peter Evans slightly laughed to himself as he sat in the squad room, reviewing over the tape of his opponent's press conference. It was in him to study every single word that came out into the ears of the public that day, cheering him up that he had backed his prey into a corner. Coming over his shoulder, Larry Donovan happened to be watching the tape as well, grasping his chin in thought and wondering just how the detective happened to prove such a thing. For years, Donovan had been thinking in vain, that he was better than Evans at all points. Evans had set out from the beginning that he had received this case, to prove Donovan wrong. He was halfway there. 'What does this have to do with anything?' Donovan asked, causing Evans to turn his head in surprise. 'Oh, didn't see you back there boss.' Donovan smirked. 'Its your case and now you're making the press look like fools.' Evans nodded to him, before turning back and facing the small television screen sitting atop a TV tray holding up a Video Cassette Recorder along with it. 'Tell me about it.' 'So where do you plan to go with this now?' Donovan questioned. Peter didn't know where he would, from the bureau probably wanting them for themselves, beating out the National Security Agency for it. Evans dreamed of being higher than every other person he happened to be placed up against. 'I haven't a clue.' 'You need one, because something's up with Jackson. His defiance had change during that press conference. I could see it in his eyes.' Evans blinked, turning back at looking up at Donovan. 'Wait, are you telling me that he...' 'I'm not sure.' the Chief replied to him, unsure for a reason. Evans began to tap his foot impatiently as he waits for Donovan to begin and give his response. 'Well?' 'Well, I have to say that people are now split between the both of you, whether they want to believe that you two happen to be telling an accurate truth.' he told Evans, before lowering his head down. Peter chuckled. 'You still don't believe me do you?' 'Yes, and no.' Evans' eyes squinted upward, turning his body a full one hundred and eighty degrees to face his boss. He sat reverse seating in the chair, with his neck on the outline head, his legs clacking with those of the chair and his elbows under his head. 'What is there a yes and a no towards?' 'Th- the thought that yes, I do believe in you. But...' he stopped himself short, placing his hands firmly on his hips. 'But what?' He just couldn't say it in his face. The fact that he didn't believe in him for the long term. The vast majority of his ego happened to overlook the entire saying that Donovan hated Evans and vice versa. Both of them couldn't say it in the other's face, and it just made it worse that they had to keep secrets from one another. Each of their values had been halted by Evans one mishap. 'Nothing.' Donovan backed away from the statement, before moving on towards something else. 'How about those Saints?' 'Shut it, Donovan. I know you're hiding something from me.' the response from Evans was something only that barely a good few could say towards their bosses. Larry grumbled, before shutting off the television himself, ruining Evans glory mid way through. 'Oh, you want me to tell you the truth Peter? Do you?' 'What I want is a clear damn answer, other than yes and no here.' Donovan began to laugh at the mere idiocy of his main homicide detective as he bent down to stare Evans right in the face. The feeling, that growing disdain for one another was at a boiling point at the moment, yet neither one of them would happen to back down from the other. Ego was too high for that. 'Here it is, the truth!' Donovan's voice boomed throughout the empty station as he dropped down a contract. Evans picked it up off of the ground and began reading it. 'What is this?' 'You're release.' 'You've got to be joking!' The truth was, he wasn't joking. 'If you screw this up, if Jackson walks away a free man at the end of this entire procedure, then you're out. Gone, and I'm letting of the pimpled faced traffic cops take your damn job just to make them feel good and you looking of an even more failure. That a traffic cop can do your job, better than you do.' Evans' fist was shaking steadily, waiting to pound on Donovan right then and there. He had been backed into the corner he'd gotten out of with the Jackson press conference. But this time, he could change his story ever so quickly, the press would believe it. Him beating the hell out of his own boss, and liking it by saying it was self defense. He stood up out of the chair, ready to sock one right in Donovan's face. 'And what are you going to do about it?' Donovan snarled at Evans. 'I have the power move here, and you don't. Remember that.' Evan's fist dropped back to the ground, in shame once more. He let his head down, but not taking his eyes off of his superior before letting his fist right back up and smacking Donovan flush on the jaw. He fell back into the chair, toppling it over and making it stand on its back. Evans pleaded with his boss to get back up so he could do it once more, but decided to leave him there, powerless. 'Y-you son of a bitch!' Larry said in a complete stumble before toppling over the chair once more. It was all a ruse played out by Evans, waiting for Donovan to drop his guard to get decked. It was a reality check for Donovan as he had lost something he had wielded over Evans since the very first day the both of them met. His ego had shrunken down towards a patrol cop, ultimately the lowest form of officer you can go, and Evans rode it out on him easily, his swagger and confidence had returned. By the time Donovan had fully returned to his feet, Evans was long gone, out of the picture. Wiping the blood away from the sleeve on his cornflower blue button down shirt, Donovan reached over towards the phone and began to dail numbers simultaneously before bringing the phone up towards his voice. 'Hello?' 'Yeah, whose this?' 'Look, you got to do something man!' 'Stop worrying, stop worrying. What's this about? Another bribe to keep you out of your own built prison cells?' 'No, its not that. I got a job for you.' 'A job? What kind of job?' 'Something that pays good and makes sure you get some payment out of this, I'm sure.' 'You're willing to put this up, just to make sure that nobody takes your job. Oh the great Larry Donovan, scary isn't it?' 'Just shut your mouth and do your damn job.' The phone clicked over, and Donovan began to laugh evilly, just making sure that he would end up having complete control over the situation, not letting anything slip away from his finger tips. He loved to be in power. * * * 1996 Toyota MarcelBaton Rouge, LA 6:30 amMonday Aaron had nothing to say, as we went fly fishing in the middle of the morning. We had packed, catching away from Bass to Flounder fish. None of them happened to be something to him and myself, just something to pass time I suppose. He didn't pay attention to the constant jokes I had thrown towards him, neither that playful jokes when a bullfrog leaped into his lap during the middle hours, between five and five thirty this morning. Yet, on the way back to normal civilization, he began to speak up, for the first time as his silence was broken, 'How many did you catch?' he wondered. 'About a good four, most didn't mean anything so I threw one back.' I retorted to him, he agreed whole heartily with the saying as he was at the wheel, myself in the passenger seat. 'You threw a few back? Why?' he nudged me in the shoulder. 'Can't hang with the big boys? We need to start getting you Depends.' 'Shut up,' I jabed him lightly in the shoulder as he kept his hand on the wheel. I turned a good twenty-four about forty eight hours ago, enough to make me a full fledged old timer to some people's eyes. Still, being 24 out of New York is a breath of fresh air, knowing that some of the 'hood' like stereotypes we get, like a man won't live to see 21, is complete hoopla and procrastination, I guess. He began to take a left over soda can from the cooler that we'd left from fishing earlier in the morning. Popping the top and letting the bubbles rise, he sipped on it playfully, before beginning to gulp down. 'Speaking of which, how in the world are you going to play off your next move?' he questioned, and from there it was all downhill. 'Play into my next move?' I played stupid with him, only trying to silence something that he and I both knew. He starred at me with obvious reasons, 'You actin' dumb isn't going to help. Now where do you go from here, I'm askin'.' 'Oh.' I quietly said to myself. 'Don't have a clue.' 'Well if maybe you turn the radio up a bit, then we'd learn things!' he swore a few times before slapping my hand. I wanted to punch back, but that would be the end of him and myself. I began turning the stereo, from hip hop, to basic current event stations. My song, 'Defiance,' was playing on the rock station, one of those B-Sides I did for a movie soundtrack or whatever. Aaron turned his head, 'Will you ever find a freakin' station!' 'Yeah!' I snarled at him, before landing on 98.7 am, that Chris Baker morning hour in Baton Rouge. It read out to me, like something from a magazine clip. Breaking news to report to you, it seems that the police chief, Larry Donovan, has been attacked as it may seem by his very own Detective, Peter Evans. Evans has not been reached for comment yet, but it figures that he might lose his job by the exact notice of the event. It didn't shock me that Evans would go that far against his boss. From what I've been hearing, Evans has quite the hatred for Larry Donovan, and the pre-empted attack wasn't just for doing it, it was self defense. Evans, as much as he wanted to bring me down, wanted to keep his job in the process. He wasn't going to flush it down by being judgemental. 'Woah,' I raised an eyebrow, shrouding my feelings from Aaron. He laughed at the news piece, whether him being a big goof or just finding others misfortune's funny. He slapped me on the back, 'Seems like that'll be your very own victory.' 'Can't say that,' I reeled back from the statement. 'He's wanting to fix me, not end it quickly.' 'True, but what about that Kendra chick?' 'Who? Oh yeah, her.' I grumbled at the last part, knowing that she'd be on my case until the end of this case. She had drive, I liked that in a woman and I wanted to oblige her very own criticism, with some of my own. But one of critiquing can't be a critic of his own can he? The cellphone cliped onto my jeans pocket began to ring, causing me only to think in what will happen if this person happens to be who I think it happens to be. I picked it up on the first ring. 'Hello?' 'Dante, we need to talk.' the voice sounded all too familiar, Tracy. 'Talk about what?' I questioned her. She clicked her teeth. 'Look, your dad called me and he happened to give me some information.' My eyes shortened, 'Information? From who?' 'Your step mother, April's mom.'I said nothing about even trying to change the subject, I wasn't ready to forgive my dad, even for having a child out of wedlock and even lying to me for 23 years of my life. The conservative ploy he wanted to play with my mind, I shut that voice off in my head. His quotes were nothing for me now, only words without meaning. 'What does she want?' I asked her with disdain in my voice.' In my stubborn way, she tried to play peacekeeper. 'That is your mother, in a way! You should give her some respect. She says she has information on finding April, your long lost sister.' 'I know that she is my long lost sister, thanks for the information, Captian Obvious!' Aaron slightly changed his view on things, 'Wait, you have a sister? Since when? Is she fine?' 'Shut up!' I hused the phone with my palm, not letting Tracy hear it. She might take it for the wrong way and I'll feel responsible for it. 'Now, where was I' I pulled the muzzle of the phone off. She sighed, 'She's at the Westlake Apartments, 219 B.' Two, nineteen, B, I relayed the letters and numbers in my mind, over and over again, trying to make sure I get it correct. It changes quickly as I close my conversation with her. I could hear Simmone speaking in the background, and myself, on the road missing it all. I wonder why I was subjected to this lifestyle, and it all being burdened down upon me. I sighed, before closing my eyes and resting my head against the arm rest. Something had to be done, and I was just the person to do it. That came after myself and April had a one on one talk. Hopefully.
