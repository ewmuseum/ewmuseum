It twisted into a knot and bow, as he had a headache coming to him at the words. His jaw dropped, and his eyes bulged. He gulped, worried. 'Car accident. Sky told me about it; your parents were driving when a driver lost his brake and couldn’t stop, and the pummeled together, injuring both of your parents. Here was Eddy, surrounded by Skylar Green and Sandy Wong, who have told him this news. He was shocked. He could hardly breathe right. 'Don’t feel bad. They are DYING. Not DEAD….' Even if Sky was trying to cheer him, using dying and dead in his sentences made him feel worst. 'Where are they at? What hospital?' 'A Georgian hospital. I can’t remember the name.' 'City?' 'Atlanta.' 'Take me.' 'Eddy, I don’t think-' 'TAKE ME!' Suddenly the waiter came back over to the table, ready for an order. He had a paper in front of him, ready to right down the orders. He had a white shirt and short dark brown hair. 'Ready to order?' 'No', and with that, Eddy slung his water across the table, splashing and crashing all over the wooden floor. He stood up, after everyone was staring at him, and ran. He ran outside, and leaned against the wall. Back inside, Sky and Sandy were having a talk. 'Maybe I should to talk to him?', Sandy questioned Sky. 'Don’t. Let the man sort his feelings out.' 'The man may slit his wrists out there!' 'Trust me, Eddy won’t kill himself. He’s not that cowardly. He can take this, he just needs time to himself.' 'If you say…' Outside, Eddy tried to calm himself. The man, usually mature, was recalling everything Sky said. Atlanta was where his parents were. He had to go. A trucker was about to leave the truck stop after he pumped his gas. He was a balding fat man with a blue buttoned shirt and dirty jeans. He stepped back into his 4 wheeler, ready to drive off. Until… 'Wait!' The truck driver stopped. He stared at Eddy strangely. 'Yeah kid?' 'Where you going?' 'Atlanta' 'Take me' Eddy took one final glance at the restaurant. He blinked. He didn’t know if he’d go back to wrestle anymore. 'Jump in kid. I’ll take you to Atlanta if you need…' 'Thanks.' Eddy went on the passengers side and put himself in. It smells like oil inside, even if he did have a little air freshening tree on this rear view mirror. The radio looks like it had been pulled out even though the air conditioner worked well. He leaned back into the seat as the fat man pulled himself into the drivers position and revved it up as the 18 wheeler chugged in return. He reversed out of the truck stop, and onto the highway. The 18 wheeler drove off down the freeway regular speed as Eddy stared outside. It was still pretty boring like Wyoming usually was. He sighed. The truck driver put his hand into his pocket, pulled out and cigarette and lit it. He puffed on it and blew the smoke out the window. 'What’s yer’ name fella?' 'Eddy.' 'Well, hello Eddy. Muh name is Jack. Jack Barnes', said Jack, puffing his smoke out the window as he quickly sucked on the cigarette again. 'Hi Jack.' Jack puffed his smoke out, before turning to Eddy. 'You thirsty? I gotta few packs of beer back there if you want some.' 'No thanks. I don’t drink beer.' 'Too young eh?' 'Yeah, but it’s also my choice not to make that my life decision. Jack shrugged. 'Suit yourself.' 'I am thirsty though. You have coke or something?' 'Yeah. It’s back there in the coola’' Eddy reached behind him and saw a red and white color. He opened the lid and looked around the beer until he saw a can of Dr. Pepper floating next to some Coors. He grabbed the Dr. Pepper, popped the top, and sipped it. 'Ahhh. Thanks for the drink.' 'Ain’t a prob.' Eddy sipped his drink as Jack puffed his cigarette again. He glanced down, noticing on the floor board scattered Marlboro ciggy cartons, oil cartons, coke cans and random wrappers. Jack puffed his cig and blow it this time right into Eddy’s face. Eddy coughed into return. 'Whoops. Sorry kid.' 'It’s *cough* okay', said Eddy, coughing afterward again. 'So…why ya need to go to Atlanta?' 'I’m visiting my parents.' 'Parents? Hell, I ain’t ever talk to any of my parents for like…sh*t, I don’t know, long ass time though. They ain’t ever call me, and they never gave a flying rat’s ass anyway, so I don’t care.' 'Don’t you love your parents?' 'Well, yah. To a point. They put me on this Earth, thassa what’s everyone says. But they never evva did much else. I dunno, I was pretty much a loner. I stayed by myself everywhere I went, and they never cared a bit. Hell, I was a skinny little puppy when I was a kid, ya know what saying? I usually fed myself some way. Heh, so looka me now. I’m a fat, ugly trucker who doesn’t have much of a life', Jack said with not many stops. This was one of the longest period since he could remember he didn’t puff his cig. 'Well, that’s you. I love my parents. They always cared for me, so I can’t say what you just said.' Eddy never thought of getting a response, and didn’t. Jack just went on driving, puffing his cigarette, not caring, and looking like he wasn’t even listening. Eddy shrugged and sipped his Dr. Pepper and stared outside. He wasn’t expecting much more then the boring Wyoming. He wanted out so bad because Wyoming was boring. He wanted New York, or Dallas, or Hawaii. Just to get the hell out of Wyoming into a much more colorful area. Since he left the truck stop, he rarely thought of wrestling. Should he go back? Should he wait until his parents were okay? Should he just go for it? He didn’t want to think. He was too busy thinking of his parents. Jack put out his cigarette and drove with both hands for a change. He paid attention, but glanced of occasionally for some reason. There was little to glance at though. 'I hate to tell you this, but…' '….' 'They’re dying.' Eddy felt sick to his stomach. It twisted into a knot and bow, as he had a headache coming to him at the words. His jaw dropped, and his eyes bulged. He gulped, worried. 'Car accident. Sky told me about it; your parents were driving when a driver lost his brake and couldn’t stop, and the pummeled together, injuring both of your parents. A car accident. That was one of the biggest reasons his family died at all. He hoped it wouldn’t happen again. He remembered he walked outside… Outside, Eddy tried to calm himself. The man, usually mature, was recalling everything Sky said. Atlanta was where his parents were. He had to go. A trucker was about to leave the truck stop after he pumped his gas. He was a balding fat man with a blue buttoned shirt and dirty jeans. He stepped back into his 4 wheeler, ready to drive off. Until… 'Wait!' The truck driver stopped. He stared at Eddy strangely. 'Yeah kid?' 'Where you going?' 'Atlanta' 'Take me' That’s where he currently was. Sitting in an oil scented truck with a smoking, hick truck driver on a long way from Wyoming to Georgia. He probably could’ve just fell asleep, but he seriously wasn’t tired. After a few minutes without a cigarette, Jack got another, lit it, and went on with his routine, smoking his cigarette constantly. He didn’t understand smokers. What does smoking help? It doesn’t help depression, or make you feel better and you have the cure for the crave. With alcohol and drugs you at least had some effect to help your mood. Cigarettes were worthless and pointless. 'You smoke kid?' 'No.' 'Why not?' 'As I said, it’s a not life choice for me.' 'How old ya’?' '19' 'I’m 40.' 'He doesn’t look to good for that age.' 'Cool.' The man puffed his cigarette again, and stared outside. Not much was changing. Same boring fields, same smoking man. Boredom. He didn’t want to sleep because he wasn’t tired. But he didn’t have much else to do on this long drive. 'Hey kid…' 'Yeah?' 'Fetch me a beer will ya.' A smoker and a drunk. He reached into the ice cold cooler, and pulled out a Coors. He handed it to the man as he popped it open. He sipped it gradually, puffed on his cigarette gradually, doing usually one or the other. Beer didn’t run through his family, but his friend Bradley had a bad alcoholic family. His mom was nice, and so was his dad, but both his bad drinkers. His sister, who he rarely mentioned, had a bad run in with drugs. He never talked to her, and never mentioned her. The trucker had a phone inside. It rang as he picked it up. 'Hella….Who you? Kyler? Skyla?' Eddy felt strangely he was trying to pronounce Skylar. But how would he reach Eddy? 'You don’t want me?…..Skyla’? You want Eddy?' Eddy was confused as the trucker handed him the phone. 'Some Skyla’ dude wants you.' Eddy lifted the phone to his ear. '…..Hello?' 'Eddy, it’s Skylar.' Eddy froze. 'Skylar? Why you trying to call me? How did you reach me?' 'Long story. I saw you leave with that trucker, and I know his number…Strange. Look, come back.' Eddy tried to escape from the restaurant in order to visit his parents in Atlanta who were dying. A trucker had happened to be heading to Atlanta. 'I have to know if my parents are okay. Please dude. I can’t let them die without them knowing I found them after many months of being disconnected.' 'Fine…but are you coming to PCW?' This was a fearful question. He may not want to continue on if his parents didn’t live, but maybe he could just quit… '…Yes.' 'Good. I’ll tell you if any matches occur.' 'Okay.' 'I hope you get over it.' 'I’ll try, I must keep faith in myself.' 'That’s the Eddy I know. Anyway, I’ll see you in awhile.' 'All right.' 'Bye' 'See you.' The phone call ended with the annoying buzz afterwards. He turned off the cell phone and handed it back to Jack. Strangely, he had no beer or cigarette in his hand, very rare for him. He would get more in less then a minute though. 'Some friend?', asked Jack, the trucker. 'Yeah. How’d he get your number?' 'The dude asked ‘cuz I knew the directions outta this sh*thole and he said if he got lost he’d call me up and I’d tell him how to get out. I know everything ‘bout tha’ roads outta here.' 'Hmm.' 'So…Can you grab me a beer?' Eddy reached back and grabbed a Coors from the open red and white cooler and handed it to Jack, who popped it and chugged a whole gulp in a few seconds. 'Uh…Sir, I’m a little worried about you getting drunk.' 'Nah boy, I ain’t gonna get drunk. It takes quite a few beers to get me drunk, if ya know what I’m saying.' 'What’s quite a few?' 'Like 8. Still then I’m able to drive, yanno?' 'I trust you sir. Just don’t kill me.' Eddy meant that in a serious way, but it came off pretty funny. Jack laughed and pulled a cigarette from his pocket and lit with another lit one before putting the other lit one out. 'Sir, can you not smoke for at least an hour?' Jack laughed. 'No way. I’m smoking all I wanna smoke.' 'Smoke gets me woozy though.' 'Oh well kid. Be a tuffman. I ain’t gonna stop smokin’, ever.' 'When did you start?' '12' 'Why?!' 'Well, my mom lefta pack a cigs on the table and I went ‘Hell, it ain’t gonna hurt to smoke one’, so I smoke one, loved it, and smoked anutha, and anutha, until I went to buy my own packs.' 'They allowed kids the smoke?' 'Hell yeah. They didn’t care ‘bout kid’s smokin’ till recently, like that We-18 card B.S. and evvathing else, yanno?' '…Right. Look, I’m going to sleep. Wake me when we’re in Atlanta, if I do sleep that long.' 'It’ll be awhile, but whatevva.' Eddy pulled his seat back and put it into a lying motion. He was glad to get away from Jack, who was a stupid ass. But it was hard to get his parents off his mind. He acted like he was asleep for 30 minutes, until he acted so much he actually did fall asleep.
