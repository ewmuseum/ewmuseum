Looks Can Be Deceiving

29 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1 - 29 - 03 Only a couple hours after Dorn’s first Turmoil, things have begun to mix up in a martini of sour and sweet. Having picked up a rather easily won victory over his rival Alan Ackart and duo of nameless openers, a feeling of refreshment washes over his still-fresh body. But yet, something bothers him… it gnaws at his conscience like an annoying insect that he cannot swat away. As he sits alone in his cramped, Australian-quality hole of a locker room, he stares at the floor with a determined, puzzled look in his tired blue eyes… Dorn, quietly: It’s strange… almost suspicious. Where was my eternally great Goddess during that whole episode? Dorn: I know that blasphemous ass pirate Ackart was at the bottom of my internal tainting by use of vaginal goo, but that still doesn’t explain as to why Goddess En’Flarim was not around… He slams his fist into the wooden bench in frustration, letting out an angered exhale. The whole complexity of it all baffles him, and he can do nothing but stare at the wall, rubbing his chin. The Astronomer leans forward on his right knee and squints, surveying the deepest depths of his mind for an answer… as if it matters THAT much. Dorn, to himself: Perplexing, indeed. The mighty Watcher of All probably saw it, but why would she not intervene? Our chances at obtaining a trophy with which to increase the credibility of the Astrology faith with could have been jeopardized!” The thought of losing to Ackart and the rest of the slime he was against puts an angered expression on his face. It would have been embarrassment. It would have been humility. It would have been one hell of a loss for his first match. The shape-changing Alan Ackart tried to disrupt Dorn’s game plan earlier in the night, during the beginning of the show, but it failed, as the Astronomer came out on top! Dorn stands up, lifting his tired body, and gives his joints and ligaments a good stretch to relieve tension and the soreness. It’s been quite some time since Dorn has wrestled, so naturally, he’s a little rusty… Dorn: What I wouldn’t do for a fucking oilcan… Voice: Your griping shows weakness, Lunis Superia… Dorn, startled: AH! Cripes… The Astronomer freezes and surveys the room, looking for the source of the voice, which is obviously the Goddess En’Flarim. His wandering eyes fall upon nothing but generic locker room and his own equipment, to his surprise. Dorn: Where are you? Voice: It surprises me that you are so foolish and blind as to not notice the Dragon Goddess herself standing before you. Dorn, a little angry: Well I can’t see you ANYWHERE! If this is some kind of test, then I guess I’m failing miserably because my eyes just aren’t catching sight of you… Voice: Look closer, my Warrior. Tracer sighs angrily, but decides it’s better to obey his Goddess. Straining his eyes to nearly making them pop out of his skull, the Astronomer looks around once more. Spotting no obvious clues of her physical presence, Dorn lets his shoulders droop and gives an annoyed smirk. Dorn: Nope. Nothing, my Goddess. Voice: Quite disappointing… And at that moment, the wall before Dorn’s eyes begins to bend and twist like a piece of rubber. The very colors of the wall and shelves upon it swirl and contort along with the matter. Dorn’s eyes grow to a surprised, alarm width and he watches in awe as the wall begins to extend TOWARDS him. Like a great jello snake, the wall continues to approach. Dorn, overtaken by fear, begins to backpedal slowly away from it. The relentless glob of wall and shelf continues towards the horrified Astronomer though! Dorn, a little frightened: What in the name of Jupiter… Voice: That’s rather pathetic for the reincarnation of a mythological hero… Dorn: Huh? Suddenly, the glob of matter snaps back into its original spot, as solid as a rock. But left in its place is the great Dragon Goddess En’Flarim, standing before her startled Astrological Crusader. She brushes past him rather rudely and stands behind him. Dorn slowly turns around and gives her a quizzical look. Dorn: By the stars, what was that all about? En’Flarim: A test of your mettle, Dorn. You, being an educated Astronomer, should know that looks can be very deceiving. And you learned the lesson TWICE tonight. Dorn: Twice? You’ve only done this once, unless I had no idea it was going on some other time. En’Flarim: And that says a lot. Have you already forgotten the episode before your contest with Ackart and the other mortals? Suddenly remembering, Dorn’s face melts into an angry frown and he nods, not even looking at the Dragon Goddess. Dorn, more to himself: Of course I remember… En’Flarim: I knew of the situation and it caused me mental grief as I watched you foolishly drink that substance without thinking. The pure irony of that statement sets a nerve off in Dorn and he gives the Goddess a bewildered double take, with his jaw dropped open. Dorn: You’ve got to be kidding!! I thought it was you and I dare not disobey! En’Flarim, sighing: I’m not asking you to disobey. I’m telling you to think. The entire episode was obviously a trick, and I had predicted it. Dorn: You… did what?! The Dragon Goddess nods and crosses her thin, golden-coated arms across her chest. She gives her knight a smug, ‘You’re a moron’ look and shakes her beautiful head. En’Flarim: You disappoint me, Lunis Superia. Your victory tonight doesn’t quite make up for it either, for you were contending with worms of the lowest kind. You should have driven the head of the mortal Ackart through the ground instead of that woman. Tracer gives a saddened nod and drops to one knee, bowing his head in utter shame. Dorn: I apologize, Goddess… En’Flarim: You should be. Collect your wits, for a battle of epic proportions approaches. Dorn, looking up: What do you mean? En’Flarim: I fell upon strong psionic waves crossing the voids of the Universe while in meditation. They’re usually not that large unless something is trying to transmit a signal across vast distances. Tracer nods his head knowingly, as the Goddess has already told him much of the details of Astrology. En’Flarim: I decided to read the waves and I was disturbed at discovering that they were from Zenander, my second in command and the God of Matter. Dorn, interested, yet puzzled: Where were they going? En’Flarim: To Sheor’s region of this Universe. Sheor is off on the other side of the Universe, billions of light years from us, eradicating a pathetic race of reptilian creatures that have failed in our eyes. Dorn: What was the message about? En’Flarim, looking up: It was interesting, to say the least. Apparently, they know of my escape from the Great Star. Not having heard THAT part of the story, Dorn’s expression suddenly twists into awkward confusion. He tilts his head, giving En’Flarim a funny look, then crosses his arms. Dorn: Escape? En’Flarim: Yes. I was imprisoned in the Great Sun for reasons that are of no importance. But now I have escaped, and it seems as if the Tiamats suspect something of me. Dorn: What would they be suspicious of? Are you not the Goddess of All? En’Flarim: That I am, Lunis Superia. With that title comes power to do what I wish, and I believe they may suspect trouble out of me. Dorn, still confused: Do they know you’re here? En’Flarim: Apparently. We should be more careful. Zenander and the rest of the Tiamats have followers all their own that either do not care about me or do not even know of me. Dorn: What would happen if we met one of them? En’Flarim closes her eyes and speaks in a low, mysterious tone. It’s almost like the Goddess is trying to hide something from Dorn… but Dorn just shrugs his suspicion off. Now isn’t the time to not trust the Goddess of the Universe. En’Flarim: They could do nothing to me except alarm the other Tiamats, and while taking them on individually would be no task, taking on ALL of them would be quite the chore. But YOU… At the mention of himself, Dorn climbs to his feet and crosses his arms, somewhat worried. He has read about these Tiamats and their immense power. Being on the side of an ex-prisoner whom those same Tiamats are hunting doesn’t exactly boost his confidence… but that ex-prisoner is also the Goddess of All, the Dragon Goddess En’Flarim! Dorn: Yes? En’Flarim: I imagine they’d try and kill you. Since they can do no such thing to me, their next target would be you. Dorn, turning pale: Why?? En’Flarim: Simple guilt by association. That’s why we must be careful. And we must continue on our quest to gain followers. The basis of a Tiamat’s power is faith from his or her followers. If Zenander were to lose followers, he would lose his divine abilities. Dorn nods, but is still unsure about the entire thing. The thought of being hunted to death doesn’t really cause his bladder to hold liquid. Dorn: Is that what this wrestling thing is all about? En’Flarim, nodding: Of course. If you achieve a big enough following through your religion and myself, then we will obtain the strength required to take on Zenander. At the same time, I’m sure glory excites a mortal such as yourself. Dorn: Well, yeah… but staying alive is MORE important. En’Flarim: Worry not, for you are my champion. You just keep your wits about you and prepare for the battles ahead. The first trophy to our cause is within reach, and we must not fail! I shall accompany you when you enter the arena of battle, just to make sure nothing goes wrong. The Astronomer nods once more and sighs, feeling a little light-headed. He slowly sits down on the bench and cradles his aching forehead in his warm head, trying to get a feel for things. His life such got a whole lot more complicated. Dorn: Looks like there’s trouble ahead… En’Flarim, confidant smile: Ah… Lunis Superia? Dorn looks up: Yes, my Goddess? En’Flarim: Looks can be deceiving… [End.]
