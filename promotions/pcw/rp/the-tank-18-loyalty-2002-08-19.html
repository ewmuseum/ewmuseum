The Tank 18 - Loyalty

19 August 2002

Date: August 19 ﻿*********The Tank __18—********* Loyalty carousel carousel caurouselspin me spin me spin me spin mewhere i let my guard down is my despisiondespisioncaroline caroline carolinewin me win me win mewhen you let go of the reigns, the wheel,all hell is going to break loosemotherfucker ~Leigh Donovan Wednesday, August 14 - Day 40 Since speaking with the two Nazis, Harvey Blake and Jake Hooper, Snow has found himselfdeep in thought about the situation. Selling out Doug Hattoni, thus allowing the Nazis to gettheir hands on him, would actually clear Snow’s conscious somewhat. He hates drugs -working for one of the largest drug distributors in the United States leaves him constantlythinking himself a hypocrite, even though he tries to bury those thoughts. Then again, the thought of co-operating with scum like Harvey Blake makes Snow feel sick inthe pit of his stomach. But in this place, among murderers, rapists, and drug-dealers, it’s hard toalign oneself with anything but scum. The familiar faint odor of sweat floats through the stale air of Unit B as Snow rubs his freshlyshowered, bearded, face. His cellmate, Darryl Jarvis, hasn’t been spending much time in theircell since the night Snow beat him for a good sixty seconds until he was pried off by a pair ofslap-happy Correctional Officers. There’s still a few hours left before lunch, thinks Snow while sitting on his upper bunk. Atlunch, he’ll find himself, yet again, under the pressuring stares of the Nazis - Nazis from Unit’sA, B, and C, which all share the same lunch room. He knows he’ll have to make a decisionsoon, before Doug Hattoni realizes something’s going on. On one hand, Snow could stick with Hattoni. It would continue this war between the Nazis andthe Italians, but then again, Snow’s safety would be as guaranteed as safety can be within theconfines of these walls. With Hattoni, Snow gets everything he needs. When he eats in thelunch room, he knows there’s nothing disgusting in his food. Not all prisoners have that luxury. When Snow needs someone to run an errand for him, he knows it will be done. To the averageperson, these things seem simple, but to prisoners in the Tank, they’re privileges not taken forgranted. Then again, Snow could sell out Hattoni and allow the Nazis to move in on him. It’s people likeDoug Hattoni who make drugs, and other life-damaging pleasurables, a disturbing factor ineveryone’s life. People like Hattoni make the world worse for everyone. And he profits fromtheir suffering all the while. “Fucking politics,” Snow sighs, collapsing on his bunk. Out of the corner of his eye, he spotsDoug Hattoni passing by his cell, back from his morning shower. Surely, there are at least threeItalian body guards keeping their eye on him at every moment. And there, directly across the gaping hole between either side of the balcony, is Harvey Blake’scell. The immense one-eyed Nazi paces the floor in his cell, his lone eye fixed on Jason Snow. *** From the Office of the Warden “Loyalty,” the Warden begins, “is a funny thing. But the real issue is not loyalty itself, but thequestion of what a man is loyal to? I mean, is a professional athlete loyal to his team? Or is heloyal to the money that the team is paying him? Is a woman loyal to her man or the life that heprovides for her? And is the man loyal to the woman or what’s under her skirt?” “Loyalty,” the Warden sighs, “is no different than anything else a human being owns... it’s forsale.” *** Thursday, August 15 - Day 41 “Has he been in contact with anyone?” Harvey Blake asks as he collapses onto the bottom bunkof his cell, surrounded by his Aryan followers. “No word yet,” Jake Hooper, Harvey’s number one man, answers. “He’s still hovering aroundHattoni’s fat ass.” “I don’t have all fucking year,” Harvey hisses, peering out through the bars of his unlocked cellat Jason Snow, who’s pacing the cell balcony. “That fucker’s got to make up his mind, andsoon. Hattoni’s going to make his move before long - I can fucking feel it. He’s been itching totake me out for too fucking long now. And without me, all of you motherfuckers aredead men.” Harvey’s words are harsh, but each of the five skin heads gathered in his cell knowthey’re soaked in truth. “He knows,” Jake assures his mentor. “In the cafeteria, the looks that he gives us... he knowstime’s running out, but he’s still making up his mind.” “Alright,” Harvey sighs, rubbing the hairless section of his upper forehead. “Enough about thatbastard. Tell me what’s going on with the rest of our boys.” “Well,” Jake begins, taking a seat beside Harvey Blake on the bunk, “things in Unit E aregetting fucking worse. I hear tension’s pretty God damn high in there right now. One of ourguys had his throat slit yesterday.” “Jesus, that’s the third one this month,”Harvey turns to his young follower. “By who?” “Some fucking nigger with a razor blade,” shrugs Jake. “What more can you expect from one ofthose God damn monkeys? Anyway, they thought our guy was going to live, but he didn’t makeit through last night.” “Any retaliation?” “Not yet,” Jake replies. “Our guys have a bad itch to spill some nigger blood, but they thoughtwith the current situation with the Italians, they’d best wait for your order before they doanything.” “Smart move,” Harvey nods. “Whoever’s running the show over there’s doing a good job. Letthat get back to him - that I said it. What are the numbers like over there?” “Even,” answers Jake. “About fifty of whites, about fifty niggers. Twenty or so of the whitessupport the Aryan movement, and about twenty-five of the blacks are hardcore niggers. Butwith the war against Hattoni going on, none of our guys are buying drugs from thefucking guineas. That leaves more for the niggers.” A slick smile comes over Jake’s hairlessface. “So,” Harvey begins to put all the information together, “you’re saying that even though we’reslightly outnumbered, it’s not going to make a difference because all the niggers are stoned outof their fucking minds?” “Exactly what I’m saying,” Jake nods. “Shit,” Harvey chuckles, “we should have gone to war with the guineas a long time ago - it putsthe niggers one step closer to their graves.” “So what’s the word you want to send back?” Jake asks. “You want to pull the trigger on thismotherfucker and start a fucking riot over there?” “No,” Harvey says sternly. “Not yet. I want to wait on this for a little while. You never knowwhen a good riot will come in handy.” *** From the Office of the Warden “Loyalty is something expected,” the Warden begins. “The only problem with that, is it’sexpected from both sides of the same coin.” *** “A favor?” Doug Hattoni perks an eyebrow as he stares at the figure standing in the doorway ofhis cell. “Of course, Jason - you know you can ask me anything.” “I hear you’re still a powerful man,” Snow says as he approaches the lower bunk that Hattonirests on. “Not only in here, but also on the outside.” “This is true,” Hattoni nods. His mustache is a little thicker than usual, Snow notices, and therest of his fat face is in need of a shave. “And Jason, I consider you a friend - a close friend. And to my friends, I am endlessly loyal. Any power that I have on the outside, is also yours.” Hattoni nods to Snow, trying to sound extra formal and extra friendly - he realizes that his life isonly as assured as Snow’s loyalty right now. “It’s about my sister-in-law,” Snow sighs, kneeling down to look Hattoni in the eye. “The otherday she came to see me and...” Snow’s voice trails off. He can’t make himself talk about thestate he saw the young girl in. He wishes all of his memories of her could stay innocent. Now,with her newfound drug use, his image of her is tainted. “I heard,” Hattoni nods to Snow, putting a comforting hand on the young man’s muscularshoulder. “You would like me to do something about this...” Hattoni hesitates while searchingfor the proper word, “...unfortunate situation.” “In a sense,” Snow nods. “I want you to have her followed.” “That,” Hattoni smiles, “I can do.” “But wait,” Snow says as he rises. “I want to make myself perfectly clear. I want her followed,and followed by someone you trust. I just want you to find out where she’s getting thedrugs, and make sure she doesn’t get any more of them. Ok?” “I can do this,” Hattoni says while nodding. “Ok,” Snow says, “and keep it clean too. I don’t want anyone fucking peeking into her bedroomor some shit like that. Just know where she is and where she’s going. Don’t let her knowanything’s out of the ordinary. And most importantly, keep her away from the junk out there,Doug. I need you to do this for me...” Hattoni rises with some difficulty to shake Snow’s hand. “Put your worries aside, Jason,”Hattoni says as he puts his right hand over his shaking left, “You don’t need them any more.” “Doug Hattoni,” Snow says sincerely, “my loyalties are, and forever will be, with you.”
