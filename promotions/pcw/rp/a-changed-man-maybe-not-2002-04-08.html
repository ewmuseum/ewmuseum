A Changed Man? Maybe not...

8 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/8/02 OOC: This roleplay is not my best work, but I knew it wouldn't be. It just explains how Rudolph turns from funny and caring into pure evil. [detail withheld] In a dark room on a dark night, with no stars shining brightly through any of the fancy windows of the dank castle, Rudolph lies sleeping in his soft bed. He lies under the dark blue comforter, covered head to toe, barely visible in the light-drained area, with the only thing noticeable being the shape of a small body lying sideways on the bed. The body rolls and turns under the comforter, before finally stopping with its back on the mattress. Light mumbling can be heard from the disturbed Rudolph, as the camera pans ever closer to his jerky movements and strange noises. As if shocked by a ghost, Rudolph darts up, the comforter falling to his waist and his black hair mangled in heaps above his head. Rudolph sighs and lies back down on the bed. He tries as hard as he can to go back to sleep, but he just stares blankly at the dark ceiling above him, silent, as if thinking about something. “Damn,” he says softly. Rudolph turns his head over toward a nightstand next to the bed. “One forty eight… Damn it’s late!” Rudolph sits up slowly. “Oh well. Can’t sleep, might as well get something to eat and drink.” Rudolph throws the cover off of him, and it lands in a heap at the foot of the bed. Rudolph slowly climbs out of bed and steps onto the floor. He reaches for the nightstand and clicks on a lamp. The whole room lights up, showing the dark blue blanket and sheet on the bed in vibrant color, and the while pillow lights up. Rudolph stands in the middle of the room wearing a pair of green and blue plaid boxer shorts with his laboratory coat over it. The fancy oak walls of the room reflect all of the light on to Rudolph, and he squints his eyes in pain. He holds his head with his right hand and his face scrunches up. “I hate light in the middle of the night…” Rudolph slowly walks toward the edge of the room that cannot be seen, and the camera spins around to follow him. He slowly opens the door as it creaks lightly, and he steps out into the long hall. Rudolph walks down the long hallway toward a staircase, the camera following his every move closely. He walks down the carpeted stairway, the steps thumping under the pressure of his feet, before entering a large dark room. Rudolph flicks a switch right next to the doorway and the kitchen is illuminated. The black and white tiled floor is spotlessly clean, as are the fine wooden walls, all the counters and appliances, and the table. Rudolph walks over to the refrigerator on the far side of the room and opens it. More lights shines into the room as Rudolph’s pale hand rummages through all the food in the fridge. Most of the food is healthy, lots of vegetables and meat, some fruit, some milk, but very few sweet things. Rudolph reaches into the bottom of the refrigerator and pulls out a half-empty bag of baby carrots. He takes the bag of carrots over to the table, pulls out a chair, and sits down. Sitting the bag down on the table, he pulls out a carrot and eats it. “Carrots are good.” Rudolph gets up out of his chair and walks over to the sink on the counter behind him. He grabs a clear glass from the dish drainer to the right of the sink and fills it with water. He walks back over to the table and sits down, taking a drink of the water and putting it down on the table. The scene slowly fades away. The scene fades back in, pointing at a clock on the wall. The time is 2:41. The camera then focuses on Rudolph, who is sleeping with his head on the table. The door to the outside slowly opens, and Mason Creeper walks in slowly. He might actually be clean, if he wasn’t sweaty. He walks toward the stairs and climbs them, leaving the scene as quickly as he entered. Rudolph’s head suddenly darts off the table and he runs toward the stairway, yelling. “Someone’s in my house!” “It’s Mason,” the man behind the camera whispers to him. “Oh. I’m going crazy…” Rudolph walks up the stairs, being followed by the camera, but instead of going to the end of his hall to his bedroom, he enters the first door on the right. He walks down a creaky set up wooden steps into a dark room. He clicks a switch next to the steps and the room dimly lights up. Rudolph’s laboratory in his castle has been rebuilt. The dark cellar looks slightly different now, like Rudolph has rearranged it. On the right side of the room there is a large closet, with the double doors closed and locked. Next to the closet, there are four shelves, each with five flasks, labeled with different names that normal people cannot pronounce. On the left side of the room are many average animals in cages on the floor, some stacked on top of cages below. Rudolph disregards them and walks to the back of the room, where his whiteboard and laboratory table still sit. The table is empty, and clean. Rudolph sits down in a steel chair in front of the table, and his head thuds loudly against it. “Oww.” Rudolph slowly lifts his head off the table and turns around. He has a bolt stuck in his head. “What?” “Um, bolt in your head,” the cameraman replies. “What?” Rudolph reaches up at his head and taps the bolt. He pulls it out of his head, and it leaves just a red mark. Rudolph breathes a sigh of relief and slowly puts his head down on the table. Rudolph slowly falls asleep on his laboratory table. The scene slowly fades to black… the next morning The scene slowly fades in to show Rudolph in his laboratory, still sleeping at the table, when the door behind the camera slowly starts to open. The camera spins around to show Mason Creeper slowly coming down the stairs. The third step makes a loud creak, and Rudolph jumps up. Rudolph looks over at Mason with anger. “You woke me up…” he says. “It’s nine o’clock!” Mason replies in a gravelly voice. Rudolph gets out of the chair and walks out of the lab. Mason follows him. Rudolph turns toward his bedroom, but Mason turns toward the kitchen. Mason walks down the stairs, and the loud thunder of his footsteps can be heard faintly as the camera slowly follows Rudolph away from the steps and toward his room. Rudolph opens the door and the camera follows him through. Rudolph closes the door and sits down on his bed. The camera looks at him. “Why are you following me around in my castle? I don’t really like being watched throughout my entire life…” Rudolph sits and waits, but receives no answer from the cameraman. “If I give you a little insight on why I wasn’t sleeping well last night, will you leave me the hell alone?” There is still no answer. “Okay. I guess you don’t need to know.” The camera sits there on Rudolph. He starts to get a little frustrated. A vein on his pale forehead starts to pop out. “I said GET OUT OF MY HOUSE!” The cameraman reaches his hand out in front of the camera with a tissue and wipes Rudolph’s spit off the camera. Still, the camera sits focused on Rudolph. Rudolph sighs. “Okay. I was having dreams last night. Dreams about things that happened to me a long time ago, back when I was very young. It basically started when I was just one or two. I can’t make it out that well; it was a long time ago! I remembered fire… Hot burning fire was scorching me. My house was burning. I don’t remember what happened that well, but most people down there in Vienna thought I burned the house down, since I was the only one who got out alive. They found that the stove was left on. Well, when I was two years old, I certainly wasn’t tall enough to reach the stove. Maybe you were, but I definitely was not! Well, then they sent me to the foster home, where I met up with Amadeus. You might have seen him before… I went to a bunch of different homes, with a bunch of different families who hated me the minute they found out about the house. Finally, John took me to this very castle, and he was as crazy as I was said to be. Together, we made the lives of those snobs in Vienna who cast us out a living hell, and I intend to keep that tradition going, in John’s honor!” Rudolph’s leg kicks something under the bed continuing his speech. “Most people believe that I was responsible for the death of John, but I wasn’t. It was nothing but that damned monster that he created. I loved that monster though… I wreaked so much havoc on the city that cast us away… But he went out of control. The damned thing went to Russia, where Ivan Larsen destroyed it. Does that name sound familiar? I thought so. Now he’s dead. That’s the result of crossing Rudolph von Hackenmann.” Suddenly, a metal clawing arm reaches out and grabs Rudolph’s leg, pulling him under the bed and dragging him out from the other side. A zombie-like creature stands up with two metallic claw arms, looking an awful lot like Mr. Larsen. The camera slowly backs away near the door, but stays to watch the beating Rudolph is receiving. The claw arms, making robotic sounds, continue to punch Rudolph repeatedly in the head, while the legs kick him in the knees and sides. All of a sudden, one of the claw hands goes around Rudolph’s neck and starts to squeeze. The grip of the monster suddenly gets lighter, and the robotic zombie hunches over. Rudolph’s lifeless body falls to the floor, and Mason stands in the doorway. “I wondered what that button did…” Mason walks away, leaving the zombie robot sitting hunched next to the bed, and Rudolph almost dead on the floor in front of him. The scene slowly fades away. later that day, at the hospital The scene slowly fades in to show a hospital room, with a counter on the back wall with a sink and many hospital supplies. In a tilted bed, Rudolph lies, sleeping, but now breathing. His eyes slowly start to open, as a doctor walks into the room. He is an old, short, stout man dressed in your average medical regalia, and he has a clipboard in his hand. “Mr… von Hackenmann. I see you’re doing fine,” the doctor says to him. Rudolph takes a little while to answer while he tries to regain his composure and consciousness. “Yes. I am fine.” The doctor smiles. “That was some pretty bad stuff. Your windpipe was almost caved in completely! What happened?” Rudolph’s face turns angry. “I don’t know…” Rudolph sits up and starts to climb out of the bed. “I wouldn’t do that,” the doc says to him. “And why not?” he replies. “You might get attacked again.” Rudolph eyes fill with evil, as if something inside his head snapped. “Yes…” The doctor looks confused. “What’s this about?” Rudolph smiles. “You just reminded me of something very wonderful…” Rudolph climbs out of the bed and stands with both feet on the floor. Suddenly, he lunges for the doctor and the feed cuts instantly, leaving nothing but darkness… Just when Rudolph was starting to become an understanding and kind person, he snapped, and we discovered that his good side was just a cover up. After he was attacked by one of his own sinister creations, he woke up in the hospital, having no clue what had happened, or why he was in the hospital. But one comment by the doctor changed that, and sent him back to his evil ways permanently. How will this affect his work in wrestling and his partnership with Mason Creeper?
