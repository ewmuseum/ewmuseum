Passing Judgment

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 1 of 2002 A.D. Shanahan leaned against the frame of the office door, eyes directed out the window. Gone was that oddly alien glaze that had accompanied his stare in the past week, replaced by the familiar steely gleam that permeated the cold stare of the Boston native. The Crawling Chaos had dissipated His essence from Shanahan after Wrestle Vessel, releasing his grasp on the mortal’s soul and consciousness. In honesty the move had surprised Bruce substantially, the track record of the Elder being less than upright. He had anticipated a long and brutal spiritual battle for the retention of his will against the malevolent cosmic trickster, but to his relief the deity had gone away rather abruptly. Nonetheless, Bruce felt as if the situation were far from settled and the feel of heavenly eyes beating down on his back from beyond the stars refused to dispel. Now he stood present at the preliminary’ hearing’ where Captain Stryker was to present his case to the Board of Operations: the committee seated below the High Council on the Blackhelm Coalition ‘chain of command. Father Randall Craven, Bishop Reginald McCarver, Father Miles Terrel and Bishop Carlton Bauer formed the presiding body of the group today. They would take into account the actions of the now-notorious ‘Tropical Massacre’ of Saturday of July 27th, where a vast portion of the foundation’s resources had been lost at sea in the blundered assault. Stryker, the field commander of the mission, would give his testimony, and the board would hear supporting witnesses give statements before passing their verdict: their vouch of support when he went before the High Council, or their neutrality in the situation. The assembly had convened nearly a half-hour ago and the situation was bleak for the youthful special forces commander. Bruce broke his stare out the window, where streams of rainwater trickled down the outer pane and a steady, driving wind sent cascading sheets of rain at an angle. His eyes fell on Stryker, whose complexion was ashen and pallid. Hr hadn’t recovered from the slaughter that had taken the lives of nearly 50 soldiers and put Delta Fox’s giant, Lieutenant Ryder Slade, in the hospital. In spite of comforting, the captain’s feeling of blame had never deviated from himself. His poor decisions and hesitancy about pulling out in the beginning had cost lives, lives lost in a senseless debacle of inhuman butchery. He had lost weight in the past days, and his comparatively-frail body lacked the strength and stamina which normally marked the rough young officer. “We wish we could offer you solace, Marcus,” stated Father Terrel from across the elongated desk, running his hands absently over the official incident report. “But that is not our purpose. We are here to make an unbiased assessment of the events that occurred on the night of July 27th. 60 men, including 43 United States Marines and 17 members of elite special forces, lost their lives, and 33 others, including your friend Slade, were injured. Two multi-million dollar ships went down, and untold equipment aboard those vessels was also lost. Now we don’t need to inform you that this is a rather hefty foul-up. Among the most bungled operations we’ve ever had, and as of right now responsible for the most heavy loss of life.” The normally proud Stryker refused to lift his gaze, his empty eyes locked on the ground beneath him. It was a shame, thought Bruce. Stryker was known to be a tad reckless, but a more than capable leader in even the most turbulent of strife. What had gripped him and cost him his normally lucid decision-making abilities escaped all, especially the captain himself. “Unfortunately you’ve given us very little reason to offer you our support,” resumed Craven somberly. “Very little of your statements up to now have offered much support to your position. This is a vital blow to the limited resources of the Coalition and you’ll have to offer up some much more staunch defense than you have so far. Otherwise….well, we cannot grant you our endorsement when you go before the High Council next week…” “Have any of you ingrates ever looked one of those things in the eyes?” The board all shifted their attention from the distraught captain momentarily, their eyes collectively falling on the stocky thug propped against the threshold of the conference room. Shanahan swiveled his head to look on Stryker’s accusers, his own eyes shrouded in darkness by dark sunglasses. A mock sneer dotted his lips, his brows arched in contempt. “I asked ya a question,” he snorted again. “Craven, McCarver, any of ya….have you ever stared one of those sea-dwellin’ fiends in the eyes? Seen and felt the unearthly glow in those hellish sockets? Or even better….ya ever walked down a corridor decorated with the eviscerated remains of your peers, hanging crucified on the walls wailing death cries that would drive a sane man mad?” Shanahan winced, the massive bicep-based bruise on his left arm throbbing as he inadvertently bumped it hard against the doorframe. He looked angrily in turn at each member of the council, Stryker still unresponsive. Stepping forward, he casually removed the sunglasses and clutched them in one hand. “Hell no. It’s easy for you guys to pass judgment, you sit around in your comfy Vatican-funded offices while your crews of mercenaries and lackeys are out risking their necks and exposing themselves to demons you only read and preach about. And the fact is….sh*t happens. Sometimes your ‘God’ turns a blind eye and things like this happen.” “Don’t bring God into this, Bru…” begins Craven. “Silence yourself, Randall,” snarls Shanahan, casting an evil look at his old friend as his tone remains unchanged. “I’m sick of it. You chumps constantly proclaim that God has blessed this little organization to destroy his enemies: the Great Old Ones. Well obviously big Jehovah isn’t the tactical genius you wish he was, huh? If ya disagree, explain last week or explain the little problem in the mountain caves of Romania a couple weeks back. And if ya give me that trials and tribulations speech again, any of ya, I’ll break your damn neck.” Bruce was in a foul and bitter mood, and they all knew better than to contest his reasoning. Even Stryker had partially sat upright, focused on his advocate out of the corner of his eye. “That man makes decisions that none of ya could ever make under the circumstances he has to,” continued Bruce. “Sometimes there is no clear path either way, I can understand that. Double backlash, it happens. And other times…well, there’s no escape. I watched from 800 yards out as your precious little ‘fleet’ was overrun by Cthulhu’s hell-spawn and I don’t think even General Patton could have turned the tides of that one. Whatever was in that pit was waiting for us all. It was waiting for the ship I was aboard and mercifully changed the course of, and it was lying in ambush for Stryker’s unit. If you’re going to pass judgment and ask questions….I suggest you stop searching for a scapegoat and start pointing fingers at the real culprit. Ask your High Council.” The entire board looked dismayed, taken back by Bruce’s bold accusation. It was no secret he had been vocal about his distaste for the Council off the record. But this was the first time he had been so daring as to challenge them officially. Craven glanced at McCarver, who in turn swept his eyes in the direction of Bauer. The latter church elder pondered, chewing his bottom lip, then leaned back in his leather chair with a heavy sigh. “Come to think of it…..” (Briefly excerpted from the memoirs of Bruce Shanahan) My body still aches. I can still feel the burns, the abrasions, the lacerations from last Sunday. I can still hear the clash of metal and the splintering of timber. There has been no break, no respite. But when you’re hardcore, you’ll have it no other way. That fiend betrayed me throughout that accursed voyage; he did so all week and he turned his back on me at Wrestle Vessel, I could almost feel his energy backfiring throughout my body while Aymin and I brawled aboard the ship. The day that was supposed to be my true PCW ‘coming of age’ turned into yet another despicable setback. Every devil has his day. And if that’s the case, I’m going to show Aymin exactly what hell is like on Friday. And on this occasion, I won’t have a vengeful celestial sabotaging my opportunity to claim my rightful crown. And there won’t be any Rush Mason or Kramer Wild Wing to slow me down. Even the vile Azathoth will cringe at what unfolds at Fusion. I’m dedicating it to that archaic abomination Nyarlathotep, I sure hope he’s watching from his throne in Leng. He may learn a thing or two from this ‘petty mortal’. Cleansed in purgatory, I plunge into sweet, sweet redemption...
