Bring Me Back To Life - Part 1: The Storm

20 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4 - 20 - 03 Have you ever lost someone that you loved so much, their very essence is what drove your cells to continue to function? That person was the only one you could go to when it felt like the world was crumbling around you. Everyone hates you except them. They're all you have. And then you lose them. Your entire system goes into a shutdown. You cease to breath. Your blood stops pumping. Your brain closes its cerebral doors. In a sense, you die. You can do nothing but cry. Your emotions remain, but are fueled by nothing but sorrow and grief. What else do you have to turn to? Nothing... some would say God, but what's that going to do? It's like talking to yourself. I've tried it, and it doesn't help me. I've tried telling my friends. They merely laugh... call me weak and stupid... what great friends. No one understands. No one. My family doesn't. My friends don't. God doesn't. Here I lie... dead... and no one cares. What do I do? There's nothing I can do. ... ... Unless... ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- 'Mr. Tracer... can I see you in the next room?' The man in the long white medical blouse asked, with a hint of plea in his voice. 'Sure...' My father rose from the cushy hospital recliner and followed the doctor into the next room, closing the door behind him. The sound of the door closing sounded like an explosion to me, since the waiting room was as quiet as an empty church. There were no footsteps, no voices, and the clock was digital, so it didn't tick. A single bead of sweat fell from my forehead and I wiped it away with my forearm, sighing with slight heat exhaustion. My brother, Marcus, was seated in the opposite blue chair as me, and had his eyes firmly fixated on whatever article the Medicine Today magazine held. I could tell he was nervous by the frantic tapping of his foot against the sterile white tile floor, though because of his shoes, it made little noise. His short brown hair stood rigid as a shell of freshly applid hair gel soaked in. His plain white T-shirt stood out brighty against the yellow wall, and his black pants casted their reflection upon the white floor. Marcus was dressed like this was an official proper gathering; I came dressed like death was imminent. 'You think she's okay...?' I asked, my voice shaking slightly. My face turned red as soon as I realized what I asked, for I knew how touchy Marcus was with mother. He never lifted his eyes from magazine. I sighed again and sat back against the rough cloth of the recliner. 'Marcus...' 'What?' My brother asked roughly. I could tell he was annoyed by me already, and I hadn't said a thing to him since he arrived at the hospital. His eyes had squinted behind the magazine and I could see he was thorougly irritated, but by what, I couldn't say. 'What is it, Dorn?' I shifted uneasily in my chair, wondering if it was such a good idea to ask again. 'You think she's okay, Marcus?' 'How would I know?? Do I look like a damned doctor to you?! Fuck...' I almost jumped in surprise when his answer came out quick and full of venom. Marcus was never one to express himself very well, and Mom always said he got it from Dad. 'Why do you wait and see??' He then turned his attention back to the magazine, but I knew his mind was elsewhere. It was on Mom. The reason of our being at the hospital was probably what was getting to him so much. Hell, it bothered me nearly to death as well. Never in a million years had I dreamed that I would be sitting in a hospital waiting room, worried sick with the urge to vomit for reasons such as the ones manifesting themselves that day. You always think you're going to the hospital for a broken limb or perhaps just a check-up. Something minor. You never expect something like what had happened to Mom... She had been struck by lightning. I'm not talking the piddly ass kind that couldn't impress a little kid. I'm talking the kind that could EASILY split an oak tree clearly in half. She was outside, grief-stricken from a fight Dad and she were having, and she felt the wrath of God upon running to the middle of the backyard. I knew why she chose the back; She had a small, precious garden at the end of the fence. She loved to go out and look at them when she felt bad about something, so I imagine that's why she went out there, storm or not. I guess she didn't take into account that lightning had already blown out the power and our neighbor's lone willow tree had caught fire. I don't mean to be an asshole... but she paid for that ignorance. Now here we are, hoping and praying that she comes out alright. Even if she's not... we pray... she's still alive. 'Don't worry about it, Dorn,' My brother's voice interrupted my thoughts, and I turned to look at him. His eyes still did not leave the magazine, and his tone was clouded with false optimism. I could tell he doubted it, but he didn't want to worry me. It wasn't working. 'The doctors are doing everything they can. I'm sure she'll be fine after a couple weeks of rest.' He wasn't buying his own claims, and neither was I. Marcus closed the magazine and placed it back in the plastic holding attached to the table, then leaned back in the recliner. Folding his arms across his chest, he stared at the floor and sat silently, though I knew he had a thousand thoughts running through his head. I was right there with him. 'Marcus... what does Dad think?' My brother didn't answer and I knew why; Dad didn't care. Our parents had been fighting for so long, Dad had grown such a strong animosity towards her that he wouldn't give a shit one way or another if she were to die. The thought of that swelled a single, gray tear in my eye and I wiped it away, sniffing as sorrow-driven snot built up in my nose. What had caused Dad to hate her so much? She was such a good person... 'I don't know, Dorn. In fact, I don't even fucking care!' Marcus suddenly bursted from his seat, his teeth clenched and an enraged look in his eyes. I was scared shitless. 'IT DOESN'T MATTER ANYWAY! IF SHE DIES OR NOT! HE'LL STILL BE THE SAME ASSHOLE! YOU'LL STILL BE THE REBEL AND I'LL STILL HAVE TO PUT UP WITH THE SHIT!!' I nearly jumped through the ceiling. Was he being serious?? His labored breathing, erratic movements, and rage-filled tone sure steered it in that direction. Marcus was scaring the hell out of me at first... but then, after repeating what he said over and over in my head, I could feel the rage building within my body. What right did he have to bitch?? He was probably the worst in the family, constantly complaining and sucking up to everyone that he wanted something from. I gripped the arms of the chair and leaned forward slightly, imposing my challege of his claims. 'Bullshit!! HOW COULD YOU SAY THAT?? I THOUGHT YOU LOVED MOM?! 'I DO!' He roars, attracting the attention of the passing people, but he doesn't seem to care. 'I JUST HATE THIS FUCKING FAMILY! SOMETHING IS ALWAYS GOING WRONG! IF YOU'RE NOT PISSING DAD OFF WITH YOUR WEIRD CRAP, HE AND MOM ARE FIGHTING OVER SOMETHING PETTY! I HATE IT!!!!!!' Marcus booted the table in front of us, scattering the magazines and books and nearly shattering the glass. With that, he stomped out of the waiting room and disappeared down the blaring white corridor, with people in police uniforms close behind him. I could feel tears of anger welling up in my eyes as I clenched my teeth, trying to control myself. 'Why is he like this...' I just couldnt' figure it out. He had a house, possessions, he was getting an education, he had food in his stomach. What right did he have to complain? What gave him the right to talk about Mom like that? If I hadn't been so grief-stricken, I would have probably chased him down. In fact, the thought was slowly becoming a good idea... before that door Dad and the doctor disappeared into opened once again. I snapped my head in its direction, an anxiety building within me. The doctor stepped out... Dad was nowhere to be found. The man in the white coat stepped up to me slowly and folded his arms across his chest. 'Dorn?' I nodded slowly. 'Yeah... that's me.' 'Where's your brother?' He asked, somewhat shaky. I just shook my head. 'He's gone... he couldn't bear the wait. So what happened? Is she alright?' The anxiety and fear was growing in my body like an acidic fungus. I stood up and started rubbing my hands together, probably appearing to to be a nervous wreck. The doctor shook his head, placed a burly hand on my shoulder, and looked me dead in the eye. 'Dorn... she's dead. Your father had the plug pulled.' At that moment, my entire world crumbled in mere seconds. It was like I was a frail pane of glass, and someone took a sledgehammer to me. I could feel my eyes grow wide as I dropped back down into the seat, staring away at the floor. The doctor shook his head again, his hand still on my shoulder. 'I couldn't deny your father's request. She wouldn't have lived past a month, and if she did, she would have permanent third degree burns and paralysis in her legs.' 'So... Dad had her killed...?' My voice was hoarse and grief-stricken. I was surprised the doctor could hear me. 'I'm afraid so, son... I'm so sorry...' Sorry? He's fucking SORRY?! Sorry won't bring Mom back. Sorry won't ease the pain. Sorry won't make up for what Dad had done. Sorry wasn't the solution. I had lost the greatest person in the world to me. Whenever I needed a shoulder to cry on, she was there. When Dad and Marcus would torment me, she would defend me. She supported me in everyway, she LOVED me. And no she was gone. What was I going to do now? I would soon find out. [End.]
