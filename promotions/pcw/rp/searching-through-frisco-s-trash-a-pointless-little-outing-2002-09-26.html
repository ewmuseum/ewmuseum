Searching through Frisco's Trash: A pointless little outing

26 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/26/02 “Ah,” End exclaimed exuberantly, taking in a deep breathe of the air on that fine Saturday morning. “Get a whiff of this beautiful air.” Rex was too busy rummaging to care. Dominic couldn’t breathe anything in his rubber Level 4 Biohazard, complete with a modified scuba tank with enough air to last him through the day. Lance didn’t really care, he was too busy cursing the day he signed up for this course, and was considering changing his major so that he wouldn’t be subjected to such an assignment. “I thought you said we were going to be trolling through the park?” Ambrose whined. Ambrose, the epitome of the dumb, annoying snob, pulled his yellow Polo pants up so the hems would be kept out of the garbage, but his brown loafers couldn’t be saved. “Yes,” The End answered. “Garbage Park.” A tap upon Ambrose’s shoulder, and he sobbed for his blue and white vertical striped casual dress shirt, marked with pigeon business. Ambrose voiced his grievances in an aside to The End, not that he paid much attention. “I’m soiled with guano, it’s dirty and smelly here. These are $200 loafers! DAMN YOU AND YOUR EUPHEMISMS!” Ambrose shakes his fist at the End to emphasize his point. Guadalupe didn’t mind the assignment that day, to catalog and record the waste in Garbage Park, Tyson City’s dump. She had sunscreen over her lightly tanned face, her long black hair tied and pulled inside her collar, wearing rain jacket and flood pants and boots. She was ready to work. The others, of course, didn’t read the course packet and had no idea what they were getting into when they got on the van this morning. At least Rex had some work pants and gloves on, but he’s not here for the assignment. Yoko hadn’t even entered Garbage Park, Lance had to go back and drag her in if she was going to get any credit for the course. “Let go of me!” she said, though in Japanese and alternated with a number of curses. Lance shook his head. “Not that I’d enjoy seeing you or anyone else rolling around in filth, but you’re gonna fail if you don’t do this.” “Really?” she queried in English. “I need to start reading that course packet a little more.” With everyone who would be participating ready to go (all eight of them), The End throws everyone some notepads and garbage bags. “Thank you for passing this class,” he said with a clap of his hands, affirming that the credit for this project is crucial to the final grade for the Anthropology course. “Now, I know that you all think that garbage is gross and there’s nothing worth studying here-- Hey! Get back here!” The End realized that the students (excluding Guadalupe) were walking away, and had to call them back. The students didn’t come back, but they stopped in their tracks. Dominic responds, “But you agreed with us!” The End waves them back. “You didn’t let me finish!” The students returned, groaning and dragging their feet through the filth and muck. Rex perused the site from his eyes for whatever he might be able to plunder. With everyone back in front of him, the End turned to the side and held his arms out at the piles of junk that stretched out far and high in front of he and his students. “The garbage that we as a society throw out tells a lot about our behavior,” The End explains. 'Especially Argentinian dictators,' referring to his Friday Fusion opponent, PCW Hall of Famer and Grand Slam Champion Frisco. “Safety first,” Rex said, taking a red Swiss Army Knife out of his pocket and cutting into a 13-gallon trash bag filled entirely with used condoms and spermicide tubes. The End walked a little bit away from the group. “I want you to look through this garbage and measure and record everything you find, especially any solid gold trash containers that have the name Francisco Cassius Valerius inscribed with diamonds, and when we get back into the classroom setting we’ll compare our findings with the reported behaviors that we get through person-to-person surveys; for instance, the areas that report the lowest beer consumption often have the highest.” And so The End walked off into the distance. Actually, behind a garbage hill where Sandy Wong waited in a buggy for him. He left Garbage Park and would sit out in the van until the designated departure time of 2 PM. That left the students with a full 5 hours to look around. They wondered what looking for and through Friscos' trash would do for anything. But they didn't want to argue with Professor Rathje (The End impersonated him to get the students to examine his trash. He'll use whatever information they can find before giving it to Rent-A-Hero for his match with Frisco at Red October.) so they just did what they were told. Guadalupe immediately went to work looking through the various trash bags. As she bent down to open up a bag, Ambrose stared at her rear end with a smile, nodding his head and rubbing his hands together. He intended to go over and hit on her, but Lance just held him back by the shoulder, shaking his head from side to side. “Not a good place,” Lance said before going over to help Rex carry a perfectly good foosball table back over to his truck. Yoko wasn’t quite dressed for the occasion in her orange sundress, but tried as she could; knelt down, she reached into an open bag of trash, picking out a single banana peel between the long nails of her thumb and index finger, holding it as far out from her body as she could. “Just pick it up,” Rex said. “It won’t bite.” A black blur jumps out at Yoko. She falls back into the garbage and it scurries away. “But the raccoons will,” Rex loosed with a laugh as he and Lance passed by with the foosball table. Ambrose runs over to Guadalupe with a small prick on his pinkie finger, only one minute droplet of blood slowly oozing out of it. “I accidentally reached into a bag of discarded medical supplies! Quick, rip off your shirt so I can tie up the wound!” Guadalupe made a silent mental note to herself. “Note to self: castrate Ambrose.” A gasp from Guadalupe let Ambrose know that Dominic was standing over them with a pair of heavy-duty wirecutters. “You’re contaminated,” Dominic said, stalking toward Ambrose wielding the wirecutters in a slightly menacing way. “There’s no other option: We have to amputate!” “Whoa, whoa, whoa,” Ambrose attempted to calm down Dominic, holding his uninjured hand out to keep him back. “I was thinking more just along the lines of an antibacterial soap or something.” “THERE’S NO TIME FOR ANTIBACTERIAL SOAP!” Dominic shouted, testing the wirecutters with a few snips of the blades. “You could already be riddled with diseases unknown to modern medical science, but we should amputate just to be safe.” “Don’t touch me.” “Let’s just do this quick, it won’t hurt... me.” Dominic took another step forward and Ambrose broke out into a full run. Dominic followed, but slower, weighted down by the Biohazard suit. “I wonder what’s in this bag?” Rex asked of Lance and Yoko, standing over an odd-shaped bag that’s actually quivering. “Dunno,” Yoko said with a shrug and a sigh as she backed away in case anything else jumped out of the bag, “but the breathing and shaking isn’t going to keep you from opening it, huh?” Rex took out his knife and cut into the bag. “That would be a no,” Lance answered, taking a position at Rex’s side with a discarded baseball bat. Down the knife slices into the bag; cut completely from top to bottom, Rex ripped open the two flaps and from inside the bag Wee-Man of Jackass fame fell out of the bag, gasping for air. “AUGH!” he yelled. After taking in some more breaths, just happy to breathe air outside of the confines of the trash bag, Wee-Man turned to Rex and extended his hand. “Thanks,” said Wee-Man appreciatively. “We were doing a prank where I’d jump out of this bag and scare people, but the trash collectors came too soon and I’ve been here for the past two days. Yoko looked around. “You hear that?” she asked. An odd high-pitched sound, it sounded like a siren at first, but with its approach it the sound became more recognizable. A man screaming. Everyone looked up in time to see Johnny Knoxville diving off a tall trash hill, and they just had enough time to move out of the way. He landed hard from the fall, sinking into the soft ground. Pulling himself back up, he spits out whatever trash went into his mouth and looked over at his cohort. 'Come on, Wee-Man,” was all he mustered, then he walked off coughing with Wee-Man following, walking tenderly for being soiled all over himself. Larry and Phil, carbon copies of Moby, sorted through the trash in their matching Ghostbusters outfits, the former making the measurements and the latter recording the trash. Larry held up a bag full of beer bottles, marijuana leaves, and a white powdery substance. “Someone had a party,” Phil quipped. Larry nodded. “I wonder whose trash this is?” Guadalupe looked over at them. “That’s Bobby Brown’s trash.” Guadalupe had become the “go-to” person in The End’s stead. The End had a great time in the van listening to old Jefferson Starship tracks. Yoko brought over a bag full of bloody gloves and kitchen cutlery and showed it to Guadalupe. “Who’s trash is this?” she pondered aloud. Guadalupe gave but a cursory glance at the contents of the trash bag before she turned back to her own work and replied, “OJ Simpson’s.” By the end of the day, the students had looked through 50 bags and Rex had salvaged a foosball table, expired though unopened cans of ravioli, a copy of Debbie Does Dallas, and numerous other small items including footballs, used notebooks, luggage, and lamps. The End joined them back in Garbage Park, refreshed from his nap. “We’ve gone through a lot of trash today--” he said. The students were confused as to how the term “we” applied in this situation. The End held his hands down to the ground to the trash they were all standing on. “There’s a lot of garbage here,” he said. “YOU THINK?!” Ambrose replied cynically, still lamenting his missing finger. Rex made one last sweep of the garbage, but commented that, “A lot of this stuff isn’t worth throwing away yet.” The End nodded his head. “Right,” he agreed. “There’s trash here that can still be used, but our consumer culture convinces us that it’s no good and we have to throw it out and buy something new. But what else?” Larry raised his hand. “Yes, Larry?” “Has anyone seen Phil?” Larry asked, looking around. They had him backed into a corner, there was nowhere else to run. “Let’s split up,” he said. Worst move he ever made, taking the tunnel he found deep down into the lair of the raccoons. He saw much of how they operated, how they run the trash collection and just supervise the humans. But he saw too much. Hundreds of them had surrounded him. Showing their teeth and closing in, they prepared to strike. He looked behind him. The wall was thick, not going to move at all no matter how much he pushes at it. Wait, a gleam in the wall gave him a ray of hope. He pulled the can of gasoline out of the wall and sprayed it on the ground at his feet. “You won’t get me,” he said, striking the lighter in his pocket. With a loud “FWOOMP!” the gasoline set ablaze. “I’m safe now,” he thought. But alas, his plan did not work. The raccoons shuffled dirt onto the flames and quickly extinguished them. “Crap!” he thought to himself, his last thought as the raccoons attacked. Their weight brought him down to his knees and onto his back, countless teeth and claws ripping into his outfit and tearing into his flesh... “I’m sure he’ll turn up eventually,” The End waved off Larry’s concern. “But back to the point I was trying to make?” Yoko raised her hand. “We... found a lot of behavior altering substances and pornography.” “Right!” exclaimed The End. “And what do we know about our community?” Lance answered, “They claim not to purchase this stuff.” The End nodded and started walking back to the van. The students followed. The sun was high in the sky and making the garbage even more pungent. Everyone covered their noses. Even the pidgeons were dropping out of the sky, unconscious. “What could we do to eliminate all this trash?” The End questioned his students. Guadalupe was the first to respond with, “Better attitudes of waste and recycling?” “Have more garage sales,” said Rex, happy with the day’s collection. Lance produced a flintlock from his pocket. “Fire,” he said. The End smiled. “All excellent answers. There are many possible solutions to the trash dilemma (though no clear right answer). However, there is one thing for certain: your research will help myself and Rent-A-Hero beat Frisco!” With a “yoink!” The End snatched the notepads out of the student’s hands, climbs into the 1996 Teal Dodge Caravan and Sandy Wong drove him off, leaving the students at Garbage Park. Ambrose was very much in pain, even though Yoko was nice enough to try to sew his finger back on. Ambrose ran after the van, an attempt in vain. Rex would have offered them others a ride, but his truck was full of the junk he had pilfered from the dump. The others just stood there. “What was he talking about?” Yoko said of The End. Dominic’s oxygen supply was running low. “I can't believe he left us! How are we going to get home?“ Lance and Guadalupe started walking away to the next bus stop about three miles up the road. “Not his fault,” Lance called back to them. “It was listed in the course packet!”
