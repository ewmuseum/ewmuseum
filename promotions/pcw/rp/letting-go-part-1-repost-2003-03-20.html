Letting Go(Part 1)(REpost)

20 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3-20-03 (OOC NOte: I reposted this because I forgot to note it as being for Turmoil. Sorry for the mistake, I'll try not to let it happen again)Jonathan Drachen is sitting on a bench at a table in an empty restaurant. His restaurant. The only other person there is the cook, and he is sitting on a counter reading a magazine, not preparing pizza as his life task seems to have been ordained. The last time Jonathan had a customer was a week ago, it was a delivery. The last time he had a customer in the restaurant was longer than he cared to remember. Drachen has had to sell all of the arcade entertainment, including his pool table an the neon Drachen’s sign that went above the pinball machine that is no longer there. He’s even had to sell a few tables. At the highest point of Drachen’s business, he had 15 drivers at the restaurant for delivery and 15 employees working in the restaurant. He had been voted the best pizza in Dallas almost unanimously by Food Critics. Fans of his wrestling had come for hundreds of miles to eat at his restaurant, and left enjoying wonderful dining and entertainment, some with autographed photos and other memorabilia if they were lucky enough to meet Jonathan. However, this success train soon came crashing into a mountain with one severe beating. Jonathan did not leave his hospital bed for two months after his beating by Cassius Deamont. His business fell almost to ruins. It had to be closed, literally, for the entire duration of the time Jonathan was unable to leave his bed. Afterwards, he was extremely paranoid and deemed unable to handle the business by his employees. The responsibility of caring for the restaurant fell upon his parents, and of course, his parents paid little mind to the issue and sought to resolve it as quickly as possible. They hired a temporary managerial company to substitute for their son, without even looking at their credentials. The rest is history, and fairly predictable. The usual occurred, mismanagement which gave way to discontent among the employees, prompting them for the most part to leave, fearing that their old boss would not recover, and not wanting to toil under the idiot temp manager while they could be spending their youth under the yoke of some random chain clothing store rather than this generic scab to cover a wound to the business that could not be healed. Jonathan spent another 6 months in therapy in a mental rehabilitation center near Washington D.C. after his bouts with severe paranoia. The business basically fell to ruins under the temp and was only abetted when the managerial company got closed after an IRS audit and suddenly most of its staff disappeared to non-extradition countries all over South America. The remaining staff lobbied Drachen’s parents to close the place until Jonathan could return. His parents complied, for it would be too much of a hassle to hire another manager anyway. Drachen’s Pizza, voted best pizza in Dallas, closed its doors without certainty of reopening with them after only 2 ½ years. When Drachen was discharged from the care of his neurotherapist, Neil Farr, he was allowed to return home to Dallas. After Jonathan realized that the world he thought to be real that he was shown to be not so, was real once more, he was supposedly cured of his disfiguring mental injury. Jonathan returned to Dallas. When he returned though, he had no home. Earlier during his period of aberration, he had sold his home and purchased a high security warehouse down in the industrial sector of Dallas. His parents had disposed of the warehouse and deposited the funds back in his accounts. They made no arrangements to see that he had a place to stay when he returned home. They didn’t even say goodbye before he left. Jonathan was forced to stay in a hotel for two weeks before he could find an available apartment. He didn’t lack money though, and was able to procure a nice apartment in a neighborhood near the Galleria in Dallas. It had one bedroom, but it was certainly not without a comfortable and even luxurious atmosphere. Immediately upon his return, Jonathan resumed his business. It took him only two additional weeks after his return to Dallas to reopen the doors of Drachen’s Pizza to the public. And nothing happened from there. Now, he had one permanent employee, Antonio the cook, and a skeleton crew of part-timers that hardly ever came to work. He couldn’t fire them, for what little help they provided was better than none at all. Now, he had only one delivery boy, himself. Now the bills for the opening were stacking up, and he smelled a failure. Drachen knew the business would not last long. He had been going for two months now and barely had broken even on last month’s payments. This month, he was going to be in the hole by a lot. Antonio can see the look of consternation on his boss’ face. He leaves his post reading by the oven to go over to his employer to whom he has remained loyal despite better job offers in the past few months. Antonio sees how Jonathan seems to be disassembling the cordless phone unit in front of him, frantically searching through it for the signal that would mean a ringing tone and thus some business. Antonio feels mournful for his friend and boss, but tries to maintain a happy façade. Antonio sits down on the bench on the opposite side of the table from Jonathan. He smiles, and Jonathan momentarily smiles back and then resumes his vigil over the cordless unit. His eyes seem wrapped with fatigue. He does not even seem to remember that he has recently been gifted with the greatest honor of his life, a PCW contract. He does not seem to remember that he had taken sweet vengeance upon a fool who had insulted him. He does not seem to remember that he had planned it all, that he had used connections with a friend in San Jose to deliver the pizza, and had planned the attack and executed it successfully. All that seems to be on Jonathan’s mind, is the little blue piece of plastic and electronic components before him and all of the power it holds over him. Antonio smiles again. “Boss, you look like a heroin junkie going cold turkey.” Jonathan’s face does not make any notion of recognition. “It’s kind of similar in a way, except I really CAN’T ever use heroin again, and I have to slowly watch as the last remaining supply is wasted away by others than myself.” Antonio loses his ability to feign a smile, and shows nothing but concern. “Hey boss, what do you worry about so much? Business will pick up. Don’t you worry at all, you’ll give yourself problems in the head…” Antonio instantly feels quite stupid after saying this and hears the resound of an imagined flat palm on his forehead. He cannot bear the look Jonathan gives him after this last statement, like that of a scared cat chased into a hole by a wild pack of wolves. Jonathan says nothing due to his friendship with Antonio, but he is noticeably reminded of worse things by this comment. “Look boss, I didn’t…” Antonio stammers desperately and apologetically. He leans forward across the table as Jonathan’s neck inclines downward and then upwards to stop him. Jonathan then cuts him off, “Antonio, I know… It’s ok. Just leave me with my thoughts for a moment.” Antonio feels even worse as he looks into his employer’s pleading eyes. “All right boss, but please, try and take it easy.” Jonathan tries a smile, but fails. Antonio graciously accepts, and removes himself back to the kitchen. The sound of palm against forehead is heard a few seconds later. Jonathan stares at the phone for a couple of more minutes. He looks toward Antonio. He thinks, If only he knew that I wasn’t waiting for another customer to make an order. I bet you he had no idea I was waiting for my restaurant’s last business call. If only he knew that in a few weeks, he’ll be working in a much better restaurant for a salary he deserves. If only he knew. Jonathan sighs at this last thought and moves his hand and arm down underneath his bench and pulls his briefcase up to the table. He places it flat on the table and opens it. He looks down at the first stapled sheets of paper he sees. At the top reads: “Contract for the Sale of Drachen’s Pizza to Restaurant Management Inc.”
