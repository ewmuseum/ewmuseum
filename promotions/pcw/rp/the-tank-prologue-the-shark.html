The Tank, Prologue - The Shark

undated

Date: not entered “I want him tried,” the aging District Attorney begins, “and I want him convicted right here in California!” He slams his fist down onto a stack of papers that has been piling on his desk for weeks. “How can we do that, Charlie?” his assistant asks, “He committed the assault in Canada! We’re not just crossing a state line here, Charlie - we’re talking about taking a criminal from another country!” She shakes her head, wondering why her boss has such trouble coming to terms with this fact. “He may have committed the assault in Canada,”Charlie responds, leaning back in his leather chair and folding his fingers at his lips, “but he resisted arrest in Los Angeles - I’ll be damned if his going to rot anywhere other than a California prison.” The assistant, standing in front of his desk, turns her back to him and runs her fingers through her long brown hair, “Why is everything becoming about the election?” The question prompts a deeper silence than the one that usually has spaced their words. Charlie, the District Attorney, sighs and scratches his grey head. “I won’t lie to you Ann,” he begins, “Sometimes it’s not about what you can do for the monkey, it’s about how big of a flame you’re waving in front of his face. And in this election, I’m going to need a pretty big flame.” “And seeing Jason Snow in a California prison will ignite that flame?” Ann, his assistant, asks. “It certainly wouldn’t hurt,” shrugs Charlie, standing from his chair and stepping around to face his assistant, “Look, Ann - he’s going to prison either way. What’s the difference if he serves his time here or in Canada? The way I see it, if I can use this little situation to my advantage without damaging anything, then why not?” “I understand that,” sighs Ann as she lowers her head. She’s at least twenty years younger than her boss, and fairly attractive, “It just seems that lately every decision you make is completely based on what it will do to your chances of becoming Governor.” “Well, that’s true,” Charlie admits, “I’m not going to deny it - but that doesn’t mean I don’t still believe in all the decisions I make. I just...” he hesitates while searching for words, “I just have to shed the appropriate light on the decisions now. Public perception is the key.” Ann is silent. She realizes that Charlie is making sense but thinks maybe that’s why he’s such a great lawyer - he can manipulate the truth easier than anyone she knows. “Look,” Charlie adds, extending an open hand to her, “How many times has this state let people off with a slap on the wrist for no other reason than the fact that they’re celebrities. If people see me finally put one of these badass pretty boys away...” “Then that’ll give them more encouragement to get behind you...” Ann nods. Charlie turns back to his desk and picks up a few sheets of paper, “Arnold Lesserburg, Jason Snow’s lawyer, is coming in this afternoon to see if we can cut a deal. You’ll need your wits, Ann - Jason Snow’s a wealthy man, and he hired the most expensive shark in the pond.” ... ... ... Arnold Lesserburg, much like Jason Snow, is at the top of his profession. He’s a millionaire many times over with most of his finances coming in from wealthy criminals in need of a good lawyer. He doesn’t question if what he does is right or wrong any more. He hasn’t done that for nearly fifteen years, from the time when he discovered that there is no limit to how well he can manipulate a jury. Now, at forty-one years of age, nothing has changed. He’s got a little more around his midsection, a little less on top of his head, and a whole lot more in his bank account, but he’s still notorious as the one of the best lawyers in the nation and in the prime of his career. He started his own law firm nearly ten years ago, but he decided to take this particular case on personally when he saw that the man he would be defending is none other than mega-wealthy PCW superstar, Jason Snow. As he walks down the hall toward Charles Jefferson’s office, he considers his options. He’s heard that the flamboyant D.A wants Snow’s case tried in California instead of Canada, and has suspicions that the upcoming Governor election might have something to do with the decision. Instead of fighting it, however, the crafty Arnold Lesserburg is planning to try to turn the situation in his own favour. He’s not kidding himself - there’s no way Jason Snow will walk away from this charge without jail time. He’s already discussed that with his client, and the young PCW star has accepted the fact. The job of Arnold Lesserburg with this case is to simply shave off as much prison time as he can from the unavoidable sentence - and to get the business wrapped up as quickly as possible. ... ... ... “Lets not kid ourselves, Chuck,” Arnold smiles, “You want Jason Snow in a California prison. I want the lightest sentence possible.” Charles Jefferson eyes his opponent up for a long time - Arnold Lesserburg is by no means a handsome man, but his wit is unrivalled. A moment later, he turns to Ann and then looks back to Snow’s cunning lawyer, “Your client will serve three years. If he behaves, he might get out in one and a half - maybe even a year.” “Chuck,” Arnold says in one long drawn out breath, “Did I come all the way down here just to turn around and fly my client back to Canada?” He smiles at the spark of fear in Charlie’s eyes - Charlie Jefferson knows that he’s playing in Lesserburg’s court. If he wants Jason Snow in a California prison, he’ll have to play by Lesserburg’s rules. “Exactly what is it that you want, Lesserburg?” Charlie says in a much more formal voice than his counterpart, yet a hint of aggravation is detected, “You’re client has twice been convicted of violent crimes in the past! This will be his third conviction! You’re lucky I’m not offering an eight year term without the possibility of parole!” “Well,” Lesserburg sighs, “I suppose we could fight it out in court and I’ll see what I can beat you down to there... then again, I could always fly my client back to Canada and eat some poor Crown Prosecutor for breakfast.” He smiles at the end of the statement with confidence oozing from him. Charles knows Lesserburg is right, “Alright Lesserburg,” he says quietly, “What do you want?” “Eight months.” “Eight months!?” Charlie explodes, “I’ve got guys doing eight months for petty theft! What do you think this is!?” “Let me finish,” Lesserburg says, holding his hand up to silence the gray haired D.A, “Eight months for the assault. Another three for resisting arrest. A total of eleven months with the possibility of parole in say, five or six.” “Eleven months?” Charles shakes his head. If he weren’t backed into a corner, there’s no way he would make this deal. If Snow had to be tried to California, Charles knows that he could probably get that much out of a sentencing hearing - even up against this shark. But with the possibility of taking the case to Canada, where it probably belongs, looming over head, Charles knows that he has no choice. “Alright... alright... eleven months. I’ll get a court date set up ASAP.” “Uh...” Lesserburg leans back in his chair, staring across at the aging D.A., trying not to smile, “There is, however, another small request that my client has.” Charlie cringes, as does his assistant who’s been standing silently in the corner all this time. “A request?” “The eleven months will be served inconsecutively.” Lesserburg says firmly, knowing that this is the point which he will receive the most arguments about. “No way!” the D.A. responds, “I just can’t do that. You’re asking for the moon here, Arnold.” “Well, that’s what my client wants,” Arnold says before reminding his counterpart - “Remember, we probably shouldn’t even be agreeing to go to trial down here.” “I’m curious now, Mr. Lesserburg,” Charlie says, “if everything will go so much better in Canada than down here, why not just try the case there?” “If I had my way,” Lesserburg sighs, “we would. But my client wants to serve his term while still wrestling for PCW. It’s been done before, with Robert Downey jr. and the like, but I know there’s no way I can get a Canadian judge to go for it. I can probably beat a sentence down to four months up there, but my client doesn’t want to take four months off work. So the way I see it, Chuck, you have two choices - either you cut a deal with me for my client to serve less than four months or you let him wrestle while serving his sentence.” Lesserburg has obviously won this battle, but Charles will still get his way - Jason Snow will serve his sentence in a California prison, sent there by Charles Jefferson, Governor candidate. “Alright,” Charles submits, “Eleven months, inconsecutively. I’ll draw it up and put it before a judge by the end of the week.” “As quick as you can,” says Lesserburg while getting up from his chair and stepping toward the door, “I think everyone would like to see this mess cleaned up as quickly as possible.” He smiles at the D.A., and then adds before making his exit, “Oh, and Chuck, good luck in the election - you’ve got my vote.” Lesserburg leaves the room while Charles grinds his molars against each other. Lesserburg knew how the meeting was going to go before he stepped foot in the office. “A shark,” Charlie sighs and turns to his assistant, Ann. “Eleven months isn’t so bad,” she says, “By cutting a couple of days out each week, he’ll actually be incarcerated for much longer.” “Shorter, actually,” Charlie sighs, “Assuming he can behave himself. He’ll be paroled in four or five months, I think - but the election will be over by then.” “What prison are you going to propose he be sent to?” asks Ann, thinking of the current prison population in California correctional facilities. “Well,” Charlie says with a small smile, “That’s the one area that I have some control over. We’ll see how Mr. Jason Snow likes spending the next few months in Peterson J. Tankerton maximum security prison.”
