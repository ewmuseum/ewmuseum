The Wishmaster --- School's In (Being Informed)

5 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/5/02 'Refresh my memory on our agenda?' The End asks of Sandy Wong as they sit outside in the cold 50 degree weather. It's a completely outdoor restaurant, Carmichael's in the more affluent district of Houston, Texas. No one's there, in fact, the employees really weren't too certain why their Nazi of a boss had them work today, since they rely mostly on tips for their wages. But The End walked in so at least there's something for them to do. Along with him, he brought 20 random homeless people with him, all of them in need of a good meal. 'Here it is,' says Sandy Wong, opening her purse and pulling out a letter addressed from Darius Johnson, a student at a local high school. The End cuts into his Blackened Lemon Tuna Steak, the cashews in the rice the tuna rests atop producing crunching sounds as the knife goes through. The letter reads: Dear The End, I hear you were doing Christmas Wishes, and I got one for ya. My school is terrible. There's almost no funding for it. We've got PROBLEMS. Let me tell you. *There's a permanent yellow sticky grime on the boy's bathroom floor. There's something living in there, we don't even use it anymore. *The police arrest innocent kids just 'cause they wanna act tough, but when we really need 'em there's no one around. *They serve us green food. It's not vegetables and there's no dye. *Every staff and faculty member has 5 jobs at school and they still get one pathetic salary. They don't even like their jobs and they may not even like us. *We've got textbooks from the 1950's. *We haven't gotten new instruments for the school band in 35 years. *Our club and sports teams don't have any equipment. *If someone drives a nice car to school, they won't be driving it back home. *Our principal doesn't even come to school. *More emphasis on sexual education than literature means students think Sisyphus is an STD. *We don't have English and Spanish classes, we got “Spanglish.” *Our guidance counselors won't even see us. *Our HOSA uniforms are old prison outfits donated from the county. Now, I know that you can't really deal with any of these problems, but I think if you just came to our school, it might help morale around here. If you can, please just come for a day. Darius Johnson. At that moment, Dante Jackson walks up, covered from head to toe in a brown wool winter coat. He takes one look at The End, dressed in a blue T-shirt, jean shorts, and Nike Airs. 'What the hell is up with you?' Dante exclaims. He points at Sandy and says, 'At least she's dressed for the weather,' referring to Sandy's red heavy winter pants and jacket, her black boots go up to her shins and she's got leather gloves over her hands, 'but you....' The End swallows the tuna and says, 'I've no problems with the weather and I've never been sick to my knowledge. It's a sad thing you can't simply greet a person with a 'Hello'.' Dante sits down at the table with Sandy and The End, holding a big cup of Starbucks coffee. 'So why'd you call me here anyway? Ain't you got a Continental Title match to be gettin' ready for?' he asks. The End takes the letter from Sandy and gives it to Dante. 'I got this letter. A kid asked me to do an appearance at his school.' Dante skims the letter; 'They'd ask you and not their hometown hero?' Giggling, Sandy Wong jokingly interjects with, 'I think Yao Ming was busy.' Shaking his head, he decides to ignore Sandy. Dante awaits an answer from The End. Instead, The End just shrugs. 'They're your people. Speaking of which,' The End says, waving his index finger in a circle above his head to draw Dante's attention to the people sitting around them, 'if you want to be a hometown hero, feel free to pick up the check for your people, they were a bit hungry.' Dante Jackson blows hot air into his hands and rubs them together for the friction to warm them up. 'I didn't bring any money with me,' Dante says. The End gives another shrug. 'Fine, but anyway my main purpose for calling you here was that it's your town. If I'm going to make an appearance at the school, it would make sense to have you there as well.' 'Damn right.' 'Besides, neither myself or Sandy is completely familiar with Houston. We could use a 'tour guide', as it were.' Dante takes a gulp of his black coffee. 'Fine. I know where the school is, let's go.' The End nods. 'Very well, let me pay the bill and we can go.' The End goes over to the pay window. The window is blurred, but a woman in white comes up to the window and charges The End $569.12 for everyone who's eating. The End takes out his wallet and feels the bills in his hand. 'This should be about four times the amount of the bill. Split it with everyone here.' Indeed, The End paid the amount he said. The woman behind the window wishes him a good day and goes back to split the money with everyone there. Dante shakes his head at the sight. 'Man, you got money, and you spendin' it wrong. You gotta be hood rich.' 'I've no desire to be hood rich. The noblesse oblige, Dante. Those who have should share. Isn't that the way many children are raised to think?' Dante gives his own shrug before they leave the restaurant.
