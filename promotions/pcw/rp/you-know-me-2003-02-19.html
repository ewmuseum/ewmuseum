You Know Me?

19 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2 - 19 - 03 The pale fluorescent light of the morning sun pours forth through the dirty glass window of the small shanty and spreads across the creaking wooden floor like a great, transparent ooze. Like a sleek gliding snake, it floats from one corner of the floor to the other, enveloping everything in its way and bathing them in a blanket of sparkling UV rays. It runs over a large, marble top counter rising about 3 or so feet off the wooden floor, casting a beautiful, mystic shine over the smooth, silver surface. The shelves lining the wooden wall behind the counter glimmer in the sunlight as well, with the various trinkets moored upon them lit up like light bulbs. A lone, balding man of about 60 stands behind the marble top counter, resting his tired elbow against the surface and leaning his wrinkled head in the palm of his open hand. His round, thin-rimmed glasses sparkle in the morning sunlight as he glances down at the counter, spacing to a zone unknown to all but himself. His tight, aged lips purse with extreme pondering while his green eyes sparkle behind their veil of glass. He waits for something… but what? He isn’t sure. Day in and day out, the hours go by like minutes, the minutes like seconds. He wakes up in the morning, wondering what the day will bring. It usually brings nothing but a hacking cough and lust for life that leaves an endless void in the pits of his heart. But alas, at least he has his health. As the old man continues to space with his gaze transfixed on the intricate designs of the marble top, a sudden light pitched jingling noise emits from a short distance away. The old man’s sparkling, yet tired green eyes shift towards the door, is slowly swinging open with a long, moaning creak. Further amounts of sunlight dart into the room like rogue bandits being hunted by the authorities and create a strange, black silhouette in the opening of the door. The old man emerges from his ethereal shell of reclusion and shields his eyes from the damaging UV rays of the Great Star. The blackened figure stands within the coating of light for what seems to be no more than a second before stepping in the store. The newcomer’s black leather boots clop against the aging wooden floor, sending a reverberating thump sound through the underside of the floor. A long, black leather trench coat dons the man’s body and wafts behind him like a chained ghoul, despite the lack of breeze. A truly mysterious sight, indeed. The door shuts behind the passive intruder with a light sound, filtering out any further intrusion of sunlight. The old man behind the counter lets his eyes adjust for a moment before lowering his UV guard and giving the man a wondering look. The newcomer has medium length black hair, tied behind his head in a loose ponytail. Dark, opaque shades cover his eyes, almost like a shield against the outside forces of the world. With a stern look, hard as ice, the man approaches the counter and stands before the senior cashier, folding his coated arms across his muscular chest. “Hello…” The man hisses, his voice low with exhaustion and mystique. “I have a problem and wonder if you could assist me.” By the tone of this strange man’s voice, it sounds as if he were wishing he HAD a problem so he could sound like he’s telling the truth. The old man, however, seems willing enough to hear the stranger out. “Sure son, what can I do fer ya?” The cashier adjusts his glasses and licks his lips, a true American sign. “Well… Ed,” The stranger exclaims, having now noticed the nametag firmly pinned to the cashier’s plaid shirt. “I’m looking for a concoction once sold at this very location. A special, rare brew that was homemade and unique to his area, mainly due to the fact that the creator of such a thing died before the recipe left his memory.” The man in the leather coat smirks and gives Ed an inquiring, sly look. The old man merely twitches his nose in puzzlement and struggles to strain his aging cerebrum, looking for any possible memory of a homemade brew sold only in his own store. He comes upon nothing. “I’m sorry, son,” Ed explains, wanting to deter from any trouble. The man doesn’t exactly look friendly. “But we ain’t got nothin’ out of the ordinary. Maybe you’re just mistaken.” Ed tries to be as nice about it as possible, but the man doesn’t seem all too happy to find out that the item he’s looking for is non-existent in its original location. The man in the coat adjusts his shades and turns his head away from the old man, almost as if he’s disgusting to even look at him now. Ed can only hope the stranger leaves soon, as he continues to get strange, spiking chills in his spine… His hopes are dashed, however, when the man turns back around and has that inquiring look on his face again. “Are you sure?” “Positive,” Ed replies, his voice becoming reclusive. “That’s not possible,” Exclaims the man with an interrogative tone to his low, threatening voice. “I once lived in this area and remember that a lot of it was put away before that coal mining company decided to pay our pigeon of a mayor off and buy the town’s land.” His voice hints at apathy and disgust, but his voice shows nothing of the sort. The old man still can’t quite comprehend. He’s lived here at least 30 years, and has never heard of some unique homemade brew. It’s very possible the recipe was lost with the owner and the remains of the concoction consumed, but surely this young man would know that. “Still, something strange about him…” Ed mutters to himself, within his own mind. True enough, the guy does seem rather peculiar. What’s he want with a weird, homemade brew anyway? Ed doesn’t know, and more yet, doesn’t want to find out. Noticing Ed’s still puzzled look, the man in the trench coat sighs irritably and leans in closer, obviously trying to intimidate the frail cashier. “Look, I’m not stupid. I know it’s here. My father used to buy it all the time when he’d go out on raids for his bosses, so don’t try and tell me it doesn’t exist!” The man’s snapping nature somewhat startles Ed, but the old man holds steadfast. “Who is your father to be wantin’ some homemade brew, boy?” “What business is it of yours?!” The man growls, hissing hints of savagery through his straight, pearly white teeth. Ed nearly jumps in response to such a quiet, yet maddened outburst, but still remains with his feet firmly on the ground. What could honestly be holding him down now? “None, I was jes’ wonderin’…” Ed’s frail, shaky voice begins to trail off with a loss of thought, not wanting to accidentally say something wrong and have the large stranger snap on him. He had seen such things, and wanted none of it. “What is this concoction, anyway?” The coated man lets his lips curl into a sly, unforgiving smirk at the old man’s question. He clears his throat slowly, almost as if he’s trying to stall from explaining, but eventually lets the proverbial cat out of the bag and explains just what the stuff is. “It’s a painkiller, Ed. A homemade painkiller. Seeing as how my dad was a part of the Special Forces team SWAT, he needed all he could get.” As if he’s been hit with a maul of memory +5, the old man perks up with a puzzled look on his face. Having lived in the town for quite some time, he faintly remembers a member of the SWAT team living here. It begins to spark subliminal flashbacks in his fading mind, bringing back visions of perhaps meeting the man once or twice… … and his son. Now seeing the mysterious figure in a new light, the old man’s green eyes begin to widen with amazement. The cloaked figure can do nothing but stare with a confused expression planted firmly on his clean-shaven face. Ed’s face contorts to a strange, quaint little smile. He remembers the little boy perfectly: Shy, withdrawn, and always looking up at the ceiling. His father would usually come in during the night, and the night sky seemed to captivate the young boy. Ed can only smile upon remembering the scenes, and this draws an even bigger confusion out of the man before him, presumably the young boy all grown up. “Hey,” The man exclaims, a little irritated. “What’s going on??” Ed looks at the man and continues to smile, “Nothing… “ And at that moment, he finally remembers the young boy’s name. “…Dorn.” At the mention of his name, Dorn’s face twists into a amalgam of confusion, irritation, and pure surprise. He carefully plucks his shades from his eyes, revealing a crystal blue glow in each of them, and stares at the old man. How did this old man know him? Did he watch PCW? Did he recognize the greatness that is Dorn Tracer? … but what’s with the smile? “How do you know who I am?” The Astronomer inquires, a little more than curious. Ed keeps his smile going and turns his eyes away, as if hiding something. “Oh I know more than your name, Dorn Tracer. Don’t YOU remember?” Though severely agitated by this whole ordeal, Dorn decides to play along and attempts to remember the little convenience store he’s at. The name eludes him and so does the previous owner, but he somewhat remembers coming in once or twice in his teen years to grab some cigarettes for his friends. But how did this guy know him? Dorn surely did not remember him, and seeing as how the guy’s mind must be fading from age, how could he possibly remember Dorn? The questions begin to eat away at the Astronomer’s sanity, urging him to burst out in a line of questioning to find out just how this old man knows him. It wouldn’t bother him so much if Gods weren’t hunting him. “No sir, I don’t…” Dorn hisses quietly, trying not to seem like he wants to rip the old man’s head off his shoulders. “Why don’t you tell me?” “Sorry son, no can do!” The man then lets out a hacking laugh, almost as if he’s taunting the Astronomer. Dorn raises an eyebrow and purses his lips into an irritated smirk, trying to control himself. The old man continues to sound like a beaten horse as he laughs, finally cutting off after a minute or so. “Nope, sure can’t! Guess you jes’ ain’t getting no help today! Ha ha ha!” The man’s heckling does little to stem Dorn’s anger, causing the Astronomer to burst out in a momentary fit of fiery rage. “FINE, YOU PIECE OF SHIT! I’LL GO ELSEWHERE! TAKE YOUR KNOWLEDGE OF ME AND DIE OF A 90 YEAR OLD PUSSY-INDUCED DISEASE!” And with that, the Astronomer whips around and goes lumbering out the door like a walking, breathing storm. The door bursts open before him, letting the sunlight in once more, then shuts just as quick as Dorn disappears on the outside. The old man finally begins to calm down and wipes his brow with a handkerchief from his back pocket. After getting his breath back, Ed replaces the handkerchief and shakes his frail head, a look of amusement on his face. “Damn fine luck the boy didn’t demand anymore than he did… or his daddy would have had my head.” Ed then looks to a glass casing beneath the marble top counter and notices a shoebox sitting on the lower white shelf. Taking it from its resting place, he carefully brings it to the top of the counter and sets it down, letting the dust clear from its cardboard edges. With a heavy, anticipating sigh, Ed carefully pulls the lid from the box and reveals many dusty little trinkets within, possibly from his childhood. The thing on top of the pile, however, is what interests him. A worn piece of parch paper is folded neatly on top of the pile of stuff, almost like a beacon of interest and mystery. The old man’s frail, shivering hand reaches to the note and carefully pulls it from the box. With cat like delicacy, Ed opens the note and reads the words of the letter he received 3 years ago: “Dear Ed, Listen, I need your help. My son is obviously heart broken by the death of his mother and has run away to join the men-in-tights circus. This is unacceptable; I can’t have him making a joke out of the Tracer name. When my term of duty is up, I shall find him and bring him back home. But something tells me he’ll be back before I find him. If so, and IF you meet him, don’t let him know I’m looking for him. No one, especially Dorn, but yourself must know of this. I want him taken by surprise… he’ll learn some respect. I guarantee it. - D.T. And with that, the old man folds the paper once more and replaces it back in the box. He turns his time-beaten gaze to the far wall of his store and just stares, a look of sadness beginning to roll over his wrinkled face. “Poor kid…” [End.]
