Date: August 24, 2002 ﻿*********The Tank __20—********* Vicariously Shattered On a daythat my eyes describe horror to my mindthat my tongue can not describe to you,I wonder where you’re always going.I wonder why you leave me alonewhen I know how much you care.But you’re blind -your eyes can’t conceive the horrorand your soul explodesin front of mine. ~Leigh Donovan Wednesday, August 21 - Day 47 It’s a few minutes past nine in the morning when Snow decides to pay a visit to his employer,Doug Hattoni. Fresh off a win over the Chef last night on Turmoil, Snow has had his match upat Third Dawning on his mind all morning - it’s his chance to win the Continental title. However, while forced to float with the stale air between these concrete walls, he realizes that hecan’t avoid the facts of life in the Tank - business issues. He’s eager to find out the progress in the favor Hattoni promised him - having his sister-in-law,Krista, followed to make sure she stays away from drugs. When he gets to Doug Hattoni’s cell, he finds the only occupant is Hattoni’s cellmate, TimMcDonald - Snow has never trusted the man known as Slick Timmy. His bleached blonde hairis spiked and he’s wearing his usual button-up short sleeve shirt. At the moment, he’s sitting onhis upper bunk, facing the head of it. To Snow, he appears to be reading a book. “Reading?” Snow asks with a perked eyebrow. Startled, McDonald slams the book shut and turns around to find himself under the heavy gazeof Snow’s always intense blue eyes. “Jesus,” McDonald mutters. “I thought you were a Cock.” Tim has quickly become adept to the slang of the Tank. ‘Cocks’ are the colorful term that theprisoners use for Correctional Officers. “Where’s Doug?” Snow asks. “Shower.” “Good book?” Snow raises his eyebrow again. He doesn’t think Tim McDonald much of areader. “Better than you could imagine,” Tim says with a sly smile before waving Snow forward. “Come here.” Cautiously, Snow approaches the little untrustworthy man as the book is reopened. As soon asSnow glances at the pages, everything makes sense - Tim McDonald is bagging powderedheroine on the open pages of the book. “Subtle,” Snow says with a smirk. As much as he hatesdrugs, running with Doug Hattoni’s crew means being exposed to them. “I know,” smirks Tim. “I’m a fucking genius.” “You know what’s going to happen to you if the Cocks catch you with that shit?” Snowwhispers, leaning in close. “They won’t catch me,” McDonald chuckles. “You’re sure of that?” Snow responds with a sarcastic smirk, thinking Tim McDonald is toococky for his own good. “Fucking positive,” smirks McDonald. Snow’s eyes narrow in curiosity before Tim beginsexplaining. “Money runs this place, Lion. You should know that by now. How the fuck do youthink Hattoni’s still kicking after all the throats he’s had slit over the years.” Still smiling,McDonald looks up to see the expression that Snow can’t hide with his blue eyes -disappointment. Snow doesn’t enjoy working for a drug dealing killer. McDonald continues,“Benji Graham, you know - the head C.O of the Unit... we’ve got him under our fucking thumb. And when that big bald bastard speaks, the whole God damn Unit listens. Lucky for us, the onlything Ahab obeys is fucking green!” Snow looks over his shoulder, out through the half opened bars, and down to the main floor ofUnit B - Benji Graham is standing tall behind the control desk, watching like a hawk over hisUnit. He’s a rather large man and quite round around the mid-section. His hair, what’s left of it,is curly and greying blond. What Snow has always noticed about the man, however, is the darkpink scar that begins at the outside ridge of his left eye brow and travels the whole way down hisface, neck, and finally disappears beneath his collor. The scar is the basis for his nickname -Ahab, as in, Captain Ahab. “You mean,” Snow starts, pointing to the tough old man, “that cruel fucker works for us? Since I got here, he’s done nothing but jump down my fucking throat.” “He’s that way with everyone,” McDonald snickers. “But trust me, when the chips are down, hisship sails under the Hattoni flag.” Snow shakes his head, still in slight disbelief that a man like Benji Graham could be so corrupt. Then again, this place could corrupt a saint. “This fucking place,” Snow sighs with contempt. “I suppose Ahab even brought the fucking drugs to you.” Tim McDonald laughs as he cuts the final line of heroine and scrapes it into the tiny white bag. “It’s not that simple,” McDonald responds. “I mean, he keeps us on our toes about surpriseshakedowns, but he’s not brave enough to actually walk through the Unit carrying our...products.” “Then how the hell do you get so much of the damn stuff in here?” “The mail,” shrugs McDonald. “The mail?” Snow says in disbelief. “The same mail that the Cock’s inspect every shred of?” Tim McDonald smiles, “I’ve got a cousin Cock in the mail room. He handles all my shit, andDoug Hattoni’s shit, personally. Hattoni’s had a man in the mailroom for years, but of course,he wouldn’t be comfortable moving too much product into the Tank on his own. However, withtwo guys in the mail room, we could sell this shit by the fucking truckload and no onewould know a God damn thing.” A lot of information has just passed through Snow’s mind - a lot of information that hepreviously wouldn’t allow himself to know. Stunned by the corruption, he takes a seat on thebottom bunk. “If the Warden ever found out about that, we’d be completely cut off from mail.” McDonald chuckles, “Like fuck. It would just be monitored better - by two or three peoplemaybe.” Tim tosses his book down to the floor and leans over the bunk to stare Snow directly inthe eyes - he’s testing Snow like a naked woman tests the temperature of her bath, and on somelevel, Snow knows it. McDonald adds, “How do you think Hattoni has so much cash lyingaround? I mean, the drug trade in here is great, but out there...” McDonald’s eyes gesture to theworld beyond the walls, “the possibilities are endless.” Snow says nothing. He’s content to silently take in this education. “A lot of Hattoni’s cash comes from the outside,” McDonald says, hopping down from his bunkand reaching under Doug Hattoni’s, which Snow is currently seated on. “You see, Doug has athing called ‘taxing’. Someone in his family fucks up, and he taxes them.” Tim pulls a cigarbox from under Hattoni’s bunk and opens it. Beneath several expensive cuban cigars, there restsa large unorganized stack of cash. Beneath the cash, the corners of a white envelope are visible. “I’m not sure if I follow,” Snow says, a little awestruck by the amount of money he’s looking atin a prison cell. “They have to pay the fat fuck,” McDonald says with a roll of his eyes. After his comment aboutDoug’s size, he’s sure to once again check Snow’s eyes for response - nothing. “It’s like this -Doug can’t go around asking anyone he pleases on the outside for money. I mean, sure, they’dgive it to him, but no one would respect him anymore. Instead, he waits until someone fucks up. When that happens, he sometimes kills them, and sometimes taxes them.” Snow is beginning to follow, but Tim can see the young lion doesn’t completely grasp theconcept so he pulls the white envelope out into full view. “Here’s an example,” he begins, watching Snow closely. “This prick here,” he says, waving theenvelope in front of Snow’s face, “slapped one of Hattoni’s cousins or some shit. Hattoni foundout about it, but instead of having him whacked, he taxed him. The guy’s a drug dealer, so nowtwenty percent of his income lands right here in Doug Hattoni’s cell.” “And your bought-off mail men let this slip through inspection just like the drugs...” Snow says,putting it all together. “Exactly,” says McDonald. “We can only pull out so much from our prisoner accounts everyday. But this way, every time someone pisses off Hattoni, we get paid in exchange for thefucker’s life. Beautiful concept, really.” Snow shakes his head, “You can take the Doug out of the mob, but you can’t take the mob out ofthe Doug.” “Cute,” Tim McDonald snickers sarcastically. He then gestures back to the envelope, makingsure Snow gets a good look at it. “This fucker just sent fifteen hundred. He’ll send another withthe same amount next week. And you know what’s the kicker? He was a fucking nobody on thestreets before he slapped Hattoni’s cousin.” “Why did that change?” Snow’s eyebrow perks. McDonald knows now that the young lion ishooked. “Because now that Hattoni’s taxing him, he wants to make sure the fucker’s making enoughmoney to be taxed,” shrugs McDonald. “So Doug actually sets him up with drug deals,” Snow says, a bit disgusted by the entire system. Somehow, deep within, Snow can tell that Tim McDonald is pleased with his disgust. “Nowyou’re getting it, Lion,” Tim says with an evil smile. “You see, this guy, this,” McDonald pausesto look inside the envelope, “Gino Lopano - the best thing he ever did was smack that bitch. Now he’s got the fucking life. He pulls in damn near ten grand a week.” McDonald pausesagain. This time, when he continues, he speaks slowly to assure that Snow hangs on his everyword. “Gino Lopano has it fucking made.” Snow looks to his left, directly into McDonald’s eyes. Somehow, after this conversation, hetrusts the slick-looking man less than ever, and something inside him is sure that Doug Hattonishould trust him less than anyone. *** From the Office of the Warden “I’d be lying if I said the Tank wasn’t corrupt,” the Warden begins. “There are those of us whotry, day in and day out to come here and ‘rehabilitate’ the men that we deal with... but whilewe’re trying to do that, the same men that we’re trying to rehabilitate corrupt the men who aretrying to do it. It’s like a war - a power struggle. Everyone’s trying to get troops to come over tothe other side.” “Benji Graham is sitting on the fence. The prisoner’s call him Captain Ahab, like the characterin Moby Dick. Twenty years ago, he fought three prisoners. He was unarmed, and was able tothwart an attempt to slit his throat - the blade however, did travel up the side of his face. Oddlyenough, all three prisoners involved died within the month... we never found theirkiller.” *** Thursday, August 22 - Day 48 Harvey Blake glares through his lone blue eye across the crowded lunchroom, directly at JasonSnow. “We’ve got to get that fucker,” he says to his right hand man, Jake Hooper, who literallyalways sits on Blake’s right. “If only it were similar,” Jake says with shaking head. “Fucking Guineas,” spits Harvey Blake. “All they know is crime, but in here, that works forthem. Snow iseating slowly with the rest of the Unit B Italians, sitting to Doug Hattoni’s right. The onlymember of the Italian gang that’s missing is Tim McDonald, who’s Doug Hattoni’s protege,despite the fact that he’s not Italian himself. “We’ll bring them down, Harvey,” Jake says with a confident nod. “You’ve brought down theniggers single handedly in the past. These fucking goombas won’t be a problem much longer.” Harvey nods, but stays silent. His lone blue eye remains fixed on Jason Snow’s bearded face inthe distance. “Listen, Harv,” Jake starts in a tone that indicates he’s about to change the subject. “The shit inUnit E is about to fucking explode. One of our guys is in the infirmary rightnow with broken ribs. ...They want you to give the order Harv - they want to start a fuckingbrawl.” “Listen to me!” Harvey Blake says sharply, turning around to face his young protege. “I run thisfucking gang and no one lifts a hand to those God damn jungle bunnies until I let themknow.” “But we can take them, Harv,” reasons Jake. “Those fucking niggers in Unit E are so hopped upon heroine, a brawl wouldn’t last twenty minutes. They want you to give the order.” “And I told you,” Harvey says in a voice that’s losing patience, “I’m not giving the order. Not until the time is right.” “Jesus, Harvey,” Jake says in harsh tones of his own. “How long can we let the situation simmerbefore some nigger kills another one of our guys in there? I know we don’t live in UnitE, but our guys are our guys!” “No!” Harvey responds, his voice growing a bit louder. “They’re not our guys! They’remy guys! And you let whoever the fuck is running my guys in Unit E know, thatthey’re to sit in their fucking cells until I give the command. I don’t want them startingshit! If one of our guys does something, J-Thug’s going to have his niggers jump uswhen we least expect it. As of now, they think they have us right where they want us.” “Don’t they?” “Be patient, son,” Harvey says as he turns his attention back to Jason Snow. “You have torecognize an ace in your sleeve when you feel it.” Little does Harvey Blake know, his words are not only reaching the ears of his fellow Aryans. Sitting at the table directly behind him, among many of the older prisoners in the Tank, is noneother than Tim McDonald - he’s been intently listening the whole time. *** From the Office of the Warden “Sometimes in life,” the Warden begins from behind his desk, rubbing his bald head, “a manthinks and believes so strongly in something, that he completely forgets about the outside world. Nothing exists to him... no possibilities... no options...” “But then, when someone takes the blinders off, they see everything at once. And suddenly,everything they thought they knew is worth precisely jack shit.” “And right now, you think I’m talking about people you see on television. Maybe people youhear about. Maybe you think I’m talking about Harvey Blake. But what if I’m talking aboutyou?” *** Saturday, August 24 - Day 50 The sound of footsteps echos off the thick walls as Jason Snow and Geoffery Ranking, Unit Bmanager, walk down the long hallway toward the open visitation area. Snow looks a bitconfused, but Rankin’s expression is undeniably one of dread. “What’s going on?” Snow asks as they stop at the door and Snow is patted down by a C.O. “When you said I had a visitor, I didn’t think you meant in here. I thought guys in max securityweren’t allowed visitors without the glass.” “Well,” Rankin says in a quiet voice as he nervously runs his fingers through his thick brownhair, “ordinarily that’s true. But these are special circumstances...” Rankin’s voice trails off - hedoesn’t want to say anything more. “What the hell is this all about?” Snow asks, narrowing his eyes at Rankin. When the C.O isfinished patting Snow down, Rankin gestures for Snow to enter the visitation room. Snow peers into the room before entering - it’s much more cozy and quiet than the heavilyguarded room that he’s used to. And of course, the biggest difference is the fact that theprisoners aren’t separated from their visitors by a bullet proof pane of glass. There are tables setup instead, where prisoners and their visitors can comfortably have a conversation and maybe,just for a second, make everything seem normal - seem real - again. Seated in the center of the room is Krista - exactly who Snow expected. She’s beautiful, hethinks in a fatherly way - that makes everything so much more dangerous for her. She knows he’s there, but she keeps her head down and her eyes fixed on the surface of thetable. Immediately, Snow knows - something’s wrong. It wasn’t so long ago that Snow saw Krista last - she was stoned out of her mind andconsciousness. He hated her on that day. She threw what was left of her innocence away, andwith it, Snow’s trust and respect. He cares for her more than most men care for their sisters-in-law. He would die to protect her - kind of like a father. “Krista,” Snow says gently as he takes a seat across the table from her. In this moment, heleaves everything the Tank has made him behind. He has to bring out the Jason Snow that Kristaknows and loves now. “What’s going on?” He was so angry with her when he found out she was using drugs on that day. He remembers hisrage vividly. He was glad the glass was between them on that day. At twenty-four yearsold, Snow still has no ideas what he’s capable of when he loses control of himself. “Krista,” he says again, reaching across the table for her hand - so tiny... so fragile... She doesn’t respond save for a small shudder. Her glassy eyes stay glued to the table. They lasttime her eyes were glassy, it was because of a chemical she had injected into her body. Thistime, the glass is made of tears. “Krista,” Snow tries again. He can’t avoid the burning question any longer - “Please tell meyou’re off the drugs.” Quickly, Krista nods, and Snow breathes a sigh of relief with closed eyes - Doug Hattoni’s manmust have done his job well and invisibly kept the drugs away from her insteadof the other way around. When he opens his eyes, however, he finds tears streaming downKrista’s face. Her mouth is pursed in an attempt to hold back a wild display of wailing. “You can tell me,” he whispers. “I can’t,” she finally speaks. Her words are shaken nearly to the point of inaudibility. “You can,” Snow reassures her. “You can tell me anything.” “You’ll be mad,” Krista whines a whisper. “You’re always mad...” Snow leans back and sighs - she’s right. This year has been a horrible one for both he and hisfamily, and they’ve all seen the worst of him - especially Krista. He loses his temper most oftenaround the people he cares about. “You have to tell me, Krista,” Snow says gently. “I know I get mad... but I’m trying my best.” Snow leans forward and takes her hand a second time. “Krista, it’s a hard life in here. Please,don’t leave me in here worrying about you without even knowing what I’m worrying out. Please, Krista, tell me...” Snow is near tears himself now. He can’t help but think of how much he’s hurt this poor girlover the last few months - and she cares for him so much... like a father. He was all she hadwhile growing up, and he’s turned on her time and time again. Now, he’s asking for forgivenessone more time - he’s asking for her confidence in hopes that he can handle whatever terriblesecret she’s holding back. Before she says another word, he remembers the dreadfully pained look on Geoffery Rankin’sface when they were walking down the hall. He tries to ready himself for horrible news, butcan’t possibly prepare for the horror that’s about to strike. “I...” Krista hesitates. Snow squeezes her hand tighter - she needs it. “I was raped.” Snow stares at her for a long moment while the news penetrates his brain, his heart, and his soul- crushing them all. ...raped... ???Raped??? !!!RAPED!!! There are no thoughts, only instincts. Snow leaps up from his seat and stares at Krista inshocked and shattered horror. “This can’t happen!” he says quietly before screaming, “THISCAN’T FUCKING HAPPEN!” His scream leads Krista to wail, tears flowing freely and wildlydown her small and contorted face. Suddenly, a thought penetrates Snow’s rage... he remembers the last words that Harvey Blakespoke to him: “You’re going to regret this decision. You mark my fucking words.” Snowhad taken those words as an idle threat at the time - now he can see just how serious the one-eyed Nazi was. “I’ll fucking kill him!” bellows Snow, making more of a spectacle of himself in front of theother visitors and criminals of a less dangerous variety than the group he’s used to. “He’s afucking dead man!” Snow’s eyes are glazed with blue fire as he picks up his chair, runs to theside of the room, and smashes it against the wall - the metal legs break free from the plastic seat. Geoffery Rankin can no longer stand idly by and watch Snow’s outburst. He stomps into theroom and points to the out-of-control lion. “Grab him!” he says to the C.O’s waiting just outsidethe room. “Get him the fuck back to Unit B. Hold him in my office until I get back!” That, however, is easier said than done. When approaching Snow, the guards immediatelynotice his wild eyes - they don’t know what he’s capable of. To their surprise, however, Snowquickly agrees to go back to Unit B. “Yeah boys,” Snow says with a sick smile on his red face,“Lets go back to Unit fucking B. I’ve got some business to take care of.” With that, Snow putshis wrists forward, is cuffed, and lead out of the visitation room. “Remember,” Rankin yells after them, “keep him in my office! Don’t let him out of yourfucking sight!” Krista screeches uncontrollably in her seat, not quite sure of why she’s crying. She needed Snowto be comforting just now, yet like always, he lost control of himself. His actions of a momentago are tearing her apart inside now, as she holds her soaking face in her hands. Her eyes burnand hurt from tears that should have run out long ago. Geoffery Rankin is compelled to comfort her. “Listen,” he says as he sits next to her. He’s liketo take her shoulder, but he doesn’t know how she will react to physical contact from a strangeman just now. “I want you to stay right here, ok? I’m going to send you to a councilor that wehave here - she’ll be able to help you. Maybe I’ll send Jason up too.
