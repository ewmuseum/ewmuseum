An Insight

22 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 22/02/03 The drive home had been long and arduous. There were no direct roads linking the remote hillsides of the Moors and the M1 and so, with seemingly expert control over the situation, Brook guided the pair along poorly surfaced, unacceptably bumpy and pothole marked roads, not even big enough to be marked on the most concise of maps. Conversation had been kept to a minimum throughout the journey. Lacuna, who had sapped all of her available energy while fighting her way up the rugged hillside, quickly fell deeply in a sleep aided by the comfortable leather interiors and the heat from the air conditioning. Brook had considered putting on the radio; four hours navigating endless country roads had grated perilously on his nerves, but waking Lacuna could well be the death of his left shin or, he shuddered to think, his family jewels. So, they had driven in silence towards Lacuna’s little slice of heaven. It could well have been a little slice of heaven if you imagined heaven to be cold, wet and draughty with a seemingly endless maze of corridors and empty rooms. Anyway, Lacuna liked it even if she was still to own enough things to have every room filled with something other than dust. She had often thought about this. It would have been nice to be able to furnish three houses the size of small villages in three separate countries. She hardly lacked the money to do so, it was just the time and, well, effort. She was far from lazy, well, sometimes anyway, but that isn’t the point. She occasionally went through stages where anything other than sleeping was on the ‘to-do’ list for the rest of the month, but usually she was the kind of person who would do something just to stop herself from thinking too much. People who know her, the minority that they are, shudder in unison upon the idea that Lacuna should be allowed to let her mind roam. Oh, she was intelligent; maybe overly so at times; she was just of… the imaginative sort. She awoke as the electrically operated iron gates that marked the entrance to her grounds opened with a sickening sound, like nails on a blackboard. She made a mental note about that as the car roved effortlessly up the smooth black tarmac through her picturesque gardens and forest. After the incident regarding the vandalism that destroyed her ‘shed’, Lacuna had asked for a step up in security. This meant that large areas of the fence had currents of electricity running through them and the guard dogs had been leashed in areas of particular danger. She didn’t mess around where other people came in to play. And now the house came in to view. To call it a house is like telling an old age pensioner that those flat caps are in fashion and that they are in danger of becoming ‘hip’. It’s just plain wrong. It’s about the size of some small villages; three floors of magnificent Georgian architecture with bay windows and ludicrously high ceilings. Most of the outside was decorated in gargoyles and wonderful archways, which reach towards the brooding grey sky with the magnificence of Paris or Rome. The size of the place was awe-inspiring; a true beacon of what can become of a normal person, the hope that making a success of yourself isn’t quite as hard as it looks. If Lacuna could do it, then why cant Average Josephine? The car pulled smoothly to a halt as the tarmac gave way to rough, multi-coloured shingle, sending a minor splash from the puddles that had formed on its surface and had not had chance to soak into the soft ground yet. Lacuna climbed out gently and elegantly, like a true figure of class, and surveyed the state of her once immaculate Rolls Royce. Mud had been flung wildly up from the wheel rims and had tainted the sleek black beauty of a machine with its dirty brown. “Set Jones on cleaning duty right away, don’t let me see Vlad in that shape again, understand?” She rattled off to Brook as if she had had it planned all along. Incidentally, Vlad was the name of the car. She liked the personal touch. “Will do, Lacuna, nothing but the best from me, hey?” Brook climbed out of the car and beckoned towards one of the large bay windows, just as a man dressed in the traditional black garb of a member of the Debris household appeared, complete with car cleaning equipment. She liked things to run like clockwork. Lacuna headed up to the door with long elegant strides, avoiding the puddles neatly. The door in itself was an impressive site, thick oak garnished in varnish with a beautiful(if that is the right word) gargoyle doorknocker and a red and gold twine bell-pull. The door was opened as if telepathically by two men in the black clothing signifying her workforce. The inners of the house were even more spectacular, yet Lacuna did not have the patience to examine it again. She raced through the lengthy hallway without moving her eyes away from the static, straight forwards position that portrayed the sheer focus of her emotions. She was not sure what had brought it on, maybe she was recovering from the sleep; as in still within its whispery grasp over her senses. She discarded her coat, and it was caught before it hit the mahogany floor by one of the guys who opened the door. She kicked off her walking boots as the sound of a Hoover picked up in the background, no doubt removing the dried mud from the Chinese rugs. Things were sweet. Another oak door greeted her at the end of this massive room, this time less decorative and more to serve its purpose as an internal door. She pressed in hard with a well-muscled right shoulder and heaved at the heavy door, forcing it open with a scraping. The room she entered was dark; heavy black velvet curtains at the far end of the room had managed to block out all of the light from encroaching into the room. “Caw” Croaked a voice from somewhere in the smaller room (by no means small, naturally) “Quoth!” She nearly screamed, she loved Quoth too much, well, too much to hint at sanity anyway “Caw” Replied Quoth from his cage at the far end of the room, by the curtains. Quoth is a raven. And not just any old Raven either, Quoth the Raven is the most prised possession of Lacuna Firenze Devries, and also nearly as important to her as Brook. Quoth’s opinions were put on nearly as much of a pedestal as those of her middle aged friend. Call it odd, call it insane, but this is Lacuna; nothing is as it seems. She walked slowly to a large white refrigerator and positively glowed in the light as she opened it, removing a bag of what can only be described as what they are: eyeballs. This transparent plastic bag was stuffed to its upper limits with eyeballs, which we can only presume to be bought from the butchers. With this bag in hand, she crossed the room and approached the golden cage of Quoth the Raven. “You hungry Quothy?” She motioned with a hand towards the bag and the raven flapped around excitedly “I bet you are, baby, but Lacuna has got a busy day ahead of her you know?” “Caw! Caw!” Whether in agreement or not, Quoth was getting more and more flustered by the second. “Those inspectors are coming soon, and I have to get back into training. I have to fight again. You like fighting, don’t you Quothy?” her tone was one of mild amusement, as if she knew that if anyone saw this then she would be labelled as mad. Big deal. “Jade is still following me around, you would have thought that beating her three times in the same match would have taught her a lesson, right? I mean, we tagged together, but she wasn’t half a lazy git. I suppose that’s American’s for you, hey Quoth? “Caw” Quoth agreed, still with his beady black eyes darting to and fro the bag full of eyeballs. “Fine!” she sighed dejectedly “You can have some food. You just don’t want to talk to me nowadays do you? She opened up a small hatch in the brilliant golden cage and tossed in a couple of eyeballs, leaving Quoth to peck away happily at the mismatched eyes while she crossed the room and placed the bag back into the fridge. “I’m… talking… to a raven? Meh, it’s been worse” she shrugs lightly and closes the door, heading back out into the magnificent hallway.
