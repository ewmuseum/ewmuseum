Behind the Ego of Jason Snow: Family and Relationships, Chapter 3 - The Dramaqueen

Date: not entered “I’m not going back,” he tells her, never taking his eyes off the road, “I’ll call them later and withdraw from the tournament. This is my place right now.” Krista is overwhelmed with happiness, sitting in the passenger seat of the mustang Snow rented when he came back to Canada (He left his Viper in Los Angeles.). “My sister will be so happy,” she says, smiling. Krista will be happy too. It’s always nice to have a protector around, even though he often goes too far with it. “I’ll have to call PCW too,” Snow sighs, and then adds, “Let them know I won’t be around for a while.” Krista nods. Everyone wishes that Snow would take more time off. They don’t understand why he’s constantly on the road - working - when he already has enough money to last three lifetimes. Sometimes Snow doesn’t understand either. “How long has it been since you’ve been at my parents’ house?” asks Krista, her voice laced in curiosity. She knows that Snow is not fond of her parents, but that’s always been something left unsaid between them. Krista stays with her parents during the summer, when not on campus. Snow has confessed to his wife, in the past, that he wishes Krista would stay somewhere else during the summer. “So you’re really not going back?” she turns to him and asks. “Really,” Snow sighs, nodding, “It’s time to get my priorities straight.” ... ... ... “What do you mean he can’t see her?” Krista asks, a stunned expression slapped on her pretty young face. The older woman is silent - she lights a cigarette at the kitchen table of her kitchen. This is Snow’s stepmother-in-law. He thinks of her as a horrible woman, and for once, most people agree with him. Cooly, she stares at him and blows smoke in his general direction on the other side of the new age style kitchen. Obviously, this is the home of Snow’s wife’s parents. “Well?” Krista demands an answer. “You heard me,” says her stepmother before taking another drag from her cigarette. Snow smirks, wondering why this old woman is always trying to play the hero when her actual intentions are villainous. She lives for confrontation - moments like these, where she gets to step up and play the leader of the guards, protecting her stepdaughter princess from the evil monster, Jason Snow. Sitting there with that so obviously bleached blond hair, those yellow teeth, and that pot belly that she tries to hide under loose clothing - he wishes she simply didn’t exist, and sometimes, he can’t believe that she does. “Where is she, Monica?” Krista demands - it’s so rare to see her stand up to anyone, let alone her stepmother. Monica breathes in a deep sigh and flings a few ashes off her cigarette, shaking her head in amazement that Krista is so persistent. By morning, she’ll be telling anyone who will listen to her about how Krista turned her back on the family. Snow feels it rising. Down in the pit of his stomach, the monster bubbles its way up into his blood stream. He soon finds himself glaring at the older lady. “Lets go, Krista,” he says, recognizing the signs of danger. He takes a deep breath as he turns toward the door, reluctantly followed by his young sister-in-law. As he reaches for the door, Monica speaks without turning to him, “And if she listens to me, it’ll be a long time before you see her again.” She takes another drag from the cigarette. She just couldn’t let him walk out, could she? She just had to push him as close to the edge as she could. She wants to play the hero again - she wants to play the victim? Snow turns around slowly, his teeth clenched tightly together - he wants to make her regret it. He steps over to her and stands above her, his shadow drowning her. She doesn’t look intimidated; This is exactly what she wants. “You want to keep her away from me?” he says to her quietly, through clenched teeth, “Hide her from me? What’s next? You’re going to talking her into suing me for alimony?” That didn’t work the first time, and he knows that his wife would never walk down that regretted road again. Monica looks up at him coldly - she lives for these moments, “I’m not so sure she wants to see you,” she says, letting her voice trail in a way that he knows something hurtful is coming, “It is your fault she lost the baby, after all.” She did it - he knew she would do it. She has just said that little thing that she knew would make him crack. He doesn’t think about the comment - he reacts to it. He lashes out, kicking one of the kitchen chairs across to the other side of the room; It stops with a crash against the refrigerator. “Kenny!” Monica yells to her husband, Krista’s father. She looks up at Snow with excited and fearful eyes. She’s excited about the story she will get to tell in the morning about how the evil stepson-in-law is crazy and she’s heroically keeping him away from her stepdaughter. She’s fearful, however, because she’s starting to understand that Snow’s lunacy might be more than just something she’ll create in those stories. “Jason,” Krista says firmly, taking him by the arm, “Lets go.” Krista understands now why Snow wanted to leave in the first place and wishes she had pushed him out the door as soon as he’d mentioned it.“What’s going on?” asks Krista’s father, standing in the doorway that separates the living room from the kitchen. Krista knows his presence is just another ingredient in the recipe for ‘Disaster Soup.’ “What the fuck does it matter to you!?” Snow explodes, turning around and stalking over to Kenny - an average looking man with dark hair and a thick mustache, “What are you going to do about it? You never had the spine to stand up to that bitch,” he bellows, pointing back to an offended Monica, “how the fuck do you expect to stand up to me!?” Kenny is speechless. He’s the exact opposite of his wife - he fears confrontation. He hates it. Maybe that’s why he’s so submissive in their relationship; That’s what Snow has always thought, at least. Now, standing nose to nose with Jason Snow, the lion, he realizes that Snow is right - Kenny will not stand up to Snow today. He will not stand up to his wife when she screams at him after Snow leaves. “I should just put you out of your fucking misery,” Snow says coldly, shaking his head while eyeing the older man over. “Jason,” Krista says, her voice growing more urgent. He hears her this time, “Lets go. We’ll deal with this some other way.” ... ... ... Things are silent on the drive back to Snow’s house for quite some time. Krista doesn’t blame Snow for his actions, but she wishes the entire scene hadn’t been necessary. She wishes none of this had ever happened - one tragedy sparked a chain reaction, it seems. “I don’t want you going back there,” Snow says suddenly, breaking the silence. “How can I do that?” Krista asks with depressed sigh - she needs a place to live during the summers after all. “You’re going to stay at my place,” Snow says without emotion, “I’ll need someone to take care of Haley, anyway.” Krista looks to the back seat where Snow’s young daughter is sleeping soundly. They had dropped her off and picked her up at Krista’s grandmother’s house before and after the confrontational visit with Snow’s in-laws. “What about all my stuff?” Krista asks, surprisingly willing to give the idea a chance. “I’ll send someone to pick it up,” Snow says, his voice showing signs that his anger is still lingering, “You can leave it there and I’ll buy you new stuff - I don’t care! All that matters is that you don’t go back there. They’re not good people.” Krista thinks it strange that Snow has chosen himself to judge good people from bad people, but she knows he’s right. “Wait a minute,” she says suddenly, realizing the message between his lines, “You need me to take care of Haley? Where are you going to be?” His silence speaks a thousand words this time. “You’re going back?” she asks in a meek voice. She wants to be angry with him, but can’t force herself to be. She’s forgiven him for a lot recently - if she were going to stay angry with him over anything, she wouldn’t be in a car with him right now. “I have to,” he sighs, “And what does it matter anyway? What am I going to do here? Wait until that b-...” He hesitates - he doesn’t like cussing around his sister-in-law, or females in general, “...Wait until your stepmother allows me to talk to my wife? God only knows what she’s telling her.” “I know,” Krista reluctantly agrees.
