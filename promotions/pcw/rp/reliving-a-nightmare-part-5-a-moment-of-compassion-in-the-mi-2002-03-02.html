Reliving a nightmare, part 5: A moment of compassion in the midst of madness

2 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3-2-02 Ripper walked out of the diner as if nothing had happened. He left two police detectives lying on the floor in puddles of their own blood. He didn’t wear a smirk, smile, or any sort of emotion on his face that said he was happy or proud of what he had done. He simply did what he felt at the time was the right thing to do. Isn’t that what we all do? Trying to choose between what’s right and wrong. Hoping beyond hope that we are right, then why should you or anyone else hold Ripper’s actions against him? After all he just did what the rest of us would have done, the right thing. As Ripper stepped out onto the sidewalk he looked around hoping that he wouldn’t spot any backup police cars. He was, he thought, in enough trouble as it already stood. If he had gotten into another altercation with more police officers he felt he wouldn’t be able to justify his actions. When he was certain no other pigs were around he began walking down the street. He turned into an alley as he heard a car coming from behind him. He didn’t dare turn to see if it was a black and white. He just made a quick duck into the shadows of the alley. He had taken this route a few times before. Therefore knew that he could get to his apartment taking this course. Although it would take him half an hour longer than his normal route, but he felt he could use the time to clear his head in the brisk night air. As he walked he thought of his upcoming match this Sunday at PCW’s pay-per-view entitled March Madness. What a fitting name. Ripper, himself, was fighting the madness. The madness that surrounded his everyday life as well as the madness that whirled inside his head, he fought each day against it all. He had been finding it hard to tell which thoughts were his own and which belonged to the freak that dwelled inside his head always hiding someplace deep within. Every passing day made that madness even harder for him to figure out and sort into some kind of reality. At least what seemed like reality to him? But then again who says anything is real? Who decides what reality is? Is it different to each person? To each culture? Ryckman was unsure of the answers. He only knew that the questions filled his thoughts. As he came near the exit of the alleyway he was met there by an old homeless man. His clothes ragged and tattered. His face unshaved in months and dirty from the ground he slept on and lack of water to bath in. He was rather thin from a lack of food, a sort of man that most people would call a wine-o. He reached out towards Ryckman, seemingly unable to form any coherent words, as if he hadn’t talked to anyone in ages. As it could have been he hadn’t had a listening ear in ages. His eyes were gray, wrinkled, and appeared cracked around the edges. Ryckman could tell that this was a man that had fallen onto hard times, a man that could have been just like you and me. Yet here he was homeless and sleeping in an alley. He was fighting for another day of life, fighting for another meal so he would have the strength so he could fight on for another day. Another day in this miserable life we call existence. Ryckman stopped and peered down at the old man. As he did so he caught a glimpse of the pain this man must have been going through. He caught a glimpse of what this man’s life must have been like. He caught a glimpse of the man’s knowledge that he would never be able to pass on. Ryckman did what so many others failed to do. This beast of a man did what most with hearts of gold would never do. He stopped and knelt beside the man who sleep; on his bed of concrete. He knelt and listened to the man’s worries, his pains, and what was in his very soul. To Ripper this wasn’t a show of compassion or a way to show his good will towards his fellow man. This was Ripper doing what he felt was right. Something most people never do. In a strained and weakened voice the old man looked up at Ripper as if peering into his soul. “Young man.” Ripper knelt lower to listen. “I would like to ask you for something.” Still Ripper stayed willing to listen to the old man. “I see many people everyday. They walk by my bed and look down at me in disgust. They spit on me and call me names. They look at me as if I’m less than human. Most of them treat their animals at home better than they do me. They cross the street to avoid me. They throw their change down at my feet as if that may keep me away.” The old man stops and begins to take in a few heavy breaths. Life was hard on him and it showed on his face and in his wary bones. Yet, he was human and deserved to be treated as so. “All I ever want is for someone to sit and listen to a tired old man. To treat me as if I wasn’t the lowest form of life left on earth.” Ripper sat beside the old man and looked at him without any sort of emotion. His face showed no sadness, no look of being bored, or even in a hurry to go anywhere or to get away from this poor old man. “Life has dealt me a tough hand to play with. I keep getting the feeling I should just fold and walk away. But each time something happens and I just can’t let go. At times I sit here and dream that some young punk would come by and kick me and beat me until I faded away. But as of yet no such luck has found it’s self to my doorstep.” The old man went off into a state of coughing that Ripper didn’t think would end. After a few moments and the old man regained composure the two of them heard police sirens somewhere near by. Ripper checked either end of the alley fully expecting a black and white to be searching for him. But his luck was holding so far. The old man seemed to ignore the sirens but his words portrayed otherwise. “Every time I hear those sirens I wished they were coming to find me. Wishing that they would find me cramped up in my cardboard home, stiff as a board from days of decay. I know you may find me rather startling with my choice of words. But what does a man such as myself have left to live for or to look forward too. Another day scrounging for leftovers from people who waste more food in a day then I eat in weeks at a time.” The two men sat there for about another hour. The old man talking and Ripper he just sat there never speaking a word. He just sat there as if lost in the old man’s world. A word of hate and disgust for a world he no longer knows or understands. The old man had seen the days of when people cared about people to days where nobody will even take a moment to listen to an old man’s war stories of life. After awhile the old man just stopped talking. He had fallen fast asleep in the mist of his first conversation in who could say how long. Tonight at least the old man would sleep with burdens released and perhaps with new thoughts of life when he woke. Ripper quietly stood and knelt over the old man. He was laid in an awkward position. At a closer inspection Ripper found that the old man had just one leg the other was amputated at mid calf. What a hard life the old man had endured. What a hard life even more people like him have to look forward too. Ripper felt as though he could do this man a service. He felt as though he owed this man something. The only thing he felt he could do was ease this man’s pain. He felt all that he could do was to allow this man to sleep and never wake to such a damning world again. Ripper placed his hands gently around the old man’s neck. With a sudden twist of the wrist Ripper released the old man from this world. His body went limp and Ripper imagined that the old man had brushed the edge of his soul when he left his body. Ripper knew that the old man was released from his pain and sorrow. Ripper knew that some people would cry out that his actions were unjust. That he had done the wrong thing. That was nothing more than murder. But to Ripper it was the right thing to do. He could have made no other decision. Nothing else would have been the proper thing to do. He may be a psychotic freak that many people felt was insane beyond anyone’s wildest dreams. But Ripper had just felt his first sort of compassion most likely ever. Yet that compassion led him to take a man life. Was it the right thing to do? Was it the wrong thing to do? And whose should make the decision of whether it was right or wrong? Ripper stood and walked out of the alleyway. He turned up 54th street upon after a few blocks he found another small diner. Perhaps he could get a bit here without any interruptions by some pig coming over and asking him questions. Once inside he sat at a booth near the back facing away from the entrance. He felt more secluded that way. The waitress walked over and handed him a menu. She smiled at him and asked what he’d like to drink. Just water he replied. After all he had a match coming up and he didn’t like any unhealthy chemicals or such in his body. He asked her where the restroom was located. As she went off to get his glass of ice water with lemon he went to the restroom and washed up some. Coming back out he noticed the waitress at the counter filling a small to go box with leftovers. She glanced up at him and smiled as she continued doing what it was she was busy with. He found his seat and his water that she had already brought over. After a few moments she returned with a pen and tablet ready to scribble his order. As he looked up at her he then turned to the box sitting on the counter. She noticed his attention on the box. “We have this old man come in here every night. We give him what we can. You know to do what we can to help those unfortunate.” Ripper nodded as she smiled at him. He pointed out something on the menu. She wrote it down then took the order to the cook in the rear of the diner. As she walked away Ripper began to think of what had happened earlier. Of how certain actions can touch so many other people. How the old man’s passing on will affect the waitress. How his actions affected to police detectives. Of how he had affected his ex Emily. Yes actions do have effects on people. Sometimes in odd and mysterious ways. As Ryckman waited for his meal to arrive his mind began to wonder. He thought about everything that has happened since he signed his name onto the dotted line of a PCW contract. He stalked a reporter, had scared his shrink half to death. Now apparently his ex was having nightmares about him again. He did send her that note. But he hasn’t been near her house. Not as of yet anyway… He has been questioned by cops twice this week already regarding this whole situation. He knew that by next week it would die down. After all he had taken every precaution as to make sure the note wouldn’t and couldn’t be traced back to him. They would soon drop the case and soon realize that perhaps the woman was just crazy. After all she had dumped him she had to be crazy. He had a match last Tuesday on Turmoil. His PCW debut against Steve Johnson, a match Ryckman would rather forget. It last all of a minute. The ref had disqualified Johnson for a low blow. Ripper knew Johnson had gotten himself disqualified on purpose. It was obvious that he was afraid. Then who wouldn’t be? At least he had a chance to get his hands on Johnson again. This Sunday at March Madness Ryckman would get to face Johnson one more time. Along with two other opponents one in Jay Sylo a guy that appears to be just as crazy as himself. Ryckman feels that this guy other than him has the best chance at winning this match. But Ryckman didn’t plan on giving him that chance. His third opponent was none other than PCW Rising Star Champion Lance Bell. A man that had just won the title not long ago from Red Rock, he may have won the title but the odds are stacked against him retaining. Ryckman was knocked out of his daze by the arrival of his food. The waitress nodded at him and smiled as she walked back to the counter. He had just taken a bit of his meal just as someone walked into the dinner. Ryckman wasn’t about to turn around and see who it was. Instead he just continued to finish his meal. Who ever walked in walked up to the counter and began talking to the waitress. After a few moments the person began walking in his direction. He tried to ignore them. But they had stopped just beside his table. He continued eating as thoughts of who the person was began running wild in his head. The first thought that jumped into Ryckman’s mind was the police. They had found him again. But this time what did they want. Either something to do with his ex or they had found the body of an old man lying dead in an alley. Ryckman knew his time had come. He knew that what was about to happen would have a big impact on his PCW career not counting his foreseeable future. As much as he wanted to turn his head and look up at the face staring down at him he couldn’t bring himself to do it. He fought it off. He ignored them. He wanted nothing to do with them. He was just fine sitting here enjoying his meal. Most likely his last meal as a free man… Your destiny stands before you… “AAHHH, SHIT” Ripper said aloud. The damn voice was speaking and he could no longer deny who was standing before him. He had to look at the face. He had to face his fate. He had to face his destiny. He lowered his fork and knife. Slowly turned his head to the side and looked up. It couldn’t be… How… It couldn’t be possible… He didn’t think he’d ever see this person again… But he was and they were standing right here in the flesh before him…
