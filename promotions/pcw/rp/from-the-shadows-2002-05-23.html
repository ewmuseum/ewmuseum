From The Shadows

23 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5-23-02 INTRODUCTION: This starts and ends around the same place as 'Subtle Deceit,' yet is through the eyes of Trigger. Thoughts of deception emerge from the shadows, and nobody is safe from the wrath of the Infinite Warrior... 'I wonder why he wanted these,' the Infinite Warrior thought to himself as he stood in line at the drugstore. The latest issue of Time magazine, a box of coloring crayons, a pack of gum, and an odd brand of juice that he hadn't been able to find. Why did Minion ask for Trigger to purchase these items? He said it was for the training, but Trigger couldn't match the two subjects together. Most of all, it had seemed, that Minion wanted Trigger to leave… why could that be? He had left early in the morning by The Insane One's request, and was hurrying to get the errands done so he could get back to the abandoned shop where the WITs lay sleeping. Today was their special monthly reward, and he was excited for them. The Infinite Warrior had great things planned. Trigger took a glance at his digital wristwatch. 8:00. This was taking longer than he had hoped for, and the Infinite Warrior wished the line would move quicker, as he was anxious to get back. He still didn't know, however, why he was doing this. Trigger was the main trainer in charge of the WITs… after all, it was his idea to begin with. Lance Minion was more of the backup that got the supplies and did the busy work. Why now did it seem as if their roles were reversed? Trigger sighed with relief as the person before him departed, and the Infinite Warrior moved to the front of the line. He placed the items upon the counter, and the elderly man tending the register calculated the price. 'That will be 9.57,' he said, without a hint of emotion. Trigger reached deep into his pocket to pull out a wallet and the produced the cash, a ten-dollar bill. The cashier snatched the money, opened the register with a ding, and placed it inside. He then pulled a paper bag from below the counter, and quickly arranged the products into it. 'Have a nice day,' he announced, handing Trigger the bag. 'Strange how it is,' the Infinite Warrior thought as he left the store, 'that there are people in this world that are barely alive. They continue to live, barely functioning, barely thinking, barely knowing that they are even here. Strange.' The store was only half a mile from the abandoned shop, so Trigger had decided to walk. Trigger liked to walk, mainly because it gave him a chance to slow down and think. There were many things on his mind right now, and it was hard to stay under control with so much anxiety. The last few weeks had been hard on the Infinite Warrior. He knew the error of his ways however, despite his constant attempts to convince himself otherwise. He had let down his guard. He had opened himself up not only to the Warriors In Training, but to his long-time rival, Minion. He and Minion had recently began to get along… which from Trigger's perspective was not a good thing. If something was wrong, it usually involved The Insane One in a big way, and he had no doubts that it was the case here. On top of his weakness and rising suspicion, there was nervousness concerning Trigger's impending trial. Ever since he had learned of the case, the Infinite Warrior had been ashamed; traumatized with the fact that such a harmless act could lead to such a terrible outcome. It was over a month ago now, back when the WIT training was still new, and Trigger's strictness was fresher upon everyone's minds. The silent motto was 'Disobey and be punished,' a concept that the Infinite Warrior truly took to heart. He dished out discipline to all of the WITs, but mainly on the receiving hand was disrespectful WIT 3, a man that took demands from nobody. Trigger wad determined to change this, no matter how cruel and unusual the penalty must be. Cruel and unusual it was, when in an event that shocked even the Infinite Warrior himself, Trigger locked WIT 3 inside the trunk of a car, then proceeded in driving him off a bridge. WIT 3 had returned a week later, fairly unharmed, but he had definitely been put in his place and did not resist again. In Trigger's mind, that was the end of it. The third WIT had revolted, and he had been punished. Why should things continue on from there? Well for a while, it was a thing of the past, but the past has a habit of sneaking up on you when you least expect it. For the Infinite Warrior, that came a week ago, when he was charged with First-Degree Assault and Attempted Murder. Now there was no way out; no escape from the treachery that was becoming his life. He had to face a crime he had clearly committed, yet was not considered a crime in his personal book of law. All great things must vanish, though, and Trigger knew this for a fact. His life had been going great… everything had been going great… but now everything was about to be sucked down into the gutter. Nearing the end of his thought process--and journey as well--Trigger approached the abandoned shop where the WITs stayed. It was a little after 8:30, and the Infinite Warrior was a tad impatient as he entered. Today was a day that he could throw his troubles aside and take the WITs for an adventure. Trigger honestly enjoyed being around the WITs, because in a way they brought back his youth. A youth drenched in bad memories due to misdirection… He walked to the back and opened the door to the WITs' Quarters, expecting to find his three pupils hard at work. Instead, he found…nothing. He looked around more closely. Still, there was nobody there but him. 'How could this be?' he thought to himself. 'The WITs aren't supposed to leave their room without my permission. They have behaved perfectly for two months. Why have they suddenly disappeared?' This was especially odd, since it was the day when the WITs were to get their monthly reward. He had told them the night before… why now would they leave? Trigger thought they should be overwhelming with joy. Could they be hiding? Training was very serious, but perhaps the Warriors In Training were playing a joke on him. He backed out into the main room, heading towards the most probable hiding spot: the closet. He crept slowly up to it, and then in a single movement flung the door open and looked inside. All he found was an antique television and dusty cleaning supplies. Now the panic began to really set in. Where could they possibly be? 'WITs? Where are you?' he tried calling to them, but no response came. 'WITs? Minion? Is anybody here?' Minion! His name shot into Trigger's mind so quickly it hurt. He didn't have a clue to what was going on, but he had a terrible feeling that The Insane One had something to do with it… As the fear and panic faded away, anger arose inside him, eating away at his heart. His solemn stare turned into a fearsome glare as the beast within gained control of Trigger's emotions. As the seconds ticked by, he became more and more sure that wrongdoing had been done. He tried to calm himself, knowing that the only way to find his WITs was to use his sense of reason. 'If Minion wanted to win over the WITs, where would he go? A restaurant? A resort?' Trigger racked his brain for answers. 'No, he wouldn't bring them there. The Insane One isn't the kind to impress others with cheap gifts and lush rewards. If he wanted to please someone, Minion would get inside their head, and then use their greatest accomplishments, aspirations, and fears against them.' This opened a whole new door to his theorizing. What had the WITs accomplished? No real accomplishments quite yet. What did the WITs fear? Almost nothing. Then there was only one thing left: what were their dreams, hopes, and desires? Trigger slapped himself across the face. The answer had been right in front of his eyes all along. 'I'm going to the Primetime Championship Wrestling local offices,' he announced aloud. With that, he dashed out of the abandoned shop, praying that he was not yet too late.
