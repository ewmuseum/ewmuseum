Confession

10 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/10/02 Churches are powerful places. The only realms where humans truly feel safe....within their walls, with the light piercing through stained glass. A place of God. I walk inside now. Quite ironic....you may think one such as I would be adverse to such a place. But I am not. I find it rather comforting. Even humans, have their beliefs. Even the lowest of the low have things they hold on to. Things that hold them together, as one. It is quite honorable. A black cloak covers my burned face, as I walk amongst the congregation. Though I am capable of seeing their secrets, into their very souls, I do not. I have seen them for over two thousand years. Rather, I walk along and let the light, the wonderful, golden light rain upon me. Past the statue of the Virgin Mary, her eternal face. I reach out to stroke her cheek briefly. And then, onward. To my destination, a small booth nearby. Confession. It is good for the soul, I hear. I take a seat, looking downward as the small panel slides open. My voice, my voice like the low rumble of thunder issues forth. 'Forgive me father, for I have sinned,' I speak. 'What troubles you, son.' Briefly I look up to take in his face. Old, though not enfeebled. A bit of white hair surrounding an otherwise hairless scalp. Neatly combing what he has. I refrain from examining his eyes, lest he see me for what I am. I seek no confrontations today. 'A great trouble, father. But I fear I do not know how to express it in proper words.' 'The simplest, most straightforward is often the best,' he answers me. 'Simply speak it, there is no judgement here.' I almost laugh at the statement. There is judgement everywhere....I should know. For so long, I have been judgement incarnate. For so very long. 'Very well father, I shall. But I hope you know of what you ask.' 'I do,' he replies. 'I have seen much in my day.' Again I nearly chuckle. But something in his voice stops me. The hint of truth. Even though his lifespan may be several times shorter than my own, comparatively, he truly has heard and experienced many tales. It is admirable. 'Father, I am a demon upon the earth.' Silence greets me. And then, he replies. Surprising me. 'Quite a few demons have crossed my path.' I had honestly forgotten how nice it is to be surprised. 'For many, many long years I have wandered,' I continue. 'I pass judgement on humans. I strike them down.' I expect him to quote scripture here, to disgust me by reading verbatim from a book crafted by liars and hypocrites. I expect a 'judge not lest ye be judged'. But again, he manages to surprise me. He simply nods, understandingly. And asks me a question. 'Why do you strike them down?' My answer comes naturally. For it is one that is true to my heart, and disgusts me to the core. 'I see their sins. I see their dark thoughts, their wants....their evil. In each and every one of them....I see what they actually are. And because of this I must kill them. ' Once again, the dreaded silence. Now will come the shouts, the oh so funny crucifix to drive away my evil. Now.... Now, he calmly speaks again. Asking another very, very good question. 'Is it your choice to see their sins?' 'What do you mean?' I ask. 'The ability to see the dark side of humanity....is it something you choose to do, or is it thrust upon you?' I ponder him, this old man behind the thin wall. And I reply earnestly. 'It....is very much as if their thoughts are my own. If I struggle I may push them away, ignore.' Beneath my hood, a sad and bitter smile crosses the corner of my lips. 'As I am doing now.' The priest regards me solemnly. 'Why do you push away your terrible gift now? Surely....' I cut him off in mid-sentence. I do not wish to hear. Not today. 'I'm aware father,' answers I, 'that even you must have your dark side. But I have lived long....for once, I simply wish not to know about it.' He nods, seemingly understanding. In spite of myself I feel an odd kindred spirit with this man. I shake it away. 'What else is troubling you, my friend.' Friend. Now I do chuckle. He doesn't call me out on it though, merely awaits my answer. 'You are bright to realize something else rests upon my soul, father.' 'Like I said, I have done this for the majority of my life,' answers he. 'One develops a knack for these things.' Indeed they do. Indeed they do, I think somewhat sadly. 'Recently,' I continue, 'I find myself in battle with one who calls himself a god.' The old man looks at me, his tired eyes curious. 'There is but one God, you realize.' 'I believe that,' I respond. 'For my heart is filled with hatred of him.' Silence greets me. I speak again. 'Though powerful in body, this supposed 'god' is very weak of mind. So wrapped up in degrading all things 'mortal', he makes the error of pronouncing me mortal as well.' My eyes grow dark, faint fires burning within. Bitter hatred consumes me. Hatred....and a faint sadness. 'I am no mortal,' I state with a low voice. 'Through two thousand years, I realize that very well.' 'This....god....', the priest asks, 'why does he trouble you so?' 'He is no match for me,' I answer. 'Though he may dwarf my physical strength in my current state, he is as a building easily toppled by the removal of one brick.' A smile rises to my mouth. I chuckle. 'You might say I defeated him recently with one digit on my hand. If this is the case, what chance he against my entire form?' 'You still haven't answered my question,' the old man replies with a friendly sense of humor. And it is true. I haven't, have I? Is old age playing tricks on my mind? At this thought I laugh audibly. The priest waits for me to stop, awaits my answer patiently. I give him what he seeks. 'He arises questions in my own mind, in my heart,' I say. 'He attempts to take me to task for not mindlessly judging the weak....for my recent attempts, after two millennia, to simply let them be.' 'You are attempting to turn the other cheek. It is admirable.' 'Perhaps. Perhaps not,' I reply with a shake of my head. 'I had been attempting to take some measure of peace in my work....to lift the burden I bear.' Looking on, I speak in a low, saddened voice. 'There truly is so much evil in this world though....so many sinners walk it.' I gaze up briefly at him, letting my face fall into shadows. Just to meet his eyes. 'The collective pain of humanity is too much to bear,' sayeth I. 'If I do not do my part and weed out the refuse, it would compound....multiply. And I, the bearer of such pain and agony, would suffer for all time.' 'So is it a selfish desire,' he asks while returning my gaze, 'are you doing it for humanity or for yourself?' 'Perhaps a bit of both,' I respond with a chuckle. 'The 'god' as he calls himself doesn't realize this, for he is in all honestly merely another ignorant man.....but I do not kill simply to kill. I kill because I have to.' I lower my head somewhat tiredly. 'I will kill him as well. It is as inevitable as the tides.' Affixing me with his gaze, the old man nods once again. 'And you will kill, and kill, and kill, for all of time to come.' 'That is my curse,' sayeth I. 'And in many ways, my blessing. Rather than be a slothy, useless faux 'god', relying on a self prescribed name and nothing more, I have a cause. And that is why he has no chance of defeating me. Too wrapped up in his own ego to realize this....but he is beaten before he can strike a single blow.' I sigh, as the old priest hangs his head in understanding. 'You call yourself a demon, and honestly you are,' he states. 'You are a horrible creature, destined to bring pain and misery till the dying days of the earth. But you are cursed. You have a soul.' I look up at him, at this simple statement of fact. 'Blackened and as charred as the face you attempt to conceal from me,' he says, 'but a soul nonetheless. And now you must be the one to make the decision. Which path will you choose, my son?' 'What do you mean?' 'You have two options. You may either end your existence, put a stop to the killing. Or you may continue down your path, lined with broken lives and endless pain. Both have their consequences. But only you may choose.' I look at him again, this old man. And I realize in a horrible moment that he is right. The question echoes through my battered and torn soul. To die in the light, or to live in the dark? Silence here, and nothing more. I ponder his words. And at last, with a heavy heart, I reach my terrible conclusion. I look up to him, my eyes burning with a sad fire. Then I am gone. He looks around for me, peering through the little hole he has gazed through so often. Then he turns to find me standing by his side. He doesn't yell out. He speaks again. He knows much, this old man. 'I see you have made your choice,' he says solemnly. I nod slowly. The pain is unbearable, as I again let the visions I hate so much enter into my mind. His past. Black and charred. Burned. Buried. But not forgotten. Oh no, never forgotten. I do not wish to do this. But I must. I have no choice. No choice at all. A short time later I kneel beside his body, his blood covering me head to toe. I hold him in my arms. A single tear rolls down my cheek. It hits the floor, hissing and evaporating into nothingness. The flames rise around me. Screams. Screams fill the church, as I kneel with this dead old man in a lonely confessional booth. The answer to his question was never truly in doubt. My path is chosen. Now and forevermore.
