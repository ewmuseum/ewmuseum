The Rebellion Saga: Part IV

28 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12-28-02 [Note: Anyone who thinks that your RPs should have only things to do with your opponent and your match, don't bother to read anything I ever write from this point forward. I have decided to completely abandon the 'trash talk' form of RP and focus more on my ever developing fantasy sagas. If you want to put this into perspective in a wrestling way, don't even bother. I write for fun, take it or leave it Plus im tired of brutally ripping off Neil Gaiman, so I need a gimmick tweek anyway :P] Christmas was never easy for DarkStar. It was always a holiday that revolved around despression embedded under the flashing lights of happiness. It was a guise used by big name companies to make money off of a religion that wasn't even respected anymore. Plus, DarkStar worked double time during the holiday. You see, DarkStar was the missing link between humanity and the gods, so therefor it was his duty to filter the empathy that humanity felt between him and the Other Realm. Almost like a secretary. He saw their dreams and he saw their nightmares, he saw their hopes and their fears. He hated every second of it. From the Widow remembering her dead husband to the girlfriend opening up her engagement ring from the man she loved. He saw it all, and there was no denying that it shaped what he had become. He was one of the seven... Destiny, Despair, Delerium, Desire, Death, Destruction, and then himself, the king of the dream realms. DarkStar. I could go into great detail about how the Outer Realms work, but that story was told much better by a different author many years ago. I only choose to tell the story from the point where he took over and on. When he shook Destiny and his old ideals and grasped ahold of the Realm with his iron fist. This holiday brought out the worst and best in everyone. He celebrated it with his new wife, and he enjoyed watching her face light up when she opened her presents, but in that space in his mind, the stories collected like a odometer and there was nothing he could do to reset the switch. He got her the new shoes she wanted. All he could think of was the couple in Mississippi who just killed themselves instead of being apart because of their parents. The Christmas Story was on the television. All he could think of was what the woman in Russia could say when her father uttered his last words. He had had enough of it. He stood up calmly and walked into his bathroom, washing his face to try and shake the thoughts away. Opening the medicine cabinet he pulled out the toothpaste, closing it he was forced to face his own reflection. The strange scar on his forhead, the blue hair that had now grown long and into his face, his pale eyes, the lips that could let the words to destroy a planet slip from his mouth, all his features. Yet even with all this, he had to face the fact that he existed solely to let humanity continue. It sickened him. It had to stop.
