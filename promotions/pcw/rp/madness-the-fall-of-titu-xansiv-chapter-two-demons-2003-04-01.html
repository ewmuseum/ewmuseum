Madness: The Fall Of Titu Xansiv - Chapter Two: Demons...

1 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 1st 2003 Location: Phoenix, Arizona. Date: March 31st 2003 “I’m not happy about this, Titu, not one bit at all!” Isiy wrinkles her nose up in disgust, the vixen’s mind clearly troubled by the recent actions of her unhinged husband, and her body still exhausted from the vastly strenuous journey from California to Arizona. “I don’t care, Isiy! My Gods demanded that I do something to show my ultimate faith in them, and by taking the cruiserweight title I do believe I have restored their own faith in me!” Isiy sighs, shaking her head to and fro. Sometimes, she realized, Titu could overlook the most obvious of suggestions, and she was not, in this case, speaking on behalf of his brilliant victory over Rent-A-Hero. Isiy simply couldn’t bring herself to call Titu’s betrayal of the PCW fans a wise business decision, and considering she was usually the one who took care of the ‘business’ side of things, she wasn’t at all happy with his sudden new loathing for the masses. “I don’t mean that! Your capture of the PCW cruiserweight title is a good thing, but turning on the fans - was that in your best interests?” she scolds, “And now, because of your abruptly irrational demonstrations as of late, PCW have decided to thrust you into a match which you probably can’t win! Dante Jackson and Gaz Green? Dammit, Titu!” The Pharaoh frowns, an aura of positive defiance lingering from his person, “You think this match has been scheduled due to the fact that I had my say? You really think that? It’s not because of that, Isiy; it’s because I’m a fighting champion!” he grins, “It’s because I deserve a difficult title defence to get over as the greatest lightweight champion in PCW history!” Isiy stalks across the bathroom. Her moist black hair drapes loosely over her shoulders and partly down her well-tanned back. Her toned physique sits adorned in the revealing confines of a white, silken towel. And her bright blue eyes stare sarcastically at the doorway before her. With a displeased grunt, she goes to tie her hair back, as from across the hall, the voice of Titu continues to echo loudly. “Just remember, I’ve already beaten these— oh,” he pauses, Isiy interrupting him quickly. “No, you haven’t beaten these two before, so whether or not you do depends entirely on whether or not you scope them and learn what makes them tick. You may have beaten the likes of DarkStar and Clyde—” “Do not utter his name in my presence!” Titu screams, a vague quality of anxiety present in his voice. “Ok, ok...just calm down! Look, all I’m trying to say is,” she halts suddenly, drying off some of her hair, and applying a thin, white bow to the jet-black locks, “you need to concentrate or else that championship could end up around the waist of Green or Jackson, and if you survive that bout, even Chet King.” In the hotel room’s lounge, Titu scowls at the name of the Chat King. Over the past two PCW shows, Chet King had quickly become one of Titu’s most annoying foes. The pair had yet to engage in physical competition, but Xansiv looked forward to the day he could silence the cocky chat show host once and for all. How dare King have the audacity to make his way to the announce table to do guest commentary! How dare he! And the things he had said, by the Gods! Titu had seen the videos of the show and now… now he realized how angry he was at King. Last week, when Xansiv had made his speech to the fans and King had interrupted him, he’d considered then how appallingly agitated it had made him. But after viewing the tapes, Pharaoh had soon concluded that the comments made by King through his match with Rent-A-Hero had been far worse in terms of embarrassment than his initial intermission the week before. Either way, he was beginning to hate Chet more and more as the days went by. “Chet King…pfft.” It was all he could think of saying. He knew damned-well he could say more, but his personal thoughts on the matter stretched so far and wide that it’d take an eon to even contemplate the complexity of the matter. He sits on the edge of the bed, the Cruiserweight Championship gleaming beneath the Phoenix sun, and falls back, coming to an abrupt stop on the soft mattress below. Looking up at the ceiling, he remembered back to his confrontation with the Gods the night he killed off half the population of his beloved Egypt, and closed his eyes tightly, that overwhelming pain seeping ever-hastily back into his aching body. “So, you and that Violence Jack fellow have to face Rent-A-Hero and Venneman Pendragon tomorrow. You ready for it?” Isiy’s voice is more than enough to break his ever-thickening wall of concentration. Like an axe to a chunk of timber, Titu’s deliberations split in two. His mind returns to the real world, but his eyes still wandering this way and that, looking vainly for an answer to his private doubts. “Err…yeah. V-Jack and I, yes…a team, indeed.” He pauses for a second, gathering his thoughts, “Besides, I have already beaten Rent-A-Hero, the snivelling little rat, and Pendragon shouldn’t be a problem. The man is more intent on destroying that ridiculous excuse for a world champion, Totally Tubular Tommy, and he’s clearly in no state of mind to compete anyhow. Violence Jack shouldn’t be too difficult to keep under bay, anyway, and rest assured, I have plans…by the Gods, I have plans…” Pendragon is in no state of mind to compete? What about you, Titu? Over the past few weeks you sure have put up a tough defence against my faith in you. Never mind the Gods, my love; it’s you who should be worrying about your state of mind! …Perhaps I should say something? But what’s the point? He’ll take my word for granted and refuse to listen, as usual. His attitude lately has been terribly strange…he talks to himself sometimes; I can hear him when he’s alone. I’ll question him about it later and he’ll deny the whole thing, but I know damned well that I heard him. Stress: I guess it can be a nasty thing. And if you ask me, this whole new behaviour of his - it’s down to all those lives he considers himself responsible for destroying. But he declines to see someone about his mentality, though I strongly wish he would just go to a quack about it. And when I even touch the subject, he flinches so hard it’s frightening. I can’t understand him these days…he’s changed… Still looking up at the ceiling, Titu takes in a deep pocket of air and breathes out again intensely. Then of course, they turned up… Location: Unknown.Date: Days after March Madness. Three tall and spindly figures, silhouetted black against a shroud of white mist, slowly begin to become visible. The pair on either side of the one in the center each bear canes to support their tired limbs and as they grow closer, their conversation becomes more audible… “You killed them, then?” asks one, surprisingly casual about his question. The center figure frowns, his voice taking up an element of annoyance, “No! Clyde did! I’ve tried to tell you all this before!” They continue to walk in silence for a moment, the sound of their footsteps on what we assume is moist soil resonating off whatever surrounds them. In this haze, however, nothing aside from their vague forms can be made out. “Yes, but you put their lives on the line, didn’t you?” This figure is a well-defined man in terms of muscular mass. His face, however, is covered by a mask, that of a jackal. His name is Anubis, the God of the dead. “Don’t be so harsh on him, Anubis. He did what he had to do…right, Titu?” he speaks as though he means to defend the center figure, but his voice is full of cynicism. This one wears the mask of an eagle. His name is Horus, God of the sky, protector of the pharaohs of old. As the mists clear about them, the man in the middle’s face grows viewable. He is Titu Xansiv, though his body is elongated and deformed. He looks similar to his appearance in the real world but is strangely distorted here, his arms lengthier, his tiny head positioned on a long, thin neck, and his body decorated in heavy golden chains. “Exactly! I did what I had to do!” he concurs after a moment, oblivious to Horus’s satire. Horus and Anubis share a brief look before the jackal-masked God continues, “So you admit that you were responsible for that loss of life?” Pharaoh throws up his arms angrily. “No! Why do you two plague me so?” “I only try to help you, Titu!” says Horus, seemingly offended by Xansiv’s last comment, “I’m your friend, remember.” Titu scowls. “That’s what you say now. But I know you two. I know you both better than you know! You’re here to…to…to—” “To?” queries Anubis, smirking. “To mock me!” Anubis shrugs, “We’re here to aid you, fool.” “Aid me?” Titu’s voice is full of venom. “You lie!” Silence returns to the void once more. The three companions walk onward, their destination unknown. “You turned on the fans, too, eh?” comments Anubis at length, breaking the metaphorical ice. Xansiv scowls, clearly peeved by the God’s words. “What business is that of yours?! I’m sick of this! It’s my decision to do as I wish!” he scolds, “Can’t you take that?” “‘Do as you wish’?” the God mocks. “Just as you wished to kill those Egyptians?” Anubis’s voice is cold, unmerciful…angry. Horus winces. “Calm down…the both of you!” he bellows, raking the sides of his mask. “I want to end this now!” shrieks Titu, his warped form wavering in the dully-lit atmosphere. “You can’t hide forever,” mutters Anubis, walking away. His back is turned to the Egyptian wrestler now. “Remember that.” He says. Horus looks on as the figures disintegrate into the mists. Titu tries to move towards them but his feet appear stuck in the soil. He cries out after them, but they disappear, leaving him alone…
