Randy Savage Supports Duke Williams

2 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Title: Randy Savage Supports Duke Williams Featuring: Duke Williams Date: 10/2/02 If Mark Twain had come from Missoula, his name would have been Duke Williams. Back in the early eighties, when Christ was a cowboy, I first met “The Malice Man” in Chicago. We were both wrestlers and we once stayed up for six nights and it felt like a week. Today, he's arguably the finest brawler and commentator the ring has ever produced. If you doubt my opinion, you could ask Hulk Hogan or wait until you get to turnbuckle heaven to ask Buddy Rogers, who are the other folks in the equation, but they might not give you a straight answer. Duke, for instance, tends to speak only straight forward. Just last week I was with an attractive young woman and I said to Duke, 'I'm not sure who's taller, but her ass is six inches higher than mine.' He responded, 'My ass is higher than both of your asses.' You see when Duke talks he is an achingly honest storyteller in a world that prefers to hear something else. “I shoot straight from the hip hoss and if they don’t like it they can lick the sweat off my balls.” I believe that every culture gets what it deservves. Ours deserves Howard Stern and Dr. Phil and Alexander Miyagawa (whom I like to refer to as the anti-Duke). But when the meaningless mainstream is forgotten, people will still remember those who struggled with success: van Gogh and Mozart, who were buried in paupers' graves; Hank Williams, who died in the back of a Cadillac; and even Badge, who had no grave at all, he just faded. I think there may be room in that shining motel of immortality for Duke’s timeless fights, beautiful beyond words in an old school kind of way; fought by a gypsy wrestler with an alcohol problem. God has sent a hat trick of grief to Duke in a year that even Job would have thrown back. His wife, Jessi, and his fans all have left him. And in January his son, Steve a sweet and talented warrior, took his life. “Hey hoss some times you’re the windshield some times you’re the bug.” Is all that Duke really said about that. I watched Duke Williams wrestle with pain, the big man engendering, perhaps not so strangely, an Hitler­like rapport with the audience. He called them names, blamed them, had them throw out, and even made them buy T-shirts. Since he’s joined up with the Front Office he is back to his old self. Only this time not as self-destructive. A man who has truly grown into the best of his field. He has faced his demons and has accepted them. Now its time to accept his as world champion. The time is now for Duke Williams my friend, my comrade, my brother. Randy Savage(Randy Savage is a retired wrestling legend who feuded with Duke Williams in the early 80’s in the WWF. Randy is now retired in Florida and writes a monthly column here in ProWrestling Illustrated) =w= ---- View Duke Williams's Biography
