Date: Season I, Episode 1 ﻿************************************ The Snow ShowSeason I, Episode 1 ************************************ The Option “So I got sued,” Snow shrugs as he walks down the woodchip trail, somewhere in Europe, if he’scorrect. “I’m sure it won’t be the last time. Just take a look at the fucking world we live in. You can step on someone’s toe walking down the damn street and the next day you’ll be short acouple of grand.” “Yes,” replies Shena, the massive mare horse to his left, walking on the grass beside him, “butyou didn’t step on someone’s toes. And if you lose, you’ll be losing much more than ‘a coupleof grand’ my dear.” She’s a beautiful mare, thinks Snow - or at least, she would be to someonewho knew about horses. He’d always meant to look into it, like all people with money do, buthe was never able to find time for it. He regrets it a little now, as he stands a good chance oflosing his fortune. “Fuck it,” Snow spits, “It’s just money. Besides, they can’t take it all. Fuck it!” He looksstraight ahead into the blue sun - Europe is so different. Shena looks down and shakes her head, trotting slowly so Snow and O’Reilly don’t fall behind. Especially O’Reilly, standing on Snow’s right, with his legs. Leprechauns have never been greatfor walking. “Fuck it!?” O’Reilly bursts in his thick Irish accent, looking up at Snow, though it’s hard to seepast the chest. “You don’t want to be pissing away your money, now do ya lad? You’ve got tofight for it just like you fought to get it. Kick them all right in their arse!” “I don’t think that will work, O’Reilly,” Shena says, giving the leprechaun a disapproving look. “You shouldn’t put these ideas in his head, little one. Sometimes I think you do more harm thangood.” “Ah,” O’Reilly says, waving his tiny hand at the old horse, “What the fuck do you knowanyway?” Snow takes a deep breath and continues walking. He’s getting tired though. His legs ache at thethighs and he suddenly feels a lot older than he did a few minutes ago. His eyes are growingheavy as they walk toward the blue sun under the purple sky. His lips crack against the bitterwind, and he collapses on his face - death by heart attack. Location: Snow’s Home, New Brunswick, CanadaDate: Monday, November 25, 2002Start of Scene Time: 12:25 pmShot: Interior - Kitchen “Where the hell is the bread!?” Jason Snow asked as he raided through the cupboards of hishome in New Brunswick, Canada. He turned to look over his shoulder at his wife, Raven, whowas sitting at the kitchen table with their infant daughter, Haley, on her lap. “Don’t use that language around your daughter!” she scolded him. He sighed and turned back tothe cupboards, not wanting to deal with that familiar look that was on her face now. Herhusband spent five months in prison, yet still, she didn’t hesitate to pull that look from herrepertoire and smack Snow in the face with it. “We keep it in the bottom now,” she finally said,the look still heavy on her pretty young face. Raven didn’t look old enough to be a mother, and at twenty-three, a lot of people thought shewasn’t. Snow was very glad to see her when he got back from the Tank, though she couldn’thelp but be disappointed that he left for Alliances so soon after returning. It was his job though,and she had always been supportive, even if it was reluctant. She had moderately long brown hair that she wore in waves and curls. She possessed the type ofbeauty that didn’t need make up to emphasise it, so she rarely wore it. Snow could never tellanyway. With aches and pains shooting through his body, Snow knelt down to the lower cupboard andsifted through to find the bread. Normally, Raven would prepare his food - it was something thatshe took great delight in. Since coming out of prison, however, Snow was determined to makeher life easier. “We have to talk about these lawsuits, you know,” Raven said, her brown eyes fixed on the backof Snow’s head - she loved his new haircut, though she knows that he’s lying about his reasonsfor getting it. She found several cuts and scratches on the top of his head while he slept onSaturday night, but she didn’t ask him about them. He had been reluctant to talk about whatwent on in prison, so she knew he wouldn’t answer anyway. “They’ll clear up,” Snow said to her, a moment after giving the subject thought. “I’ve got ahel...” he wisely chose to rephrase. “I’ve got a great lawyer.” “Your great lawyer doesn’t seem too confident.” “He wasn’t confident that I’d get paroled either,” shrugged Snow. Secretly, the matters wereeating at him. There was a good chance that he would lose everything - everything. Hewas up against three lawsuits, each worth tens of millions of dollars. His money, his land, evenhis home was on the line. He thought Raven didn’t know this, but she was often a lot smarterthan he gave her credit. “People get sued every day,” he shrugged, giving up on the sandwich he was trying to make anheading to the fridge - food came easier when it was pre-made, and Raven always kept a fewthings kicking around the fridge that he could just toss in the microwave. “It’s no big thing.” “People get sued every day and people lose every day, Jason,” she said, leaning forwardwhile holding Haley tight against her stomach. In truth, he didn’t want to talk about it for anumber of reasons. First, he didn’t want to worry her - he prided himself on being a provider. Second, he didn’t want to think about it more than he already was forced to. His lawyer wascalling hourly to let him know how awful things were going in the settlement attempts. “It’s notthat I care about the money,” Raven said, pulling Haley to her shoulder. “I just want to have aplan if the money suddenly isn’t there.” That was Raven. She always had a plan. She was the anti-procrastinator - the type of personwho made called about newspaper ads and what-not as soon as learning the number. She paidbills on the day they came in the mail. Snow was glad to have her. He knew that he’d be payingseveral salaries if his little wife weren’t so efficient. “It’s not like I don’t make a good living,” Snow argued. He was right. His PCW contract wasone worth multi-millions. But he didn’t mention that there was a chance he’d have to pay mostof that to the people suing him. “My contracts good for a few more months yet, and when it’sup, chances are I’ll even get a raise.” She looked at him cynically. The lawsuits started coming in over a month ago while he was still in the midst of his prisonterm, though he decided it would be best not to let the information out. The first came from Dr.Harold Grant, a man who Snow assaulted in 1999. He already served six months for that, butnow the good doctor was back for some money. The second came from William Brancher, his sister-in-law’s ex-boyfriend who he assaultedearlier this year. It was this crime that he was sent to the Tank for. He paid dearly for thiscrime. It was also the one that would probably cost him the most. If he had been tried inCanada, he would have likely spent at least two years in prison. Instead, his lawyer managed toget the trial in Los Angeles, where he resisted arrested. Part of the bargain made betweenSnow’s lawyer and the D.A., who wanted to make an example of Snow, was that Snow wouldserve a reduced sentence. Five months, however, is a long time in one of the continents mostdangerous prisons. William Brancher wanted money now, and chances were good that he’d getit - he’d undergone three surgeries already on his jaw and nose, and there was a chance he’dneed a fourth on his right cheek. Snow remembered when he left William lying on the groundthat night, some five and a half months ago, he wasn’t sure if the young man was alive or dead. The third came soon after, and was for a minor incident that happened in the summer of 2000,where Snow kicked a man in a bar. For this, he was sentenced to a few months in a Betty FordClinic to get his drinking under control. In total, the lawsuits added up to well over a hundred million dollars. Jason Snow was worthjust over eighty million. At best, he would come out of 2002 broke. “Maybe I’ll get a job,” Raven suggested. “Haley’s old enough for a sitter now. Or Krista cantake her, since she’s taking the year off school.” “No,” Snow said sternly. “Don’t worry about this stuff, Raven. I’ll take care of it. I alwayshave.” With that, he stood up from the table and headed off into the living room to watch T.V. He’d been fascinated with T.V. since coming home from prison. He told Raven that it was justnice to be able to change the channel without having to slap someone around over it. She watched him walk by and sighed. He was different since coming out of the Tank, but hestill held strong to his ‘hunter-gatherer’ values, obsessed with the idea that the male mustprovide. He was bigger, quieter, and often more thoughtful than in the past, but he hadn’tchanged that much. They sit quietly in the diner, though most people around them stare at the leprechaun sitting in ahigh chair at their table. They don’t pay much attention to the horse. The trio look over their menus and Snow flipd through the pages for Shena - her hooves aren’tmuch good for anything involving paper. Nothing looks eatable. Mudcakes, Fesces-flavouredjello, and fish guts with urine gravy aren’t Snow’s idea of a hearty meal. Actually, they kind ofremind him of the Tank. “Are you sure you can afford this place?” asks Shena, staring at the menu. “It seems quitepricey.” “Of course he can!” insists O’Reilly, slamming his tiny freckled fist down on the table. “If theshit were worth eating, he’d buy the whole fucking kitchen!” “You’ve never tried it,” says Shena. “I’m sure they wouldn’t have it on the menu if peopledidn’t like it.” “Fucking lawsuits,” Snow says, changing the subject. “I can’t believe these motherfuckers aresuing me!” “Jason,” Shena says in a calming voice, “you know what happens when you get angry. Don’t letyour emotions get the best of you.” “Fuck that!” Snow exclaims, leaning back in his seat. “I should just drive around the fuckingcontinent and take out these pricks. That’ll get rid of the fucking lawsuits. I can do that shit,you know! I’m capable of it! You know how many motherfuckers I took down in the Tank!?” “We know what you did,” says Shena, her voice no longer calm. Even she can’t contain hercontempt for his actions, though it only shows through her tone slightly. “And it was fucking awesome!” exclaims O’Reilly with a large yellow-and-brown toothed smile. “I did what had to be done, damn it!” Snow says, drawing the attention of the other diners in theroom. “I did what I had to do for my FAMILY. That’s what comes first! That’s what alwayscomes first!” “What would your family think if they knew you were a murderer, Jason?” asks Shena, her voicea little softer than before to make the blow as gentle as possible while keeping its heavy impact. Snow reaches down, rips a page out of the menu, and stuffs it in his mouth. He chews loudlyand open-mouthed, under the observation of everyone in the diner. A moment later, he chokes,falls on his side to the floor, and dies. Location: Snow’s Home, New Brunswick, CanadaDate: Monday, November 15, 2002Start of Scene Time: 1:15 pmShot: Interior - Living Room Snow stared at the screen without watching. Every ounce of his attention was focussed on thewords coming to him through the telephone, though none of them were good. “I’m sorry, Mr.Snow,” his lawyer said. “That’s just the way it is. These guys are holding strong. They knowwhat they can get, and they’re not going to settle for a couple of million.” “Are you telling me that there’s a real chance that I could lose everything!?” Snow said,his voice was harsh, but hushed - Raven was just upstairs in the bedroom with Krista. “You’ve known all along that it’s been a possibily, Jason,” said his lawyer. “For God’s sake,you could have killed two of them!” “I did my time for that!” Snow hissed. “Yes,” Ron said in a voice that was trying to soothe his client. “You did your time, but yourvictims have the right to be repayed.” “In millions!?!?” Snow exclaimed, a little too loudly. He glanced to his left where the staircasewas, wondering if his wife or her sister heard the comment. He could see the light from themaster bedroom over the balcony. “You can’t tell me that if I were Joe-fucking-Blow and I’ddone this shit, I’d owe millions of dollars!” “If you were Joe Blow, you’d still be in prison,” remarked his lawyer. “That’s got nothing to do with it!” Snow snapped quietly. “You’re a lawyer, learn to fuckingargue! How can these bastards sue me for more money just because I have moremoney!?” “They’re going after what they think they can get,” sighed Ron, on the other end of the line. “And you have to face the fact, sooner or later, that they’re right. You did the crime.” “And I did the time!” “Well,” Ron said, “now you have to pay the price too.” Snow, along with his horse and leprechaun, walk tirelessly up the mountain trail with the windblowing roughly on their cold skin. “I should have dressed better,” Snow mutters as he pulls hisleather jacket closed. “Could’ve, should’ve, would’ve,” sighs Shena, his horse, “all that matters, Jason, is what youDO, what you HAVE DONE, and what you ARE GOING TO DO.” “Seems like a fucking rip off to me,” Snow shrugs as they continue to journey upward againstthe cold winter air. The top of the mountain isn’t even in sight yet. “I just wish we had afucking car or something. In the year 2002, we have fucking drive-thru liquor stores, but do wehave a car that can climb to the top of a fucking mountain!? No! Technology my ass!” “Sometimes we have to do things alone, Jason,” his horse says, staring bravely through the wind,her eyes searching for the top of the mountain. “You can’t always rely on artificial things tohelp you. Sometimes we just have to use what God has given us, and make do.” “God made the people who made the cars,” Snow says defiantly, “but still we’ve got to climbthe sides of motherfucking mountains on foot! Am I the only one that sees something wrongwith this picture!?” Instinctively, Snow looks to his right, to O’Reilly, for support. Theleprechaun is motionless, however, and seems to float an inch or so off the ground and is makinghis way up the mountain in that way. As soon as Snow notices him motionless, however, theleprechaun is left behind with each step they take. “What the fuck’s the matter with him!?” Snow asks Shena. “He’s frozen.” “Of all the fucking luck!” Snow hisses, stalking back a few paces to pick the little leprechaun uponto his shoulders. “He’s heavier than he fucking looks, you know. You’re a horse, why don’tyou carry him?” “I’d rather leave him behind,” Shena shrugs, “but I suppose, that’s just my nature.” With the leprechaun stiff against his shoulder, Snow forces himself onward up the side of thegreat mountain. Shena tries to pick up the pace to hurry Snow along, but the weight of theleprechaun is keeping him from moving too swiftly. “I worry about you, Jason,” the mare tells him. “You changed a lot while you were in prison.” “It couldn’t be helped,” Snow shrugs. “You have to go through changes in that hell hole. Otherwise, someone’s going to fuck you up the ass while his buddies slit your throat. You dowhat you have to do to survive.” “Do you feel good about what you did in prison, Jason?” she asks him softly, referring to themurders that he was a part of, and the one that was committed by his own hand. “I did what I had to do,” Snow says sternly. “I had to protect my family, and people had to die. It’s that simple. My family comes first, above all else - above everyone else.” “But you didn’t want to kill them,” Shena persists, “did you?” “Of course not,” Snow answers with some reluctance. “It’s not like I’m fucking happy aboutwhat happened!” “How did it feel when you beat the man to death with the shower head?” Shena asks. “Whatwas his name? Vincent Giovanni? You know, there was a woman who gave him that name along time ago - his mother. There was a man who protected him exactly like you protect yourfamily - his father. How do you suppose they feel?” The more Snow thinks about this question, the more O’Reilly’s weight bares on his shoulder. Soon, the leprechaun feels like he weighs hundreds, then thousands, of pounds. Snow holds theleprechaun with all his might, but soon enough, his legs can’t take the weight anymore. Hisknees break, and he collapses to the ground - crushed to death by the extreme weight of theleprechaun. Location: Snow’s home, New Brunswick, CanadaDate: Monday, November 25, 2002Start of SceneTime: 3:05 pmShot: Interior - Living Room The discussion with his lawyer layed heavy on Jason Snow’s mind for quite some time after itended. He hated to accept the fact that he was going to lose this case, along with his money andlikely his home and possessions. He’d still have his job, but his salary would be deferred to theplaintiffs, no doubt. They weren’t going to settle this case. That was clear. When the phone rang, he immediately thought it was his lawyer again. “What now?” he said ashe picked it up, turning off the television at the same time. “This Jason Snow?” a voice on the other end spoke in a thick New York accent. A moment ofsilence passed. “Well?” the voice asked. “Who’s this?” Snow returned a question. “A friend of a friend of a friend,” the man on the other end said. “Is this Jason Snow or not?” “I’m Jason Snow.” “Good,” the man said - he sounded like he was chewing something. “Listen up kid, I ain’t goingto say this twice. There’s a man waiting at your airport with a plane ticket in your name.” “What?” Snow was confused. “The plane will take you to New York where a limo will pick you up. You’ll get the rest of yourinformation inside the car.” “What’s all this about?” Snow asked, standing up and keeping his voice as quiet as possible. “I can’t say that over the phone,” the man answered. “Like I said, you’ll get the information thatyou need in the limo. Not before. All I can tell you is that an offer is going to be made to you,and it’s an offer that can make your future a whole lot brighter than it is now.” “You expect me to get on a plane with no clue of where I’m headed?” Snow asked, his voicegetting a little harsher. “I’m going to give you one name, kid,” the man said. “Don Justin Hattoni.” With that, theconversation ended. The man on the other end hung up and Snow was left, standing in themiddle of his living room, holding the telephone with his mouth wide open in a gasp. Justin Hattoni, son of the late Doug Hattoni, the man who Snow protected for his first month inprison. He knew Doug wrote fondly of him while they were on such good terms - no one wasleft alive who knew how ugly things got. No one knew that Snow had actually played a part inthe old mobster’s murder. His first instinct was to dismiss the call and go back to spending time with his family. Butsomething inside him forced him to give the phone call more thought. Maybe this could helphim in his financial troubles - maybe Justin Hattoni was the answer. Then again, there was the chance that somehow word had slipped back to the Hattoni familyabout the real cause of Doug Hattoni’s death, and they were setting him up to murder him. In the face of bankruptcy, that didn’t look so bad. At least, in his death, his family would beallowed to keep their fortune. It was decided. New York was calling him, and he had no choice but to go. Now, the onlyquestion that remained, was how he was going to break the news to Raven, and what excusewould he use for leaving again so soon after Alliances, with Fusion still two days away. By the time he reached the top of the stairs, he would have to think of something. In his heart, however, he truly believed that this was what he had to do, not for himself, but forhis family. Location: The Hattoni Mansion, New YorkDate: Monday, November 25, 2002Start of Scene Time: 3:10 pm Shot: Interior - Justin Hattoni’s Office Max Hattoni, the younger brother of Justin Hattoni, hung up the phone, thus ending theconversation with Jason Snow. Sitting next to him in the dimly lit office sitting behind his deskwas the young Don of the Hattoni family, Justin Hattoni. He looked much like his father, thoughhe wasn’t nearly so fat. At thirty-three years old, Justin’s hair was already completely grey. Itcame with the job, he would always tell those who knew him well. He’d been deeply involvedin the family business since he was a teenager. “What’d he say?” asked Justin in a voice as raspy as his late father’s. “Not much,” Max Hattoni shrugged. “But the old man painted a hell of a picture of this kid. He’ll be here.” “Get the limo ready,” Justin said as he stood up from his seat behind the desk. “You’re coming too?” asked Max in a surprised voice. “This is the man who prolonged our father’s life in prison,” Justin said in his raspy voice. “Ourfather described the Tank as pure hell, and this... Jason Snow... was able to keep our father alivewhile half the inmates wanted him dead.” Max nodded to his older brother. “Naturally,” Justin said, taking a step toward the door, “I’d like to meet him in person. Hisservice to our father deserves no less.”
