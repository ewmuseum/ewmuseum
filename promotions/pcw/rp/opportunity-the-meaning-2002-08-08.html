Opportunity: The Meaning?

8 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8-08-02 A busy street. Saturday, 5:30 PM. The hustle and bustle of a city that never sleeps. Each face goes by--a blur, they could appear fifteen times yet one would never know they were there. It can be loneliest of all when there are so many people around you. So many strangers. Just waiting for something to change them-- or someone. We look around the street… so little emotion is wept upon these roads. Businessmen and women flash back and forth, wearing bland colors, almost hiding from notice. But upon further examination--there is a slight difference from this street and every other. Yes… the difference is so clear now… a sore thumb, sticking out in this picture. A warrior. Trigger stands in one spot, staring dead ahead. He stands in traffic--he stands on the sidewalk, watching life pass him by. What is in his head? What does he think? What does he long to say? Trigger: I am here to learn. He says his thoughts aloud. Why not? It couldn't be more private. Trigger: I am here to learn! To observe! I will find the meaning… He is still on his quest. Never stopping until he finds what he searches for… the meaning of life? Or something much, much more. Suddenly, he snaps out of his trance. A wallet on the ground. Several feet away stands a woman in distress, searching through her purse for her lost possession. People walk by, hardly taking notice. She is alone in her quest… that is why she is startled by the voice. Trigger: How does one gain knowledge? The woman looks up. She is a person of business. Probably a lawyer. A black suit, a briefcase. Such a cliché. She stares up at the Infinite Warrior… noticing he is serious, she attempts to answer. Woman: Umm… knowledge comes with age, I guess. Heh. A joke. Trigger does not laugh. His expression remains meaningless, giving away nothing. Trigger: How old are you. Such a forward question. Not many would answer. But when she looks into his eyes, when she sees his fiery soul, demanding the truth, accepting none other. Woman: 29. Trigger flips open the wallet, where her driver's license stares back at him. Donna McDuffel. 37. He tosses it to her, not waiting for her gratitude, and begins to walk the other direction down the street. As he does so, he speaks to himself, curious to what the response meant. Trigger: If knowledge comes from age, then why must one lie about how old they are? Is it fear? Do people lie to themselves in order to shield themselves from the underlying truth that one day they will cease to exist? How foolish. It would be torture to live forever. He continues walking down the street… silent now as time drags on. A few minutes? Or an eternity. Before long, the sunlight begins to slip away, and night closes in fast. Now the business people have gone home to their families. To their mansions and expensive cars. The street is crowded once more, but now the eyes spot most attention in the shadows, the alleyways, and on the corners of the street. He stops, looks around, and spots his next target. Against a brick wall, a man leans up, dressed in a black trench coat and puffing at a cigarette. A shady character indeed--just the kind of person Trigger is looking for. The man now looks up with his elusive, mysterious eyes… suspicion is evident. Trigger: What buys happiness? The man is confused at such a question. But once again, something hypnotic pulls him in to the Infinite Warrior's grasps, and the man finds himself answering without thought. Man: Nothin' buys happiness, I guess. It's somethin' ya gotta have… like love and commitment and all, ya know? If Trigger is offended by the slang and half-answers, he does not show it. His face remains like ice: as cold and unbreakable as seemingly possible. His next question is much more startling; much more intruding. Trigger: Why is it that you're selling drugs? The man goes on the defensive. What must be going through his head? Does he think it's a raid? A bust? He presses himself flat against the wall, eyes wide in terror--but after a few moments, looking back at the Infinite Warrior, he somehow is compelled to answer once more. Man: For money, I guess. Without another glance, Trigger turns and continues walking down the street. He is thinking to himself, once more astonished by the irony. Trigger: Some say the pursuit of life is happiness. If it is love and commitment that buys inner fulfillment, then what use is money? Some say money is the epitome of evil. They say its greed is a poison that no man can withstand. Why is it then, that money is so often associated with happiness? Are the truly happy the ones that are poor? Zooming out at the big picture, we see that Trigger has stopped once more. In front of him is a public bench, on which sits a homeless mother and her child. They are curled up in a sort of sleeping bag, and when they notice the Infinite Warrior hovering above, the mother glances up. Her face is ridden with dirt and blood, her hair unkempt. Nevertheless, Trigger speaks to her in the same tone, with the same expression. None. Trigger: What is freedom? By now it is evident that there is something about the voice of this man who calls himself 'warrior,' that drives serious answers from the lips of those so unwilling. The mother stares right back at him, forming a response. Mother: Freedom is being able to do stuff when you want to… being able to do what you want to. Trigger's next question fires back, almost immediate… almost as if he already knew what she would say. Trigger: You live in America, the land of the free. Why do you not improve conditions for yourself and for your child? It takes several seconds for the answer. The woman has to contemplate, as if she truly does not know. Mother: Nobody will give me an opportunity. The answer given, the Infinite Warrior continues his quest. All the while, he thinks over the information given to him. From what he has heard, it appears the world is so hypocrisy-ridden that he may never find the meaning of life. Trigger: An opportunity? If one is truly free, why must they have an opening to act? If one is free, why can't they take what they want, and do what they want, whenever they want? The system does not make sense. Life does not make sense. The wise are too stupid to acknowledge their brilliance. The happy are too busy corrupting their lives with what they believe to be more happiness. The free are too burdened by hardships to realize their freedom. And what do they need? The wise want to be young. The happy want to be rich. And the free need… an opportunity. The warrior stops again, stops for the last time, in front of a large house where lights illuminate the darkened sky. He has reached a residential neighborhood, the home of the one envied by all--the so-called common man. He is wise with knowledge. He is wealthy enough to be 'happy.' He has endless opportunities to make life better for himself, and those he cares about. Is this man's life fulfilled? Trigger knows he must find the answer. Where his thoughts lead him, he does not know, but along this journey he must follow, until he finds the answer. Until he finds the meaning.
