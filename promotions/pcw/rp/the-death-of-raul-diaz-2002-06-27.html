The Death of Raul Diaz

27 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/27/02 Minutes later, The End is walking down the muddy road leading away from the ranch. He's focused on one thing alone: his next victim. A man by the name of Raul Diaz, only he calls himself 'El Diablo.' The End has suffered through much by El Diablo, but at PCW Gold & Glory this Sunday... the time for retribution will be at hand. Just then, a huge explosion rips through the air, a reddish-orange fireball shooting high into the sky. The End doesn't turn back to the ranch, he doesn't stop to admire his work. He just keeps walking slowly down that narrow county road, smiling the same sick smile all the way. 'Again?' That's what I said when Commissioner Aldo Azul called me at about 3 AM in the morning. 'Can't believe it. I thought we had them all covered well enough. But another one still went missing.' He sounded stressed, fatigued. It was completely understandable; the man hadn't slept in days. He couldn't, the entire tri-county area was turned topsy-turvy. There'd been a series of kidnappings plaguing the city. I mean to say that kidnappings always happened, but these ones were different. They all had the same pattern. They all involved males living in the quad-city area. Not religion, or socioeconomic status, or sexuality... nothing matched them up. Except for one thing: they all had the same name, my name -- Raul Diaz. With all other loose ends taken care of, The End lays down a line of gasoline and oil that spans the entire farm. The sheer amount of the stuff used keeps it together despite the now pounding rain. The whole farm, the whole ranch is covered in it. The End goes inside and turns on the feeder processing lines. With a number of heavy bags of grain, The End jams up the lines. The machine overloads from work stress. The lines malfunction, and the conveyor belts snap. The rollers keep rolling and piling up the bags together, which the machine still tries to push through the narrow feeder lines. The End is already out of the building by the time that the machine short-circuits and causes an electrical fire from the sparks and petroleum mixture. 'I'm ready,' is all The End says as he leaves the pigs to eat their last meal as the farm goes up in flames. I got up to go to the home of the latest man to be kidnapped. He was in his late fifties, and ran a livestock ranch in Okeechobee, Florida. He grew chickens, pigs, and cows for the market, but sold them to the farmers' market by unattended pickup, and kept to himself nearly all of the time. So how could anyone get close enough to him to kidnap him? I pushed for these kidnappings to be designated as homicide cases so we could get the extra resources in cracking this case. We needed it, after two weeks of disappearances left and right, we weren't getting anywhere. And the list of names on the Board of Accountability back at the sheriff's office wasn't getting any shorter. In ten minutes, nothing is left but the disconnected bones that once formed the inner framework of his body. Standing above it all, Scott takes off his raincoat, revealing a black body suit. He pulls a mask over his head. It is The End. The pigs disperse from the remains of their recently eaten meal. The End jumps down from the platform and collects the bones and places them in the grinder, then puts the ground up material into the pigs' food. My partner, Janice Trickey (who was infamous for breaking down anyone who made fun of her name in less than two seconds), and I looked around the area. There wasn't anything wrong. All the animals were fed. The place was clean. Even the following week's orders were in their proper bins by the road, ready to be picked up by the people from the farmers' market. 'Nothing's wrong,' Janice kicking up a little dirt just to overturn something. 'Not quite,' I replied. One thing did stand out, though. Another guy was also snooping around. African-American male in his mid-twenties, tall, dark, maybe 200 pounds; he wore a dress shirt and pants underneath a black trenchcoat. Interesting to me, though, was the fact that he was wearing gloves over his hands and sunglasses for the less-than-sunny day we were having, expecting rain within the next few hours. Janice and I wore gloves too, but I wanted to know who this guy was. Janice was quick to approach him about his presence. Within seconds, Diaz is lying on his back, the pigs eating him alive. He tries to fight them off, but there are too many and they just keep coming. They are fast eaters, and they have voracious appetites. From the mass of pigs, Diaz stands up, missing pieces of flesh from his arms and face. His pants are nearly torn off. He kicks some of the pigs away with what strength he has remaining and tries to run away again. One of the more spry pigs catches him by the back of the ankle and drags him down again. This time, there are even more pigs on top of him than before. They are hungry... so hungry... The End hadn't fed these pigs in days. 'Sir, what are you doing here?' she said, flashing her badge as fast as she spoke. 'I'm a private investigator, Alan Scott. I'm looking into this particular missing person's case, to see if I can't gather some clues about the other one who's gone missing in Arcadia. Apparently the family didn't think the police force was doing enough to find their son.' 'This is a restricted area,' Janice told him. He pointed out that there was not police tape blocking the area, so he was free to come in and look, which he was. Janice just liked giving people a hard time. 'Not that it matters,' I pointed out. 'There's no problem here. This place is clean.' This other guy said, 'Yeah. That's problem.' We started to get it. 'The place is swept up, all the animals have been tended to, everything's fed for the day, and the shipment to the farmers' market is waiting a little in from the road. If this guy's missing, then somebody's taking real good care of his place for him.' A couple of birds, I think egrets, made some noises in the distance. Janice and I nearly slapped ourselves on the head. 'Why didn't we see it before?' I said. Janice took another look around. 'Things are a little too normal around here. The guy was reported missing... when?' I looked in my pocket notepad. 'About 5 hours ago. It was an anonymous tip though, some guy said he was passing through to pick something up, and no one was here. Said that the owner never left the ranch, so there had to be something wrong that he wasn't here.' This Scott fellow started walking toward the house on the ranch. 'I found something,' he said. 'You should see it.' Diaz doesn't even have time to shout, 'No!' as he drops to the muddy ground, which cushions his fall. Diaz stands and runs for the gate, but his weight sinks into the mud with every step, and soon the hungry pigs quickly overtake him. Diaz:'Argh! Damn!' We followed him into the house. It was simply furnished. Had a bed, wood stove, and dinner table, but that was it. There were some cupboards, and a knife rack, but little else. The place was a one-room shack compared to what one would expect a fairly well off rancher like Diaz to live in. One thing we noticed, though, was interesting. On the floor of the shack: Maggots. 'See them?' 'Yeah,' I said. Janice jumped to the conclusion that there had to be a body underneath the floorboards. Scott didn't say anything after that or help us. He just watched us as we tore open the floor and found nothing. '... Now, if you two are done destroying the house?' Janice shot him a frustrated look. 'What's your idea?' Diaz considers one last question, slipping three inches the ground as Scott releases the rope for but a moment. Diaz:'... Why pigs?' The End holds the rope in his hand up high, with it wrapped around the steel beam it moves down some by the force of Diaz's weight and drops another inch. End:'Heh. You don't remember?' Scott went back to his car and returned with a spray container that looks like the ones exterminators use. He's also got a digital camera in his other hand. 'You're going to kill them maggots?' I asked. 'No,' he said in an indignant tone. 'It's Luminol.' 'What's that?' He let out a disgusted sigh, sprayed it around, and told us to cover the windows. And then we saw it. A neon-colored trail of drops going from the middle of the floor over to the window; then, it becomes a spread-out puddle, and streaks toward the door. 'Whoa, what'd you do?' Janice asks. 'Luminol reacts with the iron in blood, producing a blue flourescent glow in total darkness. Forget how it works for now, it only lasts 30-40 seconds once the lights are off, so take my camera and snap some pictures.' He tosses the camera to me and I take some pictures. Janice, always inquisitive sometime to the point that she doesn't realize things she should, speaks up. 'What's this for?' Scott shakes his head. 'I don't believe it. Look, I want to believe that you people are competent investigators, but you don't even understand what I'm doing... must you persist in proving me wrong?' Janice kinda knew what was up, but she just asks so many questions that she doesn't seem too bright. I jump in to save her, nice guy that I am. 'Don't ya see, Jan? The maggots on the clean floor, but he sprays that stuff that's supposed to make blood glow--' She comes to her own ideas aloud; 'And it's all over the floor, drops here, splatters there, and then it streaks to the door.' Scott smirked. 'Congratulations,' he said. 'You've got yourself a crime scene.' The rain continues to fall, washing the blood off of The End. The droplets fall down to the pigs below, and seem to agitate them. The sky gets darker and darker, and the sound of thunder comes rolling in from the northwest horizon. End:'They themselves did nothing wrong. But, unfortunately, they are guilty by association.' Diaz:'Association to whom? To what?' The End loosens his grip on the ropes. End:'You truly disappoint me if you haven't figured that out yet.' He stepped into the middle of the bloodstains and circled around, holding his arms out. 'Look at this. There was definitely a fight here. Look at all this blood. Your missing person has to be dead, there's blood all over the place. No one can bleed that much and live. You've got a homicide. This wasn't done professionally, the killer probably just mopped up with some soap and thought the job was done. I mean, it had to be. If someone breaks in and you kill them in self-defense, you're not worried about being prosecuted, the jury would find you innocent of any wrong-doing.' I wanted homicide to be called in... but not like that. 'The killer probably stayed around a few days, taking care of the victim's chores to make sure that no one suspected anything. But these flies... they know better.' The maggots and flies are still huddled over the bloodstains on the floor. Scott also notices a few flies buzzing over the knife rack. 'The murder weapon's still here.' End:'Everyone is guilty of something. You for doing your duty. Myself, for doing what I've done. ... Those men for who they were.' This strikes at the heart of what Diaz was trying to figure out. He jumps on it. Diaz:'Who were they?' End:'Farmers and bakers and candlestick makers... you have their files, you know who they were.' Diaz:'But to you, who were they? If you believed that they deserved to die, they must have been guilty of something. So what was it?' Janice and I watched as Scott took all the knives out from the knife rack and took them outside. He laid them all down five feet apart from each other. 'What's this-' Janice started. Scott stopped her with, 'Please... just wait.' And we did. Five minutes. Ten minutes. And then one fly came over and flew around a little. And then another. And then there were maybe twenty. And then another twenty. And they all swarmed over one knife in the third row, 2nd from the left. 'That's your murder weapon,' Scott said. We fanned the flies away, collected the knife, and put it in an evidence bag to take back to the sheriff's office way down in West Palm Beach. Diaz shakes his head. Diaz:'It's more than that. It's not just the killing, but how you kill. Yes, I've killed in the past, but I did it in self-defense. You slaughtered those men.' End:'The problem with modern law-enforcement is that it's too narrow-minded. Come on, Diaz. Do you honestly believe that you absolutely had to kill, even in self-defense? Admit it. You're a cold-blooded killer; you enjoy it as much as I.' Diaz:'I don't like to kill.' End:'Neither do I.' Diaz:'... Then why kill innocent people?' The End seems to be agitated. End:'You just don't get it, Diaz! You think that just because those men weren't convicted of any crimes that they were truly 'innocent'?' There is silence from Diaz. He cannot answer that. Scott, Janice, and I walked back outside and took a look around. Scott walked past the free-range cattle and the open-air chicken-coop, but stops in front of the pigpen. 'Hm...' Janice asked, 'What?' 'Maybe... maybe this place served another purpose besides just the place to kill the owner. I mean, considering that the guy could've been missing for two weeks and we wouldn't have known about it... besides, what better place to hide dead bodies than a place that has it's own small meat processing facilities on-site?' 'Dear, God...' I mustered. 'But then again, it would still be too sloppy to just toss bodies in a meat locker... no, if someone was smart enough to hide here, then they probably--' Janice reached out and tried to pet one of the little pigs. It snapped at her hand, not like it was agitated, but like it was hungry. 'Geez!' A couple more pigs came over and just circled near that area of the fence, waiting for someone to stick their hand out again, I suppose. 'Perhaps...' Scott thought aloud. 'Did you know that when given nothing else to eat, 9 pigs can completely devour a human body in 7 minutes? Maybe that's what this place was for. The killer fed the pigs his victims. There's no way that they could be found like that.' 'What about the bones?' I asked. 'Nah. There's a bone-grinder on site, he could've just thrown them in there and put it in the feed. And by now, who knows if we'll be able to find any actual evidence of the bodies. Sure, we've got a murder weapon and some pictures, but we've only got victims, not a culprit...' Diaz:'You're a sick bastard!' His captor laughs at him while holding him above the pigs. He seems to enjoy this activity such as a child would enjoy their favorite game. A twisted smile appears over his face, his words seemingly cracking the air around him. End:'And who exactly is a criminal investigator suffering from a nervous breakdown and a germ-related obsessive compulsive disorder to be qualified in diagnosing me?' Diaz mouths the words, 'How did he know?' as his body moves slightly back and forth, the wind kicking up. Diaz:'You won't get away with this.' The End shakes his head, disappointed at Diaz's statement. End:'Ugh. I won't dignify that cliché with a response.' Diaz attempts to talk The End down. Diaz:'It's the rules. This, if nothing else, is the point of morality to the story. The good guys always win.' End:'Hehehe. You people are so naive. The 'good guys'? The good guys are nothing more than a created notion of who should get what they want by the end of the story. You and I both have killed many people, and neither of us feels sorry for it. But simply because you wear a piece of copper over your left breast, all of a sudden you're the good guy? You're better than me?' We were ready to get in the car and go back with the new evidence (not that it really helped us much) that Scott showed us when he came up to our car window on Janice's side. He leaned in. 'There's one other thing I think you should see,' he said. I saw a fist reach up and bash Janice in the face. I took my gun out and drew at where Scott was, but he was gone as fast as I was ready to pull the trigger. I opened my door and got out to pursue Scott. Not that he ran. As soon as my head went up from the roof level of the car, I saw the same fist hit me in the eye. A couple more hits, and I was blacking out. The last thing I saw was that Janice was already down for the count, and Scott was taking her out of the car. Diaz wakes up, bound by the wrists and hung above a pen of pigs, suspended on a steel beam. The light rain and cold wind can't as much of a shiver down his spine as does the sight of his captor. He is drenched in blood from head-to toe, and is even on his hands. He is also on a platform, raised above the pigs. So little of him can be made out underneath his blood-covered raincoat. Diaz has nowhere to go but down, and doesn't try to fight out of his ropes, though he's only ten feet off the ground. His fiery red hair is soaked dark, tamed by the ominous weather. His previously unblemished face is now cut on the right cheek, with his left eye blackened shut from his captor's hard, swift punch. Diaz:'There never was an Alan Scott, was there?' End:'Oh, there was. He's been missing for a few weeks now, but I had nothing to do with it...' Looking down over the pigs, Diaz can't understand what's going on. But more than that, he needs to know- Diaz:'Where's Janice?' End:'Oh, her. Heh...' The End simply holds up a package of ground beef.
