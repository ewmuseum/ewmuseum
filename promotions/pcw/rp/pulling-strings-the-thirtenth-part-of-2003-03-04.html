Pulling Strings, The Thirtenth Part Of

4 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 4th, 2003 'Its got to end, its got to end, its got to end.' The husky voice of Reno Enders continually repeated to fall upon deaf ears as he stands above the New York skyline with a tightly clenched fist and a Cuban cigar parted inbetween his pink lips. Thoughts run through his mind faster than a sprinting horse at Chruchill Downs, the many bets tossed through the air as a hush of the crowd solitifies its victor as the cheers rise up once more. For the age of thirty one, going through surgery after the horrific car accident that left him down in the dirt, flipped over with broken legs and tossed arm sockets. It couldn't have been more worse for the man who once could call the man he knew as Dante Jackson, 'Friend.' Slowly, the black cobalt doors circulated at the entrance to his office jerked back, sending a feeling of urgency into the mind of Enders as he waived with a simple two finger gesture. 'Come in.' As nothing even mattered to him, a drenched body walked into Enders' view with flushed eyes, black hair lowering down past his cheeks with aqua colored contacts to fufil to him, a prettier concept of beautiful. 'Boss,' he slowly tells with a drawl, a Northern tinge as Enders blows more smoke through the air. 'When's the man comin'?' 'Not long,' Enders told with a common cough from the smoke. 'He'll be here. His agent told me yesterday morning that Jackson would decided whether or not to cut something up.' 'Cut what?' the proverbial yes man told his boss with quiet jitters as his voice carried far, but carried little power behind it. 'There's nothing to cut for him boss, he's just a man with money behind his name. He'd end his own life before giving you a cut of anything!' Enders took more puffs of the hollow stick before dumping the ashes away in a cold silver ashtray. 'He'll give in, there's no way he can't. Because, if he gives in this way - no exit for him to get out another way.' 'Which is?' Yesman questioned. 'Let me tell you a story Yocke.' Enders began as he took another puff of the cigar. 'This Jackson kid, before he made it was nothing more than a common street hustler. Took work, made work, got money. It funded his way into the rap game. He owes me more than anybody. More than Russell Simmons, more than Marshall Bruce Mathers, more than Eric fuckin' Jackson himself!' Yocke could only nod as Reno began chuckling a bit, harder and harder. It would only escalate into full blown out hysteria. 'Just go pick him up, bring him here, after you get the orders from Tooley.' the final words were told as he stepped out of the room, only longing for a place where he'd be the big fish, not answering to Enders like many of the previous yesmen before him. Yocke nodded, 'Sure boss, sure.' The sky was graying nicely through the dirty kitchen window when Harrison Yocke finished the crossword puzzle and his thrid cup of coffee simultaneously. He turned off the coffeepot, got a conservative cloth coat out of the closet, and locked the door right behind him. He found himself driving a fifteen-year old, rusted out Chrysler that belong to Reno Enders. It had once been royal blue. Now it was just dirty and dark. The seats were trashed. Damage to the left front fender and hood had been repaired with a sledgehammer by an igonrant entuhsiast. The windshield was chipped and cracked. The only feature that might capture the eye of a careful observer was the new Michelin radials, mounted backward to hide the manufacturer's name on the sidewalls. All in all, the car looked like a typical D.C. heap. As an observer might suspect, the Chrysler was difficult to start, damn near impossible on cold mornings. This particular March morning. Harrison Yocke ground and ground with the starter while he played with the manual choke. Eventually the engine fired. It strangled when he pushed the choke off too soon. With a sigh he engaged the starter again. Finally, with coazing, the engine rumbled to life and gave sgins of sustained combustion. Still, she idled rough and spwewed a gray haze that was visible in the rear-view mirror. That, however, was because the original six-cylinder mill had been replaced with a huge old V-8 hemi that had been breathed upon by someone who knew exactly what he was about. Under the crinkled hood was a work of art, complete with racing cams, valves, and pistons, hogged-out valve ports, a high-capacity fuel pump, and a four-barrel crab. To handle the extra power the go-fast man had added a four-speed transmission and beefed up the suspension and brakes. This car could lay rubber for two hundred feet. When the engine had warmed and the idle smoothed somewhat, Harrison Yocke slipped the clutch to get out of the parking place. He couldn't resist: he goosed it once on the street and the tires howled and smoked. With a little paint and bodywork, he told himself, this would be a nice car. He checked his rearview mirrors constantly and darted through lights as they turned red. Finally satisfied that no one was following, he haded for the beltway. Rush-hour traffic was still flowing into the city, so the trip outbound was unimpeded. Once on the beltway, he followed the signs for I-95 south, toward Richmond. The morning was gray and windy. The rain of a few days ago had soaked into the thirsty earth and settled the dust. Still, as dry as the fall had been, the earth needed more. He exited the interstate at Fredericksburg. Five minutes later he drove past the office of a motel and went around to the back side, which faced a hill, and parked. Harrison Yocke stood in the nearly empty lot and stretched. He should have been in bed two hours ago. Get a good job, his grandmother had said, something regular, with a future. He knocked on the door of room 212. 'Just a minute.' The door opened. 'You must be Ender's yes man. Name's Dante Jackson, you pickin' me up?' Jackson talked to Yocke with a smug, as he stepped out the room with a swirl as his baggy Houston Astros 78' throwback along with rainbow colored shorts to match. Both men dropped down the stairwell, hitting the road in Enders' Chrysler once more. 'So what, are you supposed to be doing for Enders, Yocke?' Jackson questions as they drive through the residential area of New York. 'Quietly being his ass buddy?' 'Shut up, I'm third in line, next to him and O'Rourke.' 'Peachy, just peachy. Betcha get a nice fat paycheck for offin' somebody eh?' Yocke nodded. 'Of course, but there are benefits, crucial benefits.' 'Yeah, yeah.' Dante repeated without humor, dryly as his voice ran through a drought period as he latched his hands upon a stryofoam cup filled with Sprite. Slurp. Slurp. Slurp. Glup. Glup. Glup. All the more reasoning that Enders would have a hell of a time trying to coax Jackson into anything.
