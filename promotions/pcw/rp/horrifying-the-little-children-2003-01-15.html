Horrifying the Little Children

15 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 15-01-03 “It’s so wonderful for you to return here, Pete” Pete walks through the halls of William Howard Taft Elementary School in Lakewood Ohio. He looks at the walls, decorated with the artwork of the children, all excited about his return. He looks fondly into each room as he passes them, building again his memories that he cherished so long ago. “It’s great to be back, Mrs. Barr.” Pete said with a smile. “Is the cafeteria still this way?” “Of course. It’s the Pete Cafeteria now. In remembrance of all the time you spent there when you were a student here.” The 60 something woman said. She was definitely a gorgeous woman in her youth, but years of taking care of children, just like Pete have taken the toll on her. She seems tired, walking as if she needs to, not wants to. Her thin body was nearly skeletal in her old age. “Wow. Something named after me! I’m so happy!” Quietly, Pete ducked into a science room. Fifth grade students sat quietly taking a test. He smiled at the kids as they buried their heads into their papers at their tiny desks. One child looked up and smiled at Pete, as if he had seen a superstar. “Pete!” The child cried out, causing the students to look up, first in shock, then utter delight. They cheered and clapped as Pete put his hands over his head and raised the roof, circa 1994. “Hiya kids. Stay in School!” Pete said to them, as they quickly stopped clapping and started looking as if someone farted. Pete just shrugged and smiled at them. “So, what are we doing today?” “Anatomy,” one especially studious student said with a smile. “You know. Bones and stuff.” “Oh yes…I’m very familiar with bones. Lemme take a look at this here.” The student brought up her paper and handed it to Pete. He turned it over and shrieked. He threw it down to the ground and stomped on it. He then fell on top of it, and threw the little girl down on the ground with him. “COUNT!” he bellowed. The girl, scared for her life, counted the pin, and thus, Pete was victorious over the deadly sheet of paper. But this was no ordinary sheet of paper, no. This was an Anatomy test. And what’s a big part of the anatomy? Bones. Skeletal Bones. The little girl looked at Pete, as her class lay silent. Pete looked at all the little faces, and smiled at them. “You never know where that Dark Gimmick guy’s going to come from, you know.” The students laughed at his idiocy once again, and applauded their hero. Pete stood, and held his head high as he walked out of the classroom. He turned right out of the first level room, and started down the hallway with Mrs. Barr in tow. He smelled food, damnit, and by George, he was going to eat! Pete got in line for the cafeteria. A few kids were in front of him. He looked at the delightful food and his mouth watered. Today is baked chicken day, a day he was always fond of here at Taft. He looked at the almost radioactive green sauce and knew he needed it. Now. He looked at the 5 children ahead of him in line and quickly devised a plan. “Hey,” Pete said. “Look! It’s Jason Snow!” Quickly the kids scampered out of line and out into the hall as they shouted and screamed PCDUB! Over and over. Pete quickly moved into their spots and requested a triple helping. The lunch lady gladly gave him the portion. Two legs and a big breast. Pete kindly asked her to step off his plate, and said he was hungry for chicken, not some lower level lickin. She grimaced and handed Pete a tray. He slowly got out of line, and peered across the tiny room, looking for a chair. He found one near some little black boys. “Hey kids, is this seat taken?” Pete said with a smile. “Foo! We ain’t letting no cracka be sittin up in herre! Even if you do be famous.” Said the foul mouthed youngster. “That’s not very nice to speak to your elders that way…” “Listen herre cracka ass nigga! Ain’t no muthafuckin cracka be sittin wit our gang, muthafucka!” Another boy stood up into Pete’s face. “What the…fine.” Pete walks away from the little boys, glaring back at them. Next to the rowdy rowdy and bout it bout it little boys was another little black child dressed in a suit. He had two chairs between him. Pete grabbed one of them and sat down. “Hey Cracka ass bitch! We told yo muthafuckin ass that you ain’t gunna be sittin by us! You want me to pull out my gat foo?” said the first little boy. Another little boy attempted to take Pete’s tray. Pete quickly grabbed a fork and stabbed the little boy in the hand. The boy shrieked out like a little girl, but amid the loud lunch room, no one really heard. “Excuse me? I’m not here to be abused by you, you little nigglets. Sit down, shut the fuck up and let me eat in peace!” Pete said glaring at the little boys. “Awww…hell naw! You didn’t just do that. Tell me yo cracka ass didn’t just…” “Didn’t I tell your black ass to sit down and shut the fuck up? You DON’T mess with a fat man and his food.” Suddenly, the five little boys stood and charged Pete. Pete knocked two of them down with his massive girth. Children stood and cheered the boys on. One of them jumped on Pete’s back and tried to poke out his eyes. The other two grabbed his knees and bit. Pete screamed out and kicked one of the little black boys off his foot. He picked the foot up, and kicked the other little boy in the head a few times. Finally, the kid let go. Quickly, the other two boys were up. They charged Pete again, bearing sporks. Pete dodged the sporks and spun around, using the little boy on his back to hit the other two little boys moving past him. The two boys fell to the ground. Pete then grabbed the other little boy by the neck and dropped him straight forward. Children stood stunned that this grown man had just beaten down 5 members of the chess club.
