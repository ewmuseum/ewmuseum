NVG Medical Report #1

8 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Sept. 8, 2002 From Dr. Kisaragi's Medical Report on Patient Akiyama, September 8, 2002:Patient Name: AkiyamaCause of Hospitalization/Injury: Car accident, resulting in major damage to the heart and arteries; massive bleeding and blood loss internally and exteriorly.Patient Diagnosed: September4, 2002Released: September 5, 2002 Start: Date: September 5, 2002 There was something more to this man called Akiyama that I had realized. He was brought in Tuesday night at 11:09 pm by EMTS reporting of massive blood loss and internal bleeding. They brought him into ER and immediately began to operate on the large man, patching the wounds he had and working to stop the internal bleeding. I remember being called in for to help on this one just as I was getting off duty of the night shift. As I rushed down the hall, I noticed a tall Asian woman wearing some out of this world getup standing in front of the viewing window next to the ER. She watched intently as the doctors worked over the Asian man on the table. Something about her called my attention to her. Not just her appearance, but also her overall presence demanded attention. I asked her if he was family to her. She didn’t look away from the operating table, but she nodded slowly. He and I are from one clandestine, she said. Her voice had a tinkle to it that made every syllable seem magic. I didn’t know what she meant by that, but she seemed to have some reason to be there. I asked her how he got in this condition, and she replied saying that someone who betrayed her ran him down. I took that as a hit and run and nodded. We got those every once in a while…it was sad to think that people would be cowardly and run after plowing over someone in an automobile. Then I looked into the operating room and saw the patient covered in this black gel substance. I rushed into the ER and yelled at them all. Why isn’t the patient cleansed for operating, I asked. They all looked at me with strange looks. Then one nurse looked at me and motioned a cross over herself. “This black liquid IS his blood.” It took all night, but by 6:39 AM, we had stabilized his condition. He was placed in Critical Condition to heal from his wounds for a few days. His body needed to replenish his blood or he wouldn’t be able to stand for less than three seconds. All during the operation, the woman with the black and blue hair stood and watched us clot the blood flow. At some points the blood flowed like water from a faucet, but we had no choice but to keep at it. She didn’t look upset, or worried at all about her ally. All she did was stare at him with a cold look. It made me wonder if she was responsible for his condition. I came in to check on him, and the patient named Akiyama was sitting in the corner of the room staring at the wall. I rushed over to him to lead him back to bed, but found that he wasn’t moving from that spot. Then I heard something behind me. I turned around and saw the woman from before standing there watching me. I asked her why she allowed him to leave the bed, and she replied saying that he had received all he could from this place. I didn’t know what she meant, but she made it clear that they were leaving when she slipped a black cloak over the man and covered him up. The man was walking slowly…taking long strides at a careful pace. “Look at him,” I said. “He can barely walk! Let him rest!” “There is no rest for the god. All there is now is completion.” That’s what the woman said as they walked towards the door. I ran in front of them and blocked the door with my body. I had worked all night on this man, and I would not have them waste my work like this. The woman stepped dead into my face and stared at me. As I remember now, looking into her icy eyes was like staring into the sun. As I became lost in the colors, I blacked out. When I woke up, I was lying on the patient’s bed. It was noon. Date: October 8, 2002 Today I managed to schedule a checkup session with the patient. We met at a doctor’s office in Portland, Oregon, where the woman going by the name Cyclonica said that they had business there. When I saw the patient, his skin was pale, and his hair was limp. He sat on the examination table and stared ahead, his eyes were lifeless and empty. Next to him stood Cyclonica, looking the same as she did when we first met. I made sure not to look into her eyes, as I didn’t know what happened to me the first time, and I didn’t want it to happen again. I checked his reflexes, which were normal by human standards. I say human standards because I wasn’t sure what this man was after seeing his insides. Pulse was, for a lack of better words, sporadic. It wasn’t a solid beat like what you and I have…but more like it had a rapid rhythm like a techno song. Taking the blood pressure indicated that his pressure was drastically low…even for human standards. But he still looked like he was able to function…but there was no change in his condition. I informed his ward Cyclonica of my results, and she nodded slowly. I said that if he were going to be the same again, he would need a blood transfusion. The problem there would be finding a blood type to match his rare blood type. “Donors are already lined up and ready to be drained for him.” She said to me. “Our life…for his.” She walked out, with Akiyama cloaked again. I didn’t know what to make of her words…but at this point, all I can do now is wait until our next scheduled meeting. SIGNED BY: MIKADO KISARAGI, PH.D
