Give generously, won't you?

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: not entered (We see your typical suburban male playing baseball in the yard with his 2.5 children. [Don’t ask.] Begin narrative voiceover:) Voice: “Meet Jeff. He has a good job, a fine home and a loving family.” (Cut to Jeff riding a mountain bike across broken forest terrain.) Voice: “He leads an active lifestyle, biking in the summer, skiing in the winter, and kayaking whenever he can find the time.” (Cut to Jeff in an Oxford shirt and tie, talking to a suited political type.) Voice: “He’s an outstanding member of his community, he routinely participates in neighborhood projects simply because it’s the ‘right thing to do’.” (Cut to Jeff in hardhat, pounding nails into pieces of lumber.) Voice: “Once a week, Jeff helps to build low cost housing for the poor.” (Cut to Jeff ladling out food in a soup kitchen.) Voice: “And he can often be found here, donating his time at the River Street Missionary. All in all, Jeff has the perfect life… or does he?” (Cut to Jeff at a doctor’s office, getting his blood pressure checked. He looks straight at the camera.) Jeff: “Three months ago I was diagnosed with a case of Lightweight title.” Voice: “Yes, Lightweight title.” (Cut to a medical clean room, where scientific-types measure and chart and boil strangely colored liquids.) Voice: “Here at the Bradley Institute, a subsidiary of the K.Z.K. Industries division, researchers work around the clock studying this silent threat.” (Cut to tech, her identity concealed beneath lab coat, safety glasses, and one of those shower cap things. Caption reads: Dr. Eunice Aloumbi.) Dr. Aloumbi: “Every year, thousands of people become infected, and hundreds more suffer relapses. Though we have drugs and surgeries that can help treat the disease and alleviate some of the suffering, there currently is no cure.” (Cut back to Jeff at the park, pushing a darling little imp of a girl on a swing set.) Voice: “Though there is no cure at the moment, medical science marches on and no one can even begin to predict what the future will bring. Be assured though, when the cure is found, K.Z.K. I.E.&P. will be right there at the forefront to turn a hefty profit on it. For you, and most especially… for the children.” (Cut to Jeff climbing a sheer cliff face with his bare hands. He looks at the camera.) Jeff: “I have a Lightweight title, but it doesn’t have me!” (Camera pulls back to emphasize how big and dangerous this climb is before dissolving to show THE Multiple Man sitting in a comfy chair, calmly puffing bubbles upon a MASSIVE, multi-bowled pipe.) Tmm: “Hi there, my name is THE Multiple Man and I am a professional athlete. I too, have suffered from the scourge of Lightweight Title for over two years now. But with the help of the Bradley Institute coupled with the support of my good friends at K.Z.K. I.E.&P., my case has been battled into remission. Though it I might relapse at any time, I am confident that one day, I will be permanently cured.” (Superimposed picture of Anton the Kid appears.) Tmm: “But others aren’t so lucky as I am. Each new day brings more tales of infection as this pestilence spreads across the face of the planet. Please, give generously to the ‘Save Anton the Kid’ fund, because he really needs the help. Thank you.” (Camera fades out.)
