Illness pt. 2 : Diagnosis

16 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-16-03 “Yes, of course,” A.J. Trinidad calmly speaks into his cell phone standing outside of a Gloria Jean’s coffee shop on Michigan Ave. in downtown Chicago. “Everything will happen as it needs to, no exception, no worries. Yes, I understand.” Trinidad clicks his phone shut as Amira saunters from inside the aforementioned coffee shop with a steaming cup of hazelnut latte in her hands. The heat spilling from the top of the cup is intensified by the brittle cold weather on this day as business type people attempt to take advantage of the only break in the windy rain to run and grab some lunch. “Who was that?” Amira asks as she carefully sips at her cup, barely touching her bottom lip to the glass as the vapor crawls up around her face. “Pardon?” A.J. questions back Amira admiring how adorable she looks while trying not to burn her tongue. “On the phone,” she responds. “You were just on the phone, who was it?” “I wasn’t just on the phone.” A.J. explains obviously believing he wasn’t. “A.J.” Amira begins with an accusing tone in her voice. “You still have the cell phone in your hand. Haven’t even put it back in your pocket yet. Now who were you talking to?” A.J. looks down to his hand noticing he does indeed brandish the silver flip style Motorola phone before smiling back up to Amira, “I’ll be damned, I do have the phone in my hand, but I don’t remember taking a phone call. The thing ringed and there was a high pitched buzzing sound, now that I think of it, the noise was very annoying, but there was nobody there, so I hung up.” Amira shakes her head towards A.J., “I was watching you from inside, you were speaking to somebody, I can tell by your mannerisms on the phone, and your mouth was moving.” “I honestly didn’t talk to anyone, not that I remember anyway,” A.J. says trying to defend himself. “Well, go to the call log, see who called you, call them back and see who it was.” Amira insists politely. Knowing that sometimes you just don’t argue, A.J. glances at the phone, searches through the call log and finds his most recent received call. “It says that the number is unavailable,” A.J. informs Amira. “God, this is so weird.” Pocketing the phone, A.J. and the observably flustered Amira begin to walk amongst the crowd down Michigan Ave. towards the Chicago River. A bone chilling wind tears through the concrete jungle that had made people dig their winter coats back out of the closet today, accompanied by the icy rain once again. A.J. reaches up holding the side of his head and abruptly stopping in his tracks gathering Amira’s attention away from her coffee. “What’s wrong A.J.?” Amira asks concerned. “I don’t know” A.J. retorts. “I have been getting these headaches, then I feel as if though I’ve left my body. It feels like I’m floating almost, I become absent of all feeling, then everything goes black. Sometimes it’s a minute, other times it’s been hours, even days when I wake up.” “A.J., that doesn’t sound healthy” says Amira as her unease noticeably elevates. “After what happened the other day at the press conference, I think maybe we should get this checked out. Go to a neurologist or something.” “What happened at the press conference?” questions A.J. “I was at a press conference?” “You don’t remember that either? You passed out at the table, we took you backstage, but then you woke up, you said everything was fine, so we just went back to the hotel until it was time to go to Turmoil.” Amira responds, her tone becoming more and more concerning. “Turmoil!” exclaims A.J. “I missed Turmoil?” “No A.J.” Amira says as she puts her arm around A.J.’s shoulders and pulls him under a stoop to get out of the rain for a moment. “You were at Turmoil, you wrestled, we left and came home afterwards. You said we needed to get home for something.” “What day is it?” asks a thoroughly confused Trinidad. “Wednesday”, Amira answers him pulling his face to hers so she can look into his eyes. “A.J., something is wrong with you, I’m taking you to the hospital to get looked at.” “Awe Amira, you know how much I hate hospitals.” A.J. pleads against her wishes. “I’m sure I just took a knock to the head during the match at Turmoil, it’ll all pass soon enough.” “Not this time Angel, we’re getting you checked out before the PPV this weekend!” Amira says almost yelling at A.J. this time. Amira only used A.J.’s real name when she was very serious, bringing “Angel” out evidently left Trinidad with no room for argument this time, so when she grabbed his jacket sleeve to drag him away, he had no choice but to follow. The scene fades out up into the sky as A.J. and Amira disappear amongst the walking traffic of a busy downtown Chicago day.
