Accusations Part 2

30 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 30th May 2003 [ ] – Sivasian speaking directly to the reader, his thoughts. :: :: - Action, and reflection on other stuff from a narrator Krikorian: ‘’ – Someone speaking [ So this is what had happened. I’d been offered a free 3 day holiday to anywhere in the middle east. I chose Iraq, I wanted to check up on the American soldiers, and make sure they weren’t doing anything suspicious, as I assumed they were. I did, however, get arrested almost immediately. It was by some ignorant American soliders. Accusing me of something deadly serious. ] :: Sivasian stares into the bullet hole eyes of the soldier and drops to his knees. He cups his face with his hand, and screeches in desperation. :: Krikorian: ‘You’re joking me right; you can’t be serious. You think I, an intenational wrestling superstar, one of the best wrestlers in the world would do this?’ :: One of the American soldiers, obviously with a sense of humour, speaks to him. :: Soldier #2: ‘Although I have no doubt you’re in Primetime Championship Wrestling sir, I must say that I believe that you’re not one of the best wrestlers in the world sir, you’re a contender for the Rising Star Title I believe, and that’s….’ :: The third American soldier, mimicking something from the Matrix, wearing a suit and with a pair of black shades, even though he is carrying a gun, steps forward. :: Soldier #3: ‘Ok Jimmy, I think that’s enough. Listen Mr.Krikorian, you’ll need to come with us, we have some questioning.’ :: Sivasian suddenly turns aggressive and his face, full of blood, turns a deep crimson like red. :: Krikorian: ‘No, fuck you. You think you’re so big and mighty because you can shoot an Iraqi with bad weapons with your own. Fuck you, I’ve done nothing wrong, you’ll have to make me go.’ :: The scrap is brief and painless for all parties involved, if not a little uncomfortable for Sivasian. Having three men, in total about 750 pounds of mainly muscle, is something only people with super human strength like Ivan Stanislav or Meanstreak would be able to match. Sivasian, although well built at 260 pounds and some six foot three, isn’t a match, and his strength buckles. Handcuffs are soon applied, and the soldiers drag Sivasian weary body into a small van. His strength has been drained, and he now puts up no resistance as he is sat in the back of an open van. A machine gun to his left would be something Sivasian could hit anyone with from up to a mile, but he feels he doesn’t have the strength, and the handcuffs mean he would have to operate the gun in reverse. That, along with having a headhunt on him all over the world for killing American soldiers in Iraq means the idea is soon taken away. However, it makes him even more aware of the situation. He is being arrested for killing an American soldier, or at least being accused of it. This could bring serious implications not only for his career but to his life. He could be placed in jail for life, or, in a worse case scenario, his life be taken. :: :: He walked casually towards the building. He could tell it was a typical investigations building. He had been through this when he was at the peak of his sniping skills in the Ukrainian army. The Americans, with him training three rookies, had caught him in West Germany. If he were by himself, he would have shot himself out, but he didn’t want to risk the life’s of three rookies. And so, he let the Americans take, and interrogate him. Of course, had had escaped, for the guard had been foolish enough to hand him his pistol. It only had three bullets in, but one shot to the temple of the guard, and taking out each of the guards standing at their posts at the entrance to the prison, with one shot each, he had escaped. :: :: The building was dimly lit. Sivasian was dragged to a room. It felt stuffy and immediately hot. Sivasian sat in a chair and stared at the bright young man at the other side of the table. :: Detective: ‘Hello sir, you’re Sivasian Krikorian, correct?’ :: Sivasian nodded. He didn’t believe this American deserved him wasting his time on speaking. :: Detective: ‘I know you’re wondering about a lawyer, you’ve seen it on television probably. However, this is the desert, it’s the military, we don’t do that bullshit here, you get nothing, you get yourself, you and Sivasian Krikorian to help you. ‘ :: Indeed , Sivasian had been wondering about a lawyer, but that had just been thrown out of the building. :: Detective: ‘You’re under the arrest for the killing James Brown, sergeant James Brown.’ :: Sivasian smirks a little, in some ways in laughter and in some ways in the fact he simply cannot believe such charges are being brought against him. :: Detective: ‘Sir, you may be wondering why we came to you and put you under charges, correct?’ :: Indeed he was, Sivasian had thought of every question under the sun in his head, but still didn’t want to speak to the American. Still, he simply nodded, as though he were bidding for something at a public auction. :: Detective: ‘Well sir, he was shot through the head, by a 1929 wooden rifle, only 20 were made, by a man called Sergei Krikorian, you’re uncle I believe?’ :: True [check out the ‘History of Sivasian’ RPs for this fact] , he did have a rifle made by his uncle. A piece of work, a piece of master craft. It was worth money, and cost a lot back in the 20’s, only 19 others were in place. In fact, the Germans burned 4 of them in WW2 , when Russian snipers were caught. They were known as an elite rifle. They became the best once you’d had training. The Germans didn’t want the Russians to utilize the rifle, and so destroyed them. Sivasian shrugged. He KNEW he hadn’t killed that man, but he pondered in his mind who else had the guns. Most were in museums, only three were world widely used. Sivasian had one at home, sure, in fact, he brought it to Iraq to show the rebels how to snipe Americans properly. :: Detective: ‘We were informed that the shot was incredible as well sir. It was from 1.2 miles, it was in a wind, in a hailstorm. Only someone of a huge sniping calibre could pull it off. This is why we pulled you in sir, you see you are very skilled in the art of shooting. You were in Iraq yesterday when it happened, and you are the only person we believe who could have pulled off such a , and ironic for me to say, brilliant shot. ‘ :: Sivasian remained in silence as the Detective stared him deep into the eyes. ::
