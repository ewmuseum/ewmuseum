Transition Into Madness: Final Episode (4), Chapter 1

28 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 28, 2002 (Excerpted from the memoirs of Bruce Shanahan; first and final part of entry omitted for various reasons) ...Tears streamed down my face, warm and caressing against my sore and throbbing skin. My body was enveloped in a perpetual cloak of pain, skin red and cracked and bleeding under the swift climate change when the planet’s axis had reportedly shifted. Yet my own physical anguish remained trivial in light of the acute devastation wrought upon the wasteland called Earth, its effects wracking my spirit with mourning and loss. This loss was simply more than I could bear; it was a pain unparalleled in all these years of desensitization, a queer and alien emotion contrasted against the background of implacable hatred and bitterness that had consumed my life. Why had this happened? My eyes elevated from the scorched earth to my desolate surroundings. The heavens bathed in a lifeless gray hue and dotted with those formless, inky blotches that were once majestic clouds, a poison wind whistling over the barren landscape as it imparted its foul embrace on the toxic terrain of barren plains and tainted seas. Only days ago Death had swarmed like a hell-inflicted plague about this now-lifeless rock, and now as if by some stroke of heavenly reprimand I wandered as a tragic vagabond across the blackened land and the sprawling graveyards the human race once called cities. In the distance lay the decaying necropolis once known as Cleveland, with the tainted waters of Lake Erie lapping at the shore. Those waters I was assured were now infested with various species of unnamable fiends spawned from the recesses of the pit and beyond, at this very moment spreading their hellish seed in light of their recent liberation onto this crippled world. Repentance would be of no consequence; our Judgment Day had arrived. I had tried to warn against those foolish drilling expeditions into the Antarctic; but corporate America remained ignorant to my cautionary pleas, drowned by the greed in their own hearts. My cries (and that of the Blackhelm Coalition) echoed in vain, and that greed of the Global Energy Alliance had directly led to the Cleansing, darkly prophesized in archaic texts. Few of us first realized when those monstrous antiquities of millenia long past crawled from their abyssal crypts of slumber, ending a merciful hibernation stretching countless aeons. Their primordial imprisonment had been prematurely ended by the profound avarice in the hearts of the executives of that global conglomerate, their greed unknowingly being the very condemnation of the planet and ultimately the bulk of the human race. Initially their activities were limited with few implications, but within hours of their release from the divine prisons that had confined them so long, the Cleansing had begun. It had been decreed in the revelations of the Mad Arab Abdul Alhazred among the passages of his notorious Necronomicon, with parallel theories amid a plethora of other horrid texts. And now the prophecies had come to pass. From tombs buried deep beneath in the Cambodian jungle to ancient glaciers in the far north to the yet unlocated crypt of the sunken R’lyeh. Madness had spread like wildfire across the Earth as psychotic behavior has increased tenfold in the asylums, hideous visions plaguing those irrational souls imprisoned within their walls and driving them to near riots. This had been the prelude. Within hours, anarchy has become the world order and civilization had been reduced to the charred shambles that now stretched before me. The realization that those once-confined beasts now roamed about the planet left me trembling; the knowledge their innumerous lackeys also roved the planet, a result of the various dimensional gateways scattered about the Earth being opened only hours following their release, did nothing to quell my budding trepidation. Our once lush planet stood ravaged beyond repair and deprived of life. Survivors existed in fortified outposts on the outcroppings of civilization, protected by surviving members of the scattered and disordered Blackhelm Coalition and what few relics of archaic origin the group originally used to wage their eternal war on the malevolent deities. In spite of this, Earth now stood as a veritable nirvana to the rampaging cosmic fiends. As I presently trekked aimlessly over the ruined ground that was once a grassy pasture, I tried to remember exactly what had happened to cause this; I recalled the general source as being the GEA’s declination to halt drilling in the Antarctic region but little else. Things remained cloudy and I couldn’t recall for the life of me where it had all begun. Why?! It’s all a blank…. Wretched dreams…..so many questions yet so few answers….. * * Shanahan stepped from the terminal into the open parking lot bordering the airport just outside Rutland, Vermont with a small carry-on bag slung over his burly right shoulder. The rays of the April sun glinted off his reflective shades as his eyes skimmed the relatively vacant lot, searching for signs of his local host to appear. The region surrounding the isolated airport was northern wilderness, the town of Rutland itself slightly south to the airport. It was only the second time Bruce had ever been in the true countryside and the lack of fumes and smog in his lungs was an unfamiliar feeling. “Bruce! Here!” He cast his eyes left to see the lean figure of Captain Marcus Stryker resting against a support pillar under the overhang, waving one hand over his head. Bruce glanced down at his watch, taking a moment to adjust the silver band and then crossing the outer platform to meet Stryker. The younger man greeted Bruce with a firm handshake and grin. “I have the Land Rover parked across the lot,” he informed Bruce. “Let’s get going, I bet you’re fairly busy and want to get this over with.” “It’d be nice to get to Cincinatti without this sh*t weighing on my mind, Marcus. Last thing I want on my mind while I’m fightin’ through an airport is what I’m doin’ bein’ involved with some organization I don’t have the faintest clue about.” The duo plod across the parking lot, the small Adidas carry-on slung over Bruce’s left shoulder. He pitches it into the back seat as he climbs into the passenger side and slams the door, Stryker already warming up the engine. Without nary a word, he goes into reverse and pulls out of the space and likewise with the airport lot. The awkward silence continues nearly 5 minutes until Bruce casts his uneasy gaze in the direction of his host. “Before you and your ‘colleagues’ go and dump a ton of your rhetoric on me when I reach your little command center, mind givin’ me an idea what to expect? And how about tellin’ just what in God’s name happened the other night in my sleep?” Stryker, apparently expecting the question, darts his eyes uneasily in Shanahan’s direction. “It was really an insidious trap, Bruce,” sighed the captain despondently. “First you must come to understand that every man with us in your dreams has natural telepathic abilities. All humans have that talent, it’s all a matter of releasing it; all those with us have been taught to exercise their particular talent and have been trained into its use and also in the methods of our Blackhelm Coalition. And unfortunately we lost many of our best telepaths in that assault….we were oblivious to the activities of the CCD.” “I don’t follow…..” “We knew they were coming for you, our telepathic ‘scouts’ had picked up psychic messages between agents of the CCD. Unfortunately we didn’t pick up messages of it all being a trap. Major players in the Cycle realize we’ve been watching you, and they used you as bait to lure us in. They essentially f*cked with your head while you slept, instilling their own version of the Junkyard match into your dreams. We were using our own technology to keep tabs on you, and picked up their ‘scent’. We moved in to ambush, but to our chagrin it became obvious we were the ones set up. And if we would have stayed there a minute longer the massacre would have increased tenfold.” The whole situation was eerie to Jack, like something out of a bad movie. “It’s all still a haze. So what about that flyin’ thing and that big octopus-lookin’ monster? They two of those Gods?....” “If either of those accursed devils was one of “those Gods” then we wouldn’t have escaped that junkyard-turned-slaughterhouse nightmare with our sanity,” replied Stryker, rotating the wheels lightly left to turn onto the highway. “You see, the thought patterns of the ‘upper crust’ of their society are so bizarre not even our most gifted psychics or scholars can decipher them and their psychic shielding have driven men mad; some have even become possessed and begun rambling mindlessly in demonic alien tongues before being subdued. Our telepaths get their information from the lower level minions and Lesser Overlords. Shaka B’ur’athe and Dlygth Shfsd’od are more along the line of overlords, second-string subordinates to various other slumbering deities we have yet to determine. Those two get their orders from the dreams of their hibernating masters, wherever on God’s Green Earth they happen to be. And if we didn’t get out when we did, we had a few more surprises coming our way from the west. More were massed to the west and making a beeline for us when we managed to escape. They’re shrewd, Bruce. The CCD and their higher minions are clever indeed….” “Alright, what the f*ck are ‘CCD’?” interjected Jack, resting his head on a clenched fist propped on the sill of the window . “I’m still tryin’ to understand what the hell is goin’ on here and you keep throwin’ all this technical bullsh*t and these nicknames in my face.” “CCD is the abbreviated name labeled to those blasphemous cosmic deities by our scholars who have dedicated their lives to studying them. It stands for Cthulhu Cycle Deities, the term comprises all those associated with those wretched celestials from beyond the stars that we and our allies fight and attempt to suppress….” “Cthulhu!” chimed in Jack with an amused chuckle. “HAHA! Are you nuts, Marcus?! Ain’t that some story written 50 years ago? You guys are basin’ all this ‘research’ on a demented universe created in the mind of some strugglin’ author?! It’s like some cult of Star Wars fanatics tryin’ to track down a non-existent clan of Wookies!....” “Lovecraft was somewhat disturbed but his stories are more than mere hoax,” countered Stryker. “He was a vessel, as are many of his contemporaries. True, some continue his written legacy for the sake of entertainment and others as a tribute. But others have the same morbid visions he did over 50 years ago and transpose it into written text as he did. Cover-ups are necessary sometimes for sake of retaining public order.” “And those gremlin things? Were they just figments of my imagination or somethin’?” “Those things that attacked in hordes are strictly what we call ‘Dream Minions’. They don’t exist in physical form, at the very least not on our planet, but we’ve learned they can be spiritually massed into hordes from whatever foreign dimension they dwell on. Unfortunately, Shaka B’ur’athe and Dlygth Shfsd’od are real. We have yet to find the location of their Earthly slumber, but there have been references to them in a collection of cultish texts only the Coalition has access to. We believe Shfsd’od to be concealed somewhere below the Australian outback with B’ur’athe laying dormant somewhere around the Arctic Circle. But then, we haven’t confirmed anything. Purely speculation.” The car veers off the interstate highway onto the exit below, reaching the outskirts of Rutland and coming to a stop at an intersection. Stryker gazes left to right as cars skim by. “Rather awkward location for a damn monster-hunting operation HQ, out in the middle of nowhere. You sure you ain’t pullin’ my leg?” “I assure you I’m not, my man,” guarantees Stryker with a shift of the gears as he pulls out into traffic. “We’re not the Pentagon but we like to think we have a respectable little venture going here. Federal funding goes a long way for us here actually. Especially the secret funding we get from the agency we’re affiliated with…..” Bruce cocks an inquisitive eyebrow at Marcus. “Huh?” Stryker, not removing his eyes from the road, cracks a sheepish smile. “We’ll give you a short briefing when reach the university, be patient.” “I’ve never heard of a university being the hub of something this extreme before. What the hell is this place? The Vermont Institute of Monster Hunting?” “Honestly you’re probably a lot closer than you probably think you are,” replies Stryker with a snicker. “All the students are here for your normal majors and have no idea what else goes on beyond closed doors, mind you. But some of the faculty have more responsibility and knowledge reaching beyond chemistry 101 and teaching the Pythagorean Theorem. I’m more than just the ROTC instructor, put it that way. Ah, here we are….” He maneuvers the vehicle down a long avenue lined with picket-fence surrounded rural homes, pulling into a broad parking lot with only a half-dozen vehicles spread about the lot. Bruce peers at the modest row of old-fashioned brick buildings across the empty street, all painted with a basic brown coat and few other markings beside the building title scrawled in black lettering on the side walls. Stryker throws open the door and steps onto the pavement, standing between a pair of freshly painted yellow lines with hands in his pockets. His blond hair wafts as a gentle breeze blows by, the bright sun causing him to shield his eyes as he sets his eyes on the wavering American and Vermont state flags with the New Miskatonic University flag billowing slightly below. Bruce runs a hand over his exposed right bicep, his calloused hand grazing a scar adorning his upper forearm. “You’re right,” he quipped. “It ain’t no Pentagon is it? At least it’s not overly feminine or anythin’…..” Stryker started toward the crosswalk on the corner, gesturing toward Shanahan to follow. “C’mon, Bruce. We’ll give you a short tour of the grounds before we get into the meat and potatoes of what goes on here.” Shanahan lets out a sigh, slamming the door with a jarring show of force and following Stryker. “Please keep an open mind as we explain things to you, Bruce,” requests Stryker. “Ya don’t have to worry about that,” ensures Jack. “To even be here I’d wager a guy has to be fairly open-minded…”
