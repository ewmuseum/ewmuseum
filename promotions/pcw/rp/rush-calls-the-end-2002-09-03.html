Rush calls The End?

3 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/03/02 *Ring-ring-ring* Everybody and their mother looks around like an Atomic Bomb alarm just went off. Boy:“What’s that?” Young Man:“I don’t know.” Little Girl:“Mommy, I’m scared!” Woman:“It’s okay, baby; everything will be okay.” While everyone in the Coral Square Mall Food Court hides under their tables, an elderly man wearing black pants with suspenders on a blue casual dress shirt. His plae white skin is weathered and tough; it’s been through war and hardships just as much as any of the good times. He takes a sweep of the room with his eyes, watching the masses cower under their tables as the ringing continues. Fed up with the nonsense, the old man yells at everyone in an attempt to reach their faculties of common sense. Old Man:“Can’t you tell a phone when you hear it? Dang whippersnappers, all of ya so used to phones that sing and dance when you have a call... What’s the world coming to?” The coin fountain in the center of the food court, themed with many cherubs, spurts out water that splashes a few drops here and there on The End’s leg. He puts down his Chik-Fila chicken sandwich and wipes his mouth and hands with the napkin by his side. End:“Gotta phone call...” The End presses the “Answer” button on the phone and the ringing stops. Everyone comes out from under their tables and looks at The End. They recognize the phone, but he doesn’t wear his outfit in public so he can walk around without being hassled by PCW fans. It pays not to have any pictures of your face published for public records. He’s actually dressed much like the old man, except without suspenders. And or course, End isn’t white and can walk upright. Old Man:“Spoiled, the lot ‘a ya!” The crowd of modern day Americans, extremely sensitive and unwilling to tolerate anyone pointing out their personal flaws, surrounds the man and bears down upon him with punches and kicks. Young Man:“This’ll teach you to inform us about our own shortcomings!” Woman:“No one’s kicking his head! Quick, kick his head!” But enough about that... End:“Hello?” Telemarketer:“Mr. Green? Hello, my name is Mr. Mason, and I am calling from DaimlerChrysler to inform you that we have some wonderful new cars that we would like you to endorse. They’ve got a mess of new features including GPS, elastic metal frame, and airbags all around to keep the passenger safe in a crash. We’re thinking of something along the lines of you driving the car and saying, Even I can drive this car without fear of injury.” The End listens carefully to the voice of the telemarketer. End:“Rush?” Scrambling papers and a nervous voice come from the other end of the phone line. Telemarketer:“Uh... No. I don’t know what you’re talking about.” End:“Mr. Mason, huh? What’s your first name?” Telemarkter:“Russ... Um, Russell. Yeah, it’s Russell.” End:“I know it’s you, Rush. Don’t call me anymore, okay?” The End hangs up his phone. Over in DaimlerChrysler headquarters, Mr. Russell Mason closes the line from his end. He rubs his eyes and leans back in his cubicle, his bald head shedding flakes onto his white shirt. Telemarketer:“Everyone keeps calling me Rush when I call them. What are they talking about?”
