Besieged: Dawn

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 28 of 2003 A.D. “5:52...Jesus....” Stryker gripped the kitchen counter, gazing almost absently with glazed-over eyes onto his watch before glancing into the waning darkness. His legs wobbled from exhaustion as those obscene shapes prowling the shadows began to dissipate, creeping back into the thick brush in a forced retreat to return to whatever hellish abyss had spawned them. The sprawling pasture was littered with bodies, the soulless husks that had been maliciously controlled throughout the night by Shanahan and whatever forces over which he held sway. The living dead of Brayston were finally free, though the cost was more than Stryker wished he and his team were forced to bear. His eyes abruptly wandered to the vacant den where shards of glass lay scattered at the foot of shattered windows, strewn amongst empty shell casings and a collection of empty Styrofoam cups. Sunlight was beginning to peek over the horizon in the form of a meek orange hue and the darkness was lifting from the valley and surrounding mountain range, hence condemning the ill-fated night attack perpetrated by the Sect of Black Wisdom. Stryker knew Bruce’s tactics and the capabilities of his dark force, and realized they couldn’t continue a sustained campaign in the daylight; the formless demon-spawn he conjured through his blasphemous rites couldn’t exist in light and they maintained the bulk of his power, as they required more than simple gunfire to vanquish. Stryker was aware of stirring on the upper floor, and the subsequent creaking of the hallway floorboards and stairs on the second floor. A figure in a soiled coat ambled down the staircase, cleaning a pair of dirty spectacles with a thin handkerchief. Stryker, utterly worn out, managed a meager grin in the direction of his associate. “Ah….doctor. Things are dying down out there, daylight’s coming.” “They can’t subsist in light,” commented Kilborn, stating what Delta Fox’s commander already knew. “And they don’t dare stage anything during the daytime hours. All that Shanahan has are his cultists.” “And they’re too inept to be effective in combat,” mused Stryker, cradling his rifle in his arms. “Most are just kids anyhow, never even held a hunting rifle let alone tried fighting any sort of guerilla campaign against seasoned ops. Bruce knows that.” Kilborn frowned, looking to Marcus. “He learned from you during the time you were allies, didn’t he? Military tactics and the like?” Stryker could only offer a reluctant nod in response. “Unfortunately he’s a fast learner. Beneath all the hatred and resentment, there’s a brilliant mind. It’s just a shame it was tainted years ago with rage and spite instead of a desire to benefiting humanity.” “A mind’s a terrible thing to waste.” “It’s not being wasted, doc. It’s merely been poisoned with evil ideas and abominable theories I hate to even think about. And he’s bent on bringing those ideas to fruition.” “That’s why we’re here, captain. To ensure he can’t do that. Or at least to get an idea of his capabilities. Hopefully we can manage a way to catalogue the library he has in that house and find any relics we have information on in our records. Possibly any firearms…..” Stryker scoffed at the latter remark. “He doesn’t have many of those, I guarantee it,” he offered in retort. “When you have the blessing of Nyarlathotep and the very whims of the cosmos are twisted in your favor, you have very little need for conventional weaponry. The armies of the blackest voids are his to command.” The professor bit his lip, rifling through documents strewn over an old table beside the maroon couch in the corner of the room. “You’ve heard the rumor of what he’s looking for, have you not?” At this inquiry Stryker seemed puzzled, cocking his head to the side as Dr. Kilborn lowered himself into a chair at the table. He rummaged through a folder hidden beneath a collection of paper-clipped notes, mumbling something inaudibly to himself. “Rumor? What’s he looking for?” Kilborn paused, almost reluctant in distributing the information. Marcus couldn’t tell if this was due to some oath of secrecy or the abhorrent nature of the data. He wagered it was the latter by the curious twinkle in his elder’s eyes and his wrinkled brow. “A formula of some sort. We don’t know exactly what it is or where to find it, and the only knowledge we have is that it involves a symbol and a chant. If he or anyone of like mind finds it, they hold a power with so much lethal potential it makes Osama Bin Laden look second-rate.” “What?!” Kilborn jammed a manilla folder at Stryker’s chest, moving his thumb over the tab on top. Scrawled in blue ink was the serial number of the folder: AZ-039. Stryker hurriedly flipped the file open, spying a bizarre diagram with flow-charts and a brief outline. The file was rather bare, surprising the Blackhelm operative considering the reputation of the Wilmarth Foundation for gathering intelligence. “It opens a gateway. The gate is only open briefly, but it unleashes a destructive power equivalent to a thermonuclear explosion.” Stryker’s eyes widened to the size of half-dollars at the revelation. Kilborn calmly removed a cigarette from a pack in his breast pocket, lighting it with a silver flip-open lighter on the table. “How……??” stammered Marcus. “We can only speculate,” mused the aged Kilborn, “but the theory is that the ritual opens a channel directly to the court of Azathoth itself. The center of the universe, and the nuclear chaos from which all creation was birthed. According to tomes and manuscripts we’ve collected eluding to the subject, the energy at that part of the universe is immeasurable, and opening any sort of gate between this world and there would surely result in widespread annihilation. The fallout would be disastrous and even then we can only hypothesize at the repercussions of such an event.” Stryker glanced over the diagrams and flow-charts, but was baffled by the terminology used throughout. He hadn’t studied his advanced physics for years, but it was obvious how dire the situation would be if such a thing were to occur. The word ‘catastrophe’ flashed in his mind. “Doctor Kilborn…..your only purpose here is to infiltrate the cult’s stronghold atop the mountain, correct?” Kilborn took a puff of his cigarette, nodding fervently. “Indeed it is, captain,” the professor quietly replied, drawing himself gingerly to his feet. “The Foundation needs intelligence on what resources he has at his disposal. Thus we can act accordingly, and assess the threat his activities pose. And hopefully if he has anything with the sort of power of what’s outlined in that file, we can destroy it. But as long as he’s in that mansion……” “He’s not at the mansion for more than a night or so these days,” Stryker said. “His wrestling career still dominates a large part of his mind and his time, and not only does that largely play to our advantage but I think that it’s a primary reason he hasn’t caused more damage than he has.” “But his followers, captain…..” “They’re of little consequence. They’re just kids, confused kids looking for identity. Very few harbor sentiments anywhere near Bruce’s. He’s brainwashed people. It’s like Scientology, but far more sinister.” Kilborn, glanced at the ceiling, aware of muttering between a female and a male, presumably Lieutenant Wilder and Sergeant Norton by the tone of the conversation. He grinned and looked back to Stryker. “What do you propose, captain?” Marcus turned to the stairwell, watching as three of his team member descended. Norton and Wilder, with Slade in tow, shambled into the den from their positions on the second floor. The young captain grinned. “I’m sure you’re familiar with the expression “Welcome to my parlor said the spider to the fly?” “Of course.” “Good. Then I’ll elaborate…….” * * * “Yes, m’lord. The heathens shall be disposed of. Everything is going according to plan.” Bruce ‘Violence Jack’ Shanahan knelt before the deformed black pulpit he enshrined within the attic of Wyatt Manor, encircled by a ring of flickering white ash. Atop the dais, with top flipped open, rested the artifact known only as the Shining Trapezohedron. The jewel encased within, braced by metallic bands, shimmered with a sickly glow. The forbidding device seemed to emit a gentle chattering from its core, permeating the room with an ominous hymn of the damned. As we speak, they plan your doom. Today you will be gone from the mansion, and only the faithful will remain. And even then, their presence is but a red herring…. “Shall I alert them, Lord Nyarlathotep?” No, it’s not necessary. Let this be a demonstration of the power they worship. That which they call Hell will be Eden for those who enter this manor unannounced, and the disciples will be witness to this intervention. “As you wish.” Now go forth. Spread the gospel, and destroy those who defy the power. “Your wish is my command....”
