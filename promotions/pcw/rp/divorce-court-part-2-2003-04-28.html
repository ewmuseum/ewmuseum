Divorce Court: Part 2

28 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 28, 2003 The scene continues on in the Rochester Municipal Courtroom where John Gargano had announced he would be representing Kalli in the divorce case after law training for one of his movies. John grins, thinking that this is all a game. Tyler slumps in his chair, nervous to watch or see anything go wrong. Meanwhile, the gruff Al Sutton stands to his feet and stabilizes himself with his bulky legs that are covered by black dress pants. He is outraged that he wasn’t notified earlier about this little deal. The judge’s eyes shoot back and forth trying to maintain order but cannot. “Wait a second,” bellows out Al Sutton from the other side of the room. He rises to his tiptoes and speaks in a gruff and intimidating tone of voice. “I was never informed about this Mr. Gargano. Isn’t it a little unfair to have a professional in her corner and not in mine?” He asks, not wanting to be taken for all the little money that he is worth. Kalli glances over in that direction, and shakes her head in disapproval. John shoots a glance in that direction and just smiles and readies a response. “Sir, I don’t think that it is any of your concern what your ex-wife does to manage her own business,” John says in a smug voice with an all lip smile that was shot over to Al’s way only to spite him. Al is anger over John’s arrogance, especially because his marital affairs are none of his business. Meanwhile, Tyler sits on the bench and throws his hand over his eyes. The last thing that he wanted was an ordeal, and now things have officially gained the status of an ordeal. “Gargano, you don’t belong here in the first place. Don’t act like I don’t know who you are. Your movies suck and you’re not a real lawyer so get lost,” Al threatens with all seriousness in his voice. He doesn’t barge in on John when he’s making movies then why should John show up in court to go against him? John just chuckles, thinking that Al really doesn’t think his movies suck. John is confident that everyone respects him both as a person and as an actor. “I am not going to get into a shouting match with you man. I’m here to help out a friend, and that is what I’m going to do. I can understand if you are a little intimidated because I know a little more about the law than you do, but those feelings are only natural,” says John in a childish way, done this way to belittle Al once again. Tyler lets out a huge sigh, hoping that it will reach John and pose as a signal for him to shut up. “John, just let him go, you’re never going to win an argument with him. He always gets the last word,” Kalli whispers over to John, but John’s ears are out of range, and Kalli’s whisper of warning falls on deaf ears. She sighs and sorts through the papers, wanting to be ready when the trial actually begins. Judge Hayes grows weary of the childish bickering and is about to put a stop to things until Al chimes in once again. “So Gargano, you think you are the law huh? It’s hard to take coming from a guy that dumped a fellow human being in to the Mississippi River. Don’t you remember that man?” Al says referring to the instant seen on Friday Fusion. He begins to walk in John’s direction but the bailiff gets in between them. Al is enraged that a man that hasn’t behaved in any more decent of a matter is verbally abusing him. “I guess I’m lucky that I’m not on trial here isn’t that right pal?” John says sarcastically, hoping that the matter of getting rid of the Vampire in the PCW wouldn’t play a factor in this case. Tyler shakes his head. He feared that with Gargano leading things, wrestling was bound to play a factor in the courtroom. Luckily Judge Hayes had his fill of useless complaining and puts a stop to it before it can develop any further. “Order in my court right now! You two hooligans aren’t going to step a foot in to my courtroom and carry on like a couple of idiots. Miss Stearns, my apologies for this unruly behavior,” the judge shouts out, while at the same time feeling a hint of sympathy for Kalli who has to sit between the cross fire of the two disgruntled men. “I am sorry your honor, I don’t know what got in to me,” John states in a somewhat apologetic voice. He opens his eyes wide and tries to put on a face of remorse, even though he doesn’t take back anything he said about his new found nemesis in this case. “I know what got in to you, you are a wrestling barbarian that doesn’t belong in a court of law. You’re just doing what comes naturally to you,” states Al who is now seated in his plush chair as he rolls his eyes. His voice trails off at the end of his sentence knowing that the judge won’t approve of yet another offensive outburst. “That is quite enough Mr. Sutton. If you have anything else to say in this court today, you say it to me and only me,” Judge Hayes says firmly through his gritted teeth. John chuckles and takes a seat. He whispers a few tips of strategy into Kalli’s right ear as Tyler watches behind him with a more calmed look now that things have turned from a verbal war back in to the case that he came here for. He adjusts the collar of his shirt and tugs on his tie to get comfortable again. “Yes your honor,” Al says in a defeated tone of voice. He wants to continue telling John what he thinks of him, but he decides that it will be better fit if it is done at a later time. He sits back and sets his chin on his palms. “Now that all that is behind us, we can finally get down to business. We don’t have to make this thing draw out any longer than necessary. There are no children, so custody is not an issue. Am I also correct that there was not a prenuptial agreement signed in this case?” The judges inquires, wanting to speed up this process more than ever after gaining the knowledge of the hostility that exists between the two opposing men. “You are correct your honor, there was no agreement signed at this point,” John agrees, wanting to be more than cooperative after almost ruining things with his argument earlier. Tyler nods in approval at the sound of John saying something that wasn’t said to offend anyone. “The situation that we are looking at is the classic half and half break. With no other factors taking play in these issues, the total financial revenue should be split down the center and the home and property should be awarded to the person or persons that have the title written in their name. Who would that be in this case?” the judge continues to ask, glad that they are finally getting a few things accomplished. “That would be me your honor. 2244 Lilac, it’s a small ranch house purchased me before even meeting Kalli. She then moved in with me without any rent fees attached,” Al says in a serious tone. Tyler finds that he is sounding less like a power-starved monster and more like a human being. Tyler looks him over and almost feels sorry for his situation. Kalli agrees with Al’s sentiments with a quiet nod of her head. Yet, John does not quite agree and comes to the conclusion that this is an unfair assumption. “Are we just going to take this man’s word for it? Are you really going to throw this young lady out on the streets because this man says he bought the house?” John says in a horrified and dramatic tone of voice as if a murder took place in this case. Kalli and Tyler roll their eyes simultaneously at this over dramatic statement. “What the hell are you talking about? Pardon my language your honor. There is proof upon proof that I own that house. Just take a look at this,” Al states in a disgruntled tone of voice after being challenged on the matter. He holds up a yellow piece of paper that signifies the purchasing of his home. The judge doesn’t even want to see it, and throws his hand in Al’s direction. “Mr. Gargano, I’m looking right at Miss Stearns and as you can see, she doesn’t have a problem with this statement. This indicates to me that Mr. Sutton is correct. Now things would go a lot smoother if you would stop trying to make everything so difficult,” the judge logically explains. Both Al and Kalli nod their heads in approval, which could be the first thing that they have agreed on in years. Tyler almost nods off in his bench seat, finding the events of the court a little less than intriguing, even if it does involve his friend John. He fights to stay awake because he knows this is important to Kalli, and to make sure John doesn’t mess anything up. “Very well your honor, I was just looking out for the interests of this poor, young lady John begins another rant. He wants to make Kalli sound worse off than she really is, but not even Kalli is having a part in this scheme. She cuts him off mid sentence to set him straight. “Listen John, I don’t mind that you are here and I’m glad that you are willing to try to help, but please, let’s cut out the bull shit,” she says right in to his ear as she tugs him down to her seated level by his tie. John looks surprised at the flowery language, and shakes his head. He wonders why he was doing some of the things he had been doing. He vows in his mind to start being more professional as Kalli releases his tie from her grasp leaving it a little wrinkled. “Never mind your honor, please proceed without any objections,” John says in a calmer voice. He decides to ditch the high-strung attitude and just do what he set out to do in the first place, help out Kalli. Tyler smiles his first grin of the day. He finds it humorous the way that Kalli bullied John around. He also thinks that he better toughen John up before the airing of his wrestling movie, or else John will never look the part of a professional wrestler. “So the house is awarded to Mr. Allen Sutton for his permanent residence. Miss Stearns, does this fit with well with you? Do you have any living arrangements lined up at this point or are you going to need the assistance of the court?” The judge asks to show his sensitive side. He doesn’t want people to think that he is a mean man that throws innocent women out on the street. John turns towards Kalli, giving the question for her answering only. Tyler’s cheeks get red, knowing that his name will come up. “Yes your honor, I will be living with my boyfriend who has a permanent residence in a New York City apartment complex. If the relationship doesn’t materialize then I’ll be left with more than enough time to find another place of residence,” says Kalli in a professional tone of voice. She had secretly been practicing all week to make sure she sounded like she belonged in the courtroom. Tyler slumps down in his bench seat thinking of the responsibilities that he is taking one now that Kalli is moving in with him. He also thinks thoroughly about Kalli’s statement about their relationship maybe not lasting. He wants to stay with her, could she be having doubts? He shrugs it off for the moment, but will come back to it. “Very well then, Mr. Sutton, you will be granted the secondary deed outside in the office on your way out. The next order of business is the total revenue split. I’m assuming that we’re cutting it fifty percent each?” The judge asks, assuming things will go off without a hitch. In the back of his mind, he can’t help to think that John is going to have an objection to this. His intuition is correct as John perks up at the sound of this so-called injustice. “Your honor, can you please state the total revenue amount for the court?” John asks, making the case sound more important that it is. Tyler is the only patron sitting in on the case, and John’s quirky antic gets a chuckle out of him. The judge sighs deeply and shuffles through the papers strewn on his podium. Kalli has about the same reaction, but John watches things intently. Al stirs in his chair a little, knowing that he could be exposed for an injustice on this matter. “The total revenue sum comes to a total of five thousand, nine hundred dollars. This revenue lies in a joint savings account signed to the couple in both of their names. The best thing to do in a situation like this would be to break it even. I know that you see where I’m coming from Mr. Gargano,” says the judge as he lowers his silver rimmed glasses and stares at John who shakes his head in disagreement. “Miss Stearns, will you please tell the court what you do for a living and the total revenue you bring in during a month period?” John asks Kalli. Kalli hops out of a trance where she was thinking of other matters. John thinks he could be on to something with this, yet Tyler thinks opposite and just finds this as prolonging the case. “If it matters any, I work at a brokerage company here in New York and bring in a revenue of ten thousand a month. A healthy sum I would say,” Kalli says, adding a small verbal pat on the back for her accomplishments in succeeding in business. Tyler even raises an eyebrow and thinks it will be good to have her staying with him so she can even help him out financially at times. “A healthy sum indeed. Now I won’t badger Mr. Sutton by asking, but I do know that he is currently unemployed. It seems a little unfair if you will, that he is bringing home half of what is not his, do you see my point your honor?” John asks, knowing that he made quite a strong argument. Kalli and Tyler think it over, and it does pose to be true to an extent. Whether or not the judge buys it is another thing. “I can see your point on this issue Mr. Gargano, but if we want to enter that realm of complication, then this case could draw out to be weeks. If you and your party are willing to go that far, then by all means, let’s get to business,” the judge invites, knowing that no one present in the court, with the exception of John, would want to draw this matter out any longer than it has to. “John, just drop it. I can make the money back in a split. It’s not worth wasting all our time over this,” Kalli whispers up to John. John tilts his head slightly but does not heed her words. He wants to take it upon his own hands to get Kalli what she rightfully deserves. Tyler also wants Kalli to get what she deserves, but he isn’t willing to sacrifice a week to a month for the matter. “Your honor, I’m sure that it doesn’t have to take to long. I mean, the signs are obvious. Can’t we just skew the fifty percent in to say seventy percent to thirty percent?” John asks in hopes. He is fighting for the cause, but knows that his time is to precious for the matter also. He has a lot more training to do for his movie. “Mr. Gargano, I am not allowed to make such decisions all in one day. It is your call, but regardless of what is to your choosing, it must coincide with the decision of the woman you are representing,” the judge warns, making sure that John isn’t taking over things against Kalli’s will. Kalli smiles at the judge, happy that he is truly looking out for her well being. “Your honor, I agree with the fifty percent split. There is no need to draw things out longer than they have to. I’m ready to move on with my life, as is Mr. Sutton,” Kalli stands and states to the judge. She straightens her scarlet red suit coat so there are no wrinkles present. “It is quite clear as to what both parties want. I hear by split the total revenue into fifty percent. If there are no other issues, your claims can be picked up in the office,” the judge instructs, hoping to make a quick get away from this case now that he is presented with one. Both Al and Kalli stand up, ready to depart from the room until John chimes in once more. “There are a few more things that I would like to go over your honor,” John says in still a professional tone. The judge has given up on John, and departs from his podium and disappears through the swinging wooden door. Kalli smiles and she charges through the gate to grab on to Tyler’s waiting arm. Al shakes his head as he follows the couple out. John sits in the room by himself with a slew of papers strewn around him. “Come on buddy, nice work today,” Tyler says at the doorway of the exit with a goofy grin on his face. He holds the door open for his friend, and silently admires John’s willingness to help. John smiles and grabs all the papers. He throws them carelessly in the garbage bin and jogs towards the exit. “I do what I can for you guys, but I still think I could have gotten more money for you,” John says to Kalli. Kalli just rolls her bright blue eyes, knowing that money is not an issue in this case. The only thing that matters is that friends who care surround her. The once duo, now a trio exits the courtroom through the thick iron door. The door slams behind them as faint jabbering is heard on the other side. They are jokes accompanied with laughter. Laughter is the remedy that got them through the entire thing.
