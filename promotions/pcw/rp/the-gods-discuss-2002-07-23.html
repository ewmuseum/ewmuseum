The Gods discuss

23 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 23rd of July, 2002 Time doesn’t exist in a place like this. It never has and one could only presume that it never will. A rocky cliff face against a deepening orange sunset in the backdrop is all that we can see. A red sun - of some sorts – lights the merging yellow and gingery-orange sky as we begin to focus in on our environment. A castle, no, no, A TEMPLE sits in front of us, unmoved for centuries but looking no more than a day older than the day it was first made –then again that statement can’t possibly be true in a place like this…time doesn’t exist here. A distant clank of wood upon stone enters the strange and twisted valley about us. Right on the edge of our mind we seem to remember this place, but how? We haven’t been here before, have we? The echo of the sound remains for some time, and then diminishes in the rocks and trees that surround the temple-like structure. We move onward. The outside of the sanctuary is lined with both trees and huge stone pillars. Both entwine in strange ways giving the impression that the foliage was originally grown through the actual rock. The leaves and the branches are neatly trimmed to almost perfection… Ideal… everything is ideal. All is in order on the outside of the colossal building; peace and tranquillity personified in leafs and stonework. The clank erupts into the airwaves again rendering birds to flutter from their homes in the trees – the seemingly normal-looking trees that aren’t entwined with the rock pillars. Onward we go again – this time into the temple… There’s the clank of wood against stone again, only this time we match it up to the sight of a cane, tall and dark, being smashed against the cobblestone floor. A pillar blocks our view of the man – or thing – that strikes the lean but strong staff on the ground, but his voice is clearly heard. “If we are to decide of his fate we must all work together!” Abrupt and stern his voice is. Only the tone of a man in high power could speak like this. But - as commanding as his voice is - it could easily be used to grease the clockwork of the most intricate watch. “HE HAS YET TO PROVE HIS TRUE WORTHINESS TO US!” Another voice, this time gruff and fierce, enters our ears. Skates upon ice would have found it difficult to defy friction against this voice. It sounded sharp and harsh, and as frictionless as sandpaper. “I have worked with the boy in the past – Indeed, I know him well - and he has proved his worthiness to our cause, Master Seth… If it wasn’t for him, then would we not be vanquished from our sanctuary, here?” A new voice… Fresh as spring water but as old and wise as the ground we walk upon. A silence falls, consuming the interior of the temple. The thoughts of great minds were being played upon here. Thoughts of worthiness and the future were been considered with great effort, but none of the individuals in the room dared to speak... Master Horus had made a good point in his last statement, after all. Once again we move forward, this time towards the voices. As we draw ourselves into a good point of view we see a circle of chairs – constructed from rich stone and vivid marble, and carved with different designs to resemble the immortal who sits upon it through their attitude and character – upon which each of the Egyptian Gods sit. Seth: “AS TRUE AS YOU ARE, HORUS, HE HAS YET TO PROVE HOW RELIABLE IN SPREADING OUR WORD HE IS!” Horus sighs, shaking his head. Horus: “Seth, in a world such as the one he lives in today, is it not hard to do such a thing? The people of earth are not like they were when we ruled over our section of them. They belong no longer to just the religion of us, but to many others… Taking this into account I would have thought that spreading our word would have proved difficult enough without your harsh words burdening him through the ages of his life!” Seth: “AND WHEN HE DIES, WHAT HAPPENS TO US, THEN? IF WE ARE TO LIVE ON THEN OUR WORD AND BELIEFS MUST BE SPREAD! WITHOUT THE EXISTANCE OF OUR BELIEVERS THEN WE SHALL DIE!” Horus, in his aged form, shakes his head in disappointment. Horus: “Do you not see how devoted he is to us? He believes that everything that happens in his life is through our influence… When he and Isiy, his wife - for those of you who don’t know him very well - have a child then he will surely pass on the beliefs of the Egyptian Gods on to him or her and the cycle shall continue!” Seth frowns, his face a portrait of anger – then again, it always had been. Seth was the Egyptian God of war, hate and basically everything bad. And as human’s have a tendency to do they had decided his form to be that of a gruff leather-clad warrior. An axe on his belt and the bloodied heads of his fallen enemies by his side at all times. Seth, over the years of being alive in his Godly form, had decided that the smell of the rotting skulls was beginning to wear thin and so he’d dropped them from his gimmick a long time ago… Seth: “MASTER AMUN-RA, TELL US YOUR WISE OPINION ON MATTERS AS DIRE AS THESE!” The man who had been hitting the ground with his cane earlier - a tall elegant looking man, or God as he truly was - looks up from his set of steepled fingers and sags with a deep, methodical sigh. Amun-Ra: “In this form I am suppose to be as wise as the wisest of the Gods in all this realm, but look at me! Am I not as confused on this situation as the rest of you? Even I, Amun-Ra, the most powerful of the Egyptian Gods, cannot add my counsel to this situation.” He rises from his seat and looks out over the circle of Gods. Amun-Ra: “I am afraid, my fellow Gods, that fate has taken a new turn of events. For you see, the work been done by Titu Xansiv on earth is indeed going well, as Master Horus has stated. But, as Master Seth has put forward, he isn’t doing enough to ensure that The Egyptian Gods stay a reality in the minds of the religious community…I, for one, cannot say whether he is doing a good job, or whether he is doing a bad job…at present he seems to be doing neither--…” Horus: “And what of his exploits in the professional wrestling circuit? Is it not true that his fan base grows? And if what I say is correct, and, my friends, it is, then doesn’t that mean that more of the people on earth will watch and learn from his wise ways?” One of the many Gods - this one wearing a bronze jackal-designed helm - stands, his face clearly visible under the helmet. His eyes black as night and his fingernails a tone of royal blue - but certainly not painted that way – a frown grows on his face before he speaks in protest. Anubis: “And what has he done since he joined with those ragtag fools? I stand here and watch over Xansiv and see his exploits, yes, but, Horus, O wise protector of the pharaohs, I have yet to witness anything of any relevance to us in this…P…C…W.” Anubis was an angry and tormented God these days. Horus and the others put it down to the fact that ruling the under world was getting too much for him - which was unusual since he’d ruled it for so long. But the fact of the matter was that with no real believers in The Egyptian Gods these days then one would presume that Anubis would have the easy job to do. Preparing dead that never really turn up in your realm was easy to deal with, right? Well The Gods agreed as well. But Anubis continued to be a cold and angry old God with a bad temper, even though he had no real reason why he acted like he did. Horus: “Anubis, O dark lord of the underworld, you misjudge him. For you see he has actually accomplished a lot in this wrestling career of his. Take for example his induction to the PCW roster – no easy task to accomplish I can tell you now! And his TV title victory over Akiyama, the Neo Void God a while back! And that’s not to mention the tag titles that he held for the second longest tag title reign in PCW history! He also seems to be in a feud with Xavier Kannon…” Seth: “XAVIER KANNON…HMM, I KNOW THAT NAME!” Seth looked fiercely into the eyes of Master Horus, as though expecting the old God to answer an unspoken question. Horus: “Yes…it is quite possible that you do. Xavier Kannon has been the thorn in Xansiv’s side for a while now. This coming Sunday, in fact, Titu gets a chance to finally end his squabbles with Kannon.” A God, whose head had been shaped to look like that of an ibis, gazes harshly in Horus’ direction. Thoth: “AND THE PROBABILITY OF OUR SUBJECT COMING OUT WITH THE WIN?” Horus winces. Horus: “Kannon is a very determined if not enthusiastic character, he’ll take a lot to beat…” Anubis smiles. Anubis: “So you don’t believe he can win?” Amun-Ra, beginning to sense the tension in the room, stands from his seat and cracks the cane in his hand down upon the cobblestones of the temple’s floor. Amun-Ra: “Horus, why not tell us of his other ventures.” Amun-Ra, the single most powerful God in the council of The Egyptian Gods had patience, which was a thankful thing, for not many of the other Gods did. He was well respected and thought of in high regards by the others, too. He and Horus were classed as the wisest of The Egyptian Gods, since they both knew a great deal of the humans who followed them. But as of late Amun-Ra had begun to feel that his powers were beginning to lessen. The other Gods were beginning to say what they wished, especially Seth and Anubis, who, up until this seminar had usually kept their mouths closed through meetings. Horus was the one who kept a lot of peace and order with the other Gods, these days…Amun-Ra remembered when he used to do that… Horus: “I believe he has to fight a man named Big Alan Steele, and another named Frisco… They’re wrestlers, undeniably. Big Alan Steele is the World Heavyweight Champion of PCW, the federation that Titu resides in, and Frisco is one of his major contenders. There is one thing of relevance that I fear I must point out in regards to this ‘Big Alan Steele’ character, however, Amun-Ra.” Thoth: “AND THAT IS?” Amun-Ra sighs. Thoth was the God of wisdom, but look at him these days. He couldn’t even listen to a conversation of great importance, such as this one without feeling the need to interrupt. He was another one of the Gods who had begun to establish themselves as significant Immortals in the Egyptian God council. Amun-Ra looks at him, it was clear he was beginning to take on a certain amount of arrogance, noticeable only from his smile…well saying that was like saying he actually had a choice to smile. He had the head of an Ibis! He always looked as though he was smiling…but this, Amun-Ra was sure, was an arrogant smirk…one that a God shouldn’t really bear. Horus looks at Amun-Ra, as though asking the inevitable question: “Should I continue?” The most important of The Egyptian Gods nods approvingly. Horus: “Big Alan Steele, AMUN-RA, worships Zeus, of the Greek Gods.” Mummers and silent questions fill the air like a plague of locust. Amun-Ra stands valiantly from his seat and waves his hands in the air in an attempt to hush the circle of Gods. Moments pass before he ACTUALLY gets any kind of response…it didn’t used to be like that. Amun-Ra: “If what Horus says is correct then we do indeed have a problem on our hands.” Amun-Ra waves a hand in the air, a sweeping noise following the brush of his hand against the realms atmosphere. Mysteriously the God renders a stone table in front of him. On top of it sits a chessboard carved from marble. Hieroglyphics surround the table and the tabletop game and four small pieces of stone appear to stand upon it. One is carved to look like The Pharaoh, another carved in the appearance of Xavier Kannon, the third to look like Frisco and the forth, of course, to resemble Big Alan Steele. Amun-Ra: “It is unclear of what will happen as of yet, but whatever is to take place within the battle of these four I can almost guarantee that the Greek Warrior has a good chance of winning it. The Greek Gods, led by Zeus, are of a great calibre when it comes to power, wisdom and overall belief, rendering us to only hope that our keen young Warrior can stand his ground and not be pushed around by the bigger players in this game…” Horus steeples his long fingers and nods thoughtfully. Horus: “Deeper into the water our standard bearer adventures, but whether it is a wise choice is yet to be decided. With time we will surely find out where the road heads for Titu Xansiv.” Seth: “INDEED YOU SPEAK WISELY, MASTER HORUS. BUT THE QUESTION REMAINS ‘CAN HE DEFEAT THESE ‘OPPONENTS’’?” Horus: “As I have said, Seth, only time will tell. It is true that Xansiv has the skills to succeed in this game, but as Amun-Ra says his opposition is much tougher than it has been in the past.” Anubis frowns. Anubis: “So what you’re saying is that we must wait for the results rather than guide things the way we wish them to go?” Thoth: “IF WE WISHED TO REARANGE THE FATES OF MORTALS THEN WE COULD, BUT DOING SO IN SUCH AN INTERESTING SITUATION AS THIS WOULD BE FOOLISH. THE KNOWLEDGE WE COULD GAIN FROM THE BATTLES OF THESE FOUR IS SOMETHING THAT I, FOR ONE, WOULD RATHER WATCH FROM A GOOD VANTAGE POINT WHILST FATE RAN NORMALLY FOR A CHANGE.” The Gods all seem to concur with Thoth’s words, even Amun-Ra and Horus nod. Amun-Ra: “Then let fate flow as it should…the battle of these four men should determine whether Xansiv is worthy of spreading our beliefs.” Once again the Gods nod in agreement. Anubis: “And if he isn’t?” Horus looks over at the stern face of the jackal-helmed God. Horus: “We will discuss that when the time is right.” Anubis smiles grimly. Anubis: “Very well…” Amun-Ra: “For now, however, this senate has finished discussing its matters. Until a later date, my fellow Gods…” And with those words it was over…
