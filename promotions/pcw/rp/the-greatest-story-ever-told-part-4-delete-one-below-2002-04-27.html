The Greatest Story Ever Told Part 4*Delete One Below

27 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 27th, 2002 *Monologue* The day draws nearer. The day when I make my first true step up the ladder in an over crowded industry. In just over 3 days I will have my chance to make my mark. Not only will I be making a mark in this company for ages to come, but I will also be given the chance to take all of my frustrations out on the one man who has been screwing me over for the past few weeks. Davey can you hear it? Tick Tock. Tick Tock. Tick Tock. Time is no longer on your side. The hourglass has almost emptied. Our inevitable meeting is coming. Tick Tock. Tick Tock. Tick Tock. Is your blood curdling? Can you feel it boiling? The day is drawing near, the day of your judgement, the day of defeat! *End Of Monologue* Playing my guitar in the neighborhood club had become my life. By now I was standing at a tall six feet four inches. Now I look back and realize my father was a tall man. He was probably around six four and he beat a defenseless child standing at two feet four. This brings on a cold shudder. But now at the age of 19 I was a freebird rocking out to now classic tunes. In the year 1996 I felt I was on the path to a great music career. My hopes high and my brain too, Hell's Den and I honestly believed that we would finally earn our deal. Time passed. We soon realized our deal would never come. We still played at clubs for a measly 50 bucks a night. Then the greatest thing to ever happen to me took place. It was late 1998. The month currently escapes me. But the night is forever burned in my brain. We were on stage just finishing up our closing number, Iron Man when I was approached by a man who would start me off on a career that I would love. The man introduced himself. He was a regional wrestling promoter. His name Sully. He was not only a promoter but a trainer. Sully made first contact. Sully was a gifted man. He not only trained myself but also trained other current ring legends. Sully took a kid off the street who had a natural fighting ability and turned him into a man who could wrestle with some of the best technical wrestlers around. I was on my way. I soon began my career in several small promotions. I wish not to speak much of this as nothing ever came from my career in the smaller promotions. Except for one. I was their golden boy. The main reason behind it was I was the owner. You see, every week a man, a man who I never met, would send me three hundred thousand dollars. Along with the money a note was written telling me what to do. I was told to purchase a defunct wrestling business and all the contracts available. I soon was a wrestling promoter. I noticed that there was a tremendous lack of talent so I chose to put myself in as the golden boy. I was the world champion and this only changed when I chose to make a change in my life. I was soon discovered! The man who discovered me would soon be my inspiration for the future, the man known as Painkiller would be behind my success. I would soon be known as the greatest FSW wrestler of all time!
