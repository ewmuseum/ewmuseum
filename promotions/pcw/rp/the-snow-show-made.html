Date: Season I, Episode 16 ﻿************************************The Snow ShowSeason I, Episode 16“Made”************************************ Location: Snow Residence, New York CityDate: January 22, 2003Time: 7:20 am The bathroom mirror was particularly kind to Jason Snow this morning. He straightened his tiefor the millionth time and then reached for his hair, but didn’t dare touch it - it was too perfect,even if it was still a little damp. He took a deep breath and left the bathroom. Today was going to be a big day for the young man. And he was told to look sharp. Of course,that was a common command in the Hattoni family, he was finding. As he walked down the hallway, and then down the stairs, the phone rang from the living room. He hurried to it and answered. “You ready, kid?” Johnny Silver’s voice was on the other end of the line. *** Location: Hattoni Mansion (Great Room), New York CityDate: January 22, 2003Time: 11:00 am Surrounded by the mafia’s upper class, Jason Snow stood alone in the center of the human circleof suits. He wondered what the collective death toll of the men in this room was - probably wellinto the hundreds. In just a few months, Snow already had to think hard to remember his owndeath toll, and when he included his time in the Tank, the number he came up with almostalways included decimal places. Justin Hattoni stood opposite him, wearing a sold black suit and tie. To his left stood hisyounger brother, Max Hattoni. Rumour had it that Max was slowly being left out of the familybusiness more and more lately, especially since it became apparent that Justin’s right hand manwas now Petey the Vice. Petey stood at Hattoni’s right. “We all know why we’re here,” Justin Hattoni finally said, slapping the rest of the room intosilence. For the first time ever, Snow saw warmth in the Don’s face - something that he rarelysaw in his father, either. The young Don and the old Don were so similar in so many ways,Snow thought. Petey nodded to Snow. Max nodded to Snow. “At this time, for reasons that some of you know, and some of you may not,” Justin began, “Ihave decided to make this young man, Jason Snow, a card carrying member of this family. Andby doing so, I take him fully and completely to this family, as if he were my own blood.” Hiswords rang through the air, for everyone in the room knew that Snow’s blood was more differentthan anyone else’s. Jason Snow was Irish by descent, not Italian. No one made a sound. “And if anyone has a problem with my choice, he’ll now swallow it, for I have decided as theleader of this family that this man is deserving. And now, your card...” Justin Hattoni pulled along decorated knife from the inside of his jacket, unsheathed it, and held his other hand palmup. “Roll up your sleeve, Jason - the right one.” Snow followed the command without hesitation. He pulled his right sleeve up to the elbow andplaced his hand in Hattoni’s palm. Justin turned Snow’s arm over and then held the knife overthe inside of Snow’s forearm. Slowly, and with considerable force, he carved an “X” into theinside of Snow’s right forearm, so he would forever bare the mark of the family, even if itswitched names through the years as new leaders might come and go. “It’s done,” Justin said, patting Snow on the shoulder. Snow looked down at the bloody mark on his arm. Suddenly, everything seemed much morereal than it did before. *** Location: Hattoni Mansion (Office), New York CityDate: January 22, 2003Time: 1:50 pm Snow shut the door on the party in the large party room, where strippers and loud musicoverwhelmed the senses of the other mobsters. He took a deep breath and looked at the manwho called him away from the party - Justin Hattoni. The young Don smiled. “Things have been going well in the short time since John Mossilano’s accident,” he nodded toSnow. The ‘accident’ was actually Jason Snow putting a pair of bullets into the old man’scranium. “While the Don of the Mossilano family has been ‘missing’, his successors have beenvery, very, friendly to us. Almost thankful.” His eye twinkled as he picked his cigaretteup from the ash trey that was sitting on his desk. “Glad I could be of service,” Snow said with a smirk. Justin chuckled as he leaned back in his seat. “You know, I gave The Dragon a cash reward forthis job. A cash reward, and the option of an early retirement. He can walk away from thisbusiness, right now, with enough money to last him the rest of his life. No worries about futurejobs. Completely stressless.” “Is he going to take the offer?” Snow asked. He was genuinely curious. Although The Dragonwas well into his middle age, he seemed to have plenty of life left in his bones. “I don’t know,” answered the young Don. “This business is in his blood. It was in his father’sblood and it’s in his son’s blood.” Snow cringed. “He’s been working for this family for years,” Justin sighed. “I don’t know if he has anythingelse he’d rather be doing. It’s kind of ironic though, isn’t it? He has the opportunity to get out,right while you have the opportunity to get in? And for working together on the same job?” Snow stayed silent. “But being made was not the reward that I had in mind for you, Jason,” Justin said. “When youkilled John Mossilano, you proved yourself so loyal and useful that you deserved to bemade. But your reward is a separate matter.” Justin Hattoni reached into a drawer of his desk and produced three envelopes. “What are those?” Snow asked. “Tickets,” Justin answered. “Airline tickets. One to Chicago, one to Ontario, and one to NewBrunswick.” Snow was confused. He picked the envelopes up and slipped them into the inside of his jacketwhile waiting for an explanation from the Don. “I understand that you’re in a bit of financial difficulty,” Justin said. “Three separatelawsuits. These three tickets will allow you to... settle... those lawsuits.” Snow finally understood. “You see, Jason,” Justin said as he stood from his desk, “being ‘made’ isn’t only about gettingdrunk with the boys and getting blow jobs from the stripper of your choice. It’s a license to dowhatever you want. You want to cheat on your wife? We cover for you. You want to cut a fewcorners to a couple of bucks? We cover for you. You want to kill a few of your enemies... youget the picture.” Justin stepped over to the bar and poured himself a drink. Knowing about Snow’s previousproblem with alcohol, he didn’t bother offering him anything. “I’ll get caught,” Snow said, prompting the Don to chuckle. “No,” Justin said, still chuckling. “You won’t be caught because you’ll be at the ball roomtomorrow night watching David Chassily’s cirtically acclaimed play, ‘Starlit Morning’.” Justinwent back to his desk and pulled out a ticket to the play that was registered under Jason Snow’sname. “Fifteen people, no less, will see you at this play, and swear in a court of law, if need be,to conversations that they had with you. In fact, your seat will even by occupied during theentire show. After the play, you’ll go to a party at Petey’s house.” Justin smiled as he handedSnow the ticket to ‘Starlit Moon’. Snow looked down at it, realizing that the only reason why hemight need it would be to help prove himself innocent in a court of law. “If the three guys who are suing me, for millions of dollars each, die within hours of each other,people will know that a I did it,” Snow told Justin. “It won’t be a question of where Iwas or with who. It’s too obvious.” “You’re wrong,” Justin said. “Always remember, Jason, it’s not what one knows, it’s what onecan prove. And no one will be able to prove anything. Will they ‘know’ something? Ofcourse. Everyone will ‘know’. And then, they’ll fear you. Because they’ll also know that youwalk the streets abiding by your own rules. You’re a made guy now, Jason.” Snow slipped the play ticket into the inside of his jacket with the plane tickets.
