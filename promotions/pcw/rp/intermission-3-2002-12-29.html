Date: 29.12.02 It's around 7 in the morning in sunny California and there are signs of life on planet Aftermath. While the five building-sized tour buses that make up the cord label's moving HQ were speeding through the night, every bed, bunk, and couch held a sleeping member of Def Jam's supreme entourage. But as the caravan approaches its next stop, Soul Train's Hollywood sound stage, the crew kicks into high gear. But first, Dante Jackson needs his bottle. Jack, the Lyrical Messiah's man of all trades, puts out three cups: one filled with ice, one with carnberry juice and one with Absolut. By the time, Aftermath's hotly contested rookie other than 50 Cent emerges from his master bedroom at the rear of the bus, his rise and shine liquid breakfast of cranberry vodka has been chilled, strained, and served. For all of Dante's 'lifestyle of the rich and famous' habits and indulgences - we've all heard about his lavish house back in Houston, the six million dollar figures he's pulling in for every single matches and playbook rhyme he dishes in - you'd think he'd wake up to a breakfast of Cristal mimosa and caviar blintzes. Nope. Everywhere he goes, Jack mixes the same Absolut and Ocean Spray for Dante. This morning there are some Mickey D's pancakes, too. And there ain't a Fonsworth in sight. In fact, it's tough to tell who does what; everyone just seems like family. And now that the Messiah's up, he wants attention. 'Jaaaaaack, we ready?' he says with a drawl. 'Traaaaaaaaacaaay, you slept OK? Cookaaay, I gotta get fresh for Soul Train! Bluuuuuuuuuue, you seen our schedule? Bluuuuuuuuuuuuue you mad 'cause I'ma be on the cover of The Source again? Bluuuuuuuuue, this time we goin' diamond, ya heard?' While everyone humors Dante and gets dressed for their Soul Train taping, he gets his daily facial and shape up from Tracy, his wife and groomer, before trying on a red tracksuit with red Gucci-logo trim and a blue tracksuit embossed with the Prada signature. 'You like my Gucci suit?' Messiah asks, adding on a couple of hundred dollars worth of platinum and diamond jewelry, a Cartier cage-watch and some Gucci-trimmed Timberlands. 'You know, I got to have the finest clothes on my back, they're all custom made.' But this isn't the just a case of rapper vanity. As he walks toward the stage to perform 'Dissin Tha King', before co-performing with 50 Cent and Obie Trice, Dante's got good reason to stunt. In fact, its his new job. The stout New Yorker once was the slick-talking, show-boating, wrestler of the Primetime Wrestling Academy. But in nine short months, Dante has become a story unlike any other, growing from the small leagues to the big leagues. And maybe the big sell in all of hip hop period. During the inevitably long wait of a television-show taping, Messiah works the room like a politician, kissing babies, hugging ladies, handing out daps and pounds. Always looking for a pretty victim to practice his pimp (when his wife isn't around that is) Dante spots R&B ingenue Amerie. 'Ay Mamaaay, holla at ya boy!' he yells out. Without skipping a beat, she turns around in full supermodel-mode, big hair, leather getup and all throws up a nice wave and a cute smile. Dante in his mind knows that he still has it. Its a damp, chilly Saturday night at the Los Angeles Ampitheather. A multicultural crowd of all afges scream in anticipation inside the packed arena. A tennage, suburbaned-out white dude, wearing a wife beater, bandana, and black saggy jeans, stands in a well-practiced gangster pose. Jackson DJ, TracBurna is on stage amping the crowd by asking them who they came to see. The next thing you know, a huge pyrotechnic hits the stage and out pops the man of the hour, clad as expected in the Messiah uniform. Tilted Lakers cap, with the white version of the Lakers' Kobe Bryant home jersey almost swallowing the rapper whole along with oversized black jeans. Twelve year old girls dressed like rap video accessories gyrate and sing along with the New York party and rhyme slayer, 'Twurk.' 'Shake it all out from the left to the right, then drop ya ass down like ya gettin' dropped from outta sight,' the mini-seductresses chant along with Dante, who works the left, right and middle of the stage. The obscene reality suddenly sets in that being a pop star actually has its privileges. After the show, DJack retires to the comforts of his tour bus. Once again, he has proven the he's undoubtedly pop-ular. And though he scoffs at the stigma that goes along with the three letter word, he gladly accepts the glory that comes with it. 'If you define what pop is, no matter if you call it fake, or not even hip hop, but I'm street,' he explains. 'Street and pop to me are the same thing, except if you lookin' in Benzino and Nelly's case. One is a fake dude thinking he's street where there isn't even a STREET of black people in Boston, and the other is a singer, not a hip hop artist, but a SINGER.' The explanation may sound like an overused killing of two MC's that's been heard by Hip Hop smarks over the past year or so, but that's what you're getting when you hear a bar from Jackson, the truth. His attitude might come off as abrasive or even cocky to those who know him, but being a fake isn't one of his morals. People like Chet King, The End, Totally Tubular Tommy, they're on his shit list for a reason, either they've done something to him or he's doing something to them, plain and simple. Whether it be fact or fiction, just know for certain that there is one thing that goes on in the life of Dante Jackson - drama. PCW is straight drama, understand?
