Magna Stella - Part 4: At The Altar

4 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3 - 4 - 03 Kneeling before the monolith of dark, brooding marble, the disciple sits like a perched gargoyle in a state of total, uninterrupted prayer. With one knee firmly planted upon the cold stone floor and his dark hands clasped together in front of him, the figure hums a theological hymn of mysterious nature, almost as if his mind is wandering and the tune is matching its journey. The only light in the cramped area of worship is a pair of Gothic style lamps on either side of the large stone altar, emitting a soft orange glow that casts a long shadow of the disciple behind him. The shadow dances in the light but never leaves its spot, acting as the disciple’s caretaker almost. Sitting atop the shrine is a statue of a dark dragon in mid-flight, with a soft golden aura emitting from its being. The eyes are the true enigma of the statue, however, glowing brighter than anything else. As the waves of mystic sound whistle forth from the disciple’s lips, the eyes of the dragon statue seem to slowly flash in response. The disciple’s humming grows louder and louder, and the resonance of the statue’s eyes begins to pick up the pace. Soon, a ravaging fire bursts forth from deep within the statue, shattering the stone and causing the disciple to stumble back onto his posterior. The flames of the two lamps reach in like monstrous hands and join with the inferno born from the statue, becoming one large dancing pillar of fire. The disciple scrambles to his knees and bows down before the gyrating inferno, never letting his eyes sink into its beautiful, yet deadly depths. The column of flame dances for a moment more before bending like a great worm and crashing to the concrete before the disciple, completely coming off the altar and leaving a smoldering pile of stone. The fire rises forth from the stone and seems to implode on itself, leaving the golden visage of the Goddess En’Flarim in its place. Adorned in her golden dress and tiara, the Dragon Goddess reaches her hand out slowly to the disciple. “Arise…” Her voice is high and echoes off the cold stonewalls, yet strangely deep. “You’re troubled, my warrior. I can tell.” The disciple slowly raises his head, and in the bright glow of the Dragon Goddess, is revealed to be Dorn Tracer. He kisses the Goddess’ hand gingerly and nods, the hood of his cloak wafting with the motion. “Yes, my Goddess. Something troubles me,” The Astronomer relays, his voice quiet and dark. “A friend of mine by the name of Jessica has been making claims that I am in need of help. Do you agree?” The Dragon Goddess brushes past Dorn slowly without answering, sending a wave of unforgiving heat through his body. His lowers his head and looks back at En’Flarim as the Creator of All paces away, taking long, slow strides. When at least a good 10 feet away from the Shooting Star Champion, she turns her head to him and gives him a slight grin. “Of course not, Lunis Superia. The foolish woman knows not of your problems, so take no heed of her advice. Have I not briefed you during your journey?” “Yes, my Goddess…” Dorn replies, lowering his gaze to the cracked stone floor. I want to believe her, as she’s done so much for me. The internal battle he’s been facing as of late as been mentally draining, and he has now looked to his Goddess for advice. As always, she’s optimistic about Dorn and shuns any ill news. But is she being truth? That’s the only thing that bothers him. “Then I shall hear no more of this. I do not with to hear that you’re being trouble by some mere mortal woman who thinks she knows what’s good for you!” The Goddess’ tone begins to rise, as anger seeps through her teeth. Dorn nods meekly, but yet, something still bothers him about it. And obviously, it bothers him enough to actually argue with the Destroyer. Lifting his gaze from the floor, Dorn pulls his cold, emotionless eyes to meet En’Flarim’s pupil-less eyes. “But she is my friend, Goddess. Why would she wish to trick me?” Bewildered at her disciple’s attempt at argument, the Goddess whirls around and storms over to Dorn, leaving a trail of fire in her wake. The expression on her face is enough warning for Dorn as he braces for the incoming palm of her fiery hand as it smashes him across the face. The force of the blow sends him crashing to the floor, his face burning like a fire has been lit that will never die. The Astronomer raises his hand and gingerly holds the burning wound, trying not to cry out in agony. En’Flarim stares down at him with a look that could bring the entire place up in flames if he even speaks. “How dare you undermine me, creature! If you wish to REMAIN a bearer of gold and if you wish to remain walking upon the mortal coil, you will NEVER challenge my words again! Do you understand?” Her tone slams Dorn like an abusive mother’s would a small child. The Astronomer doesn’t reply, but En’Flarim can tell by the look in his eyes that he understands. She whirls around and begins to speak again like the entire situation really didn’t just happen, “The stars have brought word of your contest coming in a tri-day. It seems you have a mortal enemy in the form of one of the competitors. Tell me, do you fear him?” Dorn turns the corner of his eye to En’Flarim and shakes his head, “No, Goddess. He’s nothing but an insect beneath my skin. Nothing more. He does represent a credible challenge to my title, however.” Dorn climbs to his feet and brushes his brown cloak off, trying to ignore the burning sensation in his cheek. The Dragon Goddess smirks, not really believing it. “A credible contender, you say? Blasphemy, my warrior. How does he compare with you? You said yourself, nothing but a mere insect. Insects present no problem, unless in great numbers.” Her confidence boosts the spirit of Dorn, as always, but yet, her last comment seems to stir some uncertainty within him. “Yes, my Goddess, only in numbers. But the insects will indeed be in numbers come this Friday. I have to face not one, but three of these said insects.” Dorn’s concerned tone brings about an irritated look on En’Flarim’s face, so the Astronomer quickly covers. “But no matter, with the stars watching me from above and with the strength of the Tiamats running through my veins, it shall prove as no challenge. Yet, something else bothers me, my Goddess…” He makes sure to pick his tone carefully, making sure not to show that he’s concerned, just that he’s merely curious. It seems to work. “Yes, Lunis Superia?” En’Flarim’s tone comes out peaceful, so Dorn’s act has actually pierced En’Flarim’s angered state and found its way to the tranquility within the fire. “A contest of great proportions looms over the horizon in the form of the March Madness pay-per-view. Contenders of all kinds will meet in a single battlefield and compete for the ultimate trophy…” He suddenly trails off and looks to see what En’Flarim is thinking. Her wondering expression hasn’t changed, as a mass battle isn’t really something unfamiliar to her. Seeing as how she’s unresponsive, Dorn continues, “I am a participant in this great battle of wits and strength. I have to outlast many others, and I desire your blessing to guide me through the tough times ahead.” En’Flarim’s eyelids slowly begin to come down in an angry squint, as the Goddess just stares at Dorn Tracer, reading him like a book. Her lips curl into a sly smirk and she turns her head the other away, as if dismissing him, “I refuse your request for my blessing, Lunis Superia. You’ve done quite well by yourself, and this woman you’ve been traveling with seems to believe she can aid you better than I.” Her snide, irritated tone sends a wave of subtle panic through the marrow of Dorn’s bones, unnerving him greatly. The Dragon Goddess turns away from him and strides off into the darkness, leaving Dorn alone to his dark thoughts. Has she forsaken me?? Have I lost the favor of my Astrological deity? Jessica be damned… I think I’m screwed… [End.]
