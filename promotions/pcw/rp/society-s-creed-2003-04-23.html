SOCIETY's Creed

23 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 23, 2003 Begin Transmission: “When we establish our order of perfection, bellhops will be the FIRST THING to expire!” (An angry Michael L. Chang is speaking of a bellhop at a small motel in Lexington, Kentucky. Actually, the whole day really hasn’t been going too well for the President of the Order of Society and Logic.) “Why are you so angered,” SOCIETY asked, standing at the President’s side. “This is all insignificant!” (Chang could probably respond to that with a short, obscene comment. He decides against it however, using this example to continue SOCIETY’s education.) “I am angered because our Order has not given us the funds we need to stay in decent lodging! I am the President of this Order! I should travel in style!” “It matters not,” SOCIETY said, matter-of-factly. “We shall only be here one evening, before pressing onwards to the site of Fusion, where I shall annihilate my opposition.” “But it is the PRINCIPLE of the thing, SOCIETY! We are superior to the average man, and thus, our accommodations should be better. Those who are superior must act as such.” “But that would seem to go against our current goal of equality. If we seek equality, even if that equality can only exist when all are as perfect as we, eventually, we will be right BACK in the same accommodations with everyone else.” (Chang pauses to ponder that point. Despite being President of the Order, SOCIETY presents an interesting argument. Even though he had been subjected to thousands of hours of indoctrination, they did not remove his ability to think for himself. Weakened it, perhaps, but did not remove it.) “But then, if we were all perfect, we would be able to build better facilities than this, and the employees would be perfect enough to NOT DROP OUR LUGGAGE!” (Chang looks contemptuously at the college student bellhop, which has managed to drop the suitcase containing his expensive suits off the cart and on to the floor. SOCIETY responds promptly.) “Then, why not just do it ourselves. We’re perfect, why do we have to rely on the idiots of the world to do our jobs for us?” “Think about it, SOCIETY,” Chang responded. “If we had to do all of our own menial tasks, we would have time for nothing else. That is why these menial servants exist, to serve the rich and powerful. Men of our scope. Now, I know you probably have more arguments, but let us table this discussion for the time being. We will check into this hotel, and then we will head over to Lexington’s arena, or stadium, or whatever, and demonstrate unto a world of non-conformists the true power of SOCIETY! (The ultimate conformist cracks his knuckles, and looks over at the bellhop, struggling with the cart.) “It is possible, Mr. Chang, that all the boy needs is some…persuasion.” (SOCIETY walks over to the bellhop, trying not to look contemptuous.) “Bellhop, what seems to be the problem here?” “Oh, nothing sir..the cart is just a little bit heavy.” (SOCIETY looks at the man quizzically, stepping over to the cart, and kneeling down.) “Heavy?” (With a massive heave, SOCIETY lifts the cart up from beneath, raising it to chest level, to a stunned bellhop’s shock.) “T-t-that’s impossible!” “For a non-conformist such as yourself, perhaps. Now, I’m going to put this cart down, and you are going to get it up to our room. Woe be unto you if you drop a single item. Now, go. (SOCIETY lowers the cart, as the terrified bellhop carefully begins wheeling it down the hallway to the elevator. Mr. Chang is suitably happy.) “Where did you learn to deal with people like that?” “A little of the philosophy of Niccolo Machiavelli, little bit of Thomas Hobbes, and a little bit of the fact that my body mass composes two bellhops.” “I cannot argue with that.” “No, you cannot. Now, what room number are we?” “129,” said Chang. “You go up there and get us settled. I’m going to go to the gym, and improve my strength. I must be in peak form for Friday’s bout with Ahutmoth and the Ukranian whose name I would rather not have to pronounce.” (Chang shrugs.) “Suit yourself. Shall I order you dinner from room service? The moronic treasurer didn’t give us enough money to go anywhere suiting our abilities, so we’ll have to settle for here.” “Hmm…get me some fried chicken and mashed potatoes.” (Chang was somewhat surprised.) “That doesn’t seem like something that would be on your diet, you know.” “It’s not. But, after what I have planned for today’s training, I will have earned it.” (The President of the Order of Society and Logic nodded to his colleague, and headed for the elevator, as SOCIETY turns, to head towards the gym.) “I must not dissapoint Mr. Chang. I must not dissapoint the Order. For I am the soldier of the divine qualities of perfection, tranquility, order, strength, logic, and most importantly, conformity! No god, not even the Ahtumoth’s Amon-Ra can stand before my inquizition. For, ultimately, they are all flawed creatures, flawed in and of themselves, or flawed in that their creations are flawed. The Order of Society and Logic is alone perfect! And on the gods of the Ahtumoth, I will execute judgment! I, SOCIETY, and none other! The Ukranian’s gun will be slapped aside, broken in two, and after I am finished with both men, they will have but one choice remaining.” (SOCIETY’s purposeful strides cease, as he forcefully opens the door to the gym.) “CONFORM…TO SOCIETY!” End Transmission:
