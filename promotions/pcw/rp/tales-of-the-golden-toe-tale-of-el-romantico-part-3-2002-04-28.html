Tales of the Golden Toe: Tale of El Romantico (Part 3)

28 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/28/02 Tales of the Golden Toe: The Golden Heart as passed and the Golden Toe has come back again. The Golden Toe is the old Juan Borro, the fun loving foreign soccer player who did his all to help others. Through the tales of the Golden Toe Juan gives us insight into present experiences with his life and duties and encounters with people. The Golden Toe has seen his calling, and that calling is to do good, be good, and be a power for good. No force will stop Juan’s GOAL~! Time: 11: 34 A.M Location: Barcelona, SpainScene: Market “In our world everything that’s never been seen or done before is said to be impossible. If you think about it nothing is impossible, there is always probably one person that has done that certain thing sometime in their life.” Part 3 The scene opens with Juan Borro buying some food in a local food market in Barcelona. Juan got up early this morning and has been out and about the whole time. He still has El Romantico on his mind. It’s usually odd for Juan to think about a guy, but never before as he seen such a mysterious force. He always thought his wrestling equal Rent-A-Hero is something like El Romantico, but the more Juan thinks about it El Romantico is even more mysterious. Juan thinks about a lot of things, like how strong El Romantico really is? If he is as great as everyone says? Or even if the past of this man is true? Juan then thinks about some of the similarities between himself and El Romantico. They both seem to be well loved by their respected country. Both are slick with the ladies, and well loved. Both even seem to be smart and quick. The more Juan thinks about him the more he is reminded of himself. The biggest headache Juan just can’t seem to get rid of, is what El Romantico meant when he said “I’m always there” or something near that line. Juan forgot what it exactly was which makes it even harder for him to understand. He just wishes all the pieces could come together to make sense. Also, if this guy was so good at wrestling then why doesn’t he go to make big money in a real federation? Instead, he stays in so low paying underground piece of you know what. It’s probably because he isn’t as good as everyone thinks. Sure he’s incredible for low standards, but how would we be when he has to fight real competition? Juan isn’t sure, but he decides to try and get it off of his head and go on with his shopping peacefully. As Juan makes his way down the street he sees a tent with a fortuneteller inside. Juan has seen many of these before around Portugal with gypsies usually inside trying to make money. At first, Juan walks right by it not giving it any thought, but then he stops and thinks for a moment. “What is that Fortune teller does have the answers to my question?” Juan asks himself not being sure if he should try or not. Juan battles with him, until he figures it’s only ten bucks so he might as well give it a little try. When Juan approaches the tent he hears humming that gets louder and louder as he gets closer. When he finally gets there he slowly opens the curtain. Inside he sees all types of bizarre objects, everything from long and short mirrors to voodoo dolls to odd sculptures and pictures. Juan has a feeling of doubt and is about to leave, but before he can he hears a voice that speaks to him. “Don’t leave my son, I behold all of your answers and I know the truth behind El Romantico, so just come in and I can hopefully fulfill your expectations.” The fortuneteller says, even though Juan can’t see her. Juan also never thought she would be able to read his problem so quickly without even meeting or seeing him. “That’s some pretty impressive work you just did right there.” Juan says as he walks into her room and sees the young scandalously dressed and free natured gypsy that beholds the answers to his questions. “Well I try hard. I see you’re a Portuguese man.” The fortuneteller says as she offers Juan a seat next to her. “Another good read by you.” Juan answers. “I didn’t have to read fir that one. You have a Portuguese accent and you I’ve seen you on the television. “Oh.” Juan replies and the two laugh together, “So I see you want to learn about El Romantico. You do know very little people know nothing about this man don’t you.” She says with her eyes closed, trying to free her mind to help Juan. “That’s why I’m here talking to you. I just need to learn something about this man. Something I can use to solve his mystery.” Juan answers anxious to here his pastime and motives. “Before I begin May I ask you one question?” The young fortuneteller asks as she opens her blue eyes and stares at Juan. “Sure, ask me what you like, but you probably already know the answer.” Juan states as he tries to get comfortable in his seat. “Why must you feel the need to find this mans past? Some things are better left alone, you do know that right.” The fortuneteller adds. Juan takes a second to think of why he really does want to know. He’s not completely sure why, but he’s gotten so involved in it he can’t stop now. Juan settles back down and responds, “I guess it’s just something I have to do to fulfill my curiosity.” “Have you ever thought that maybe your curiosity could deeply hurt someone else?” The fortuneteller asks. “Yes, I’m aware, but I just…” Juan takes a step back to breath calmly before he violently screams out the rest. “I HAVE TO KNOW OKAY. I paid you so just tell me.” The fortuneteller gives Juan a dirty look for responding in such an aggressive manner, but does like she was told and grabs Juan’s hand, squeezing it hard. “What do you see?” Juan asks quickly. “Wait! I need some time for this.” She answers. Minutes pass without a word. Both Juan and her eyes are closed. Finally she speaks to Juan. “I see a poster that says 1881, all come and see the great El Romantico. Now I see a stage. People are gathered around it and looking at someone in front.” The fortuneteller says as she massages her head. “What else?” Juan asks in need of more information. “All I see now is candles and a man around them. The man has flames in his eyes but a heart of gold. All dressed in white, with ladies loathing him, and men wanting to be him.” The fortuneteller states. “But that’s the same way this EL Romantico is, but how could he be around back then.” Juan says as he turns around and scratches his head. “Tell me if it’s the same man as now?” Juan asks as he turns around, but surprisingly when Juan turns around the fortuneteller is gone. All that’s left is a white rose. Juan looks at it in disbelief. Is the fortuneteller really El Romantico?Is El Romantico playing mind games with Juan?Is El Romantico a modern day copycat?Or is El Romantico maybe not even real? All of these questions have aroused, but none will be answered until later, as the end of the tale of El Romantico draws closer and closer.
