Date: not entered She sighed, a somewhat customary motion for Lacuna who tends to sigh at least every fifteen minutes. She wasn’t down, she just couldn’t tell what exactly she was trying to feel. There was definitely something there, an emotion nagging at the back of her head with a renewed vigour. She wasn’t very good with emotions at the best of times, they blurred her clear vision with their impurities, and clarity was nearly all Lacuna wanted from life, well, that and extreme wealth. She passed a hand through her pitch-black hair and examined the insides of her eyelids. She could never try and get enough sleep, tiredness was like a plague to her; it ruined you, befouled your brilliance with its aura of foul emotional exaggerations. She really shouldn’t have gone to her downstairs bedroom, the sheer size of the bed itself was enough to make most people want to sleep, and she was beginning to feel the effects of tiredness on her senses; she was starting to feel the drowsy urge to lie down and close her eyes forever But she couldn’t, and so she lifted herself to her feet with great effort. The room was dark due to its position at the rear of the house, the late afternoon light blocked by the front of the foreboding house, and this did not help trying to avoid sleep. The inspectors would be around soon and she wanted to hear what they said. She stretched her long arms towards the ceiling and her tight purple t-shirt raised slightly, revealing her slim stomach up the belly button. Her deep brown eyes roved again and this time settled on the six-foot tall mound of fluff known to her as ‘Mr Bear’ She smiled; this bear had once featured in a rather long conversation with her that ended up being broadcast as part off the broadcast for the first round of the Primetime Central tag team tournament. People thought she was mad because of it, people would probably think she was more mad for talking to Quoth the Raven, but she did not care at the moment. She shrugged and headed out of the room and down the cream coloured wide hallway. It was perforated by old oak doors and works of art, including a few of her own attempts at impressionism that appeared only successful in her own eyes. Her feet sunk in the deep pile of the crimson red carpet. She appeared underneath the double-split stairways and walked out through the centre into the far end of the hallway, just as the doorbell rang. Now, she was left with a dilemma; she not opened the front door since moving in to the house, but there was nobody around! If she opened it, she would surely lose class, NOBODY opened their own doors nowadays, did they? She looked again, and there was still nobody around. Now, she was confused. “Brook? Brown? Jones? Williams? Doran?” She called futilely into the empty hallway. Surely they had heard the bell! She should not be expected to open her own door when she pays people to do it for her! Slowly, she began to approach the door as if scared of its immense size and stature. It was probably the inspectors, so she could not do it herself, could she? She thought about it and took a step back, raising a hand thoughtfully to her chin. This may seem odd, but this was her life. She would always stick to her morals, as without them then there was nothing left to guide you. Morals were strength and in strength was success. But the door was still there and the bell was still ringing mildly like some sort of obsessed bee that wont stop following you. Her eyes widened and she flicked her head from side to side violently as if looking for someone or shaking off something. She sighed again, breathing out slowly as if to try and compose herself as she ran a hand tentatively through her hair. Right, she thought, positive thinking solves problems. Maybe this was right and not quite as much crap as she believed, so she gave it a try. The bell rang angrily, the volume rising with every second the door stayed closed and she thought positively. …Still thinking positively… And the door was heaved open by the sweat covered middle aged man that is Brook, as Lacuna yelped and ran around a corner. To this day, she still could not explain that action. “Good Afternoon” said a smarmily dressed, silver haired man “I am Don Spare, I am here to inspect some fire damage on the pretence of insurance?” He ended the sentence with a Wes Country-ish tone, making his voice rise at the end as if asking a question. He reached inside his pinstriped suit pocket and pulled out a leather wallet, unfolding it to reveal some form of identity, to which Brook nodded. “If you would like to step this way, Mr Spare, I will fetch Ms. Devries for you, she is the owner of this property.” He waved his hand into the centre of the magnificent hallway and stepped inside, grinning slightly at the look of amazement on Mr. Spare’s face. Lacuna, who was hiding in the second cloakroom, made some noise. No reason really, possibly to try and make herself sound busy and not at all as mad as she seemed. She turned and stepped out of the room into the hallway, gasping in a laugh at the horrendous suit of the inspector. She was more embarrassed about the door answering that she let on, and Brook recognised it easily. “Ah, and you would be the inspector?” She knew full well who he was, she was making general small talk “I am Don Spare, yes” He flashed his ID again, seemingly proud of his position and the amount of money it must be paying “Do they not pay you or something? What on earth made you buy such a hideous suit?” Silence… Spare twisted awkwardly as Lacuna’s eyes roved his body for further opportunities to dig in the knife. She had taken an instant dislike to the man and intended to make that clearer, maybe even make him fear for his life… yes, that would be fun. He tried to break the silence, clearly unnerved. “Shall we get of with it then?” “Of course, I will be down in a moment, Brook will lead the way” Brook turned and motioned for the inspector, now feeling as if he was on trial, to follow him through one of the many oak doors lining the hallway. Lacuna turned away and began to head back up the stairs, seemingly picking a room at random and turning inside. The room was not decorated, the walls a shade grey and the floor covered thinly with a blue mistake for a carpet. Ignoring the mess she of boxes on the floor, she bounced happily to the window and watched Brook and Mr. Spare head down to the burnt out ruins of her shed. She wanted to gauge his reaction from a far before heading down, his face could tell her how she needed to handle the situation and so she watched. Brook stepped over a large puddle and motioned his right hand for Don to follow. In all his middle aged wisdom, Brook had developed an interesting sense of humour, and seeing Spare dressed to the teeth in shiny new clothes; he decided to take the muddiest route, completely ignore the buggies parked next to the rear door for such an occasion. Spare’s shining shoes were now coated in a thick layer of brown sludge and the back of his ridiculous pinstripe suite jacket was dotted with spots that had flicked up as he made his way across the lawns. “I presume the contents was insured, Mr…” “Brook, its Brook. And yes, of course it was. Now hurry up, will you? I’m sure Lacuna will be down soon” “Good, good…” - - - From her vantage point, Lacuna smiled. Brook was doing a good job in out casting poor Mr Spare, and that could only result in a better outcome for her. Now she remembered what exactly it was she paid him for. Standing to her feet, she paced out of the room and back into the corridor where she reached deeply into a pocket, removing a pink bobble and tying back her hair roughly. Still walking, she zipped up her jacket and turned left into another corridor, this time smaller. The wind whistled roughly at the end, pointing out that this corridor apparently leads outside. Well, it usually wouldn’t but the door was left open. “Don’t try this at home kids” she muttered to herself through gritted teeth as she set her eyes upon the zip-line ahead of her. Yeah, that’s right, a zip line. One leading to the ground below. No harness or padding were provided, she doubted whether it was even legal. But it served its purpose and she hadn’t been injured yet. So, she grasped hard onto the handles and through herself off the ledge. - - - “Well, this is it” Mentioned Brook with distaste. “Really, I would never have guessed” he hit back sarcastically, to the anger of Brook zzzzzzZZZZTHUD. Lacuna connects hard with the soft ground, sending water and mud splashing around the point of impact. Unfortunately for Mr. Spare, it cascades up his awful suit creating quite possibly the single funniest image ever as it splatters into his face. Brook struggles to hide his laugh as Lacuna gets to her feet and attempts to brush herself down a little, just getting a little muddier. “Done yet?” he asks as if nothing has happened to the surprise of both men. “Er, we were just getting to that Ms. Devries, I was, er, examining the outer reached” “Ok, what’s stopping you then? We don’t have all day do we Brook?” And so, the inspector went to work. Removing a clipboard from his briefcase, he stepped cautiously inside the fire-gutted building. “Hmm, yes, you sure did have a fire here didn’t you?” Lacuna turns and looks at Brook who raises a quizzical eyebrow in return. “No shit Sherlock, now get on with it” [TBC]
