The Scarlett Dress; Act I, Scene I

2 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09-02-02 The Magnolia DistrictNew Orleans, LA 1:19 amOne Week Before GTT The Magnolia in the deep neck woods of Louisiana was a known place for bootleggers to wet their beaks a bit, shipping in some imported brand of liquor to the raving and ravenous patrons. These would be the men who'd shoot with itchy trigger fingers and ask questions about it later. One man, sitting off in the far right corner was Frankin 'Frank' Tyrell, dressed down in a pin-stripe black suit and a pair of gator loafers. A mayoral candidate in his own right, seeking a second term, people wondered why was he in this bar when he could have gone to any other bar in the state. Tyrell was white, starring into a predominated black realm where guns and booze ran the minds. His assistant, Raymond Williamson happened to enjoy this type of life, while being one heavy drinker and known to shoot a few people on sight if they happened to piss him off well enough. Frank sat back, eying a glass a bourbon in a shot glass. The quart happend to sit a few inches away from the glass. Raymond, dressed in a burgundy jacket with black slacks and a top hat nudged him on the shoulder. 'What's wrong Frank?' the yes man asked. Tyrell with his eyes tiring and his blood laced with brown alcohol slurred out, 'This, r-race might n-not be e-e-easy to win.' His raven hair, mopped down into a toupee look of style happened to drip down, thanks to hand grease found at the mayoral mansion near the docks of the ferry port. Thirty-four, young and a hot shot at that. He'd beaten the past incumbent before him with ease, landslide after he happened to struggle with the young vote. 'The election isn't for months, and you're worried about it? Seesh, you need to cut down a bit.' the younger man, Raymond, chuckled. Tyrell shook his head. 'Its not that man, people know I'm going to win - hell I'm the youngest Mayor ever!' 'There's always caution in the wind Frank, always.' Raymond told him as they both clashed shot glasses together and drank up. They drunkenly chuckle, before Frank gets up from the chair and swings around a woman with reckless ease. He was the attractive type of power women wanted to be with - a love horn fool. He drags her down into Raymond's seat, much to his chagrin. The dark colored woman, wearing a red sun dress and her auburn hair rolled into a bun, laughed at the drunken incumbent, before he gave her a drink. Looking at his watch, Raymond taps Frank's shoulder. 'I think its time for you to get out, you have a speech upcoming in about seven hours.' 'Ri-right.' he slurred out. 'I-I'll be headed home now, but if you'd love to come, you can.' he whispered to the woman, who obliged by raising her hand, being taken by the mayor of New Orleans as they exited. Frank climbed into the backseat of his 81' Rolls Royce as did the woman. Raymond climbed in the passenger side of the car, as the driver, Nathan Green, pulled out of the bar. They turn into a right alleyway, as the mayor happens to slur and laugh along with the woman as he jokes around with her. 'So, you like white men?' Frank asked. The woman nodded her head, before jokingly telling, 'I hear that white men got money, and the tools to back it up.' he began to rub on his crotch. He pushed her hand off easily. 'Can't have a scandal before the race, now can we?' he told her, before instructing Nathan to head on towards the house. As the car began to pull into the mansion, Frank climbed out first, almost stumbling into his mailbox. Raymond hops out of the passenger side, helping Frank out and towards the front door. Pushing his way through the hi tech maze, Raymond bellowed out, 'Home!' to the voice activated security system, cutting off red beams and attack dogs stationed in the manor. A few minutes pass as the party carry themselves upstairs, complete darkness surrounding them all as feminine grunts and screams are heard. A pounding sound echoes, before the screaming stops and all is silent through the house. 'Clean this up, CLEAN IT NOW!' a man yells out as he struggles to regain composure. He thinks to himself, calming himself down before dialing 911. Police arrive minutes later, coming into finding the body of the incumbent Mayor, dead on the floor with a bullet wound to the head. 'Time of death?' the doctor asks, examining the body. Looking at his watch, an officer tells him, 'I'd say about 2:12, 2:13.' 'A murder in the heat of the night.' the doctor jokes. 'Man that's catchy.' The officer turns his back to the doctor as they load the body of the mayor onto a stretcher, not even caring to stare into the honey dew look of the sunrise in the morning. Morning happened to be coming up in New Orleans, the Big Easy and the man who ran the show happened to be gone, snatched from his fame. Peter Evans, the man who told the time picked up a piece of paper from the mansion and stares at it peculiarly. 'Hmm, April Jackson...' He folded the paper in his pocket and climbed into his patrol car, back to headquarters to find more about the woman in a red dress. * * * American Gardens Building; 7th Floor, Room 324BLower East Side, New York City, New York8:14 amDay 1 of GTT Fresh morning coffee, dewed out of the pot and ready for consumption. Early morning coffee gets you amped, pumped up, and ready to tackle things you wouldn't usually have to tackle. I might have been half sleep, but I smell coffee early, it tells me something needs to be done for the next morning. You see problems mount up on you, as soon as you take a step out of your home. Problems don't care who you happen to be, just as long as they emphasis on your biggest one and blow it up even further. Turning on my side, I could feel a sharp pain in my lower back, maybe its my disc that keeps spinning, or some inflamed spasm. Us wrestlers are bombarded with different things, such as exercise tapes, sponsorships and money laundering to take different things and put them at the next level of things - to please the fans. My eyes opened, starring into the blank red expression of 8:12 in the morning. That inanimate convenience of that clock giving you a thoughtful kick in the face to wake up. I notice things now, that I didn't six months ago when I first got back into the game. The sun crept into the window adjacent from the bed as I stepped out, with my eyes filled with sleep and other chemicals made by bodily fluids. Walking out of the room, I could hear the voice of my lovely, cooing the baby to sleep. 'Morning.' I wonder, how'd she sneak off from the bed? 'Hey.' I kiss her on the cheek, the baby - a mixture of Creole and mixed color happens to close her eyes. That's my Simmone, quiet and never there when the excitements a dull manner. I move towards the computer, taking a mug of coffee with me. Although my album since its June 16th release hasn't let its swing on the charts go I figure its time for it to drop down a bit. Bringing up the MTV site, after seven weeks - 5.1 million sales, the thing takes up 105, 019 copies to stay at number one. 'Damn, this thing won't stop will it?' She joked, 'Maybe you'll murder enough people to put Clyde out of business.' I laughed hardly at that. Because, I've heard a rumor that when you get in touch with the name of Clyde, you have to be satanic, or he castrates you. Of course, its a rumor - I hope. 'So what's new for today?' she asked me. I shrugged my shoulders and just said to her, 'I'll play it by ear.' The common meeting with record execs was next, after the meeting with my agent and then mapping out things to do with my baby - it was a hell of a week. I joined in, sipping down some putrid gunk that was called coffee. 'The hell is this?' I question its maker, Tracy. 'Herbal Juices, with a lot of sugar. You're going to need it.' she told me point blank, clutching Simmone and resting her on the outside of her shoulder. 'Call him.' 'Who, Paul?' She eyed me with a smile on her face, 'Who am I thinking about silly? Your darn agent.' I muttered to myself before retorting, 'Agents and enemies are bed-fellows. Same with executives and corruption.' 'You've been getting those quotes from your father huh?' she asked me dead on knowing the truth of it. Unlike many people, both my parents were alive, living their 60 year old lives out in the Hamptons. My father was the great philosopher of the family, always having a quote for anything he laid his mind towards. Him and my grandfather, some thirty years his elder happened to say the same things, often trying to out duel one another at who was smarter. 'Hey, he cares about your safety for this tournament!' Torunament? Please! What in the world is an agent going to tell me? Work on the upper body? Figure out strategy? Nope, he is the money man, wanting in whether I lose or win. I'd rather bust my spleen and feed it to him, before I pay him. 'I'll handle Greenbaulm, my-damn-self.' I told her. 'You worry about just getting the true information about this...' I trailed off before finishing. 'Lana Day?' She chuckled, 'A woman is it? I'll get on it soon as you leave out and breakfast is on the table, if you want it babe.' 'Thanks.' Taking a sigh, I move in towards the televison, before the phone rings out of curiosity. I shrug towards Tracy, leaning down in my white NYU T-shirt and some black Nike shorts. It been a few weeks since I started taking phone calls and all, with the entire broken story about how somebody happened to try and ice me at the gym. I'm a cautious man, knowing his boundaries and when stepping them. 'Um, hello?' I question out with hesitation. 'May I speak with a Mr. Dante Jackson?' the voice on the other end told me. It was that thick, raspy Southern drawl of a voice, something I hadn't heard for months. 'I'm Dante Jackson, who is this?''My name is Peter Evans. I'm with the Magnolia Police District sir.' For a quick second, something in the air changed once I heard his voice. 'Wait, you are again?'
