Beyond Reason

13 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10-13-02 Beyond Reason “A Warrior’s Struggle” Thursday, October 10th The nights are long, and the days are cold. Life passes minute by minute. There is no sleep, there is no alertness. Pain passes second by second. The agonies will me to my breaking point. I break. A great warrior falls. A kingdom is defeated. But…I haven’t been pushed off the edge yet. I stand solid, like a warrior should stand. Down but not out, the kingdom stays, sparkling in the sunlight. I am re-gathering my strength. An evil deed has been cursed upon my life, and the last hope for humanity rests upon my shoulders. I am placing my forces. Preparing for a fierce battle. It is time to go to war.Today... The cursed of all cursed… the day when luck is not a charm, but a disease, spreading its contagious infection throughout the lands. Luck has no meaning to a warrior. I train, day in and day out. Mentally, I lift boulders. Physically, I am strapped to a hospital bed. Another torture. Another agony. I am writhing in pain, and I can stand it no longer! There must be an escape. There is an escape. There is no time to write. The first battle is coming. It is time to go to war. I’m a warrior till I die. The Infinite Warrior brought his arm into the air. He grabbed a cord from his vision, ripped it away in fury. Pain. Blood. A cloth is tied tightly around his forehead. Trigger slid his hands under the bandage, and clawed his way out. Sweating, he sat up, tried to get to his feet, but was trapped in place. As his fury strengthened, so did his body, and with a final burst of adrenaline, the warrior broke away the cast-like object wrapped around his left leg. The warrior is free, and he stands up, looking down below him. A polished tile floor, glimmering white as a pearl. So delicate, so untouched… Trigger shook his head, and blood-soaked hair hurled red liquid to the floor. A polished tile floor, glimmering white as a pearl… splotched with murky blood, a fiery statement delivered from a fiery soul.He looked around, taking note of his location. Creeping around a corner, he came across a small restroom, and entered cautiously, always aware that the next step could be his last. A mirror sat above the sink, and he aimed for it. A warrior does not mind his appearance. A warrior’s appearance is that of a warrior. His hair seemed to have grown since he last checked… The dark brown hair came down to his neck, and bloody strands dripped along his forehead. So rugged. He tore a strip from the sleeve of his black hospital shirt, and tied it around his head in bandana fashion. His eyes were swollen and baggy. How can one not sleep when he is not awake? His lip was split and a deep cut ran down his cheek. An entirely different person. Definitely not Trigger. How long had he been out? One week? Two? So many concussions, yet so little rewards. What had made the Infinite Warrior enter such a career where the sacrifice of the body was the only hope of glory? When Dr. Bernard Marshall entered the room, he looked up from his clipboard and his heart jumped at what he saw. He had just been making the daily doctor’s rounds, and now this… bandages and cords strewn about? Fresh blood on the floor? And the patient… the patient was missing. It couldn’t have been like he got up and walked away. The man had been unconscious for nearly a week, damaged from some kind of extreme sport. It angered the doctor so to see so many talented young adults throwing their lives away for a little bit of fame and a few dollars. Then something caught Dr. Marshall’s eye. Light coming from underneath the bathroom door. Something was going on, and the answer would lie behind this door. The doctor stepped out into the hallway and quietly picked a phone off the wall. He called hospital security and waited in fear. Trigger sat on the floor of the restroom, trying to make sense of the situation. He tried to concentrate, but something was missing, something was holding him back. What? Was something wrong here? Yes… but not with the situation. It seemed to the Infinite Warrior as if something was wrong with Trigger himself. He’d heard the stories, of wrestlers taking a bad bump and ending up with permanent brain damage. He’d always thought he was above that; stronger than the rest. He tried to think back. What was the last thing he remembered? A face standing above him, holding a weapon of some kind… Ah yes, it was all coming back to him now. Eddie Mandrake from Primetime Championship Wrestling. Beating him senselessly. The rest is fuzzy. A long delay, and then… BOOM! Senior security officer Dan Withers kicked open the bathroom door and raised his gun. “Freeze!”Standing before him stood a living nightmare. Although seemingly under six feet tall, the character was menacing. His hair was, tied back in a bandana, was wildly strewn about, and his face, his expression… the savage look in his eyes… It was enough to cause Dan to hesitate. The man got a crazed look upon him. He raised his arms as if to strike. Dan’s reflexes were too quick. Bang! He hit the trigger and shot a bullet through the man’s stomach. Friday, October 11th They’ve locked me away. At one point I was the victim, yet now I am the criminal. Perhaps I speak too harshly. The truth is that I am not quite aware of the entire situation myself. I remember finding myself in a hospital bed… but not the same one. No, it was a whole different room; a different building for all I knew. I don’t know whether to scream for help or attack with all my might. They could never silence a warrior. Yet I am silenced. Have you ever stood still and listened to the emptiness that fills the room? I have. Usually it is unbearable. Like someone screaming into my ears, I pull away, breaking the silence. But recently things have changed. Now when I go quiet, things are calm at first, but ever so slowly, voices begin to emerge. Not recognizable, not understandable… not even voices, perhaps. But I can hear them. I want them to stop—I want them to go away.These voices, they haunt me all the time. They tell me to kill; they tell me to die. They tell me to laugh; they tell me to cry. Why won’t they stop? I can’t hold firm any longer. I think I’m about to break. Dr. Marshall walked briskly to the front desk, a look of urgency upon his face. “I heard he woke up today.” “That’s right,” said the receptionist.Fury grew inside the doctor. “Why wasn’t I told?” “He was transported this morning to the mental institution wing. He’s no longer a patient here.”This should have been enough. Dr. Marshall should have left the situation as it stood, and let the appropriate specialists deal with it. He should have… but he couldn’t. What was it about this patient that intrigued him so? Maybe it was curiosity. It wasn’t every day that the doctor saw such an injury. It was as if someone had taken a jackhammer to the man’s head. But even more curious was the fact that the patient had simply gotten up and walked to the bathroom the day before. How could this be? An injury of such caliber could have rendered the man dead, or at least in a coma. But it didn’t… and something was telling Dr. Marshall that there was something behind this. Why did he always have to get so involved with the patients? He shouldn’t do this. He should know better. There was a fine line between patient and doctor. He shouldn’t care. Be he did. And before he knew it, he was at another receptionist’s desk, asking for the man’s room number. He walked right up to the room, and began to open the door. It had only gapped a crack when a woman’s head stuck out, and asked what he was doing. “Who are you?” the doctor demanded in response. “My name’s Sarah Mercury. I’m a new nurse here.” “I need to find out what’s going on with this patient. I need to study the results of his injury.”She smiled. “I’m afraid that’s not possible.” “What the hell do you mean?” Dr. Marshall didn’t know what was coming over him. He’d never acted this rude to a co-worker. Her expression didn’t change, however, as she politely replied. “This patient has been classified as a danger risk; we cannot let anyone in that is unqualified.” “Unqualified!? Unqualified!?” His face grew red with anger. “Please leave now,” she said sternly, “or I will call security.”He paused for a moment, and then marched off, embarrassed and angry. Who did she think she was? The realization then dawned on Dr. Marshall that it was he who was in the wrong. Ashamedly he told himself that he had been out of line. But the anxiety and curiosity didn’t disappear. He walked right past the receptionist, not stopping even for a polite goodbye. She stopped him anyways, however, and spoke to him in a tone that made him want to snap. “Things didn’t go the way you planned?”She smirked. He bit his lip. “No,” he replied, “Miss Mercury seems to have things under control back there.”The receptionist looked surprised. “Miss Mercury?”Dr. Marshall’s patience was wearing thin. “Yes, Sarah Mercury; the new nurse.” “Hmm…” She raised an eyebrow, but nonetheless typed something into her computer and nodded. “Dr. Marshall,” she started. His blood ran cold. “There’s no Sarah Mercury working at this hospital.” Trigger opened his eyes. Everything was fuzzy. He tried to get up, but couldn’t move. He started to feel dizzy. He closed his eyes. The Infinite Warrior had repeated this exercise all morning, and things didn’t seem to be getting better. Except now, when he opened his eyes again, he could see a face looming over him. Sort of like an angel carrying him from his coffin. Must be dreaming. Either that or he was dead.Then, Trigger felt soft skin against his cheek, and his head was gently lifted up. He tried hard and was able to move his arms, bring them up to his face, and rub his eyes. Ah, now the picture was clear. But the face before him didn’t go away. No, now he could see it perfectly—see her perfectly. His first thought was of her beauty… long blond hair rolled down into her smooth face. She wore a smile that made Trigger himself feel happy. Then he wondered who she was, and what she was doing there. He then wondered what he himself was doing there. He tried to get up, but felt a searing pain in his stomach. Oh yes, the gunshot wound. The warrior winced. Why did he put up with all of this? The answer hit him like a sack of bricks. He was a warrior.“Are you all right?” The woman spoke, a voice that was as nice as her appearance. Trigger cleared his throat. “I think I’m okay. Who are you?” “I’m Sarah Mercury.” A devilish smirk appeared across her lips. “They think I’m a nurse here.”He didn’t have time to question the statement before she helped him up to sitting position. Her grip was strong. She giggled, feeling his muscles as she steadied him. “Wow, you’re strong.”The woman who called herself Sarah stared into his face and a sense of realization hit her. “Hey… I’ve seen you on TV. Aren’t you that guy from…”He tried to respond with a yes, but was thrown into an uncontrollable coughing fit. Blood spewed from his throat as he tried to calm himself, and she rushed to his side to help. He shoved her away, staring deep inside her mind. “…You already knew who I am… before you asked… You know where I’m from…”Sarah looked befuddled. After a long pause though, she answered. “Well yeah, I guess I did. Didn’t think you’d realize that though.”Was Trigger smiling, frowning, or simply grimacing from the pain. “I realize a lot of things.” “I didn’t mean anything by it,” came her response immediately. “I just didn’t want you to know… didn’t want you to get the wrong idea about me. See I’ve got a plan, Trigger… it’ll change both of our lives forever.”It was now the warrior’s turn to be confused. She tugged at his arm. “We don’t have much time,” she exclaimed. “Come with me!”He didn’t budge. Who was this strange woman prying him away from the room? He didn’t understand what was going on. He wanted to sleep. Yes, sleep… “I need to get you out of here. Come on!”No reaction, but she went on anyways, now more urgently than before. “They’ll try to keep you here forever, Trigger! Come with me, it’s your only hope!”Why was she in such a rush? The Infinite Warrior wanted to go… but not now. No, he was too tired. There was a banging against the door. Trigger saw a bulky security guard yelling into the little window. So noisy… but Trigger couldn’t hear it. Or maybe it was a sound-proof room. He lay back down on the bed, and a nice warmth came over him. So tired… He wanted to sleep. “Trigger, come on! Please, I’m begging you…”Yes, sleep… that was the answer… He closed his eyes, sinking into a deep rest… Saturday, October 12th We’ve been on the road for hours. Originally when I woke up, I was inside a dark space, the trunk of a car. My head pounded like a jackhammer. So much pain… I wanted to cry. After a while, she took me out, this Sarah Mercury. She tried to explain things…too fast, too much… she talks a lot. Probably a bad person… but I haven’t had the energy to figure things out. All I know is that she came out of nowhere, knowing me, and took me away from the room. I don’t remember much. Less and less, in fact. Why was I in the hospital? There was a reason, I’m sure of it. I even knew it… but slowly, my memories seem to be fading away. They don’t even seem real anymore, just like a dream. It looks like my only hope is this mysterious woman. I don’t fear her. I don’t fear myself. But I fear what I am becoming… “You’re not crazy,” she said, after a long silence. Was she talking to herself? “I’m not crazy either, you know.” No, apparently not talking to herself. She was addressing him, addressing Trigger. Didn’t make much sense though… he was pretty sure that one of them was insane. Did it matter, though? “I’d thought about this for a long time,” she continued, despite a lack of response from the passenger. “Didn’t think I’d actually go through with it though—especially not how I did. Basically, all I wanted to do was—” The Infinite Warrior interrupted. “Why me?” Sarah stared intently at the road, as if she didn’t even hear him. For the first time, Trigger himself looked out the window, and saw as the sun peaked out from above hilltops and plains. The weather was calm, the subtly changing from summer to fall. Not much of a view out on the road. Few bushes along the sides… the grass was that ugly brown color, such a contrast from the majestic green that sprouted a few months ago. There was nothing to see here. This in itself made the scene beautiful. Trigger turned to face Sarah. She matched his gaze, but not before securing the view of the street. “Well… you… I guess I saw something in you. It cried out to me… and when I get this kind of feeling, I usually can’t let go.” The drowsiness was wearing off, and Trigger began to see more of the picture. Not letting her slip away, he questioned more. “What the hell do you want with me!?” “I thought I told you—” “—I wasn’t listening! Now I’m listening.” She hesitated. “Well, as stupid as it may sound… I want—I want to help you become the best at what you do.” “How are you gonna do that?” “I want to manage your wrestling career!” That through him for a loop. Wrestling? Oh yeah… that was what he did for a living, wasn’t it? His mind had awoken, but the fog over Trigger’s brain did not let up. Mistaking his silence for fear, Ms. Mercury continued. “Don’t get the wrong idea—I am not crazy… But I have these ideas that will make you the greatest star of them all!” For some odd reason… Trigger believed her. But the original question still stood. “Why me?” Now it was obvious that Sarah was getting impatient. “I already told you: I like you.”Oh yes… she’d already told him. Why couldn’t he focus? As Trigger stared out the window of the car, time sped by. It seemed like only moments to him, but before long, the car curved up a steep hill, around a bend, and parked outside a country-style house. The locks clicked, and the driver side door opened, as Sarah stepped out. The Infinite Warrior followed, but didn’t know why. “This is my house—for now at least.” Trigger didn’t respond, so she went on. “I move around a lot. Let’s go inside though, I can tell you what I’ve been planning. Come on.” He followed her up the steps to the porch. Why? She stuck a key into the lock and turned… opening the door and entering, before beckoning the straggler. He went inside. Why? Dropping the keys onto a crate on the floor, Sarah began her explanation. “I’ve wanted to become a manager my whole life. ‘Been trying to break into it for a while now… I just can’t seem to get a break. But then I got this idea, I thought ‘Why try to find a big star, when I can manage a small star, and make them big?’ Want some lunch?” He shook his head and she went on, collapsing onto a couch in what seemed to be the living room. “So anyways, that’s where you come in. Anyone who watches PCW can tell that you’ve got talent… but your career isn’t going anywhere. I mean, it’s almost like you want to do bad.” This irritated him, but he kept it to himself as she went on. “You don’t really have a gimmick… that’s the first thing. And whereas I see all these other guys on commercials and in movies and all—you’re nowhere! Hell, Trigger, you’ve been in PCW for almost half a year, and where’ve you gotten? Nowhere.” The warrior could take no more. Rage burned within his soul as he let out a fury of words upon his victim. “Why does it matter? I have no gimmick—I’m proud! I don’t sell myself to cheesy advertisements, and I don’t disgrace my soul to excel my career. Don’t you understand that? If not, then you’ve chosen the wrong person to kidnap.” “I—” “And speaking of which, what the hell am I doing here??? Where am I? I don’t even know what state I’m in—” “We’re in California, but—” He didn’t let her finish. “Well I’m leaving! I’m going to buy a plane ticket, and I’m going to get out of here.” Now she grew silent. She seemed afraid as she meagerly told him, “…I don’t think that’s gonna be possible.” “Why is that?” Now her voice was so soft, almost like a mouse. “I took all the money out of your bank account.” Although his mouth remained silent, his eyes lit fire to the house. A thousand daggers pierced his heart and the demons of his soul called to be free. His muscles bulged, his throat choked up. Sarah looked worried. “I-I- I needed to buy this house… I promise I’ll repay you… I-I—” Trigger clenched his fists and opened his mouth, but was interrupted by the ringing of a phone in another room. After three rings, the answering machine clicked on, and the caller began to leave a message. “Hello, this is the talent directors from Primetime Championship Wrestling, and—” The Infinite Warrior spun his gaze back to Sarah, his presence alone demanding an answer. She gave him one, as she stood there, petrified. “…I also had them redirect all your calls to this number…” Without waiting for more, Trigger burst into the next room, and immediately spotted the telephone. The message was still going: “We are aware of Mr. Trigger’s injury, but he is not covered under our insurance and therefore will be released from—” He picked up the receiver, but from his hands, Sarah grabbed the phone, and when she talked into it, it was not the trembling child that spoke, but the stern businesswoman. “Hello? This is Trigger’s manager. No—Yes—Of course I am aware of—” The warrior made no attempt to pry the phone from her grasp. She took control of the conversation. “Listen, Trigger’s injury is healed, and he will return to action! What’s that? This Friday? He’ll be there!” She hung up without saying goodbye. Now it was Trigger’s turn to be anxious. “They booked you for a match,” she started. “This Friday, in Tokyo, Japan. If you don’t show up, you’re fired.” He stayed silent. “I told them you’d be there.” A pause. “Do you have enough money for a plane ticket?” he asked. She nodded. “Then I’ll be there.”
