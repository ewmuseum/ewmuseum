The Flashbacks [Chapter One] - Infirmary Blues

20 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 20th of June, 2002 The Pharaoh sat upright in bed. A pair of thin-rimmed silver spectacles sat upon the bridge of his nose as he read through a thick book found within the confines of his elegant hands. A month or so had passed since he was injured and yet he still seemed to be recovering from the concussion he received in his TV title defence. The wealthy Egyptian groaned in sudden agony, he threw himself frantically to his left simultaneously tossing the book to the black and white tiled floor beneath him. Slowly he clenched his forehead in pain and groaned again. He shook his head to and fro in a vain attempt to shake off the pain flowing through his badly damaged skull, but alas, it was to no avail. He looked up towards the door of the white room he sat within and reached out towards its shimmering surface. It wasn’t long before he gave up hope of reaching it, but he found that next to him a small table was situated. A tall glass of water glimmered in the light from a tall lamp in the corner of his room, and next to this a small bell, golden in colour and writhed in silver hieroglyphics was adequately located. He grinned, ignoring the throbbing for a second. He picked the bell up and shook it with great force. “Nurse! Nurse!” he shouted as he continued to ring the small yet well crafted bell. His head was still recovering slowly from the bump he took a month and a half ago at Supremacy. But even this was a slow recovery; after all it had been almost six weeks since he took the fall. A few moments passed before the door of the room was franticly thrown open in a hurry. A good looking woman with long, well toned legs in a nursing uniform stood breathing heavily as if she’d rushed to Pharaoh’s aid without thought for her own health. “Yes, Mr Xansiv?” she asked after a moment. Pharaoh groaned slightly and then pointed to the glass of water by his side. “I need some more tablets… to ease the pain,” he stated with dramatic expressions that wrinkled his face into something that vaguely resembled a ball of tightly assembled rubber bands. “But sir, I can’t do that! There’s a certain time you have to wait before you can take another-” “Do I look like I care? Just get me another painkiller, dammit! My head’s killing me!” bellowed The Pharaoh in frustration. The nurse shook her head in pity for the Egyptian athlete and then left the room with a sigh. As much as he wanted another paracetamol she couldn’t give him one. Outside Pharaoh’s room, sat upon a small white seat attached to the hospitals wall, was Isiy Xansiv, Titu’s gorgeous wife. A worried look flushed her already troubled face. As the nurse passed she leapt to her feet. “Nurse! How is he?” The nurse stopped in her tracks and turned to The Pharaoh’s loving wife. “He’s demanding more painkillers everyone ten minutes, Mrs Xansiv, and I’m afraid that I can’t give him them. I’m surprised by the way he’s acting. The concussion was bad, yes, but surly not this bad. He’s in a lot of pain, I’m afraid, and I really don’t know why.” “But its been almost two months…” said Isiy in distress. The nurse sighed. “I know…” The long legged woman smiled sympathetically and walked away shaking her head as Isiy sat back down on her seat. Next to her a man sat holding his arm in utter stress. Isiy looked him up and down and sighed in sorrow. The man breathed heavily, and he ground his teeth trying to ease the hurt that his arm was causing him, a hopeless attempt, but an attempt nevertheless. “Are you alright?” asked Isiy conversationally. The man turned to her and frowned. “I ‘ate frickin’ ‘ospitals!” He turned away from ‘The Charm’ and looked down the long corridor to his left. It was obvious that he had no intensions of speaking with Isiy, and so she took the hint and continued her worrying of Titu. She got to her feet and walked towards her husband’s room’s door. She peered inside through the tiny glass window. Titu sat holding his head groaning in agony. “It will get better, my love…I promise.” she said softly. Suddenly Pharaoh’s condition seemed to lessen. He breathed easily once again and he removed the hand from his forehead. Slowly he slumped back down into the white hospital covers around him and seemed to fall into a deep sleep… “Get better, my dearest.” Isiy walked away from the sight of her husband and made her way towards a coffee machine in the distance. As she walked she looked down at the watch upon her wrist and sighed deeply. “Eleven thirty…” She paused. “I need some rest.” She looked at the machine in front of her. It was a quick and easy way to keep awake, and what if Titu’s condition grew worse in the night? She couldn’t go home. Not yet. Rest would have to wait; her husband needed her now. She took out a coin from her pocket and slotted it into the coffee machine. She turned and looked back towards the direction of her husband. “It’s going to be a long night…” The Pharaoh had drifted into a slumber, a very strange and disturbing one. He witnessed a small child, quickly digging with a silver object in the backyard of a green, flourishing garden. A tall, dark skinned man, much of the same colour skin as Titu, ran up to the boy and picked him up. The child was only young and immediately began to cry. “Hush, Charles…We aren’t mad at you.” Said the man. A woman came into the blurred vision. Titu’s unsuspecting eyes ran rampant when she entered, he knew her, but how? “We just don’t want you digging up the backyard, honey.” She joked with a laugh. The man put the boy down, and he ran off into a distance that Xansiv’s mind could not make out. The man and woman remained for a few seconds before fading away to nothingness. The morning came and Titu woke early, and with a slight jump. The dream had startled him somewhat, yet he didn’t know why. A distinct feeling of remembrance filled his heart before another feeling hit him. One that immediately took away any other thoughts he had in coalition with the dream. The concussion was back with vengeance. Outside the room Isiy still sat, a cup of hot, steaming coffee in her hand. Her eyes were half shut but also half open. It really had been a long night. A long night for her that was! Titu had slept all night without anything more than a snore to be heard from his room. She shook her head with a smile before a low and pain-filled groan hit her ears. “Titu!” she murmured. She ran up to the door again and looked on into the room. There he was, once again his hand had found his forehead and he moaned in agony, sweat flowed from his aching head. A nurse, this time a short and fat stubby woman, who was in desperate need of a shave, ran up beside Isiy and looked inside. “Don’t worry, Mrs Xansiv. It’s time for his painkillers anyway.” The nurse opened the door and took out a small white tablet as she did. She gave it to Xansiv, who seemed to know exactly what to do with the medication. He placed it on his tongue, and picked up the glass of water by his side. “That’s’ it,” started the nurse as ‘The Jewel’ took a deep gulp of the water, swallowing the pain killer. “The pain should ease again, soon enough.” She stated with a relaxing smile. Titu threw himself down onto his pillow and turned onto his side. “All I wanted to do was defend it you know…” The nurse, who had begun to edge towards the door, rubbed her bristly chin before turning back to ‘The Egyptian Jewel’ with a curious expression. “Excuse me?” Silence filled the air and then Titu spoke again. “The TV title! I only wanted to defend it. And look at me now. The pain isn’t giving me a break, I’ll be surprised if I make it back to the ring.” The nurse smiled. “Mr Xansiv, you may have concussion, but it will go, and when it does you’ll be back in the ring in no time.” Titu didn’t feel the same way; he had become quite darkened in the time he had spent in the hospital… who wouldn’t? He hated the place, even the stench of disinfectants made him feel sick! So many days, with little to do had made him bored beyond belief, he was sick of the hospital more than ever. “Leave me now, I need my rest.” He said doubtfully. The nurse began to leave the room again, this time reaching the door without a word from ‘The New Hope’. “As you wish…” she said before leaving. Outside the room Isiy watched patiently. “When can I see him again, nurse?” she asked. The nurse looked in through the window, Titu was asleep again. “When he wakes, I’ll tell him you wish to see him.” She replied. Isiy looked relieved. The last time she had spoken with her husband was a nearly a week ago. Since then he had been in more pain than he had been when he first took the bump on the head. He said he didn’t wish for her to see him in such a state, but she didn’t care. It was as though he thought she’d love him less if she saw him in pain. But that was ridiculous. “Thank you.” Isiy conceded. The nurse smiled and made her way back down the long turquoise corridor. Titu had not fallen into a slumber as the nurse has previously thought. The book he had been reading had been picked up and placed back on the table next to his bed and considering that the pain that had troubled him after he woke had seemingly vanished he decided to catch up on his reading. The migraine-type headaches lasted for unmentionable lengths of time, and they varied rapidly. There was no telling when the aches in his cranium would return. He turned to his right and picked up the book. He pushed his pillow up so that it would support his back and as we began our tale, he sat upright against the pillow-cushioned wall. “Now, where was I?” He turned a few pages of the book and smiled. “Ah! Here we are!” His voice was fairly loud and outside the room his wife Isiy heard his words. She leapt up and peered into his room. There he was reading carefully. “Now, where are my glasses?” he asked himself silently. Isiy reached for the door handle and opened the door, she ran inside as a shocked Pharaoh watched on. “Isiy!” he shouted in joy. “Titu!” she replied in an as joyful voice as her husband. Isiy ran up towards Pharaoh and embraced him in great bliss. Pharaoh kissed her lovingly and then she pulled away with a grin. “What are you doing here?” he asked. “I’ve been here for the past few days. I’ve only been home a few times.” Pharaoh frowned in confusion. “Our home, Isiy, is I Egypt, surly you haven’t been paying for flights back and forth every so often?” Isiy smiled like a slave who had just managed to finally outsmart their master by unleashing a plague of poisonous snakes upon their master’s place of rest, thankfully Isiy had never had the viciousness to do such a thing and obviously she wasn’t a slave to Titu, its just that that was the only thing that it almost resembled. “Of course not, I aren’t stupid!” she began. Titu smiled; obviously relieved that Isiy had the sense not to pay for flights back and forth to Egypt with his money. “I purchased a small home right here in America. I know how hard it is for you to fly in and out of Egypt all the time when half of your wrestling shows are over here in America!” she said. “Excellent! Well done, my love.” Pharaoh kissed Isiy again passionately and then pulled back with a deep sigh. “I doubt I’ll be back in PCW anytime soon, anyway. The after-effects of the concussion have still to leave me…” he groaned. “Titu, these migraines will leave you, so don’t worry! And of course you’ll be back in PCW soon! After all, you did make the new Roster after everyone was fired at Spring Fling!” said Isiy. Pharaoh smiled, but a spell of doubt was still in his mind. “Well perhaps you’re right…” Pharaoh looked up cautiously towards his room’s door before looking back into Isiy’s brown eyes, a deep expression of discontent was evidently about him. “Isiy, I need out of here,” he began, his voice no more than a whisper. “I can’t stand this place any longer! Week upon endless weeks have passed and still I am to be kept here in this dismal Hospital, now with the news of this new home, I wish to go there immediately.” Isiy stared at Titu doubtfully. “But you’re still going through the repercussions of your fall, I don’t think it would be wise-” Pharaoh frowned, a little angry, and broke his wife off in mid sentence. “Isiy, I mean what I say! I need to get out of here at once! This place is depressing me… You are the only beckon of light my heart has felt for many painstaking days!” Isiy looked out of the room’s door in deep, deep thought…at length she turned back to Titu and smiled, a little worried. “Ok,” She paused “But we need a plan…”
