Visiting A Homie

23 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 23.11.02 The scene opens up in the back of a black 4x4, driving through Harlem. The sun is shining brightly, but the wind is strong on this cold November day. In the front of the jeep, is Jay, a great friend of Duzza’s and a talented driver. In the back, diagonally opposite to Jay is Duzza. Duzza is dressed much smarter and classier than usual. Today Duzza is dressed in a pair of baggy black pants, a black T-shirt and a black undone shirt over the top. He has tanned Timberland boots. He also has on a black leather jacket. His hair is set back in long cornrows. He has little jewellery on for a change. Today he only has on his silver Rolex and two earrings. In his hand he holds a pair of Ray-Ban sunglasses and a cell phone. Duzza has the window all the way down and is starring out of it, looking around his old neighbourhood, seeing children playing in the streets, reminiscing about his own childhood. The car comes to a slow stop, at the uptown Harlem cemetery. Duzza slowly gets out and walks around to the drivers window Duzza’s face is bruised and when walking, he walks with a visible limp. This is from the beating he took and gave, in the black on black violence main event on fusion… Duzza:“ Yo, I’ma be ‘bout 20 minutes max a’ight?” Jay:“ Take all tha time you need man, take all tha time you need” Duzza turns from the car and slowly walks through the cemetery gates, he looks over and sees the lowering of a coffin, and a group of sad, some crying people, he puts on his sunglasses and continues to walk through the grave yard, eventually he comes to the gravestone he was looking for. The gravestone reads ‘James J. Jones Lays Here…Gone But Not Forgotten…..1979 – 2002’. Duzza looks down at the grave for a short moment, then takes of his sunglasses Duzza:“ Yo Jimmy, I miss ya dawg, I miss you so much man, I got a lot of things on my chest y’know and back in the day, you was the guy who would help me through my troubles, but now you gone man, you been gone for almost a year man, and still the pain of losin’ you ain’t gone man, I wish you was here man Man… PCW is getting me down dawg. It’s all so twisted. Playaz Inc is falling apart at the seems dawg, for real. We almost killed each other in our match the other day man. No lie. And at the end of the show, I was laying on the mat, beaten and bloody and I looked into the crowd and it hit me we was killing each other… and it was exactly what PCW wanted us to do. They don’t want Playaz Inc winning Alliances… they don’t want four strong black men at the top of their company… hell no… they turned us on each other…and it worked…damn them cracka’s. Yo I was jus’ in the hood, thought I’d stop by, holler at you for a minute, poor out a bit of liquor or somethin’. Yo n*gga we been through a whole lot of shit together, y’know from running these streets to bein’ down for what ever and now that your gone I got a whole lot of shit to tell you things I should have said way back when we was younger Do you remember way back when we was younger, when we used to roll hand in hand? And now I’m trippin’ on how I really miss ya man. Remember when, you and me would say we’d get up out this hood and everything will be ok? Well, its all good now, my n*gga we out the hood now, you know we had the same ideas, but now the same careers, we would share the same ol’ laughs but diff’rent tears. You was my homie, my dawg, my roadie, my n*gga, you would let no bitch get infront of me, man I swear to God I love you for that shit, why’d you have to get hit? Where was I? What time was it? You was supposed to get older wit’ me, I imagined us bein’ on stage, hands on shoulder wit’ me, or coppin’ them Range Rovers wit’ me, sittin’ on thangs and smokin’ tree’s. And if I could I would turn back the hands of time again and take your place, you see I’m always sittin’ here sippin’ on this hennessy, thinkin’ ‘bout what you meant to me my n*gga ‘Cos even like R. Kelly says in that song, even though your gone you will always be my n*gga and I guess you’ve made it home, but I’m still missin’ you my n*gga, I thinkin’ ‘bout the times when you would say ‘wants wrong my n*gga?’ ‘cos you know, sometimes my nights can get long my n*gga, and sometimes I feel God did me wrong man, so if I have to tell you, that even though your gone you will always by my n*gga, always man And your little son looks at me like ‘wheres my daddy’ but you thirteen year old daughters mad ‘cos she understand’s what’s happened man, I promised your momma I’d take care of your family, but she’s so hurt she turns away my helping hand Damn I wish you ass was here, Jimmy, so we could smoke and chill once again, I remember we would talk about you getting out of the drug game but you would tell me how it keeps callin’ your name. Man you was never afraid to die man, but sometimes we would cry, while axin’ the Lord why there tearin’ down the projects. Man we was homies for like 15 thug years, y’know we’d sit in Church and cry the same thug tears, and it’s so easy for folks to say ‘Duzz jus’ live on’ but I’m dying every second that your gone, nevertheless I try my best to stay strong. Man you remember how back in the hood, whenever there was fight you would take cover for me? I miss you man, I need you, it’s hard knowin’ you ain’t here and now all I have left are these ghetto memories, man Yo dawg, I can’t explain how I miss you, we’d stay together coppin’ cane and poppin’ pistols, and I remember how we’d put tha doo-rag over you beanhead, you was the closest n*gga I had, look how I’m doin now, you should be wit’ me, enjoyin’ my wealth man. Man I jus’ wish we could trade places, y’know fuck givin’ you ice, I’d rather give you life and things you had, I’d give you twice Like what’s the deal my n*gga? I know your up there, holding it down and if you could see me, you’d be sayin’ I’m talkin’ soft right now, I know it’s fucked up I gotta talk like this, but it’s hard for me to say when I’ma see you again. But you died for the love of the doe, love of the block, you remember we was 16 and running the block? And man you momma would hate it when we’d hang out wit’ them thug n*gga’s, but I’ma keep pouring this liqour and that’s my word man. You know, I always roll through this hood, stop by jus’ to say ‘what’s up’, and even though you passed goin’ on one long year, I’m still wakin’ up late at nights cryin’ tears dawg, jus’ thinkin’ about those days you used to talk to me, smiling while I’m sippin’ on this hennessy You remember how we’d brag on how rich we would be? And to make it out this hood was like a fantasy? Well now I hear what people are sayin’ ‘bout me man and I just can’t belive what my ears are tellin’ me, every one thinks they know me, but they don’t know me, no one in this business knows me, man I would give it all up jus’ to take one more ride wit’ you man I remember how I used to kick it on the front porch wit’ you, and how we used to just lay back and smoke weed wit’ you, and all them little basement joints we’d do, man I’m jus’ missin’ you. How I wish that I could like, hold you now, just talk to you again, to see your face, that’s what I wish for man, jus’ like R. Kelly ol’ song, that’s how I feel, but I know your in a better place, even though I can’t see your face, I know your smilin’ down on me, sayin’ every things ok. And when I make it out this thug life, I’ll see you again some day, I’ll see you again. You know we’d dream of getting rich, but ever since this money come, it’s been nothing but stress, sometimes I wish I could just trade in my success, all of them look at me and they like ‘ boy you been blessed’ but they don’t see the inside of my unhappiness man, no one does any more. Man I swear this shit gets heavy like a ton, that’s why I’m always hollerin’ of like a gun man, hmm I wonder how my friends would treat me now, if I wasn’t iced up wit’ a Bentley and a house, you see that’s why fake ass punks get fake ass digits and fake as players get a real player hating them, everytime I’m on the TV they all want to come around and celebrate but ain’t nobody wantin’ to raise the roof unless my shit’s goin’ down man Do you remember how I used to hoop of in them tournaments? Or how we used to club hop on weekends? Man your family called the morning of your tragic end and I felt like some one has just taken a shot at me as well. I jus’ wish I could see you now man, talk to you again But I know one day, I will see you again, one day, some how I just now, we’ll be together again and If Heavens’ got a ghetto, that’s where we’ll be one day. Jimmy, voices in my head be tellin’ me to come to Church, they say the Lord is the only way to stop the hurt, but I jus’ keep dream of blacked out windows like a hearse and y’know sometimes wakin’ up to real life, well it just seem worse man You know all I ever really wanted was to be a better man, and I still try to keep it real wit’ my homies man, they want me to save the world but I don’t understand, how did I become the leader or so many fans? I miss how we used to street perform on Friday, but you see now I go to Church on Easter Sunday, but still all they do is throw stones at me man, I need somebody to jus’ prey for me Jimmy I need answers man, I worked hard my entire life, harder than any one else in PCW or anything else, I came from the gutter y’know man, you was there wit’ me, but I made a success of myself, I’m livin’ the life we always would talk about man, but it jus’ seems kinda empty wit’ out you man, and it life don’t seem worth livin’ any more I know that no matter how hard I work in PCW, I’ll never get the respect which I deserve, you know it Jimmy, I know it, every one knows it, I could go out there tomorrow, win every belt under the sun, still I would be wit’ out respect. My album could go platinum another seven times and still you know everything would be the same. I created the entire market for thug wrestlers and I don’t get a inch of respect. The only people who respect me are my people back in the ghetto, no one else cares and all the negativity it’s, it’s started makin’ me stop thinkin’ straight man, I wish I could get out, but I’m too far down now, but you know, I guess what I’ve done on this planet won’t mean shit to me, when I’m walkin’ through those pearly gates and see you, moms, pops, my bro, all my dawgs standing there, waiting for me, but Jimmy, I want out man, but first I need answers man, I need answers… I miss you man, I miss ya” Duzza slowly, looks up, sees he is all alone in the graveyard. He slowly puts back on his sunglasses and sadly and slowly starts to walk back to the jeep, he is half way there when the Reverend of the Church walks up to Duzza Reverend:“ Malek? Malek Williams? Is that really you?” Duzza looks at the old man, and smiles recognizing himDuzza:“ Yeah, that’s me” Rev:“ How are you doing child?” Duzza:“ Oh you know, been better, been worse I guess” Rev:“ You been visiting young James’ grave?” Duzza:“ Yes father” Rev:“ He was taken before his time, such a sad tragedy, but life goes on as it is said” Duzza:“ Yeah, well I just had to get some stuff of my chest, been missing him bad latley, jus’ hope he hears me” Rev:“ Oh he hears you child, he hears you, he’s watching you every time you step into the ring, just like your father is” Duzza:“ I like to think so” Rev:“ So I hear you’re a rich man now? Moved out of Harlem” Duzza:“ Well I gots some cash, yeah I’m out of Harlem though, me and Jimmy would always talk about getting up out of the ghetto, guess he made it out before me, but I’m out now” Rev:“ You should come back to this Church, listen to me preach once in a while, bring your wife” Duzza:“ Maybe I will, one day soon” Rev:“ Malek, child, I have to go, but it has been great seeing you again, keep your hear up and remember, never apologize for speaking your mind, because if you do, your apologizing for being real” Duzza:Ok Father, see you around” Duzza and the Reverend part ways and Duzza slowly walks back and gets into the back seat of the jeep Jay:“ You ok dawg?” Duzza:“ Yeah, yeah I’m fine man” Jay:“ Where you want to head now?” Duzza:“ Err I’ve got the Alliances match man… I gotta head for the arena… The scene fades to black as the jeep slowly pulls out of the Church grounds
