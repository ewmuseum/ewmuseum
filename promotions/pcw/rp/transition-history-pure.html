Date: not entered *Five weeks ago, while Skylar was in FBI detainment.* I started in Pureoresu Ultimate Ring Entertainment in April of 2002. PCW's Alexander Isawa Miyagawa had formulated the idea for a WrestlingTattler recognized puroresu federation, to be run in Japan and have it's shows televised by syndication in the United States. He got the funding together, got some of the premier Japanese talent (and otherwise) to participate, and made it happen. They named the main show Ikusa, Japanese for 'war.' It would be the main stage for everyone to wrestle and show what wrestlers could do with all the sports entertainment aspect stripped away, when it was just two or more wrestlers, fighting in honorable combat because the spirit of competition drove them to battle to see who was the better wrestler on that given night in that given match. I had been inspired to take up wrestling (though as a hobby) by seeing Japanese puroresu, so I was eager to get involved. I'd been in America wrestling, but this country has a problem with offering me outlets do wrestle the way I wanted to. There was always some stupid gimmick involved in the match, and there'd be an endorsement riding on it or something. In Japan, wrestlers wrestle because they want to. In America, wrestlers wrestle because they get paid for it and it looks good on their resume. Also, they've got major lack of social skills, are generally pricks, have Type A personalities mixed in with superiority complexes, and a whole bunch of other mental problems which really amounts to the fact that 90% of wrestlers today are not of sound or sane mind. Anyway, I went over, and because I was a a tall blind cruiserweight, they pushed me in the Junior Heavyweight Title tournament from the get-go. It was a fairly large tournament, I believe a field of 7 competitors was whittled down to 3. There were more wrestlers under 230 than over anyway, but since most of them were Japanese, it made sense. Aside from the genetics of sumo wrestlers in Japan, most of them just don't get too large. My first match was against Ebola III and... some guy whose name I can't remember. He was ultimately useless, some guy no one had heard of from... well, I don't remember where he was from, either. All I remember is that he was dead weight. He and Ebola had tried to double-team me, since I was a lanky guy who couldn't see. Apparently the fact that I'd been wrestling for about half a year without the faculties for sight, and they didn't think I'd gotten the hang of it. Odd... Anyway, I took on both wrestlers, Ebola being at least a threat as he is a third generation wrestler and had been trained in multiple styles, and in the end I cam eout victorious. It was a mini-tournament, only two matches to decide who would be wrestling in the finals, but there were three qualifying rounds. Aside from myself, another qualifier was former PCW wrestler (one of the only wrestlers to that point to win titles in PCW, though never successfully defend them) The HIT, who for his PURE tour changed his name slightly to the Horribly Insane Trapezoid. Yes, that actually happened. No, we never figured out any rhyme or reason for the name switch. Yes, even HIT himself would say the same thing. He has, I'm quoting his answer for a reason to the name. In PCW, he was the guy who would wrestle for odd reasons. No one was really quite sure why he suddenly decided that he would take up wrestling as a part-time job (he wasn't getting any of the usual appearances and things that came with wrestling, and thus it was a part-time job). All they knew is that he would show up and wrestle. He tended to bring cameras with him to talk about opponents while in class and stuff, odd. And now, class, I must leave to cut a promo. I'll leave you to take this extremely difficult test, and trust that you won't use your notes and your books, even though you have them with you. I'm naive like that. See you!' No one failed his tests; shame, he thinks it was because he was a good teacher. He was good at letting them cheat. He never admitted to it, though. He is convinced that he is infallible, and defeats in any sense of the word are flukes. He bases losses not on his own inadequacies, but on an adaptation of the quantum uncertainty principle. He won some in PCW, and he lost some. As I said before, he managed to win a few titles, but his credibility as a champion was hurt by the fact that he couldn't seem to successfully defend them. People got into calling the #1 Contender to a title the new champion as sooon as things were set clearly. Why did he leave PCW? A combination of two things. The first, an attempted feud with the Ivan Stanislav's Red Army. Ivan kept beating the crap out of him, but he never got a clue. At some point, I think HIT managed to actually beat Ivan with a double-rotation Shooting Star Press at an interfed pay-per-view dealing with a national pride match, or something. All I know is that though he finally got a win against Stanislav, the count was still some 30-odd 'victories' for Stanislav to 1 single win for HIT. Things were not even, or avenged at all. In PURE, he was essentially the same guy, same wrestler, save for a mask. He didn't go off into the math jargon as much, because, hey we were in Japan and they were all smarter than him on the subject. He didn't like getting owned in math debate. It was his 'thing.' So he restricted himself, and his vocabulary, to 'I'll kick your ass!' On second thought, that was much of what he said in PCW. Oh well. He wore a mask, because he felt it was a trendy thing to do. I suppose he had a point, masked wrestlers are taken a bit more seriously than in the States, where the question we're always asked is 'why don't you wrestle without the mask.' I don't know, why has your face not seen plastic surgery yet? I'd always worn a mask in wrestling. It was more a practical reason; I didn't want my face all over television that I couldn't walk down that street without people coming acting like they know me, and there would be crowds forming for autographs and pictures with a professional wrestler. In small, controlled and scheduled settings, I don't mind. But away from the ring, I just want to be able to live like a normal person. So many wrestlers forget that before they were wrestlers, they were normal people. I guess they could in a way be called sellouts, but then again thanks to the music culture of this country, as well as the urban culture, that word has been tossed around like party favors to the extent that it effectively means nothing. The other wrestler to advance to the final match was Kensuke Suzahara. He was most famous in Cyberspace Championship Wrestling (damned if that doesn't sound like an e-fed name), but had been in PCW before. Whereas HIT was just a white guy math wiz, Suzahara was a legitimate Japanese star. He wasn't Japanese when it suited him to be called so, he was Japanese. Even though it was common knowledge (and true) that they pretty much hated each other, Miyagawa knew that Suzahara being onboard for PURE would be a boon for ratings. The Japanese like him. He does things his own way, and they like it that a Japanese man could achieve success in America despite lingering booking practices that keep the majority from attaining a high card status. Suzahara was a bit of a pill. In smark terminology, he was a heel. Even if he was talented, you weren't supposed to like him. There was a little substance to that. He was as personable as Clyde in a Teletubbies convention. He had a policy of blatantly stealing people's moves. Not that they had invented them or anything, but he stole their names for it, uses for it, any mannerisms that went along with it... and he'd call out their names when doing it. You'll notice I'm going into much more detail about my PURE opponents. The records for PURE's past incarnations were lost, so unlike PCW where people could check for themselves, I must give more information in this case. Suzahara, HIT, and I met in a 3-way match to decide the first Junior Heavyweight Champion. Again, they felt that double-teaming me was the way to victory, even though it hadn't worked before. Though it momentarily worked for them, I overcame it and struck out at the both of them with the best I had to give. And I walked out with the title. I won, I held it over my head at the end of the night. My fellow wrestlers have always had a way of underestimating me, as though every win I've ever earned was a fluke, and their particular matches with me would be cakewalks. Human nature, I suppose. They want to feel as though they have an advantage over me. They want to feel superior. I won't lie and say I've never lost, but I've never lost without putting up a good fight. No one has had an easy victory over me. It was two weeks until I defended my title, as I was put into the Asian Championship match the next week. I had no intention of winning, I gave the win to a newcomer, Yuka Hamada. She was a former porn star in Japan. That combined with her undeniable womanhood, made the wrestlers look down upon her. I fault that because they had been in America, got to thinking like Americans do. Women are to be weaker than men. I think I was the only one who gave her a chance. Stepped back in the match I gave to her, and the next week when I gave her a title match for the Junior Heavyweight title. She put up a good fight. That's what happens when technical ability, skill is pushed in training over the size of one's muscles. I'd like to say that I won that match on my own, but I didn't. Brian Simon, an English guy who had the presence and charisma of tube socks, got involved and attacked Hamada because she wasn't attracted to him. Simon apparently hit on her and she rejected him. As for myself, The HIT and Charlie Loc interfered and attacked me. That was a sham. I came to PURE expecting things like that not to happen. Of course, one of the featured feuds was Jordan X Shabazz against Ali Khadafi. Shabazz wasn't so bad, but Khadafi was too vulgar for PURE to have even considered hiring. How he got on the roster will forever be a mystery (though I believe he worked for peanuts). The next week, the match was made. Myself, Shabazz, and Brian Simon against Takumi Umeshita, The HIT, and Charlie Loc. Whomever pinned me would be the Junior Heavyweight Champion. Despite Shabazz going off to fight Khadafi, and Simon being the worse wrestler PURE has ever seen (well, I assume, not that he did anything), I did well. However, Loc and HIT collaborated to get the belt off of myself and onto The HIT, and their teamwork managed to work in all the distractions and useless partners, they won for HIT. He got the Junior Heavyweight Title. The next week, there was a rematch scheduled... of sorts. Myself, Inphino Blitz, Charlie Loc, and The HIT in a match for the title. It was untelevised, but I won that one and regained the title. From here, the story goes back to PCW, so I'll shift there again. *Next Session: PCW, Part 2*
