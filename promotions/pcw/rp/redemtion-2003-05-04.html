Redemtion

4 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5-4-03 “Forgive me father… We find ourselves accompanying our resident Adonis, Dan “Big O” Osbourne at confession. “…for I am going to sin.” “You’re planning on committing a sin?” “Yes, Father. I’m going to beat a man of the cloth to within an inch of his life.” “You must be talking about Shane Robertson.” “Yes, Father.” “What has he done now?” “Honestly, Father, it’s not his fault. I am scheduled to meet him Tuesday at Turmoil.” “I see. You have feelings of guilt for what you must do to him?” “I do, Father. He seems to be a good man. I see no real reason why we should be in conflict, but it’s my job.” “And are you good at it?” “Without trying to sound vain, Father, yes I am. And more importantly, I am better than he is. I don’t want to hurt him, but it doesn’t seem that I’ve got much choice in the matter.” “So you’ve come here in hopes of getting my approval?” “Of sorts.” “I’m sorry, My son, but it is not my approval you should seek.” “You mean…?” “Yes, My son. You need to seek the approval of the Holy Father.” “So you’re telling me to go home and pray for guidance?” “Well that wouldn’t make for a very exciting RP now would it?” “Good point. So what do you suggest I do?” “Go to Hell.” “Father!” “No, I mean it. Really, go to Hell and smack the Angel of Light upside the head. That should put you in good favor with the big guy, then you can beat the stuffing out of Shane Robertson with no problem.” “Can you tell me how to get there?” “Well, if you’ve got time to spare, raping nuns is a sure thing, but you’re in a hurry. Hmmm…” “Do I have to do bad things to get in? There has to be another way.” “Oh. You don’t want to be forever condemned to the burning flames and unending torture and pain that is eternal damnation.” “Yeah, that’s bad.” “Okay, in that case, there’s an entryway in Hoboken that will lead you right there. Here’s the address.” “Thank you, Father.” “You’re welcome, My son. And be sure to take $.50 with you. And a dead pig.” “Wish me luck.” “Good luck. And don’t forget the pig.” (You know he’s going to forget the pig.) Dan hops in his private jet and heads to New Jersey. (Bet you thought I didn’t know where it it, didn’t you? Well, nyeh!) He deplanes and gets into the back of a waiting limo. He pours a glass of champagne and picks up the phone. “Heya, Molly-girl.” “Hoboken.” “It’s a long story.” “I have to go to Hell.” “Yeah. Hell.” “I have to get in the good graces of God.” “So I can beat up Shane Robertson with a clear conscious.” “That’s what the priest told me.” “Well it was either this or I had to rape nuns.” “That was a movie. They weren’t real nuns.” “Uh huh.” “Uh huh.” “Yeah.” “Uh huh.” “Okay.” “I will. I promise.” “I love you too.” “Everything okay back there, Mr Osbourne?” “Yeah, Jimmy. Thanks.” “So where are we headed, Sir?” “Here’s the address.” “I know where that is. We’ll be there in about twenty minutes.” “Okay, Jimmy. Thank you.” Twenty minutes later… “Here we are, Sir. Have a good time.” “Thank you, Jimmy.” Dan walks away from the limo and into the front door of Bea, Easel, and Bub’s Bakery. Dan looks around for some unholy sign of entry but there is none. Before him, Dan sees nothing more than a neighborhood Mom and Pop type business. The proprietor of this particular establishment, a jolly older woman, welcomes her newest potential customer. “Welcome to my bakery, Mr. Osbourne. We’ve been expecting you.” “How do you know my name?” “Like I said, we’ve been expecting you. You seek passage into the darkness. Am I correct in this?” “I don’t know how you know this, but yes.” “Then step this way, my child.” The old woman leads Dan behind the counter and through the kitchen and into the basement. She leads him to an eerie black door, covered with runes, pentagrams and many horrific pictures. The Pictures depict a scene of torment and pain. “Is this the doorway to Hell?” “No, it’s the bathroom. Over here is the door to Hell.” She points in the direction of a plain wooden door. A painting of a man covers almost the entire surface. Dan, who is completely secure in his own sexuality, marvels at the stunning beauty of the winged figure. “Who is that?” “That, my child, is the Angel of Light. This is a picture of Lucifer.” “I thought he was red with horns and stuff.” “You watch too much TV. If you had ever read your scripture, you would know that Lucifer was the most beautiful of all the angels.” “I don’t understand.” The old woman explains the history of Lucifer’s failed attempt to overthrow God and his exile from the Kingdom of Heaven. “So he’s still just as beautiful as he always was?” “That is correct. In fact, that is his true power. His beauty is inviting and seductive. The horned, red beast is nothing more than an image created to scare children.” “So I can just go in?” “Yes, you may go in. But be careful. You are going to see things that no mortal was ever intended to witness. Go forth, my child.” With that, Dan opens the door and walks into the painfully bright light held within. Fade to Black
