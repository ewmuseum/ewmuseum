Time For A Review

28 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 28/Feb/2002 'Oh my God. When I said I'd be a reviewer, I didn't think I'd have this much to do. Oh well. Best crack on. Get it out of the way. Let's see what we've got shall we?' The screen fades in on D-Kay Calyx walking towards his living room with a plastic box in his right hand. He is dressed in his black jeans and Red Dwarf t-shirt, and has a sweatband around his forehead. His skin seems to have been leaking, so we assume he has just come out of either a gym, or the training room he has in his house in Chicago, Illinois. We follow Calyx as he walks into the room, and sits down in the black armchair, pulling a wooden table in front of him, and placing the box on that. He quickly flicks through the contents of the box, shown to be cardboard mailers for vinyl's. After flicking through them, he grabs the box again, and leaves the living room. We join him a few seconds later as he enters the room we saw him in yesterday, the one full of electronic and audio equipment. Calyx walks over to the far corner, and turns on a lamp that is attached to the wall, before hitting the power on a plug socket, sending blue and red lights into overdrive on the equipment in front of him. Spending a bit of time to set everything up, D-Kay turns on one of a pair of Technics SL1210's, a Gemini PS600 mixer, a Kenwood amplifier, and his Samsung PC. A minute or so later, he reaches for the first mail out and empties the contents. Ignoring the reaction sheet for now, he takes the plain black vinyl sleeve, and withdraws the 12', placing it on the deck, and hitting start with the needle at the beginning of the track. D-Kay stands back a few feet as a complex melody hits the speakers in the room, filling our ears with the sounds of the strings and digital bass. D-Kay listens attentively through the track, our ears getting accustomed to the dark break beat of the drum and bass track. Calyx then lifts up the reaction sheet after it has finished, and reads to himself. 'Powerpoint by Digital and Dylan. 'A dark and moody affair that has been setting the dance floors alight as of late. A forthcoming release on one of the UK's biggest labels, Renegade Hardware, this stormer has been played on dub plate by the likes of Andy C, Grooverider, and Dylan himself. A must have for fans of dark and rolling drum and bass.'' Calyx shakes his head and frowns, before placing that and the vinyl back in the sleeve, and putting it to one side. Calyx then quickly turns to the computer, and reads aloud as he types in his reaction. 'Powerpoint by Digital and Dylan. Another top class roller from Renegade Hardware, settling in with a dark intro and furious breakbeat to work the track upwards.' D-Kay looks at the screen, and stops writing, but continues with his analogy. 'Reminding me remarkably of one of my March Madness opponents, Afwa Kilmanjaro. Has a dark feel to it and works upward with a fury. Too bad the end of the tune fizzles out with no real distinction.' Calyx smiles to himself as he reaches for the second track in the pack, and reads the response sheet first. ''Shy FX with Shake Your Body, the Future Cut remix. This is one track that you are guaranteed to know. Played everywhere and anywhere, this track has been licensed to Positiva for a UK chart release, and has been tearing through crowds for the past year on dub plate, and promo from it's original label, Ebony. Top tune.'' Again, D-Kay shakes his head, and reaches for the vinyl. The intro kicks in with a piano that sounds remarkably like the theme tune to Sex And The City, before a high breakbeat kicks in, with jazzy vocals, and a jumping bassline. We listen to about half of it before D-Kay hits stop and takes it off of the deck. We again hear him read out what he is typing. 'The Future Cut remix of Shy FX's Shake Your Body. Everyone knows this, and that's why it fails. It has bits of dark drum and bass, a touch of the Latin d&b sound; some jump up work, and jazz style vocals. The Future Cut mix at least takes the bassline to a new level, but leaves too much of the original in.' For the second time, D-Kay stops typing as he carries on talking. 'Reminding me of THE Multiple Man. Has many different faces and styles, but doesn't excel at all in any of them. Trying to play off of success and indifference, where it should be concentrating on developing and expanding.' D-Kay laughs to himself as he grabs the third vinyl. 'Who's this going to be? 'Colorado by Dillinja. Another top Dillinja track on Valve from the UK. Typical Dillinja breaks and beats, with a classy bassline, and soulful vocals. Goes down well with the intelligent crowds.'' D-Kay plays the track, and we listen to a metallic breakbeat, a funky sub-bassline, and a piano with vocals sounding like TLC. D-Kay again writes down his thoughts on the computer. 'Dillinja's Colorado. Typical Dillinja. That is the only good point, but one of many bad points. Other reviews hype it up to be one the ladies will like, but reality is far from it. Everything in this track is worked well from a production point, but it fails to deliver what is expected. One for the fillers, but not something you'd like to hear too much.' And here comes the after thought... 'That's the Big Daveylicious of the group so far. As all the hype, but doesn't perform well. Next?' Calyx again reaches for the next tune from the box, and places the vinyl on the deck, reading the review sheet before hitting start. 'One from the US know as DJ Baron makes his debut on the Higher Education imprint, with two tracks that are rocking like a horse. On the 'A' side is Impact, while the 'B' side hosts the storming Pity The Weak. Impact is what you'd expect from Higher Education, a ground floor filler, with enough bass to build a house out of, whereas Pity The Weak uses the samples from the Mortal Kombat films to great effect on this dark roller.' D-Kay shakes his head as he listens to the track; both of them sounding the same, with rumbling basslines over a brisk Amen cut beat. The only difference is the Shang Tsung sample on the 'B' side. D-Kay immediately turns off the track, and throws it back in the box. 'What can I say about DJ Baron's new release? Impact and Pity The Weak. It explains it all. Impact lacks it, and Pity The Weak is searching for that response. The only good thing to come out of this track is the Mortal Kombat samples, and even then, they are cut up to high hell.' D-Kay stops typing and continues. 'Hmmm. Impact for Impact. The least of my worries. Filled with fake hype, and going nowhere fast. Asks for pity in a roundabout way and certainly lacks its namesake. Both side's of the coin failing to live up to any expectations anyone had, and is something not to get excited about. In fact, it's not even worth my review.' D-Kay deletes the last paragraph on his computer, and puts the DJ Baron track to one side, on top of a Southern Comfort ashtray. He then takes the final package out of the box, and empties the contents, taking out the vinyl and reading the comments sheet. 'Bad Company launch their new label Anno Domini, a sister label to BC recordings, with a single sided test press titled Control. Bad Company and The Usual Suspects have been playing this for quite a while, and it finally works its way onto the pressing plates for the rest of the massive. Typical BC production, the analogue stabs and dark bassline that that have become trademark of this group are still there, but tweaked to an extent it almost doesn't sound like them. A must have for BC fans, and one for the rollers crew as well.' D-Kay again listens to the track, and actually smiles as the track finishes. He quickly leans over and enters his review. 'The best of the batch. A funky number from Bad Company on their newfound Anno Domini label, and Control it has. Deep basslines, analogue stabs, and a little 303 lines in for the Dogs on Acid. Lacks a little bit in morals for the samples used, but effective. Only bad thing is the bassline is a little too low, and doesn't actually peak at any time. A set filler none the less.' D-Kay then continues his verbal review again, leaving the keyboard alone. 'The final one, and it is definitely a Walden. Shows so much promise, and has an excellent foundation to it, but stays down way too long without getting back up quick enough. A lack of morals in the samples don't quite make for good viewing, but the simple-ness is its best quality. One not to under estimate for the near future.' D-Kay sits back, and eventually deletes the comments on screen except for the last one. 'Damnit. I'm meant to be giving the good points about these. Too bad that four of them have a basis making it so hard to find good components within. At least one shows promise, and no matter what, I better not take it too lightly when it's aired this weekend. Oh well...For the weekend I can always get rid of them quickly, and see if the crowd notice. Better see if I can find some good points though. Damn these reviews.' D-Kay grabs hold of the first four tracks, and lays them out on the table in front of him, grabbing the one nearest to re-listen to first. He sighs as he hits start, enabling the string chords to come back into the equation. Deciding to leave him to it for now, the screen fades to black.
