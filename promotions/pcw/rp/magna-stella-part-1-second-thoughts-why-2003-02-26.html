Magna Stella - Part 1: Second Thoughts? Why?

26 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2 - 26 - 03 ‘I don’t think I understand’, Dorn declares with bits of irritation snaking their way through his calm, collecting demeanor. His crystal blue eyes stare deeply through the charcoal-colored veil of his shades, fixating themselves on the lovely young lady sitting only a mere three feet across from Dorn. The long surface separating the two bodily entities is a furnished oak table of high-priced quality, perhaps custom made. Very fitting for the room the two are conversing in. With two crystal chandeliers hovering above the inhabitants of the room, the nice cream-colored carpet is complimented along with the burgundy shelves containing various pieces of expensive china and crystal sculptures. The room appears to be an expensive living room of some sort, or at least a show room. The young lady sitting across from the Astronomer runs an idle hand through her straight, dark brown hair with a mask of nervousness adorned on her pretty face. The side of her lip curls into a smirk and rests inside a small dimple on her face, setting off the emotion of hesitation. He doesn’t believe me…, the thought runs through her head again and again like an ever-lasting broken record, but she’s starting to believe it. Getting her long time friend to sit down and understand his behavior is insane is a rather tedious, if not impossible endeavor to undertake. Even for a person as adept at debate as herself, it’s proving to be quite the strain. ‘Look, Dorn… you’ve known me for quite some time. Would I ever lie to you?’ Her voice rings with a passive, interrogative tone. Dorn can already tell she’s trying to block him into a corner, but luckily, he can smell it like a rotting corpse on a hot day. ‘I doubt it,” The Astronomer declares, his voice somewhat bloated with hesitation. Truly enough, he’s known Jessica for well over two years and she’s proved to be one of his best companions. ‘I just don’t understand what potential harm could come of this? I am now a champion, after all, and I have my Goddess and the stars above to thank for such things.’ His point spears Jessica like a heated steel point, but she remains steadfast against his stone defense. ‘I know you think highly of her, Dorn,’ Jessica chimes, trying to butter him up so he’ll see her rather obvious point. ‘But you’re becoming a fanatic. That’s dangerous, and I’m… well…’ The expression on her face melts away from her persuading puppy look into certain withdraw, as if she’s frightened at the very thoughts circulating through her head. Sure enough, she is. I can’t tell him, he wouldn’t understand… She thinks to herself, not really wanting to believe the obvious reality. Dorn interrupts her thoughts, however. His tone shows irritation, if not disbelieving irritation. ‘A fanatic?? Why on Earth would you think that? She gives me intensity, and she gives me purpose! I was lost without her!’ The Astronomer’s face has already slowly begun to mold into anger, with his eyebrows drawing forth toward the bridge of his nose. Noticing his look of agitation, Jessica decides to pick and choose her words carefully as to not anger her friend more. ‘You weren’t lost, Dorn. As a matter of fact, you did just fine. Look, I’m not trying to get you to renounce your faith, I’m just trying to tell you that what you’re doing might be wrong,’ The tone of pleading begins to creep up in her throat as the thoughts of the consequences of Dorn’s actions worry her. ‘I just think you should-‘ ‘Just stop right there!’ Dorn’s voice suddenly booms with a sudden saturation of fury. He bolts up from the fine oak chair and slams his meaty fists into the grain of polished wood, which manages to withstand his attack. Jessica lurches in surprise and feels her heart leap into her cranium. What’s happened to him?? She ponders, fright coursing through the canals in her brain. The Astronomer pushes his chair back and begins to pace away from the table, heightening his voice as to stay within the conversation. ‘You don’t know what you’re talking about, Jessica! I respect your opinion but you don’t realize what great things En’Flarim is bringing me! I’ve got power, I’ve got a wonderful title, I’ve got-‘ Dorn tries to say, but alas, it is not to be. ‘DORN! Please, just listen to me!’ Jessica’s cry for understanding completely cuts the Astronomer off, getting a furious look from the Ace of Astrology. However, upon seeing the watery, saddened look in her beautiful hazel eyes, his expression softens and he stops dead in his fleeing tracks, willing to listen. Jessica sees this, and decides not to push her luck. ‘Just think about what could happen! You’ll get hurt! I’ve watched you and you’re turning into some sort of… of…’ She searches for the correct word, unable to really bring herself to describe it. Not wanting to waste time, she gives the obvious answer, ‘…Monster!’ Cut deeply by the words of his trusted friend, the Astronomer finally begins to contemplate what she could mean. True enough, he has doubted some of his actions. He felt no remorse, only sadistic pleasure, as he watched En’Flarim torch his newly won prize. In reality, no damage was done, but it was the principal of the fact that fire was consuming his hard-earned prize. Why? She never did say, The thought wanders through Dorn’s mind like a lost fish, trying to find its place. But something deep in his consciousness is telling him this is all foolhardy. What could be right? Jessica notices the look on her troubled pal’s face and decides to press on, in a more delicate manner. Dorn means a lot to her, and the last thing she wants is to mentally rip him to shreds. ‘I understand you’re confused, and even I am. I don’t know how you got into such a thing, but you need to get out. And I can help you.’ Her promising, comforting words send a ripple of warmth through Dorn’s heart, but alas, something still pulls at him… ‘I just don’t know, Jessica…’ His voice trails off into nothingness, as if controlled by the unsure feelings in his mind. ‘I just… I’m confused… She’s brought so many things to my once empty table.’ His words shock even himself. Is his greed taking over? He definitely wants the glory and power that comes with being a champion and fighting machine, but is it going to cost him his health? His respect? Or even his life? Is that what she’s trying to tell me? The distraught look on the Astronomer’s face is enough to explain what’s running through his troubled mind, and Jessica understands perfectly. She rises slowly from her chair, wearing a small smile on her clean, smooth face, and makes her way over to Dorn with slow, calculating strides. Her socked feet make no noise against the carpet, making her seem like a phantom as she approaches Dorn. Upon finally reaching his rigid, troubled figure, she places a hand softly on his shoulder and look into the white corner of his beautiful blue eye. ‘I can help you, Dorn. You just have to trust me…’ But how can I be sure? Dorn thinks to himself, torn between his friend and his deity. Seems like a simple decision, and he’s beginning to wonder why it’s even a struggle to decide. What’s this doubt running through his mind? She hasn’t steered him wrong yet, so why would he sudden turn on En’Flarim? There’s no reason to. What’s Jessica getting at? I wonder what her motive is. ‘I trust you, but why do I need help?’ His voice is interrogative, as if he’s challenging her to actually come up with a reason. Sure enough, Jessica is taken back by the sudden 180 of her friend and desperately searches through the crevices of his cerebrum to come up with an answer. ‘I just think you need it, that’s all…’ Her voice trembles, but only slightly. However, it’s enough for the ever-alert Astronomer to catch. He can’t understand what she’s getting at, but decides he owes it to her to continue with this. ‘As I said, I trust you, Jessica. But I’ll tell you right now,’ His voice suddenly becomes firm, trying to pressure his point in. ‘I won’t turn against the other half of my very being just because of your superficial concerns! But, if you think I need some help, then by all means, help me.’ His challenging tone unnerves Jessica, but she realizes she must resist his stubbornness. He just doesn’t see the potential danger. She has to break him away from this fanatical life style he’s turned himself to… it’s not the lust for the title he wears around his waist. It’s not the fact he wants to bathe in glory. What drives Dorn? What causes him to practically become a martyr for this strange being known as En’Flarim? Whatever it is, if I don’t help him… it may cost him his life. [End.]
