Jigen, the third.

undated

Date: not entered Cyclonica and I watched as the large red ferry pulled up against the dock, the motors dying down to a purr before us. The ramp was extended from the deck of the ferry, and out walked a small, old man with graying beard wearing a black fishing cap. He looked at us as if we didn’t belong here… and in a way, she didn’t really belong, nor me, together nor apart. We were almost anomalies in this world, a rare breed that people choose not to acknowledge rather than take the time to understand just how and why they exist. But Cyclonica had taken mind to push me into figuring out who I just so happen to be, and I had a feeling that she wouldn’t just give up so easily after one botched attempt. “My, my,” he said, stretching his arms out over his head slowly. His voice was tired, old, yet still had a bit of friendliness in him. “I didn’t expect to see any passengers out this late at night.”I bowed to the elder. “My apologies, but this is an urgent matter.”The ferryman waved his hand at me, a smile cracking across his weathered face. “No trouble at all! It’s my job of course. Of course… you have to have tickets to board.”That was Cyclonica’s department. I looked at her, finding her staring off at the sea. With a nudge I brought her attention back. “Huh?” she said casually. Obviously she hadn’t been paying attention. “You said you’d take care of boarding, did you not?”“Ohhh… that.” Me remembering her little promise didn’t seem to sit well with her. She looked down at the old ferryman, a look of disapproval on her face as he held his hand out to her, either for ticket or fee. Then her eyes went to the ship, and opened wide. “Your BOAT!”As soon as the old man turned around she threw her arm back to strike him… but it didn’t get anywhere, my hand grabbing her wrist before she could assault the ferryman. She glared at me with a cock of her head to the side. “What… the hell… are you doing?” she said softly. “I could ask the same.”She snatched her arm back and shrugged. “Well then, guess I won’t be handling the boarding then, now will I?” spite had laced that last part quite well. I knew the importance of getting on this ferry, but not by her means, no. “That’s funny…” the old man turned back around scratching his head underneath his cap. “I don’t see anything wrong with the ol’ girl. You sure you ain’t seeing things, miss?”“My friend’s just weary from the journey here,” I covered up quickly, putting a smile on my face. “To be honest, we didn’t bring any money on us, sorry.”He nodded understandingly. “I hear you.” Then he motioned towards the ramp with a smile. “Go right ahead, on the house. Consider it the midnight special.”“Many thanks indeed,” another respectful bow was given, and as I looked up I watched Cyclonica walk up the ramp silently, arms hidden underneath her shawl. I rushed up behind her, looking back at the ferryman to see him putting a cigarette into his mouth. As soon as I caught up with her, I broke into a slower walk at her side. “I see your father didn’t teach you anything about manners, oracle.”She glanced at me slightly. “On the contrary. He taught me many things, and one of them was how not to be a kiss-ass.” She put a cigarette of her own into her mouth daintily. “What makes you believe so?” that was the first time anyone ever called me that… to my recollection anyways. “Now where did I put that lighter?” the ferryman muttered to himself as he patted the pockets of his blue windbreaker jacket. Cyclonica snapped a red lighter from underneath her shawl and lit the cigarette in her mouth. I watched as she tossed the lighter over into the sea, exhaling a cloud of smoke into the air. “All that bowing, and the pleases and thank-yous was just sickening.”I watched the lighter go into the waters, “Not my fault your father didn’t teach you anything about respect.”“Oh, and would you like to teach me that lesson, monk?” she whirled around, pointing a finger towards my cheek with the hand holding the cigarette. “Don’t forget… your in no condition to do anything, not if you couldn’t stop me from giving you that scratch or taking that old prune’s lighter. I’d be surprised if you even advanced in that tournament you’re in now.”I stopped, staring her down as she stood her ground. “… I’ll advance…”I moved past her to get onto the deck of the ferry. She turned back and smirked, following behind me. “If you do, I’d be surprised if you won… unfocused mother fucker…” she tried to keep that last part quiet, but I still heard it. Cyclonica turned for the cabin as soon as she boarded the ship, the ferryman following on board not too far after us. The ramp was cranked back onto the ship mechanically, the old man humming a tune to himself. As soon as the ramp cranked back into the ship he came walking towards me, that same smile still on his face. “If you feel the need, there’s some cots inside,” he thumbed over his shoulder to where Cyclonica had entered. “It’ll be a couple hours ‘till we hit the mainland. About two and a half, to be precise.”I nodded, and the old man patted me on the shoulder as he passed. “Enjoy your ride, youngster.”My brow wrinkled as I watched him walk past into the cockpit of the ship. There was something about the old man that brought a hollow feeling inside of me, something I couldn’t recognize. “That’s your problem.”I whirled around to find Cyclonica standing in the doorway of the cabin, smoking her pilfered cigarette. Her eyes were focused up at the stars as she plucked the cig from her lips and blew smoke out. “You think to much.” She took one last drag and flicked the remains out into the sea. “Everything isn’t significant. Some things are just there just to be there… monk.”She turned and walked into the cabin, leaving me only glaring at her from behind. This whole night was starting to get aggravating, especially with this girl trying to teach me philosophy. I had a match to prepare for, and I needed to meditate. My body was in order; it was the mind that needed training now. I knew my body would be able to take whatever Skitzo had to offer, whatever he had to throw at me…his best… his worst… everything he has, I’ll take and give back tenfold. That… was a fact.The boat jerked into life, and our journey had begun. -=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=- Those heels clicked across the floor, all the way to the end of the cabin. Then back they came, down to the other end of the cabin. This went on for as long as I’ve been sitting here, it was almost disturbing to say the least, this one single sound piercing through the peaceful silence. I opened one eye slowly, following Cyclonica as she paced down to the right side of the cabin. Her shadow trailed behind her in that fluorescent lit room, the windows black with the night awaiting outside, reflecting the Divine Prophetess’s trail. She didn’t look too pleased to be sitting around here doing nothing. “Something wrong, oracle?” I said, a hint of amusement in my voice. I didn’t intend for it, but seeing this devious woman actually bothered like this was a bit humorous… like holding her over the pier by her ponytail. “Shut the fuck up,” was her muttered response, her hands folded across her chest. I opened the left eye and looked at her entirely, trying to read her expression. “Don’t tell me the almighty Divine Prophetess, the daughter of the Neo Void God himself, is claustrophobic.” She cut her eyes at me for that response, “Why would I stay in here with your boring ass if I was claustrophobic when there’s a whole wide open deck just outside that door? You –do- have a brain… I suggest you try using it for once in a while.”I shrugged, closing my eyes and listening to her pace continue. “Something’s the matter with you… I find talking about things helps.”I heard her stop in front of me. “Oh? Well then, let’s talk about all the messed up shit you’ve done in –your- life, shall we? I’m real familiar with all the good deeds you’ve done back when you lived in Tokyo, monk. My father told me… if only the police knew of the things you’ve done in your life… the people wouldn’t think you so high, now would they?”“…” I answered her with silence. Cyclonica was pleased with the outcome; I could almost hear her smile spread slowly across her face. “Touched a nerve, did I?” indeed, the amusement was thick in her voice. “Good… you need to be shook. If I didn’t shake you up, Skitzo definitely would’ve shook you in the ring. Then we’d all be back at square one again, right?”When I didn’t respond she chuckled, a tinkle of a laugh that nearly didn’t seem possible for a human being to produce. “I’d love to pluck your strings a bit more, but if you’ll excuse me.”Her footsteps headed for the deck, the door closing behind her. There was only one thing on my mind as soon as she left…. “Finally, silence.” There was only so much silence on the ferry, with the sloshing of the waters outside and the engine humming over everything, but still it was better than hearing that girl’s nonsense. My breathing grew deep, my body at ease, and began to practice the meditation exercises I was taught so long ago. I breathed out… took back in air… allowed it to flow through my body like the blood that also gives life… exhaled again… This went on for a few minutes. Then something changed; something that pricked the hairs up on the back of my neck. As I breathed out… the breath blew back in my face… I stopped for a minute, seeing if it was just a gust breezing past me, and as I did the air so did. And so I tried again… and it happened again, blowing at the same pace and force as before. My eyes fluttered, wanting to snap open to search for a culprit, but I kept them closed. If I opened them now, I’d lose focus and have to start all over again. I relaxed and started the breathing exercise again, inhaling and exhaling… and the breath also exhaled back in my face. Yet, as I took in air, the breath blew hard into my face, like someone trying to whistle and producing no sound. My eyes snapped open immediately… and found nothing… Standing up, I looked around the cabin. Nothing but empty cots lined against the wall, a closet with blankets locked up for sleeping passengers, tables and chairs… and nothing more. That whole event made me feel uneasy, quite possibly it was the night messing with me. Maybe I was just tired, and needed to rest more than I realized. Maybe I was worried… about Mina… about the tournament… about what I may allow Skitzo to do if I can’t keep myself focused…I leaned against the railing near the window, looking out with half-closed eyes. I could feel my body’s internal clock taking over; normally I would be in bed by now. Rest was all a part of the game, if you weren’t rested well enough for a match, you would be sluggish and weak. My eyes were dropping slowly as I watched the waters pass by, that monotonous humming of the engine a mechanical lullaby. I looked in the reflection of the window, just to glance behind me, and then looked at my own reflection. I felt a bit uneasy looking myself in the eye after the reflections I saw of myself earlier on the dock. But I managed to pull my eyes up into my own. The oracle’s talk about knowing who I am was strong; strong enough to mess with my mind, yet things were always complicated and tricky when it came to dealing with her and her father. My eyes drew downwards, noticing something moving behind me. Slowly… a small child stepped out from behind me, wearing a black hooded jacket and blue jeans and black shoes. The child’s black hair hung from within the hood hiding his face… but I had seen enough to know who he was… and knew enough to be scared… -=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=- I jumped startled, and everything came into focus. Cyclonica stared at me, leaning against one of the posts in the ferry. “Bad dream?”I looked up at her from my seated position against the wall, a blank look on my face. “Ever since you appeared oracle…”She rolled her eyes, looking out towards the window. I stood up from the ground, where I realize now I had been the entire time, and looked around. The clock on the wall read 2:43am… we were nearing the mainland in about a half hour or so. “You let me sleep…” I said flatly aloud. Cyclonica looked ahead at me, her brow arching as to say ‘So What’.“And?”“I didn’t need to sleep now.” I responded. “I needed to meditate.”“Oh well, monk.” She said coldly, giving me a dirty look. “If your name isn’t ‘Daddy’ then pardon me if I don’t give a shit.”It was my turn to roll the eyes. “No respect at all…”“And what of it? What has respect gotten anyone out of life besides a beating?” she pushed herself off the pillar she leaned against and stepped forward. “You live your life by your code, and what have you to show for it? Some crappy little beach house out in the middle of nowhere and a wrestling job where you’re treated like a lower midcard Jap on minimum wage. Your peers don’t respect you, and your bosses don’t respect you, that’s for DAMN sure! Otherwise you would’ve had a TON of opportunity in your life. But no, you keep quiet, you be humble, and you bow and apologize like a stereotypical chink with big teeth and squinty eyes.” She unfolded her arms, pointing a finger at my face with her arm stretched out entirely. “You start to respect yourself… and people will respect you. Fuck all that shit you did in the past, stop wallowing in it like a pig in its own shit. You think anyone else has respect for you? None at all… you show the least bit of honor and respect in that ring against Skitzo, and he’s going to pick you apart. You gotta realize this ain’t the feudal era… this is the 21st century. Honor and bushido are dead, and if you can’t change then so are you.”I stood there and stared at her for a moment, letting her words sink in. Then I shoved her arm away, walking towards the door. “You talk too much.”I could see her reflection in the window of the door, standing there with her hands going to her hips. “It’s either put up or shut up in this world, monk, and your silence makes you look weak. If you can’t do anything to stop me, stop Akiyama, or stop Skitzo from running all over you… then shut the fuck up.”I had enough. I opened the door and closed it on her, and on that conversation.Most people weren’t in this business for respect, only for fame, and wealth. I couldn’t even say that I was in it for respect either. I never knew why I came into wrestling… only that the path in life led me to it. In a way, I think I’m still trying to earn respect, despite everything I’ve done so far. It’s always been something striving inside me, a yearning that only could be satisfied by another admitting my talent. And in this tournament, the man who wins will be respected as the champion, and as the best in PCW. These other men in the tournament… they wanted that belt for their own reasons, but something inside me wanted it just so people would know what I’m capable of, and never to underestimate me. With this match coming… I’ll earn my respect… one way or another.
