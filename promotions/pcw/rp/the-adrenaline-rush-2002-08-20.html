The Adrenaline Rush

20 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8-20-02 It gets you going before a big game or a big event. It pushes you to the limit and then some. Sometimes it allows you to do amazing things that you believe that you would never do in your life. It’s the rush, the emotion, and the adrenaline that allows you to do that. It runs through your veins through the blood stream, giving you a surge that allows you to the impossible or it gives you the surge to allow to do something even better then you thought you would possibly ever do. It’s the moment the pushes your body to the maximum that you could possibly go and I live for that moment. When it’s either getting in a fight, playing a game or wrestling in the squared circle, one thing is for curtain and that is when I get pumped up the adrenaline rush begins to flow and it pushes me to be as successful as I could possibly be. When your facing an archrival in a sport related game, you want to be the living piss out of them; they start beating you and become cocky. The only thing you want to do at that time is shove it down their throats and prove them wrong. That’s where the adrenaline starts flowing. For me, it’s way before a match and when I played a sport it would be way before the event as well. I would just sit in my room playing rock music as loud as I could possibly go, cranking it to the top volume bar until my parents yelled at me and I would just listen to the beat, then think about the opponent. Reminding myself that I have to go big or go home and to give it my all to get the victory at the end of the day for my team or if it’s individual sport it would just be for myself then. I have always been a real intense person, someone that will go all out playing a game of horse just to prove to my friends that I’m still the better person. Michael Jordan was intense when he played and he did not like losing. Neither did I so I was happy to compare myself to a basketball legend like him. I would go all out when I played if it was a pick up game I would go hard to the rim and rebound every possible shot by out witting the man under the board for the other team. People would get pissed off because I was so intense but I wouldn’t care cause I wanted to win and that was the result of the adrenaline. Running through my bloodstream pushing my body to the limits and I loved it because I wanted to prove that I was the best, that no one could beat me. Sometimes my adrenaline rushes were used in the wrong places, for instance one time in a shoot fight before my wrestling career. I was fighting a Latin American decent man who was known as one of the wildest shoot fighters in the world. So I had to prepare myself, hype myself up and I did the only way I could and that was getting the blood flowing through the music. It pumped me up and I began to get a wild look in my eye, something that is not normal for me to do We battled it out in the dome for twenty minutes straight. Near the end of the twenty-first minute something inside of me went crazy and I snapped like Ken Shamrock would do. I grabbed the guy, wrapping my forearms around his forehead and throat area. Performing a modified sleeper into a choke. I very rarely snapped and I wouldn’t stop putting pressure on the man. It knocked him out cold, claiming me the winner and I wouldn’t let go. I was shaking my head and bobbing my head back and forth like a complete psycho. It took four fighters to finally break the hold and the man was totally blue. The paramedics were there and put an oxygen mask over him and he began to get back to a normal color again. But I sat there in a dazed and confused state, like I just took a drug or something and don’t realize where I am. I just sat in the corner bobbing back and forth as the crowd booed me out of the dome. The other fighters lost respect for me and kicked me out of shoot fighting forever. Now that I look back on the situation, I’m glad they did what they did and threw me out for good. The shoot fighting association that will be undisclosed put me in an anger management program on behalf of them to straighten me out. The teachers at the classes didn’t know what overcame my sudden aggression, but I did and that was that I clearly snapped due to much pressure from my adrenaline pumping me to keep going and to win the match. I snapped, I clearly snapped and I call it an adrenaline rush gone wrong. It can be compared to a druggie taking a hit but he overdoses. Something that went wrong, well in one way or another that happened to me and since then I’m happy to say that I haven’t snapped since that dreadful day. Do I regret what I did? Yes.Will it happen again? I hope not. I was clearly pumped up for the fight like no other fight and this was my chance to prove to everyone that I was a somebody, not a nobody. In one way I succeeded in what I did cause everyone knows me in shoot wrestling as Snap Steiner. Not a name I would be truly being known for but it’s better then someone saying that he “sucked” and was “the worst shoot fighter in the world.” Do I still feel that rush of adrenaline running through my veins when my desire to win in a competition sport still takes over my body? You dam right it does and it always will, you can’t stop that. Even when I’m in the ring now, I still get that rush. The rush to keep pushing me to do my absolute best in the squared circle and when I know I’m down for the countdown, it reminds me that I still have some gas left in the tank. Pushing my way back trying to make a comeback. I credit the adrenaline rush for allowing me to do that. If it weren’t there then I would have more losses then wins in my record. Every athlete knows what I’m talking about. Football players get it all the time. When they need someone clutch to come through to win the ball game there is always the person who is always clutch, they can push themselves to the limit and when the ball game for there team cause there adrenaline flow was pushing them to keep going, doing impossible things that was thought could not be done, happened. Adrenaline is a fluid that cannot be described except for its outcome when the adrenaline is flowing through the veins pumping the heart to go faster, pushing the body to exceed maximum force. It’s the fuel that feeds my fire.
