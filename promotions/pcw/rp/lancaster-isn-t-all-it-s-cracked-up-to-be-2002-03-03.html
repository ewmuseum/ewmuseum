Lancaster Isn't All It's Cracked Up To Be

3 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/3/02 Last time we encountered Rush, we saw that he had finally made his escape from Damien and the other evil men who were trying to take him and torture him like their father did for claiming himself to be a god. Rush did escape, but he lost a valued friend in the process. He lost a man who was named after the devil himself, Lucifer, but was loyal to Rush in the short time that they had to work together. Last we saw Rush, he was asleep somewhere next to a road sign, which showed he was near civilization. Rush’s next stop on his comeback tour to March Madness: Lancaster County, Pennsylvania. This scene opens up with a shot of a beautiful vivid sunset over a lonely paved highway, which is the site of the road sign where Rush is sleeping. The scene is like that of one out of a postcard, and it seems even more beautiful to Rush because it means he is somewhere near real people and a real town or city. Rush, the sun’s warmth beginning to heat his body, drowsily wakes up from his good sleep, rubbing his eyes and stretching his arms. He steps up, and is amazed to see a small town up ahead, though the details cannot be seen. He can see smoke rising from chimneys, so he sets out towards his next destination. As he walks down the road, he thinks of his luck, when just the last night he was cursing himself for his awful luck. It seems that Rush’s fate was changed, and that his destiny has brought him here to Lancaster. He starts to walk down the road, and he quickly comes closer and closer to the village, and it looks more like something out of a fairy tale. As Rush walks, the road turns to dirt, and he looks around confused at what he sees. Around him are surroundings that don’t remind him much at all of civilization. He sees what looks like a primitive market. All around him are horses, carriages, and people dressed like they’re from the 1800’s. Looking around again, he can’t see anything else, a gift shop for this historic part of the town or anything. Rush walks up to a young woman and asks her about his ideas. Rush: So, little girl, where is the gift shop, I need to make a phone call to these people I know. The girl looks at him blankly, and Rush tries to smile at her, trying to ignore the fact that she is wearing an ugly brown plain woolen dress. She frowns and just stares at him, as he walks away, muttering to himself angrily. He now goes up to a middle-aged man, who might actually give him this information. He speaks to the man in the nicest tone possible. Rush: Sir, I’m looking for the gift shop and a telephone that I might be able to use, is there any way that I could get use of any of these? I really need to talk to a person in the Lou, can you tell me where I could find a phone? Suddenly, Rush realizes that every person in the market is staring at him. All of the people, all dressed in the old style clothes, are staring at him, and now he is in the middle of a giant circle of people. Rush: Do any of you people know where I could get to the phone? Please, I’ve been close to death twice in the past five days and some people think I’m dead, some very important people. If you could show me to a phone, I’d be very grateful. All of the people around him, who he has just realized all are white, still just blankly stare until a middle-aged lady proclaims to him blankly, just like everyone else has talked to him in this village. Woman: We don’t have a phone here. This is Lancaster County; the closest phone is about 100 miles east of here. Rush collapses to his knees, and puts his head into his elbow, sighing heavily at this. He has come all this way, only to find that he is far off anywhere that would have a phone for him to use. The people, tired of staring at one of the few black people they’ve ever seen in their life, walk away, leaving Rush distraught standing, with one of the men, dressed in a white collared shirt, brown overalls, a black top hat, and brown shoes, stays to watch Rush. Rush: What do you want? Do you want to tar and feather me? Well you don’t need to humiliate me any more, because I feel like shit already. Just leave me to sulk, because I realized that I failed. Man: You didn’t fail my friend. I’ve heard of your trials and your voyage. You haven’t failed at all. In fact, you’ve brought me new hope with this church of Crunk. It may not mean much from a small town man like me. A primitive man like myself can’t call himself a true fan, but sometimes I hear about you in the newspapers. In fact, I heard that you were killed. Is that true? Rush: Well, I’m as good as dead. I’m not gonna make it to March Madness now and it’s already Sunday by my account. It’s early in the morning and I’ve got until eight tonight to be in the Lou. Man: Where is this Lou you speak of? Do you need to go to Louisiana? Rush: Jesus, you people really are cut off from the world. The Lou is St. Louis, Missouri. There’s an important Pay Per View tonight and if I don’t get there my ass is toast. Man: Do you mean they will burn your bottom? Rush: No, that’s just what they say. It’s a figure of speech. It means that I’m going to be in trouble and I’m in a bad situation. Man, how come you guys get into your characters so much? I mean, I guess it’s cool that you live like the olden days but don’t you ever get sick of it and just want to go back to the lives that you used to have? Man: What do you mean, my old life? I’ve lived here all my life. We are content without appliances and means of modern life. Rush: Wait a second; do you mean to say that you actually live a totally primitive life, always have, and that you’re about as technologically as advanced as CANADIANS?!?! Man: Canada? What’s that? Rush: Bah~! Jobber country. They can’t stand up to the godly powers of the United States anyway. So, are you saying that you have always lived like this, you don’t know the technological advances work so that now instead of calling a letter and waiting weeks, we can go on a phone and wait for hours, or go on a computer and wait while we get a virus from some porn hungry kid. Man: I see. So, you enjoy this outside world? This utopia of technology? Rush: Yeah, I mean it’s kinda screwed up, there’s some crazy guys out there but I’d much rather take the risks with technology than to be holed up here cleaning up horse shit all day. Man: Do you think you could show us how to, how to live like you? Rush: Well, I can’t show you everything but I suppose I could give you a few tips. But, I’m different than the people here. Will they listen to me? Man: They will in time; We just have to make them see that you are the same as us, just another person. Then they will listen to you. Rush: Hey, man, actually I never got your name. What do they call you around here? Man: My name’s Ezekial Flothenberg Geralderian Buckerwine Hairmen. Rush: Ohh, well we’ll have to change that a little to give you some cultural appeal. For now, I’ll just call you Ezekiel. But, why did you come and talk to me when everyone else left and thought I was a fool? Wait a second, you’re not gay are you? Ezekiel: Of course I’m gay. Rush starts to move away from the man, trying not to look suspicious. Ezekiel: We’re all gay. Rush tries to smile at the man, but now he is babbling, trying to somehow find a way out of this strange, strange town. Ezekiel: We’re gay and joyful all the day. Rush: Oh. Hey listen Ezekiel, here’s out first lesson of the day. Instead of using the word gay, I want you to use content. Ezekiel: Why can’t I use gay? Rush whispers in his ear, and Ezekiel’s mouth drops open and his eyes get wide as he hears Rush’s explanation of the word gay and it’s present use. Ezekiel: Wait a second, what does ream mean? Rush: Uhhh…that’s a lesson for another day. Before the end of today, I will have brought Crunk to the Amish, that’s all that’s important. Ezekiel, if I teach your people the way of being crunk, do you think that you could help me get a ride to St. Louis? Ezekiel: Well, I suppose I could lend you a horse or something like that. We’ll find a way for you to get to the Saint Lou. Rush: No, just the Lou. Ezekiel: Ahh yes. We will find a way for you to get to the Lou. And we will find a way to make you content. Rush: Good job, the most important part was to learn your problem. Now all I have to do is help you start to solve it. The two men walk off toward Ezekiel’s house in the early morning sun, a very unlikely pair. They talk as they fade off down the road. Ezekiel: Isn’t Ream that stuff that comes from goats? Rush sighs as the scene fades to black…
