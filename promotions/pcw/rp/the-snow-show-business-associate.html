Date: Season I, Episode 7 ﻿************************************The Snow ShowSeason I, Episode 7“Business Associate”************************************ Some people say it’s the life that chooses us, and not the other way around. That’s bullshit,you know. Like I said before, we choose the life. That doesn’t mean we necessarily like it, or the people that we meet in it. Me? I fucking hatemost of the people. Hell, I hate half the damn country. I’m a hypocrite in most ways. Why do I say that? Fucking look at me. I am the American male, regardless of where I was born. I’ve completelyAmericanized myself over the past four or five years, and oddly enough, most American’s andthe whole American way of life pisses me off. It’s no secret - never has been. And sure, it feels great when I walk out into an arena, filled with screaming fans. And I’m suremost of them are decent people. Most of them are better than I am, I know. But they’ve beenhanded this list of priorities from the time they were babies and they’ve grown to live by them. Problem is, the list they were given is completely fucked up. So, like I said, I fucking hate most things about the American way of life. I hate the blindpatriotism that devours the country like some grotesque disease, while they look over their ownflaws to see what’s wrong with everything else. But like I said, I’m completely Americanized. Does that make me a hypocrite? Sure fuckingdoes. But I never claimed to like much about myself either... ~ Jason Adam Snow *** Location: Snow Residence - New York SuburbDate: December 26, 2002Time: 11:35 pm The long night was finally over. Snow stepped through the front door of his house to find its female inhabitants waiting for himin the kitchen. Great, he thought, just when I thought nothing was worse than having a gun waved in my face. He sighed deeply and took a seat at the kitchen table, running his right hand through his slickedblond hair. Raven was leaning on the fridge with her arms crossed at her chest. Krista was by the counter. Both of them stared silently at him. “What?” he finally sighed. “Nothing,” Raven spoke first. “Well, except for the fact that you left this morning without aword, with people I’ve never seen before, and now you come marching back in at midnight.” “It’s eleven thirty,” Snow argued. Raven’s eyes narrowed. “Eleven thirty-five.” “What were you putting in the trunk of the car?” Krista spoke up. “Oh,” Snow said with a sarcastic smile. “You were watching for me.” The women shrugged. “Bananas,” he admitted. “There are bananas in the trunk.” Raven looked confused and angry at the same time. “Bananas!?” she burst out. “Why did youput bananas in the trunk instead of bringing them in the house.” “I like bananas!” Krista added. “You don’t like these ones!” Snow snapped quickly. The bananas, of course, were laced withheroin, and he suddenly worried that bringing them to his home wasn’t the best of ideas. Seemed like a good thing to do at the time, he thinks, then again, there was a gun under his chin. “Jason,” Raven sighed, “what’s going on?” “They’re a gift,” Snow lied. “A gift for Johnny Silver. I forgot him at Christmas time and I hadto make up for it.” “So you got him bananas?” Raven was shaking her head now. She didn’t believe a word. “It’s a joke gift,” Snow shrugged. “An inside joke. Now, if you girls don’t mind, I’ve got to getsome sleep. I’ve got a big night tomorrow on Fusion.” With that, Snow rose from the table and headed up to his bedroom. His wife’s eyes followedhim as he left the kitchen, and then she let out a frustrated sigh. *** Location: Crestview Mall - New York CityDate: January 2, 2003Time: 11:40 am Krista ran her hand along the sleeve of a pink and purple sweater that she had been eyeing sinceshe walked into the department store. Her other hand found its way to her purse at her right hip. Maybe, she thought as the rest of her wardrobe swirled around through her head. Her prettybrown eyes danced as she pulled the hanger from the rack. “Hi there,” a deep voice came from behind her. It startled her for a moment, and she cursedherself for still being so jumpy at the sound of a male voice - she hated letting the rape take overher life. It was probably just an employee of the store, she thought. She was wrong. She turned around to find herself staring at a dashing young man in a long leather jacket andexpensive matching shoes. Everything about him shined, from his short black hair to his pearlywhite smile. “Hi,” she said awkwardly. People weren’t so forward as this in Canada. “My name’s Marty,” he said as he extended a hand. Suddenly, she recognized him. “Yeah,” she said as her eyes narrowed. “Aren’t you one of Jason’s friends?” “You could say that,” said Marty without letting his smile fade. “More of a business associate,really.” Krista was forced to smile. Marty’s smile was contagious. He was tall and looked yonger thananyone she expected to be a friend of her brother-in-law. She constantly had to remind herselfthat Snow was actually very young. He seemed eternally old to her. Marty was different. Hewas youthful and vibrant. “I’m Krista,” she finally said. “I know,” Marty responded. “I saw you that time at Snow’s house - I guessed you lived theretoo.” “Odd meeting you here,” said Krista. “Not so odd,” Marty chuckled. “I kind of followed you,” he admitted. “I saw you and yoursister pulling out a few seconds before I dropped Snow off, and I followed you here. Hard ashell to find you in the mall though.” Krista should have been offended, but she couldn’t be. There was something in the young man’sface that wouldn’t let her. An awkward moment of silence followed. The two young adults simply stared at each other,smiling, while people passed without either of their notice. Finally, Krista spoke up. “I’m meeting Raven for lunch in a few minutes,” she said. “Would you like to come?” Marty didn’t answer - he just smiled. *** Location: Crestview Mall - New York CityDate: January 2, 2003Time: 11:55 am The hustle and bustle of major malls was something that Raven was not used to. She sat downat a table in the food court and watched for her sister, placing her young daughter’s carrying seatin front of her. They had agreed to meet for lunch, and then decide whether or not their day ofshopping was indeed over. She didn’t expect Krista to bring a young man along. “This is Marty,” Krista announced as she approached. “He’s a friend of Jason’s.” A little shocked, Raven gave a pleasant smile. She had met Johnny Silver, another of Snow’sfriends, and wasn’t fond of him. She didn’t expect much more from this young man, even if hewas much more pleasant to look at. “Raven,” she introduced herself. “And this is Haley.” “So this is the little girl I’ve been hearing about,” Marty said as he peeked in at the sleepingchild. Her curly blond locks sprouted out from the sides of her pink snow suit hood. “The one and only,” Raven said, and then joked, “as far as I know.” “So what are we eating?” Krista asked as she and Marty took seats across from Raven. Martyfolded his hands on the table, trying to appear polite. Raven bought it, somewhat. “I was waiting for you to decide,” Raven answered. “You know I’m no good with decisions!” Krista looked around at all of the options, and thenturned to Marty. “You’re from here - what’s the best spot.” “Depends on your tastes.” Marty was no help. So... Marty, is it?... you’re a friend of Jason’s?” Raven changed the subject cooly. Her largebrown eyes locked on the young man. “More of a business associate,” Marty said with a floating nod, smiling all the while. “Wehaven’t really known each other long.” “And just what business is it that you’re in?” Raven asked, getting straight to the point. Martystill smiled, but she could see the change in his eyes. He suddenly felt hot. Both Raven and Krista’s eyes were on him, and it suddenly becameobvious that Snow, his business associate, had never explained to his family just what line ofbusiness they were in. Even without Snow there, Marty felt the intense blue eyes beating himdown. “A little of this and a little of that,” Marty answered with a shrug, trying to downplay things. “I don’t know what you mean?” Raven wasn’t going to let the subject drop. “We work for a guy up North,” shrugged Marty. “We run some errands, check some stocks. Our job is basically to make as much money as we can for our boss, and then we take a cut. Your husband must be a work-a-holic though, because he must already be making huge dollarsin the wrestling industry.” “A work-a-holic only rubs the surface,” Raven rolled her eyes. Marty had all but admitted thathe and her husband were involved in illegal dealings, and more than ever, the word ‘mafia’ waspopping into her head. *** Location: Snow Residence - New York SuburbDate: January 2 - 2003Time: 3:35 pm Snow was startled from a doze when he heard the kitchen door shut. He sat straight up on theliving room sofa and rubbed his eyes to remove the signs of weariness. The television was stillon, though he couldn’t remember what he was watching when he drifted off, nor did he knowwhat time it was. His wife entered the room and pulled Haley gently from her carrying seat. “She missed you!” Raven said with a warm smile as she removed the little girl’s snow suit. Snow always thought Raven was too rough when she did this, but she would always respond thatshe knew what she was doing - and of course, she did. Snow knew when he met her that she wasborn with the strongest motherly instinct he had ever seen in a girl - especially one so young, atthe time. Raven passed the child to her father, who cradled her lovingly in the crook of his massive arm. She was a little tired, it seemed, but she stared up at him with large blue eyes just the same. Snow couldn’t help but smile at her. “I got some things for the kitchen,” Raven said as she got up from her crouch and brushed offthe front of her red sweater. “And I got a cute little outfit for Haley.” “I’ll never understand it,” Snow said as he shook his head. “Why don’t you just dress her insomething she’d be comfortable in? What’s the obsession with cuteness?” “Because when she grows up, she’ll want people to remember what a cute baby she was,” Ravenretorted, hollowly. Snow rolled his eyes. “Where’s Krista?” he asked suddenly. “Didn’t she go with you?” Raven had made her way to the kitchen and back by now. “Yeah,” she answered him finally. “But she met a boy there and she’s at his place watching a video. Don’t worry, she said she’d behome before dark.” “New York’s not as safe as New Brunswick, Raven,” Snow said to her for the thousandth time. “Don’t worry,” Raven said with a shake of her pretty head. “He’s a good guy. I met him. Andbesides, it’s good for Krista. She’s spent the last four months practically terrified of the oppositesex.” Snow sighed. He didn’t like thinking about the rape. In reality, it was the rape that linked all theevents that now had him as a lowly mob henchmen. If not for the rape, he would never havetaken part in Doug Hattoni’s death, and then never got together with Tim McDonald, who wrotesome higher-up mobsters. None of that mattered anymore. Tim McDonald was dead. DougHattoni was dead. What happened in prison was in the past now. But the rape still haunted him. There were nights that he dreamed about Krista, lying helplesslyon the cold and dirty ground while some sick sleazeball took forced his snake-like self on her. He closed his eyes and turned his head at the thought. He cared about Krista too much, and thereality of what happened to her was overwhelming. He suddenly became aware of Haley’s weight against his forearm. He could barely hold backtears now. “Maybe you’re right,” he finally said. “Maybe it’s good for her.” “It is good for her,” Raven said again. “I’m not saying she should rush into anything. But thisboy seems nice and safe.” “How do you know that?” Snow asked, turning to his wife. Raven was sitting beside him now,rummaging through her shopping bags. “Well,” she shrugged. “He’s a friend of yours for one thing. And seeing that he knows you, Ican’t imagine that he doesn’t know you would likely kill him if he stepped out of line withKrista.” Snow’s eyes grew wide, suddenly, and his face went pale. With his free hand, he ran his fingersthrough his slicked blond hair. “I know him?” he choked. “Yeah,” his wife began, “a business associate or something. Nice looking young guy - I can’tthink of his name. Morty, maybe? Does that ring a bell?” “Marty!?” Snow’s deep voice rose an octave and he suddenly leaned forward, causing his youngdaughter to whine. “She’s out with Marty fucking Verrelli?! And you let her go!?” “She’s eighteen Jason,” Raven said, a little stunned. “She can do what she wants now. And forthe last time, if you have to use that language, do not use it around yourdaughter!” “Alright, I’m sorry!” Snow said with a hand up. He then went back to the subject: “Legal age istwenty-one down here though, and if there’s one thing good about us living in this country, that’sit. So she’ll still do what we tell her, and she’s not going to see Marty Verrelli. Not for acouple of hours, and not for five f-... minutes!” Snow handed Haley to his wife and sprang up from the sofa, heading for the kitchen. Stunned,Raven yelled after him. “I don’t understand what the big deal is,” she said. “I thought he wasone of your friends! I thought she finally found someone you would approve of.” “For the love of God, Raven,” Snow said as he reappeared in the doorway, “don’t let her goanywhere with any of my friends!” “Don’t you mean business associates?” Raven perked an eyebrow. Suddenly, Snow realized that he was caught.
