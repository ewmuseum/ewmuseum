Why There Are No Holidays For Evil People

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1.1.03(#2 AD) “Ahh…no booking for Turmoil or Fusion, so I can just take my sweet time making my way to a certain victory on Adrenaline.” Rush Mason’s voice, sounding rather relaxed, is the salutation to open this promo, as always. He lets out a deep sigh, but it sounds more like a sigh of relaxation than of stress or anxiety. “I’ll curl up with some nice obituaries and have some good laughs before I head off to bed.” Of course, relaxation isn’t nessecarily a good thing when one is a Voodoo follower with a very strange sense of how the world works. Either way, as Rush walks around his house, in gray un-labeled sweats and a white tank-top showing off his muscular upper body, he picks random things up. Even evil folks need some cleanliness in their house. Rush: Ugh…this place is a pig sty. How’d all this dirt get in here? Doesn’t the dirt have somewhere to be….like on a hobo somewhere? With a sneer towards the dirty living room, Rush turns towards his bedroom, having dealt with enough problems for one night. He moves into the doorway, looking at the entire bedroom, and finding it fit for sleep. Rush: Bon Dieu…I need some sleep. With happiness rarely seen in Rush’s face, the Voodoo Villain hops into the king-size cradle that he calls his nightly abode. Stretching his arms out, lying on top of a down comforter, Rush lets out a sigh and talks to no one in particular. Rush: So nice to be back home in this new year. I mean, not that the new year means anything different. People will still die and have horrible accidents, I’ll still find it funny, that old thing. Rush turns and fluffs one of the large pillows, which like all the other pillows is covered in a soft purple cloth of some sort. He then pulls himself underneath the covers, getting himself comfortable. Lying, stretched out, on his back, his head in the middle of a soft pillow, Rush stares up at the ceiling for a moment. Rush: Hmm…I suppose I should have some sort of resolution for this whole new year bit, shouldn’t I? Bringing his hand up to scratch the stubble on his chin, thinking of what a perfect individual such as he could possibly improve upon. Rush: Damn, I always near there’d be a drawback to being so perfect. What the hell is wrong with staying exactly how I am? Of course, Rush seems to be starting an argument with no one but himself. He still seems genuinely concerned about the matter of a New Year’s resolution, and it seems to have overtaken his wish for a quality sleep. Rush: Well, let’s see…I’m better than everyone. Oh man…this is gonna be tough. Were he a self-knowledgable guy, Rush might realize that he’s won only ONCE at Pay Per Views, the matches that have really mattered, but of course….he’s Rush Mason. So, he will, undoubtedly choose something egotistical rather than actually improve his clutch performance. Rush: Wait a sec…since I don’t need to improve, there’s LOTS that other people can improve on, since they’re not me. Scratching his chin in contemplation again, Rush thinks of how to get others to improve, barring going to random people and explaining to them what exactly he finds wrong with them. Rush: How can I tell six billion people that they need to be a lot more like me…? Contemplating this for a moment, Rush then gives a guttural yelp of some sorts and his eyes light up, exclaiming his plans to the walls and ceiling. Rush: Of COURSE! I’ll send letters to them! This doesn’t exactly makes things any easier, because six billion envelopes and letters are not only hard to come by, but will give one quite the hand cramp. Coming to a quicker conclusion this time, Rush’s eye’s light up yet again as reaches for the bedside phone, picking up the yellow pages that are neatly placed on the floor, amidst dirty clothes. Rush: Let’s see… Flipping through the book, Rush reads aloud the sections as he passes them, moving towards his goal. Rush: Ku Klux Klan…Lame Workers…Lebanon….Less Pay Products….HERE WE ARE! Looking down at the number for ‘Letter-Writing,’ Rush glances down and recites the number aloud as he dials. Rush: Hmm….341-6372….c’mon, c’mon, pick up! Despite it being late evening/early nighttime, there is a pick up on the other end, and Rush’s face turns into a large grin, despite it looking rather evil. “Yes, this is Morris and Jones calligraphy and professional letter-writing services. How may we help you?” Rubbing his hands together, quite happy that his plan is going well thus far, Rush gives the gist of his idea to this employee. Rush: Hi…listen, I was wondering, a ballpark figure perhaps, of how much it would cost to send a non-personalized letter to….say, six billion people? The woman on the phone is clearly taken aback by the large number that this customer is asking of, but she dutifully punches away at her calculator, answering back, voice somewhat wavering, not sure if this is a joke or not. “Well, sir, that would come to around nine hundred million dollars.” At this, Rush’s face droops, because, although he is a very well-paid member of PCW, he is certainly not a billionaire, or anyone in the position to spend nine hundred million dollars on anything. Rush: Hmm…nine hundred million, you say? The woman, still unsure as to whether Rush is serious, responds as if he is a serious buyer, trying to maintain a professional attitude. “Uhh….yes. Just to ask, sir, why would one need to send six billion letters? Rush, eager to stall, in hope of finding any ideas, is eager to divulge this information, hoping to impress the woman on the other end of the phone. Rush: Well, I’m perfect, but the rest of the world isn’t, so I’m going to tell them to shape up and get their acts together, because I haven’t gotten the proper treatment. Not sure whether to laugh or gasp, the woman stays silent, as if waiting for a punchline. Finally, after a moment of silence, she replies, her voice exuding no emotion. “Erm….that’s lovely. Are you, perhaps, a WTO employee, trying to reach out to the world’s citizens as part of your business? Snapping his fingers and wishing that he were one, Rush begins to answer, but then his eyes light up, more so than before and with a much more devious gleam to them. Rush: No, no I’m not. I can pay you for those letters, though. Taken aback with a slight gasp, the woman asks, wonderingly, how Rush might’ve gotten such funds. “Sir, might I ask how you’re going to pay, because I’d certainly like to know where you’ve got nine hundred million dollars stashed away?” With a smirk, Rush looks directly at the camera and chuckles to himself as the scene begins to fade to black. “Oh, just a little account that my company sets up for all the employees? After all, what are business expense accounts for?”
