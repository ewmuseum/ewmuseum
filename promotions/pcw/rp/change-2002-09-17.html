Change

17 September 2002

Date: 9-17-02 RP 3 for Turmoil17 Damn, I need a change. Don’t you think Teresa? Teresa stands in front of her hotel mirror and nods. It’s been only three days since her former ‘friend’ attacked and humiliated her. With drastic measure usually results in drastic change. Ventilation process perhaps? Yes, a change is definitely in order, and we’re starting with the hair. Damn if I do it myself. I need a professional’s touch. Hell, I got the money. Within minutes, Teresa pats her back pocket of her Levi’s making sure her wallet is still there. This chick thinks purses or pocket books are a waste of time and space. Once the wallet check is made, she pulls on her Nike’s and heads out the door where a cab is waiting. Since Jack also took off with her most favorite vehicle she owns, a 1978 midnight purple short wheel base step side pick up truck with four wheel drive and an 8 ball on the gearshift, she has to take taxi cabs till the law enforcement does their work. The cab driver is an older man. To Teresa he appears to be old enough to retire but like most average Americans, he has to continue working to make ends meet. His hair is white as a ghost and he is clean-. He wears a white button up shirt. The first two buttons are left undone and his sleeves are rolled up twice between his wrist and elbow. He watches Teresa step in the back seat and looks in the rearview once her door is shut. Where to Miss Tomas? Teresa sits back in her seat. The cab is surpassingly clean and well kept up. She’s impressed. I take it you’re a fan. I’m honored. Take me to Pam’s Salon. My grandson is. I’ve watched your show enough with him to know what you look like. No offence, but his favorite female is Anarchy. Personally, I like the way you don’t prance around in skimpy clothes like most females do in your line of work. In my day, women that dressed like that was considered prostitutes and arrested. Teresa pulls out a small box from her shirt pocket. Her shirt is dark blue, long sleeved and rolled up to her elbows. Her Levi’s are the regular blue denim and the carpenter style. She reaches in her front pocket pulling out a dark purple lighter. The cab driver glances to the rearview just in time to watch her light a cigarette. He shakes his head before making a left turn at a traffic light. Ashtray is installed behind my seat. Burn my seat and I’ll have to charge you extra. I’m sorry Miss Tomas, but you don’t seem to be the type that would smoke. Teresa exhales cracks her window and exhales a cloud of smoke and takes a moment before finally responding. A lot of stress lately. I’m sure you understand. My own company tosses taxes at us every time we turn around, being attacked at McDonald’s by someone I used to trust. The sorry son of a gun stole my damn truck, and to top it all off, I’m sitting in a cab with an old man talking about my problems while I worry about getting my hair done. The long-winded Teresa takes another puff on her cancer stick and exhales toward the barely opened window. Shit, these things tastes like cardboard. Before the taxi parks in front of the salon, Teresa had smoked the entire cigarette and left the butt in the ashtray. She thanks the driver and pays him. What caught Teresa’s attention about Pam’s Salon are the inviting pictures of people with flawless hair dos and perfect cuts. The pictures were photos of customers. The building itself looks no different than the other hometown looking buildings in the shopping center. Teresa steps in to see a heavy set woman with very short hair standing behind the chair with a huge smile on her face. Teresa had made an appointment. Welcome to Pam’s Salon. I’m Joyce. Just sit here and we’ll get started. Teresa looks the woman up and down once, then nervously walks to the chair. She sits down and holds her head back as Joyce begins to wash it. What do you have in mind? A cut, trim, perm? Perm? Hell no. That style got lost in the 80’s. I don’t know all the names of all your fancy cuts or whatnot. Joyce sits Teresa up and begins to towel dry her long straight sandy blonde hair. She then begins to comb it gently, careful not to pull her hair. Streaks. I want streaks. Highlight is what y’all call it I think. I want the darkest black you have. Teresa looks at Joyce through the mirror in front of her. Joyce has finished combing her hair and is searching through a cabinet under her workstation. She takes a couple of bottles out and sets them on the counter. After half an hour of applying and another rinsing and drying, Joyce turns Teresa around so that she can see the damage, or the masterpiece she done to our own Diva’s hair. AHHHHHHHHH!!!!!!! Teresa turns around facing Joyce grasping a huge lock of her own hair with each hand. Her baby blue eyes grow wide staring at the shaking hairdresser. What the HELL is this? Joyce steps back as Teresa stands and takes a step toward her. Teresa’s hands reaching for the big woman’s large neck. Joyce opens her mouth to speak but Teresa interrupts her. These streaks are so damn dark black! You couldn’t get it any blacker could you? Teresa places her hands on Joyce’s shoulders and begins to shake her violently. Joyce screams in fear not knowing what Teresa will do to her. She has heard of Teresa’s violent ranges and read about them in magazine’s. Just when Joyce thinks Teresa will sling her against the wall, Teresa’s arms wrap around the woman and embrace her in a tight hug! I LOVE IT!! Here take this. Teresa releases the overwhelmed woman and overwhelms her even more when Teresa pulls out a one hundred dollar bull and places it in her hand. Teresa closes her fingers around the bill. Keep the change. Joyce’s moth drops unable even to thank Teresa. Teresa sees it in the woman’s eyes and smiles before heading out the door. About 2 minutes of staring at the door, Joyce opens her hands and finds a ticket to Turmoil hidden inside the folded bill.
