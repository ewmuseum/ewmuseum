Evening Struggle

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: *Trying* to write something decent for my Violence Jack match... Eddy stroked his long black hair nervously, with a tight red jacket wrapped around his torso. A newspaper blew past him, and it was noticeable he was at a bus stop, in a cold evening climate. He say pretty much inert with an occasional cough or shift of his spot, with cars blowing cold air on him as he sat, waiting for the bus. He sighed impatiently, with white steam floating from his mouth as he coughed into his jacket sleeve and sat. He stared uncomfortably around him and sat cross legged on a cold bus stop bench. Suddenly, the diesel chug and screeching halt of a coach bus arrived. Eddy stood up, coughed, and walked toward the opening door into a warm bus. The bus driver sat, staring at Eddy strangely. His eyes were arcane and his hair was silver and hung in shiny threads along the side of his head with his glistening bald head clear in the middle. He had a thick gray beard which, obviously shaggy, had a young appearance to it, as if a 20 year old would wear it, except it was silver and not and shade of brown, black, red, blonde, or anything along those lines. Eddy stared at him as the bus driver groaned impatiently. 'C’mon, drop the 75 cents in!' Eddy, forgetting almost completely of money, stuck his wet hands into his jeans pocket and came up with nothing but lint. He stood silently for a second, but, in the end, had nothing in return. 'You don’t have 75 cents?! Ha, get off my bus, you cheap ass.' His voice was familiar in Eddy’s mind but wouldn’t allow him to perceive who he could be. He glanced unassuming at the man, and talked soft and passionately. 'Sir, I didn’t bring my money. Could I please--.' 'NO! Get off my bus, no cash, no gas', said the bus driver rudely. Eddy sighed and walked backwards off the bus. He slipped on a wet part on the steps and fell backwards, landing in a puddle of cold water and gashing a cut on his right arm. He looked up from his laying position to the driver, as he laughed, and the bus drove off, chugging, and splashing water on Eddy. Eddy sighed and trekked off for another way out. He, cold and forgetting his wallet, walked around the city in a strange way. He was back in Dallas from pleading to Bob Vurdo to stop selling drugs to his sister, but Vurdo not only rudely slammed a door on him, he had said if he won next weeks Fusion, he would stop selling drugs. Eddy, uncertain and untrusting of Vurdo’s motivation in this, accepted because he was desperate, he didn’t need his sister on drugs when he parents were dying. He blew warm air into his hands to get them warm, and wrapped himself as if he were enclosed in a straightjacket to keep himself warm. Eddy, did win his match against Mark Makonie, even though he was proud of a winning redebut, he had just found out his sister was on drugs afterward, and that Bob Vurdo was indeed selling them to her, not directly, but through a man in his Mafia named Frankie Robertso, who had said he worked for Vurdo. Eddy, in desperate need of a ride, glanced down on the asphalt and noticed 3 quarters lying there, almost begging Eddy to pick them up. Eddy reached his hand down to pick them up, but a Corvette with rap music blaring zoomed by, hitting a slab of rock that clocked Eddy in the head. Eddy, woozy, fell to the sidewalk slowly, hitting with a clang of his head hitting a road sign. He fell into a daze, a strange dream in his unconsciousness, slowly slipping from reality… He arrived in a dream of a restaurant. The atmosphere was open, like a bar without windows, but was warm and cool if you asked about the climate. It, with the outdoor of beach sand and booze, was situated in Hawaii, not a doubt about it. Eddy walked in, glancing thoughtlessly around the room without looking to well at anything. He sat down in an isolated stool at the bar, with chatter around him but seats down. He cleared his though and glanced down at the wood of the bar. A young bartender with blonde hair and a white outfit ambled over to Eddy, standing in front of him without Eddy noticing. 'What is it?', said the bartender, looking at Eddy. 'I’m just a little depres-.' 'I don’t care about how you feel, I’m not your boyfriend fag. What do you want to drink is what I’m asking.' 'A Dr. Pepper would be good…' 'Sissy. Why can’t you take something like a vodka?' 'I’m not choosing to drink.' 'Why the hell not?' 'It’s a life choice sir. Respect it or not, I’m not drinking alcohol. Just not happening.' 'Are you even old enough?' '19…' 'No wonder. Well, hey, I’ve had a rough day so you’ll get a damn Dr. Pepper for you', said the bartender, lifting a beer mug a cleaning the interior with a white napkin. 'OK', said Eddy, laying his elbows on the table and lying his cheeks on his palms as he stared mindlessly into the ceiling fan. He turned in his stool, and was face to face with a man he knew from awhile back, named Marcus Stanford. Eddy smiled. 'Marcus? Is that you?' 'Yeah, how do you know me?', said the man in a confused voice. 'You remember Eddy? Eddy Ramirez?' The man cocked his head to the right, in remembrance of whatever he could remember, and suddenly struck an idea and looked at Eddy, then smiled and sat to the stool next to him. He was about 25, solid black hair and clean shaven. He had a blue shirt on with Khaki’s below that. He turned in his stool towards Eddy. 'How’s it been? Where were you the past year?' 'Down in the gutter, but I’m back in wrestling now so I’m happy.' 'That’s great. I’m getting married.' 'Great man. When?' 'August 9th.' 'Amazing. Hope it does end up being one of those 50% that do work out.' 'Me too. Why are you in Hawaii?' 'I’m here to tell a wrestler to stop selling drugs to my sister.' 'Who?!', said Marcus. 'Bob Vurdo…' 'That bastard. Well, hope it works out.' 'Me as well.' 'I hope you beat him, and your Turmoil opponent, what is his name…?' 'I HAVE a Tuesday opponent?' 'Yeah', said Marcus strangely. 'Oh I remember, his name is…' Suddenly, the ring of a cell phone came up as Marcus dug his hand in his front pocket and lifted a flip-open phone to his ear. He stood up, and walked to a corner of the bar for privacy. The conversation lasted for about 2 minutes with Marcus’ face turned from Eddy the whole time, but after it was done, Marcus shut his cell phone and walked for the door. 'Hey! Marcus! Wait!', Eddy hollered. 'I have to go', said Marcus, pushing the door open and walking out. Eddy jogged over to it, but once he opened it to see where he went, his truck was down the road and out of sight. *** 'He’s alive, just knocked out', a deep voice groaned. Eddy opened his eyes and he was on a hospital bed, staring face up at a man with thick Coke bottle glasses and brown hair. To the other side was an attractive brunette with bright red lipstick and a clipboard. 'Uh…', said Eddy, sitting up. 'You OK?', said the man doctor. 'Yeah. My head hurts…' 'We dabbed it with rubbing alcohol and bandaged it. It’ll be fine. How’d you get hurt?', said the female doctor. 'A car kicked up a rock and hit me in the head.' 'All the way from the ground to your head?', she asked. 'I was crouching down…' 'Why?', the man doctor said. Eddy, wanting to be dishonest about the reason why, tried to lie but couldn’t make himself do it. 'I wanted to pick up some change off the ground so I could catch a bus…' The female, who tried to not laughed, turned the other way to not show she was amused, as the man doctor kept a normal expression. 'Well, you’re OK. We won’t charge you this time, you probably don’t even have the money for it if your picking up change..', said the male doctor who almost laughed to. 'I didn’t bring my wallet. Be nice.' 'Oh well. You can leave', said the man doctor as Eddy lifted himself off the bed and for the white door. Eddy turned the knob and opened, thinking one thought: 'Who AM I facing next Turmoil?'
