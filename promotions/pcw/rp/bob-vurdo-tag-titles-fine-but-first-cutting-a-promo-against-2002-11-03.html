Bob Vurdo? Tag Titles? Fine, but first CUTTING A PROMO AGAINST BLADED!

3 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/3/02 *Cue Jingle* We enter into the blue-backdropped studio, see the white neon-light bordered podiums and blue screens. It's Jeopardy! Not the pathetic Celebrity Jeopardy, the ACTUAL Jeopardy (says Skylar Green: thus making this RP premise less-than-commonly utilized). An light-skinned Indian woman, cute face, wearing a navy women's business suit and her dark brown hair tied back at the top of her head stands at the far left in the camera's view. To her immediate left, a Sailor for the French merchant marine by trade. He's got a handlebar moustache, a white shirt, and a shoulder-length brown mullet. The Jeopardy music hits the sound stage and the camera zooms in to the host's podium where Alex Trebek is standing. He looks totally different. Same dorky dark blue suit, but he's trying black hair and a shave this time. Oh, but who's the third box? We'll get to him. The music stops, the camera is on a bust shot of Alex Trebek, and the crowd has stopped clapping as the teleprompter has gone off. Trebek:'Hello, everyone, and welcome to Jeopardy. I'm your host Alex Trebek, and joining me today are three contestants from different walks of life. Our returning champion in the far left box is Debbie Pinto, an Indian-Canadian forensic anthropologist from Miami, Florida. In the center is Chris Walker, a professional sailor who lives in France for 8 months out of the year. And off to the far right is a man who seems to only refer to himself as JuiceR. Welcome everyone.' Debbie:'Hi.' Chris:'Hello.' JuiceR:'Sir!' Everyone looks weird at JuiceR (who wrote his name just like that). He's sporting a brown mop-top framing his fair face, wearing a green shirt, fatigues, and combat boots. He's quite muscular from military training, the green crew shirt fitting skintight. Alex Trebek is confused. Trebek:'Your name is JuiceR?' JuiceR:'Yes sir!' Trebek shakes his head. Trebek:'Don't you have a normal name?' JuiceR:'Sir, my name is JuiceR Darius, sir!' Trebek:'That's not much of a name either.' JuiceR:'Sir! I suggest that you take up any matter of a bad name with my creator, sir!' Trebek:'You don't have to say Sir all the time.' JuiceR:'Sir, that's just my military training.' Trebek:'It says here you're a wrestler.' JuiceR:'Yes sir. Admittedly not a very good wrestler, sir. But my training in the military does not serve me well outside of war, sir!' Trebek:'Right...' Ignoring the contestant, the categories come up on the screens behind Alex Trebek. He turns around to check them out. Trebek:'Well, let's get started with the game. Here are the categories for the first round. The Simpsons, The Russian Revolution, Cooking, Latin America, Gothic Art, and Tennis Greats. Debbie, as champion you've got control of the board.' The very attractive Debbie peruses her choices on the board before one category in particular piques her interest. Debbie:'I'll take Tennis Greats for $200.' The graphic of '$200' is replaced on the board by the text that Alex Trebek reads. Trebek:'This tennis great's numerous championships were doubly impressive because of his race, though he died of cancer at the age of 47.' JuiceR is quick to press his buzzer, and is recognized by the judges. Trebek:'JuiceR?' JuiceR:'Sir, yes sir!' There is some unprompted laughter from the audience, Trebek asks them to calm back down. Trebek:'You may not understand, JuiceR, but that wasn't a statement, that was an answer. In Jeopardy, you are given the answer and must supply the question. Do you understand?' JuiceR:'Yes sir!' Trebek:'Do you have a response question?' JuiceR:'No sir!' Trebek:'I see.' *25 minutes later.* Trebek:'Okay, now it's time for Final Jeopardy. We coming into this lightning round we once again have a commanding lead by Debbie Pinto with $14,800. Chris Walker has a respectable $5,100 and JuiceR is trailing by a-- BAFFLING negative $9000 dollars. Pragmatism would dictate that Debbie would be the automatic winner, but we've had longshot comebacks because the overconfident leader bid too much money on a wrong answer. Will that happen tonight? There's a possibility as the Final Jeopardy category is...' The screen immediately behind Alex Trebek to the right switches graphics with a xylophonic 'Ping!' Trebek:'Philosophy. We'll see if Chris Walker or JuiceR, who is allowed to bid up to $10000 dollars that he doesn't have, right after this commercial break. Come on back with us.' The Jeopardy theme hits again and the prompted audience claps as the cameras zoom out to a wide shot. The feed is replaced by a commercial after 10 seconds. Trebek walks over to JuiceR. He normally talks to all of the contestants during commercials but this time it's all about JuiceR. Debbie stretches and Chris winks at a woman in the audience. JuiceR stands at attention, as he has been for the entire game. Trebek:'Did you know any of those answers?' JuiceR:'Sir, yes sir!' Trebek:'Then why didn't you respond correctly? All you needed to do was form the answer in a question.' JuiceR:'Sir! The military doesn't teach you how to ask questions, the military teaches you to shoot the enemy if the need should arise.' Alex Trebek shakes his head. Trebek:'I feel sorry for you.' The commercial break ends and the camera comes back to Alex Trebek at the host's podium. Trebek:'Welcome back, Jeopardy fans. Our contestants are ready so let us proceed with Final Jeopardy. The answer is...' The 'Philosophy' category text graphic switches to the answer. Trebek:'Why not? You have 30 seconds to provide the correct question, starting now.' JuiceR gets tense, sweating as he is forced to contemplate a question whose answer is a question. It's five seconds into the jingle and Debbie and Chris are already finished. 15 second remaining, JuiceR shakes nervously, scribbling on his answer pad with his left hand. 5 seconds left, JuiceR is shaking violently. Finally, the music stops and JuiceR is still writing, though by now having thoroughly disturbed all in the studio. Trebek:'Time's up, please put down your writing utensils. Let's see what you answered. We'll start with Debbie, our current champion. She wrote...' Debbie presses the button on her podium to reveal her answer. Trebek:'Why? That is correct. And you wagered $4,000. That puts you in the lead and makes you the winner of the game, but let's see what your opponents put. Chris, you were at $5,100 and you wrote.' Chris presses his button. Trebek:'Why? Again, the correct answer. And you wagered all of your winnings to tak you up to a total of $10,200, though that's still not enough to defeat Debbie. Now, let's go to JuiceR.' JuiceR does not press the button on his podium. Trebek:'JuiceR?' At a second prompting, JuiceR presses the button. A long message comes up. Trebek reads it aloud. Trebek:'Sir, I am unequipped to deal with questions. I cannot think analytically, I am trained to eliminate an identified target. I it my duty and occupation to kill my enemy, not to think. But I have no enemy and cannot function in a thinking man's world. Why not? Unfortunately, sir, I do not have the correct question, but there is a question of my own to which Why not? perfectly fits...' As soon as Trebek finishes reading the statement, JuiceR pulls a fully-loaded .45 revolver from the back of his pants, inserts it into his mouth with the barrel pointing up at his brain, and pulls the trigger.... The EndWhen a man trained to kill his enemy must operate without an enemy, he becomes the enemy...
