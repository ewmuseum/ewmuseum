The Dragon Stirs: Bruce's Folly Revealed (Part 2)

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 15-16 of 2003 A.D. “We came to warn you honestly,” came the mind-transferred message of Rumiko. “Rest assured your dealings with ‘that cretin’ are very much our business.” “Can’t you speak like a normal human being?” said Bruce flatly. “Or.…whatever you are?” “Rumiko is a mute,” retorted the wiry young male. “She can speak only using telepathic and psionic abilities. Typically I would act as her interpreter, but in this unusual rift in space and time, such laws are negated and you can communicate with her freely through mental channels. Even if you typically don’t have that gift that is.” Bruce cracked a devious grin, arms folding across his brawny chest. A stream of fog wisped between the conflicting persons, the perimeter of darkness beyond swirling under an everlasting current. “So what you doin’ here then, boy? You her bodyguard?” “Rumiko doesn’t need a bodyguard, Bruce Shanahan. She’s able to take care of herself. But we both act as representatives of the Celestial convention.” The cultist rubbed the stubble of his chin, peering with a deformed grin at his visitors. “Sorta like senators huh? Gotta deal with both of you fucks then?” “Coarse language for such a ‘holy’ place,” commented Kenshiro dryly, fog wafting past his stoic form. Bruce grunted his amusement at the comment. “Your views of ‘holy’ and those of my own seem to conflict, kid.” “Your views, like your beliefs, are mad. You believe in a pantheon of beings who will offer not even gratitude in return for your service, yet you consciously flaunt your reverence for them without question. Your conduct is absurd, how do you justify it?” “You are no less mad than I am, you insolent little pest,” spat Shanahan. “I have seen the fate of the cosmos, and I have more sense than to vainly attempt to defy it. I’ve seen my destiny and chosen to go with the flow, so to speak. Fools of your insipidness couldn’t comprehend such a thing. You, of course, are much like the one you’re protecting: simply blind. You can’t alter that which is predetermined. Or did someone like that fool Akiyama lead you to believe otherwise?” “We’re not protecting Skylar, Bruce. We’re actually protecting you.” At this comment he couldn’t stifle his laughter. Deranged cackling echoed throughout the void before he regained his composure and offered a response. “While I appreciate your concern, I do believe I have everything well in hand. Thus if that is your solitary reason for desecrating this place with your filthy presence, you may go now.” “We have no intention of leaving until our peace has been said. The Celestials despise those who command you, and are most unhappy with the crimes you have perpetrated again your own. But even so….the situation with Skylar is much more vital at this time….” “What crimes do you speak of, mongrels?” breathed Bruce, gazing into the black eternity beyond the sphere. “For one, we know of the sacrifice,” came the mental transmission of Rumiko. “The mass murder you perpetrated on an entire community, and justified as the whim of the Old Ones whom you serve. That is your greatest transgression.” “Pfft….they will live on, and now their purpose is greater than it ever has been. They will serve the Great Old Ones and, in death, exist as everlasting servitors of the Outer Gods. The town still thrives. I only took the lives of those I deemed worthy of such sacrament, and their spiritual fates I’m not privy to. The others were spared.” “Stop twisting the truth,” snorted Kenshiro. “Their bodies live, but their souls have been ravaged by the dark forces you summoned that night. They’re drones, the walking dead animated to serve lazy, grotesque immortals. And from what we were shown of those whom you chose to make physical sacrifices of…..I don’t think ‘ravaged’ does that sort of depraved carnage any justice.” Shanahan merely shrugged, as if indifferent to the situation of Brayston village. “Meh….no suspicion from the outside. The Brayston community will serve the Old Ones without question, and to me they will act as loyal sentinels. To all but a few, that hamlet never existed to begin with. They are the perfect servants.” “Who are you, or the Old Ones for that matter, to pass judgment on any in the world of mortals?” “It is their divine right,” sneered Shanahan again, his fury scalding. “They are immortal and they hold the key to the power of the universe. Thus, they may judge whomever they choose. It is their choice. And I merely rank among their earthly executioners.” “The Dragon will be the one to pass judgment on mankind, Bruce Shanahan,” Rumiko transmitted with a grin. “When he awakens from his millennia-long slumber, his verdict will be the only one that matters.” “You are wrong, heathens. Azathoth, the great daemon-sultan and Creator Of All Things himself, he shall be the one to pass final judgment. Great Cthulhu shall rise from his crypt in ancient R’lyeh when the stars become right, and his resurrection will bring forth the others imprisoned across the desolate world called Earth. Together, they shall cleanse the Earth of all humanity and instigate a new order on this planet devoid of the cancer called mankind. And then, the entire universe will tremble under their established might.” “You obviously know nothing of the Legend of the Sleeping Dragon.” “Bah! You forget I have access to archives of which stretch the very annals of time itself. Yes, ‘Rumiko’, I have read the trite little fairy tale you refer to with so much unmerited glory. I know of that story just as I do that of Digo the Grigori, and the Children of the War. I know the revelations of the Koran and the Christian Bible, the edicts of the Scientology movement, and am studied in the decrees of the Celestials. The courts of Sharnoth and the Library at Celaeno hold all realms of knowledge and together harbor the key to all cognitive perceptions. Trust me, worm. Background research merely comes with the territory.” “The territory of being a mindless mercenary and assassin to a pantheon of complacent, debauched immortals?” Shanahan, amused at the insult iterated by the boy, could only laugh at this comment. The Oracles, however, were earnest in their stance. “You really believe your brash heresy will ring in the demise of the human race? Has your hatred driven you that far, Bruce?” “It has transcended hatred,” he replied devoutly. “It is simple destiny. The feeling of enmity has its limits. I can attest to that truth.” “Courts of Sharnoth…..” muttered Kenshiro, interjecting. “The dwelling place of the Crawling Chaos, Nyarlathotep, outside this material universe?” Bruce’s evil grin was all the response necessary for the courier oracles. “Nyarlathotep…..” mused Kenshiro as Rumiko, sitting cross-legged, continued to lick her ice cream. “Yes, he’s a treacherous one. Only the most foolish and asinine of beings would serve such a god. Not to mention outrageously ludicrous.” “Those you revere are somewhat less malevolent to other races of the universe, there is little other difference,” retorted the cult head. “They are no less warmongering than the Old Ones. At least the forces I choose to serve are genuine in their intentions. They have no need to beguile followers.” “News flash,” replied Kenshiro. “They’ve certainly beguiled you.” Shanahan, fed up with the bickering, directed his eyes on the pair. A meager half-grin crept up his face. “Heh….you both claim to be prophets. Then take your crystal ball or handheld radio or Palm Pilot or whatever you use there, and give me a reading. Scan the future, indulge me. I implore you. ” Rumiko offered only a shrug, projecting her thoughts with a woeful mental sigh. “It has already been foreseen. You are going to be defeated.” “Lies….” “You can’t comprehend the power you have awakened here, Bruce Shanahan,” disrupted Kenshiro. “Your attack on Sandy Wong was the catalyst to this entire dispute. She was the only thing that kept Green under control. Her love was his regulator in essence, a safety valve of sorts. You’ve removed that safety valve, and now he’s become like a grenade missing its pin. And woe unto us all all if provoke him further. The havoc he is capable of producing is immeasurable. He could tear apart the very fabric of the universe if his full strength is unleashed. You made a fatal error in your strategy. I’m rather shocked you weren’t informed by your…..um……’lords’ to leave Wong alone.” ”You are not welcome here, petty children.” The voice permeated the foggy-ridden domain, radiating the echo of a thousand crying souls. Kenshiro stumbled backwards at the threat until nearly brushing the barrier of rushing chaos that enveloped the isolated sphere of space. Rumiko didn’t flinch, instead gesturing for Kenshiro to calm himself. “Pay him no mind, Kenshiro. The Dark One will do us no harm.” ”I have far more important matters to attend to than worrying about answering to the fallacious diatribe of the Celestials’ mortal drones. However, your very presence within this hallowed sphere is blasphemy. And you, along with those vain immortals whom you serve, are a desecration to the great creation birthed from the daemon-sultan. A guttural chattering emanated in the background of the Crawling Chaos’ disembodied words, presumably the primal tongue of the ‘hunting horror’ race that attended the god’s needs and accompanied his presence. At this, Bruce extended a malicious grin. “Harming us would be tremendously erroneous,” responded Rumiko. “The wrath of the Celestials will be beyond fierce in the case of our injury.” I care little for the vengeance of inferior immortals. They don’t dare face us. Our war would only result in their extinction. And you children would no longer serve a purpose. Kenshiro, still a neonate to the discipline of the Celestial order, was still awe-struck by the power generated by the mere tone of the Crawling Chaos’ abyssal voice that thundered through the isolated realm. Rumiko, having finished her ice cream and taken notice of Kenshiro’s perturbed demeanor, decided the time was right to leave the dimension. “We have warned you of the power you have unleashed upon the universe. Do not provoke Green any further, or the consequences could be too dire for reparation.” You overestimate that whelp’s strength. He is but a drone of the Celestial plane. His demise will be no great difficulty for our agents, I assure you. Now take your leave, children, else the Celestials lose two of their more promising seers. Be gone. Bruce grinned, the throaty gibbering of the hunting horrors still echoing with the words of Nyarlathotep. “And kindly remind that fool Akiyama to stay out of my way come tonight, lest he be annihilated by the power of true gods. I’ll crush two of your precious Celestial forces in one fell swoop if he interferes in my business.” Rumiko’s image dissipated with a single sentence and parting word echoing in her wake. “Your arrogance will prove to destroy you, Bruce Shanahan. Farewell…..” In a swirling vortex of light, the figure of Kenshiro gradually vanished from sight. Rumiko soon followed suit after her words had died away into the abyss. Accursed Celestials. But no matter... The god paused and Bruce leered intot he darkness of the all-encompassing void. We have other matters to attend to......
