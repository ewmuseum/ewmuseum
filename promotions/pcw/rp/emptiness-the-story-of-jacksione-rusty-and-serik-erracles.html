Emptiness (The Story of Jacksione Rusty and Serik Erracles)

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: not entered The story of Jack Rusty and Serik Erracles. What an empty weird world. That was what Serik Erracles think of everyone’s existence. A life full of emptiness and pointless journeys to find the precise essence to fill in the barrenness. Serik Erracles has been living in Weesville, Greece for 20 years, Since 1879 – yet hitherto, no one really knows who he is, or if the people living around him even know a man named Serik exist. Everything around Weesville revolves around Jacksione Rusty. Jacksione is the man everyone loves; the man that always makes people laugh; the man, along with his most precious talisman he calls Rising Star, everyone is attracted to with immense interest. With Jacksione Rusty’s popularity, uncanny personality, and mystic possession, comes Serik’s bleakness and drabness to the populace. Serik Erracles isn’t important in Weesville, Jacksione Rusty is. And just like a knife that is stabbing Serik in the heart, slowly draining his blood, he detests and hates Jacksione. But hatred is such an abstract thing when two strangers in the eyes on both and each men are overpowering themselves. These are two men that haven’t face eye to eye yet. Two men that know absolutely nothing about how each other act, feel, or behave. Two men, one man in the periphery of isolation and one man in the happiness of fulfillment, that doesn’t even know how the hatred came to be. But soon, they will discern. Very soon. ~ o ~ o ~ o ~ Jacksione Rusty grasps the Rising Star in his palms as if it is his last resort. The gold-plated medallion was given to him by his father – who received it from his father himself. An item his grandfather say have the ability to cure any person’s misery, give them happiness and laughter, move mountains, and even make every star in heaven rise to the edge of the universe; thus the name Rising Star. An item signifying magic, greatness, and completeness. Jacksione is just like the Greek gods imbedded in the round medallion – powerful and unconditional. No sign of emptiness whatsoever. The Rising Star is everything to Rusty. It is now part of his family’s bloodline; wherein the next generation will be given the talisman and hence become just like Jacksione Rusty, his father, his grandfather, and the Greek gods. Losing the item is like losing their dynasty; Jacksione will never lose The Rising Star. Never He situates The Rising Star in his pocket as he kneels down before the effigies of the Greek gods, from Zeus to Jupiter, of the temple to say his prayers. To the gods and the goddesses, For five years in this town, you have all guided me. I have come all the way from Italy to this place in search for the purpose of my life. And I have undoubtedly found it. I am someone in this place, my lords. The people love me. I make them laugh. I am the jester of this consign. And that’s all because of your powers. It was all your doing, my lords. With your grace and guidance, I am and will always be complete. “Are you coming?” Jacksione turns his head around to see the door of the petite temple open as Kimberlene’s voice cut through the holy air of the temple’s insides. Jacksione stands up, wiping off his green and golden tunic lightly, a little startled, like a dragon interrupted in his surreptitious and private lair. But Jacksione eventually gives a sly smile to his friend while she, with her traditional Greek dress, steps inside the temple wholly. “So, are you coming?” she asks again. “Does Aphrodite want to be ugly?” he returns with a sharp analogy, a mannerism Jacksione seemed to have grown up with. Kimberlene just respond with a smile, realizing that the beautiful and stickler goddess, Aphrodite, will never want to be unattractive. Jacksione and the Rising Star just made another individual laugh. “Of course I’m coming,” he finally concludes without any jokes. “The people wouldn’t want to come to the gathering and not see me! I’m the star of the night, isn’t that so my good friend, Kimberlene?” “Sure you are,” she responds sarcastically, the impatience in her face is now forming, “when you are naked and running around like a fool.” Jacksione laughs and starts walking toward the door of the temple where Kimberlene is tolerantly waiting for him. The rhythmic echo of his footsteps created a sense of life inside the temple that utters rationale and at the same time vagueness. Here is the threshing floor of a temple being walked by a man with a purpose, to reach the exit door. But when he finally reaches that gate and exits it, is he still the same man? Is he still the same man that earlier prayed to the gods and thanked them for all the grace given to him? Or will he be a conniving, shrewd man that wants all the attention to himself outside these walls? Emptiness and fulfillment seems to pull apart Jacksione at the same time. In the horizon, what will finally take him entirely? Without saying another word, the two friends, Kimberlene and Jacksione leaves the temple to get ready for the Weesville festival – an occasion where the culture and the heritage of the town is celebrated; an occasion where people commemorate the fulfillment of their town; there will be no emptiness in this joyous event, just pure bliss and laughters. Or so we think. Jacksione and Kimberlene, collectively, pushes the door open and walks out. The minute the door of the temple used its last breath and closed before the two, a shout of barrenness is left inside the temple. The prayers are said and the saint has departed, what else is left of the sanctuary’s legitimacy?~ o ~ o ~ o ~ A prophecy was said of this town that one day, a man would come and fill in the sadness of the people. The prophecy said that for five years, he would make himself known, obtain friends, and be the supreme being of the town he arrived in. He would bring an item, untouchable and mystical, that will lure the people into him. He will bring purpose in their lives just like how he found the purpose of his – this is his purpose. For five years, he would heal and bring contentment and fullness to the people, just like what God did. But one day, amidst the joy and mirth, on a very special day, that supreme being along with his miraculous item will meet his maker. And a more supreme being will be the one to show him the darkness. After five years of arriving to this town, he will finally abscond the people and die in the hands of the villain. The villain will come out of nowhere. A sudden bolt of Zeus’ thunder will come through this villain and suddenly eradicate the healer and the jester, who the people love, just like a typical man unpredictably getting hypnotized by the devil. After five years of filling in the emptiness and giving hilarity, this man will die. But he will die peacefully and then he will not be empty, eternally, after his bereavement. The prophecy said that’s the only thing that matters. And then, his purpose is served at last. “Everything changes tonight,” Serik Erracles murmurs to himself as he opens the door of his diminutive house. Stepping out, the night air is bracing and the grass outside the house of Erracles is damp. He looks at the lifeless foliage and notices himself in it as if it was a mirror. He was alone and cold, waiting for the end of the world that seems to serve no purpose for him. But not tonight, he utters. Tonight is the night he will become a lifeless grass no more. He will become a merciless vulture that will eat all the inert grasses of the earth, or better yet the wild animals of the forest. Serik Erracles would not let himself be a weakling anymore. No, that’s not acceptable. Serik Erracles is more than that. Acknowledgment is in his overriding hands; all he has to do now is make it occur. Yes, tonight is the night, all right. Prophecy is nothing but an erratic prediction. Serik Erracles’ plan is precise and bona fide. He strides away from the grass of his front yard and to the important celebration. Oh yes, he’s a damp grass no more. ~ o ~o ~ o ~ “Are you done yet?” Jacksione Rusty snarls through the bathroom door while Kimberlene puts the final touches on her make-up inside. “We’re going to be late! What are you doing there that’s so important? Talking to King Elvis Presley?” The door finally opens in the relief of Jacksione after three hours of waiting, typical time for women to dress up. “Elvis Presley? What are you talking about? That’s not even a name, better yet a king,” Kimberlene responds with a confused look on her newly decorated face. “It’s a futuristic name.” “How do you know? You’re not even a prophet.” “I know that, but I have the Rising Star, don’t I?” Jacksione Rusty smirks toward Kimberlene as if there’s no problem in the world. “You’ve seen it heal, you’ve seen it make people’s lives better. And most importantly, you’ve seen it give me the most beautiful friend I can have in the world.” Jacksione moves his hand lightly, gradually pointing at Kimberlene. Kimberlene nonchalantly blushes and bows her head, a little beam forms in her face. “Uh, I’m talking about your sister.” Kimberlene lightly smacks Jacksione in his shoulders, just like how loving and close siblings treat each other. “I hate you,” Kimberlene fires up, knowing that she didn’t mean what she just said. “I know, I know.” Jacksione puts his arm around Kimberlene’s shoulders as the two walks toward door, afraid of arriving in the gathering late. Nothing is better than two beings together, for when you have someone, you will never be empty inside. “Wait a forgot something!” Jacksione searches for his golden treasure in his pockets but it isn’t there. He will never go anywhere without it. He takes his arm off Kimberlene’s shoulders and goes back to the closet in his room to look for it. It wasn’t there. He looked under his neat bed, wasn’t there either. All the around the house, the talisman is absent. “Did you see the Rising Star?!” Jacksione asks Kimberlene furiously like a dragon that cannot breath fire any longer. She jumps up in shock for Jacksione Rusty seldom raises his tone. “I – I didn’t t – took it…” she responded with reverence, aware of Jacksione Rusty’s personality without his most priced possession. “But there’s only the two of us in this house!” His voice is now empty in its intention, a sudden swing of mood indeed. “Who would take it?”
