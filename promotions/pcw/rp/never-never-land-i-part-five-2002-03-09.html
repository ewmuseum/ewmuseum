Never Never Land I: [Part Five]

9 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 9, 2002 A flight for those who can fly...CENTER> The wind blew softly to the east, but that didn’t stop Peter Pan from preparing to take off for one of his routine flys around Never Never Land. He used them to clear his mind, especially when he had a lot of things testing his patience. The entire Hook scenario had him worried, and staying at the base would only drive him crazy. As he tucked in his shirt, preparing to take flight, Tink fluttered into his view. “Peter, where do you think you’re going?” Tink demanded. She sparkled as she flew, making sure she stayed in his view. Pan turned away, not looking at Tink. “Don’t try to stop me, Tink. I’ve made up my mind.” “You can’t go on a flight now. There’s a war going on out there.” “Don’t you think I know that?” Pan snapped back. “But there’s nothing I can do about that now, is there? I have too many things on my mind to just sit here and wait for everyone to come back with their reports. I have more productive things to do.” “Like flying around the forest?” Tink made her way around in front of Pan’s face. “Don’t you see? We need you here.” “I am no use to everyone if I’m feeling down,” He answered back. “I’ll be back soon. Don’t worry.” “What do you expect me to do?” She replied. “Belly and Bat haven’t even come back from retrieving Bobby and Tom.” “I thought you sent out a couple of the boys to get them back?” “I did,” Tink said, with a sorrowful look on her face. “But they haven’t returned yet.” “Keep waiting,” Pan said, tucking his sword into his pants. “I’m sure they’ll show up.” “Don’t do this, Peter,” Tink said, almost demanding now. She stopped on his shoulder, tugging away at his shirt. Peter grabbed her and set her back on the ground. “Tink, I already told you I’m going. Stop this.” “But we need you here! The boys need you!” Peter sighed. “I’ll be back soon, don’t worry.” Peter took off just as quickly, before Tink could say anything. She just shook her head, knowing that he would be in trouble sooner or later. The sky was the limit for Pan as he soared higher and higher into the sky. It was a beautiful day, with only a few lingering clouds still making their way into the forest’s canopy. Peter loved to soar into them, being blanketed by the white storm. It was an exhilarating feeling to just be lost in a cloud, never looking back and never worrying about when you would come out. He felt bad inside, leaving Tink to fend over the base for herself. But he had little choice, as if he had stayed, he would have surely gone insane. Being the leader of the Lost boys had always been a game to him. A game he loved to play day in and day out, but it wasn’t a game anymore. It was becoming a job. Being a Lost boy always meant being a child forever. To never lose innocence, to have fun, to be a kid. Years of fighting Hook, fighting pirates…it’s all becoming too much. No one else can pick up the slack but Peter, though. He is the leader, and he must be there for them through thick and thin. But he would have to take breaks, clear his mind, and remember that he is still a person. Not superman, not a superhero, but a boy, still. It was with these thoughts that he soared over tree tops, bouncing gleefully on branches and tearing leaves off and throwing them into the sky. Watching them fall was like a green snow pilfering the ground. Peter let a smile cross his face, and suddenly he felt content once more. “Fly too high, and you get knocked down.” Pan stopped in mid-air suddenly, looking about him. He heard the voice, but he didn’t know who it was, or where it was coming from. It did, however, sound familiar. “Who’s there?” Pan asks. “Show yourself!” Just as soon as the voice appeared, it disappeared. Pan turned around, and comes face to face with Preston Pot, his mirror image. Pan has a stunned look on his face, and unsheathes his sword just as quickly. “Oh, Peter, put that away. I thought we were friends now,” Pot says, a sarcastic smile on his face. “I’ll never be friends with you, Preston. Not today, not tomorrow…not ever.” “Well, at least we know where we stand. That’s always important.” “You’re evil,” Peter replies, swiping wildly at Preston with his sword. “True, true,” Preston said with a snicker. “Then again, what did you expect? I mean, you were the one who looked into the Lake of Mirrors, weren’t you? You wanted a play friend, someone who could be your equal.” “I didn’t realize it would mirror the darkside of me,” Peter said sadly. “Yes, the worst parts of you are exemplified in me. Isn’t that beautiful? I always knew Never Never Land had a weird sense of humor, but none more humorous than that lake. Oh, the fun we will have, Peter.” “I’m not going anywhere with you, Preston. Do I have to remind you? You tried to kill me once.” “You’re still upset about that?” Preston mocked. “C’mon, it was only one attempt. You know that I couldn’t do it. You’re like a brother to me.” “You couldn’t kill me because if you did, you’d kill yourself in the process,” Peter pointed out. “That’s the whole point in being a mirror image. One doesn’t survive without the other.” “Does that bother you?” Preston asked. “Are you ashamed of the fact that we’re one in the same now?” “Maybe if you weren’t so different from me, I wouldn’t hate you so much,” Peter admitted. Preston made a face, as if he were shocked. “Hate me? You hate me? How could you say such a thing?” “I don’t have time for this,” Peter begins to say, flying off. “I have more important things on my mind.” “Like fighting off Hook, I presume,” Preston said, flying after Peter. “I know all about it.” “Stop following me,” Peter warns. “I have nothing to say to you.” “You’re going to have to lose me first.” Peter flies quickly, but Preston stays right behind. A flush shot right through the clouds, and Peter emerges hoping to be free of Preston, but he again is right behind. He curses under his breath, wondering what it will take to free himself of his alter ego. Peter takes refuge in the forest, darting into the overgrown trees. The leafy undergrowth offers some protection, but not if Preston continues to shadow his movements. Peter ducks into the shrubbery, watching Preston fly into a clearing, searching for him. “Where have you hidden yourself, Peter?” He asks, checking some bushes. “You know you can’t hide from me forever. We are one in the same, remember? I could feel your precense.” Peter knew it was true. Even he could feel Preston’s passion and anger. He didn’t want to stay to see how that would develop against him, however. “Peter, come out…I’m beginning to get angry…” Peter shot out from the bushes like he was shot out of a cannon, spearing Preston from behind. Preston yelps out, trying to get his sword up, but he cannot. Peter keeps him pinned against the ground. “Well, well, well…” Preston said, a smile on his face. “I suppose my brother does know something about stealth, after all.” “I’m a tricky fellow.” “As I’m beginning to find out. Why don’t you let me up, and we can finish this properly?” “No.” “No? That isn’t a good answer. And why not?” “What’s the point in fighting? We can’t kill each other. What would we be fighting for?” “Supremacy. That, and the fact that I just like beating the living hell out of you. I’m not leaving until we fight, Peter. That’s the truth.” Peter let him up, unsheathing his sword. “Then we fight, and you will leave.” Peter swung his sword towards Preston’s legs, which he quickly jumped over. As he lands, he ducks under and catches Peter with a leg sweep, knocking him on to his back. Peter avoids the sweeping sword thrust towards his chest, moving out of the way quickly. “Are you insane?” Peter exclaimed. “If you kill me, we both die!” “You can’t live life without some risk,” Preston replied with a dark smile. “Now, where were we?” Preston lunges forward again, but this time Peter blocks the thrust, taking Preston’s hand into his own and sending a kick directly into his gut. Preston loses a breath of air as Peter headbutts him back, causing Preston to drop his sword. Peter responds with a backhand, which causes Preston to fall to the ground in a dazed state. Just as he’s getting up, he finds Peter’s sword at his throat. “Very good, Peter. I see you haven’t lost a step.” “You will leave now. Leave me alone. We’ve had our fight.” “I cannot.” Peter looked frustrated. “You said we would fight, and you would leave. I have fulfilled my side of the bargain, fulfill yours.” “A battle is anything less than a bargain, dear brother. I have important information for you. Something that you need to know.” “Then tell me.” Preston shook his head. “I can’t tell you here. It’s not safe. Meet me in the Alps, up there…” Preston points to the towering mountains which were always surrounded by dark and sinister clouds. “Meet me there, and I’ll tell you everything.” Peter relinquished the sword on Preston’s throat. He got up and retrieved his weapon, and flew into the air towards the mountain. Peter watched on, unwilling to do the same. The mountains were dangerous, and always had cold, unbearable conditions. He hated to go up there, but it seemed he had no choice in the matter. He flew as well. It took close to ten minutes to make it to the top. Peter found himself shivering at the conditions as he searched for Preston. “Preston! Show yourself!” “I’m right here…” He watched Preston emerge from the snowy mountain. “Tell me what you have to say.” “It’s about your savior, Tom Lexian…” “I don’t know what you’re talking about.” “Save it, Peter. We all know you brought him back. But he’s in Hook’s hands now. He’s going to die.” “What?!” “That’s right, and this time tomorrow, if Lexian doesn’t save us, we’ll all be dead.” All locked up and nowhere to go... 'I can't believe I'm trapped here!' I moaned, pounding away at the door which imprisoned me. Here I was, the man everyone thought was a hero and a savior, and I was caught by pirates and being held against my will. Some hero, huh? 'Stop your crying,' Bobby said. 'We'll get out of here somehow.' 'Oh yeah,' I replied. 'And how, my I ask, are we going to do that? If you didn't notice, we're trapped here against our will. No one knows we're here, no one cares, and our only hope might be a couple of kids that have been children for centuries.' It sounds oddly depressing when you put it that way, but I didn't see a way around the obvious. Only a fool would get himself in a predicament like this. Who would have thought that the great Tom Lexian, Legend Inc. himself, would die in a make believe world? This is almost as bad as being beaten by Pharoah or Akiyama. Being banished by the void is fairly embarrassing by itself, but I was going to perish under the iron fist of a swashbuckling pirate. And a pirate with a hook for a hand, no less. Little red, riding hood - eat your heart out. 'You are so damn negative,' Bobby said, a little irritation in his voice. 'I'm a cactus, and I have more positivity than most humans I know. Want to know why that's so?' I made a sarcastic snort and put my hand on my hip. 'Why is that so?' 'Take your hand off your hip,' Bobby said. 'You're making me nervous.' I blew a kiss at Bobby, who retaliated with a flying spine near my crotchular area. I decided to stop playing. 'Being positive is one of the traits I find most appealing,' Bobby continued. 'I mean, why should the cup be half empty when it can be half full?' 'Stop thinking of your stomach,' I murmured. 'I don't have a stomach, remember?' I rubbed my eyes and slumped against the door. I could feel the slow rocking of the ship, back and forth, back and forth, which was odd because the ship wasn't even moving. What else do you expect from a world where tigers ribbit and frogs try to eat zebras? 'You don't look so good, Tom,' Bobby remarked. 'Those never berries must have hit you pretty hard.' 'I guess,' I moaned, watching the ceiling turn into blue spots. 'I need to get back to Tink so they can fix me.' 'Fix you? You're not a car. You're a human being.' I rolled my eyes. 'I doubt it matters in this world, Bobby. Here, we're as screwed as a nail in a board.' 'That's pretty damn lame.' Yes. Yes it was.
