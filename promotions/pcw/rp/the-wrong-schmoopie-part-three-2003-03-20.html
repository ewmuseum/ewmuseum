The Wrong Schmoopie (Part Three)

20 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 20/03/03 The following morning was one of agony for Lacuna Firenze Devries, agony both mental and physical as she was recovering from the mother of all hangovers. She didn’t remember much of the night before, except that Lacuna and Jade had been to a carnival, pissed of the carnies and got royally drunk to top it all off. She remembered Jade diving into a pool full of beer cans and crisp packets, followed by a sharp stab of emotion, one that still stabbed at her this fine morning. She rolled over in bed and covered her eyes with a tanned arm. The room had got very hot over night and the purple satin sheets had stuck to her slightly, as well as her neat black hair. The room was bright with the sunlight that shone through the windows invariably, lighting the dark shaded room with an unnatural glow. The clinical brightness hurt her eyes as she tried to avoid the inevitable waking process, prolonging sleep for a few more minutes. “Urgh!” She gave up trying to sleep and sat upwards, still dressed in the clothes from the night before which were stained in beer and crisp crumbs as well as creased to buggery which could not help but add to the morning after the night before look that she so unwillingly adopted at the moment. The bags underneath her eyes looked stretched and red as she rubbed at them with the side of her left hand, trying desperately to remove the sensation to lie back down and sleep. She threw off the dark covers and sat gingerly to her feet, which she swung about trying to find the cold laminate flooring. The throbbing coming from her head was like a jackhammer; loud and constant as she tried to find her bearings. The smell coming from across the room insinuated that breakfast had been served and a quick glance at the bright red display of her digital alarm clock on her oak bedside cupboard solidified the fact that it was past her usual breakfast time. The smell appeared to rouse her from her slumber and she made her way to her nimble feet and practically slid across the floor towards the large oak table in the far corner of the room. Next to the table and singular chair was a metal slide-shoot, which lacuna opened and found a large plate adorned with two smaller one coated to the rims in fried, greasy food. “Yay! Still warm!” she gesticulated as she grabbed hungrily at the large plate and scooped it and its contents out onto the table. Attached to the plate was a small post-it note, which Lacuna examined closely. Ma’am Lacuna, Work has begun on cleaning the mess left in the pool house, and it should be fully up to your standards of inspection but about 1500 hours. Your breakfast was sent up around 1100 hours, so as to give you a little lie in. Last night you sure knocked a bit back, if you don’t mind my saying so. Head Chef Brown Lacuna glanced at the red LED display again, this time actually noting its contents into her memory. Her breakfast had only been in the shoots for ten minutes, so she had arrived back into consciousness just in time for a good meal, punctuality had always been her prized asset. She picked up the knife and fork, examining the large meal out of the corner of her hazel eyes, promptly throwing up all over the wooden flooring. A few minutes later, after the retching had finished, she was feeling on hell of a lot better as well as thinking clearly now, so she went back to bed. Lying open by the side of her luxurious four poster bed was one of her most prized possessions, and the thing that could so easily kill her; her diary. March nineteenth, two thousand and three. You will have to excuse me today, diary, but I have a lot to get off my chest, as it where. I have been doing a lot of thinking and I feel that I need to spill it out to someone, anyone, anything. Today I realised that everything I hold so close to my heart is wrong; false and plain offensive. My morals are mislead, my truths are plain lies and my heart is the biggest torture device in human existence. It didn’t really take much thinking to realise this; I just had to look at it from a different angle, that of a lesser-privileged American called Jade, to be precise. Yes, I have been mean to this little evil I house for free, yet she has taught me much more than I could have learnt from any scholar of life. She showed me what it was to have fun. It had been a long time coming, really. Too long had been spent in my own personal prison of isolation, my self-derived morals had pointed me away from what I have always strived to reach. That pinnacle had been moving further away through no conscious fault of mine own. I suppose it was my fault from the beginning, that early loss had caused much of the pain in my life. Maybe if I hadn’t bottled it up to such an extent, maybe if they hadn’t even have died. It all would have been different, and so would I. The extent into which I punished myself from their deaths was inexcusable, and it sanctioned me into the small section of society that people call lunatics. From this, my money was my redeemer. It helped with my process of self-isolation and I grew independent to the core meaning of the word. Maybe this, as apposed to the loss of my parents was the problem, or maybe a symptom of the earlier problem. I’m just rambling now. Where was I? Oh, yes. My point was that I have brought it all upon myself, through fault of mine own or of others. Whichever way it happened, it hardly matters. I have seen the path that would undo all of the errors of my life so far and it comes in the form of one of the least likely objects I could imagine. That little girl I was stuck with via the fool of a mutual acquaintance, Sorrow. That little girl called Jade. Jade has shown me much more than I could have ever imagined. More than how little my life of seclusion actually means to me; so much more. She has shown me how to have fun, without really bothered about the whole ‘moral’ story any more. She has shown me that petty revenge is useless and that we should love for the moment. She has shown me the error of my ways. But what she doesn’t realise she has shown me is that there are much more hideous ways of revenge than actually plotting it. She doesn’t share my hatred for Storm and she doesn’t feel the requisite that we should actually teach him a lesson for the early exit from the tag team tournament in Primetime Central. But she did show me that Storm is not the only target I could consider. I should not be upset about facing Tessa Windsor, with her petty little squabbles over those little brats, I should focus on my desires. Why bother about ‘The Wrong Schmoopie’ when I can make one pay so much that it hurts the other? Jade has helped me realise the bond between friends, and how much it hurts when that bond is threatened. I can hurt that bond, and my revenge will be fulfilled. I need not wait any longer as the revenge factor is right in front of me. Lacuna Firenze Devries. It’s a pity she didn’t remember writing any of that, because she had a mission to fulfil, and she was determined that it would be done. Who was it that said ‘The blood of one is equal to that of another’? Or did she just make it up?
