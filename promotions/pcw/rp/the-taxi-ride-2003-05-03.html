The Taxi Ride

3 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5 - 3 - 03 Coming down in flowing silver sheets, the rain is like an ever-wafting set of curtains, waving in the maniacal winds accompanying the thunderstorm. The blackened skies rumble as violent streaks of lightning strike the land below, splitting trees and destroying entire city blocks worth of power. Bystanders in the drenched city of darkness scurry about like frantic mice, trying to escape the wrath of Ar’Katan’s thunderous call. A man in particular is scurrying about faster than the others, wanting to feel the wrath of the God of Being no more. Holding his leather trench coat tight around his body, the man keeps his gaze low to avoid possible blinding from the rain and trudges through the enlarging puddles of water to the curb, gazing into the fluid chaos of the city traffic. Vehicle upon vehicle rushes by, nothing more than a mere silver blur storming through the living wall of water. Gripping his coat with one hand, the man raises his other to the dark heavens, flagging down anything that will pay a passing glance. Though it takes a minute or so, two beaming eyes burst through the elements and a yellow taxicab pulls up to the curb. The man flings the door open and quickly gets in, slamming the door behind him. He rolls the window down slightly to ring his long black hair out, then rolls it back up and lifts his gaze to the driver… the rider in the back is ‘The Astronomer’ Dorn Tracer. ‘Thanks,’ He says, relief ringing forth through his rather stern tone. The cab driver, an Italian-American wearing a funny-checkered hat, nods and looks back at the Astronomer. ‘Where to, pal?’ The Television Champion flings his coat open, revealing the lustrous title within, and stares out the adjacent, as if the answer lies beyond the pouring rain. ‘Would it be a problem to pick someone I know up?’ ‘Not at all, where’s he at?’ The driver asks, his Italian accent amusing the Astronomer. For being such a grease ball, he keeps quite a clean cab. It even smells nice. ‘He should be waiting outside the First Methodist Church across town. Make haste, please, he doesn’t like to wait…’ Dorn’s voice trails off as the sentence ends and the driver nods, turning back to the road before him. The Astronomer can feel the vehicle rumble and jerk forward slightly as their trek towards the church begins. Dorn merely spaces out, if you will, and stares out the window. A blur of dismal blacks and grays passes him by each second, like a painting being sprayed with thinner through a high-powered hose. None of the pedestrians are apparent, but Dorn doesn’t care. His mind is elsewhere, and he cannot be concerned with the blasphemous populace. I know he’s out there… Those words pierce Tracer’s mind like a seething blade, cutting into his very emotions and bringing forth a swelling feeling of rage. Dorn of course means the mysterious Druid, and the very mention of the troublesome being causes the Astronomer to clench his fist tightly, turning his rough knuckles white. He presses his other fist up against the underside of his chin, as if to suspend his heavily weighed head. He’s watching me, waiting for me. Waiting for me to make myself apparent. Waiting for me to lower my guard. Woe unto you, you shit head infidel… Dorn Tracer is never unprepared. A grin crosses Dorn’s face as that thought cycles itself through his mind, but the satisfying thought is shattered like cracked glass by the annoying sound of the driver’s force. ‘You look kinda familiar. You from around here?’ The blatant stupidity of the question baffled Dorn, and if that wasn’t bad enough, the driver continued. ‘You look like one of dem… uh… dem wrestling guys from the look of dat belt on your waist.’ The driver gazes at Dorn through the rear view mirror, giving him an inquiring look. Though irritated, Dorn decides to be civil with his transport, ‘That I am. Maybe you’ve heard of me?’ ‘Nah, I don’t watch wrestling much,’ The Italian man replies, with a hint of a chuckle in his voice. ‘Never been a big fan of men touching each other like dat.’ Though that comment spurs enough rage in Dorn that the Astronomer could KILL the driver, he manages to control his temper like Titu and Bruce have been telling him to do and merely smirks, ‘Trust me, it’s nothing like THAT. We’re doing battle with each other. In my case, I’m just vying for respect.’ ‘Well, you ain’t gonna get no respect doin’ DAT, man!’ Then the driver lets out a large, hearty laugh… while the Astronomer just smirks. Though he knows he probably should, Tracer could care less about his opinion. After all, he’s just some fat slovenly Italian working for the taxi company… Dorn thinks with a grin. His thoughts are suddenly interrupted as the rumbling of the engine begins to die down and the vehicle comes to a halt. Tracer looks outside and only the wall of rain is visible. The taxi driver in the front waits for a moment before turning around and giving Dorn a puzzled look. ‘You sure he’s here?’ The Astronomer gives an acknowledging nod and almost as if that was a cue, a cloaked figure bursts through the rain, flings the backseat door open, and drops down in the seat next to Dorn. ‘Drive…’ He commands, in a deep, menacing voice. Obviously not one to argue with his customers’ demands, the taxi driver lets off the brake and just begins to drive, not knowing where he’s going. The man in the cloak lowers his head and reveals himself to be the Baron of Brutality: Violence Jack. ‘Hello, Bruce,’ Dorn’s voice is low and distance, as the Astronomer doesn’t even look at his partner. ‘How’d it go?’ Violence Jack gives the taxi driver a wondering look before turning his attention to Dorn, ‘I couldn’t burn the altar because it was made of stone, so I had to go with dismembering their big statue of Jesus.’ ‘Damned altars never seem to burn anymore…’ Dorn exclaims with a roll of his eyes. ‘I wasn’t able to find any explosives, so I doubt we can blow up the Second Baptist across town.’ At this point, the taxi driver’s eyes wander from the road to the rear view mirror with a look of shock gracing his face. What the hell… He thinks in a panic, thinking he’s harboring terrorists. In a way, he’s right. ‘Why explosives, though? We’d have priest giblets everywhere,’ Violence Jack states calmly, seeming WAY too comfortable for this. His partner, Dorn Tracer, doesn’t seem to have a problem with any of this either. The Astronomer folds his arms across his chest, casting a shadow over the glowing TV Title, and shakes his head, ‘I know, but it would have been one crazy light show. Besides, Tiamats willing, the Druid could be in the building at the time…’ ‘Still stuck on the Druid, I see?’ The Dark Bringer inquires with a little grin on his face, trying not to be amused. The pissed look on Dorn’s face only brings forth a bigger grin from Violence Jack as the Baron of Brutality tries to compose himself. ‘You’re STILL stuck on the Druid… Dorn, you’ve got other things to worry about. We have a match against Totally Tubular Tommy on Tuesday, and it’s our to teach the blasphemous bastards around us a lesson. Give the damned Druid a rest.’ ‘NEVER!’ The Astronomer cries out in a fit of righteous fury. ‘That Druid has made my life a living black hole for WAY too long. I can’t stand walking into the arena and constantly looking over my shoulder, Bruce! The first chance I get, I WILL destroy the Druid!’ ‘The Druid?’ The taxi driver chimes in, seemingly interested. Both Dorn Tracer and Violence Jack shoot him an evil look, so he just turns back to his driving. Dorn turns his gaze to the outside and gives a long, deep sigh. Violence Jack just folds his arms across his chest and stares at the floor… a puzzled look on his face… The Druid… I wonder if he’ll interfere against our dismantling of TTT on Tuesday… well, if he does… Woe unto him. [End.]
