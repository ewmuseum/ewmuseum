Shadows Within

10 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7-10-02 'What am I; a criminal? A villan? A… monster?' I waited, but the voice never came. My conscience did not arise, and I was left without a voice of reason, left to sulk in my own thoughts. Somewhere deep, deep inside of me, I longed for the pleasant feel of assurance. I wanted to be told over and over again that I was worthwhile, that I was powerful, and that I would succeed. I would never be told so again. Suddenly I realized that the old saying is true. You do not know how much you miss something until it goes away. The old Trigger was now gone. The good Trigger was gone, dead for all I knew. And now this burden pressed against me like a boulder pushing me to the ground. 'What have I done?' All at once I knew. The old Trigger was never coming back. Never again would I be good; never again would I be pure. Forever now I would be marked shamefully as a pity, a lost cause. I needed no voice to tell me. I wasn't worthwhile. I wasn't anything. I was just a warrior with a dream and no power to execute it. Sure, I was PCW Rising Star Champion. I had won some matches, I had lost some. I had gained respect, and quickly lost it. All glory is fleeting. Was I so wrong to desire the coveted spotlight that generously frolics over my foes? Was I so wrong? Yes. Maybe for the common man this behavior could be acceptable, but not for me. Not for a warrior. 'What is it that I truly want?' I asked myself for the thousandth time. And for the thousandth time, I had no answer. Was there anything that had ever been pure in my life? Not my trouble-ridden childhood. Not my sorry attempt at a wrestling career. Not my relationships… or so I supposed. But the more I thought about it, the more I realized, there had been one relationship--one out of the many--that had made a pure impact upon my heart, one that would remain there as long as I lived. The WITs. They were nothing at first. Rookies that needed training. Children that needed a way. But soon, they became much, much more. I knew soon after the teaching began that one day these Warriors In Training would carry on my legacy. Inside each of their eyes, I could see myself, fighting viciously towards a perfect world. They would carry it out. I knew they would. Even if it wasn't them personally… they would train, and generation after generation would view my vision, and do their part to push the ways of the warrior. I blinked. I shuddered. I turned my head away. As if these actions could truly distract me from my thoughts and my feelings. But I had to try. I couldn't let myself think these thoughts and feel these feelings because I knew that my aspirations would forever remain in my head. The WITs were gone. They were never coming back. Was that a tear that dripped down my eye? Yes. A single tear, signifying a lifetime of pain and suffering. A single tear telling me that there would be no glorious future. There would be no generations of warriors carrying my dream into reality. There would be no Trigger. There was no Trigger. What was I? Not an Infinite Warrior. Not a wrestling superstar. Was I ever either one? It was true. I was a monster. A sadistic creature bent on havoc, unsure even of his own path. What was happening to me? I just wanted things to go back to the way they were. I just wanted to be a warrior again! This life, it was not worth living. Anyone who ever had hope in me made a terrible mistake, as did I for carrying on so long. I had turned myself into this animal. I had done it by choice. There was no going back. I was sick of being the good guy, always looking for the decent route to take. Always showing respect, and getting none in return. I wanted more. I had brought this plague upon myself, and it was I who had to cure it. It was me who brutally beat on Kris Burden after our match at Gold & Glory. It was me who left the side that counted--the side of the fans--and asserted my attention to the respect that I felt I so very deserved. I didn't deserve any of it. All lies I told myself to hide the horrid truth. I was a monster. Deep down, I always had been. It was only a matter of time before that side of me surfaced for the world to see. I was a symbol of the treachery that lies down the road of life. I could never forgive myself, because… there was nothing to forgive. 'I was what I am. I am what I am. This can never change…' It was the truth. The cold, hard truth. So why did shame not sweep over me once again? Was I in too deep? Was this the last straw? No. I was not shamed because… because… because I was a savage beast--and I loved it!
