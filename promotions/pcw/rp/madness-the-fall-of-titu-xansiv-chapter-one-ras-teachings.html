Madness: The Fall Of Titu Xansiv – Chapter One: Ra’s Teachings…

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Monday, 24th of March, 2003 Ra tells me what to do. He considers my future and directs it as he sees fit. He is all-powerful and he knows what’s best for me. I trust Ra. I don’t trust Set, however. No one – no thing – can trust Set. When one contemplates the ancient scrolls, it was he who killed the living form of Osiris and sliced him up, casting forth his parts into the Nile and thereabout. With Ra you can put faith in what he says, in what he does, in what he commands. He sees me and tells me, though no one else aside from I can see him, what I should do upon this cesspool they call Earth. He tells me now that this world is filled with impurities; filled with lies; filled with falsehoods. What I must do for Ra, and for Horus, and even for Sekh-met, is purify this wretched gutter, and bring about some kind of balance. Just like the heart against the feather, there must be balance. Balance is what keeps the world together… didn’t you know that? Balance is what is needed. Indeed, much must be changed here to suffice the Gods. For, you see, the Gods are ever desiring… Shrouded in darkness, the temple lies silent. He sits, the shadow consuming him, as he prays. All he can do now is pray. Pray to his Gods for forgiveness. But gaining it, acquiring that element of pardon, will be harder than this man thinks. After what happened earlier today his life could be over within moments, his Gods will be difficult to convince after all. He knows now more than ever that his Gods, though they have always acted ever faithful to him, are mad. He fears them. Look at him now, shivering, a spiteful chill running down his quivering spine. What they have in store for this being is unknown, but surely…surely after something as despicable an act as that they cannot look upon him gleefully, as they usually do. “Tell me, Titu, tell me what you believed you were doing that night.” I didn’t know what I was doing. Blinded by madness, I drew forth my arm and pelted the beast with what I thought a tempting devise. “I don’t know…” His voice is brittle, scared… mournful. True, he didn’t know what he was doing that night, but he still did it, didn’t he? He still went out there and announced to the world in general how he would put the lives of hundreds on the line in a pitiful bout. What was the point in that? He thought of himself as a messiah? A man who could overcome evil and save the inevitable deaths of these men, women and children. Remember now that the first-born were not all children. Some were grown beings with families of their own, and they too died that horrific night. “You don’t know? Hardly a sufficient excuse, my dear mortal. …Hardly a decent excuse at all. Please, feel free to consider the effects of that act…that stupendous act you brought about when you decided to go forth and challenge the devil. Please…feel free to consider it for a long, long time.” I did it because I love my country. I did it because I love my people. Can’t you remember when I came to you days beforehand and asked your immortal opinion on what I should do? Hardly helpful then, were you? Cast your mind back, Ra, and think about it. “You don’t remember what I did even before the act though, do you? Are you so—” “Silence! How dare you even try to bring me into this! This is your fault! Those people died because of you! No one else can be blamed other than you!” He sits silent for a moment. His mind is confused now. He doesn’t quite know what to think. Would you if your Gods had done this to you? If your Gods had exiled you in such a manner. How they look at you now, eh? How they test your ever-faltering mentality now. They aren’t as brilliant as they once were now, mortal. Are they? …Are they? “He would have killed them anyway.” “Passing the blame shan’t help you now.” The voice of Ra rumbles menacingly… “But he would have nevertheless. T’is the final plague, my lord. Remember?” How can they remember anything you do? They don’t care. They don’t feel sympathy for you anymore. You just killed hundreds, and you know you did, don’t you? They won’t help you now and when you think about it…they never did. Why do they test me so? Is this a test? Or are they really angered by my deeds. I tried, why can’t they see that? Why can’t they? “I beg of you, milord: let me try again! I’ll bring about your ways this time, I swear! The people listen to me! But I have a new approach! Please…I beg of you…” …Silence. Ever noticed how terrifying it is at times? When you wait in it for so long, the mind can falter beyond repair. That’s exactly what was happening now. The Egyptian ‘warrior’ waits patiently, no other sound filling that gawking gap in the expanse of reality. He waits, unsure of what to think. Some would call him insane…and perhaps he is…but without the words of Ra and the Others to protect him he is afraid. Afraid mainly of what they are thinking, but fearful mostly of their current opinion of him… “My God?” he murmurs after some time. Are you there anymore? You haven’t left me, have you? …In this shadow, in this world of doubt… “Yes?” The voice is sudden, almost shocking. The freshness of it ruptures feelings in the soul unknown to most non-believers. He frowns a little, upset by the lack of interest present in the tone, and goes on, questioning as he sees fit… “You heard me, didn’t you? I asked—” “Yes…we heard you. Give us time to think, mortal Titu.” But there is no time here. What do you mean? Can’t you see that you contradict yourself all the time? Why are you doing this to me? It tortures me so much to see you in this state…angered, displeasured…disappointed… He thinks back slowly as he waits…to the events that shaped this meeting, to the things that aided this all to take place, and he sighs, shaking his tired head to and fro… ~***~ “He won’t be able to resist, Ra! He won’t see it coming; he’ll be blinded by his own sick perversion!” Said Titu, looking up hopefully at the shimmering golden light before him. Ra quivered, shining brightly. This was but one of his numerous forms. Sometimes he would come to Titu in a humanoid form, other times he would appear like this, a brightly coloured neon glow. “And you believe this will bring down his defences enough for you to strike, gaining the victory and securing the world’s safety?” He questioned, moving vaguely from side to side. Xansiv nodded, ‘Indeed! Sick beyond belief, is what Clyde is; and believe me, he won’t be able to oppose this much of an opportunity. With his confidence up and the stakes high, he’ll be blinded by his own desires! I can’t lose!” There was silence for a moment, Ra deliberating the possibilities. “So many people could die, Titu. To go through with this could mean the imminent destruction of countless innocents,” said Ra doubtfully, “But if you wish to put so much on the line, fine. Don’t get them killed, is all I ask,” He paused, “Is all the Gods ask.” “You have my word, milord!” Titu declared proudly, before leaving the temple, a strange assurance in his voice. ~***~ You don’t remember that, do you? You gave me the right to do as I wished. T’was you, Ra, who handed me those lives, and bestowed upon my shoulders the divine permission to put them on the line. But you are mad at me, milord? Confusion is a terrible thing. And confusion is exactly what Titu Xansiv was full of right now. He didn’t understand how Ra could treat him like this. After so many years of following his Gods’s ways he didn’t know how to come to terms with such a new kind of encounter. This new disgust for him was not something he had learnt to comprehend. He never thought he’d have to… “A decision has been made, Titu.” The voice took the Egyptian by surprise; he wasn’t expecting it when it thundered out through the catacombs of his Anaheim mansion, after all. “What…what have you decided, oh great Ra?” His voice, he noticed with some shock, was more so a plea than a monotone of authority. He wasn’t used to this either. Throughout his life, he’d always been the one in ‘charge’, as it were, not the one under pressure. The Gods, he then realized, were the only ones with this ability, to make him weak at the knees. “Why, to give you another chance, Xansiv!” Ra scoffed, “Prove yourself, and remember this teaching with much awe. We know you feel fear, and that is only a natural reaction, my dear mortal, so take it not as an insult. Take this lesson only as what it should be…a teaching…” A moment of awkward silence ensued. “I am…forgiven?” Another minute of calm… “No. But you have been granted a second chance. Do not take it lightly, however, Titu for if you do not show us your faith soon you will receive worse a fate than to be shunned for a few weeks by your loved deities. Now leave this sanctuary and go. You are required to take a leave of absence for a few days to recuperate your mind.” The room darkened a little, a sign that the Gods had left him. They’ve gone now. But whether they have indeed pardoned me is beyond even my thought. I can only pray, as usual; t’is the only thing one can do in this sort of a situation. All that remained now was the dull light the torches barely gave off and the tedious silence Titu had learnt to hate so much over the years. At length, he rose from his crouched position upon the cold, stone ground and walked away, glad that his fate had been given more time to progress, and that his Gods had forgiven him, even if it was only for the moment… What he wondered now was how long this moment would last…
