The Flashbacks [Chapter Three] - Returning home

23 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 23.6.02 “Well that went better than expected!” exclaimed Pharaoh with a distant smile, a far off twinkle in his eyes. Isiy looked at him doubtfully. “So what happens if they come looking for you?” Pharaoh froze and pondered this thought; after all it was one that certainly needed deep, deep contemplation. “Well,” he began…and then gave up. It was true that some people did this sort of thing all the time. They’d plan something spectacular and amazing, such as an escape from a hospital, and then afterwards, when they actually came to thinking about the possible consequences of this action, they would simply sigh in bitterness and then walk slowly, with they’re head hung, to a bridge and jump off, mainly because the human brain has a tendency to rattle until a problem as nagging as a consequence is solved. The thing was, people didn’t always have the time to think so much, and so suicide seemed like the best option. Thankfully Titu Xansiv wasn’t like any normal person, he thought things through a little differently. He simply shrugged his shoulders and continued to walk slowly down an unknown street towards an unknown destination that Isiy promised him was the site of their new home “Ah!” he said, finally realizing a possible comeback against Isiy’s ignorance towards the ‘cunning’ escape. “They don’t know where our new home is! So therefore they’ll never find us…” Pharaoh grinned smugly. He was well known for his overwhelming arrogance, but then again, many people in the wrestling industry were. “Good point; I don’t think I told them where the new house was.” said Isiy thoughtfully. “Exactly! So we’re safe to go about our everyday life, my dear,” He grinned again, extremely over the border of smugness. “Praying to Horus will never have felt so good!” he stated with stars in his eyes. “If you ‘say’ so…” said Isiy wearily. Titu picked up the tone in her voice and turned to her, slant eyed. “What was with that tone of voice?” “What do you mean?” “Just then! You said that as though you didn’t want to pray to our great and illustrious Gods!” “Oh I do,” Isiy began, “It’s just that I’ve been at that boring hospital for so long and I’m pretty tired …ya’ know what I mean?” Pharaoh smiled caringly. No longer did he suspect that his gorgeous wife had turned her back on Horus, and Imhotep, and Ra, and above all others, Amun-Ra, whom Ra turned into every now and again, only really when he felt like it. “I’m sure that when we get home the both of us will need our rest, especially me! I feel as though the migraines may come back…they are banished from my head for now, but I fear that they will be back and with a substantial amount of unadulterated vengeance.” Isiy looked at her husband ironically. “Well it was you who wished to be free of the hospital, so don’t complain if they start coming back…” Pharaoh threw up his arm, as though deflecting his wife’s words, brushing them away with ease and a dangerously high amount of stubbornness. “I needed out of that- that-“ He tried for a word to capture the true feelings he had for the hospital. “-That place of …evil!” he finished lamely. Isiy sighed. “It was no a place of evil, Titu, it was a place of heeling…” Once again Pharaoh threw up his arm in protest, figuratively knocking the words out of the air. “And have ‘I’ heeled since I arrive at that place? No! I haven’t, I missed home, I missed you, and I missed the ring! I need my home to get better, Isiy…and that’s the truth.” “Ok…” Isiy said at length. She grabbed his hand and held it tightly as they continued down the street towards there new home. Pharaoh hoped it would be some sort of great palace, formed from the finest marble in the world. He hoped for a great garden of tropical life, perhaps with a miniature River Nile flowing through it. And most of all he hoped for a brilliant temple where he could pray to the Gods. He was most disappointed when he found his new home to be a mere mansion… “Mr Xansiv? It’s time for your medication, sir…” There was no answer from behind the tall, looming white door. “Sir?” the nurse repeated, her voice trailing off. She opened the door and found a bed, unmade and messed up within the confines of an empty room filled only with the sound of dull silence. “Shit!” she stated as she speedily left the room. The Pharaoh peered into the bright light of the hallway. It was well decorated and up to his standards of pure excellence. He smiled, but deep down he still wished the new home had been designed at least to resemble a temple. “Excellent work, Isiy!” he said with a pleasant smile that made Isiy’s spine tingle. She blushed. “I gave PCW the new house number, so they know it in case they need you for anything.” The metaphorical light bulb shone brightly above Pharaoh’s head. “That reminds me!” He slowly walked towards a small table upon which he found a modern silver telephone. He picked up the receiver and placed it close to his ear. Isiy followed him, puzzled, and placed her hands on her hips in a fashion that only a women who had a vague feeling she knew what the love of her life was doing would do. “I’ll hope you’re not doing what I think you’re doing…” “I’m just giving PCW-” “No you are not!” Isiy said, with a tone of authority. “But-” “No ‘buts’, Titu! You need to fully recover first!” “But I feel fine! The doctor upped my medication, and I feel great.” Pharaoh protested, even going to the lengths of performing a little jig. Isiy stood, obviously not impressed by her husbands antics. “Titu, doing some sort of childish dance isn’t going to prove that you’re fit and well.” She said flatly. Pharaoh sagged. “Now come on, I’ll get you up stairs to your bed-” Pharaoh raised a hand to his wife’s face and smiled sarcastically. “Isiy, I am NOT going to bed when I feel perfectly fine. Alright, I agree about not going back into the wrestling industry just yet, but I hate to spoil your fun in saying ‘I am not going to bed when I feel perfectly fine!’” Isiy sighed. “Fine then. Go into the lounge and watch TV, like you usually do.” Pharaoh’s brow wrinkled in shock. “Excuse me, but I hardly even watch TV, and you damned well know that! I’d much rather sit down with a good book whilst listening to some Black Sabbath!” “And that music is going to do your head no good, either!” Pharaoh shook his head and then laughed, not a laugh of humour but a laugh of annoyance, as though someone had just gotten the better of him and he couldn’t take it, in this case it was Isiy, and as usual he wasn’t going to continue to fight a battle he couldn’t win, especially when it was against the wife. “Fine,” he said. “I’ll be heading off to bed then…” Pharaoh put the receiver back down upon the telephone’s charger and walked up a staircase towards wherever in the hell his bedroom was. Isiy watched, arms folded over her amply sized bosom waiting for Pharaoh to ask where the bedroom was… “Errr,” he began as Isiy looked on with a pleased smile. “Where is our bedroom?” After Isiy left the room Pharaoh laid his head down upon the pillow of his new bed and found that he couldn’t sleep. His mind was frowning smoothly again and the figurative cogs and mechanisms of the Egyptian’s brain were turning with thoughts of returning to PCW. He had to reach the phone, but how? Isiy had told him to stay up here. “I must get to that damned phone!” he said faintly. He turned, swung his legs out of bed before the rest of his body swiftly followed them. After he’d arrived home he’d got dressed into something a little more traditional for a man of his sort. He now wore a long white loincloth and a selection of golden rings and bangles. His long hair ran smoothly down his dark back as he tiptoed towards the edge of his room. He pressed his ear upon against the door. “I can hear nothing from outside.” He stated, as though addressing a group of people. He slowly reached down for the door handle and turned it in a clockwise motion. There was a loud click as the door opened. Pharaoh flinched at the noise, for it may have given him away to Isiy. He waited a moment, frozen in a sort of ninja attacking position, before he felt that his wife had not heard the door open. He breathed a sigh of relief. “Excellent!” Isiy sat in the new houses lounge watching TV. She was perched upon one of the brand new white leather sofas and, fortunately for Titu, didn’t see the shadowy figure of her husband pass in front of the open door behind her. She did however notice that, on the promo channel for PCW, it was clearly stated that Clyde had once again struck another unsuspecting PCW wrestler in the form of The Supreme Machine. Pharaoh crept quietly towards the phone. It was right in front of him now. He reached down and was about to pick up the receiver when it rang. He panicked and looked around frantically. The sound of his wife’s feet upon the well-polished wooden floor of the lounge echoed throughout his skull. He spotted a small door just a few metres away from him; seemingly it led to a place where shoes and jackets could be conveniently hidden away from prying eyes. He ran towards the door, opened it, and dived inside performing a forward roll just as Isiy casually walked into the hallway ready to answer the phone. “Hello?” he heard her say. There was a pause. “Well I don’t know. He sounded as though he wanted to return.” She stated hesitantly. There was another pause. “Well I’ll ask him and we’ll call you-” It was PCW! Pharaoh was not going to let this opportunity pass him by! He lunged out of the door shoulder first and leapt to his feet. Isiy stood mortified. Titu looked at her, a little embarrassed and then grabbed the phone out of her hands. “Yes, hello? Titu Xansiv here…” he said. He could feel the laser-like eyes of Isiy penetrating him straight through the back of his head. There was a pause. Isiy placed her hands upon her waist and waited while The Pharaoh finished his conversation… “Duzza?” He said… There was a pause. “Well yes, of course…” He said Another pause. “Memphis? Well- well yes, that’ll be fine…” Yet again there was another pause as the person on the other end of the phone spoke. “Brilliant, yes, I’ll see you then…” Pause. “Alright, thank you. Goodbye!” Isiy looked at him in anger as he put down the telephone receiver. “What the heck are you playing at?” she said sternly, “You haven’t even recovered fully and already you’re going, where? Memphis was it?” Pharaoh shuffled his feet in embarrassment. “Yeah…” Isiy sighed in annoyance. “And who are you fighting? Did I hear the name…Duzza?” “Yes…but that’s nothing that I can’t handle, Duzza is nothing in comparison-” “Just shut up! I can’t believe you sometimes!” Titu suddenly began to feel a growing pain in his forehead. He shuddered and then fell to his knees. “Titu?” He began to cry out in pain as Isiy rushed towards him. “Is it the migraines?” “I-I don’t know?” he replied in distress. Inside Pharaoh’s skull he was seeing things that you were only meant to see in dreams. In front of his eyes there was a man, his words could not be made out, but he continued to point towards a cavern of some sort. Ancient hieroglyphics writhed upon its outside. He smiled and then began to walk towards it. A tall young man, in his late teens, followed after him. His face could not be made out but Titu had the distinct feeling that he knew whom he was. At length it faded, along with the distressing pain. “Its alright, there, there…” said Isiy smoothly. Titu raised his head out of his wife’s lap and shook his skull to and fro. “The third…” he whispered… “What do you mean?” “Oh, nothing for you to worry about, my precious. I think I’ll just go and get a little bit of rest before I set out for… Memphis…” Isiy sighed. “Well why not just cancel your booking? So you can get some more rest? The headaches are-” Pharaoh interrupted, “No, no, I’ll be fine…honestly!” He got up off the ground and trundled back up the staircase to his room. What were these strange dreams about? What did they mean? And why the hell did he feel as though he knew the people he saw in them? Answers were needed… He’d find them soon enough. “Gone?” exclaimed the doctor, in puzzlement. “But how? And where?” “Well possibly back to Cairo…” informed the nurse. The doctor deliberated carefully, rubbing his hand across his chin in a thoughtful way. “So what are we going to do?” asked the nurse, breaking the silence that had enveloped the room. “What can we do?” exclaimed the Doctor. He shuffled across the room that had, three or four hours ago, belonged to Titu Xansiv. Something twinkled in the light. He moved towards it in wonder. He knelt down and picked it up. “What on earth?” The second nurse hesitated to answer and then said: “Y-yes, doctor?” “It seems that Mr Xansiv has left something behind.” The doctor held up a tiny diamond in the shape of a heart. “Nurse, if Mr Xansiv were to arrive back looking for this, send him straight to my office, would you?” “Yes, doctor.” The good doctor stared at the diamond in his hand and smiled in disbelief. It was such a beautiful little thing. It must have fallen from the bag Isiy had prepared for Titu before they left the hospital. Something so amazingly well crafted and surly expensive was something nobody would have purposely left behind. They’d be back, he though. And he was to be right in saying this. There had always been something strange about Titu’s life, he could never quite put his finger on it. He knew he was a Pharaoh from the distant past, who had ruthlessly kept Egypt in his stranglehold of…well not terror, but…well…sternness. Yes! sternness was a good word for the way he’d handled the responsibilities of being Pharaoh. All through his life had had lived on a dream, a dream to become the best at whatever he could do but there always seemed to be trouble ahead, blocking him from his goal. He had a tendency to attract trouble. He never quite knew why, but he did. Many questions had popped into his head when he asked himself that. It was like a web being untangled. ‘Why do I attract trouble?’ had lead onto ‘Is it the way I talk?’ and ‘Is it the way I dress?’ not to mention many more, but he never could quite place his index digit upon the problem. It all seemed too much hassle to go through. All the questions that spiralled out of the previous one, bah! There were far too many. And truth be told he had neither the patience nor the time to ponder every one of life’s little problems. He lay there, upon the bed and thought; pondering on the only questions he had to really think about for now. This coming Tuesday he’d be back in the squared circle and he’d be facing off against Duzza. Duzza wasn’t exactly an impressive warrior, but he wasn’t exactly an unimpressive one either. Pharaoh needed to think of a way to tackle him. But that wouldn’t be that hard. His array of insane high flying skills and patient technical wrestling would see him through. All he really had to do was bide his time in the match and then when Duzza was most vulnerable…strike! Like the perpetual rattlesnake. And thus was the end of that problem. The Pharaoh had other things to think about for now though. Like getting to Memphis for Turmoil. That was the main thing prying on his mind at the moment. Hopefully Isiy had purchased some sort of transportation. If she hadn’t then he didn’t know what he’d do. He laughed to himself. It wouldn’t be that bad if she hadn’t though, he’d quite enjoy a trip to the car garage to buy a new vehicle. He didn’t, however, know of a problem that would soon become an extremely large one. Pharaoh had kept the Diamond that the good doctor had just found for many years. He didn’t exactly know where it came from or what, if it actually had any, use it was for, but he had always kept it by his side as a lucky charm of sorts. Soon enough he’d find that he’d lost it and then all hell would break loose. He swung his legs back out of bed and stretched, he groaned as he did. “I must set off for Memphis. No doubt there’ll be a lot of stops on the way.” He walked out into the hallway outside his room and peered about. He hadn’t been asleep, he had simply been thinking. About his match, about how he’d be getting to Memphis, and more importantly about those extremely strange dreams. What they were he didn’t quite know, but he was sure he’d find out sooner or later. He had a plan to find out. A psychologist in Memphis may be able to help him, he thought. Hit was true that he certainly had enough money to book an appointment straight away with the man, whoever he may turn out to be, all he had to do he was find him first. He had a few days spare and, unless something more pressing came up, he’d be out searching Memphis for a quack to sort out his problem, or at least shed some light on it. He walked down the winding stairs and into the lounge. Isiy sat watching TV with a smile. “I’ll be setting off soon, my love.” Isiy turned. “Alright. The cars in the garage… just take care.” She got to her feet and kissed Pharaoh passionately. “And drive carefully too! I’ll give you some painkillers for the journey in case you start to feel the headaches again.” He smiled bashfully. “Thank you.” He walked out of the room and back upstairs where he began to pack his bags. He crammed a few items of clothing, some toiletries, his mobile phone, and a few other things into a pair of two blue suitcases and then walked back into the hallway. Isiy watched from the living-room window as he backed the car down the driveway and left for Memphis. She couldn’t help worrying for him. She waved as he drove out of sight, just over the horizon… No sooner had she sat back down to watch the television when suddenly the ringing of the telephone hit the sound waves. She slowly walked towards it, but didn’t get there I time before the answering machine kicked in. After a few moments of Isiy talking about leaving a message a distorted voice began to speak. Hello? Charles if you’re there then answer, it’s Peter StCross! I really need to speak to you, Charles. Thank god I got your new number or else I’d have never managed to contact ya’. Where in the name of god have you been all these years? It’s been such a long time since you and Isabelle left. The police were baffled…look; I’m going on a bit ‘ere, so jus’ give me a ring back whenever ya’ get home. Thanks, bye… The answering machine switched off as the unknown caller put down his phone. Isiy stood, baffled. Then she came to the conclusion that it must have been a wrong number. Who the heck was Charles? And besides they’d only just moved in, he must have meant the last owners. She ignored the call. But she shouldn’t have.
