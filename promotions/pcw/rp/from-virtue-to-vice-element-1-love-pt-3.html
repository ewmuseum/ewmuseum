From Virtue to Vice: Element 1 – Love (Pt. 3)

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 24 of 2003 A.D. Muldoon plunged through the bristly undergrowth of the evergreen woodland, needles prodding his naked arms and grazing his exposed calves. His mind, hell-bent on the destruction of the maniac Shanahan, barely registered the fresh blood that had begun to trickle down his arm and drip into the snow alongside the chill rain. He could make out the faint screams of his beloved above the rising chant of the obscene cult atop the ridge, and he knew without fault that his revelations would prove faithful. He trudged on vigorously, brow drenched in sweat and the icy rain that seeped from the blackened clouds overhead. Rage invigorated the banker as he dashed through the drifts of snow and through the prickly pines, his only guiding light that of the bonfire blazing furiously on the mountaintop. The cries of his terrified wife shattered the night and served as the only to drown out the blasphemous, droning mantra of the deranged cult holding her captive at the peak. Shanahan knew he was coming; Desmond was playing right into the cultist’s trap, the mad game that he now realized the lunatic had orchestrated ever since their arrival. The abominable night terrors, the lapse of madness, and the aura that had cloaked the dark hills were all his doing. And though he knew that doom may be inevitable when he finally reached the end of Shanahan’s wicked labyrinth, only one thing now propelled him up that snowy incline into the arms of madness. Krissy’s innocence would not be punished for his own transgressions. Not far from where he breathlessly trekked, a twig snapped. As did another, now to the east. He paused abruptly, breath escaping his lungs in erratic gasps and mist issuing forth from his lips to evaporate into the frozen night. A shuffling emanated from the direction of the first crack, and as he heard the splintering of tree limbs under some monstrous weight, Muldoon first became conscious of the absence of the whippoorwills and their unholy song. Panic enveloped Desmond and he hastened through the brush, the taste of pine filling his mouth from the evergreen needles that stuck in his teeth through his ascent up the colossal knoll. He tried to drown out the shamble of the monstrous mass that he heard not so subtly stalking in his wake, in his prayers hoping it was only a bear or other denizen of the Vermont wilderness. Only the crunching of ice and branches echoed to his rear, his pursuer’s vocal silence further proving to dishearten the scrambling man from Buffalo. He hit a slope leading up to a flat-topped embankment, slipping temporarily and allowing his unseen pursuer to gain ground. With a muffled curse and grit teeth he managed to haul himself up the incline and roll onto the embankment, taking a moment to catch his breath. A gaunt shadow, illuminated minutely by the glow of the lofty bonfire on the cliff jutting overhead, flashed across the trees from where he just come and prompted Muldoon to sprint down the path a distance before taking back to his climb. “Ph-nglui mglw’nafh Cthulhu R’lyeh wgah’nagl fhtagn” That monotonous chant continued to drone at the crest of the hill, all the while complemented by the chilling shrieks of Krissy Muldoon. His drive to rescue his wife, coupled with the feeling of dread for the unseen thing lurking in his tracks, empowered Muldoon to finally elude his trailer and work his way onto the flattened highland of the mountain via a nearly forgotten trail spied through the brush. Breathless, Desmond emerged onto the snow-capped summit and cast his eyes further across the tundra of the plateau. An unearthly mist bathed the area in a perpetual haze, and nearly a hundred yards to the north he could make out the tell-tale ring of torches and a vast bonfire crackling and chattering, its immensity towering to touch the night sky. Outlined against the encircling flames rested the obscene altar of his dreams, and with renewed vitality he plunged into the ghostly mist. “Desmond!!” He darted through a gap in the circle of worshippers and lurched to a stop at the base of the pulpit, eyes transfixed on the same malformed ankh serving as the altar’s parallel to the Christian cross. Bound helplessly against the ghastly symbol, stripped devoid of clothing and painted with outlandish markings, hung the writhing form of his darling wife. The rain clung to her quivering body and the look in her eyes pleaded wantonly for his aid. “Desmond! Help me!” Muldoon sprung forward, nearly losing his footing on the slippery surface of the rock-carven shrine. He paused for a moment, running his finger through the slimy substance coating the exterior of the altar and glanced at it. Illuminated in the pallid light of the torches was a sticky red substance, and Muldoon nearly threw up. All the while Krissy continued to sob and wail, vainly fighting her restraints. “It’s blood….” muttered the exasperated financier. “Blood….all over the fucking altar….” A nefarious laugh split the night. Desmond arose from the foot of the pulpit and glared with fuming hatred at the one standing haughtily before him. Garbed in his rune-embellished robes, just as the heinous visions had implied, stood Bruce Shanahan. His own demeanor was a complete contradiction to that of the banker from Buffalo, as he appeared serene and calculating as opposed to the loathing that contorted the features of Muldoon. “Release her, you son of a bitch.” Shanahan merely raised an eyebrow, as if taken aback. “Temper, temper. You’re never going to get your way acting like this you know. Didn’t mommy ever teach you to be polite?” “Mommy never had her soul-mate strapped to a blood-covered altar by the crazy leader of a God-forsaken religion either. Let her go.” “Alas, you’re in no position to be threatening me, Desmond.” The mocking tone of Shanahan further served to rile up the infuriated Muldoon, and Krissy’s pained cries did nothing to impede his blossoming anger. Shanahan, however, paid this no mind. “I told you I wouldn’t tolerate you for very long, old friend,” the sect leader growled. “And I stand by my word. But I’m quite sure you recognize this little trinket….banker?” Bruce spoke coolly, drawing from his robes a cubic antiquity that shimmered in the light of the torches. At the sight of the sinister artifact Muldoon’s eyes widened to the size of quarters and his mouth gained the consistency of cotton. Within Bruce’s grasp lay a box of yellowish metal, a crystalline sphere suspended within its confines by a metal band and queerly-designed supports. “No…” he hoarsely whispered. “By God, no….” “Oh, I’m glad to see you recognize this little apparatus. I’ve made some good use of it in the past. And my intentions haven’t wavered in that respect.” “You!” cried Muldoon. “You caused the incident in Anaheim last year! The dead police officers…..ripped to pieces in that warehouse….!” “Fair enough,” acknowledged Bruce. “A sacrifice was demanded, and I delivered what was asked of me. You seem shell-shocked or something, Desmond. Have I done something to offend you?” “That thing is a blasphemy!” exclaimed Muldoon, temporarily ignoring the pleas of Krissy for her release. “It should still be at the bottom Narragansett Bay in Providence where Doctor Dexter hurled the accursed thing! How dare you reintroduce it to humanity?!” Bruce looked at Muldoon like he was mad. A long and awkward silence gripped the pair, the obscene monosyllabic chant continuing to rise and fall in the background. Muldoon was torn between his yearning to liberate Krissy from this haven of demons and to destroy the vile contraption wielded by Shanahan. The Shining Trapezohedron was an infernal device incapable of having any redeeming value, its very presence on Earth a mockery of all that was sane and righteous. The look that had come into Bruce’s eyes was vile. He sported the relic sardonically. “Tsk tsk. Your fear for this ancient item is utterly pathetic, it makes me truly weep. As does your chivalry for coming to rescue your fair damsel.” “Tonight, the Dark Mother requires sacrifice. You, Desmond Muldoon, could have fled these lands and warned your peers of what is to be birthed in these lands. Indeed, this disloyal whore could have served nicely and alone pleased Her. But because of your love, your adoration for this treacherous woman, your associates will remain in the dark as my plans continue to unfold. You failed the test and now you shall share her fate!” Shanahan’s smirk was wrought with depravity, his evil gaze forcing Muldoon to cringe. And now he could only despair, as the cultists’ chant ceased and they came to form a crude ring around he and his desperately screaming spouse bound to the perverted ankh. “The altar has been prepared as decreed in the teachings, cleansed in the blood of the innocent. The price will be paid not only by you and your despicable wench…..but by the entire world. We cannot be stopped now. Your weakness has solidified our power. My revelations have come to pass.” A putrid stench abruptly tainted the air before a great crashing resonated from the west. It surged through the woodland and emerged into the clearing with nary a sound, its movements swift and deft for its immensity. The sight that passed through the gentle glow of the torches and materialized in the light of the bonfire was the final straw, the last temptation that drove Muldoon into final madness. “Her Children, they have come to accept her sacrifice, Desmond,” spoke Shanahan, his words falling on deaf ears. “You could have been free; free to return to the sphere of civilization and continue your normal life. But that is no longer your option. You will be free. But free to wander the world among the blessed of the Dark Mother, instilling her gifts upon the wretched as you forever walk the veil between divine worship and the greatest sacrilege.” Muldoon involuntarily began to tremble, watching in stoic distress as the monstrosity loomed over his petrified wife. The cultists bowed in reverence to the black beast, its innumerable mouths dripping green slime as it slavered over his wife. “Embrace Her, Desmond. And embrace Her Dark Young…”
