And the Trigger was Executed |4|

18 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6.18.02 Fury The sun was shining brightly, and the air was full of the elation of early summer. The sky looked bright but there is a shiver in the north wind that says there may be a sudden storm. The road was pretty bleak, for if one were crossing, there’d be a sort of high-unsheltered plateau. Down further, the road that looked but little used and which seemed to dip through a little winding valley was curved. Dark clouds drifted rapidly across the sky. The sunshine passed away, and a breath of cold wind seemed to drift over the world below. It was only a breath, however, and more of a warning than a fact, for the sun came out brightly again. On down the road...as the place was concerned, it was desolation itself with some a scattered tassel of wood, but not much. Upward, the great thick clouds were drifting rapidly across the sky from north to south at a great height. There were signs of a coming storm in some lofty stratum of the air. The ground was now much more picturesque. There were no striking objects that the eye might single out, but in all there was a charm of beauty. The air was cold, and the drifting of clouds high overhead were more marked. They were accompanied by a sort of far away rushing sound. It seemed like a deserted place, with a wide stretch of open country, shut in by hills all around. Their sides were covered with trees, which spread down to the plain, dotting in clumps the gentler slopes, and hollows, which showed here and there. Eyeing the winding of the road...it shows that it curved close to one of the densest of these clumps and was lost behind it. Slowly, there came a cold shiver in the air, and the snow began to fall. Darker and darker grew the sky, and faster and heavier fell the snow, till the earth before and around was a glistening white carpet the further edge of which was lost in misty vagueness. The road was here but crude, and when on the level its boundaries were not so marked as when it passed through the cuttings; and in a little while for I missed underfoot the hard surface, and my feet sank deeper in the grass and moss. The air became icy-cold. The snow was now falling so thickly and whirling around in such rapid eddies that one could hardly keep their eyes open. Every now and then the heavens were torn asunder by vivid lightning, and in the flashes one could see ahead of them a great mass of trees, chiefly yew and cypress all heavily coated with snow. Presently the blackness of the storm had become merged in the darkness of the night. By-and-by the storm seemed to be passing away, it now only came in fierce puffs or blasts. Now and again, through the black mass of drifting cloud, came a straggling ray of moonlight, which lit up the expanse letting you see only a few feet ahead of the once calm road. Then, there was a sudden stillness. The snowstorm had passed. The air was now a cool breeze and the sun is folded deep within the clouds, nowhere to be seen. The clouds make up the shadows below and the gray of the Cumulus suddenly let it all out and the rainfall begins. Bolts of lightning now light the sky every couple of seconds and the sound of thunder shakes the ground beneath its sky. Demolition The rain still hits the sidewalks with impact, with no chance of letting up. This time the storm bore on its icy wings, not snow, but great drops of rain which drove with intense force. At length there came a flash so appallingly bright that in its glare nature seemed to be standing still. Abhorrence Out of nowhere, the scene jumps to a nearby town, where an aged lonesome man walks the streets of this gloomy town with both hands in his jean pockets. This man is hopeless of a good life. This doesn’t keep him down, though. He may have no money, no family, or no friends, but he presses on to live his life although it eats at him to end it every single day. This life that he lives he hopes to have washed away by the driving rain. So much has happened to him over his lifespan, most that he regrets every single day that he opens his eyes...every single day he takes a single breath...and every single day that he is alive. Once with a job to live up to country’s expectations, he now has no job, nor a chance of ever having one again. Hatred Years ago, he woke to the chirping of birds, knowing that today he would do all in his power to help his beloved country. He was a hero to his country and a hero to himself. His dream was met when he joined the force of his country, and he always fulfilled his expectations. Back then, he once woke up to the love of his life every single morning and his beautiful children playing in the next room. That was then... This is now. He now has no wife or children. They were all taken out of his life in an instant, brutally murdered by ungrateful individuals. He did nothing wrong to them, but they took it all out on his family when he went to the store early in the morning to buy some early Christmas presents. All he wanted to be was a good husband and a good father. These entities that took the pride in murdering his family did no time in prison for what they had done. The ones who did this were normal people with families also, but had disgust in the old man and his family. They had no interest in the life that the old man had or even cared. They hated the line of work that this man had to do every day to live up his dream and to put food on the table for his family. Why is the world so mean to this man? He hasn’t eaten in three days and still nobody in this town stops to even notice him. He opens his eyes with a depressing look, staring up at the sky. The rain beats down at his face, but it seems to have no effect on the man, for he just lowers his head down in shame and keeps walking down the street. Ruthlessness A few neighboring Gothic teenager males dressed in all black come into view around the corner. The two in the dark baggy jeans and tight black shirts just walk forward smiling horridly as they notice the man coming their way. They look almost identical, both with long-length black hair and an attached fishnet sleeve to their tight shirts, down to their black fingernails. The old man just keeps up his walk and tries not to look up at the teenagers with no intention of saying anything...and within an instant...the teenagers pass with no intent to hurt the elderly man. The man sighs in relief as he continues his miserable walk down the narrow street. Sorrow Clink...Clink...Clink...The man’s footsteps are not the only ones heard, as more come from behind him. He keeps up a steady pace so he doesn’t show a sense of being frightened. If he shows any part of being scared, the one behind him could perceive it and take advantage of it. A silent tear rolls down his colorless cheeks. He couldn’t stop thinking about what had happened so many years ago. It seems like it was just yesterday when everything was happening with his country, and with his family. In reality...it was 84 years ago. He feels that living as long as he has is a way of punishment. The man is now 104 years of age...and can walk the streets without much trouble, only regret and pain. Despondency The sound behind him gets closer as he just closes his wet eyes, hoping for it all to go away....it doesn’t. The sound of footsteps become closer until the old man finally tries to run, afraid of what is behind him. His legs become unstable and he falls to the wet sidewalk below with the rain pouring on him from above. His face lies in the watery concrete as he weeps from anguish. His old legs have finally given up on him, and have snapped in an instant from the fall...broken on impact. A bolt of lightning flashes in the sky and the old man looks up....to see the two Gothic teenagers watching him on the ground. He tries to yell out for help to no avail, due to his walking in the rain has caused him to lose his voice from becoming ill. The old man just closes his eyes, and awaits what will happen. The boys pick up the old man off of the ground, with his legs dangling and not able to move. They then start beating the old man with a stick off of the ground, hitting him in the back of the head. This knocks the man back down face first into the concrete. The old man doesn’t do anything besides just cry with his eyes closed, praying that it will all end soon. There is nobody around to help this man, because the storm has left everybody indoors. The Goths begin kicking the man in the ribs and hitting his legs with the stick as hard as they can. One of them picks up the man’s head and slams it straight back into the concrete, causing the old man’s head to start seeping blood. The two Goths smile evilly. One grabs the old man’s feet, and the other grabs his arms. They carry him to a gape in-between two buildings, which is a very small alley. They lay him face first on the concrete beside a dark green dumpster and upon newspapers. After that, one pulls out some sort of knife, which glimmers at every flash of lightning. The rain now begins to hit even harder and the thunder booms throughout even tougher. The old man heard the sound of the knife being released from its case, and clenches himself for the outcome of his life. The Goth bends down to the old man and slides the knife calmly up to the back of his neck. Then, he looks at the soaked shirt and takes the collar, lifting it up, digging the knife in deep. He pulls back with force cutting a giant slit down the back of the shirt. The other Goth then rips the t-shirt off and motions for the other to come with him...the old man begins to spit up blood. As the two leave they say nothing; they only look back in disgust and approval of what they have just done. The old man lay face first on the ground almost lifeless and no sense of feeling in his legs or his face. His whole body is bruised and battered, open flesh, and blood trickles from everywhere. Hostilities The only thing that the man can do is sit there and wait for it to end as he prevents his eyes from opening. He thinks about the life that he has put upon his shoulders and in ways he could’ve changed it. He could’ve waited that day to buy Christmas presents for his family, he could’ve taken them with him, and he could’ve been there with them to die...he blames himself. Coming home that day was the worst day of his life. He opened the door of his home to find a puddle of blood in almost every corner of the house. The lampshades were bloodstained along with the shredded couch. The television still played the cartoon video that his children were watching and the breakfast was still set out. Everything was all over the place and not in order. Broken windows...maybe the way the individuals got in...kitchen knives missing...maybe the way the family was massacred...the blood on the walls...3 dead corpses...the last remains of a beautiful household. The old man begins to cry again. This is the only thing that has been in his mind for 84 years. He always wonders ways he could have altered this and hates himself for not being able to modify the past. Occasionally, he believes that it was not his fault and that fate had played its part. But, then he realizes that he could have made a twist of fate if he just would have stayed asleep with his beautiful wife that day. Bereavement The rain shows no chance of letting up, but it does just that. The sun doesn’t come out, but the rain stops and the cold air begins again. The man opens his eyes for one last time. He gazes ahead of him without moving his body and another bolt of lightning allows him to read the newly painted graffiti on the building in front of his desolate body. He uses his last ounce of energy to reach into his back pocket and pulls out an old piece of paper and unfolds it. Inside of it, a small knife was placed. He stares at it for a second and with a quick movement, the knife falls to the ground It seems that the old man is alive, though he is now deceased. One last tear falls from the man‘s open eyes. The man seemed to be looking at the graffiti on the wall when he took his own life. The graffiti shows what kind of world we have. They never forget...even if one changes, or does it for the love of their country. In any country, if you’re doing it for the country...then it is supposed to be for the good. No country is ever a bad guy; they all have families and the same things that anybody has. They all kiss their children good night just like us. Why blame all of them...when it is usually the work of one single person? That one person is the only bad guy. The old man’s eyes stare blankly at the words on the wall...: Once a Nazi…always a Nazi…You executed the trigger. End
