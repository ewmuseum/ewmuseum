Djed-tefen - Chapter One - Murder in the air...

8 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8th of Sept 2002 Ren–ramet Loc and Frank Matthews wandered back from the Glosimir’s Head in the early hours of the morning. It’d been an interesting evening to say the least. To the men’s astonishment not a single fist had been thrown, which, taking into account the kind of men who resided at the local tavern, was both interesting and quite the revelation in its own right. The fact, however, that fights were practically mandatory in the lodging didn’t mean to say the two thieves actually enjoyed brawling, in fact they were probably the only two who hated fighting more than the Religion Guild...well, with the exception of the House of Sethituros. It wasn’t because they found punch-ups a bad influence on the Gods or even the fair children of Djed-tefen; it basically came down to the fact that they were cowards. The thing was, Loc didn’t consider himself one… then again he didn’t think he was a bad thieve, either. Frank: ‘Ow much you ‘ad, tonight? Loc’s cloudy eyes squeezed together to make out the blurry shape speaking to him… Loc: Frank? Frank: ‘S’right… Loc pointed a spindly finger in his friend’s direction, stumbled, got to his feet again and continued to point. Loc: Y-You…you nuthin’! Frank and Ren–ramet shared a strange friendship. They were both Djed-tefen’s worst thieves and they’d been partners in crime from the very beginning. But the unusual thing about the pair was the fact that Loc didn’t actually like his ‘colleague’…or so he said. Deep down he had admiration for Frank, though, that was only because he was there to help him flee from struggles or drag him home after he’d had too much to drink. Frank: You nick owt good tonight, then? Loc straightened up a little and reached into his filthy pockets. Loc: I got these, an’ tha’s it. In his grubby hands were a few shining coppers. Matthews’s eyes grew wide as Ren-ramet proudly showed off his bounty for the night. Frank: Ya’ nicked all tha’ tonight?! Loc nodded as they continued to edge down Third and Forth Street. Loc: Yea’! Course I did! I’m Ren–ramet Loc, Godsdammit! This City’s greatest thief!! Frank’s head tilted to one side. His eyes were abruptly drawn away from his companion to a shadowy figure in the darkness of an alleyway up ahead. Slowly he tapped his partner on the shoulder, not daring to take his gaze away from the shape. Frank: Ere! Can y-you see tha’ over there, Loc? Loc strained his eyes and followed the direction of Matthew’s grimy finger. Loc: Fuck! Wha’s tha’?! And then realization dawned. Loc: Ah, dammit! The figure approached broodingly, its arms tightly situated behind its back. Man: Stealing again? Tsk-Tsk. And neither of you are members of the Thief’s Guild. I’m afraid I’ll have to take you both in… Loc narrowed his eyes. Desperate times, such as this, called for desperate measures, and denial seemed as perfect a portrayal of desperation as it got. Loc: But, Captain, we ‘aven’t even been s-stealin’ ‘t’all! We’ve been down then Glosimir’s Head… He nudged his partner in the ribs. Loc: Ain’t we, Frank? Matthews nodded sluggishly and tried to look sober, but failed miserably. Captain Inpheeno of the City Guard wasn’t someone you trifled with in a situation like this. If he caught you off your guard then that was it; your days as a well-respected thief in town were over. When Inpheeno had first arrived in the City he and Loc had got acquainted immediately. Needless to say The Captain had busted Ren-ramet like there was no tomorrow and it hadn’t been a pleasant way of being caught thieving either…Loc still had the scars. Frank: ‘S’right, Cap’n. We’ve given them days up now! We’re the best citizens Djed-tefen has to offer, we are! Frank had the sudden urge to persist. With a dreamy look about his eyes he continued: Frank: Yep…best citizens ever. Why, on Horusius’ good name we ain’t never been stealin’… Inpheeno tilted his head to one side. Inpheeno: Frank, you don’t even worship the Gods…you’re not Djedian. Matthews fell silent, opened his mouth to speak and decided against it. Inpheeno folded his arms over his chest and sighed as Ren-ramet stuttered his way back into the conversation. Loc: Look, what my friend is trying to say, Captain, I-is: w-we don’t hunt in that… jungle anymore--… Inpheeno: And it’s just as well, too! You two are the worst thieves in the damn City! Now I advise you both to get out of here before my pity wears thin! The two thieves glanced at one another before sprinting away back down Third and Forth Street. Captain Inpheeno smiled with amusement and shook his head. In a way he liked the pair, they were too bad of thieves not to like. Frank Matthews and Ren–ramet Loc, he thought. The Captain smiled, sniffed the air, and set off back on his patrol. The meat cleaver came down on the chopping board with a hard thump. Jakk Stockk wiped the sweat from his brow, looked up at the moon outside the window to check the time and moved onto the next stack of various meats. He recognized this as lamb. Jakk: Take that! Some people got the impression that Jakk wasn’t all-together ‘up there’, which was probably as true as a statement came. The man was a wonder with a meat cleaver; the only trouble was the way in which he cut the meat. He dragged the next unfortunate piece of lamb onto the chopping board and brought the cleaver through the air in a wide circle. Jakk: A-ha! It struck the mutton with such force that its shimmering blade became stuck in the wood. Jakk: Shite! ‘Violence’, as his regular customers had dubbed him, reached out to grab the instrument when a panicked array of knocks stopped him. They were coming from his door. Stockk turned, his brow furrowed, as they gradually faded and a ‘thump’ took their place. Jakk: The hell was that?! ‘Violence’ Jakk walked away from the counter, opened the door and looked down. Jakk: Ah dammit! Bellow him the body of a man, who appeared to have been killed via a deadly variety of throwing knives, littered his doorway. Stockk looked up at the murky buildings above and waved a fist. Jakk: And I’ll suppose you’re going to clean this up, then?! His gaze fell back on the corpse. He shook his head mournfully. Jakk: Damned assassins. Well… I’d better call The Guard. Lord Kreller Wonderious of the Assassin’s guild heard the distant ‘snapping’ outside his office’s door before any of the other sounds even dared to echo through the cold building’s interior. It was, as he’d expected, abruptly followed by an even nearer ‘whooshing’ noise and then that too was followed by a huge ‘bang’. All the while, however, he never took his eyes off the paper work in front of him, not even when the knock came at his door… Lord Kreller: Enter. The entrance creaked open and ‘The Rush’ He-sen Khet, top teacher in the ‘rare and dangerous knives’ sector of the Assassin’s school of Death, entered, brushing himself off. Lord Kreller: Ah, Mr Khet. He-sen always wondered how his lordship managed to do that. He’d knew, without a shadow of a doubt, that Lord Kreller hadn’t looked up to see who had entered the office. Lord Kreller: I see you got past the new traps, then? Kreller finally took his eyes off the work beneath his nose and grinned wickedly. Rush: Yeah, milord. Not your best effort, though, I gotta say. Kreller’s brow furrowed. Lord Kreller: Oh well…better look next time, eh? So what--…Not even the crocodile pit? Khet shook his head. Rush: I wasn’t expecting the carpet to fall through like that, milord, but…no, ‘fraid not… Wonderious sighed, looked down at the quill pen in his hand with deep thought and then raised his eyes again. Lord Kreller: Well what about the poison dart shooters? Rush: I’m sorry, sir. Lord Kreller: Blast! He punched the air angrily and then a sly grin merged upon his lips. Lord Kreller: But surely you weren’t expecting the boulder? Khet, or Rush as many of his former ‘acquaintances’ had nicknamed him, shook his head sorrowfully. Rush: Nope, sir. Lord Kreller: Oh well. Better luck next time, that’s what I always say. Now, my good man, what brings you here? Rush: I jus’ got a pigeon message from the Guard, sir. Lord Kreller: Oh? His eyes wandered back to his work. Khet was losing his interest. Rush: Someone’s been…murdered. Kreller looked up in surprise. Lord Kreller: Murdered?! That can’t be right! There hasn’t been a murder in this City for years! Assassinations, yes, but Murders?! Ha! Speak rightly, man! Rush threw up his hands in protest. Rush: Then why the Voodoo is Alfy McGee dead?! Lord Kreller: Alfy…McGee? The candlestick maker on Third and Forth Street? That’s who’s dead? Kreller rose from his seat. Rush: Yessir! Lord Wonderious turned on his heels and walked towards a tall bookcase at the far side of the room. With a probing finger he found the volume he required and flicked through a few pages. Lord Kreller: But he’s only worth 50 pieces of gold! He-sen nodded. Rush: Exactly, sir! I mean, why would someone want him assassinated? And plus I’ve already checked with the others and no one had any orders to assassinate him…not tonight…not next week…not even yesterday! Wonderious shook his head and began to walk towards the already open window in his office. He stared out over the sandy City far bellow and huffed. Lord Kreller: For years I have tried to keep this city under a tame watch. No murders, no ‘accidental slaughtering’, no ‘oops, my crossbow went off’! I put all my effort into forming this guild and now someone insults me by daring to actually – He shuddered - murder someone?! Find whoever did this, Khet, and bring them here… They should have known there’d be consequences in undertaking an act so vile! Wonderious turned back from the window, opened his mouth to continue and stopped. He was standing in an empty room. Smiling, he walked back to his desk and sat down. Lord Kreller: Damn, he’s good. “So the assassins didn’t do it?” Jakk Stockk looked down at his feet in surprise. He was impressed. The Guard had only been on his premises for twenty minutes and already they’d sent a pigeon message to the assassin’s guild, called on the Commander – who incidentally hadn’t seemed very pleased about being woken up at 4 in the morning - and had begun to look for clues out in the street. Captain Inpheeno walked back to the body outside of the door. A Guardsman was drawing a white chalk line around the unfortunate man. Inpheeno: Found anything, Corporal? Corporal Lexicon looked up. Corporal Lexicon: As of yet, no, Captain. But I am only drawing the chalk line, after all. Perhaps one of the others has found something of interest? Inpheeno looked about his surroundings. Why would anyone want to kill a simple candlestick maker? Debt? He’d heard that Alfy McGee had a strong disapproval for gambling, so he ruled that out of the complex notebook that was his mind. What about enemies? Well how many enemies could the City’s best candlestick maker possibly have? It really was no good thinking about it now. Inpheeno: Did you see anyone about, Jakk? Stockk took his attention off the meat cleaver still stuck in the chopping board and turned. Jakk: Well, no. I thought it’d been the assassins again. Always killin’ people, they are! It wasn’t until I called on you lot that I noticed it was ol’ Mr. McGee. Shame, it is. That poor woman’ll be devastated. Inpheeno: Yes…a shame indeed. Commander J’Kazak Endimus shuffled past Corporal Lexicon at the doorway and re-entered the butcher’s shop with a murmur of disapproval. Endimus: Why would anyone do this? You’d have thought they’d have the sense to realize the Assassin’s Guild would take an interest in a regular murder. Inpheeno leant against the workbench and sighed. Inpheeno: My thoughts exactly, sir. What I don’t understand is the reason for someone wanting to kill old Alfy? He’s a bloody candlestick maker for the Gods sake. There isn’t a motive! J’Kazak rubbed his chin in thought. Endimus: And he had no debts? Inpheeno: Apparently he was well against gambling of any sort, sir. And besides he was a candlestick maker, there’s a lot of money in that business. No real reason for him needing to pay off debts, sir. Endimus strained his mind. Endimus: Well, what about enem--… Inpheeno: None, apparently, sir. Mr Stockk here knew Mr. McGee quite well…he says he had a lot of friends but no enemies he could recall. Jakk: Yeah. Endimus walked back across the tiled floor towards the door. He knelt down beside the body of Mr. McGee and shook his head. Endimus: It looks like an assassination, but you say the assassins have already cleared their name, right? Inpheeno: Right, sir. J’Kazak stared at the knives. They did indeed appear to be the sort of blades the assassins used. He recognised them as the throwing-knives the Black Blade clan had become accustomed to using. Then again that particular group of assassin’s hadn’t wandered the City’s streets in years. Endimus: Then it appears we have a mystery on our hands, Captain. I want this entire street brushed for evidence. Mr. Stockk… ‘Violence’ Jakk raised an eyebrow. Endimus: Are you absolutely sure you saw no one out there? Jakk: Absolutely, Commander. J’Kazak got back to his feet, found his eyes fall on the other Guardsmen exploring the scene of the crime and then abruptly left the butcher’s store. Inpheeno followed him hastily as he set off down the sandy, cobblestone road. Inpheeno: Sir, where are you going? J’Kazak chuckled in the way only a man in dire need of sleep could. Endimus: Back to bed, Captain! He faded from view. Inpheeno: Damn! I hate it when this happens! Nobody ever said working for The City Guard was an easy job. The sound of the footsteps hit Pharaoh Titutopolis II’s ears before his head clerk, Ner’heeza, even managed to get to the door. He opened it and found his lordship sitting upon his throne with a look of contemplation about his face. Cautiously, for everyone who worked at the palace knew what his pharaohship was like, he edged across the floor and then, feeling that he was in safe distance of any possible traps, knelt down in front of the ruler of Djed-tefen. Pharaoh: Ah, Ner’heeza! What news do you bring me today? Ner’heeza looked up. Ner’heeza: Milord, there has been…a murder. Titutopolis shrugged. Pharaoh: Yes, yes…I know. The head clerk’s eyes widened. Ner’heeza: You do? Pharaoh: Yes, and please, get up off the floor when you’re talking to me. I do hate it when people do that. Ner’heeza: Yes, sire. Ner’heeza rose from his position on the ground and bowed. Pharaoh sighed. Pharaoh: You don’t have to bow, either. Ner’heeza blushed a little. It was always easy to tell when the head clerk felt embarrassed. His skin was such a pale shade that when he did blush – and it was on rare occasions - it was known almost straight away. Some people can contain it for a while, but Ner’heeza didn’t fit into that category at all. Ner’heeza: So how do you kno--… Pharaoh: My good man, you should know better than to ask a question such as that! Deep down the head clerk knew exactly how his lordship had known about the murder. It had been remarked more than once in the palace kitchens that his pharaohship had spies and bounty hunters sitting guard in Djed-tefen, all of whom eagerly awaiting his word to set off and do whatever it was that they had been hired to do. Together they collected information for him… and sometimes they even collected people for him – people who he needed to see on pressing matters involving illegal thievery, false coinage exchange or even slandering of the Gods. Ner’heeza: I’m sorry, pharaoh. Do forgive me. Pharaoh sighed and placed his thumb and forefinger on the bridge of his nose. Pharaoh: You don’t have to apologize all the time, either. Ner’heeza bowed again. Ner’heeza: I’m sorry, milord. And as he usually did, Titutopolis sighed. It was just going to be another one of those days…wasn’t it? The drum of fingers atop a wooden surface echoed throughout the hallways of the Kreigmene estate. Alan sat, blank faced, staring at a small silvery devise in front of him. He couldn’t quite remember why he’d invented the ‘thing’, but he was sure that with enough effort put into staring blankly in its direction he’d remember. His attention was abruptly diverted when there came a knock at his personal office’s door. Kreigmene: Come in. The hinges on the entrance creaked wearily as it opened. Kreigmene turned his head to see the leader of the Scholar’s Guild Sir Howy Porterious standing in the doorway. Kreigmene: Ah, Howy, what brings you--… Howy Porterious: Have you seen this?! Porterious tossed a yellowed scroll on top of the table and allowed Alan time to read it through. At length Kreigmene’s face turned pale. He raised his eyes and stared at one of the room’s far walls. Howy Porterious: That’s right. It’s happened…again…and this time he wasn’t so careful in his work. Kreigmene rose from his chair and walked across the length of the room. Kreigmene: B-But, we told--… Howy Porterious: There is no we in this, Alan--… Kreigmene: You can’t lay all the blame on me! He stormed back towards the table, picked up the scroll and began to read: Kreigmene: “Djed-tefen was shocked this morning when the City learned that its beloved candlestick maker, Mr. Alfy McGee, had been brutally murdered in Seaview Street. Local butcher, a Mr. Jackk Stockk, who found the dying man outside his shop, reported the incident to the City Guard at once. As you will know Djed-tefen has not had to deal with a murder in some time. The Assassin’s Guild has already reported that the incident had nothing to do with them and as soon as the murderer is found they will take the matter into their own hands.” He shook his head and tossed the paper back at the leader of the Scholar’s Guild. It fell to the ground in a crumpled mess. Kreigmene: You had as much to do with this as I did! If you lay the blame on me then by the Gods I’ll make sure your part is known. Howy’s expression didn’t change. Kreigmene sat back down and returned his attention to the shimmering mechanism under his nose. Howy Proterious: If that is the way you want to handle this then fine. But we need a plan…and you, my friend, are the only person better than the pharaoh in the ‘cunning’ department. Kreigmene grinned. Kreigmene: How true. But I’ll need time, you understand? The scholar nodded. Howy Porterious: Quite so. Howy stood quietly awaiting Kreigmene’s response. It was then that he noticed Alan’s interest in the machine on the wooden surface. He furrowed his brow and took a step forward. Howy Porterious: What’s that? Kreigmene looked up with cloudy eyes. It was, thought Howy, as though he’d just been woken from a distant trance. Kreigmene: Sorry? Howy Porterious: I said: “What’s that”? The scholar pointed in the device’s direction. Kreigmene: Well that’s the thing…I can’t quite remember. You see, I pressed this button--… It was too late...he’d already pushed it. Kreigmene: Ah, Howy, what brings you--… Howy Porterious: Have you seen this?! Kreigmene: Seen what? Howy Porterious: Thi--… Porterious stopped when he realized the scroll was already on the floor, slightly crumpled. To Be Continued…
