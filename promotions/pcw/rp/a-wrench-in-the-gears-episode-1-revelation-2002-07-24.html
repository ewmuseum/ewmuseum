A Wrench in the Gears: Episode 1 -- Revelation

24 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 24 Prologue A stocky, shaven-head figure leaned against a stack of heavy freight on the loading docks. Mirrored sunglasses veiled his eyes, the broad shadows concealing his presence to all but the most alert of passerby. At his feet lay a black Adidas bag, its exterior coated in a thin glaze of grime and its contents scant, containing only a change of clothes for several days and a few miscellaneous items. A great horn resounded across the bay, puffs of smoke ascending skyward through the smokestacks embellishing the crown of the glorious vessel chartered by Primetime Championship Wrestling for the coming weeks’ festivities. Already the solitary figure on the corner of the dock watched the others begin to board. Jordan Shabazz, Lexian, Loc, Pharaoh, Pendragon…one by one they filed like drones up the retractable incline, bags slung across their shoulders as some took time to sign autographs for wave after wave of their adoring fans. From his place in the shadows, Shanahan looked on with disdain. “F*ckin’ yuppies,” he mumbled minutely as he watched reigning Television champion John Crichton happily sign a 8x11 glossy wielded by a cluster of youths presumably in their mid-teen’s. “It’s all a ploy….a damn marketing tool. Nelson and that wretched Booker have turned the business I love into a full-blown mockery…..” He crossed his thick arms across his burly chest, casting his vindictive eyes onto the ocean as he tilted his full weight against the cargo. The boxes creaked ever so slightly as Shanahan’s eyes lit up from behind their reflective shroud. “Bingo,” he murmured forebodingly, watching an afro-adorned figure stride across the wharf and toward the line of wrestlers as they progressed onto the vessel. Moments later two more all-too-familiar figures trudged down the pier with gym bags in tow. “They’re here.” A smile crept across the face of Boston’s Bastard Son, his posture unchanging as he watched the trio board the luxury liner. “Indeed they are, old demon.” The 31-year old veteran glowered vindictively at the swaying afro of Aymin as he trod up the bridge, casting a nonchalant hello at the crew as they went about their business cleaning the deck and readying the massive liner for departure. “And frankly, Sunday can’t come soon enough for me. Just looking at that damn afro makes me wanna f*ckin snap….” “Ah, that’s the spirit. For all the faults you mortals have, I must admit I’m impressed with many qualities. The wrath you embrace within yourself is but one.” An ominous chuckle echoed in Shanahan’s head as he leveled his eyes on The Rush, the reigning Extreme champion wheeling his luggage onto the ship. “What else about us impresses you?” Shanahan said aloud, attempting to hide his contempt for the ancient devil whose essence now partially pervaded his mind. He knew to an extent such confidentiality was fruitless; that which shared his body for the next few days was omnipotent and realized there was no love lost between the pair. This deal was strictly business and forged of what little honor the fiend had. At the very least maybe he could beguile the antediluvian entity into a sense of mutual commitment. Surely it could only help his case come the events of Sunday. “Ah, a great many things, mortal. Such an intriguing nature for a species of weaklings and mongrels. The way you can destroy something so ruthlessly and yet become infatuated with the subject to the point you dedicate entire fields of study toward it….remarkable. Not even the Mi-Go or the Yithians have such an acute curiosity. The Native Americans, Aztecs….all crushed as a result of the avarice of civilization, and today entire subcultures exist dedicated to the study of their societies. Truly uncanny.” The surly Boston ruffian leaned forward and snatched up the handle of his bag, setting his stern gaze on the elegant vessel. “May as well get settled in,” muttered Bruce, slinging the lightweight carry-on over his husky shoulders. “I’m not here for my damn health.” “More benefit to your health than Wild Wing, Mason or Aymin. Alas, if I could feel sorrow I would weep for them. But then the anticipation of shattering mortal bones strikes and such a feeling is lost in vain anyhow….” Striding forward out of the shadows, Shanahan boarded the boat with an arrogant swagger and went to find his assigned cabin for the voyage. * * * The Warning Shanahan’s eyes fluttered, a sudden jolt awakening from his slumber. He could remotely detect the monotonous whirring of muted rotors overhead, and the thump of a heavy force setting down on the roof. With a groan he pulled himself up to a sitting position, gawking at the door and the roof in turn. “It appears your comrades have arrived. Embarking on yet another fool crusade I wager…” Moments later, he had pulled on his shorts and stumbled toward the door in a befuddled daze. Grasping the handle he whipped open the heavy metal door and staggered into the salty summer night. A gentle ocean breeze caressed his rough skin and the odor of seawater permeated his nostrils, the rise and fall of footsteps rounding the corner causing the still drowsy ruffian. Underneath an incandescent lamp mounted on the roof strode the stalwart form of Captain Marcus Stryker, his compilation of military equipment conspicuous by their absence. Behind him hovered the chopper that the Delta Fox unit commonly employed, with several of the regiment in tow and the figure of the sultry Vanessa Wilder silhouetted against the searchlight of the helicopter. “Bruce!” greeted Stryker with a firm handshake, his tone offset by the stern expression on his face. “Sorry for this rather rude awakening, but this simply couldn’t wait. You got yourself a problem.” Shanahan cocked his head to the right, eyes narrowing as his arms crisscrossed his chest. “The Coalition has monitored the course of this ship….not only because of your presence, but due to the growing boldness of the Old Ones and their minions we feared they may be planning a disaster with place in the public spotlight. And to a point, we were right. “This little love-boat is on a collision course with a colony of Cthulhu-spawn. Several dozen miles to the east is a trench several miles in depth, and its been a hotbed of activity in recent days. They’re mobilizing, Bruce, and we can only venture guesses why. And I wouldn’t be surprised in the least if that tentacle-faced abomination himself is ringing the dinner bell a day or so early. We need you.” A feeling of dread swept through the perturbed Boston resident. He grinded his teeth, uttering forth a forlorn sigh. “What ya need of me?” he asked dryly. “Something fishy is going on here,” responded the commander, his own fretful gaze falling on the calm waters of the sea. “Do some digging, the course this vessel is taking is a bit peculiar. It’s virtually looping south, unnecessarily. Passing right over that damned trench. Find out why, try to get the course changed. Naturally we don’t expect it’s a very plausible reason if you tell them directly that a colony of sea-dwelling, intelligent reptilians are planning on making a smorgasbord of this little this cruise.” As the other opened his mouth to speak, a single word permeated his thoughts. No. Damn, he had nearly forgotten. That old devil was still implanted in his soul, his will as much a part of Shanahan’s as Bruce himself. “Damn you!” he unconsciously screamed back at the primordial fiend in his thoughts. “You know about this! You planned it!” “I do not control the actions of all our subordinates, mortal. Great Cthulhu has the authority and the will to command his own spawn without our command being given. But that’s not to say I ever denied it….” “F*ck this! I’m breaking the deal off now! Get the hell outta my body, I can do this alone!” “Bah. Until the deal is complete, a part of my essence will accompany you everywhere. I’m attached to you, Bruce, we’re bonded. Your soul and my omnipotence……we are one. And the only catalyst that will cause me to separate this binding is the fulfillment of the objective. Sunday night, it ends. And not a moment before.” Defeated, he looked monetarily at Stryker before looking away with his response. “I can’t.” Bruce sighed deeply and glanced away from Stryker and his unit. His eyes went to the moon, a magnificent silver orb glistening in the sky on this clear night as if painstakingly polished by the hand of some night-dwelling divinity. What he had done was a mistake; to trust one as treacherous as the Crawling Chaos was a massive blunder. Even if he could break his word and send the ship off-course, would Nyarlathotep allow it? Christ, he was a god! If he decreed it, Bruce feared the beast could possess his body and destroy all he had worked for in recent months. “Dammit, Bruce, aren’t you listening?!” shouted an irate Captain Stryker, Shanahan refusing to turn and meet his gaze. “This liner is about 24 hours away from cruising directly over f*cking Monster Central! There’s a trench over 10 miles deep in this thing’s path and, according to our readings, it’s a damn metropolis for Great Cthulhu’s little servitors. It may not be that accursed R’lyeh but at this time tomorrow night this little ship of yours is going to be crawling with those despicable reptile things! And let’s be honest…..all the X-Bombs, Absolute Destiny Apocalypses’ and Legend Death Drops on Earth aren’t going to save your pals against a veritable army of ravenous star-spawn….” “THERE’S NOTHING I CAN DO!!” roared the frustrated Boston native, shifting his beady eyes to the captain as his voice pierced the loud whirring of the propeller. “I can’t give ya the details, but I can’t help ya! I can’t get into it right now…..but if I could aid you then I would do it. But right now my knowledge would only detrimental to your plans.” Stryker was obviously taken somewhat off-guard, having counted on the support of his ship-bound informant. Slade pursed his lips in the background, also taken aback. In the shadows, leaning silently against a smokestack, Sensaki scowled. “I’m sorry,” continued Bruce before returning to his quarters. “But right now I’m nothing but a pawn. Do what you can. But if I die…then it’s the proper compensation for my sins.” He left the Delta Fox unit in stunned silence. Stryker could tell there was more to the story, but Shanahan was upset by it. There was credible reason why he couldn’t help and he knew it. But pushing the issue further would gain no ground. So with a command, they boarded the chopper again and soared off into the night. And inside, Bruce could feel the essence of Nyarlathotep gloating.
