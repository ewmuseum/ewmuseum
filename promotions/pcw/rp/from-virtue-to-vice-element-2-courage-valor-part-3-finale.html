From Virtue to Vice: Element 2 – Courage/Valor (Part 3- Finale)

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 11 of 2003 A.D. “This storm is crazy…..that wind, where’d THAT come from?” The council stared outside the windows of the town hall at the darkened village. Only moments before, they had watched as the hurricane-like winds sent a tree cascading to the ground, only to bring with it the crackling wire severed from a nearby telephone pole. The town was abruptly immersed in the blackness of night, the pallid light offered by the moon too meek to illuminate anything with the offer of palpable detail. This had all interrupted the speech of old Lana Williamson, who had begun on yet another of her tangents about things risen from the blackest gulfs of the universe to ravage humanity. Most of the board was more than glad to have her crazed diatribe silenced, but the reintroduction of light via lanterns and candles in the building only refueled her quest to impart her knowledge on her neighbors. “Like I was a sayin’ before, that Bruce Shanahan and his men worship things that done shouldn’t be worshipped by men anywhere near good society. Vastly evil thin’s, dark monstros’ties spawned by forces even feared by the demons a’ Hell. The books he done has in that accursed library left by the Wyatts’, they’re books that should be done destroyed and burnt to ash, for no man should ever hold that sort o’ knowledge. The Wyatts’, back then in the olden days, people said they heard screechin’ and a hollerin’ comin’ from the top of Fire Ridge on certain nights, ‘specially ‘round Hallowmas and May’s Eve. Inhuman sounds, unearthly even. And the shadows seen up there on certain moonlit nights…..” “Enough!” cried John Isaac, whirling a lantern around to face the old woman. “You’re out of your mind, Lana. You heard too many stories from your grandmother, and you’ve certainly delved too far into those evil books your family has passed down through the years. It’s poisoned your mind. You’re as loony as Shanahan.” “I’ve heard the same stuff there, John….” disrupted Randolph. “You’re as gone as she is!” Isaac yelled. “Both of ya, you’re too steeped in that mumbo-jumbo passed down from the old world. Folks are civilized now, we know that sort of thing doesn’t exist.” The tension between Williamson/Harley and Isaac/Dorsey was thick in that cold chamber as the others on the board scrambled about setting up candles and lanterns in areas about the room. “Folks don’t know nothin’!” Williamson spat scathingly. “People fear what they done don’t know, and if anythin’ should be feared it’s what those maniacs are conjurin’ up in that sinister manor!” Young Corey Waltman, an old friend of the now-mad Jude Lambert and member of the town board, nudged Dorsey. “Not sayin’ I agree with all o’ this, but I’m all for callin’ the police. At leas’ just for tonight.” “We’ll be fine!” Dorsey exclaimed, John Isaac nodding his approval. “It’s a little freak storm and a power outage! It ain’t nothin’. We’ll see this thing through courageously and with some self-respect! A year ago the Graham boys disappeared overnight and no one went and got this worked up!” “There also wasn’t a demented cult bent on world destruction nearby then, John Isaac! That couple last week was sacrificed to appease whatever those lunatics claim to worship, and now they’re jus’ becomin’ bolder! A power is bein’ brought into this world, an awful power that was never done meant to be invoked by human bein’s. And with this power is gonna come a presence never meant to tread Earthly ground durin’ the age of mankind.” “You’ve been reading those blasted books of yours too much! We’ll be fine and that’s that!…..” “This is….really weird…..” They all paused their conversation and glanced toward Gail Daniels, stooped over a table and gazing stupefied into the dimly lit night beyond the quaint little town hall. Her head shook furtively back and forth as if in disbelief. “The wind, ya can hear it. But….there is no wind!” She was quickly joined by Dorsey and council member Wilbur Dawson, both sharing in her bewildered sense of awe. “This is bizarre, John,” croaked Randolph as he peered at the still trees despite the howling of a phantom wind. “Sounds like a damn tornado out there, but there ain’t nothin’. Just that howlin’. And somethin’ else….” Everyone simultaneously became aware of the distant piping carried with the howl, its tone like that of a monotonous flute melody being played without end or pause. Several of the board just wandered about the room, listening to the tune as it accompanied the spectral howling. As this realization hit them, a pounding knock reverberated through the room. This was followed by a frantic shouting that echoed from the exterior, and Randolph Harley threw open the door. Karen Paxton stumbled into the room, wide-eyed and gibbering unintelligible words and phrases. “Jesus….it’s Karen. Stan’s wife!” She collapsed onto the floorboards with a thump, sliding up against the wall with arms clasped around her chest. She trembled vigorously as some attempted to calm her. “Dammit,” mumbled Dorsey from across the room. “Not her too….” “She’s been runnin’ from somethin’,” informed Waltman. “Girl’s plumb out of breath. As if somethin’ was chasin’ her or she was damn afraid of whatever she escaped from….” Lana Williamson lay a wrinkled hand on the woman’s shoulder to soothe her shaking, the others pacing about the room shaking their hands in disbelief. Her eyes narrowed into near slits as she gazed into the eyes of Karen Paxton, the pupils wide and glazed with the telltale gleam of insanity. “She saw ‘em.” Dorsey, not looking at the old woman, tried to soothe their disturbed visitor. His curiosity, one of his chief flaws, got the best of him as Karen continued to sob and gibber. “Saw what, Lana?” “Them. The agents of the dawkness called forth by the cult. They are responsible for all’a this. This here presence…..I can feel it in the air. And it ain’t Earthly.” “Lana, shut up!” roared John Isaac, lantern rocking wildly in his hand. “Someone go find Stan and tell him about this!” Isaac wasn’t the least surprised to find none of his peers willing to venture into the ghostly darkness, the howling still having yet to subside and the piping growing increasingly louder. “What the hell is wrong with you all?” he dubiously exclaimed. “Are you letting a little trick of nature and a crazy old crone’s bedtime stories freak you out? Someone at least grab the phone and call over at his goddamn house!” “Tree took out the line, John, remember? Won’t work. We’re between a rock and a hard place here.” “THOSE EYES!! MY GOD THE SHADOWS HAD EYES!!” Kathy uttered a blood-curdling shriek after stammering out those words, lapsing into hysteric sobs and wails. Outside, the unearthly howling had begun to diminish, but the obscene piping had only increased in volume. “She’s lost it,” replied Dawson with a sigh. “Dammit, what the hell is going on in this town?! Nearest next town isn’t for 20 damn miles!” “We can hold out,” assured Dorsey. “Nothin’ we can’t handle.” There was a bumping on the roof, and the building creaked under some force that had seemingly set down above their heads. Shapes seemed to lurch and shift outside the windows, barely illuminated by the lanterns resting by the windowsills. A tension had crept into the air in the last moments. “Somethin’s on the roof…..” whispered Dawson, glimpsing upward. “Dammit, this is unbelievable……” The door burst open and a chill swept through the chamber, swallowing its denizens in its frigid grasp. The exasperated board members turned to see a stocky figure garbed in spangled robes smirking on the walk, winged to his left and right by men garbed in flowing black garments with hoods drawn. “No…..no….” sobbed Gail Daniels at the sight. “This can’t be happenin’….” Shanahan, flanked by a dozen of his men, ambled nonchalantly across the threshold of the hall and stood before the town board, huddled and aghast the center of the room. Lana Williamson, stricken with dismay and horror, finally let out a scream that echoed through the valley and the dark wood encompassing. “What the hell do ya want ya maniac?!” shouted Dorsey. “Isn’t it quite apparent what I crave?” spoke the diabolical cultist in a cryptic timbre. “And what I want….I will have this night. Heheheh…..” His voice was passionless, almost robotic. His expression was somber, altering only when twisted by the smirk distorting his pale lips. Several of his peers clutched daggers and machetes, and thus the horrified party made nary a move to escape or attack. “I, along with my followers, have been besieged by great trials. To emerge through these trials, a sacrifice must be offered. But not a paltry virgin-maiden sacrifice like in fairy tales, no no. The Old Ones demand a much greater offering. They require the blood of those claiming innocence, and with this sacrifice we of the Sect of Black Wisdom will be imbued with the power to crush our foes. Surely you didn’t think I would leave these grounds oh so ripe with lambs without offering them up to my masters?” “You fucking maniac!” bawled the half-mad Karen Paxton from against Dawson’s shoulder. “You…..you killed Stanley! It’s all your fault! GO TO HELL!!” Shanahan, stoic as ever, only shrugged. “No, my dear, ‘twas not I that took your husband’s life. Unfortunately, when the forces of the void are first evoked into this dimension, they require immediate sacrifice to satiate their thirst. Your husband….heheheh……well, he once destroyed one of our altars on Fire Ridge and thus I felt he would make a tasty morsel for the devils I brought into this world.” No one dared confront the maniac. His servants seemed to poised to strike down any who appealed his sentiments. “All of our enemies shall fall, and be swept into the great void between life and death. We have many adversaries who plead for our destruction, and others who we simply are instructed to eradicate. You…..” Bruce extended a finger in the direction of the assembly. They gazed toward him and into the stark night past the open door. They watched together as curious lights, dancing amongst the treetops of the surrounding hillside, descended upon the sleeping hamlet. “Yes, you shall all be numbered among the casualties of the enemy, and your broken spirits shall be reaped to serve the cause of the Old Ones forever. There will be no Christ and no God to save you. There will be only eternity. Eternity serving at the court of the daemon-sultan Azathoth and the immortal children of the outer cosmos. They shall be pleased, and we will benefit from their gratification.” Gail, aware of a scratching at the west window, spun her head to peer into the darkness. Whatever she saw at the window threw her into a fit of hysterics and caused her to hurl herself onto the ground, releasing gut-wrenching cries laden with the same claims of ‘shadows with eyes’ that Karen had burst into earlier. The diabolical Shanahan seemed to revel in her madness, laughing with all the charm of an insane megalomaniac. By this time terror had flooded the previously quiet village, and now screams had erupted from the stillness. The lights had gradually appeared amongst the avenues and in homes throughout the town. Whatever sourced the gleaming emissions remained obscured by darkness, but the horrified screams left in their wake served only to strike greater consternation into those within the walls of Brayston Community Hall. With a wicked grin, Shanahan drew forth a metallic yellow box from the inner pocket of his robes. Williamson’s visage warped into one of utter aversion and dread before letting out yet another ear-splitting cry and collapsing to her knees. She averted her eyes almost shamefully from the infernal relic as Shanahan released the clasp and opened it, revealing a shimmering, red-striated jewel. As it glittered and glistened among the pale light of the candles and lanterns, Shanahan’s words penetrated the minds of his now-entranced victims. “Just as love ultimately betrayed and sealed the fates of Desmond and Kristen Muldoon, so has the passion for courage and valiancy among your hearts. What you all see as virtues and merits, the Old Ones see as frailty and impotence. Stare now into that which will offer your redemption. Let the darkness envelop your minds, embrace your souls.” A shadow passed along the north wall, eclipsing the lights set in the rear of the broad atrium. The lambs, the entire Brayston town board with the exceptions of Lana Williamson and the now-mad Gail Daniels, all still hypnotized by the brilliance of the enigmatic gem embedded in the box, were unaware of the brooding presence that had materialized in the chilled foyer. Only Lana noticed the monstrous fiend creeping about in the scuttling shadows, but hopelessly forlorn and with mental faculties shattered by what she had seen and heard, she could do nothing to warn her neighbors of the demon looming over their bewitched collective. She could only utter a timid gasp before her heart abruptly gave out, and she crumpled to the floor in merciful death. The others that night were not so lucky. For by peering into the Shining Trapezohedron, that crystal harvested outside Earthly domain, they had unwittingly summoned forth that incarnate avatar of the darkness which could not exist in the light. The side windows burst into a veritable cloud of broken glass as other gaunt shapes plunged through the shattered panes, eyes burning with a fire like that of the forges of innermost Hell. Like Paxton, the screams of the dying were drowned by that demonic piping generated in the great abyss as symphony for the daemon-sultan Azathoth. Atop Fire Ridge, where numerous other worshippers danced and reveled in blasphemous orgy amongst the raging ceremonial bonfires, the demon flutists writhed in melodic cacophony amidst the tree-vested shadows. And somewhere beyond this dimension, Nyarlathotep was pleased……
