Who's The Man?

11 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/03/03 Blood. “Has anything ever been more symbolic? To scientists, it’s the thing that carries oxygen to our muscles and keeps us going. You can prick yourself and seal a friendship even deeper as “blood brothers” with someone. You can make a thousand times more impact on a contract signed with it. When you see it, it means you’re hurt. On the battlefield, the difference between the quick and the dead is if it is on your chest or your boot. And without it, you’re nothing. But there’s something else that springs to the mind of a select few when you think of or mention blood. These are the people who are born but suckle not at their mother’s breast, but the cathode of the Television set. The ones who think that the epitome of sexiness is some aging woman in a brown suit and metal on her face, or some prosthetic-laden nerd who happens to speak well and fate placed him in the auditions instead of McDonalds where he belongs. And the other association to them is Vampires. The morons raised on TV dinners and consider discussing last night’s episode of some fantasy show with rubber suits will tell you that vampires are predictable creatures. They all dress in dark clothing, or sometimes fetish wear - Latex, corsets, PVC, things like that. Despite reproducing through their bite, apparently vampires are always up for a quick screw. You’ll also be told that vampires always bite the neck and that they hiss a lot as though they were snakes. Especially at the moment they show their fangs. Don’t forget, they also turn into bats, wolves, mist, or whatever.” Nerezza leaned forwards. The chair beneath him creaked below 260 lbs of weight as he lowered his head. The leathery smell of his own wings invaded his nostrils as he took in another breath to speak. “So why the fuck is it that every time I face a new opponent, I have people saying they went out and immediately found a vampire and killed them, just to say it’ll be easy to do the same to me?” the vampire roared. His wings whipped out like the sails of a ship, knocking down a lamp and plunging the room into darkness. Fluffy, the small grey creature at his feet scrambled and whipped his spines around, quickly taking off in the direction of the closest door. The only source of illumination came from outside, where the neon lights of Akihabara danced around and occasionally peeked inside Nerezza’s main room. “Perhaps, they need some education…?” piped a voice in the corner. Nerezza’s red eyes stayed trained on the window, not straying to the companion. From a few flashes of powder blue light cascading over the room, the man’s face is obscured by some sort of mask. Plastic and covering from crown to chin, the rest of his body is hidden away by a thick gown of some sort, simple and undecorated. “I’m… just tired.” Nerezza sighed. “I grow weary of humans in general. Gaz is possibly the only one I can stand. I don’t even like that girl he hangs around with.” The vampire lifted his head back up, twisting it to see what he could of the shady character. “But what would you know? After all, you’re not human either. Nor are you a vampire. You don’t even have a bloody name.” “If you must refer to me, just call me… a man. That Man. The Man. Whatever pleases you. I’m searching for a name of my own.” replied the man. “Heh… you ghosts are so pitiful. But I’d like to know how you found me. What drew you to me? Surely it’s not a case of mutual dislike for humans?” Nerezza laughed. His false kinsman quickly raised his voice, lifting his own head and staring straight towards the vampire. “I never said I disliked humans!” he said sharply, as though Nerezza had offended him. “Don’t raise your voice to me, wraith.” Nerezza calmly sung, pressing the tips of his fingers together and leaning back. “We’re both at a stalemate. As long as you’re in that body, I can’t drink from it. But if I destroy it, you can’t inhabit it anymore and you certainly can’t take my body. It already has two occupants.” “Very well… now, for your answer. I know of few things from my former life. All I know is I was once like you… a wrestler. I died within the ring, but I knew no peace. I wandered without feeling, without shape. Then I felt a desire to be more, without consequence. With a random choice, I took this man. That is why I will not take a name. I am just a man. That Man.” A long pause ensued between the two. The Man waited for a response while the words echoed in Nerezza’s head. Possession of a random body. Just like the vampire Ireul had taken the body of Matt Jay, this thing had taken someone and forced a new entity. But not of any conscience, nor any determined identity. Just… a man. “What’s that got to do with why you knocked on the door of this apartment?” The sounds of a techno tune blasting from a car outside drew The Man’s attention. He heaved himself to his feet and placed his palms on the wall next to the window, supporting himself as he mask pressed up against the glass. “I need to find out who I am, where I was from, why I’m still here. Something in my… if you’ll forgive the pun… in my spirit pulled me here.” The Man said, gazing out at the sea of people and technology in front of him. “You’re like me… you’re not human, but you’re a wrestler. I’m not human, and I”- “WAS a wrestler.” Nerezza interrupted. “So you want me to abuse my power as a Front Office member and look through the archives of PCW to see if you’re one of the dead people, get a clue about why you’re still here and solve your problems so you can happily pass on to the next life?” The Man nodded. Nerezza smirked and slowly raised himself off his chair. “I have some bad news. There IS no afterlife. No Heaven, no Hell, no Nirvana, no Limbo, no Purgatory, no reincarnation. There’s just rot, decay and if you’re lucky, you’ll get to move about a little.” The Man exhaled in a long, disappointed manner as reality hit him. Nerezza was undead, after all. He also followed the usual traits that most of the older books said about vampires – a point that The Man had noticed. “Yes, and in return, I’ll tell you exactly why people stereotype you. I’ll answer your questions if you answer mine.” He said in a shaky agreement. Nerezza’s mouth almost split apart into a smile. “Then tell me… why am I always mocked in the same way? Who does everyone who faces me suddenly think that their last name is Gellar? Why am I always thought to be the creation of Bram Stoker? WHY DOES CHRIS STORM FAIL TO SEE WHO HE’S DEALING WITH??” Nerezza rasped, raising his arms in resignation. The Man looked towards Nerezza and took off his mask. In place of The Man’s face was skin as smooth as an egg white. The shock of the appearance made even the vampire take a step backwards and switch into a fighting stance. His claws unsheathed themselves and his fangs showed among the rest of his teeth. The Man placed his mask back on and giggled. “It’s because you’re everything they say you are, Nerezza.” He chortled. “Look at yourself… sitting here with your hair all long and down, your wings spread, claws out, teeth bared… and you’ve not even taken on your true form yet. You act savagely with the same air of arrogance that the storytellers said. You vampires deserve the labels you get because you reinforce them. You’re every bit as elitist stuck-up and full of yourself as any gypsy or novelist would write about.” An uneasy silence ensued. … Simple breathing … “Fine.” Nerezza grunted, retracting his teeth and putting on a normal appearance. His claws remained out as he tapped his chin, rolling his eyes to the ceiling of his sparsely lit room. “Actually…” he began.“I do remember something about an ex-wrestler being killed not so long ago. It was fairly low-key, no funeral, but the man in question was due for a big push.” “What was his name?” The Man demanded. Nerezza pressed his claws to his head with a look of resignation. “Guh, damn… let me think, now…” He groaned. “His name was… it was…” “Uhhhhhh………” “Well, you don’t have a name now, do you?” Nerezza purred and withdrew his claw from The Man’s chest. “At least, I was telling the truth in part. But killing off one of Skitzo’s personalities went unnoticed. Just as you, a random nobody both in life AND death shall. Maybe you were that personality. It doesn’t matter. I don’t need to feed and you can’t mock me any longer without your body.” “Wrong.” Nerezza’s eyes opened as wide as saucers as he looked up into a white fog that parted right through in one place to suggest a pair of angry eyes. “And now that body is useless to a creature like you. An undead thing like you can’t take any life-force from it.” “Perhaps…” Nerezza grinned. “Then again, I don’t need to eat anything. On the other claw… Fluffyyyyyyy! C’mere!” The sound of a shrill buzzing filled the room as the chupacabra floated in. His huge, almond shaped eyes glistened as he saw the body on the floor. The smell of blood seduced him as he dropped to the floor, scampering over to the corpse. “Hmph” the fog seemed to say. “Fluffy will eat almost anything.” Nerezza boasted as the chupacabra’s sharp, tube-like tongue darted out from his mouth and sunk into the corpse’s right cheek. A gurgling sound like a child trying to finish up his coke through a straw resounded throughout the room as the chupacabra fed. “But more importantly, he works better than 90% of evidence eliminators you find online. Bones and all.” The fog began to dissipate and clear, the eyes closing. “WAIT!” Nerezza cried. The fog’s eyes opened again and it thickened once more. “Before you leave, I’d like to try something.” Curiously, the fog floated closer. “Yeeeees?” It moaned. Nerezza bared his fangs and claws, cleaving deep into the fog. It parted as though it was white cotton candy being slashed with a white-hot metal bar, moaning in a long, descending howl of pain. Nerezza simply laughed in his usual, sadistic way. Even the pain of the undead was a snack to him. “As I thought… I’m dead enough to exist in the same planes as you. That means I could throw you around like a rag doll if I wanted to. But I’m not going to hurt you.” “Traitor…” the fog gasped. “Traitor? You should have watched your mouth, you mook. If I were you, I’d find myself a body quickly. By the way…” Nerezza cackled. “You should just refer to yourself as The Man. It’s so fitting, a generic term used to describe such a random, undefined person.” And with the final insult, the fog vanished, leaving behind a cold presence. Fluffy sniffed the air, then went back to chewing away on the upper arm of the corpse. “But he did tell me one important thing, Fluffy.” Nerezza said to his pet for the sake of it. Fluffy paid no attention and continued eating. “If Chris Storm wants to play vampire hunter… then I’ll have to do the same. After all, if stereotypes are to be brought into play, then every one of those vampire hunters needs the motivation of having their loved ones killed or turned by one of us…”
