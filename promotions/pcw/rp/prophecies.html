Prophecies.

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: not entered “I do have to say, Big Alan Steele, I am a trifle disappointed in your performance. I expected a personal confrontation far earlier…” We rejoin our Big and Bold hero, Big Alan Steele, as he stands amidst a rounded room of shiny onyx carved by some ancient hand. He heaves, his body glistening with sweat and skin bronzer, as he looks up at a robed figure on an intricate dais, the cloth draping off of noticeably frail bones. The Propheteer looks down at the tattered Steele, only the bottom half of his pale face visible in the limited light. Except those eyes, which glow with an almost unholy inner light. Big Al shrugs, his tattered toga rippling over his broad muscles. “I had some trouble hailing a cab from Death Trap number three, as most were unwilling to accept a slime-coated Greek warrior as a proper passenger.” “Ah… but I already knew that,” the Propheteer says smugly, his shoulders shaking in silent laughter. “You knew?” puzzles Big Al, “Then how could one who knew be surprised?” “Oh, now we see how your comprehension fails you. Though all has been planned out thoroughly in advance, as well as my standing among the elder gods that offers me a certain degree of omnipotence, even I, the Propheteer, Master of Human Chess and Orchestral Maestro of all that is and shall be can still be surprised by the visions of the future that become known to me.” “I find your posturing tiresome, old man,” sneers Steele, “For if you knew of my coming, then surely you would have known your defenses to be less than a challenge for me, Big Alan Steele! A wiser prophet would have used his future knowledge to design a more cunning, more secure entrapment. As is such, your words ring quite hollow.” A tight lipped smile from the Propheteer as he leans forward on his gnarled, wooden staff. “Do you really believe that all has not played out as I have foretold? Do you REALLY believe that you have changed your final destiny? Come now, Big Alan Steele… you do me an injustice. I’m quite hurt at your lack of faith in me.” Big Al bristles, his voice gaining a booming quality, “My faith, as you so aptly put it, rests with Lord Zeus of Olympia, NOT in a falsified prophet bent on manipulating the world to suit his own nefarious purposes!! You may be blessed with the powers of the Oracle at Delphi, but you shall be unable to counter the will imparted to me by the gods!! Hi-Keeba!!” “Bah! Your claims of ‘will’ fall upon bemused ears,” snickers the Propheteer, “You are a mere pawn in the game, my boy, where both sides have been equally controlled by myself alone! I pity you in your lack of understanding at your own insignificance… for there is no honor in destroying a barely sentient being. As it is, your time will come to be sacrificed to enable me to attain a more fitting position on the board. All at my amusement.” Steele shakes in anger below the dais, streaks of crimson flaring up his neck and toughing his cheekbones. The Propheteer laughs aloud, turning his back to the demi-god, as he fiddles with arcane and intricate instruments. “Who do you think allowed you have knowledge of my worldly schemes? I planted the clues beneath your very nose. Who do you think sent forth the various waves of troglodytes to arouse your passion and further the chase? Surely, you don’t believe this all to be YOUR personal quest?! Who would you have supplanted the easily escapable traps for you to progress through, all the while stalling your arrival here, in my domain, whilst I made plans elsewhere? FOOL!! Not ONCE have you held control of your own destiny! I have pulled your strings and made you dance whimsically while I enjoyed your comedic attempts at ‘free will.’ And now you find yourself here. Why? Because I have DEEMED it so!” “Lies!” hisses Big Al, “For it makes no sense to allow me safe passage into yon chamber! I do believe that I have proven a greater agonist than even your visions could have foretold… and soon you shall pay retribution for your devilish crimes against man!” “Just because you do not believe me, doesn’t make it so. But then again, I expected such insulting ignorance from you. I just had not thought it would be so… boring, as it were.” “Merely a false prophet!” retorts Steele, putting his foot upon the first dais step, “For why should one as all powerful as thee devise a mere credit card scam from his register position at Wal-Mart?” “Insolent fool! You dare mock that which you cannot comprehend?! I have lived for eons, changing forms and toiling in many an ‘odd’ job, all the while bending the very fabric of time and space to my every whim! I am the end-all, the be-all. I am a god!” “Blasphemy!” “I control the destiny of every man, woman and child of Earth! Of course I am a god! Even your precious Fates bow before my mighty hand! The paths traveled by your Olympic gods were preordained by myself! I allowed man to discover fire! Wars are waged for my amusement! The ages begin and end at my command! I allowed the world to know the enrichments, and detractions of technology! Puppies are cute because I made it so! Water is wet because I desired it! The sun shines so I may read ‘Tolkien’ in the park! I hold the keys to the Kingdom! Me! Me! Mine! Mine! I’m the god! I’M THE GOD!! BWAHAHAHAHAHA!!!” “You are, indeed, a loon.” “I knew you’d say that. All as I have scripted out in advance,” The Propheteer leers back at Steele, a sinister look on his face, “But I believe this conversation to be nearly at an end. A pity, as while you have proven an unworthy participant, I must admit it is rather enjoyable to allow others to see me in my full magnificence…” Steele begins his way up the steps, pushing up the sleeves on the right arm of his toga. “Indeed, this conversation is at an end, as I tire of your blasphemous posturing! You are nothing but a crazed villainous scourge, though capable in many ways, you lack the art of being a good loser.” The Propheteer finishes with his device, pointing a bony finger at Steele as he advances up the steps, “I KNEW you’d say that!” “Stop it!! Your posturing is baseless! You have lost, old one! Your visions have failed you!” “They have not!! I’ve known of this all along!! Prepare for the final battle! HA HA HA HA HA HA HA!!” Steele pauses on the top step, wary of the Propheteer’s words. The robed man leans forward, malevolent in his ministrations. “I see my words intrigue, as I knew they would. Yes, Big Alan Steele. We shall battle this day… an outcome already prepared in advance!” Steele waits a moment, his eyes darting left and right for a trap. The Propheteer cackles, rubbing his hands greedily. “Yes! COME, Alan! Come and face me upon this platform, as I have seen in my visions!” Big Al places a step on the top and slowly advances. The man’s eyes narrow as he watches Steele advance towards him. Big Alan Steele shoots out an arm, and grabs the Propheteer by the collar, lifting him high into the air with ease. The glowing eyes focus on Steele, his lip curling up into a wicked smile. “It seems,” states Big Al plainly, “That you have not made provisions for our ultimate confrontation! A mistake I intend to exploit, old one!” “Oh, haven’t I?” comes the venomous reply, “Or do you just fail to see that I have knowledge of all, including this little play act we thespians produce at this moment! Even now you play my game by my rules!!” “Hi-Keeba!!” Big Al unleashes a backhand, that send the Propheteer sprawling from the dais, crashing hard to the slick, black floor. A trickle of blood runs down his chin, which he wipes away with the back of his wizened hand as he struggles to his feet. “Ah ha!” he cries out, “The game is afoot!” “And apparently you had not seen that coming.” “On the contrary! I know all of what shall happen!” SMACK! Steele leaps off the platform and crunches a fist deep into the belly of the Propheteer. The air rushes out of the seer, and he collapses again. Steele starts to look around, nervous, as the prophet gasps for air. “I… knew… you’d… do that…” “With an omnipresence such as yours, one would believe you would have made counter-plans to assure my destruction!! What goes on here!” “You… merely play the role… for which you were destined… by my hand of course!” “You are nuttier than an outhouse rat! What man orchestrates his own demise?!” “Your comprehension again fails you,” wheezes the Propheteer, “As can a mere man-god such as yourself foresee what I have in store for you? Dance, puppet, as I shall see you in Hell!!” Steele crouches, expecting an attack, a trap, anything. Nothing. Just a thin, bleeding man in a robe. “I see your hand falters with the killing blow… and thusly you see my plan unfold before your very eyes!!” Steele scowls at the man, giving him another backhand as he contemplates the situation. “Bow before my power!” Big Al raises his hand, and the Propheteer squeals, falling down and covering up. “What sort of treachery is this? I cannot fight one who does not fight back, especially one as frail as he!” “A warrior is not a warrior without a war! See how I manipulate you to the end!” Steele steps forward, reaching out and lifting the Propheteer by the neck, growling in his face. “Then I suppose I shall have to deposit you into Arkham Prison, allowing others to mete out proper justice!!” The prophet gurgles back, “And thus, you fate is sealed!” Steele rolls his eyes, dragging the Propheteer to the exit. “You do not believe me? Who do you think orchestrated your upcoming match?” Big Al stops. “Why, Tyler Nelson, of course.” A snigger. “Fool! I control destinies! Though I have seen my incarceration, I have laid out your OWN destruction! And my hands remain unsoiled by your blood!! My revenge shall be sweet, oh yes. Very sweet indeed!” Big Al holds the Propheteer up and cracks him hard with a closed fist. The man’s eyes roll around in his head. “Well, I didn’t see THAT one coming…” as he falls off into unconsciousness. “Hi-Keeba!” Cut to Arkham Prison, a bruised Propheteer smiles smugly, as he paces around the small outside exercise area, packed with villains. “So… let me get this straight,” a literally faceless bad guy asks curiously, keeping stride with the robed seer , “You won?” “Indeed!” smarms the Propheteer, “As everything went off according to my diabolical plan! Big Alan Steele was HELPLESS as my pawn!” “So… your plan was to get beat up by Big Alan Steele?” “Ah, we see where your comprehension fails you…” he sighs, “Allow me to explain it to you again…”
