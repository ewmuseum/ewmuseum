Never Never Land I: Finale

10 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 10, 2002 A Helping Hand The mysterious man in the blue cloak was fairly spooky looking, but wasn’t something that I couldn’t stomach. After all, I was sitting here in the middle of Never Never Land, wasn’t I? The cloaked man was about five foot three, with graying white hair and a blue cloak that went from his neck to his ankles. His hair spanned all the way down to his hips, and if I wasn’t mistaken, I could have sworn that Merlin the magician was his next of kin. He held a cane in his right hand, which looked like it had been torn straight from the branch of a tree. Yikes. “Uhm,” I began to say, not knowing where to begin. I guess that’s what most people say when they see an old man walk out of thin air. “Did you call for a miracle?” The old man asked, now stepping away from the blue, swirling portal. “I hope you were, because I hate these stupid prank miracle calls.” I watched him move away from the portal, which immediately zipped close once he was out of it’s proximity. I glanced over at Hilda, who had an astonished look on her face. “It’s a miracle man,” She remarked, nodding her oh-so skeleton like face. “I have heard stories of them, but never did I think they were real.” Bobby scoffed. “Miracle man? What the hell is that?” “Excuse me,” The man said, glancing over at Bobby now. “But are you, by chance, addressing me?” If I wasn’t mistaken, I’d say the mysterious old man was being insulted by my cactus. Score one for the Bob. “Yeah,” Bobby replied. “What the hell is a miracle man? I’ve heard of miracle whip, but that’s something you put on pies or directly in your mouth.” Miracle whip directly into your mouth? That’s gross. “A miracle man is someone who passes through the voids of time in order to aid those who are in dire need,” The old man said quite confidently. “I, my friends, have come to your aid.” “Sounds like a good deal to me,” I remarked. “So, what’s the plan?” The old man began to open his mouth, but then had a dumbfounded look on his face. “I haven’t the foggiest clue.” Great, so out of all the miracle men who are passing through the void, I get the one that ‘doesn’t have the foggiest.’ Damn me and all those fortune cookies I used to eat. The old man dropped his cane on the ground and sat down next to Hilda, who still looked like she was in shock. “I used to be so young and so full of energy,” The old man began to say. “My name was Robert, and I had a beautiful home on the west bank of the Omagh river, just over the Never Mountains. Oh, it had luscious forests and the most clearest, most thirst quenching water in all of Never Never Land. And then one day, this fairy came to me and told me that I was to be a miracle man.” As I watched Robert tell his sob story, I immediately began to think of my grandfather, and the stories he used to tell. He would spin tales of ghosts and wild locomotives that would ricochet past the farm that he used to live on. Then he would go off on the fact that he never wanted to be a farmer, and in reality, loved mathematics. Yes, I know – someone in this galaxy that loves math? It must not be true, right? Wrong. “So, why didn’t you just tell them that you didn’t want to be a miracle man?” Bobby asked. “You don’t just tell them no,” Robert replied, sighing once more and slumping over a little more. “Once your destiny is set, it’s set for good.” He chuckled. “Serves me right for thinking that I was going to be an average Never Man.” “So this is what you do,” I said. “You go around, waiting for people to ask for a miracle, and suddenly you appear in a big light show and save the day?” “Not in so many words,” Robert said with a grimace. “You wouldn’t believe the world that I live in. Inside that portal is just a trans dimensional elevator. I can see into different worlds, times, alternate universes…there are so many miracles being asked for, I couldn’t possibly answer them all.” “So what made you pick us?” Hilda asked. “A whim, I suppose,” He said shrugging. “I hadn’t been to Never Never Land since Hook’s ship first took up post here so many centuries ago. That seems like such a long time ago. I think the last time I stopped by here, I was helping out this one fellow…what was his name? I believe he was a boxer in the Earth realm…” “Mike Tyson?” He waved his hand in a negative fashion. “That ear biting lunatic? No way…” “Lennox Lewis?” “I hate England,” He responded, turning his nose in a disgusted manner. “It’s always raining and so dreary. It just flat out depresses me. No, I believe his name was Muhammad Ali. Yes, that was it. A big man that could always talk up a storm. I remember he had this big championship match, and he asked for a miracle so that he could knock out his opponent in the first round.” “And you helped him,” I said, remembering the story. “That was Sonny Liston. Ali knocked him out in the first round, in the first minute of that match.” “It was beautiful,” Robert said, nodding his head. “Oh well. I guess I should help you folks out now. So, where do you want to go?” I looked over at Bobby with a skeptical look. Ol’ Merlin the magician wanted us to step into his portal so we could get catapulted into another whacked out universe. That didn’t sound too appealing to me. “I’m not sure,” I admitted. “How about back to the Earth realm?” “But that’s so boring,” Robert complained. He stood up and picked up his cane, and with a “Z” motion, opened up another portal. “But if that’s where you want to go, let’s get out of here. I believe Hook will be here soon.” Hilda paused. “How do you know that?” Robert looked back at the skeleton woman before stepping back into the portal. “My dear lady, I know everything. Now come. We must be on our way.” And with that, he disappeared into the void, leaving me, Bobby and Hilda looking at each other. “What do you guys think?” I asked. “It sounds a little sketchy to me, but choice do we have?” Bobby asked. “It’s either step into the glowing hole or face the vengeance of the hook.” Hilda nodded. “I think your cactus friend is right. We should follow him.” We all slowly walked towards the glowing hole which beckoned to us, and suddenly, we disappeared. Oh, the humanity. Go Egypt When my eyes opened, I didn’t find myself lying in bed, next to me a cuddly teddy bear. I found myself surrounded by sand and wind, and I realized that I was in my own virtual hell. Oh my gosh, I think I was in Egypt. My mouth was agape as I stared up at a mountainous pyramid before me. It was only half finished, with the crest of the structure still under construction. It looked marvelous from my standpoint, especially since I had seen the version that had gone through a millennia of environmental catastrophe. I looked over to my right side, where I saw Hilda standing there holding Bobby. She looked as surprised as I was, and maybe if she had some skin, she’d look pale with shock. Instead, she was just a skeleton. “No, no, no!” Robert shouted behind me. As I looked behind myself, Robert came running up, his arms full of maps. “This can’t be right! This isn’t your Earth realm.” “Actually, I think it is,” I replied. “Only, it’s not quite the right time.” “Where did we take a wrong turn?” He asked. As he said that, his maps fell to the ground and he scrambled to pick them back up. Bobby just laughed. “Not the best time to be making fun of our would be miracle man, Bobby.” “Oh come on, Tom,” Bobby replied. “Can’t you see the humor in all of this?” I looked around myself, and shook my head in disbelief. “I don’t see the humor in being stuck in ancient Egypt.” “We’re getting to see a part of history, Tom,” Bobby said. “Take it for what it’s worth. Besides, who knew that aliens made the pyramids?” I paused. “What?” I looked up at the crest of the pyramid, only to see a bunch of green beings hauling up stone blocks to the top. I stared in amazement. It was always rumored and talked about that humans could never have made these incredible structures, but never did I think that aliens had done them. Where was their space craft? “I don’t believe it,” I murmured. One of the beings noticed us near the pyramid, and leapt all the way from the top to our position below. Obviously, he startled us. “MEHA KRESHOB BEEP BEEP DOBOOP!” It bellowed at us. “I think he’s talking to you,” I said to Bobby. “Har har.” The being was no taller than three feet, with big, blue eyes and a head larger than your average frying pain. It cocked its head to the side and appeared to be adjusting something in it’s ear. Suddenly, it looked back at us and smiled with its non existent mouth. “Sorry about that,” It said to us. “Universal translator is off. Damn things are always acting up.” “Oh yeah, no problem. Happens to us all the time.” “So, what are you earthlings doing here near these pyramids?” It asked. “According to our calculations, human beings aren’t supposed to be in this region for another few centuries.” “We’re not actually from this time,” I replied. “We took a wrong turn somewhere and ended up here.” “AH!” It exclaimed. “Time travelers, eh? We dabble a bit in time travel, although we find the risk is much more greater than the reward. We could send you back if you’d like, although I must ask how you got here in the first place.” Robert raised his hand. “I believe I’m the fellow you’re looking for. Robert the Miracle Man, at your service.” “A miracle man?” It said with a puzzled look on its face. “You guys still exist?” “Well, I am the last.” “Serves you right. The last I heard, the majority of you all lost yourselves in time because you forgot how to use your time canes.” The being looked at me and shook its head. “Miracle men mean well, but all of that time travel makes you forgetful. Suddenly you forget little things like your name and how old you are.” The being pressed a button on its arm. “I’ll send you back. You won’t remember any of this, who I was, or even this entire adventure. I hope you don’t mind.” “Well, actually…” ZAP! BOOM! POW! Too late. (Wait, what was I saying? Oops, you already forgot. Damn aliens and their time technology).
