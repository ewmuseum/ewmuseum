A Meeting Of Minds

27 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1 - 27 - 03 Only an hour or so after the largely successful Shanghaied pay-per-view, the locker rooms have already cleared. Stars and jobbers alike are packing their stuff and returning to the hotels for much needed rest and cable television. Two in particular, who had a rather tension-filled night, are already there. “So please enlighten me, my Goddess…” “Yes, Dorn?” “What’s the next phase? The blasphemer is obviously pissed off at our proverbial ‘cleansing’ of his stagnate locker room,” Dorn exclaims, with a satisfied, dark look on his face. His trademark black shades block any indication of emotion through the eyes, but chances are, he’s probably squinting while remembering the whole event. A lone, strange man known only as Alan Ackart to Dorn and En’Flarim decided it was probably best to piss off the lovely Watcher of All by running into her and spilling water on her golden garment. Dorn noticed and proceeded to calmly inquire as to why the foolish idiot would do such a thing. Alan gave him a bad attitude and Dorn then demanded that the blasphemer apologize, which he refused to do. The Astronomer was only seconds away from creating a giant crater in the concrete of the arena floor from Ackart’s body, but the kindness in the heart of En’Flarim forbade him from doing so. However, as necessary, a punishment was carried out… a message, of sorts. Dorn TORCHED Ackart’s entire locker room in secret, strangely enough, since it should be pretty obvious when something’s burning, and left his new mark. But… it’s not over yet. “I shall show you when we arrive at our accommodations.” “As is your decree, my Goddess…” Dorn gives a slow nod as a sign of respect and continues down the long, marble corridor of the hallway. The floor is unknown, but the number plates on the passing doors read in the 500s and the 600s, so they’re most likely on a high floor. A long, multi-colored carpet stretches before them light a beacon, signaling them further with its pictures of strange animals and acts of sexual manner on those strange animals … or so it appears. Upon reaching the elevator, Dorn drops the couple of suitcases he’s carrying with a thud and reads the numbers on the buttons. Sighting a ‘35’, he presses it, getting a bright green glow out of it. He takes up the bags again and waits at the mechanical whir of the elevator comes closer and closer to their position. En’Flarim, in her glowing golden dress and tiara, stands patiently with a gentle, calculating look in her bright blue eyes as they await the elevator. DING! Moments later, the elevator arrives and the door slides open. Both warrior and Goddess step in, getting a strange stare from the little old lady within. They crowd her and stand right in front of the door, blocking any wanted exit. And if that wasn’t bad enough… “My star-born gladiator…” En’Flarim asks softly. “Yes, my Goddess?” “I believe I have a plan for disposing of this Ackart mortal…” “Yes?” “A proverbial, blood-filled sacrifice by impaling him with weapons of steel, then torching his body high in the night sky for all his blasphemous mortal kin to see.” At the sound of this, the old lady spits her teeth out in pure horror and gasps, backing up against the polished wooden wall. Dorn turns and looks at her, giving her a small, awkward smile. “Oh don’t worry, ma’am, it’ll all be very quick. We haven’t the time to deal with him further.” By now, the poor old lady has probably shat her pants. But that’s all well and good, because the elevator floor marker has reached ‘35’, and it’s time for the Lunis Superia and Dragon Goddess to leave. The silver-colored doors of the elevator slide open and the two exit onto a floor identical to the one they were just on, except with different door markers. The two travel in silence down the hallway until they reach ominous door number ‘700.’ Dorn drops the two suitcases again and takes out a small white plastic card for his coat pocket. He slides it in the small locking device in the door, and then withdraws it quickly, signaling a green light. A small ‘click’ is heard and Dorn opens the door to the hotel room. He holds it open and allows the Golden Goddess to enter, then enters himself. “Abode sweet abode, my Goddess.” “Indeed… I feel rather small and hollow in this filthy pit.” “I apologize… it was all I could afford…” Dorn hangs his head in shame and kneels before the Golden Goddess, who merely smirks and crosses her thin, shapely arms. “I can easily forgive you, my warrior. I’m afraid we have no time for any petty matters, for there is a true issue at hand.” Dorn looks up with an inquiring, almost ‘puppy’ look to his eyes and tilts his head. “My Goddess?” “I shall have to make sure you’re mentally ready for the task at hand, and it should be… quite interesting.” “How are you to do that?” “You shall see…” The Dragon Goddess clasps her hands together, closes her eyes, and begins to mumble to herself in a strange, echoing language which Dorn doesn’t recognize. The room begins to swirl in a whirling storm of colors with En’Flarim being the vertex. Dorn jumps to his feet and looks around wildly, completely shocked at what’s going on. “WHAT THE…??” Dorn yells, feeling the swirling madness pulling at his body. Soon, the colors begin to melt away into an unforgiving red and black maelstrom, in which En’Flarim and Dorn now find themselves. Various images of Dorn at a variety of ages flash through the whirling madness, giving the Astronomer only a brief second to observe them. En’Flarim ceases her trance and nods. “Welcome to your mentality, my warrior.” Dorn is speechless, however. The images flashing before his eyes are ones of sadness, sorrow, horror, and anger. His father leaving the family… his mother dead in the backyard… a kid assaulting him at school for merely running into him in the hallway… all things that twisted Dorn’s personality and character during the fragile stages of his life. He has to turn and close his eyes from the frightening images, as they tear at his bits of sanity. “Why have you brought me to this place…” He stammers, really not understanding. “As I said, Lunis Superia, to test your mentality. I don’t think you’re truly ready for the task at hand. And the task is NOT Alan Ackart, but the entire blasphemous human race!” Dorn just merely stubbles onto one knee and hides his eyes away from the horror that is the reality of his life. “I… don’t know…” “Having doubts already?? That is unacceptable, and I predicted you would feel this way. Remember your childhood, Dorn?” Dorn nods slightly, remembering clearly. “You were either too weak, too clumsy, or too stupid to do anything worth remembering, yet your entire life has been a series of memorable moments. Unfortunately, they all look bad upon you,” En’Flarim exclaims, with that smirk upon her beautiful face. Noticing Dorn’s increasing irritation to anger, she continues on. “In fact, your life under my watchful eye has been a stringed event of mishaps, all of which I rightfully predicted.” “WHAT?” Dorn shrieks. He can’t believe his ears. “You mean that YOU killed…” The Goddess nods and Dorn can feel his entire body being pulled into some abyss of sorrow. His knees give away and he cradles his head in his head, trying to hold back the wave of tearing emotions that are now clawing at his consciousness. “Why…” “Because if you hadn’t been exposed to the grim reality of the way Fate works, you wouldn’t have been strong enough for the task ahead,” She snarls, getting rather irritated with the questions from her servant. “Just realize that this is not the last you’ll see of my work.” “You mean there’s more?!” Dorn roars, not believing any of this. “Of course there’s more… there’s so much more…” [End.]
