The Tape - Part 3

9 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/05/03 The sound of equipment shutting down flooded around the factory. The sinking whine of deactivating machinery slowly faded into silence, leaving only one more noise – a faint giggling coming from the far end of the main production room. The lights dimmed, the conveyors belts stopped and finally, only one red orb of illumination and one movement remained. Nerezza’s red eye glowed in the dark and his chest shook as he continued to cackle to himself. Finally, the factory fell silent, save for one more noise, the slamming of a door. Production complete. ~ Nerezza clamped a padlock onto the latch of the factory doors and took a step back. For the first time since the incident that had him cocooned in metal and trapped under the water, he tossed the mask of hair over the back of his head and stared with the eye that had been kept in the dark for so long. His mouth transformed from the long, horizontal slit into a normal sized jawline, closing up the slice on his face. For a second, Nerezza almost looked human again. “Only one saved…” he sighed, shaking his head. “Perhaps, I should apologise. Justice for this planet should be justice, after all. Maybe not everyone should suffer…” “It’s you…” someone spoke from behind him. Nerezza spun around quickly, dislodging his hair back into the front of his face, splitting his mouth apart again and staring through his left eye once more at his accuser. Standing in front of him in jeans and a thick fleece coat was a girl in her late teens, with dark hair blown about by the wind. She stared directly into Nerezza’s eye through her glasses, coming to the realization that Nerezza was indeed the guilty party. “Who are you…?” Nerezza croaked, lowering his head inquisitively. A long silence ensued as the two stared at each other in the light of one solitary streetlamp in the yard in front of the factory. Neither knew what to say next, nor would either do anything physically. Eventually, it took the barking of a dog nearby to break the silence. Nerezza’s eye squinted as he looked at her, peering past her, into her. “You… you’re the one.” “I tracked the manufacturer of the tape. It’s… kinda easy. But I never expected it to be you… why, Nascosto? Why are you doing this?” the girl asked, with tears welling up in her eyes and trickling down her cheeks. Nerezza extended an arm out to her, literally, to pat her on the shoulder. “You must understand this. The human race is an anomaly. It’s a genetic accident that has let to a species of primate receiving an imbalance of chemicals in the brain. This imbalance upset the natural harmony of the planet. Vampires are the remainder of a species which was wiped out by greedy humans, now forced to live parasitically inside the bodies of infected humans. Vampires are technically extinct, only the ones which infected humans live on now. Bit by bit, we must either convert or eradicate humans to stop them destroying Earth and themselves with it.” Nerezza explained with an air of self-righteousness. “But isn’t that just as bad as humanity? Isn’t this just a grudge match that you people are taking too far?” the girl pleaded, pulling her glasses off her face and making direct eye contact. Upon seeing her face, distraught and red, the vampire pulled some of her hair away from her face and tilting her head up to face him. “What’s your name?” He asked, sympathetically. “Donna…” she replied, sobbing. “Donna… alright. Then know this, if nothing else. The acts that have taken place over the past week are an act of vengeance on behalf of us all. But you suffered innocently. You are spared…” he softly spoke, then looked away from her, up towards the dark sky and the blanket of stars. “But the others… this was a test to see if they were the same. I may have stopped this genocide if they had not committed the same crimes. But it still hurts me to see that so many are guilty. Only one passed my little test.” “So… Darren…?” Donna whimpered, looking hopefully up at Nerezza. The monster shook his head and closed his eye. “Guilty” he wheezed, withdrawing his hand from her face and turning to his right to leave. “So you’re going to let me go home to a dead brother… you cold bastard.” she stuttered, trying to stop herself from exploding into tears and rage. Nerezza stopped in his tracks, slowly turning his head around and smiling a face full of needles. “Consider the alternative… you may never go home at all as you are. There is hope for you… but I don’t need a follower so bitter. Maybe some other time…” the vampire said, issuing the grim prophecy. Donna closed her eyes and opened her mouth as far as she could. “WHAT DID WE ALL DOOOOOOOOOO!?” Her answer never came. When she opened her eyes and finished screaming, her conversation partner had vanished without a trace. Crushed by the knowledge of what may happen, Donna turned around. With no proof other than the feeling of a cold thumb on her cheek and just another abandoned warehouse to show, she turned and took the first step back towards her home… knowing that her brother had been judged “guilty” and by the time that she arrived home, even in a car, his death sentence would have been carried out. ~ Darren sat in his bed, propped up by pillows and gazing at a basketball game. Sitting on a shelf above him were various objects with no seeming connection – a small neon fish no longer than an inch, a crowbar, a toy boat and a hand buzzer. The room was engulfed in darkness, with only the light of the screen providing enough glow to light the room. The game was in the 3rd quarter so far, ticking down to the final seconds. Darren moved around a little, trying to get comfortable. Then something caught his eye. The screen remained still in a freeze frame and sound suddenly cut from the feed. “Stupid video…” he grumbled and reached for his remote control. The device did nothing despite the rapid pressing. The game’s clock was frozen at 00:07 and refusing to move. Then the clock on his video suddenly altered to show 77:77, joined in by his bedside clock. A figure from the crowd of spectators on his video stood up, dressed in a red sweeping coat, despite the rest of the audience not moving. Darren strained his eyes to make sure it wasn’t just a trick of the light, but the man kept walking towards the camera, his face masked by a mass of purple hair. Slowly swaying from side to side, he stalked towards the screen. Darren began to panic, now literally punching the power button for his TV. It failed him and the man placed one hand in front of him, as though feeling blindly. As his palm hit the screen, it emerged from the TV’s confines as though it was passing through a waterfall. Darren opened his mouth wide to shout, but as he did, a torrent of dirty river water poured out, spilling rocks, small fish and chunks of rotten flesh onto his lap. “…” He closed his mouth again, horrified and too scared to move. Meanwhile, the man continued to drag himself out of the TV screen, pulling his foot free. On his hands and knees, the man paused briefly before scuttling forwards like an insect, then slowly brought himself to a standing position. Still masked by his hair, he advanced towards the petrified boy. The last thing Darren saw was his own reflection inside a wet red circle inside a white horizontal oval… ~ Donna returned home, pale from fear and the cold. Removing her scarf from around her neck, she took each step upstairs one at a time in contrast to her usual zipping up and down them. Her instincts pulled her away from her room and across to her brother’s. Opening the door slightly, her fingers crept towards the lightswitch and pressed it on. Taking a deep breath, she opened the door fully and walked in with her eyes closed. “NOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOO!” Lying in his bed amongst a mixture of water, blood and most of the contents of the Mississippi riverbed, Darren’s pale corpse rested with his mouth forced wide open to inhuman capacity. In his neck were two punctures with faint red markings around them. Nerezza’s words echoed inside her head and Donna finally realized the meaning of his anger at humanity. “But the others… this was a test to see if they were the same. I may have stopped this genocide if they had not committed the same crimes. But it still hurts me to see that so many are guilty. Only one passed my little test.” she whispered softly. The answer to why she was spared was written on the wall in Darren’s blood. “Please be kind. Rewind.”
