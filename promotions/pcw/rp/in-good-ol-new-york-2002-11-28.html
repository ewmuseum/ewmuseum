In Good Ol' New York!

28 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 28th November Time 0700 Date November 28th FF#4 New York New York… Insight into the man that is Kai’s spiritual guidance. The city provided an immediate answer to the problem of escape. Anonymity. Amongst eight million citizens, and three million transients, it is easy to get lost. The streets are scattered with entrances to crowded subterranean tunnels. Thousands of enormous buildings, stores, and theatres provide instant invisibility and sanctuary. The streets themselves clogged with traffic, make pursuit of the swift but impossible. The attitude of the average citizen – Matsushima thought it charitable to call them uninvolved – made them willing accessories before, during and after the fact; the average citizen in New York City were not out shopping, no, they were fighting a daily battle to survive. This, Kai strived to change. Knowing the answer to the question of escape, given a security climate of ordinary difficulty, he focused his attentions on targets of opportunity. He found his answer in the proliferation of two new facilities – bank cash dispensing machines, and the wide variety of athletic outlets. It took a while to test the waters, the robbery of Muffy Grant’s was only the beginning of something a lot bigger. One had to learn which robbers were worth robbing and which were not. Through a Japanese/Shaolin businessman who was keeping an expensive blonde in an apartment on the east side, as well as his family in Westchester, on a salary as a sales manager for an electronics firm, he knew how to dispose of the goods to the people who needed them. Matsushima, using time and intelligence at his disposal, planned and blueprinted a dozen variations on the taking of money from the bank consumer who stood foolishly trapped, waiting for the return of his card and receipt, with a handful of cash in front of a machine. No doubt, Matsushima was the mind behind Kai’s continuing spiritual enlightenment. He helped him apply philosophical ideas to a bustling metropolis, whilst keeping him fresh and sharp. There were nighttime plans, and afternoon plans, plans for the east side and the west. Athletics offered many opportunities; Matsushima diagrammed locations and possibilities down to the timing of the last detail. He was also detailing a profile on Abner, so Kai could come away with the victory. He had detailed several match strategies for different outcomes, for different points in the match. Abner…surely…Kai thought implicitly, not allowing anything to block his concentration. While he recognised the part that luck plays in life, he was a firm believer that discipline and training, combined with stamina and the deadly weapon of Taijiquan, would make the operations for the clan a success. Through his studies, Matsushima had become an expert in the format and layout of most of the better class gymnasiums and clubs in New York. It was very easy for him to have a thorough tour, and if that did not suffice, a second. He was the ideal client for the profit-making club. The home office would doubtless pick up a highly paid foreign executive with a fat paycheque, whose dues and initiation fee were large. With a little luck, they might buy a corporate membership. When he needed to see the women’s facilities, he brought his wife questioning her closely on the layout and amenities of the club. After six months, he joined a gaudy health club to legitimise his search to Mrs. Matsushima. Although unknown to Kai, Matsushima had begun his master plan; this would enable all of the poorer community of New York to live in relative comfort for some time. It would be his masterpiece of criminal genius. Matsushima planned to heist the most elite and accomplished health club in all of New York. The Angles. ~**~ Kai lay wrought, rigid on the icy floor of his warehouse dwelling; the only sign that he was alive was the slow rising of his stomach as he breathed in life giving oxygen. Kai slumped deeper into the rut and waited patiently for deaths sweet kiss. It would not come…Kai had become too strong to quit now. He rose from his pains, using the nearby wall to push himself to his feet. Mustering the last vestiges of strength he had, he drove hard with his legs. Kai stood swaying side to side, as if under the tenuous grip of alcohol. The air was fresh with a new chill as winters theme was beginning to settle in the night air. “Has Jack Frost lost his mind?” Kai tried to humour himself; he failed, as the icy November air whipped through his clothes, sending chills shooting up his spinal column. Perhaps this time…he had gone to far. Even Robin Hood had a crib…
