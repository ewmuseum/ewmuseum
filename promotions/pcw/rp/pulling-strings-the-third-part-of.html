Pulling Strings, The Third Part of

undated

Date: January 26,, 2003 The road starring in front of his headlines struck out while running yellow lines passed him by like early nights and late days. The focus, the drive, it wasn’t there. All it was for now he wasn’t doing this for anyone anymore, not Tracy, not his daughter, not even his family. Maybe this was what Eric Crouch was feeling when he retired before his rookie season ever began? His eyes, slowly a midnight black in short, oval shaped pupils surrounded by black skin and sticky eyelashes that hung from him more than they did his own wife. These eyes have seen daily events and not one of them has shocked him for those pupils to grow wide, or shrink down afterwards Cuts and bruises have been healed by doctors, outstanding with high intelligence quotas, licenses and bars examinations to go along with diploma upon bachelor’s degrees upon Master. The list ran more odd sayings than a Chinese Phonebook, and all of those had to do with some reason, some retarded form of misconception that he couldn’t swallow. A mere speck of what used to be his usual repertoire of wearing flashy jewelry and have his wife on his arm like a supermodel pendant wrapped around your finger, those were gone as well. All that lay left were a man crying for help. “Shitty radio stations, never play the good music late at night.” He flickered with popular stations all based around the ports and seaways of Houston’s mixed community. He couldn’t trust major black stations because all they would do, whether it is on the hip-hop, or jazz, or oldies bevy of choices, would play mainstream acts. “Ja Rule, Ashanti, the hell?” He stops himself, before looking over and seeing his CD case sticking out like a beacon of hope. The black case with zipper pulled back three quarters to full exposure has seen its shares of good quality and filler placed in by its owners. Dante Jackson on the other hand pops in a quick Nas compact disc, “Illmatic”. A classic, nonetheless, it was something that brought him back to the roots, where he wasn’t always known just for his skills on the microphone. He could hear his father hovering above him, shouting obscenities about, ‘how this move wasn’t perfected correctly’ or ‘you need more time to do this, work on this skill’. Comparing him to anybody else, it burned him deeply like a cut right above the wrist. Logged in his right pocket was a plane ticket on Air Japan, a distinct brand of Asian dining and customary flight travel for him to Shanghai, Japan – and a meeting with the rest of the crew before the standardize seven o’clock Showtime curtain rise. “Why the hell Kannon wants to book a show on the date of the Superbowl is beyond me,” he studied the dates of everything to a niche, not in perfect order, just as long as he could fit a schedule to fit those dates. “hope they carry the game over there.” It was a hell of a chance that he would even be able to make the halftime festivities. Game started at six eighteen eastern, meaning it would probably be Monday morning in Japan, close to even telling what time he’d get back on his flight. “Damn, and now I’m barely out of gas!” he shouts at the car odometer, as the gas dial reads closer and closer to E, signaling for empty. “Shit!” he roared once more. The car, a Mercedes truck with black tint and larger than life twenty something inch wheels and gimmicky rims called spreewells stops in front of the Bayou, streaming water flushing from pipelines drawn in from Downtown manholes, the rivers of Houston aren’t naturally clean, yet the people accept it. His eyes fixate upon the small spacious lot in which he once thought was abandoned. The lights are on, as silence embarks upon the house, not a soul rest inside as Dante thought to himself as he shut off the ignition with a flick of his wrist. “Great, now how am I going to get gas? I didn’t even decide to bring my phone.” He mutters to himself before stepping outside to frigid weather, rubbing his arms together to keep the blood flowing through his veins. “Hello?” Looking ahead at the small lot, now shrouded by a run down factory, dankly put up with coiled steel and faded green pastures, Dante takes his steps towards the area, checking for anyone to give him a hand. In his mind, it’s more like a suicide trip for the mentally impaired, no sane person would take something like this on. All except the man taking slow paces as his Timberland designate their imprints into the soil. Howling winds chase him down, as a hood covers his head, walking and inching closer and closer to his very own shelter for the night. Pushing the metal hangar like doors to the side, he steps in, a slight creek rumbling through the shack as Dante calls out, “Hello?” The lights flicker on, in an odd “Field of Dreams” oddity towards them, before Dante looks ahead at what’s facing him, stripped down ring ropes sharply made from fibers and wires used in standardized rings as of today. No insignia placed in the middle of the mat, only the blank empty spot for house shows. “What,” the sound of his voice was booming, before a man stepped inside of the ring, quietly and cautiously making his way through the ropes. “Seems like you’re lost, prag.” “Prag? This isn’t no prison and I’m sure as hell not some pretty ass white boy that kills people for contract killings and films them on television, although there was this one time –“ The man clears his throat, “Young one, shut the fuck up!” Dante slants his head, before the ropes dim down upon an old Caucasian man, no more than forty-six with stalking gray hairs and a woven sweatshirt on along with the jogging pants to match. “I’m sorry, but you are?” Dante questions. “Don’t you think that you’re supposed to climb inside of this ring and figure out just who I am?” the man rested his shoulders along his appealing chest, although he might not have been one of the more physical men to ever step in a ring, it was known that he was officially someone that had experience at the job. “Old man, I’ll break you.” “Heh,” the old man chuckled with slight gasp in-between words, as he moved closer and closer to where Dante happened to be. “Names Gotch, Avery Gotch. And I’ll be your torturer for this evening. “Go for it.” Dante responded, and the bell rung inside both of their heads.
