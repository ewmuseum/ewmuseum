Becoming Mad III: Understanding

19 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5-19-03 The scene opens up to a park bench painted green freshly with in the last few days obvious from the bright green splotches located directly below. The park blooms beautifully on this fresh spring morning, the trees stand tall and the even the grass seems to have taken on a more brilliant tone. A stone circular water fountain spouts up as a few white birds scavenge around the land for breakfast morsels and leftovers. Seated upon the green bench is A.J. Trinidad, fresh off his win on Friday Fusion, and the man with whom Trinidad has been traveling, dressed in his normal white suit, pressed neatly book-ended by a white top hat and freshly polished black dress shoes. “What you’ve spoken of thus far has all been true,” Trinidad admits to his companion, “but what I feel unsure about is how you knew St. Jon and my time spent in Argentina?” The man shakes his head from left to right slightly before peering up into A.J.’s face with a look of frustration cresting his brow. “You ask too many questions at what are undoubtedly very inappropriate occasions. What you fail to realize is that these facts that you waste your time worrying about are very inconsequential to where your life will go. Waste your time on such meager issues and you will find yourself here, in the same place ten, twenty, thirty years down the line. Continue to live in the past and sulk in what has happened and you’ll continue to stand in this same spot.” “I don’t understand” states Trinidad, “but for some reason I’m beginning to get the feeling I’m not supposed to at this point.” The man dressed in white stands up from the bench brushing down his suit to remedy any lines or wrinkles, and beckons for A.J. to walk with him. Apprehensive at first, Trinidad slowly arises and begins to follow, but a few steps behind as the man dressed in white begins to speak again. “Do you remember all of the things that St. Jon had said to you on your first visit with him?” he asks to A.J. “Almost exactly.” A.J. responds. “Why?” “Do you remember when St. Jon spoke of your destiny?” The man asks while his eyes remain closed deep in thought. “St. Jon was always confident you would find your destiny in time, that you would have some trouble formatting to the program and taking the first step. His elders had always thought that you would be too stubborn, but against their wishes, you remained. He saw in you what nobody else could, he saw you as the man to make the difference, and believed it so blindly that he slowly convinced the council, and one very important being to believe it as well.” “I am still not understanding Mr….. um, I don’t even know your name.” Trinidad states. “Men like me really don’t have names,” the man responds. “You can refer to me as Mr. White if you must.” “OK Mr. White then,” A.J. begins, “I had thought that I had already fulfilled my destiny after leaving St. Jon and Argentina behind. I received his packages and followed them all the while through the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament almost over a year ago already.” “You fail to see that the GTT was only just another training tool for you. We had to see if you could withstand the competion at that point in your maturity eventually pitting you against some of the best, and possibly the best figure on the earth. We had learned many things through your run in the GTT, but most importantly we had learned that you were indeed not ready, not ready to accept your responsibility, and certainly not ready to walk your line. So we took a step back and began this form of waiting game with you in hopes that eventually you would come around and we’d be able to move forward once again.” “And now you think I’m ready?” Trinidad asks. “Not exactly.” The man simply responds. “You know your headaches as of late, you loss of consciousness and over all feeling of disembodiment?” “Yeah, sure, the Doctors said it was stress and a viral infection, they gave me medicine to clear that up, “Trinidad explains. “Amira was even there, she can vouch for me, just let me call her.” Beginning to remove a cellular phone from his pocket, Mr. White calmly reaches out his hand and stops Trinidad from dialing any phone numbers. “You don’t need to prove anything to me, I already know what has been ailing you and trust me when I say that you are only a short time away from being freed of that burden, just be patient and bare with me.” “Well, let me at least call Amira then and touch base.” A.J. pleads. “You cannot have any contact with her right now, and even if I did let you call, she would not be there to answer the phone.” Mr. White explains in a humble tone. “What do you mean?” A.J. asks. “Go ahead, try to call.” Mr. White encouragingly suggests. Trinidad reaches down and hits his speed dial number 1 button to phone straight to Amira. A.J.’s face shifts in a quirky manor as he fully expects Amira to pick up on the other end. Phone mannerisms of people when they know that they are being watched can be quite humorous when the person watching them already knows what is going to happen. Mr. White remains across from A.J. almost mimicking his every movement as A.J. brings the phone from his ear and lets his arm fall lifelessly to his side as he turns away from Mr. White. “What did you do with her?” A.J. asks in a very soft, confused voice. “She is of no importance anymore A.J., you must realize this to move on, let her go and everything will be fine.” Mr. White explains. “NO! That’s Bullshit!” A.J. screams out as he whips around to face Mr. White but suddenly freezes as he notices that the park is now filled with strangers walking around, but Mr. White has disappeared. Trinidad searches around furiously for the man dressed in all white, turning from side to side, his head looks as if though it was on a swivel but to no avail, Mr. White was indeed gone, and apparently, so was Amira.
