Memories Turned Into Ash

3 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/3/03 Outside the houses frame was burnt down to the paneling, and the outside was stained with ashes, but inside it was even worse. As shameful as it may seem I had no regrets about what happened. The house burned down, the cause at the moment unknown and now my parents turned to me for a place to live, yet still it bothered me none. Perhaps their ruined paradise would cause them to realize I was worth a lot more to them then they could ever imagine or it would make them regret me even more. “Tessa what are you doing here?” Shouted my mom from her car which was parked several feet away from the disaster area. Her voice expressed that she didn’t want me here, even if I was only here to offer a helping hand. “Holy…” My mouth started up as I looked inside one of broken window that has obviously exploded and shattered into pieces under the pressure of the heat and smoke. I couldn’t believe the image that stood before my eyes, my childhood home burnt to ashes, yet for some reason my mom didn’t want me to help her with recovering the few items that survived the fiery inferno. “Watch your mouth young lady.” My mother snapped, glaring at me with her intense blue eyes that could intimidate anyone into doing anything she demanded and I did just that, shutting my mouth and walking away from her direction, inspecting the burnt down house. “I can’t believe that it’s gone just like that.” I thought to myself as the smell of smoke and ash lingered in the air around me, causing my lungs to slightly choke on the poor quality air. Looking at the house I was reminded that in just a few seconds anyone’s world could disappear, but that was a lesson I had grown to know very well in the past year. Starting with the death of my brother Brandon, then losing custody to Alex and Christa along with losing the tag titles a week later to P.W.A. and then being denied to see Chris and have him walk away without any contact about where he was going and what he was doing and to top all of this off the house that was my childhood home was gone, but it didn’t bother me much considering most of the memories spent there was ones I’d rather forget, because who would want to remember seventeen years of verbal abuse and a pair of parents who would constantly tell you that you would get nowhere in life. “Tessa what are you doing?” The voice of four year old Evan questioned from the side of me. I was surprised at first at to hear it, figuring Evan would be a bit nervous to get near what remained of the house considering that my mother was around and might snap at him at the drop of the hat telling him that he did not belong there and that he should mind his own business. “Just looking at the damage done.” I replied, as I turned the corner and walked into the backyard of the house seeing the better half of the house and the side that had not been damaged as much, telling me that the fire must have been started somewhere in the front of the house. Evan shrugged confused as to what type of value a house burnt down to the ground could have as he walked back in the direction of my car which was to his and my convenience was parked on the sidewalk paved in front of the house. To him the house had as much value as a loss to El Diablo on Friday would have to me, but to me this house brought back memories that would never stop abiding inside my mind. I looked over at the car and noticed my mother was going over to investigate into Evan’s private life considering she knew nothing about him but her name. Instead of going over to help Evan with the difficult questions my mother may shoot at him I continued to sift through the debris letting Evan fend for himself considering he would have to learn with the woman at one time or another if she was really going to move in with us like she had asked. As the wind increased I noticed a slightly aged sheet of paper drift across the remaining ashes of the house. It had been slightly creased perhaps from being stepped on, or being carelessly stashed away in a closet or draw. I did my best revive the paper into a readable state and read the words that were imprinted in a black ink on the piece of paper. It’s quarter past sixAnd the televisions off,The front door is closedAnd dinners on the tableWhere are you dad?The sun has risen And the birds are chirping The clock reads sevenAnd I’m ready to goWhere are you dad? It’s two o’ clockAnd school is outI’m standing in the lobbyPacing back and forthWhere are you dad? Silence comes over the houseThe dog lies and waits beside the couchI’m waiting in anticipation by the phoneIt rings and I answerWhere are you dad? As my eyes finished scanning the paper I remembered the thoughts written down on the paper and traced them back to when I was fifteen years of age and slowly lost the place in my fathers heart that I once held after he had been brain washed by my mothers constant criticism of me. Suddenly I felt my heart tear itself into two as the thought of suddenly turning my mother away from the shelter I could provide her nagged at my mind but as usual my softer side gave in and I found myself creasing the paper once more and shoving it into the back pocket of my jeans, keeping it out of my sight and mind before I changed my mind and left my mother homeless to defend on her lone rich self to find out just how tough life can be. “Mom are you ready to go yet?” I questioned as I walked away from the ashes and towards the woman who looked to be nothing more than an aged version of myself with an attitude. I hated to rush her, but I didn’t want to hang around here much longer if all we were going to be doing in sifting through ruble of which we knew we would find nothing valuable hidden under. “Excuse me, haven’t I taught you any manners? Just for that I’m going to make you wait twice as long.” She responded, still trying to treat and punish me as if I were a child. I simply sighed and bit my tongue before walking off towards the car so that I would not stir up a big family feud that would end as usual with me being the guilty one and my mother being the right one. “She’s the one that’s going to live with us?” Evan questioned from the back seat on the opposite side of me as I took my place in the drivers seat pushed it back so that I could recline in peace. “She sure is.” I answered to Evan reluctantly, really wanting to take back my word now. Quite unfortunately this was what he and I were going to have to live with now and I knew we weren’t going to like it in the least bit but sadly enough we would both have to learn to deal with it and it was all because of my soft heart.
