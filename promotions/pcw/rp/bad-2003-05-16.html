'Bad.'

16 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 16, 2003 Begin Transmission. “Once more, I am alone. Appropriate, because once I am in that ring, Chang does not help me. No one helps me. I am on my own. And I really would not have it any other way…” (SOCIETY stands in Kabul, Afghanistan, looking around him. As it has for most of the trip, the poverty strikes him..) “This is what society causes. Destruction, pain, and poverty. I guess I am aptly named after all, for that is what I bring to all who oppose me.” (Off of that, he thinks of a quick tangent.) “Although…at times I wonder why the Order chose to name me SOCIETY. Most of my opponents have more ordinary names, like Mark, Matt, or Brianna, although others are named Ahtumoth, Violence, and SyN, so I guess it’s actually quite varied.” (SOCIETY’s eyes scan over to the Stadium behind him where he will be competing. Even at this early hour, fans are congregating outside, laughing, talking, and playing.) “For these people, watching me compete is an escape from their own lives. My wars in the ring take their minds off of the all too real wars that they have been faced with. They fought the Soviets, were oppressed by the Taliban, and finally have been liberated, but the long road to recovery wears on, and for them, little relief is in sight.” (The massive Conformist’s long legs quickly bring him to the stadium. As he reaches the stairs, various youths call out to him, speaking to him in a half-dozen different languages that he cannot understand.) “This demonstrates the need for conformity. As human beings, we speak so many different languages that we cannot understand each other. This divides us. Differences destroy. We must be united. Those who will not unite willingly will be forced to do so. That is the way it must be. And that is the way that it WILL be once I am finished making this organization conform to my ideaology!” (The voices continue calling out, until SOCIETY hears the first word of English he can understand. A simple word, three letters.) “Bad.” (One of the children had said that. Simple enough, innocent enough, until the whole group began chanting it, over and over again. SOCIETY had arenas full of people booing him, but somehow, these ten children screaming “bad” at him had a greater effect.) “I’m bad? How? All I am trying to do is make the world a better place!” “Bad!” “No! If you want to look at someone bad, look at Mark Makonie! The man is a neo-nazi! A white supremacist! He truly is a bad man!” “Bad! Bad!” “He kidnapped a man’s wife, and has forced that man to be indoctrinated into his ways of evil!” “Bad! Bad! Bad!” (SOCIETY sensed he was fighting a losing battle, and walked on.) “It is truly sad that the children don’t understand, but then, I cannot expect them to. They have not lived in the world long enough to see society’s oppression. I fight for them. I fight for their future, to create a world in which our children all speak one language, and all are uniform! SOCIETY, and society, will be absolute!” (The children giggled among themselves at SOCIETY’s tirade, as he stepped up to the top of the stairs, the door of the stadium beckoning. He turned down to his small audience, before addressing them again.) “Tonight, you children shall see SOCIETY defeat two truly bad men. I know that you really cannot understand me, but my actions in the ring will speak for themselves. CENSORSHIP, EQUALITY, CONFORMITY!” (SOCIETY stepped into the building, and closed the door behind him, leaving the children with the same word on their lips.) “Bad.” End Transmission
