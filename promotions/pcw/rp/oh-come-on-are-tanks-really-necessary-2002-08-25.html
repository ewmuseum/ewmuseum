Oh, come on! Are tanks really necessary?

25 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 25th of August 2002 The Pharaoh Chronicles: Volume#1: Adventures with a pharaoh RP#2 for Third Dawning: Vs Frisco – Tomb Match “Well here you find me on the run from Frisco’s Argentinean foot solders in his home town of Buenos Aires, Argentina. It just so happens that Frisco sent out some of his men to track me down around about the time I set foot on his home turf. Abruptly I was captured, forced into an armoured truck and told that I was labelled a dead man. Frisco wanted me dead on sight, the bitch. Nice guy, that there Francisco, that’s all I have to say. Thankfully I’m still alive, which is quite obvious since I’m briefing you on dire matters at hand. I have a few hours left to escape this damned country and get to New Jersey for the PPV. It’s been two days since my capture and ever since I’ve been keeping my head low in some crabby bed sits. I need to get back to Isiy and get the Duat away from this place. Hopefully I might be able to catch a plane…surely the rest of Argentina isn’t after me too, right? Well it’s another lovely day in downtown Argentina, but, as is the norm as of late, I’ll have to stay careful to stay alive and well. Frisco’s troops could be anywhere by now and I doubt all of them are as ‘nice’ as the one who helped me escape the truck earlier in the week. As for the match at the PPV, I’m fairly confident. In a way it’s set in a sort of home environment of mine, being a former Archaeologist and all, and I’m hoping my knowledge of the Egyptian burial tombs will come in handy. Anyway I’ve once again gone on too long; so let me leave you now. Adios, amigos…and wish me good luck!” DATE:25TH of August 2002TIME: 1:00 PMLOCATION: Buenos Aires, Argentina. ..::..So there I was. In the middle of the street where, two days ago, I’d escaped the wrath of Frisco’s Argentinean secret police. I stepped cautiously; even the Gods knew that spies could be lurking in the windows of cafes, doorways of houses and even the rooftops of select shops; waiting silently for me to make my move. The sun beat down, just as it had for two days now. It certainly was warm and even I had to admit that the climate was a little too stuffy… I needed to get back to the hotel, find Isiy and get the Duat out of this country. I’d spent a couple of days hiding out, so hopefully the guards might have ceased their relentless searching. People milled in the street, I had the impression that from above it’d have looked like some sort of ant farm. I, just one in a thousand different ants, was possibly the only person in this particular street that Argentina wanted out of its country. I was like the thorn in its side, or at least Frisco’s side. I wondered where I’d go from the hotel. It was highly possible that Frisco could have set security forces at the airport and at the hotel for that matter. But if I were to compete later on in the tomb match I’d need to get the Duat out of this joint and back to safer soil. In the USA, or even Cairo for that matter, Frisco’s forces would have no real power. Over here it was a different matter all together. I stepped cautiously as tourists and villagers passed me by without a single look or even an expression saying ‘Hey, I’ve seen him before.’ Maybe I was wrong, then. Perhaps it wasn’t Argentina that wanted me dead, maybe it was just Frisco. I sighed as I came to the outside of our hotel. Why did he despise me so much, anyway? I mean I hated him, but I had my reasons… he didn’t. What could possibly motivate the ruthless bastard’s intricate mind? Pure evil was a certain runner for the answer to that question, but his motive needed more than that to back it up, right? I slipped around a corner and stuck close to the wall. A small lizard, bright green in hue with thin purple stripes upon its back, slithered past as I stuck my head around the corner of the wall. Our apartment complex was like most in the fair city: huge, yellow in colour, and painted in such a fashion that the brick work underneath wasn’t supposed to be seen. It stood looking out over the South Atlantic Ocean, its stern rocky face a pinnacle on the coastline. I reached for the door handle and turned it. Remarkably, and to my astonishment, it opened soundly. I edged in, only to be pushed forcefully against the wall in the lobby and grabbed by the throat. I looked at the shadowy figure and sluggishly made it out. ..::.. The Pharaoh: What the heck’s the matter with you?! Isiy: Oh, Titu, I thought you were one of those soldiers! ..::..She let out a sigh of relief and hugged into my chest. What a wake up call it’d have been for a soldier if they had indeed entered the room instead of me! She was strong when she wanted to be; there was no doubting that. But deep down, isn’t every woman? I looked into her thankful pale blue eyes and forced a smile. At length I came to a conclusion on what to say. I just decided to change the subject; she could wait to hear of my daring escape later, or, more preferably, when we were well out of the country. ..::.. The Pharaoh: Come on… ..::..We had to get the Duat out of here, and fast. It was a Gods send that there hadn’t been any guards on the hotel and I wasn’t willing to take any more bold chances. We were leaving right now! Isiy looked at me expectantly. Concern riddled her beautiful visage as I opened my mouth again and then turned to look about the room. It was dark inside, as though she’d kept the curtains closed in a vain attempt to keep the warmth out or, more logically, to keep the spies from doing their job. ..::.. The Pharaoh: We’re leaving… ..::..Deep down I knew exactly what Frisco wanted. He wished for me to disappear from the picture. You see, he feared me, and he knew I could beat him, and possibly would do so again at Third Dawning. He wanted me dead because he couldn’t get the job done himself. The coward. Isiy walked further back into the room and picked up a pair of large suitcases. She’d obviously expected my re-arrival, then. She handed me one and walked out of the room, locked the door and turned on her heels. With a smile she said: ..::.. Isiy: I thought you’d never say so. ..::..I followed as swiftly as I could, checking behind me for assassins and other combatants of Frisco’s Argentinean regime. To my surprise I found no sign of anyone, which was quite unusual really. I’d have thought he’d have the hotel under close watch, but obviously I was more than wrong. We came to a passageway and walked down it, still I kept a watchful gaze. It was something, which over the past few days, I’d become accustomed to. ..::.. Isiy: How did you escape? ..::..Her head turned swiftly, my eyes meeting her stare just as she stopped in mid-step. Not catching onto what my brain was telling me I shuffled into her rear as it shouted ‘You idiot, take a step back!’ and so I did and took a step back. Isiy stood in front of me as though I’d done something terribly wrong. Her brow wrinkled as I processed her words in and finally realized what she’d asked. I had planned on telling her later, but I may as well have done it now. ..::.. The Pharaoh: One of the Argentineans wasn’t as bad I’d first thought. He helped me… Isiy: Helped you? But I thought the Argentineans hated you? The Pharaoh: I never said they didn’t…although I’m beginning to think it’s only Frisco’s soldiers who hate me. Not the rest of the people around here. ..::..We walked hurryingly for at least ten minutes before wearily arriving at the Buenos Aires airport. I looked around cautiously, keeping Isiy back from view with my left arm. There didn’t appear to be any sign of the enemy, once again I found this strange. I felt anxious. Something wasn’t right here…at all…..::.. The Pharaoh: You’ve got everything, right? Isiy: Yeah, everything: Passports, luggage, toothbrushes…the lot. The Pharaoh: Good. ..::..I took a step forward and flung myself back again. Isiy jumped in dismay as I grabbed her around the waist and dragged her to the ground. I knelt, cowering…nothing happened. Very strange, indeed. Not even a trip wire carefully placed so only I could possibly befoul it, triggering a mass array of gunfire in my direction. I suddenly got the feeling he was playing games here. It just didn’t…feel right. ..::.. Isiy: What the hell was that all about?! The Pharaoh: Sorry, my love. Just taking the correct precautions… Isiy: For what exactly? ..::..Slowly she got to her feet, wiped the dust off her dress and huffed a deep sigh. Isiy moved on without a care in the world. She just didn’t get this at all. Frisco wanted us both dead and she didn’t seem to care. I suppose it was good in a way. At least Isiy didn’t get nervous when she saw a small tin can on the street, which could, of course, possibly be some sort of ingeniously concealed bomb. Frisco was a sneaky devil, after all. ..::.. The Pharaoh: Come on, Isiy we haven’t got much time! ..::..I was running now. The sooner we got the flight the sooner we’d be out of here. She caught up, heaved with exhaustion and shook her pretty little head. ..::.. Isiy: Ok, what’s the matter? Something’s wrong… ..::..I hadn’t told her about the soldier’s words. She didn’t know we were both being hunted down like dogs. Frisco’s secret police could have been any of the people surrounding us, but I didn’t care now. We’d made it to the airport and that was about as much a victory as anything else. If they were going to kill us it’d be in front of the other tourists and Argentineans. Surely Frisco wouldn’t be so dumb to have us killed in front of people who could possibly vote for him at the up-and-coming election he was so intent on winning. It’d be extremely bad publicity. ..::.. The Pharaoh: Isiy, look we just have to get out of Argentina as soon as possible. I can’t tell you because, right now, you’re the only one between us two who’s of a cool state of mind. I need you to get the important stuff done so our flight’s fully certified. Ok? Isiy: Well…Ok. But later on you’re telling me what the heck’s going on. I don’t like it when you’re like this… I could cut the tension! ..::..I forced a smile. Isiy got in line to book the flight as I watched for any sight of Frisco’s men. About me people went about the normal things one’d do in airport. It was too less action. If he had his men searching for me then surely he’d have kept the airport and the hotel under tight guard. But no…he hadn’t. Another ten minutes passed before Isiy strolled back in my direction, two tickets in her hand. ..::.. Isiy: Here… ..::..She handed me my ticket and grinned. ..::.. Isiy: Stop worrying, Titu!! ..::..Isiy sighed in the way only a concerned wife could. ..::.. Isiy: What’s the matter? The Pharaoh: I’ve already told you, dear: I can’t say. ..::..Her expression changed abruptly as my worst fears came to pass. People scattered in all directions as the sound of a riffle being shot high into the air filled the room with a thunderous echo. I watched, trying to blend in with the crowd as a battalion of troops marched their way through the airport. ..::.. Troop: ‘Ave Ju seen thees amigo? ..::..I saw him hold up my picture to a fleeing woman. She looked around frantically as though someone might help her out. Her eyes met mine. Shit. She’d spotted me… it was over now. I could run and be shot down or face the troops and still be shot down. Choices, choices, eh? ..::.. Woman: He’s over there, look! Please, just leave me alone. ..::..The woman pointed in my direction. The troop grinned and took a pull of his Cuban cigar. After having one forced into my eye I’d grown used to their appearance. He waved an arm to the rest of the squadron and led them in my direction. I stood, pushed Isiy behind me for her own protection and gaze them all a stern look. ..::.. Troop: El Presidente wan’s to see ju, Titu Xanseev. ..::..His last word, that being my surname, emerged from his mouth with venom dripping off every syllable. He reached into his pocket and produced a set of handcuffs. Oh, no! Not again. They weren’t putting me in those things this time. He approached smugly and went to put them on me; pulling away I received a hard thump into my abdomen, a lesson for my insolence. I heaved in distress as Isiy began to cry…again. Showing no mercy the troop took another pull of him fat cigar and breathed out a smoke ring. He laughed broodingly and attempted to once again put the handcuffs on me. This time I got a small amount of revenge, kicking him in the chest and upper cutting him thus sending his hurling to the ground. My action was followed by the distinct sound of riffles being loaded, his battalion readying themselves for battle. ..::.. The Pharaoh: Ok, Ok…I guess I’ll go without any more trouble. ..::..I pushed forward my hands as the wheezing troop flashed an angry look before clamping my wrists with the metal binds. Isiy was led out in the way I’d have liked; she wasn’t bound like a criminal. Then again I wasn’t a criminal, was I? I hadn’t done anything wrong, after all. I wondered how this could even actually happen. As I’d said many times before: I hadn’t done anything wrong to Argentina and yet I was being led away by armed men like a common thief. I looked forward and to my horror a mass army of soldiers surrounded the airport! Yet this wasn’t the thing that startled me the most…..::.. The Pharaoh: Oh, come on! Are tanks really necessary? ..::..The ominous tanks led the force of at least a hundred brave Argentineans. At the forefront of it all, and riding atop a colossal green war machine, a man, heavily draped in awards for his bravely or even his stupidity (for joining legions with ‘El Presidente’) sat smiling…..::.. Heavily decorated man: Geev up jurself, Xanseev. ..::..I sighed, in fact, no…I laughed. Couldn’t he see I was bound in frickin’ handcuffs. The idiot. Somewhere along the lines Frisco’d softened his brain so much that he couldn’t even realize the simplest of things. I stared at him in disbelief and chuckled again. ..::.. The Pharaoh: You idiot…I’ve already given up! Heavily decorated man: Do not argue weev me, just geez jurself up! ..::..I sighed and watched as a troop, not as highly awarded as the man speaking to me, but fairly decorated, walked up to the Argentinean and whispered something in his ear. The man’s face blushed a little and then wrinkled as he turned his attention back to me. ..::.. Heavily decorated man: Eet Appears Ju ave already geeven jurself up. Very wise choice, Titu Xanseev. The Pharaoh: So what the Duat happens now, one-who-will-feel-the-wrath-of-Horus for his laughable display of ignorance?! Heavily decorated man: Ju mock me, no? The Pharaoh: Look, you came here to arrest me, right? And you brought an entire army, not to frickin’ mention the tanks. Are you completely brain-dead? How about you just--… ..::..Suddenly a man, about five feet and nine inches high, a pair of thin-rimmed glasses found upon the bridge of his nose and wearing a finely-made suit walked into my view. I observed with curiosity as a large group of soldiers followed him. He walked up to the foot of the tank, in which the heavily awarded man sat, and began to shout in Argentinean. I looked around as a few faces belonging to Frisco’s footmen turned red through embarrassment. What was going on? All of a sudden a short man wearing a poshed-up suit comes into the picture and everything changes? Who was he? Eventually the tanks began to rolls off the sidewalk and back onto the thin main road. The troops walked off, too, their heads hung in shame. The man who’d handcuffed me removed my cuffs and jogged after the rest of his departing comrades. ..::.. Isiy: What’s going on? The Pharaoh: How the Duat should I know? ..::..The well-dressed man walked up to us. I hadn’t the slightest idea who he was but somehow he’d managed to make the Argentineans to leave us unharmed. For that I owned his a debt of gratitude. His soldiers milled about the area, some helping distraught civilians and others moving Frisco’s men along the road. ..::.. Well-dressed man: I’m sorry about tha’, Mr. Xansiv. Please excuse Francisco’s men. The Pharaoh: You…know…Frisco? Well-dressed man: But of course! Why I’m the president of Argentina. I heard what was going on and got some o’ my men to get down here immediately. We can’t ‘ave him thinking he own the place, after all! ..::..It all made sense now. The man in front of me was none other than Carlos Saul Menem, president of the republic of Argentina. ..::.. Carlos: Get on your plane, folks, and get some rest. Tha’ must have been a distressing situation. Do not hesitate to come back, mind you. ..::..Like I’d want to come back after this! We left the area and headed for the plane. It’d been an interesting trip to Argentina on a whole. One I’d certainly never forget. Tonight I’d have to face Frisco, the very same man who’d set all this upon me, in a ‘tomb match’. Good luck, Frisco. I can only hope you’ll be ready for the beating I’m ready to give you. I looked out of the window at the country miles below... Argentina wasn’t so bad…when you dismissed the bad bits…..::..
