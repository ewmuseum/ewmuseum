Alpha [3]: ...Drunk Victory...

27 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 27.10.02 I could barely sleep, keep my eyes open or even focus on the road ahead. Looking at the clock in my 1999 black Caddilac Escalade, I stared deeply into it. 8:15 PM. For some odd reason, nothing happened to satisfy me at the moment, not a thing. Not the victory of beating Pranks With Attitude, not the 8th place finish in Mall Brawl, and not the fact that six wins in a row were making my ego develop. Absolutely nothing. By that time, my free right arm hit the driver side chair, filled with CD's rangning from Notorious B.I.G's Born Again, to Tool's Lateralus. I was a heavy metal fan I'll admit, but that was when I needed to think. This was one of those moments. Inside the CD Player happened to be Undertow, another classic Tool album with track number three - Sober. Turning the volume down to a low pitch, enough for me to hear Maynard's voice and read into his mind. This wasn't a time for pitching out some false ideology of whom I happened to be, he needed to find myself. These days coming towards an end to the days of losing, things were bugging the hell out of me. It rolled into the dust, those previous moments of victory as I pulled into a Mom and Pop store on Normandy. For the fuel before a hyped show, it was pretty obvious that I was hungry for something to eat. A down and out place, looking for closure, had three booths set back- to-back of one another, with the counter stationed in front of the kitchen. Bland white walls, with daisies sprouting up in the cracked floor. He didn't happen to notice it all though, it was his damn life to see things so yeah, he guessed you could say something like that. 'May I get a Hamburger, light on the fixing, Large Pepsi, and a two Nerds Rope to go please?' I was an avid fan of candy, it got you ready for something big. 'That'll be $6.98.' the cashier told me. Laying a rolled up ten I had been clinching in his plam for almost five minutes, the cashier picked it up, placing it inside of the cash register. 'Count the change correctly.' I told him, he doing exactly the thing and holding the contents in a brown paper bag. I was wondering why the hell was the cashier still working at eight at night. Pushing the door open, I threw it in the passenger side seat and hopped back in. It was an experience that some don't happen to experience, those thoughts of feeling confident about yourself. I knew a few people did, but tense moments breeze through my mind. Thursday went about blankly and right now, I'm prepping for another moment in his life. It seems this entire week is going to have those kinds of moments. I quietly thought to myself. The cellphone rang blankly, as I scarfed down on the burger. Looking up, I could see by the single name in the call box that he had to take the call, it was something for the mind to not. 'Hello?' 'Hi.' the voice belonging to a female was soft and easy to the ears. I kinda knew this wasn't a reasonable moment for her to call him right now. Tracy Jackson was a hang on to me since junior high school, you couldn't ask me about my love life without me mentioning Tracy. Been steady for almost a decade, hell I was Tracy's first. Good thing for a wife to care about you. That has to mean something to her and me, I have to even talk to her, unless I died for some odd reason on the road. 'Hey yourself.' 'What happened? I thought this was settled back in San Antonio?' 'It was, and then we kept it all off past this. Why call now?' 'Because, I miss you.' I blinked, before chuckling into the phone. I had to get down to Club Maxis for a hour showcase for Tha 2nd Comin', before heading back to the hotel for a scouting report on my next opponents. I had heard of them, not seen him in the ring, couldn't tell if it was a plus or a negative to just study off of achievements and accolades. Something was different fromTotal Violation, they were completely different, and more determined for victory than we were. Our opponents had something to work for, a passion in which we needed badly. Of course, I was joking. Pranks With Attitude fell by the wayside on three straight occasions, and a fourth is imminent. Its not a reminder, its the truth. 'You miss me? I didn't know you cared.' 'Oh please, you thought we'd been together this long without me missing you sometimes?' 'I knew, I was just pulling your chain. Take a break from work and studies. We're not restricted to a Master's degree are we?' 'No we aren't but still...' she stopped in mid sentence, snapping a pen along a desk as she continued writing. 'You don't have to get an attitude for being on the road all the time.' she continued on. 'Just what are you writing right now? Its still sunny. Shouldn't you be enjoying your Californian summer vacation?' 'Its not my fault I went back for a Masters to support you.' 'You need to stop watching Oprah. It rots women's minds and causes them to start drowning their kids.' 'You're blaming Oprah on one single case?' 'Why shouldn't I?' 'Because you don't blame television shows for life's instances.' I shrugged my shoulders, keeping my free left hand handling the phone while my steady right hand clenched tightly on the wheel. I changed from his orignal get up to a throwback Houston Astros jersey and deep black Fubu Jeans. On my feet were a pair of Retro Air Jordan XI, colored to match the jersey. A small, but roped off platinum chain rested along my chest. This was fashion in the African American community at its very finest, and I loved the glamour of it. 'Anyway, besides your poignant views on society, what's up with this Ricky Starr anyway? You look like you've been born for this actual spotlight.' she chuckled, the small insight joke. I shooked my head and looked deeply into the Boston skyline. 'Wrestling isn't one of your biggest subjects.' 'I - erm...' she tried quickly to change the subject and get onto something different, a part that would make him question a few things. 'When are you coming back?' 'Don't have the slighest clue.' I responded to her, pressing my foot harder along the gas pedal. 'Jordan's out his damn mind, calling back to sixties revolutionist for heroes, and now he's gone.' 'Baby, they helped get you a job!'Tracy told him with redundancy to my saying. 'They even helped you gain status in the mainstream.' I look back at Jordan X Shabazz, a Civil Rights Activist whom many would respect for taking a no nonsense approach towards things. And then, one beating took him out of the game and into retirement. 'I remember when he was something. Being himself and all, he gained a place within the black community.' 'He did, and now he's gone - your hero is gone. Live with it.' Tracy said solemnly, as if Jordan had died on them as he announced his changing of the guard. 'A complete sell out.' 'Well I gotta go, pulling up to the club. Care to wish me luck?' She smiled. 'I always do.' 'That first album began it all, so now, its my world.' I told her, before saying my goodbyes to her. A click from the ignition caused the headlights to turn off. I stepped out of the car, shaking my head with a confident smile on my face. I've had that same smile on my face since the ninth grade and it hasn't left. The bouncer stood at the entry point for Maxis, the urban hang out spot in Boston. It was my moment of glory to step inside of this club and make himself worthy to the people who held his check in order. The bouncer, shiny bald head and muscular build stood in front of me. He happened to be the black clone of Vin Diesel, same height of 6'2' and all. I didn't mean to bug him, althoughI happened to be two inches taller than the bouncer. 'Wade, no time to get pissy now is it?' 'Dante, you wonder why Russel doesn't give out free paper contracts to boys like you.' Wade told him with efficiency. He was right, while wearing all black from head to toe. He could have gone into wrestling, if the acting bug hadn't bit him at first. 'Shit, you crack me up.' I cried out to him, before stepping into the large industry building. Pulsating strobe lights hanged overhead as a bar stood in the very back, the stage in the next room where the dance floor happened to be. 'Ready or not.' I whispered to myself. The DJ hyped up the crowd, scratching out music from current radio hits. He happened to have a niche for making crowds wild within one touch of a synthesizer. He picked up the microphone while Missy Elliot's party anthem, 'Work It' slowly died down. 'Ladies and gentlemen, we have a surprise guest for tonight. He happens to be, the almost 9x platnium signed artist to Def Jam South records and his debut album is still number one after a five week reign... Give it up for DANTE JACKSON!' A low sounding voice escaped from the microphone I held in my hand. I whispered in the mic, 'What do it do?' and then the shit hit the fan. My first song set the tempo, 'I Am' the track that brought me the platinum plaque. You couldn't tell if the Lyrical Messiah rapped it or I did it as it was so freely done that it almost sent the crowd into an uproar. 3 hits, two singles, and one unreleased one later, I was done, and left the crowd in a satisfying manner. As I began a walk back to my dressing room, I saw the note by Russel Simmons: Good job. I smiled to myself and headed back to the Escalade in a hurry. * * * I thought back, hours before the exact venue and conversation, to the place where I found out that I'd advanced. It was seriously nudging me like a mosquito bite I had to scratch, what to do next in life? Remembering back to a place in which, maybe my entire ego began, the original source. 'Class, take your seats.' Back in Brooklyn, New York. It didn't feel like it was the time for idol memories of the past to get to me. It was the ninth grade, a burning feeling logged itself into my mouth, a feeling I get almost everytime something bigger than what the original brought to the table. I ate at this feeling and didn't feel morbid towards it. But first, let me set up the scenery before we get into all the psychology. This was a public school, housing and rent were very high as so was the school's prestige. Having atleast three floors to its credit, all of them housing the various grade levels, it was unrare for pickings and hazing to occur. It happened, more often than usual. Quiet chime pitched bells rang at the end of every class, a ode to a heavenly choir, or something. We all stepped out, my friends and usual partners in crime happened to treat it as somewhat of a joke, although we still found it cool in places. It happened to dawn upon me then, that freshman year changed everything you thought about yourself before. Before I was just a fourteen year old scrap from Brooklyn using my voice to get around the block. By the end, I'd be maybe valedictorian, or Prom King. Both of those happened, but I wasn't excited by them. Particularly one day, March 16th 1996, I think this is where it all began, and it started out innocently. That day, things went around pretty normal. A day away from Spring Break and everybody had the itch for getting out of school. Me and my friend Bishop (you'll learn about him later.) happen to start pulling pranks on people, most notably people that happened to piss us off or do something that we hadn't come up with yet. We both shot for the one single goal: Legend. During third period, things took a turn for the better as I dozed off in my Bio-Chemistry class. The teacher, an old Caucasian woman of fifty five with short brown hair in curls had a pink sweater and some faded blue jeans on. Me and Bishop called her 'Turtle' because of her tendency to slouch and move slow like a turtle. So one of our tricks happen to have one of those, cherry bombs in her frog experiment, sending frog excrement everywhere. It happened to land in the face of the biggest jock in the school. Gary Owens was the 'freak' athlete of the school, a body fat of 3% and a neck the size of a thick belt. A crimson flushed his face, causing my laughter to cease at that very moment. He glared at me, silently motioning to me that it was me and him, at the parking lot at the sound of the final bell. It didn't fear me that much, it kinda made a sick smile come across my face. Bishop, a few seats away from me looked at my smirking face and looked in distress. 'Are you out of your damn mind? He's going to fuck you up!' His obvious answer dealt with atleast some thrown in foul language. It his nature for him, and probably why he's in the amount of shit he's in right now. My only conscience towards it all wasn't how this man was going to tear me inch by inch and limb by limb. It was on the part of whether or not he would be able to do it period. 'He ain't doing a damn thing to me.' I chuckled at Bishop who shook his head in disgust. I could have found it only for me to be the underdog in such a situation, but its my role. Better yet, its my lifestyle. Shit, this has to go as I plan it, or that ape is going to crush me!, I thought to myself, tapping a blue ink pen on the lab table. Tracy was only a few seats away from me, thinking about me or something other. 'You dared him, didn't you?' 'Dared him?' She seemed like it was a rhetorical question for what I did, she had brains, but no common sense. 'That went off without my planning!' I sulked down in my seat, playing the role to a perfection. Everyone inside of the room thought I was the underdog going to into this. The bell rang, students filed out of their seats, starting up hype and build up for the fight. It went click in my mind, I had to do something in order for this entire thing to blow over. I bald up a fist. The sun was at full mast, glaring down with the unsafe rays on us, as a circle of people locked, barring escape from either myself or this 'ape' known as Gary Owens. He grunted like an ox, pacing around and waiting for me to pounce on him with tendency. Enough to say: I got my ass kicked. A few bruises and welts on my chest and a small knot on the top of my head, I kinda found it out that I'd cut my lip by taking one of his knees to my face. I thought he'd break my nose from the feel of it. He had a broken nose from a punch of mine and a concussion from a series of stiff kicks to the face. I felt good from that. 'OW!' I shouted out in the nurse's office, painfully as the alcohol pad was placed along a cut on my head. 'Stop yer squirmin' and hold still.' she had a deep Cajun accent to her, a mixture of Creole and Anglo Saxon for her background. The nurse uniform stuck out in my mind. Deep inside, I could feel a build up, clogged up blood from spilling out of my skull. Easily, she should have told me to find a Band Aid and leave, but this was a office stay, just to prove to people that I wasn't a wimp or a shrimp or a chicken by far. I'd just fought to a draw with the biggest person at school and she wants to kick me out of the nurse's office? So damn callous, nurses are cruel. Then I was the underdog, you could have placed the Superman esque suit on my body right then and there, but it wouldn't have made difference in the next outcome. Stepping out of the nurse's office with a smile on my face, she shook her head. 'That der boy has a screw loose or two.' * * * 'Let me get drunk tonight.' That's what I told myself, walking in at one o'clock in the morning with a slight jig to my step and a veer towards the bar. Not one of those lacy clubs hangouts you would find the young stars of Hollywood getting trashed, but a low budget place playing old Miles Davis classics out of the jukebox. A pool table rested in the center of the establishment, with the pool balls racked in a diamond shaped entrapment, pool cues slanted along the edges. 'Hit me man.' The bartender shook his head with deep wonder in his mind. 'With what?' I tried to play stupid with him, 'A bourbon, on the rocks.' Yeah, then follow that up with a lemon twist and a mixture of codeine and cough syrup. I wanted him to figure out that I was off of my game for the night, I wanted him to fall in to the trap. What kind of a plan was it to get drunk at one in the morning? I didn't know, weren't a soul in between me and the jukebox for a twenty foot radius. After that I gulped down the burbon shot and asked the man to hit me again. A damn night is only for one person, I could have been anywhere in the world, but a Italian pub suited me well, and I still had the Violated Prank effect to play upon... By myself, playing the role of: Dante Jackson - The Drunk Victor.
