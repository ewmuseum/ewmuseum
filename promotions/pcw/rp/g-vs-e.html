Date: Nov.1/02 “The wise will break the cycle” Those words still lingered in my brain as if there was no escape from my cranial cavity. No matter what I was doing, those words continuously brought me a sense duty . . . and a sense of utter independence and solitude. I alone have the ability to set the PCW on the right track, and cleanse it from the mediocrity that has held it down over the past few years. Only I can bring the PCW the pure intelligence and aggression it so desperately seeks . . . ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ As I walked down the streets of Zürich, I watched the people scurrying around like sheep being led to the slaughter. I couldn’t help but think they were nothing more than slaves to their mediocre lives, but were still too naive to realize that they would, in the end, amount to nothing . . . their existence was pointless. However, on the other hand . . . as I watched these ‘sheep’ hurry through their lives, I looked down at myself . . . and I knew that I had a higher purpose than too just simply live. I was no ‘sheep’ being led by my miserable life . . . no, I was the master of my life . . . I was the captain of a little vessel called ‘my life’. It is the same with wrestling . . . the Front Office is the shepherd that leads the poor little ‘sheep’, like Rich Rollins and BladeD, to the slaughter. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ I resumed my trek through downtown Zürich, and eventually came to the city limits. The outskirts of the city were nothing like the downtown area. It was rolling, green countryside as far the eye could see. The leaves fell from the trees at an exponential rate, leaving the branches as bare, colorless appendages. As I continued through the countryside, I came across what looked like a museum. Sure enough, the billboard near the driveway read ‘Museum Rietburg Zürich’. Since it had been such a long time since I’d been anywhere even slightly educational, I decided I’d take a little look-see. By the contents of the exhibits, I came to the conclusion that the museum was more of an art gallery. However, due to the fact that most of the works of art were quite old, they decided to call it a museum, but I wasn’t complaining. This was all a nice little escape from the drudgery of the wrestling ring. Not to say that I didn’t like wrestling, but I just always felt more comfortable in an educational setting. As I strolled through the museum, my thoughts began to wander back to when I was a freshmen at the University of Michigan. I remembered walking through a museum a lot like the museum I was currently visiting, but those many years ago I was far from the man I am today. Back then I couldn’t care less about education, and learning. I was just another ‘sheep’, being herded towards some meaningless life of less-than-satisfactory manual labor. All I was concerned about was girls, and having a good time. However, it was around that time that I began wanting a better life. I shied away from social events and partying, and concentrated more on bettering myself and my mind. I guess you could say I wanted to break the cycle of boring and medial living. I started doing schoolwork twenty-four/seven, and I even began working out . . . it was the beginning of the path I was currently on. It was that decision that has led me to become the man I am today. If I hadn’t resolved to better myself, I would ended up like one of those ‘sheep’ scurrying around the downtown of Zürich trying to make a living. Instead, I am now in a position to be envied by the common man, and feared by those who rival my abilities. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ As I examined an exhibit of two century old pottery, an elderly man stood beside to do the same. Elderly Man: “Excellently amazing, aren’t they?” I was amazed that he could speak English, and we engaged in some small talk about the exhibits, then shared a little history about ourselves. Turns out, he was a Swiss professor who taught at Oxford. He was a professor of theology . . . which led me to ask him a few questions about right and wrong. Was I right to smack Rollins with the chair? Was I right to try and help the PCW by taking such drastic measures? The professor then divulged to me a small quote by some guy named Rabindrinath Tagore . . . “He who is too busy doing good finds no time to be good” And you know what? Tagore was absolutely right. Over the past few months, I’ve done nothing but try to do the ‘right’ thing. But in doing so, I have brought a side of me I’m never seen before. A malicious, ruthless, arrogant personality that, I suppose, has be suppressed my whole. Until recently I’ve always had to be on my best behavior . . . I’ve had to mind my manners, and be as gallant as possible. But now that I’m in a situation where manners and courtesy are irrelevant, I find that the evil that resides deep within my soul have broken free, and are now flowing out like a wave of ruthless aggression. As I continued to talk with the old man, he gave me a little advice . . . Elderly Man: “If you just concentrate on being a good, wholesome man, then the good will towards others will come naturally. You don’t have to try to be good to others if you are already good to yourself. Remember, do onto others as you have them do on to you.” Unfortunately, another piece of advice had sunk into my brain . . . only this advice came from a source deep within my brain. My conscience was telling me, if I tried to do good onto others, and all it did was make me a bad person, then what was the point? Why should I suffer? Why not focus on being as rotten as possible, and sharing my ill conduct with others. Everyone in PCW could know what being Mark Michigan is like. Being forced to be such a goodie-two-shoes my whole life . . . I could now open up, and let all the malicious feelings flood the PCW, mixed with the blood of my opponents. Without thanking the elderly man, or even saying goodbye, I turned and exited the museum. I caught a cab back to the hotel . . . the whole way home, my dark intentions swirled around my body, sending me into a daze. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Back in my hotel room, I just sat on the bed and started into a mirror hanging from the wall. I felt so different, yet looking into the mirror, I could see that I was still the Markus Eugene Riley. The sight of myself brought back a rush of emotions from the past . . . emotions of happiness, joy, and satisfaction. Suddenly, something clicked in my brain. As if a light had been turned on, and I could now see the big picture. What was I doing? How could I possibly be some evil-doer? How could I even conceive being such a dark and shady character? I used to be Mark Michigan the mild mannered, wholesome athlete; now I’m just that old Mark Michigan’s shell, with a sweet, nugget center of pure evil. Somehow, none of this seemed to be right . . . As my innards twisted and churned, I felt as if there was a battle between good and evil taking place within my body. I just hoped I could get things straightened out before my match at Fusion . . . because you don’t want to get caught up a battle of morals when your holding a chair above someone’s head . . . What to do? How to act? My mind is made up . . . you will see . . . just who I really am . . .
