Finding Solace in Faith

5 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 5, 2002 “You don’t have to go, you know?” I stepped out of the door with a heavy sigh. Natalie hadn’t been making this very easy on me. In fact, this was probably the fifth “You don’t have to go” that I heard in a matter of two seconds. Impossible, I know, but it happened. “Yes I do.” I turned around to face her, and those beautiful brown eyes, but I found myself looking back down at the ground again. Something always kept me from ever arguing to her. She crossed her arms. “Tom, you’re in no condition to be going out. You just got out of the hospital.” I chuckled. “Usually when they let you out of the hospital that means you don’t need to stay. But I could be mistaken.” “Don’t joke.” My smile evaporated into a grimace. “I’m sorry. But I have to go, Nat. I’ve been cooped up in this apartment for too long. I’m like a bird…I need to spread my wings.” I began flapping my arms, but she wasn’t buying it. “I don’t understand you. Why go out there now? You’re still hurt…” She touched the bandage on my head from the blow I took from Duke Williams. The glass shards cut fairly deeply into my skin, causing the bleeding to continue even after it was bandaged up pretty well. I would have to repay Duke for that. But not this week. I took her hand in mine and kissed it lightly. It made me feel special to remember how much she cared for me. It was something I took for granted, and every time I woke up I needed to remind myself how lucky I was, but once again wrestling would get in between us. “You know why I have to go,” I replied softly. “I can’t let them beat me. I can’t let them not allow me to live my life. I’m not going to sit in this apartment like a terrified animal.” She rubbed my check with her free hand. Her eyes dawned on mine. “But you are terrified.” I backed away from her and nodded. “Yeah…yeah I am. That’s why I have to go.” I walked away from the door and down the hallway, avoiding neighbors that passed by me asking how I was doing. They meant well, but I had no time for pleasantries. They were just patronizing me. As much as I tried to be nice to them, they shot suspicious looks at me and spoke behind my back. He’s a wrestler. He must be on drugs and alcohol. Poor Natalie, being with a man like that. I feel sorry for her. You should, I thought. My life as a hero is often overshadowed by the fact that I am a wrestler. It doesn’t matter though. While Thinh and Robert often get notoriety for their roles as The Crimson and Night Shard, it is I that lead them. I give them their inspiration to fight evil, and now I needed my own inspiration. I needed guidance. * * * The mahogany doors of the confession booth squeaked open as I stepped into the box. I made a hissing noise to quiet it down, but it did no good. He already knew I was here. “Tom…Tom Lexian? Is that you?” Oh, god dammit. How the hell does he recognize me? Oh shit, I probably shouldn’t be cursing. After all, God is all knowing.” “Hello Father,” I replied, sitting down at the bench. I looked over my right shoulder to see the screened window that separated myself and the good Father. We weren’t supposed to recognize each other, but hell, it was a little too late for that. “It’s been awhile.” “Quite awhile. As I recall, the last time you were here was the Christmas service, was it not?” “That’s right. Her niece was in the play. She played Mary.” He laughed joyfully, as only a man who spent his life devoted to religion could. I was cynical about anything theologically related, but I respected it. That came from my upbringing, although my cynicism was deeply rooted in my education. “She was a wonderful Mary,” He replied. “I couldn’t have pictured a more perfect mother of Jesus. You must have been very proud.” “We were.” I hesitated for a moment. As I searched for the right excuse, I said, “I’m sorry I haven’t been to church more often. It’s just that…” The screen slide open and suddenly I was staring at the man’s face. He was an older man, around his forties, with a tinge of gray in his hair, but the sparkle in his eyes told me the entire story. He had a life to him that made me smile, something that Santa Claus would do to you, only he wasn’t fat and didn’t have a bag of goodies to give me. Well, unless you count holy water and a handful of blessings. “We don’t need that,” He said with a smile. “Look Tom, you don’t have to explain yourself to me. Some people don’t have the time to come down here on a regular basis. I just hope that they don’t lose their faith or their belief.” “Well, it’s sort of a combination of things,” I answered, uncomfortably scratching at my head. “I’ve been at odds with my religious beliefs for a long time now. It’s not something that’s likely to change anytime soon.” “I see.” He looked away for a moment and I almost felt guilty. I wanted to tell him that it was nothing personal, but who was I kidding? Of course it was. But I was being honest with the man, for after all, I was in confession. He leaned against the screen. “I don’t blame you, Tom. A lot of people have been moving more and more away from the church. What, with the latest scandal and everything. But whatever problems you have with the church, don’t let them be problems with God.” “I understand.” “But I get the feeling that you didn’t come here to talk about the church, so why don’t you tell me what you want to say.” Straight and to the point. Why can’t women be like this? “How did you know it was me when I walked in?” I asked curiously. “Is it important?” I nodded. “It is to me.” “You have been wearing the same cologne since I knew you when you were in high school.” He noticed my face turning read and laughed. “Nothing to be ashamed of. There are things about us that we all never change.” “And what haven’t you changed about yourself?” He gave me an amused look. I guess he was used to asking the questions, not vice versa. I had a feeling he would humor me, though. “I’ve been dyeing my hair since you’ve been in high school,” He replied half jokingly. “It shows.” “Does it now?” He ran his hands through his hair and laughed. “I thought I covered it up pretty well.” “You can’t really take days off with that kind of thing. You’re beginning to show tinges of gray.” He shrugged. “You can’t fool everyone I suppose. It’s rather silly for me to be trying to hide my age. I have nothing to hide, nor am I ashamed of my age. It just becomes routine after awhile.” I was quiet. “I guess sometimes people see through to our true selves.” He stared at me for a moment. It made me comfortable, but I had a feeling he was just analyzing me. “Tom, I know you’ve been going through a lot. I still talk with your parents from time to time. They still follow your career, you know?” I didn’t know. “That’s nice,” I replied half heartedly. “I know that you’ve been having problems with your own identity. Hero or not a hero? Do what’s right or do what’s wrong? It’s all a paradox to you.” How did he know so much? How could he have known? I looked up to him with eyes that searched for answers. “What do I do, Father? Please tell me.” “You have to find out for yourself.” He brushed my jacket open for a moment revealing the amulet I carried around my neck. “This gives you extraordinary powers, Tom. Powers that allow you to do things that no mortal man is able to do. Yet you choose to fight for the righteous. I think you’ve already made your decision on who you are.” I rubbed the amulet with my hand, feeling the cold gold underneath my fingertips. I could feel it’s power resonating in the church, and suddenly I could reach out to the entire city. From the deepest depths of the streets to the far stretches of the ghetto, I could see evil strengthening it’s grip around the city. “I can’t stand it anymore,” I cried out. “I have to be out there, stopping all of the madness. But how can I do that when the madness is right at my own doorstep?” “You have to confront it. Face to face, head on.” And so I would, because at Turmoil, DarkStar and Clyde would realize that I bring light to darkness, crashing shadows with triumphant rays of righteousness, and I would make them pay for the crimes they have brought upon PCW. Robert would be avenged, and by god’s own hand, I will make sure they feel the pain he did. But how could I do that with Duke Williams at my side? The one man that I thought I could rely on now turns out to be one of my most daring opponents to date. A war on two fronts? Germany couldn’t do it. How was I supposed to?
