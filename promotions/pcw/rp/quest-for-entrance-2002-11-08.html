Quest for Entrance

8 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 8th, 2002 The light flashed in the sky, giving the city streets just enough light to see as the rain poured down onto the black tar and washed way into the drain. His foot slams down into the puddles still not washed way as he paces back and forth in the rain. Soaked from head to toe he waits for the large doors of the temple to finally swing open and allow him in. He stops and looks into the night sky as streams of light flash by, ever so slightly getting more furious with his decision to come. He looks at the creeping large doors and again continues to pound with the handle looking for some kind of responds. “You can’t leave me out here for ever,” he shouted catching the attention of a few passing walkers enjoying the sounds of the raindrops on the trashcans. He continued to pace back and forth, as the water streamed off his coat and onto the tile city streets. He cursed to himself, pondering the reason he was out here. But he knew why and they knew it as well. That’s why they didn’t allow him inside because they were afraid of what he might become and this made life difficult. “Are you alright Sir,” said a small child all covered up from the rain. He was still far away from her but could catch the few words she spoke. He turned around and smiled and continued on with his pacing back and forth. The little girl stood still and watched this grown man do nothing but walk back in forth in the rain. “May I ask what you are doing Sir,” she questioned which still didn’t move him from his course. “I’m waiting for something,” he responded not even looking the girl in the eyes as the rain filled them with water and forced him to look away. She titled her head in a questioning fashion looking for greater responds as she to start to question why he was here in the first place. She walked up and stood behind him following him from behind. He turned around and slammed into her knocking her down to the cement. His face filled with anger as he extended his hand to help the child up. “Why are you here,” he questioned the girl as he finally looked her in the eyes. She was maybe ten or a little younger. She simply held out her umbrella and let the rain pour into her hair as she offered to keep him dry. A stranger she didn’t know and she wanted to help. He simply pushed it aside, not wanting to take anything from the girl for his own care. He continued he pace and wiped the water off his face. He looked back up at the high rising temple and cursed out loud. “Dammit, don’t you know what you are doing,” he shouted startling the small girl. He continued to pound on the door and try and gain entry to the large structure. He stopped and looked at the girl knowing he should have controlled his actions and not have screamed out in such hatred. He looked down and slammed his head into the door in shame. The child moved up closer and looked at him in a questioned state again. She started to laugh and giggle for no reason but to amuse her self. “Do you find something amusing here you would like to share,” he questioned in a demanding ton. For a small man he sure did have a forcible voice. She stopped laughing and pointed at a sign that hung beside the door. No solicitors were painted in bold letters. He looked at the small girl that was still giggling. He went back to his pace and left her standing their laughing to her self. She tried to cover up her laughter with her hand but she couldn’t stop. She turned around to take the tears from her eyes off her face and returned to watch him pace back and forth. “They won’t allow you in to sell what ever it is you are selling,” she said still adding a little giggle to her laugh as she looked down again to cover it up. Him stood back and looked at her with a flush expression as the light flashed again overhead and lit the space between them. He shook his head at her and she stopped laughing for a split second but started again when he turned around and faced the temple again. “What are you selling anyway,” she asked. He again shook his head continued on with his walk back and forth. He looked up at the temple walls and smiled at the sights he could see. He looked back down at the little girl who had a puzzled looked across her face that anyone could see. He calmly spoke, “I was here for years and did nothing but train and work for them day after day. Sadly enough when I left so did everything I stood for and what they thought I was.” An even more puzzled look came as she walked up in front of him and again asked the same question. He shook his head and simply pointed to the top of the tower where a bell stood quiet almost like it was waiting to be rung. “That’s the city bell, but what does that have to do with you.” He smiled down at looked up along with her as the rain continued to pour on their faces. She was dry but he was soaking wet. He looked back down and looked at her still in questioned. “This was my home and every day that woke me up. I am not selling anything but rather here to by back what is rightfully mine and what was mine all this time,” he answered which didn’t really help her at all. She now began to pace and look for an answer to what he was saying. She walked back to him with more anger in her then he started with. She stopped in front of him and kicked him in the knee. He grabbed his knee and rived in pain for a split second. “Why must you lie to me like this? I came here to help you and you do nothing but lie,’ she shouted causing a few more people to look but still go on about their business. He looked down at her and slowly backed her up into the walls of the temple. Her anger quickly turned to fear as he corned her. He shook his head and again went back to pacing and waiting. “You don’t understand. I am not lying and I don’t know how to prove it. But believe me, this country owes me what is mine,” he calmly added. Now her fear turned to curiosity, as she now wondered what this mans life was like before all of this happened. She wanted to know he past but couldn’t bring her self to wait out the long story. “What does Thailand or more important this temple owe you,” she questioned again. She had hit the soft spot. He stopped and slouched down and sat down up against the large wall of the temple. He looked up at the girl still looking for an answer. He knew he couldn’t get rid of her unless he told her so he just blurted it all out into the open air. “They allowed me to model my mind into what ever I wanted it to be in exchange for country tournaments. I got something and they got something into return. Only problem is I need them to give me my life back and have the debut repaid.” The answers where finally coming in and she now began to understand. Many people came and did the same thing but they stayed loyal to Thailand’s culture. She still had many questions to ask but now was not the time. He got back up and continued to slam the door now getting more impatient as time passed on. “Have my life and do what you want,” he shouted back giving up trying to get in. The girl saw this and looked at him. She smiled and walked towards the door. She gently knocked and waited. Seconds later a large rumble came as the large doors swung open. He couldn’t believe it, this girl was the key to getting his voice heard. “Much like you I to did make a deal and hope to move on from it. Think of this as my first step to some day freeing my life as well.” He slowly walked in and followed the girl and the guard into the temple. The large doors began to swing shut and closed off the outside door. The closed shut and darkness filled the hallway. _____________________________________________________________________________________________ “That doesn’t seem like much, even though you did go a little to far,” she said with a smirk still holding her back as he sat by the fire telling the tale. He looked back into the blazing inferno and continued to see the same images over and over again. “That was only the beginning. So many memories but so little time to explore them all.” She waited for him to start again and urged him on to continue. She to was curious to find out who this other girl was and what made her the key to his way in. They looked at each other, still with the fire burning in their eyes. She placed her book down and walked up to him grasping his arm. “You still have much to tell. Do not stop till we reach the end.” They both returned to the coach and took a few sips from the wineglasses available on the small table in front of the fire. The heat added the right warmth to the room as they sat in the light of the glowing coals. “This may take awhile. But it’s better to get it out into the open now then it will be to repeat it over again later. Where was I.”
