Mamas and Dr. Pepper

27 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2-27-03 RP 1 Fusion 28 “Don’t do it Teresa dear, please.” The woman popularly known as Momma Tomas pleads to her daughter Teresa. Momma sits in a leather recliner rocker. As she rocks the chair, her hands are busy with two knitting needles. What appears to be an almost completed sweater hangs from the two needles. Her gray hair is short and permed like most women her age wears their hair. Her dark blue granny dress is homemade as well as all of her clothes. No make up is seen on her wrinkled face, but a set of bifocals rests comfortable on her nose. Teresa is relaxing in the corner of the black leather couch. She looks to her mother from the book by Dean Koontz she is reading and closes her eyes with a sigh. Her mother has been in Louisiana visiting for a month now and the poor woman is driving her nuts. “Mother, I’m not going through this with you again. I’m as tough as they come and you know that. Friday’s match against Darkstar and Clyde will no doubt challenge my abilities and I’m all for it.” The old woman stares at her only daughter who returns to her reading. Her mind returns to when Teresa was a child. She would rather go fishing with the boys than playing house with the little girls. Momma Tomas thought it was a phase and she would grow out of it when she starts growing up. When Teresa reached adolescence and her tomboyish ways didn’t fade, Momma Tomas knew it was too late to make a lady out of her baby girl. Momma can’t help but smile as she looks to Teresa now fully grown. In a few years, Teresa will be 30 and Momma Tomas wonders if she will ever live to see a grandchild. While Eric, Teresa’s brother is overseas preparing for a possible war; Teresa is fighting her own war, inside the ring once, sometimes twice a week. As the fortune telling 8-ball would say, “My sources say no.” All concentration is lost when the owner of this fine Louisiana home, Scott Taylor walks in the room. He enters the living room from the kitchen. He wears a green shirt and baggy black pants. His shoulder length black hair hangs free. Before sitting beside Teresa, he hands Momma Tomas a glass of sweet iced tea. Momma thanks him though her disapproval of his involvement with her daughter still lays heavy in her eyes. Scott as always ignores the cold shoulder and hands Teresa a Dr Pepper, her favorite drink. After a quick kiss to the lips, he sits down opening his own Dr. Pepper. “Hey Baby. Ready for your tag match Friday?” Teresa places a bookmark inside the book and places it on the coffee table. Momma Tomas sits her needlework on her lap listening as well. Teresa nods confidently before opening her Dr. Pepper. Her mother sighs deeply and returns to her needlework. “You know I am. This will be Duke’s last match before March Madness and let me tell you, it will blow the roof off. That man is a legend and he will not be forgotten.” “He’d make a better son in law than…” “Mother!” Momma Tomas mumbles something about low life punks, picks up her needlework and glass of tea and leave the room. She never did like Scott and probably never will, but that’s a different story. In the meantime, Scott wraps his arm around Teresa holding her close to him. “There’s no changing her mind is there?” “Nope.”
