Never Never Land I: [Part Three]

6 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 6, 2002 My head hurts…a lot. Birds flying overhead are a wondrous sight, only they weren’t normal birds. No, these were gigantic vultures, about twice the size of myself, with a wingspan of over twenty feet. Their feathers had a vibrant color to them though. It was like looking into a kaleidoscope, swirling with different hues of red, orange, and purple. If only those razor sharp claws didn’t keep holding my attention, I might be able to enjoy it somewhat. I sat up from where I was laying, which was a patch of grass in a forest. Yes, this all seemed very familiar. Most of Never Never Land is unforgettable terrain, but I still remembered the lush forest and beautiful vegetation that surrounded it. It is so incredibly diverse, however, that not one part of the land is repeated. It’s all sectioned off, from the ice capped mountains of the north, to the savanna like plains in the east, to the lush forests in the west. South of the island were mostly docks and settlements where the pirates took hold. I didn’t any part of that, but something told me I’d be heading down there anyways. Next to me was Bobby, who was laying on the side of his pot. I lifted him up gingerly so that he was upright. He hated being on his side. It made him queasy. “Easy on the pot, bud…” Bobby said. “So, you are awake,” I replied, taking a deep breath. The sun was shining brilliantly over ahead. As I looked farther north, I could see dangerous looking clouds looming over the mountains. They usually stayed there, but it could bring some heavy rains to the forests. “I’ve been awake for awhile. Sleeping next to you makes it hard to catch any kind of rest.” “What do you mean?” I asked. “What do you mean, ‘What do I mean?’ I’m talking about all of that tossing and turning you do. It’s like you’re sleeping with ants in your pants or something. Not only that, but you snore.” “I do not!” I exclaimed. “Snoring is for wimps and nerds who can’t rest comfortably. I, Tom Lexian, do not snore.” “You do too!” “Do not!” Bobby sighed, as only a cactus can. He took a leaf from a nearby tree, using his mind abilities of course, and floated it down slowly. That was his way of sighing non-verbally. It was almost like cactus sign language. “There you go again. Always overreacting. How do you know you don’t snore? Can you hear yourself while you’re asleep?” I was quiet. “Yeah, just sit there. You know I’m right. Part of being a face is knowing when to argue and when to shut up. You should be arguing with me right this second, but instead you’re sitting there feeling sorry for yourself. C’mon, be a man.” “I’m trying my best,” I admitted. “Your best isn’t good enough. If you’re going to be a top of the line face, you have to be devious and cunning. And that doesn’t come with practice. It has to be natural. I know you can do it, because deep down inside, you’re one bad ass mother…” I put my hand up to stop Bobby. “I get the picture.” “Good, because if that didn’t work, my next plan was to take a branch and whack you over the head with it. Maybe you’d wake up as Triple H or something.” I shook my head in disbelief. Mostly because I knew he would do something like that. I stood up from my spot on the grass and stretched out a bit. Light was streaming in gorgeously from the patchy branches that the trees provided. I listened closely, and I could even hear the birds rustling in the background. Not only that, but something…bigger. “Tom look out!” A huge, crushing force slammed in to me from my left side, and I quickly doubled over in pain. It felt like a train ramming in to me at full speed, yet I was still alive. I opened my eyes, wondering if I’d see a light at the end of the tunnel, but instead, it was an old friend. “Belly!” I shouted, grabbing the Lost boy by the shoulders and shaking him. “Aha! The old man still remembers me!” Belly said smiling. “See Bats, you owe me ten Never berries now.” I looked over Belly’s shoulder, who was admittedly very hefty, and saw Bats bringing up the rear. He was a short kid, although he was eternally only eight years old, but still small for his age. Around four foot even, his hair was a mess that even the most elaborate Hollywood combs would not be able to fix. His clothes were run of mill shirt and shorts, popularized by a new breed of Lost boys. Gone were the days of making clothes out of vegetation and the all green uniform that Pan brought. “Bats…” I started to say. But he wasn’t listening. He just fell to the floor, laying motionless, as if he was dead. I watched in horror, suddenly rushing to my feet, although I couldn’t. “Hmph…errr….” I struggled to say, as I attempted to lift Belly off of me. He was a big boy. “Belly, do me a favor and get off of me. I think you crushed my pelvis…” “Oh, sorry Tom…” Belly apologized, sliding off of me. I held my rib cage, hoping it was still intact, and stumbled over to Bats. I placed my hand on his forehead, which felt normal, and took a pulse. “He’s…alive…” “He doesn’t look alive,” Bobby remarked. Bats opened his eyes, much to my relief, and sat up. “Dammit…you scared the living hell out of me, Bats.” “Sorry, Tom,” Bats said. “I’ve been having sleeping fits for the last few weeks. I just fall asleep for no reason.” “I don’t know what’s wrong with him. I think its stress.” Belly said. “You’re stressed out?” I asked, wondering what an eight year old boy has to stress out about. “From what?” “I don’t know…” Bats said, staring off into space. “Life, maybe.” Belly shook his head, grabbing a branch from a nearby tree. He took out his sword from it’s holster and sliced it off in one quick movement. It was leafy, nice and green. It was such a waste to just cut it off. Much to my surprise, a few seconds later the branch grew back. Belly took the branch and slammed it in the back of Bats head. Bats was not amused. “Hey! What did you do that for?” Bats shouted, getting visibly upset. “We have a job to do. Stop screwing around!” Belly yelled back. “Boys, boys…” I started to say in a stern voice. “I’m not screwing around!” Bats responded. “Is it my fault that I keep falling asleep? I can’t help it!” “Who in the blue blazes has sleeping fits? Just you!” Belly retorted. Bobby laughed. “It might be post concussion syndrome, considering that big guy keeps slapping him around with tree branches.” I sighed. “Bobby, stay out of this.” “I’m tired of your mouth and of your constant nagging…” Bats said, unsheathing his own sword. “There’s not enough room in the Lost Boys for the two of us…” “I guess you’re right,” Belly said, preparing to fight. “Bats, I’m going to miss you.” “STOP!” I shouted. The two lost boys looked over at me, as I had both my hands in the air like a referee signaling a good field goal. “What do you think you guys are doing? I thought there was a war going on. Instead, the war is happening between you all.” “He started it!” Bats yelled out, pointing at Belly. “No, he started it!” Belly shouted, kicking a rock over at Bats and striking him in the knee. “You both started it. Now, calm down and tell me what’s happening.” “Uhm, Tom…” Bobby began to say. “Can I see you over here for a moment?” I was going to tell Bobby to stay out of this again, but I thought better of it. “You two stay out of trouble. I’m just going to have a word with Bobby for a second.” I walked over to where Bobby was laying and crouched down on one knee. “What is it?” “What in the hell do you think YOU’RE doing?” Bobby said, chastising me. “What do you mean?” I replied. “If you haven’t noticed, we’re stuck in the middle of Never Never Land…” “Yeah, I noticed. I noticed the second that kid cut off a branch from that tree over there and it grew back in less than a second. I noticed that pretty quick.” “Then I’m sure you noticed that we’re not getting out of here anytime soon,” I shot back. “I don’t know how to get out of here. The only way to do so is to get Tink or one of the other spell casters around here to teleport us out. Until then, we’re stuck.” “So they basically kidnapped us,” Bobby said. “They kidnapped us, and we let them do it.” “I didn’t let her do anything.” I answered back. “She hit me, remember? I’m surprised you came along for the ride.” “I couldn’t let you just get kidnapped by yourself like that.” “Don’t you think I’ve thought about that?” I replied. “I know it won’t be easy. Hell, just thinking about it gives me goose bumps. But like we agreed, I need to be the heel now, right? So I have to be tough and confident. Like nothing scares me.” “Okay, but you have to keep practicing.” “What do you mean?” I asked. “I saw the way you acted with those Lost Boys. You acted like they were your kids or something and you were their mama’s daddy. You have to be more serious and more strict. This playtime shit isn’t going to pass as heelish.” “What do you suggest?” “Act tough with these kids. Show them whose boss.” “Okay, I’ll try…” I said hesitantly. “Don’t try…DO!” “OKAY!” I screamed, kicking Bobby’s pot into some shrubbery. I marched back to Belly and Bat, who were still arguing and built up some anger and rage. “Listen you two, I’m tired of playing monkey games with you both. We’re here to do a job, and that’s that. No matter fighting, no more yelling, and no more horsing around. THIS IS MY HOUSE! DO YOU GET ME?” Belly and Bat saluted me, frightened. “WE GET YOU SIR!” “That’s a lot better,” I replied. “Now, I want the scoop on this Hook fellow, and I want it fast.” “Hook has stolen a magic ring that can make you super duper strong! But if mishandled, it can unleash the ghost pirates, who would kill everyone and take over the island!” Oh gosh, that’s some heavy shit. I wiped some sweat from my brow, which had accumulated from my yelling spree. Being a heel was a lot tougher than I initially believed. “Okay, let’s go see Peter Pan. I think he should have some answers for us.” And so we trudged along, heading for the camp where Peter Pan lived. It was Lost Boy central. Oh, and I forgot to pick up Bobby. He’ll never forgive me for kicking him like that. I have to remind myself to get him ice cream the next time we get home. Hmmph.
