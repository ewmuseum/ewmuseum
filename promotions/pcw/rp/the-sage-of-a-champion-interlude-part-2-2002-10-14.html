The Sage of A Champion: Interlude Part 2

14 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10-14-02 BladeD: The Sage Of A Champion“The Return Of A Hero! Friend! And Amazing Wrestler!The Interlude Part 2” Chronicles!Chronicles!Chronicles! Chapter 1: Nuclear Weapons? :: The weirdest thing is that Russia hasn’t really had any problems with the United States and the only problems they have had are minor ones not even worth worrying about, but for some reason it seems that Russia wants to attack the United States with this nuclear weapon that we have no clue of. But the question is why Russia has picked the United States? Cause it is so big? It is so powerful? Russia is jealous? There are really a lot of possibilities to why exactly Russia would want to attack the states, but the fact of the matter is that no matter what we must find out what this nuclear weapon is and the capabilities of it’s powers. We are hearing that it is a “monster” of a weapon, but little clues are all we can find right now, and I mean little like your pinky finger type of little. Maybe in the process of finding out what this weapon is and when it is set to go off we can also find out why Russia wants to attack the states. All we have right now is hope. :: Commander Fuller: Hopefully my brother won’t screw anything up or get caught. General Powers: Is there really a chance that he could get caught and/or get part in a Russian jail? Commander Fuller: Yes, there is a chance that could happen, but right now I am not trying to think about that because I don’t want that to even be in the picture even though it is. General Powers: That just gives us more to worry about for a while then. Commander Fuller: A few minutes ago I got this fax and I think you may want to read it. General Powers: Have you read the letter yet Commander? Commander Fuller: No general. I wanted to wait until you were here, so we could read it at the same time because I have a feeling that some of our questions are going to be answered in this letter. I was told that the letter concerned Russia’s “Top Secrete Weapon.” General Powers: Before we open the letter commander. Answer me one question. Commander Fuller: What is that? General Powers: How do you know that it concerns the “Top Secrete Weapon.” Commander Fuller: On the back of the letter you can see there is a note that sealed the envelope that says “Top Secrete Weapon.” General Powers: Oh. I see now. Ok, let’s read it. :: Commander Fuller hands over a letter from headquarters in Russia. :: Dear… Whoever this may concern, I am… well, for security reason I am not going to reveal who I am or what I do for Russia, but I just wanted to let you in on… “Metal Armor.” Metal Armor is the nuclear weapon that I know you are wondering about. It is Russia’s “Top Secrete Weapon.” Metal Armor is designed…. All most like one of those… transformers or power ranger machines that you see on television in the “states” from what I have been told. Metal Armor is the most diabolical machine created in our time (not in the letter – keep in mind this in 1994, don’t get confused with Metal Armor and Violent Ray… because this is way before Violent Ray’s time.) This machine is 2 stories tall. General Powers: :: in a scared voice. :: 2 stories!!! MG!!! This machine… Can’t even be described in one word. There is nothing like it and the technology of this machine is probably too good for it’s time, but the fact of the matter is that it is coming after you (The United States of America.) I bet your first thoughts were… Why us? … Well, remember back in 1990. Commander Fuller: Gosh no. It couldn’t be what I think it is. When Russia was having economic and financial issues, the United States was asked for aid. The type of “you scratch my back and I’ll scratch your” deal. The United States was going to (in one year) donate 2.7 billion dollars to Russia to help them out in their problems. In return Russia would send out 2.7 thousand of its troops to serve war for the United States. Early in the year 1991 the United States was just about three or four months away from it’s due to Russia and the United States requested that it have its troops. Well, Russia trusted the United States and made all the contracts final, so basically it was time for the Russian troops to go over to the states. The United States had one month left by that time to get in their dues and there was no word from them. Russian officers were becoming very agitated that they weren’t hearing anything from the states and were getting worried. They paid their dues, now where are the dues of the United States. That was basically what everyone was thinking. General Powers: But… but… Commander Fuller: I know, I know. Keep reading. So the officers tried checking up on the whole ordeal and all they got in response were either busy signals or “call back later” from secretaries. So the Russian officers waited a week and tried cooling their heads. There was only three weeks left and they were thinking. 2.7 billion dollars is a ton of money, so it may take that much time to get it all together, getting it all “legally” ready and tried not to think about it. One week was left and there was no word from the states yet. Now the Russian officers were getting really worried. On top of that they still couldn’t get a hold of any legal people that were involved in this to see if everything was going to be ok or not, then the day had come and nothing happened. The next day Russia was still without money, so obvious they weren’t happy at all. One full week at pasted… Russia officers decided they would fly to the United States themselves and find out what the problem is. Russia found sometime with unknown officials and these officials told the Russian officers that the money is coming soon. That is all these “officials” would say. The money is coming the money is coming. Several months went by and there was still no money, so Russia decided that they needed help to get back at the states for “stealing” their troops. Now you first thoughts were that somewhere in the contracts it would say that if Russia gives the U.S their troops, then the U.S. must pay their dues. Well, what the Russian over looked was the fact that in small print it said “at if anytime Russia sends their troops over early, then the deal is off.” The reason why this was enclosed wasn’t because the U.S. wanted to trick the Russians, no, no, and in fact, it was because the U.S. didn’t want the troops early simply because they didn’t have room for the troops. Commander Fuller: That just doesn’t seem to make any sense. General Powers: There has to be a logical explanation if it wasn’t for the use of tricky. I didn’t find much sense out of it, but basically the U.S. was scared that the Russians wanted to try and send their troops right away and the U.S. didn’t want that because not only did they have to collect money for Russia they also had to find room for all the troops. In fact, the U.S. first missed this little comment in the contract the first time around. It was one of the contracts that put the comment in because neither side knew about the comment, but once the U.S. requested the troops they didn’t do that to break the contract, they did that because they had room for the troops. Well, a contractor was looking over the contract because the U.S. needed more time to get the money and they wanted to find away around the money issue and that is when the contractor came across this comment in little printing. Once he found this the U.S. decided not to pay off Russia after all because they had the troops and they got them earlier. It is rumored that the contractor that found this comment was also the contractor that put in the comment. General Powers: Jesus, Jesus, Jesus. Once those several months went by a Russian contractor found the same comment and let’s just say that once Russian officers saw this comment and read it for themselves they weren’t the happiest people alive right then.Commander Fullers: I can’t believe this. Why didn’t anyone ever tell me about that problem? I mean… I just can’t believe this. No one ever informed me of this. I have been wondering for years why we never paid off Russia, but I was always told the information was so classified that I couldn’t be told. General Powers: That is truly amazing. We need to go ahead and stop reading the letter here. Just take a few minutes to clam down a little bit before we lose ourselves. Commander Fuller: I can’t believe this! :: The scene cuts here. What an amazing story so far by this person. It seems like we are getting closer and closer to finding out what exactly Metal Armor is. All we known right now is that it is two stories high which is amazing enough itself. Stay tuned! ::
