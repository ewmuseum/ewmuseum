Oregon Times It was almost a year ago I first wrote about Donovan Emerson, the 22-year-old boy wonder from the most famous family in Oregon. At the time, Emerson was grappling at small Portland venues in front of 50 people each night, with little or no monetary motivation for taking all the bumps and bruises. He had the talent, the look, the ring psychology and the charisma to be a superstar but, aside from a short-lived developmental deal with the IWO a few years earlier, he was just one of many up-and-comers waiting for a chance to earn a spot in the big-time. Emerson managed to shine during several opportunities for exposure, including a PWI house show at the Elks Lodge last June and a vicious hardcore brawl with Simon Perry at Oregon Wrestling's anniversary event in August. Last month, his hard work finally paid off. IWO came calling and Emerson made the jump to the major leagues of American wrestling. His pay-per-view debut was at IWO?s Seventh Layer Of Hell show, as part of the critically acclaimed Juniorweight Tournament. In the tourney's opening- round match, Emerson went just three minutes with Keith Rodgers before nailing the PA king with a shooting star press for the clean 1-2-3. The bout was more of a spot-fest than Emerson is usually involved in but it allowed him to advance to what will probably be remembered as the match that made him a star, at least in insider circles. It was the semifinal tilt of the tournament and Emerson was set to face the eventual winner and arguably one of the best workers in the world, J Mysterio. When the smoke finally cleared on the IWO ring, the duo had produced a 12-minute mega-match that was the talk of the industry on Friday morning. Both wrestlers pulled out an awesome arsenal of high-flying, high-energy, high-impact moves, many of which I didn't know the names of and some I don't even think have names yet. The action was logical and believable, unlike so many other high-flying clashes, which look more like gymnastics contests than wrestling matches. Even when Mysterio hit the Mystery Death Driver from the middle rope to get the duke, the Nashville fans were still hot for both competitors. Wrestling guru KSZ described the bout as match-of-the- year caliber and mat columnists around the world nominated Emerson as the MVP of the show. In just one night, while participating in a tournament he didn't even make the final of, Emerson has gone from being a relative unknown with a famous last name to a hot commodity in the wrestling business -- a commodity that will almost certainly be fought over in the near future. Although the rejuvenated PCW hasn't formally announced interest in signing Emerson, I was talking to a senior company official last month and let's just say that when the subject of local talent came up, the PCW exec was full of questions about Emerson's ability and a little too anxious to get a copy of some Oregon Wrestling tapes. For now, though, Emerson is expected to continue working for the up-and-coming IWO promotion, which you can check out Wednesday by ordering their Best Of pay-per-view, which is being offered for just one cent by most providers. Emerson is the youngest son of Samuel and Carol Emerson, brother to James Nash, Susan and Tony. Sam Emerson is one of the richest men in Oregon worth over $400 billion.
