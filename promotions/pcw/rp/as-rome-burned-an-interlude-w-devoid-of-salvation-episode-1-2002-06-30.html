As Rome Burned: An Interlude w/ Devoid of Salvation: Episode 1

30 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 30 (Excerpted from the memoirs of Bruce “Violence Jack” Shanahan) In the year 64 A.D., Rome burned to the ground. Nero, the then-current emperor of the glorious Roman empire, was several miles outside the city as the majority of the ancient metropolis was reduced to charred rubble. And what his response as he watched from afar as that celebrated bed of post-Christ civilization toppled to the ground in a fiery glory? He played the fiddle. Or, to be more historically precise, the lyre. To many, it’s a pretty absurd reaction to such a catastrophic tragedy. But as for me, I relate. His rule was exemplary, and inarguably Rome flourished under his regime. Interest in the arts hit a peak that had never been seen in past Roman history, taxes were reduced and slavery was regulated like never before. Yet all that could be said of Nero was what a terrible poet and artist he was. Unappreciated, talent and accolades going unrecognized in the face of ignorance. It became such that he didn’t care, many claim madness crept into his mind and that he himself created those fires as a form of wicked reprisal to smite his enemies. Sort of instigates a feeling of déjà vu inside…. Yes, tonight is Gold & Glory. As surprising as it is, I find myself going into the inevitable bloodshed as a heavy favorite in the eyes of many of my peers. Obviously the fact that my body is embellished with battle scars from wars fought in the Orient hasn’t bypassed the eyes of my colleagues. Sooner or later, my eyes are set on greater trophies; for the time being, I’m prepared to finally prove my dominance in the realm of hardcore and show up the multitude of flamboyant pretty boys that asinine front office has repackaged as weapon-wielding maniacs. Years of drudgery in the death match circuit are about to pay off in spades. Unfortunately, with the flood of informed comes the imminent swarm of ‘educated’ detractors. Some nameless chump under the moniker of “That Column Guy” has already trashed my work under the PCW banner, ridiculing my methods and production. Then again, to prove his ‘genius’ he went as far as to pick Kramer Wild Wing as ole’ Reggie Lovecraft’s successor. Typical of the elitists to band together, ain’t it? I expected as much. So just to prove a point, tonight I’ll be sure to thrash that sorry Kramer to within an inch of his life and maybe mail his head to our anonymous compadre’s doorstep. Complete with a fruit basket and a ‘Love V-Jack’ postcard to boot. It would make his day I’m sure, maybe break up the daily toil by actually dragging his fat ass from the computer desk and getting a true life look at whats going down in this company. And I’ll be sure to dedicate that to all the other internet-glued smarks out there too. Merry Christmas, boys. The competition is undoubtedly a step-down from what I anticipated in such a prestigious organization. Let’s face it….Duzza? BloodAngel? WHAT?? Frankly I didn’t realize those wannabes were even still employed as janitors, let alone Pay Per View match caliber athletes. THE Multiple Man is a deranged lunatic, and as such he may be worth looking out for, but overall I think he’s rather harmless in an environment like this. Lovecraft is indisputably a solid tag team wrestler, but he’s just that….a tag team wrestler. His prance down the Yellow Brick Road of singles championship success in PCW is about to hit a roadblock, and its going to come at the hatred-driven, bloodstained hands of Primetime Championship Wrestling’s most jaded ruffian. This one for Mr. Pogo, it’s for Jason the Terrible, and it’s for Matsunaga. Boys, I’m bringing this one home on behalf of the true warriors. Enough teenybopper-fed antics in this division. Yes, it’s been a long time since I broke out in an old-fashioned rant in this journal, but I felt more compelled than ever in light of several reasons. I sure as hell pray Semore Smoke gets an eyeful of the events taking place in that trailer, Tuesday he takes his own personal trip into purgatory for a baptism by fire. I will be that purgatory, and I will become that fire in which he is baptized. All the cosmic forces I’ve battled in the past months will beam with pride at the carnage in the ring at Turmoil. I may even make that malicious bane to humanity Nyarlathotep cringe. Ah well. With all criticisms and betrayals, the crusade against the Old Ones and their minions, the lack of appreciation for the services I do for the ingrates surrounding my anguish-laden life….. Maybe I’ll just go out and buy myself a fiddle. * * * Captain Stryker peered mystified at the cluster of arcane insignias engraved into the granite walls, stooping to a high crouch to get a better look. His flashlight danced erratically about the enclosure with sky blue eyes gazing with awe at the foreboding symbols garnishing the chamber as a form of primitive wallpaper. Footsteps clattered on the cavern floor and a meaty hand pressed on the wall just beside the captain’s head. “It’s no Van Gogh, but it has its charm, eh Captain?” Stryker grinned mildly at the rough voice of Lieutenant Slade who towered over him, his interest now also transfixed on the collection of archaic glyphs. “If only they’d let sleeping dogs lie,” muttered the ranking officer as he ran his calloused fingers over the crude characters. “It’s almost as if that damn corporation is drawn to these things. Every time they go drilling for resources….” “Their report stated that all indications pointed to a massive deposit of uranium,” replied the brawny first lieutenant. “Two digging teams vanished and sensory devices were twisted like f*cking pretzels. If it was up to me I’d have their drilling license suspended permanently. Seems like every month we get sent on a little spelunking expedition to clean up a mess that the GeoCorp fouled up.” “Where’s Bruce?” inquired the captivated commander without glancing up. “He’s still not used to our field trips into the wilderness like this. I don’t want him wandering into some unguarded passage and into a den of dholes…. “He’s in eastern quadrant with Vanessa, so don’t worry. Most capable woman I’ve ever seen. And not too hard on the eyes either if I might add.” The captain chuckled minutely before pulling himself up, cradling the sleek Blackhelm-issued rifle in his arms. “I don’t anticipate Bruce putting any moves on that woman. Mainly because she could possibly thrash him if he so much as eyed her the wrong way, secondly because he’s not the relationship-type. Maybe the blowup doll type, but beyond that I can’t see that bitter man with a sensitive girl in his lifetime.Either way, I need to check up on that metal deposit on level 3. Hopefully that professor’s services will be worth the price the Coalition paid…..” He paused to grab his radio while his monstrous cohort paced around the small chamber, glancing at all four passages exiting the intersecting underground cavity. At the end of each tunnel before winding out of sight was stationed an armed guard, each supporting a primed M-16A2 automatic rifle. “Squad leader to Kafelnikov. Please report.” A wave of static was abruptly replaced by an elderly voice, permeated by a thick Russian accent. “Kafelnikov here, go ahead Marcus.” “What’s your status on that metal deposit, professor? Have you analyzed it yet?” “Too say it is a tad bizarre would be a severe understatement, captain,” mused the ex-Soviet operative. “How much do you know about metallurgy, Captain Stryker?” “I can tell a slab of timber from cast iron, how’s that? The sciences weren’t my forte back at Carolina….” “Then I won’t bore you with details,” replied the befuddled technician solemnly. “Let me only say this substance is off the charts. Extraterrestrial origin is my preliminary wager, though it may also be some experimental material. But it’s obviously not produced my natural Earthly means. The weight and rigidity correlation, molecular composition….I’ve never seen anything like this in all my years under the KGB.” “Keep me posted on anything you find. How many guards are with you?” There was a brief pause and an audible cacophony of chatter from the opposing end. “Four, captain. Three at each intersecting tunnel and one by my side at the moment. Is there a problem?” Stryker pursed his lips and lowered his head, grudgingly fighting the instinct rising in his gut. It was a feeling he had grown accustomed to after all these years, and typically signaled disaster. Yet the sensors had remained silent and there was no cause to alarm the laboring metallurgist. “Exactly why did we need a damn metallurgist anyhow, Marcus?” Slade asked from his position propped against the far wall. “Did they know about that or what? There’s absolutely no f*cking way the council could have known we’d run into that sh*t down here. It’s never happened before.” “Obviously I need to have a talk with the High Council,” breathed Stryker, responding to the same question that had been hounding him since undertaking the mission in the first place. His unit had never been accompanied by a metallurgist before, especially not one of the experience and class of Professor Kafelnikov. Various scientific teams with knowledge in an array of areas true, but never one with a lone specialty like that of the professor. Seeing as it was an unprecedented action, there were no plausible grounds for it. Captain Stryker despised being left out of the loop. It had happened all too frequently during active duty serving his country, and along with his signing with the Coalition came the promise he would be granted full clearance into operations. And with that, the feeling in his gut returned again. And with a vengeance. “Something’s wrong,” he sneered, spinning to face his stone-faced ally. “As commanding officer of this operation, I say we’re pulling out. Something doesn’t smell right….” * * * Lieutenant Vanessa Wilder gawked at the shaven-head man before her, his head in his hands and weapon laying in a heap on the cavern floor at his trembling feet. “Bruce!” she shouted, golden hair caressing her shoulders as she stepped backward. “What’s wrong?!” He threw his head back, his glazed eyes set absently on the low ceiling. There was no emotion present in that icy stare, no presence of mind evident. Vanessa shrieked in stark terror at the pure absence of sentiment in his glassy blue eyes. “That is not dead which can eternal lieAnd with strange eons even death may die” A lump formed in her throat as those all too familiar words of the blasphemous Necronomicon uttered forth without passion from his lips. Her grip tightened on the weapon in her sweat-coated left hand, her opposite hand closing around the necklace she wore around for luck around her slender neck. Ph’nglui mglw’nafh Cthulhu R’lyeh wgah’nagl fhtagn….”
