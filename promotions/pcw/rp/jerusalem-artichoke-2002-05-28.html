Jerusalem Artichoke

28 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 28th May 2002 I bet I know what you’re thinking. No, I’m not David Blaine or anything, but I still know. You think that this promo is going to take place in the Asylum Gym, in the outskirts of Anaheim California. But you would be wrong... oh wait... actually... damn. Ok, so this promo is going to take place in the Asylum Gym in the outskirts of Anaheim, California. I said to have a change and do it in Death Valley, but the producer said that it would be too expensive. Syko liked the idea. I liked Syko. Production Crew: GET ON WITH IT!!! Oh right, darn. Well since we saw the dining room for the first time the last time you were visiting, we’re going to return there for the moment, because at present, Miyagawa is attempting to cook. Yes that’s right, cook. He figured that if you could read, then you could cook, because obviously you just need to follow the instructions in the cook book to be able to actually produce something worth eating. Meanwhile, and this is what the camera is on, Natalia Miyagawa is sitting patiently reading a copy of some trashy Mills and Boon paperback in Russian whilst sitting at the dining table, waiting for dinner to be server. The table is all laid out in anticipation of Miyagawa’s home delivery. But because cooking takes time, we are going to hit a flashback right now to forty minutes ago. Miyagawa is standing in the kitchen of the Asylum Gym with all its nice stainless steel stuff all around. He’s holding a book up to his face; it appears to be a copy of “Happy Days with the Naked Chef” by Jamie Oliver. Natalia walks into the kitchen and takes an apple from a fruit bowel on a counter just inside the door. She looks over at Miyagawa deep in thought with the cookbook and smiles at him before taking a bite out of the apple. Natalia: So what are we having for dinner tonight... cheese on toast? Miyagawa glances over and grins at her before looking back at the cookbook. Miyagawa: I figured I’d have a go at cooking tonight. We can’t order in gourmet takeaway all the time you know. Natalia: Darn, I was starting to get used to KFC and Pizza Hut. So what are you going to be cooking up for us tonight? Natalia takes another bite of her apple while looking over towards Miyagawa with keen interest. Mainly at how he’s going to screw this up and they’ll end up eating at Pizza Hut anyway. Miyagawa: I was thinking about trying this Baked Jerusalem Artichokes with Breadcrumbs, Thyme and Lemon. It seems pretty easy, I think I’ll give it a go... it’ll take about forty minutes though. Natalia: You’d better get cracking then, because I’m eating for two here. **Chuckles and points at her pregnant torso** Anyway, what cookery book is that, I haven’t seen it before? Miyagawa: I picked it up today from the bookstore down town. It’s called “Happy Days with the Naked Chef” by some British chef called Jamie Oliver. Natalia: Naked Chef? Be careful when chopping things then... and don’t dip it in any type of sauce, you’ll have a hard time explaining the burns when you get to the hospital. Miyagawa chuckles over towards his side of the kitchen as Natalia takes another bite from the apple still watching with keen interest. Miyagawa: Right... let’s see... preheat the oven to two hundred and twenty... Natalia (pointing at the oven): It’s that one. Miyagawa looks over towards her and looks annoyed and then smirks at her. Miyagawa: I do know the oven from the dishwasher... apart from that time with the pasta bake, but that was when we got the kitchen first installed... and the dish did come up all nice and clean. He turns a dial on the oven and then gets a bowl out from a cupboard above the sink. He places it firmly down on the counter and opens the nearby fridge as Natalia watches keenly at her husband attempting to cook and not make a fool of himself at the same time. Miyagawa: I’m sure you can find something more interesting to do. Natalia: Nope, I wouldn’t miss this for the world. He measures out some double cream into a jug and pours it into the bowl, doing the same with some lemon juice and then pauses, glancing over at the cookbook, lying open on the counter. Miyagawa: Do we have any cloves of garlic? Natalia: Nope, but I think we have some frozen. Miyagawa: Frozen garlic? Natalia: Yes, frozen garlic. You can buy frozen herbs you know. They keep better than freeze dried ones. Here, I’ll get them. She glances at the cookbook and then opens the freezer. She pulls a draw out and takes out two small boxes and hands them to Miya. Miyagawa: What’s this one for? Natalia: That’s Thyme. You’ll need that too. Miyagawa: Oh... right... thanks. How much should I put in then? Natalia: Just put both boxes in, we can get some more at the store when we next go grocery shopping. As Miyagawa tips the garlic in, Natalia closes up the freezer and opens the fridge, taking out a small tub of finely pre-grated cheese. Natalia: And there’s your parmesan as well. Miyagawa: I think I can find the cheese on my own. I do know the ingredients of a Spaghetti Bolognaise you know. Natalia: I should hope so too, it’s the only thing you’ve successfully cooked before. Although you did burn the garlic bread. Miyagawa turns to Natalia and closes the fridge next to her. Miyagawa: I don’t mean to be rude or something but perhaps... well... go read a book or something. Then I can get on, otherwise I won’t really be doing this on my own because you’ve helped me so much. Natalia: Alright. But I’ll keep a phone near me to phone the fire service in case you set fire to the kitchen. Miyagawa: Deal. Natalia heads out of the kitchen and Miyagawa turns back to the book reading the next step. He suddenly looks up in fear. Miyagawa: What the hell is a Jerusalem artichoke? We focus in on the black fear of Miyagawa’s eyes until the entire screen is covered in darkness and before you know it, the screen has faded to black.
