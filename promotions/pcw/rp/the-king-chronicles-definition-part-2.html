Date: 03.03.02 'When two men of a somewhat same cut cloth battle, it is an epic. When two men of a different cloth battle, it reeks of suckitude.' CVIII: Dreams of Armageddon Part 2 'Died?' 'Smashed and killed, and now, so much of me as that dream was, is dead. Dead forever. I dreamt I was another man, you know, living in a different part of the world and in a different time. I dreamt that night after night. Night after night I woke into that other life. Fresh scenes and fresh happenings--until I came upon the last--' 'When you died?' 'When I died.' 'And since then--' 'No,' he said. 'Thank God! That was the end of the dream . . . ' It was clear I was in for this dream. And after all, I had an hour before me, the light was fading fast, and Fortnum Roscoe has a dreary way with him. 'Living in a different time,' I said: 'do you mean in some different age?' 'Yes.' 'Past?' 'No, to come--to come.' 'The year three thousand, for example?' 'I don't know what year it was. I did when I was asleep, when I was dreaming, that is, but not now--not now that I am awake. There's a lot of things I have forgotten since I woke out of these dreams, though I knew them at the time when I was--I suppose it was dreaming. They called the year differently from our way of calling the year . . . What did they call it?' He put his hand to his forehead. 'No,' said he, 'I forget.' He sat smiling weakly. For a moment I feared he did not mean to tell me his dream. As a rule I hate people who tell their dreams, but this struck me differently. I proffered assistance even. 'It began--' I suggested. CIX: The Dream 'It was vivid from the first. I seemed to wake up in it suddenly. And it's curious that in these dreams I am speaking of I never remembered this life I am living now. It seemed as if the dream life was enough while it lasted. Perhaps--But I will tell you how I find myself when I do my best to recall it all. I don't remember anything clearly until I found myself sitting in a sort of loggia looking out over the sea. I had been dozing, and suddenly I woke up--fresh and vivid--not a bit dreamlike--because the girl had stopped fanning me.' 'The girl?' 'Yes, the girl. You must not interrupt or you will put me out.' He stopped abruptly. 'You won't think I'm mad?' he said. 'No,' I answered. 'You've been dreaming. Tell me your dream.' 'I woke up, I say, because the girl had stopped fanning me. I was not surprised to find myself there or anything of that sort, you understand. I did not feel I had fallen into it suddenly. I simply took it up at that point. Whatever memory I had of this life, this nineteenth-century life, faded as I woke, vanished like a dream. I knew all about myself, knew that my name was no longer Cooper but Hedon, and all about my position in the world. I've forgotten a lot since I woke--there's a want of connection--but it was all quite clear and matter of fact then.' He hesitated again, gripping the window strap, putting his face forward and looking up to me appealingly. 'This seems bosh to you?' 'No, no!' I cried. 'Go on. Tell me what this loggia was like!' 'It was not really a loggia--I don't know what to call it. It faced south. It was small. It was all in shadow except the semicircle above the balcony that showed the sky and sea and the corner where the girl stood. I was on a couch--it was a metal couch with light striped cushions--and the girl was leaning over the balcony with her back to me. The light of the sunrise fell on her ear and cheek. Her pretty white neck and the little curls that nestled there, and her white shoulder were in the sun, and all the grace of her body was in the cool blue shadow. She was dressed --how can I describe it? It was easy and flowing. And altogether there she stood, so that it came to me how beautiful and desirable she was, as though I had never seen her before. And when at last I sighed and raised myself upon my arm she turned her face to me--' He stopped. 'I have lived three-and-fifty years in this world. I have had mother, sisters, friends, wife and daughters--all their faces, the play of their faces, I know. But the face of this girl--it is much more real to me. I can bring it back into memory so that I see it again--I could draw it or paint it. And after all--' He stopped--but I said nothing. 'The face of a dream--the face of a dream. She was beautiful. Not that beauty which is terrible, cold, and worshipful, like the beauty of a saint; nor that beauty that stirs fierce passions; but a sort of radiation, sweet lips that softened into smiles, and grave gray eyes. And she moved gracefully, she seemed to have part with all pleasant and gracious things--' He stopped, and his face was downcast and hidden. Then he looked up at me and went on, making no further attempt to disguise his absolute belief in the reality of his story. 'You see, I had thrown up my plans and ambitions, thrown up all I had ever worked for or desired for her sake. I had been a master man away there in the north, with influence and property and a great reputation, but none of it had seemed worth having beside her. I had come to the place, this city of sunny pleasures with her, and left all those things to wreck and ruin just to save a remnant at least of my life. While I had been in love with her before I knew that she had any care for me, before I had imagined that she would dare--that we should dare, all my life had seemed vain and hollow, dust and ashes. It was dust and ashes. Night after night and through the long days I had longed and desired--my soul had beaten against the thing forbidden!' I felt sorry for his soul the trials and tribulations it had gone through just to survive. I knew now that within greatness. And all over, they have stood as time has tested them to a proved fact. It is not like Xavier Kannon ever went on about Scientology, he does that everyday. It's not like Jeremy Howard thought about getting something or revenge on someone, he will never be satisfied until his grasp on the world is complete. Howard is a pure example of greed, for his want of the World title, to now wanting his brother out of my clutches. I couldn't fathom the moments of which Jeremy Howard could be reunited with Justin. His greed still wouldn't be mounted by that oh no. He must do everything imaginable in order for him to fufil whatever destiny has been set forth in front of him. I must not let that single objective occur. You look at the people over time who've shaped this world, the way it is. Albert Einstein, Alexander Graham Bell, William Gates, Thomas Jefferson, the list goes onward into the annals of time. If it had not been aforementioned before, these are pioneers. There is a funny saying, 'Imitation is the sincerest form of flattery.' That is none truer that these people. Nobody on Earth can match innovators in what they do...as they are the first. Nobody on Earth can keep it a true as them...as they are the first. Nobody will ever know originality as they did...as they are the first. That small, or rather large, ability is known by all to keep themselves strong throughout their life. It kept these men going...it kept everybody else going in persuit... However, greatness always spawns one thing: duplication. As soon as Bell created the telephone, there were duplicates. Once Ford created his Model-T, there were duplicates. Why must somebody 'one-up' the originator? Deep down, everybody seems to know that the start was the best. Sure, nowadays, people can drive their Audi's, Toyota's, Chevrolet's, and Dodge's. However, as someone looks under that hood, the reminiscence of Ford is always there. From the beginning...to the end...his dominance is always known. Not Stormbringer, nor could he even come close to a World title at March Madness, if he could not defeat Red Rock, nor Karl Leblanc when he tried to ambush him, what thought does he have of defeating myself in a manner that could be honorary? Legend Inc. the name rings somewhat of a bell to the lives of the Dream Wrestling Federation. A man who has fought Jeremy Howard before, wrestling him to a Disqualification finish. It was then only weeks later than Inc. and Howard would be in PCW along with Caged Explosion and Mike Extreme. He continued his undefeated streak by defeating Ganker Friday night. Now you meet myself Legend, the Good Luck could or could not come in handy, whatever the case might be once there was a glory, now there isn't. The moniker fits like a glove to you, but legends are fictional, only made up to rake up interest in the common eye. Soon, it will be determind. CX: For When The Bell Tolls Romulus could barely hold his look at Neperus who was stationed in a chair directly in front of him, his green eyes looking with modest appreciation. It could have been told that he was a tired man, constant eye among the public with the well known Senate squabbling. He knew that the public was well aware of the constant scrutiny, yet he held it to himself. And pointed the blame directly at Neperus. 'Why counselor are you in my presence right now?'he asked him with a solemn prayer of wish. Neperus looked down to the marble flooring trying to evade any constant reminder of his own wear for the worse. 'LOOK AT ME!' he bellowed out, the birds flocking outside of the windowsill had scattered once his voice reached their ears. Neperus could only hope for a lesser head to prevail. 'Fine, feel that way for your own mind tells you not to even say words to my face. Coward are you and now, it is my type to speak on this plane and you've done nothing to help everything. It is with such a jurisdiction that I-' 'No.' Neperus said solemnly trying to let his own small state of mind try to over come. 'What was that Neperus?'Romulus asked. 'I know about your trivial imprisionment of Aries at the moment, and I must say I am not pleased at your decision.' 'I know Romulus, I know.' he stated out loud. 'It will still not affect my judgement on your behalf. You are here by...' Neperus didn't want to hear the words come out of Romulus' mouth, but it was bound to happen sooner or later. 'Terminated.' Neperus stomach sunk deeper into the bowels of his anus than ever before, he could not survive at his old age as just a common worker who looked up to the government, he wanted to be apart of the government. He sprawled out of the chair, in fear of what he once loved, he now could not do. He picked himself up, and walked out of the door, silence followed and Romulus sighed to himself. 'That was the hardest decision I've ever had to make.' Back in the room of his quarters, Neperus paced around angrily tossing things around trying to find what to treasure and what to discard, he could have just left with his head held high into the air but that wasn't his stlye. He wanted to go out lavishly, in a way to nitch his own way into the books of history. 'Commodus,' he called for his second hand man, a skinny fellow with a pointy nose and tight eyes. His complexion was that of Asian with a somewhat American mix to it. He wore a black karate jump suit similar to the one worn by Bruce Lee. 'It is time.' 'Prepare the general Neperus?' he asked. 'Yes,' he said clutching his fist with a evil glare in his eyes. 'Romulus' reign...will be finished.'
