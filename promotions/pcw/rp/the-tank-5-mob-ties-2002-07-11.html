The Tank 5. - Mob Ties

11 July 2002

Date: July 11, 2002 (OOC: I Misnumbered the last RP. It was actually #4, and this is #5. Enjoy.) Thursday, July 11 - Day 6 Slowly, as if being sailed down by angel servants, Boris Valinov strides down the long and lonely staircase from the cell balcony to the main floor of Unit B. This being his first day in the Tank, the well-dressed Russian has drawn more than a few stares and has caught several of his fellow inmates whispering the moment he passed. This is the first time they’ve seen him in person, but already they fear him. They fear him in the same way that they fear Doug, or ‘the Doug’ as he’s usually referred to. Boris Valinov is a powerful man beyond these walls and that makes him very powerful while within. Everyone would like to work for Boris Valinov because they fear their friends and/or their enemies will be working for him as well. But there’s one man who fears him more than all the rest. A man who fears him as an enemy more than an authority, like the rest of the inmates. This man has known Boris Valinov on the outside - known him for years. His employees have shot and killed Valinov’s employees and Valinov’s employees were always sure to return the favor. Yet for as long as they’ve hated each other - planned each other’s horrible demise - this is the first time that they’ve been in the same room together. But still... Doug Hattoni recognizes Boris Valinov instantly. How could he not? Boris’ features are things of legend throughout the mafia world - from the way his snakey brown eyes observe everything in the room without moving to the way his footsteps are so quiet yet everyone can always hear them. A thick black goatee blemishes his face, hiding scars from a fight he got in back when he was a simple headbuster in his father’s mob. Now look at him - Boris Valinov... Fourty years old and ruler of the underground world. Doug watches him from the open gateway of his own cell, leaning against the thick bar that begins the row. He hates that man, but he can’t help but smile slightly. This is no shock to him, after all - he’d known Boris was coming to the Tank for months. Boris got sloppy and personally oversaw the murder of one of his enemies - not even one of the more important ones. The cops caught him in the room with blood all over his thousand dollar pants and took the opportunity to hang the murder on him. At least that’s how Doug sees it - he can understand Boris watching the murder, but can’t imagine the saavy Russian actually getting his hands dirty. Still, the pants were dirty, and that’s all the D.A. needed. Doug retires into his cell for a game of solitaire and to await the arrival of his cellmate, Bruno. *** From the Office of the Warden “Who rules the world?” Warden Gavin Miller asks while sitting comfortably behind his desk staring numbly into the empty corner of his office. “George Dub-ya? The United Nations? Jesus Christ himself?” “Wrong!” the Waden says as his eyes flash away from the empty corner. “I’ll tell you who rules the world. Mafia’s rule the world! Underground gangsters rule the world! George Dub-ya ain’t got shit on Joe Bananas. And why? I’ll tell you why!” The Warden pulls a deck of cards out from his desk and sits them on the flat surface in front of him. “In life, everybody plays by rules. You and me, we play by the rules that George Dub-ya - and yes - even Jesus Christ lay down for us. Don’t kill people. Don’t steal from people. Don’t fuck other people’s women. Those are the rules that we play by.” The Warden shuffles the deck for a moment and flips a couple of cards face up - a two of diamonds, a four of clubs, a six of spades, a seven of diamonds, and a jack of hearts. The Warden sets the remaining cards in the deck down on the surface and produces a second deck from his drawer, “The Mafia don’t play by those rules. The Mafia play by their own rules. So while we, our justice system, and our wonderful police department play by our rules...” The Warden points to the face-up cards on the table. “...Doug Hattoni and his kind are playing by their own set of rules. One that gives them all the advantages.” He flips a few cards from the second deck face up - an Ace of Spades, an Ace of Hearts, an Ace of Diamonds, and Ace of Clubs. “The problem is, the set of rules that we play by gives them all the advantages too.” “So while we’re looking for warrants, going around red tape, and following all the proper procedure when we’re pissed off,” the Warden says as he folds his hands over the cards in front of him, “The Mafia pull out guns and blow some fucking brains out.” *** Some time later, long after Doug Hattoni has finished with solitaire, he hears footsteps approaching his cell. Loud footsteps - nothing like those of Boris Valinov. These are the footsteps of Doug’s most trusted friend, Anthony Gasperoni - Bruno for short. He slowly steps around from the shadows and into the cell, staring at Doug’s fat body stretched out onto the bed. “It’s true, ain’t it, Doug?” “Bruno,” Doug says as warmly as he can with his vocal rasp, “You have been my right hand man from the day we met in 1977. When I came back to the Tank, you were still there - ever loyal to myself. I am more grateful for that than you can ever imagine.” Bruno is a massive man - by any standards. He stands six and a half feet tall and weighs upwards of two hundred and fifty pounds. He used to be solid muscle, but now, at fifty-six years of age, the muscle is noticeably sagging. Signs of age are all over his body and face. The black is beginning to drain from his hair leaving colorless white. Doug can remember when Bruno was a smooth skinned young man in his early thirties - such a strong man. Now, age has bound his bones in the way that age does - slowly... cruelly... “Doug...” Bruno says in his low tones, laced with a thick New York accent - he often tells stories of his youth which was spent in the Big Apple. He can’t help but let his voice shake even though he wants to sound tough, “Are you cutting me out, Doug?” Doug’s head snaps up in surprise, “Cut you out?” “Are you?” Bruno asks, pointing two fingers in Doug’s direction. “Bruno,” Doug says as he sits up and rubs his own aging head. “How could I cut you out? When I came here back in the seventies, who did I get to watch out for me? Huh?” Doug pauses, looking at Bruno, “And then, when I came back again some years ago, who was right there by my side? You, Bruno - you.” “So what is this, Doug?” “This is...” Doug sighs. “You ain’t getting cut out, Bruno. You’re still going to be the guy I go to when I need some scouting done. When I think something’s going wrong with the drug import, you’re still the guy I’m going to send... I just... I ain’t going to send ya when it’s time to bust heads, Bruno.” Bruno nods, trying hard to accept Doug’s words. “Come on, Bruno,” Doug says warmly, “you knew this day would come. We all know our day’s going to come. But in your line of work - headbusting - you can’t be a kid forever, you know? You have to get old, Bruno - we all do. And that’s why I want you to step back and help me out a little behind the scenes. You know? You ain’t gotta go cracking your knuckles on people any more - you can get a taste of what I do now. You know, make the decisions.” “Who’s gonna bust the heads?” Bruno asks the old Italian, trying to keep things to the point - that’s a task not often completed when talking to Doug Hattoni. Doug sits back against the concrete wall and stares up for a moment. “There’s this kid,” he says. “He got busted for assault up in Canada but he resisted arrest down here in Cali. He’s a good kid, Bruno - a real blue chip.” “Have you talked to him?” Bruno asks, stepping over to the wall opposite Doug. Doug nods. “And?” “And I told him I’m going to put him to a test,” says Doug. “Not a test of his headbusting abilities - the kid’s a pro wrestler, so I don’t have any doubts there. You know how things in this business are, Bruno - I have to test his loyalty. You know? Same way I tested yours.” “You think he’ll pass?” Bruno asks with a curious eyebrow raised. According to records, Bishop went into Vietnam in 1968, killed Charlie by the boatload, and was looking at a promotion by the time he left in 1970 by Helicopter.” “That’s not what made him a hero though,” the Warden says while scratching his chin. “On the way home, they say Bishop thought his boots were too tight and took them off just as the chopper was heading into some nasty weather. The nasty weather turned out to be a damn tropical storm, and the chopper was sent down into the ocean. After the impact, Bishop was the only man left conscious. But still, seemingly without care for himself, he rescued four of his companions and perched their unconscious bodies onto a piece of floating cargo - there was no room for Bishop. And remember, he wasn’t wearing any boots.” “A little over a day later,” the Warden sighs, “a rescue mission found Bishop and the five people he saved drifting out in the ocean. Bishop’s four companions came out with some head injuries - nothing that lasted, thankfully. Bishop himself ended up with hypothermia and a pair of feet that damn near had to be amputated.” “After he got back to the states, hard times came on the war hero. Even with his medal of honour, a lot of people looked at him as a murderer - the anti-war movement was in full effect then. Bishop went into a deep depression. He was drinking way too much and eating way too little. His wife left him, he lost his job - the world was crumbling down around him. He started passing out all over his hometown and the local police, feeling sorry for him, would have to wake him up and return him home. Well, except one night...” The Warden steps behind his desk and begins flipping through his filing cabinet until he finds the work on Barney Bishop. “Police reports say Bishop passed out, as usual, on a park bench near a spot where some homeless people were noted to scrounge for food at night. When one of these homeless people saw the nice mountain boots Bishop was wearing, they thought he wouldn’t miss them. Bishop woke up and beat the poor homeless man to death.” “If the crime hadn’t been so particularly vicious,” the Warden says while looking through the file, “the judge probably would have gone easy on Bishop - he is a war hero, after all. But when the police examined the body, there was barely anything left of it. Chunks of flesh had been chewed out, his nose was missing, his genitals had been severed... there was no way Bishop could be shown any mercy after that. So... they sent him to me... and there’s no such thing as mercy in the Tank.” *** “D-D-Damn, man!” The sandy haired aging man says, “Them’s some f-f-fucking nice b-b-boots you got there!” “Thanks...” Snow says awkwardly, looking to his left at Barney Bishop - known to the rest of the inmates as Nam - as they sit in wait of Jennifer Vegas, prison-renowned anger management councilor. Snow looks down at his pair of green, black, and gold Snake Skin boots. “I’ve always liked Snake Skin.” “Can I have them, man?” asks Nam, trying hard to look directly into Snow’s cold blue eyes, but having a tough time because of a nervous twitch that occasionally snaps his head in various directions. “Have them?” Snow repeats with surprise. “I l-l-like them,” the stuttering vet says. “I like boots man. Fucking warm. Warm fucking boots. Can I have them?” “I don’t think so.” Snow says, looking the other way in an attempt to ignore this strange old man. “C-C-Cocksucker!” Nam says in a voice just loud enough for Snow to hear, folding his arms across his chest and turning the other way. Snow can’t help but smile. “You know what?” Snow says turning back to Nam, “Fuck it - you can have them.” “R-R-Really?” Nam asks with wide eyes. “Why not? I’ve got another pair in my cell.” “Dude, your t-t-too fucking cool!” Nam says excitedly. “Forget that C-C-Cocksucker thing, man. You’re n-n-not a cocksucker! You’re a f-f-fucking pussy-licker!” Snow raises an eyebrow, “Uh... thanks.” “Name’s Bishop,” says the sandy haired vet as he extends his hand to the young lion sitting next to him. “Barney Bishop. You can call me Nam.” “Interesting name,” Snow replies coldly. “Call me Snow.” “S-S-So what the fuck are you in here for?” Nam asks Snow as he looks around at the surroundings of the room - all white, calming colors. Nam then turns sharply to Snow, “Fuck yer cellmate?” “The opposite actually.” “He fucked you?” Nam asks with curiosity. “He tried,” Snow smiles, “He didn’t get too far.” “Ah,” Nam nods, understanding. He then snaps his head back toward Snow, “You know why I’m in here? Punched a fucker. Fucker tried to steal my boots. S-S-So I punched him. Fucker. But I don’t mind it h-h-here. Nice tits. Tits are nice.” Snow doesn’t understand. Then again, he didn’t really expect to understand much about his stuttering new friend. Dressed all in camouflage, the old man looks more like a cartoon than a war vet - sounds more like a cartoon too. Instead of persisting on the matter, Snow decides to turn the conversation in a different direction. “Hey, have you heard from Miggy lately? I haven’t seen him in a couple of days.” “Miggy?” Nam looks confused. “Miggy,” Snow says while trying to think of a way to describe him. “Kind of old, kind of small, Latino.” “Oh!” Nam exclaims a little too loud, “Little fucker! Loafers. Always wearing loafers. Dumb fucker - l-l-loafers aren’t warm.” “Have you heard from him?” asks Snow. Nam shrugs, “In the infirmary last I head.” “Why?” Snow asks, startled at the new information. “F-F-Fuck if I know,” Nam shrugs before turning to Snow, “Froze his feet?” At that moment, Snow learns what Nam meant at the end of their earlier conversation - Jenny Vegas struts into the room. Her long brown hair curls and lightly touches her shoulders entrancing each of the eight men in the room. Her face is soft and a pair of big blue eyes peer out at the room of drooling inmates. Her lips are curved to a smile. Snow glances over to Nam and can’t help but say, “That’s Jenny Vegas!?” *** From the Office of the Warden “When Jenny Vegas came into my office five years ago as an eighteen year old looking to ‘make a difference,’ I thought she was nuts,” says the Warden as he shakes his head from behind his desk, looking through a newspaper. “Fact is, I still think she’s nuts. But after a couple of months of pestering, I thought I’d let her learn her lesson the hard way - from the inmates. She wanted to ‘help’ them. Fine. Let her try.” “She tried,” sighs the Warden, “And it seems that I was overlooking a pretty simple fact - slap a pair of tits in front of an inmates face and he’ll sit quietly for as long as you ask him to. There you have it - the Jenny Vegas phenomenon.” “You’ve probably heard of this phenomenon,” The Warden holds up the newspaper - the headline reads ‘Jenny Vegas Makes Amazing Progress With Inmates’ - and then pulls it back down and shakes his head. “For years now, Jenny’s progress has been tracked by every paper in California. And I’ll admit, Jenny has talent - a pair of D-Cups full of it.” *** J-Thug, unofficial leader of the Thugs gang - made up entirely of black men between the ages of seventeen and forty - leans against the outside of his cell wall, staring down to the main floor at the door where within minutes Jason Snow is sure to emerge. Since hearing that the Nazi’s are trying to recruit the dangerous young lion, J-Thug has known it will be necessary to put him in his place. As much as J-Thug hates to admit it, Darryl Jarvis - Snow’s bisexual cellmate - is a member of the Thugs, and when it comes to the gang’s appearance in the eyes of the Nazi’s, he will be looked out for. If not for that, J-Thug would have found Snow’s attack on Jarvis, late Tuesday night, amusing. He has men posted near every C.O, ready to “inadvertently” block their path once the trouble starts. Two more men are posted on either side of the door, ready to seal off any retreat plans that Snow may have. A rather large young black man in the middle of the main floor is the fall-guy for this particular mission. When all is said and done, he will be the man throwing the punches and it will be him who will most likely spend the next week in the hole. But a week alone in the hole is nothing compared to a minute alone with J-Thug, and the fall-guy knows it. Suddenly, the gate opens and Jason Snow - barefoot - along with Nam - holding Snow’s boots under his right arm - enter the main floor of Unit B. Slowly, J-Thug’s troops begin to close in. Immediately, Nam’s instincts kick in and he sees the danger looming. Quickly, he grabs Snow’s arm and the pair stop in their tracks as a sea of large black men closes around them. “Mr. Jason Snow,” J-Thug says in his impossibly low voice a moment after stepping off the final step in the staircase and turning toward the main gate. “I’ve heard a lot about you - lion. I’ve heard a lot. But I also heard that you decided to bring your knuckles out on Darryl. You see, motherfucker, Darryl’s one of my boys. You fuck with him - you deal with me.” Instinctively, Snow’s fists close and he takes a step toward J-Thug - it’s now that he sees the large young black man that’s blocking his path to the Thug leader. Snow smiles and prepares to destroy the young man in front of him, thinking then he’ll move on to the massive and muscular J-Thug - there is no fear in Jason Snow. What he doesn’t realize, however, is there’s no chance he’ll get to J-Thug. Before he gets the chance, the Thugs behind him will attack, and plenty more will jump in. Without a thought in his mind other than rage for this ambush, Snow pulls off his wife-beater tank top, revealing the thick black bruises on his back that were left by the C.O’s who saved Darryl Jarvis from his wrath. Snow doesn’t understand that he’s in a no-win situation. Luckily for him, however, someone else does. “Jeremy Doris!” a raspy voice booms from the cell balcony above the main floor, calling J-Thug by the name his parents gave him twenty-nine years ago. J-Thug looks up to find that the speaker is none other than Doug Hattoni. “I hope you’re not thinking of harming one of my boys, Doris.” J-Thug looks surprised - he had no idea Jason Snow had any connection with Doug Hattoni and his powerful Italian gang. Snow’s not even Italian, he thinks. Of course, Snow’s not a member of Hattoni’s gang yet, but as the young lion peers up at his saviour - standing just outside his cell, scowling down at J-Thug - he can’t help but feel a little closer to the family.
