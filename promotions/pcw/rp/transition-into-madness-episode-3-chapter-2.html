Transition Into Madness: Episode 3, Chapter 2

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Posted on Sat. April 27 (OOC: Apologies for not finishing this against Rush, writers’ block kicked in along with the end-of-the-year flurry of final papers and tests. Read Chapter 1 of this RP before reading this one (which I may post on Showcase forum with this in its entirety).Listening guide: The action part/majority of this RP was written to Children of Bodom’s “Children of Decadence”.) Violence Jack stumbled against a thick plate of metal set against two sturdy poles as the latest aftershock quaked below. He nearly lost his grip on the razor-edged pinwheel (1), palms now greased with a film of sweat. “I’m afraid Mother is out,” informed a blatantly shaken Stryker, “and she’s rather pissed off. If I were you I’d drop to my knees and say a prayer or two.” “You’re a f*ckin’ enigma,” grumbled Jack, gazing around at the consort of armed soldiers. “You just wasted an entire army of those slobberin’ things so what ya worried about one of ‘em for?!” “Because it’s their damned master!!” bellowed Stryker. “Think your average hotel. And you get a good idea how big this one is.” A cry rose up across the junkyard, soldiers fortifying defensive positions and securing behind hills of heaped scrap. The other wrestlers meanwhile, confused by what was happening, scattered and sought refuge around the armed soldiers. Stryker shot a burning look into the sky, a thin coat of sweat shimmering on his forehead. “For the love of all that is holy, Christian, get your ass here right now……” At that moment came the most violent tremor yet, causing the ground to tremble and mounds of contorted metal to tilt and topple to the ground all around the unsettled men scattered across the junkyard. Urgent cries for order arose from the higher ranking members of the regiment, beseeching members of the armed company to take up defensive positions in retaliation of the persistent series of tremors and in preparation for what malevolent force they may precede. Stryker shot a glance over his shoulder to catch one of his immediate subordinates, the mammoth Ryder Slade, jog up behind with helmet clutched in one hand and a soiled semi-automatic rifle in the other. Jack was semi-alarmed to perceive a distressed look, mixed both of terror and of anxiety, on the ashen face of the hulking warrior. He whispered a few words to his superior, both men casting forlorn gazes to the west. “Holy mother of God…..” Jack distinctly heard Stryker mutter. His current demeanor was a stark paradox to his typical nature, or at least of what VJ had thus far seen of him; his previous conception had defined Marcus as ever cool and calculating even under the most severe threat. It instilled fear in the Boston thug seeing this extreme look of apprehension now dotting his brow. The ground exploded in a massive shower of soil and debris, a gaunt and colossal shadow hurtling forth into the sky from the tunnel winding beneath. Stryker, Slade and Jack all immediately dived to the ground as a searing howl pierced the night. VJ rolled sideways and launched himself back up into a crouch as he set his eyes back at Stryker and his lieutenant. The young man swiped dirt from his hair and met VJ’s gaze at the same time as Slade hobbled to his feet, clutching his thigh before dashing off in a limp gait to return to his own unit. “That prayer, Bruce. Get on it…..” Stryker elevated his eyes and weapon heavenward, releasing a volley of gunfire at the floating monstrosity as it hovered ominously overhead. Its eyes, glistening with a vile red shimmer like something forged in the inner furnaces of Hell itself, beat down with all the intensity of the sun’s life-giving rays. Yet portrayed within their fiery sockets was a sinister intelligence of unspeakable archaic origin. Broad leathery wings beat the air behind it with the force of a driving storm, gusts of heavy wind bearing down on the aghast regiment divided below as many grasped at rusted out car frames for support. The horror’s head was contoured like that of a squid, yet its bulky frame lacking the tentacles associated with the sea-bound animal; the latter fact of which puzzled Jack as he thought of what had appeared earlier…… “I can’t f*ckin’ believe this!” roared Stryker as he ripped the clip from the weapon in his slippery grip, voice marked by tones of befuddlement and ultimate consternation. “What the hell is she doing here?!” “She?.......” The ground trembled ever so remotely once again, drawing yet another dismayed look from Marcus. “What now?!” Seconds later, as the mortified duo watched, a slew of bipedal fiends poured from the pit with a chorus of howls and maddening shrieks. Their eyes, while lacking the gleam of abyssal rapture of their enormous predecessor, glimmered with an intellect contrasting that of their primal movements. Chaos ensued once again around the yard as Stryker’s regiment launched into attack on the rushing enemy, their commander gripping Jack by his ragged shirt and pulling him away to take shelter as the diabolical winged overseer surveyed from above, producing nary a sound from its position high overhead.VJ tumbled onto the worn leather seat of an old rusted out Ford chassis with a grunt, Stryker collapsing beside him as frenzied gunfire and shrill inhuman howls permeated the air. “I’m going to be blunt with you, Bruce,” said Stryker in a fearful voice, hurriedly checking his ammunition. “Bottom line is that we’re in a hell of a lot of trouble. I’ll go in more detail if we get out of this…..but even if we do I’m afraid it won’t be without a considerable amount of casualties….” A great tremor shook the earth beneath them, disrupting Stryker in mid sentence. “They outsmarted us,” continued Stryker, trying to keep his voice firm amidst the surmounting odds. “We should have seen it coming, we were fools….” “COMMANDER!!” “What?!” roared Stryker, tossing his head back to look at a camouflage-garbed youngster seated outside the car frame. “More subterranean activity to the west, news from the scouts!” reported the shaky soldier, his rifle wavering in his unsteady grasp. “Lieutenant Slade asked for your help! His forces are being torn apart and the lines are collapsing!” “This is getting ridiculous,” grumbled the officer with a roll of the eyes before throwing himself to the side and into the open, dashing off in pursuit of his subordinate. Jack glanced at the pinwheel provided him by the unit commander, grasping it in his sweat-clad palm. The night air was warm, yet his spine tingled with a paroxysm of cold from the mounting sense of dread consuming his mind and body. “You bastards get guns, and I get a godd*mn oversized throwin’ star? This is f*ckin’ preposterous…..” VJ clambered from the shell of the abandoned car as the roof dented in, the interior echoing with a metallic grind as the weight of whatever was on top bore down on the stressed metal. His eyes shot up to take in the contorted form of one of the slavering monstrosities from the pit. Jack sneered, spitting a glob of mucus to land at the feet of the drooling, semi-portly demon. “You interrupted my match, you son of a bitch! If ya wanna play then here I am!” It crouched down momentarily as VJ watched, a gurgle rising in its throat before lurching forward and releasing a thick stream of pasty red goo. It splashed down beside VJ, who sidestepped before lunging forward and swinging the pinwheel in a horizontal arc to bite through the lumpy flesh of the beast. It tumbled off the car roof and landed in a heap, convulsing in a flurry of nerve spasms before gradually dissipating away into a repulsive pool of noxious juices. Violence Jack scowled at the reeking puddle of alien liquid before being abruptly thrown offbase as the ground trembled yet again, the mightiest quake since that which introduced that squid-bat, or whatever the hell it was, that now hovered over the scrap yard. More horrified shrieks reverberated across the yard from the westerly side, causing the Boston thug to shift his attention to his left. The sight sent the jaded street ruffian stumbling against a pile of metal rubble, eyes wide and the pinwheel tumbling to the ground with a clatter on a protruding rock. Ascending from behind a mountain of debris arose the same hideous thing Jack and his comrades had seen earlier, its features now more clear with upper appendages thrashing erratically about. Like its kin hovering above the yard, it iterated no audible sound save the crunching of metal beneath its towering mass. A mass of tendrils sprouted from its head, arms thick and ending in barbed hooks. No eyes were visible and in contrast to its brethren it appeared to act of pure instinct, flaying its whip-like arms wildly about. “BRUCE!” He swiveled his head to look upon Stryker, the officer dashing up from behind a stack of compacted steel to his north side. In tow was Lieutenant Sensaki, his uniform sullied with unnamed grime and the blood of his fallen company. Sensaki maintained a sense of composure at the side of his superior, sending the butt of his weapon into the lurching form of one of the little demons as it rushed from a rusted overhang protruding from nearby. Stryker, obviously distressed and strained both physically and mentally, looked on the verge of collapse as he struggled to stay upright on his wobbly legs. “Norton is here, Bruce. Evac unit is only a couple kilometers off. C’mon, you’re leaving with us!” “I don’t hear any….” “Well its standard procedure to not fly over a junkyard being aerially guarded by a CCD overlord,” responded Sensaki, nodding in the direction of the fiend drifting menacingly overhead. “Tends to do more harm than good for the chopper and anyone aboard. Even when we’re using means to keep the bastards subdued getting too close still isn’t advisable.” “Norton is outside, landed and ready to take back off,” informed Stryker. “We already evacuated your comrades, they’re secure with a team of our men outside the boundary waiting for the evac unit to arrive. We don’t have a lot of time, if we don’t get out now things are going to get really hairy!” “This is madness….” declared VJ, glancing around at the carnage enveloping the once-tranquil junkyard. “No, madness is what happens if we don’t survive this,” retorted Sensaki as Stryker unclipped the radio from his belt. VJ heard the estranged captain barking orders for retreat into the radio, barely drowning out the distant array of ghastly death screams and frantic weapon fire sending chills down his spine. Sensaki grasped his wrist, the Asian’s powerful grip contrasting his thin and wiry form. “We’re pulling out immediately,” Stryker informed with a nod to his lieutenant. “Activity increasing to the west, this is only the tip of the iceberg. We’re outta time.” The trio dashed off for the exit, navigating the narrow corridors between looming hills of mounted scrap metal. VJ had picked up the silver weapon provided to him and his hand clenched around the hilt constructed in the center, though he still felt more secure around guns than this thing that belonged more in a Hong Kong martial arts flick than a battlefield. * * * VJ awoke with a start, bathed in a think film of his own sweat. He swung his body over the side of the bed, snatching his ticking alarm clock. 8:27. He sighed, dropping it absently onto the moist sheets and running his hands over his greasy face with a groan. His eyes darted around the room, his vision still in a haze. He drew himself to his feet and stretched the worn muscles in his legs, running a hand over his brawny chest to wipe a bead of sweat away. Still in a daze, he strided across the room and slumped down beside the phone into a creaky wooden chair. Running his hand over the stand he found the strip of paper given him the other day, and rather leisurely dialed. One ring….two… “Hello.” VJ instantly recognized the gruff voice of Captain Stryker, the clinking of metallic instruments resounding softly in the background. “I’m in.” Silence, followed by a pained groan on the opposite end. “Eh, ya change your mind about that offer now or what?” inquired Jack with a snicker. “Ya don’t sound overly joyous…” “My apologies, Bruce. Doctor sewing my damned arm up while I’m talking to you.” “You ripped your arm up in real life?!” said VJ. “Why didn’t I….” “No, no, not that. Awhile after I came out of the trance I slipped and cut my arm on a jagged hunk of metal. Damn thing was jutting over the edge of the table in the operations room. We got some sh*t laying around here that needs fixed, I’m telling you….” “You’ve saved my ass twice now so I guess the least I can do is give you and your boys some info when ya want it,” quipped VJ further. “Beyond that I don’t know what I can do for ya. Right now I’m tryin’ to focus on my match Tuesday night so I may be distracted…..” “We will never ask you to help us in our ‘peace-keeping’, Bruce. We’re trained military operatives, that’s our duty. We just so happen to also know a few things most people don’t, and we feel we have an obligation to let those fortunate enough to be ignorant stay ignorant. Sometimes ignorance is actually a blessing you know.” “I’ll agree with ya. But before we go any further with this little deal, could you do me the common courtesy to let me know what happened last night?” “I’ll explain everything to you Bruce, don’t worry. Unfortunately things have come up and I have to run for the time being, but first things first. Get a plane ticket tomorrow, and come up to Vermont. Let me know the time and I’ll be there to meet you when you get off the aircraft. I’ll take you on a little tour of ‘headquarters’ in Rutland, Vermont, so you can get a better idea what we’re doing.” Rutland, Vermont? The location was a surprise; it seemed a rather obscure location for some sort of monster-hunting expedition, covert or not. He had actually expected a secret wing in the Pentagon or something. “What’s in Rutland, Marcus?” “New Miskatonic University.” Click. He set the phone down and exhaled, eyes darting to the wall above his messy bed. Sheets were clumped and tossed in disarray half on and half off the bed itself, a result of his fitful and nightmare-laden sleep. Over time he had taken the time to organize something of a ‘collage’ of PCW superstars, which now was suspended with thumbtacks over his bed; the poster of photos needed somewhat updated, as many had been dismissed from the company and others hired in recent weeks. Rush, Akiyama, Sylo, Chef…..mainstays of the Primetime Championship Wrestling empire, and all adversaries this coming Turmoil. VJ had hoped all his problems would be laid to rest prior to any upcoming bouts with this sort of magnitude on his PCW career; unfortunately things had become yet more complicated and elaborate. With every step forward, obstacles would arise and impede his progress back into normalcy. Cosmic gods, blasphemous revelations, ridicule from society, the ever-rising hatred in his black heart……all had ascended to thwart his march into the hands of glory. But he had yet to be sent spiraling into the depths of oblivion by any hardship in that wretched business. Perseverance had always seen him through, and Turmoil would hold no exception on the 30th. Kick around a few overrated wannabes, humiliate a couple upstart chumps, claim his Extreme crown. The time was right. Malevolence incarnate.
