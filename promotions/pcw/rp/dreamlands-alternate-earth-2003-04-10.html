Dreamlands: Alternate Earth

10 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/10/03 'Kazaki!' she calls. I cover my ears; that seems to help. I love hearing her sweet voice, it's like hearing the Virgin Mary singing to you. I knew it wasn't Mary, though. I've no right to hear that. I didn't want to. I never wanted to hurt anyone. Lucky, this morning Lexi's voice woke me from my nightmare. Again, this night and every night, the same horrific images. People screaming and running, panic in their eyes, being crushed beneath broken concrete and dense steel girders, trampling each other in a frantic dash for the exits. Men, women, and children; all innocent people who had just come out to enjoy a nice sporting event. I killed them. All of them. The worst recorded disaster in California's history; the Rose Bowl Massacre of 2001. They wondered how such a monster could've just appeared and brought down the stadium, but no matter what the explanation, that wouldn't change the fact that 73 thousand innocent people died. I was solely responsible. It rained afterward. Rained moderately. Not a tempest, not a drizzle. It touched everything, those little drops from above. Seeped into the rubble, beat upon the rescuers, begging them and driving them to work fast to move the debris. Water is said to be the element of life. Without it, there can be none. The rain fell that day; perhaps trying to bring life, or preserve it for those trapped under the walls and seating of the stadium itself, and those who were crushed by the weight of their fellow victims. Oh, how the rain fell after the disaster. It fell for me. Tears from heaven, tears that weren't for me. Teras from heaven, tears for the victims. I'd sinned. Unintentional sin, but sin. They cried because my sin; they cried because my existence. It was the worst kind, what I could never escape from. I never went through penance, but what good would it do? I feel extreme guilt every day of my life, and I can't imagine getting past it. I could confess to a priest, but he'd tell authorities. The authorities would kill me or lock me away forever... and at that point absolution wouldn't matter. How could I enter Paradise with my head held high, with a clear conscience, knowing that I put tens of thousands of people in there before their time? I curse God sometimes; he has done so to me. I can't stay around large groups of people, because I spontaneously go crazy and start ripping people apart with my bare hands. I didn't want these 'powers' and yet I have them... Why didn't I get a choice in the matter? I would have turned them down. I had plans. I wanted to just live a normal life. Go to school, have a girlfriend. Maybe become a doctor or something. I had plans. But God had plans. Even more, he has a sense of humor. And he falls out laughing when you tell him YOUR plans. Forget him; he already forgot me.... 'Kazaki!' again she called. Lexi didn't care that I didn't like to be called out by name. I didn't think I deserved a name, though I have to have an ID card just to get around anywhere. She didn't care that I might lapse into my bloodlust and kill her. She did care, however, that I'd been sleeping all day and that I was going to eat dinner, if anything. I pulled myself out of the white cotton-sheeted double bed. Lexi's bed, neatly made-up no less than 12 hours ago... I looked at it, she left some clothes out for me. Black-and-white checkered pants, a black shirt with a blue flame print going around the bottom to rise up the back... 'Thanks, Lexi,' I thought, 'but I'm not trying to make a fashion statement tonight.' I could see it in my mind, the chalk outline and police all around. Detectives Sippowitz and Friday standing over my body. Friday would say, 'Well, he's dead. Poor kid.' Sippowitz, only slightly less serious, would point out, 'Snazzy dresser, though.' Meh, she was still calling. 'Okay, I'm up...' Upupupupup... I kept repeating that to myself, maybe I'd trick myself into waking up or something, I never know why I do that. 'Gah!' Stupid dog, why did it always have to sleep at the side of the bed? I rubbed my jaw, getting up off of the floor. Lexi's 7-year-old Golden Retriever, Bojangles, stirred slightly, but went back to sleep. Grabbing a towel from the door, I opened the door near my bed and made my way across the hall to the bathroom to take a quick shower. I don't open my eyes when moving through Lexi's house, no need. If there was anything here, I might look at it. Save for the dog. But aside from some beds, there's nothing around to look at. And she insists I don't go into any other rooms. So, I'm allowed to walk through 3 rooms (out of five) in this apartment, 2 of which are effectively off-limits if Lexi has a date over. In and out of the shower, I don't get too dirty or smelly each day. In fact, I figure it it's time to move again, soon. There isn't anyone in this town to serve my purpose. She put the steak and eggs on my plate. I wondered aloud where she'd gotten the money to buy steak and eggs; she said that her new boyfriend gave her some money. I kept it to myself, but mentally I chalked up her newest boyfriend: the tenth one this month. She didn't get steak knives, though, so I had to grab it in my hands and eat it that way. The television was on, playing reruns of 'In The Heat of the Night.' I hate that show, always have. That made me work extra fast to eat my food. I'd more plans tonight. There was the fight club meeting in the Third Street Gym after hours. If there was any place to get beaten to death, that would be the place. I scarfed down the cheese-scrambled eggs and took a few more bites of the pricey yet overcooked steak. Drank the milk in one gulp. A few moments later and I was out of the door. I had to go and fight. I had to find a superior opponent. There was a circle to complete. I kill with my bare hands. I must be killed by bare hands. Hopefully before going into the bloodlust. It's been happening ever since that sunken continent mysteriously rose to the surface in the Pacific... I called back to Lexi. 'Bye.' I had never said that to her before, it was always 'See you later.' I wasn't coming back, and Lexi knew it. She just whispered under her breath. 'Bye...'
