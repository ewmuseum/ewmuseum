Genesis [3]

22 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 22nd, 2003 Rush Wrestling, founded November 2001 by Joey Flash, picked up by Jeremy Howard May, 2002. Sold to Chris Sloboda June 17th, 2002. Inherited within PCW as by buyout between Xavier Kannon, PCW Owner and Skillz, Rush Owner November 24th, 2002. There is the entire background that RUSH ever had in its existence, prevailing as the number three spot in-between the head of the crop, Old School Wrestling and Primetime Championship Wrestling. Below us at the time were a small upstart but almost closed federation whom had Semore Smoke as its champion. New True Internet Championship Wrestling or NTICW for short. For then, it was always RUSH atop NTICW in the rankings, even tying PCW in the ratings a few times. All of this, these good times was under the Jeremy Howard administration where guys like Cassius Deamont, Chris Matthews, Statuz Quo, Marvin Harris, and El Spiffy would all become house hold names. We went on, under nearly packed arenas that PCW somehow left off of their venues for the summer season and we trekked on. I was still in Rising Star Wrestling once RUSH had re-opened towards the public’s eye with its brash tones and all around more developed on what occurred in the ring, not the soap opera outside of it. Jeremy looked at me from a few RSW tapes, then pushed them over towards the XWA, which was my next way of position before it even had gone a week, the employees in the main feud were gone, one to PCW after the Supremacy hiring’s and another just vanished completely. I said yes, wrote my name on the contract and let it ride. Officially, the set debut was for June 16th, at Rush’s crowning jewel of a Pay Per View. In an ECW type of way, this was supposed to be our jump off from the small leagues and into a national powerhouse. It satisfied, and the angle was nice, but then you’d go back and think about it. Joining Playaz Inc, the second version of it. Not to say that this version was as more talented or less talented than my own, but it was in its own little way. Check Statuz Quo, Dark Haze, and Ali Khadafi. I think that was the best group ever put together for a stable containing me. Yes, you had Quo, the champion (second tier), Haze a contender, and two fresh guys with a lot of heat behind their names. Sounds good right? Well, we lost, became a joke and then I ended up in singles competition against Andi the Magnificent, whom is now warranting PCW access as I write this. I remember when this guy was Ice Cold Andi, and ended up losing his moniker to take up magic while in NTICW. PI was cruising right now, with weekly battles with the Shack Pack highlighting the night. It was good exposure for us and everything. But then, Dark Haze got fired for something or another and left us as a trio. I think this is when Quo lost the Syndicate title to El Spiffy!Didn’t matter much, but this was the time when Jeremy Howard “vanished” for a good while. And Boda stepped in, bunked me down from my World title chances and put the title on an unsuspecting Brock Newbludd. Right place, right time. I packed my stuff, got out of there as quick as possible because I knew that if I had to get somewhere, at some place, it would have to be the big time. P…C…W.
