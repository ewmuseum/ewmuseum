The Now Politician has to go to TRIAL!

28 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/28/02 Time: 3:42 P.MLocation: Lisbon, PortugalScene: City Council Meeting “I’m King of the World, or maybe I’m just a City Counselor of Lisbon, same thing.” The scene opens with Juan Borro at a City Council meeting where he is going to be introduced to the other council members. Juan is a bit worried about the meeting, and how everyone else will think about him. He knows some people will favor him, but some others might notice how I’m not nearly as educated, and trained under certain topics as they are. Juan states his uncertainties to the city council leader, who helped him along in his path. Her name in Maria Gonzales and she was daring enough to put her beliefs in Juan. Juan- I don’t know about this Maria. I kind of feel like I’m invading these peoples jobs. I truly have no right being here. Unlike them I didn’t win any election or get voted in, I’m just here. City Council Leader (Maria)- Don’t worry Juan you’ll be just fine. As long as you follow my commands and do what I told you to do you’ll be just fine. If anyone has a problem with you, they’ll have to bring it to me. Juan-Thanks. Can I ask you why you chose me for this position to begin with? How would an escaped prisoner be a good representative for the people? It’s not like I don’t want this role, it’s just odd how I got it. Juan replies with a bit on concern in his voice. He then stares Maria straight in the eyes while speaking. When responding, Maria looks away and starts sorting out a bundle of papers. Maria- I guess I just like your look or something. I guess you’ll have to show me it was the right decision to make. Juan- Oh, don’t worry, I sure will show you I deserve the job. I even tried to do some research before I came today. Maria- Great! Maybe you’ll be ready for the meeting today. Did you go over the topics I showed you before, so you have a good impression of what we’ll be talking about? It’s very important that you read that. Juan- Of course I read it. I actually feel I can put up some good discussions on some of those topics. Maria- That’s great. And don’t worry if people begin randomly arguing with you. Some of these people just like to hear them selves speak. Juan- Okay. The scene goes to a group of five councilmen and councilwomen coming into the room and taking seats. They all begin to make small talk and make noise, as Juan takes a seat and looks around at the others. They all keep to themselves and segregate Juan from the rest of the crowd. Council Leader (Maria)- All right, everybody settle down so we can get the meeting started. Let’s start off with our national anthem. From there we’ll see what we discussed last meeting in our minutes, and then we’ll go on to current and new issues. We have a few fundraisers to pass by, and find locations for also. Everyone stands up and looks over at the Portuguese flag. They all recite the national anthem in a light tone, as Juan says it a little bit louder and prouder. He draws attentions to himself as everyone looks around, until the anthem is finished. Maria smiles to the others and signals for Juan to come up to the front of the room. Juan gets up and waives to everyone in the front. Council Leader (Maria)- All right everyone, I’d like to introduce our newest councilman who will be representing the people. You may remember him as a former soccer player here in Portugal, and he is currently a wrestler in the United States. Everyone give a warm welcome to Juan Borro. Juan Borro smiles to everyone who gives him a nice welcoming. Everyone either waives or smiles back, accept one man who does neither. Councilmen- (Under his breath) Oh great another jock taking over a political office, the world is slowly tumbling down. Juan tries to make like he didn’t here it, but he notices how some others begin making faces and talking to each other in private. Juan looks over to Maria who shrugs to Juan who seems a bit confused on what to do. She then signals for him to take a seat, as she goes over and grabs the gravel. She bangs the gravel three times to officially start the meeting. Council Leader (Maria)- Okay, we’ll start off right now by going through last weeks minutes, and issues. The Secretary comes up to the front and speaks. Secretary- Last Week we did a few things. At 4 o’clock we adjourned the meeting and said the national anthem. At 4:08 we did the previous weeks minutes. At 4:23 we went over old issues. Then at 4:58 we closed all old issues and went on two new ones. We finished that at 5:05 and from there we discussed certain fundraisers until 5:46. At 5:53 the meeting ended. The secretary sits down, and starts making down the agenda for this week. Maria gets back up at the podium and speaks. Maria- All right well let’s goes over some issues. We have the issues of fixing sidewalks to make them wider, speed limits near schools or other danger zones, scheduled busing for the elders, and a need for shelters in the down town area. Does anyone have any opinions on any of those issues? Councilmen- Yes, I think we talked about the streets last week, but I think it’s a total waste of time. Wider streets mean smaller roads. We have bad enough traffic to begin with, so I think it will just lead to chaos. Juan timidly cuts in. Juan- I don’t know what you guys think, but I think instead of making the sidewalks wider, we should just make a walking lane. Councilmen- Wouldn’t the sidewalk be a walking lane in it’s own, or am I the only one who has noticed that in the last hundred years. Juan- I’m saying we set up a separate lane, one that’s very small, and just for people who are walking by, and not shopping in that area. Maria- That’s worth consideration, any other opinions? Juan- I also think there should be speed limits near schools and buses for the elders. We have to please the children and elders in this city, because those are the majority of taxpayers in this area. If we don’t please them, then were doomed. Councilwomen- I agree we can’t be taking things away from them after they have supported us this far. Councilman- I don’t know what you two are talking about. Where are we going to get money for this? I know it’s nice to dream, but it comes down to financing and that’s something were low on. If Mr. Borro wants to go over and direct traffic near schools him self then go ahead. Maria- Why don’t you just stick to your job Richard, there is no sense in making fights that don’t have to happen. Councilmen Richard- Of course you side with him, I can just wonder what you’re getting from him behind the scenes. Juan-What are you getting toward, jackass. Councilmen Richard- Oh nothing at all. Maria- Why don’t you head home a little earlier today. I think the group would vote that your just being a disturbance. All in favor of having Councilmen Richard Diaz dismissed from the meeting, raise your hand. The majority of the groups raises their hand, and the councilmen just smirks with an attitude and walks out the door. Before he leaves he has one last comment. Councilmen- Just remember that I tell the truth, that’s something the Golden Toe over there will never know the concept of. He then fully leaves the room, as Maria continues as if nothing has just happened. Juan seems a bit embarrassed, but tries to not have it phase him to much. Maria- All right let’s move on to…….. The ringing of Juan’s cell phone interrupts Maria. Juan answers it and goes to the corner as they continue the meeting. The expression on Juan’s face suddenly turns to horror, as he hangs up and walks back slowly. Maria- What’s wrong? Juan- It seems the Portuguese government fought, but didn’t win. I’m still going to have to go to trial for the murder of a Chinese official. Maria- You’re going to TRIAL? Juan- Yes, it seems like I am. Everyone just looks in awe, as Juan is frustrated and just storms out of the room. Juan grabs his coat throws it on, and holds his face in disgust. The scene then fades to black.
