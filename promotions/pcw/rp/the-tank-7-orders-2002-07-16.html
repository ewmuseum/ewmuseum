The Tank 7 - Orders

16 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 16, 2002 Sunday, July 14 - Day 9 Bruno rubs his aging face as he enters his cell and takes a seat on his lower bunk to take off his weathered leather boots off. Age hasn’t been kind to Bruno Gasperoni, who has played the part of Doug’s number one man for leaning on people for the better part of the last twenty-five years. At fifty-six, Bruno worries that the value has completely drained from his body. Doug Hattoni’s getting a new hitman now. A younger man, of course. A man who, as far as Bruno can tell, has shown no fear to any of the dangers that come with the Tank. He’s cocky, thinks Bruno. But other than that, there’s no conceivable weakness - a tad small, maybe, but he’s likely to put on ten or twenty pounds in the Tank like everyone else. He’s a good kid, thinks Bruno, and he’ll do a good job. Doug Hattoni, Bruno’s cellmate, lies in the top bunk of the cell, his intuition telling him every thought on Bruno’s depressed mind. “Bruno,” Doug says his raspy voice as they weight for a C.O to cut off the Unit’s lights for the night. “Yeah Doug?” Bruno says in his own low tones. “I got a job for you, Bruno.” “For me?” Bruno asks as he stretches out in his bunk and plants his hands behind his head. “A headbusting job?” “Yeah,” Doug says, quiet enough to be sure no one is overhearing the conversation. “One last thing.” “Why don’t you have the kid doing it?” Bruno asks - he can’t help letting a bit of suspicious creep into his curiosity.” “Ain’t ready for it.” “Jesus, Doug...” Bruno says as he sits up in his bunk. He knows the job must be fairly important because usually the only thing it takes to complete on of Doug’s tasks is a knack for breaking people in half - Snow has that knack. So what more does this job require? “Get this, Bruno,” Doug says with a slight chuckle, “The kid came to me last night. You know the kid - Jason, Jason Snow.” “I see him around.” “He came in here and asked me to let him kill Harvey Blake!” Doug can’t help but chuckle louder at the thought. That same thought that Doug chuckles over shocks the aging massive Italian. Bruno stands up from his bunk and looks directly at Doug. “He wants to kill Blake? Why?” “Something about one of Blake’s little jokes. You know the kind I mean.” Hattoni says, his eyes closed and his hands folded over his fat stomach. “He was serious though. He wanted to kill Blake. He’s got balls - the little fuck - you’ve got to give him that.” “Don’t sound like he’s got much brains though,” says Bruno as he shakes his head. The thought of someone as new as Snow, with such limited experience about the way the Tank operates, trying to take out such a prominent figure as Harvey Blake seems insane. He has no doubt, however, that a man as physically gifted as Snow could do it - and talk of Snow’s violent temper has already circulated through the Tank. But what Snow is not thinking of is the aftermath of his actions. How would the Nazi’s respond? And who would they respond against? What would that lead to? Snow knows nothing about prison politics. “He’ll learn,” sighs Doug. “We all had to learn.” “Crazy bastard.” “It may be crazy, Bruno, but we’re going to do it for him,” says Doug, cutting to the chase. “We? You want me to kill Harvey Blake?” Bruno asks, having trouble keeping his voice down. “Doug, have you thought this through.” “I have,” Doug says without opening his eyes. “I need to show my loyalty to this kid. He’s a loose cannon, but he’s a hell of an asset to have. You know that Bruno. You, of all people, know that. This is your big hurrah, Bruno. After this, you’ll be sitting back with me and handling the supervision - no danger. Guys like Jason Snow will be protecting you too then. But this job, Bruno... you’re the man I trust for this one.” Bruno looks down at his wrinkled and scarred hands, “One last time, right Doug?” “One last time, Bruno,” Doug opens his eyes and puts his hand on the back of Bruno’s large head. “One last time.” *** From the Office of the Warden “The easiest way to knock out a superpower is to do the job before it actually becomes a superpower,” says the Warden as he stares out his office window, overlooking most of Peter J. Tankerton prison. “A tree can’t grow without a seed.” “When Doug Hattoni sees a tree,” the Warden sighs, “especially a dangerous tree, he digs up the seed... well... he sends someone else out to do it.” *** Monday, July 15 - Day 10 Snow sits in front of the television though his eyes never meet it. He stares through the corner of his eyes at Boris Valinov’s cell up on the cell balcony. Valinov’s been in there all day. He doesn’t seem like much of a threat, thinks Snow. He’s old, keeps to himself, and doesn’t seem like much of a physical threat. Still, as he’s noted about Doug Hattoni, these mob bosses are not to be underestimated. Hattoni said yesterday when they met that Snow would know when the time to move would be. No other description of the moment was granted. So now, Snow sits in front of the television, waiting for something to occur, cueing him to go up to Valinov’s cell. “Nice f-f-fucking boots!” says a stuttering familiar voice to Snow’s left. He looks over to find that the speaker is none other than Barney Bishop - known to most as Nam. Bishop is staring down at the pair of Snake Skin boots that Snow gave him a few days back. “F-f-fucking warm man. Warm. Waaaarm! God damn lining man. Fucking great. Great!” “Glad you like them,” Snow says before turning his attention back to Valinov’s cell. “Like them? I fucking l-l-love them! Need a favor man? I’ll d-d-do it for ya.” Snow has learned never to trust the word ‘favor’ in this place. “It’s ok, Nam. I’m set up pretty good for now.” “Suit yourself, fucker,” says Nam as he turns his attention back to the television where last night’s sports highlights are currently showing. “Nice boots though. F-f-fucking nice.” Suddenly, it happens. Behind Snow, two men begin to fight - both Italians. One, a portly man with a thick mustache, grabs another, a thin bald man with plenty of missing teeth, and the two begin slugging away at each other. With a crowd gathering around the incident, and every C.O in the Unit scurrying down to break things up, Snow takes the opportunity to quickly head up to the top of the cell balcony unnoticed... by everyone except Nam. Once at the top of the unguarded cell balcony, Snow slips over to Valinov’s cell and peeks inside. Boris Valinov, famed Russian mob boss, is putting on his eye glasses in preparation to see what all the commotion on the main floor is about. When he tries to step out of his cell, however, he’s interception by the young blond-bearded lion - Jason Snow. “What in the hell?” Boris sneers in his thick Russian accent, glaring up at Jason Snow through his barely noticeable eye glasses. And then, in an instant, he understands. “Hattoni sent you, didn’t he?” Snow says nothing. Instead, he shoves Valinov back in the cell, and then a second time against the concrete wall. “I expected you,” Valinov says while trying to catch his breath. As Snow suspected, he’s physically unimpressive. Snow is able to handle him like a rag doll. “Not you, of course, but I knew Hattoni would be sending someone for me. Doug Hattoni - always old fashioned.” Snow almost feels sorry for the old Russian, but he knows there’s no turning back now. He slams his elbow into the side of Boris’ face, sending his eye glasses flying across the cell. Before Boris can mount any type of defence, Snow drills him with a second elbow that sends the old Russian to his knees. So malicious, Snow thinks. Boris is more than twice his age and physically there is no comparison. What kind of a monster am I becoming, he wonders - a moment later, he finds himself kicking Valinov’s fallen old body in the ribs. Again, again, again... No mercy. Snow hates himself right now as he’s beating a man who he knows nothing of. He has no doubt that Valinov is a killer, but Snow has never seen the old man kill. Still, in this moment, there can be no mercy. Without a word, the young lion picks up Valinov and hurls him into the wooden post of his bunk. A moment after the impact the old man crumbles down and rolls over onto his back and stares up at his attacker. His face is bloodied now with little drops of the crimson liquid trailing through the wrinkles on his aging forehead. He’s had enough, thinks Snow. Snow has had enough too. This is not the wrestling ring. This isn’t even his sister in-law’s ex-boyfriend. This man has done nothing to Jason Snow, and Snow can’t bare to send another blow into the old man’s head... at least not this time. Under the heaving gaze of Boris Valinov, Snow rushes out and heads toward Doug Hattoni’s cell. The old sighs and stares up at the ceiling with blood pouring down in his face from gashes on his forehead, thankful for his life. *** Prisoner Profile File Photo: Coming Soon...Name: Anthony Vincent GasperoniPrisoner Number: 356500Nickname(s): BrunoRace: White (Italian)Born: 01-03-1947Height: 6'5Weight: 250Hair: Greying brown, bald on top, kept short.Eyes: BrownFacial Hair: noneTattoo(s): Tiger on his chestBuild: MuscularFrame: LargeIdentifying Characteristics: Very large, bald, characteristic walk because of aging joints.Current Sentence(s):1975 - Two Counts of Murder 1 (Life, without the possibility of parole)Previous Convictions: NoneFamily: Wife (deceased), son From the Office of the Warden “Some men kill people and go to prison for a year - maybe ten years - while others kill people and end up in the slammer for the rest of their natural lives.” The Warden is sitting on top of his desk as he speaks, flipping through a yellow newspaper dated in the mid seventies. “Bruno Gasperoni is serving for the rest of his life,” the Warden says glumly. “He was an infamous figure in the Italian mafia living in New York. A hitman and a bodyguard. The man saw more dirty money change hands than maybe anybody else on the planet. Drug deals - he was there. Gang murders - he was there. So when the FBI finally got some dirt on him after they pinned a double California homocide to him back in 1975, they were sure to make it stick.” “Rumour has it,” the Warden sighs as he hops down off his desk, “he could have walked off with a slap on the wrist had he agreed to testify against his employer. Stubborn bastard wouldn’t say a word. That’s loyalty. Stupidity? Well, sure, it’s that too. But as soon as Doug Hattoni stepped into the Tank, he made Bruno a king. However, even kings get old. No one can rule forever, but there will always be a prince in waiting...” *** “Just lie in the bunk, kid - like you’ve been in here all afternoon with me,” Doug says to Snow as the young lion comes crashing into his cell. Obediently, Snow plants himself on the lower bunk - Bruno’s bunk - and stays quiet, remembering the helpless look on Valinov’s face. “I trust you paid Boris a visit,” says Doug as the ruckus on the main floor below begins to die down. “And be assured, my favour to you is also in the works.” Doug is talking about the murder of Harvey Blake. “Damn it, Doug,” Snow whispers, “you didn’t say he was so... so... so fucking helpless!” Doug chuckles, “Helpless? Kid, you just did the world a favour. That Russian bastard put more men in body bags than most national militaries. I should know - a lot of the men worked for me.” Snow’s eyes narrow slightly - he wonders just how far Doug expected him to go with the ‘visit.’ Just then, he catches a glimpse of Nam, wearing his second-hand Snake Skin boots, rushing by the cell like the madman he is. “Crazy bastard,” Snow mutters under his breath, too low for Doug to hear. “Listen, kid, I have to talk to you,” Doug says in a tone that indicates the subject will soon be changed. “About what?” “About image,” Doug says sternly. “If you’re going to be working for me now, you’ve got to understand that everything you do - everything you say - has a reflection on myself and the operation that I run here. That does extend to the wrestling ring.” Snow’s eyes narrow. “Since you’ve been incarcerated in the Tank, PCW programming has been the most popular television show around here. I know these things kid - I know everything. From now on, you have to show no weaknesses, Jason... no weaknesses. That means you... and that means those who you associate with.” Doug sits up on the bed and pulls a deck of cards out his breast pocket. “The people I associate with?” “I think you know what I mean, kid,” sighs Doug. “I think you know what I mean.” The old mobster begins shuffling the cards in his aging hands. “And kid,” he says pleasantly, leaning down over the top bunk to get a good look at Snow below. “Yeah?” Snow says as he closes his eyes and tries to forget the look of horror in the helpless Russian’s eyes. “Welcome to the family.” *** From the Office of the Warden “Loyalty is an important asset anywhere in life,” begins the Warden. “But no where - no where - is it more important than within these walls. If you can’t trust the people around you in here, you have nothing. Absolutely nothing. Everything that you THINK you have is actually the property of that bastard you can’t trust.” “The problem with loyalty,” the Warden sighs, “is that it comes in so damn many forms...” *** Geoffery Rankin, Unit B manager, paces around his office with sweat running down his face. In times like these, he can’t help but regret taking this job. He always wanted to help the world, but this isn’t quite what he had in mind. His right hand is tangled in his thick brown hair as a Correctional Officer rushes into his office without knocking. “Finally!” Rankin exclaims, “I’d like to have some fucking answers about what’s going on out there! One minute, everything’s fine, and then all hell’s breaking loose! I thought you guys were trained to isolate problems like that! How in the fuck does one fight turn into four!? Would you please tell me!?” Geoffery is irate until he sees the colorlessness of the C.O’s face. “Sir,” the Officer says while swallowing a nagging lump in his throat, “Boris Valinov is dead!” “Dead?” gulps Rankin. All the while, he was concerned over a brawl that broke out while one of the prisoner’s he’s responsible for was dying. The Officer nods - “Dead.”
