Another kind of plague...

Date: Today. From the journal of Titu Xansiv: Entry dated Sunday 16th of January 2003: (Written before the following incident) We lost, but only because of that idiotic new owner, Syko the Goblin. What the Duat was he thinking? He let the fans down; he let his workers down; and more importantly he let Jason and I down. We went out there on Fusion to win a damn match and look what happened! We were locked in some dark and gloomy corridor and forced to find our own way out while DarkStar and Clyde somehow picked up the victory and the titles!!And then there were the incidents alone with Clyde. First, Isiy and I arrive at our locker to find a dead cow sprawled out over the floor, and then later on the demon captures my dear wife and holds her at knifepoint! She’s utterly shocked by the ordeal and won’t be at Tuesday Turmoil when I face Duke and DarkStar. I just can’t believe the PCW allow things like this to happen. It sickens me and it certainly sickens the Gods!In my sleep last night I foresaw a vision. It showed how a God would visit me in my sleep tonight and discuss matters with me. Obviously Ra now knows how I feel about the other day when I saw him in The Catacombs. I was strongly aggravated that he left so soon, but now, with the promise of a second visit, I can do little but forgive the ancient, wisdom-filled God. He is, after all my guiding light... *** Religion…...It’s a funny thing. People grow to be so obsessed with it that their lives suddenly seem far from worth living. They become so eager to meet their chosen deities that they see death as the only means through which they can succeed in such an act.Of course, not everyone goes to those extremes. Not everyone is that insane. And thankfully, not everyone is that incapable of maintaining a reasonable grasp on reality.As the educated man knows, though, religion comes in all shapes and sizes, from the small belief in Scientology to the colossal Faith in Christianity. And to some, no matter its size, their religious conviction is so much more than a belief. To these people their chosen creed is life, death, and the great unknown that follows. To Titu Xansiv, religion is just that… *** The words ‘Anaheim, California: 8:30 AM’ light up in bold, black italics as the scene begins to fade in on what appears to be a lavish garden. Already coming into view are the distant trees surrounding the garden’s perimeter, and a mansion-like house situated within the center of the vegetation. Through all of this runs a thick pebble path, leading off towards a pair of interlocking iron gates at the very end of the grounds. And there, standing patiently behind the gates in description, is a woman dressed in very basic, morbid clothing, a set of leaflets and books in her hands. Currently, the lady appears to be in mid-conversation with one of The Palace guards, a man by the name of Harold McMeansway, and the current longest serving member of the establishment in question. Woman: “I demand to speak with him at once, Mr. McMeansway! I won’t take no as an answer any longer! You’ll go fetch him now or I’ll bring the power of Christ down on you like a ton of bricks!!” Harold blushes violently. He really isn’t used to this sort of treatment. He was assigned as a guard of The Palace to guard the place, hence his job description. He’d never questioned whether or not he’d ever have to argue with a member of the Roman Catholic Church before! But then again, this woman was a regular, so to speak. He’d dealt with her time and time before, but never had quite got the hang of it. Harold: “Mrs. Livingston, his lordship demands his rest! I can’t just go up there and--…” Mrs. Livingston: “The power of Christ, good sir… Her voice edges Harold out, and with a gulp he nods in concurrence and makes his way off into the distance, his destination, the house. If one thing really sticks out about the place, it’s its size. On closer inspection one may take in the flamboyant and vivid stonework forged on the outer walls, but its dimensions are really what renders it to stick out from the other houses along the street. The lady, known only to us at the moment as Mrs. Livingston checks her watch in disgust as the early morning sun beams down upon the suburbia below. Her face twists and grimaces as she begins to strum her fingers over the papers in her hands and checks her watch again. In actual fact, only two minutes have passed since she last spoke with the guardsman, but she had always been a person who valued time dearly. This was not at all acceptable! A few more minutes pass before voices are heard from up ahead. Mrs. Livingston cocks a smile as the irritated visage of Titu Xansiv comes into view, trundling down the track, his hair a mess, hanging loosely over his shoulders and a crimson robe adorning his frame. The Pharaoh: “Excuse my frankness, Mrs. Livingston, but what in the Duat do you want at this time in the morning? Can’t you see it’s only half past eight?!” Calmly, the woman checks her watch again before raising her eyes towards the dark orbs of the Egyptian wrestler in front of her. Mrs. Livingston: “Twenty to nine, actually, Mr. Xansiv.” Titu sighs. He wasn’t up to this at this hour. Perhaps the ever-relentless Mrs. Livingston had done this on purpose. Perhaps she knew the Egyptian warrior would be in an unstable frame of mind for the conversation to follow, and would make a fool of himself and his religion in the process. The two had a long past, and although Titu never spoke of her, he did indeed think of the Roman Catholic believer as one of his greatest nemeses. She had first started to visit The Palace about a year ago, just after they’d moved in, and her reason for these visits was quite simple… To rid Anaheim of what she described as a ‘cultist without a real grip on reality’. The Pharaoh: “That’s beside the point, feral woman! I demand to hear your purpose for coming here today!” Livingston sparked a grin. Mrs. Livingston: “I think you already know why--…” Titu heaves a drawn out sigh, abruptly cutting the female off in mid-sentence. The Pharaoh: “You come here week after week in a vain attempt to try and convince me that your religion is better than mine and what in the name of Ra for?!” Livingston visibly cringes at Titu’s mentioning of Ra. The Pharaoh: “You never get anywhere, yet you relentlessly come back, and to be perfectly honest, Mrs Livingston, it’s beginning to piss me off! So take your bible, and get off my property!” Aside from a few frowns, Livingston remains unfazed. Another grim smile overcomes her face as she points in the direction of the pavement. Mrs. Livingston: “I’m not on your property, Mr. Xansiv.” Pharaoh scowls. Livingston grins. Pharaoh scowls. She grins. He snaps… The Pharaoh: “Right! That’s it! Guards!” Then Titu realizes she isn’t on his property. He’d never really been good at portraying a ‘morning person’. His memory lapses; his face looks ever-weary; his body feels tingly, and his head…by the Gods, his head never feels too great. And this, this is all he needs. Around five guards rush into the courtyard near the gate. They spot the irritated mug of their boss and sprint to his side, halberds raised high in the air, and each of them ready to aid him in whatever way they can. The Pharaoh: “I’m sorry, men, you’re not actually needed. It’s just her, she bloody screws up my mind!” The guardsmen’s attentions diverts from that of The Pharaoh to that of Mrs. Livingston. Upon sight of the mellow-looking woman, a collective sigh goes up. Guard#1: “Oh…her…” Guard#2: “Not again, boss!” Guard#3: “She’s like herpes; she just keeps coming back!” And with that the group moves out to do whatever the heck it is they usually do. A tense air consumes the area around the front gates, Titu trying to stare out the unmoved Roman Catholic. Mrs. Livingston: “So, Mrs. Xansiv, are you going to hear me out?” Pharaoh grits his teeth. He could ignore the woman and go back inside The Palace, but that would be like losing, something he wasn’t all that well prepared to do. So he decides to stick it out and battle her back at every opportunity. The Pharaoh: “Fine…go ahead…do your worst. But be warned, foul woman, you will not move my faith in the deities of ancient Egypt, remember that!” Mariah Livingston, as she was christened, beams again. It was that same old smile Titu had learnt to hate. It was full of overconfidence, full of wit, and full of ignorance. He loathed her, and he loathed that grin, and he sometimes wondered if had she not attained that skilled smirk and that stupendous ignorance for other religions whether or not he could put up with her. Long ago he’d decided against it. Maria Livingston: “Mr. Xansiv, we can offer you so much more than that filthy--… The Pharaoh: “No you can’t, so go on, leave me in peace!” He folds his arms and watches in delight as Livingston frowns. Mariah Livingston: “Why won’t you accept your fate and join us, the Roman Catholics church? We can offer you so much more than your cult--…” The Pharaoh: “Cult? Cult?! Let me ask you something, Mrs. Livingston…do you know the meaning of the word ‘cult’?” The lady shrugs. Mariah Livingston: “No--…” The Pharaoh: “Webster’s New Collegiate Dictionary describes the word cult as a system of religious beliefs and ritual. Did you know that?” Livingston looks a little perplexed. The Pharaoh: “And do you follow a system of religious beliefs and ritual, Mrs. Livingston? (Brief pause) Yes, I think you do, don’t you? So, it turns out we both follow a cult, Mariah! How odd, eh? So then, what else have you got to say?’ Mariah looks lost for words. She shakes her head this way and that, her mouth hanging lose, and her eyes full of anger. Mariah Livingston: “The dictionary is clearly wrong! Why, Jesus--…” The Pharaoh: “Was sometimes worshipped as Anubis of my religion!” The Roman Catholic scowls, stamps her feet theatrically and storms off down the road, beaten. Mrs. Livingston: “I’ll be back! Mark my words!” If only I was a Greek! Zeus would have surely shot her down with a lightning bolt by now! The Pharaoh: “That woman pisses me off greatly, Harold! Why can’t people accept who I am? Why can’t they accept the beliefs I chose to follow? I must show the world that I am a one-man religion, and not some fool who follows false Gods! …And I have an idea how I can do it too.” Pharaoh turns on his heels and heads back up the pebbled path, Harold following quickly in his tracks. Harold: “How, milord?” The Pharaoh: “Get the men to set up the press room. We’ve yet to use it, but today is its lucky day! Sort things out, will you? I’m going to get ready. And try not to make too much noise; Isiy’s sleeping after what happened on Fusion.” Titu sighs. The Pharaoh: “Why is my life such a complication, Harold? Why can’t things go my way? Why does this demon follow me?! Why…Harold?” Titu turns back to find the old man panting for air, his lungs unable to keep up with the brash, young figure of the PCW worker. McMeansway waves a hand in his boss’ direction, beckoning him to wait for him, but Xansiv simply sighs, shakes his head and carries on, the scene fading to black.
