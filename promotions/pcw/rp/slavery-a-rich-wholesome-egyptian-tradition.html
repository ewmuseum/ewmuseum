Slavery…a rich, wholesome Egyptian tradition…

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: As of late... [Part 1 of 2] Through the countless dynasties of ancient Egypt, one thing has always ensured that The Pharaohs of old kept their power, dignity and ability to produce great wonders of the world. It was a mandatory function in the cogs of life and most Egyptians, almost ninety percent to be exact, had to do it. Slavery, in ancient Egypt, was not always what it seemed. It was a way of living; you weren’t always a slave because you’d been caught by the Egyptian army and brought back to work on the pyramids, you were usually a slave because you were a peasant. Slavery, though sometimes extremely cruel, ensured that life in Egyptian went on, because if these men and women had refused to help in the matter of building The Pharaoh his monuments then he wouldn’t have been very happy whatsoever. This subsequently would lead to the possibility of mass slaughter, after all, you learn by example. But thankfully the peasants had brains and so, with little free time to spend doing anything other than building (or perhaps even harvesting the fields, if they were lucky), they kept their beloved Pharaoh, beloved Gods, and not-so beloved minders happy by doing as they were told. The Pharaoh Titu Xansiv is a man who you may say is a little old fashioned. He loved all the ideas his predecessors proposed, be it slavery, burial rituals, or even the way The Great Pyramid could tell the future, he adored them all. But living in a modern world can be hard. You can’t really go around saying to people that they are needed urgently to help in building some sort of monumental statue in remembrance of Ra. You can’t go around in the buff because it’s a little too hot outside, or else the cops would have you done for indecent exposure, and you certainly could not, by any means, walk into a modern world reciting verses from ancient holy books, otherwise you’d be ridiculed and mocked. It sure was tough. And Pharaoh, though he loved the people who adored him, hated the idea that he couldn’t act like his forefathers did. And so, with the patio in The Palace grounds now totally wrecked by the monster that Pharaoh believes Clyde and DarkStar sent to kill him, things are looking a little bleak. But why hire out men to do a job slaves could easily finish in a few hours? Now that was the problem… *** She stared for a while, surveying what lay before her behind the sheet of glass. What had happened yesterday was something the pair would rather forget, but how could you possibly be expected to forget something like that? God knows what the guards had done with the ghastly thing, but whatever it was she preferred not to think of it. She shuddered and decided to switch subjects. ‘We really need to get that fixed.’ Said Isiy, looking out onto the patio through the windows, ‘It looks awful like that.’ Pharaoh shook his head. ‘Don’t worry, my love; I’ve ordered the men to round up a few slaves to work on it. You know, a number of wrestle-crazy fans that would give anything to meet their hero. I just wish they’d bloody hurry up!’ Isiy sighed, ‘Well, fair enough, but just remember, we can’t actually address them as slaves. I mean we could be sued, or something, you know? I mean people generally look upon slavery as a bad thing these days.’ Pharaoh walked back across the room, a frown on his face. ‘I can’t see what would compel people to put an end to slavery; I mean, look what those Hebrews did for The Pharaohs of old! The Pyramids - he marked them down with his fingers – ‘the Valley of the Kings, the glorious temples, et cetera, et cetera. By Anubis’s maw, I’d have thought—’ ‘Ok, shut up before you dig yourself into a hole you can’t get out of, huh?’ Isiy interrupted swiftly, ‘We don’t need you under pressure from those damned anti-slavery people again, do we?’ Pharaoh fell silent, his eyes darting back across the room. Outside, the guards had finally gathered a group of Californians and marched them back into the Palace grounds. ‘Finally!’ exclaimed Titu, darting for the door, a grim smile almost evident on his mouth. ‘I’ll be back inside in a few minutes, jut let me get them started!’ And then he was gone. Isiy looked back outside at the growing scene with a sigh. The guards had indeed gathered wrestling fans, you could certainly tell by the way they looked. Each of them wore some sort of wrestling t-shirt. Whether it supported PCW or OSW, or even the recently crumbled FSW, they all had them on. They were all of a certain body size, too; well the men were. They were either hugely built, your typical jock stereotype, or they were slightly plump and rosy-cheeked. The women, on the other hand, were huge breasted bimbos, who more than likely had about as many brain cells as a brontosaurus! They were half human, half plastic and wouldn’t survive the day ahead, not for a million dollars. Pharaoh came into view, shaking a few hands as the fans began to circle him wildly. Their faces were bright and ecstatic as they gazed upon the PCW fan favourite gleefully, and a few notepads shot up out of the mass of bodies. Isiy gave a little wave to some of the people as they caught a glimpse of the beauty looking out onto the drive. She left her room soon after, once again with a sigh. Outside, Pharaoh addressed the men and women… ‘Ok, well, you’re probably wondering what exiting things we here at The Palace have got in store for you all today, right?’ ‘Well, no, we’ve already been told.’ Said one of the fatter men. Pharaoh glanced at the guards, who shrugged. ‘Oh,’ he said, ‘well Ok, then. Firstly I’d just like to point out that you don’t actually have to be here, and nobody is forcing you to do anything you don’t want to do.’ There was an edge to his voice, as though he’d practiced the line time and time again. Damned ban on slavery, he thought, it wasn’t like this when Pepy ruled Egypt! Then another part of his brain said: Then again Pepy sucked as Pharaoh, which therefore led to the peasants revolting and his subsequent downfall. ‘True…’ he mumbled to himself, a distant look in his eyes. ‘Sorry, sir?’ said one of the guards, puzzlement drawn across his mug. Pharaoh flinched, turning to the man, ‘Sorry?’ ‘Oh, just for a minute there, sir, you looked a bit…dreamy.’ He said, a jolly smile coming over him. ‘…Yes. Anyway, so as I was saying, you don’t have to—’ ‘Hey, kingy, dude, can I like take a look at your personal gym?’ interrupted one of the jocks with innocent eyes. Pharaoh sighed. A while back this man would have been killed for addressing him as ‘Kingy’. Then again, Pharaoh was only a descendant of the original Kings of Egypt. He wished things were like they used to be. Then he’d be in charge, and nobody, nobody, would be able to lay a hand on him without having that very hand chopped off. ‘No—’ he began…only to be cut off again. ‘Oh man,’ the youth kicked up some of the pebbles in anger. ‘I thought you looked out for the people!’ he whined. A general buzz of agreement circled the gathered workers. I really don’t need this! I have more things to think about! I mean I have to be at Fusion by Friday and Isiy’s going to kill me if I don’t get this patio finished soon. At least I’ll get a chance to finally end this losing streak though. Maybe even get my first win over Duke Williams and Snow. But this, well this is just about out of hand! I bring these people here and this is how they treat me?! Pharaoh knew something had to be done, but what? He needed the patio to be rebuilt quickly; it truly was an atrocious sight. And if the group began to think things weren’t fair here then they’d leave. He glared at the man until he realized he had no other choice than to… get rid of him. ‘Guards.’ The Palace guardsmen turned to their leader. ‘Sir!’ The men, each clad in their ancient Egyptian-style uniforms, saluted. ‘Take him away.’ Pharaoh waved a hand in the jock’s direction, ‘But don’t kill him…’ ‘What was that, milord?’ said Harold, the eldest of the guard, ‘You say to kill him, sire?’ Pharaoh saw the expressions of disbelief, and he heard the astonished gasps as the other men and women began to chatter feverishly amongst themselves, short sentences and volume enhanced words such as ‘My goodness!’ and ‘The bastard’ shrouding the air. ‘No, NO! I did not, did NOT, say to kill him, Harold!’ Bellowed Titu. This immediately silenced the frantic chattering of the fans and relief quickly filled the eyes of the highschooler in question. Pharaoh knew that if Harold had ordered the other men to kill the teen then they most certainly would have! Authority is seen in age, and Harold, as has already been articulated, was no spring chicken. The guardsman wagged a finger at Titu, ‘Right you are, milord; we’ll just toss him to the curb.’ He smiled wickedly and grabbed the man by the arm. ‘Whoa, take it easy!’ said the panicked jock. ‘I’ll ‘ave no lip from you, sonny!’ exclaimed Harold, frowning. ‘I wasn’t even getting—’ ‘See, there you are, at it again! Now cummon, you; let’s get moving!’ Harold forced the teen into the arms of the other guardsmen. They marched away across the pebbled drive, their elder trailing behind, and his halberd occasionally stabbing the youth in the back. ‘Hey, watch it with that thing would ya’?!’ he demanded only to be lifted up onto the other men’s shoulders and tossed over the wall. There was a thud a few seconds later. It was quite a high wall. Pharaoh turned back to the silent fans, their faces each carved with looks of pure, unadulterated shock. ‘So,’ he clapped his hands together giving the men and women a warm smile, ‘let’s get started!’ *** It was almost nightfall before Henry, one of the men brought in to help in building the new patio, called the others around for a team talk of sorts. His face was red and beads of sweat were accumulating on his forehead by the time word had got around the construction site and the last of the group had arrived for the briefing. He wasn’t the biggest of the men gathered there that day, but he had soon established himself as the most intelligent. ‘Right, I’m going to keep this short, guys, so listen up. Look at us; we aren’t slaves for that Pharaoh up there in his comfy little mansion. We all got told we’d have a nice time helping around The Palace today and now look…we’ve been transformed into some worthless bloody slave rats!’ Ralph, one of the high school football team members paid him a look of deep concentration, ‘I know what you mean, man, it’s like we’re building something other than this patio.’ ‘Indeed. I think they’re actually trying to make us build…well, this’ll sound crazy…but a pyramid.’ ‘What makes you think that?’ said one of the busty blondes, confused. Behind them the basic formation for what would appear to be a small triangular-shaped building had been formed. Slings and other contraptions for lifting had been set up too and guards were holding whips in the background, on a coffee break. ‘Oh, it’s just a hunch…’ said Henry, shrugging. ‘Anyway, we have to get out of this place, he can’t keep us here, you know.’ ‘Well, I was once watching Star Trek when—’ a nerd began only to be slapped hard across the face. ‘Pull yourself together, man! We have more to think about than Star Trek!’ said Henry, shaking some life back into his hand. A tear came to the nerd’s eye, ‘I’m sorry, I just’ – he whimpered – ‘I just can’t seem to stop thinking about it…I think I may need some…help.’ He sobbed some more before one of the jocks knocked him out with a solid punch. The others stared. ‘What?! He was beginning to annoy me!’ he argued. Henry sighed, ‘…Right. Anyway, here’s what we’re gonna do…’ To be continued…
