Road to Gold: The Roadblock

23 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/23/04 I hoisted Propaganda onto my shoulders and walked slowly toward the center of the ring. The crowd had sided with me as the lesser of two evils on this night, and was cheering wildly. I smirked slightly and was prepared to drop Propaganda. However, I felt him slip down behind me. My grip had been broken, and I turned to feel myself lifted into the air and then I felt pain shoot through my spine. He'd dropped me on my head..I could do nothing but stare at the lights on the ceiling. I heard the referee counting, but I could do nothing to raise a shoulder. I had been beaten. The plane jerked and I awoke in my seat, staring out of the window. It was a beautiful sight to see the clouds pass by the window, but it was lost on me in my current state. I was sweating from the nightmare I had just experienced. My head pounded with the sudden realization I had made. I wasn't the first two times we face, or even before I boarded the plane . . . but sometime when I was asleep I'd grown afraid of Propaganda. I sat forward and put my head in my hands, and then leaned back deep into my chair. I looked to my left to see that I was seated alone, with my bag on the chair next to mine. I looked around the plane, and noticed how clean it was. I also noticed that the majority of the passengers had grown interested in a movie playing at the front of the plane. I leaned my head back and closed my eyes once again. Propaganda would not stay down. I'd give him all I had. I had even gone as far as to beat him with a steel chair, and he was still relentless. I continued to beat Propaganda with the chair, but he continued to stare up with fiery red eyes. He'd continue to pull himself up, and I'd continue to put him down. In the background I could hear the time keeper ringing the bell repeatedly . . . almost to the beat of Metallica's 'Master of Puppets.” I awoke once again and shook my head. I could hear my cell phone ringing and reached down to pull it off my belt line. I pulled the antenna up and flipped it open. 'Hello' 'Buzz. It’s Dames.' I yawned and rubbed my eyes. It'd been two weeks since I'd talked to my agent, and it must be something important for him to call me already. 'Whadya' need, Dames?' 'Did I wake ya’?' I cracked my neck side to side, and looked out of the window. 'Don't worry about it..I don't think I want to sleep right now. I'm not that tired anyway.' 'Well . . . ', Dames' voice trailed off. 'Oh come on..what now! Is it about the PCW fine?', I was growing slightly ignored with the recent string of bad news. 'It's not that . . . I just wanted to tell you that you snuck your way into the World Title very quickly once already. Now you've been earning it for the past few weeks. You gotta’ beat three more guys . . . that's it. Three!', Dames' voice cracked as he exclaimed the word three. 'Well, tell me something I don't know. I mean I got everything under control. So don't worry about it.' 'Well, I just want to let you know that you gotta' win the belt now. You've got everyone's attention . . . if you don't win the belt . . . ' 'If I don't win the belt?' 'Buzz, if you don't win the belt from this tournament, and you win it down the road . . . people might not even care.' I felt my forehead wrinkle in confusion at the comments made by Dames. I slumped back in my seat and questioned his statement. 'What the hell is that supposed to mean?' 'Buzz, calm down. It's just when a baby takes it's first steps, everyone wants to watch. Second time around...it's almost just as good. But as it becomes more frequent, it loses it's novelty. What I'm trying to say is this. Everyone knows your good. But everyone also knows you've yet to consistently prove yourself. But right now you got a winning streak going, and you big match to prove yourself may be drawing close. Win it the first time or people might not care when you finally do prove yourself.' I let out a sigh before responding, 'Well, thank you for that philosophical incite. But it's just a matter of time before I end Skitzo's career, and win my second World Title. This time on my own. You know I can do it, I know I can do it.' 'Then all I have to say is do it. You're the only client I have with any talent since Joe Montana retired. SHOW ME THE MONEY babaaay...' I hung the phone up, and tossed it into my bag. I had nothing left to say to the old man, and to tell the truth I didn't want to think about what he said. The truth was I knew that my next 3 matches would probably be the matches that defined my career. I looked at my watch, and realized that we were probably 15 minutes away from Chicago. Almost simultaneously the captain came onto the loud speaker to confirm my assumption. I laid my head back against the seat, and decided that I'd take another quick nap. My thoughts became jumbled as I thought about the last 5 months. So much had happened and I knew the ride was just beginning. My road to the gold started as a long one, but the light at the end of the tunnel was drawing near. Sure there was a big road block in my way, a seven foot tall bible thumping madman. But I now realized once again that he was beatable. Propaganda was beatable. I'd done it before.....I'd do it again. I stood in the middle of the ring, with Inphino Blitz, Boda, Jimmy Diablo, and Propaganda all laying unconscious in the ring. The referee walked toward me with something shiny in his hands. As he approached closer I recognized it as the PCW World Championship. He handed the belt over to me, and I raised it high above my head to a mixed reaction from the crowd, albeit a loud mixed reaction. A response so loud my following words were lost forever except to me. 'Damn it's good to be champ'!'' The End
