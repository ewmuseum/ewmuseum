Date: THE CONCLUSION OF THE TANK SERIES ﻿*********The Tank __42—********* Master of Puppets And in the end, there’s nothing left to say. ~Leigh Donovan Saturday, November 23 - Day 142 He sits alone in the tiny waiting room staring, though not really looking, at a painting of a shipon the wall farthest from him. In the room beyond the closed blue door to his left, his future isbeing debated. Will they grant him freedom ? And even if they do, where will he go? Will hesend for his wife to pick him up and try to go back to living his life as close to normal as ever? With all of these new lawsuits flowing in the air, it’s hard to say what normal will be for himfrom one day to the next. Today, he’s a multi-millionaire, tomorrow he could be broke. But why get ahead of himself? The chances of him walking through the front gates of Peter J.Tankerton prison are just as good as the chances that he’ll be forced to spend the next fewmonths of his life back in his cell. Judging by the latest developments in his social life aroundUnit B, he knows the possibility of his survival of another day in the Tank is invisible. The decision that the parole board is making, just beyond the wall to his left, is whether or nothe will live or die. It’s been a busy morning. No sooner than he dressed this morning, Tim McDonald appeared in his cell and as usual,commanded Leigh Donovan to leave. Leigh agreed, though he didn’t go far from the cell - afterblinding Harvey Blake yesterday, he knew the Nazis will make an attempt on his life soon. “Today,” McDonald said when they were alone, “it all ends. We settle everything today, myfriend. Absolutely everything.” “It’s about time,” Snow said in a low voice. He realized that some very ugly tasks lay ahead ofhim in the next few hours. His involvement in Doug Hattoni’s murder had haunted him - untilthis day. Today, everything would end. With any luck, ‘everything’ would include his stay inthe Tank. “I’ve already taken care of one,” McDonald said, his voice growing even quieter. “Who?” Snow asked with genuine curiosity. “Shane Hood.” “What!?” Snow couldn’t help but sound somewhat upset - Shane Hood hadn’t even turnedtwenty years old yet. “He was just a fucking kid!” “He knew too much,” shrugged McDonald. “He was easy though. He’s been buying heroinefrom some of my boys and was a few days late on payments. We made an example of him.” Hedidn’t mention that a C.O found the young man in his cell late last night while Snow was atFusion, his throat slashed three times. By now, murders in Unit B weren’t even uncommon. The C.O’s considered a day without murder lucky - a day without violence was a miracle. Things went straight downhill since Geoffery Rankin’s death. “You ready for your part?” heasked Snow. The lion kept his silence. There was plenty of time for roaring later. Saturday, November 23 - Day 142 Only a few moments have passed, but Snow can’t help but feel fidgety. He nervously rubs hissweaty palms together. One thing that Harvey Blake’s vicious haircut has done is make Snow look presentable. Ofcourse, he had to have the blood washed out and it had to be recut by someone not using ajagged blade and punching him in the face. Still, Snow has never looked more suitable orrehabilitated. His hair is very short now and it’s natural yellow is gleaming under the bright lights. He’s alsowearing a full black suit for the first time in recent memory - his lawyer dropped it off justbefore he went before the parole board and tried to prove himself not to be a danger to society. There’s still blood under his nails. Tim McDonald announced to his mafia boys this morning that Vincent Giovanni was a rat andhad been ordered dead by their superiors on the outside. Understandably, none of them wantedto go up against Giovanni, a man known for systematically killing men with whatever washandy. Instead, they agreed to provide a distraction for the C.O’s and threatened the otherprisoners while a ‘designated hitter’ did the job. Word got out that Giovanni was to be taken down, to everyone but Giovanni himself, and theprisoners of Unit B took great pains to stay away from the bullish Italian. They knew not whothe mysterious hitman was to be, only that the hit was to go down soon No one followed Vincent Giovanni into the shower. Vincent thought it was his lucky day at first - for the first time in months, he didn’t have to stareat nude males while he washed himself. It was rather refreshing, really, until he heard the heavyclicks of footsteps from behind him. Naked, he looked over his shoulder to see who hiscompany was, but his eyes were stinging with water and a few strands of hair that were longenough to reach them. “It’s over now,” a voice broke over the sound of the shower. After rubbing his eyes, Vincentrealized that he was standing naked in front of a beardless, short-haired, Jason Snow. Snow wasfully dressed - he hadn’t come to shower. Before Giovanni could escape or attempt an attack, he felt the impact of two square punches ringoff his jaw. A third connected, and Vincent slipped on the slippery shower floor, falling harshlyon his back. Snow loomed over the fallen Italian mobster, ready to fulfill his orders. The hit that themobsters on the outside had ordered came in under false pretenses - Tim McDonald was the realrat, and he framed it on Giovanni. McDonald, who ironically had been spoken highly of byDoug Hattoni himself, wasn’t questioned on his sincerity. “Do you know who I fucking am?” Giovanni asked in a booming voice. He was proud of hisposition in the Hattoni family mafia, though he didn’t know that the very family he was so proudof had ordered his death. “You’re the man threatening the lives of my family,” Snow said as he turned the shower off,careful not to get his clothing wet. Before Giovanni could make a grab at Snow’s leg, the lionkicked the Italian cleanly in the face, opening a wide gash on his aging forehead. Giovanni attempted to crawl away, but Snow was determined to remained focussed - to remainruthless. He reached up and broke the shower head free from the wall and then used it as a tinyclub as he beat viciously away at Giovanni’s face. Snow’s main objective had always been to protect his family, and this was what the protection ofhis family called for today - murder. He beat Giovanni for a full two minutes after he was surethat the bullish Italian was dead. He then turned one of the other showers, tossed the brokenshowerhead in the flow of water, and left the room. In the commotion that Tim McDonald had started (one of his Italian gang members were posinga mock suicide attempt), it was easy for Snow to slip into the line of spectators and take a spotbeside Tim McDonald. McDonald gave an inquisitive look. Snow nodded. Saturday, November 23 - Day 142 He hears a sound from the next room and immediately rises to his feet, hoping that someonewith some news will come out. Nothing. In disappointment, he sits back down and scratches his clean shaven chin - his wife had beenpushing for him to adapt this clean-cut look for quite some time. Silently, he wonders if they’llbe able to overcome this latest obstacle. They’ve overcome so much already, but he fears that aday will come when she won’t stand for any more of the constant trials that their relationship isforced to withstand. He patched up another relationship earlier today... or something like that. With his meeting with the parole board growing ever closer, Snow asked Benjamin Graham,head C.O of Unit B and currently the man in charge of all decisions, if he could visit theinfirmary to have the cuts on his face made as little visible as possible. Graham didn’t deny Snow his request. Since defeating J-Thug in the monumental brawl in thegym, Snow was a legend, especially among those who had attained significant amounts ofmoney from the fight. Benji Graham was one of those men - he was rumoured to have bet in thearea of two thousand dollars on Snow’s victory, meaning that he walked away with six thousand,on top of the two that he laid down. When in the infirmary, Snow immediately searched out J-Thug, who was being treated for thejaw that Snow crushed to end their epic battle, just two days before. The infirmary stank ofrotting flesh, infections, and second-rate medicines. The beds were sloppily lined on either wallof the hall-like rooms, and Snow made his way from one to the next, checking each bed for themassive black body of J-Thug. He found Harvey Blake first, blind and moaning with his arm covering his heavily bandagedface. Snow couldn’t help but stop and stare at the pathetic man for a moment - in truth, HarveyBlake was the real reason why Snow came to the infirmary, but one thing that he’d learned fromTim McDonald is that the direct approach wasn’t always best. He found J-Thug a few minutes later, resting peacefully in the bed with his massive arms foldedacross his bare, heavily bruised, chest. When he spotted Snow, J-Thug made no motion - hecouldn’t talk, of course, thanks to his shattered jaw. The majority of his head was wrappedtightly in bandages. “Quite a fight yesterday,” Snow commented, checking over his shoulder to make sure no staffmembers had entered without his notice. J-Thug made no motion. “I know what you think of me,” Snow said to him, momentarily thinking that maybe J-Thugdidn’t recognize him without the long hair and the beard. J-Thug’s hard glare, however, toldthat he knew all too well this was the man who defeated him yesterday in the gym. “And I knowwe’ve never gotten along too well. But there’s someone that I hate a hell of a lot more than you,and you hate this person a hell of a lot more than you hate me.” J-Thug’s eyes become a bit more receptive. “You know who I mean,” Snow said quietly. J-Thug nodded. “This person just so happens to be a few rooms down,” Snow added, pointing over his shoulderin the direction of the room. “He’s in the blind ward as we speak. You know, should anaccident happen to a person in that room, with all of the other occupants having some sort ofsight-depreciating ailment, it seems to me that no one would see it.” J-Thug’s arms began to unfold and he made a motion as close to a nod as he could. “And I hear,” Snow said quieter than ever, “that this hospital is terribly understaffed at night.” J-Thug knew exactly what Snow was saying. In a gesture of affirmation, he reached his massivehand out and the two shook with a mutual respect for each other, even over this ugly agreement. When night falls, J-Thug will creep two rooms down and find Harvey Blake’s bead - he’ll makeit a deathbed. At first, Harvey will think it’s a doctor, and then, maybe one of his Nazis come toconsole him. In the end, he’ll find his life snuffed away as J-Thug holds a pillow over his face,breaking his neck in the process. The other inmate-patients won’t know a thing. Any whoactually can see will be sleeping. Harvey Blake will die at the hands of a black man, blind, and in complete silence. Satruday, November 23 - Day 142 Suddenly, Snow’s lawyer, Ron Elseworth, emerges from behind the blue door. He’s not animpressive man, standing just five and a half feet tall, yet weighing well over three hundredpounds. He’s completely bald on the top of his head and sports a pure white beard. But the onlyimportant thing about Elseworth’s appearance is the smile on his face. “You’re fucking kidding me!” Snow exclaims joyously, leaping from his seat to embrace the fatman. “Say it to me Ron! Tell me that I’m getting out of this fucking place.” “As of this moment,” Ron takes great joy in saying, “you’re a free man!” Snow is overwhelmed by the news. He hugs the fat man again, and then runs over a list ofthings he must do before leaving. He must take anything from his cell that he may want to keep- then again, there probably isn’t anything there worth keeping. He must say his goodbyes to aselect few people. And, as Tim McDonald said earlier in the day, he must ‘settle’ everything. Absolutely everything. Within minutes, Snow is strutting through Unit B, wearing his expensive black suit. A few ofthe prisoners cheer, though most just eye him in envy. Snow nods to the smiling Tim McDonaldas he walks by, then to the battered Leigh Donovan, who saved his life just yesterday. It was areturned favour. However, the man who Snow wishes to speak to is none other than Benji Graham, the head C.Oof the Unit. Graham smiles smugly at the newly-freed man, sitting behind his control desk. “Jesus Christ,” Graham says when Snow finally approaches, “I thought you’d be on a bus to oneof your mansions by now.” “In a minute,” Snow says with a slight smile. “There’s something I have to talk to you aboutfirst.” “Talk to me?” Graham asks, startled. His conversations with Snow had never been more than afew passing comments. “I want to talk to you about your relationship with Tim McDonald,” Snow says, leaning close toGraham. “I know he gives you money.” “What the fuck is this!?” hisses Graham. “Are you trying to fucking blackmail me? Because ifyou are, motherfucker, I can throw a God damn wrench in your parole pretty fucking quick, youknow.” “It’s nothing like that,” Snow assures Graham. “I just have some information that I’d like topass along.” Graham’s eyes narrow suspiciously. “Tim isn’t a very trusting person,” Snow says quietly. “He’s always feared that one of hisItalians will turn their back on him, because he’s Scottish and all, and rip off some of his money. He keeps it all in his cell, you know.” Graham’s eyes widen now, with genuine interest. “All of it,” Snow emphasizes. “He’s got more money sitting underneath his bunk then somewealthy men keep in the bank. Now, no one knows he keeps the money there. So, if somethingwere to happen to McDonald in a shakedown, then all of that money could just disappear. Noone knows it’s there, so the only person who would notice it missing is McDonald himself, buthe wouldn’t be around to complain.” Snow and Graham exchange looks. Graham’s is inquisitive and Snow’s is serious. Startled bythis information, Graham nods, and Snow gets back on his way. Saturday, November 23 - The Final Day A straight-faced C.O takes his time unlocking the heavy main door of the Tank - a cab is waitingfor Snow, just on the other side. It’s a fifteen minute drive just to the end of the Tank grounds,another ten before they’ll hit civilization. Impatiently, Snow waits for the massive door to swingopen. Meanwhile, back in Unit B, a pair of staff paramedics zip a body bag closed, and carefully liftthe carcass that it incases onto a stretcher. “He refused the shakedown,” shrugs Benji Graham to one of the lower ranked C.O’s. “I wasfinding drugs all over the fucking place.” He nods. “Then the fucker got violent. He reached formy night stick, the stupid fuck. I had no choice but to give him a couple of cracks.” In reality,the ‘cracks’ were given fourteen times in all, each of them straight to Tim McDonald’s cranium. He made sure the young man was dead, and then found a cigar box hidden under the lower bunk,just as Snow said. “You just don’t know what these animals are capable of,” says the other C.O, shaking his head. Graham nods, his hands sweeping over either of his hip pockets, which he split Tim McDonald’s cash between. The paramedics wheel the body out of Unit B. “Another fucking death,” the lower ranking C.O sighs. “These pricks are dropping like flies. Either they’re killing each other off, or their forcing us to kill them.” Benji Graham’s not toosure which this specific killing is a case of. “Today,” Snow remembers Tim saying, “we settle everything.” And indeednow, with everyone, including McDonald, who could link Snow to Doug Hattoni’s murder asdead as the old Don himself, everything is settled. Saturday, November 23 - The Final Moments The door slowly pushes open and Snow is hit in the face with the rays of a stranger sun - sure,he’s seen it plenty of times in his trips to various wrestling shows, but this time, it’s so muchdifferent. Today, the sun is his again. He possesses it, since he is no longer possessed. He steps toward the light, leaving an eternity behind in the cold stone walls of the prison, andinto the warm winter of California. He stepped into the Tank, five months earlier, as a badperson. He knew he was a bad person. Prison did not rehabilitate him. Instead, it transformedhim from a bad person to a murderer. Life taught Snow violence, the Tank taught Snow murder. Nothing will ever be the same now, he knows. He’s locked so many doors in his time in theTank. There are so many places inside himself that he can never return to. The world is newnow, and he can do nothing but try to make the best of whatever opportunities are handed tohim. With that final thought, he lifts his foot to step from within the confines of the stone wallsof the Tank, and back into freedom. Whether or not freedom will accept him is a story as of yetunknown... Snow’s foot comes down on the black pavement of the outside world - hisfirst step as a free man in months. Even in that step, Snow finds elements that are refreshing. With a straight face and a smile on his heart, he makes his way to the cab that’s waiting for himjust a few short, yet so long, feet away... Snow: “Ready for a long drive?” The cab driver smiles through the open window of his car. He’s a middleaged man with a thick head of black hair and a thin mustache that moves dramatically withevery word... Cab Driver: “I was about to ask you the same thing.” Snow smiles slightly and then gets into the backseat of the cab, reluctantly. He wishes he could stand outside for a moment longer, but there are matters that he must attendto... Cab Driver: “Where to?” Snow sighs and scratches the back of his head - he’s still not used to hisnew short hair... Snow: “I need to find a phone. Take me somewhere that I can make some phone calls.” Cab Driver: “Don’t they have phones just inside the prisons.” Snow looks through the window at the inexplicably large torture chamberthat he’s endured for the last five months. Every barred window is screaming at him to runwhile every concrete block in the wall mocks him to come back inside... Snow: “I don’t think I want to use any of those.” Cab Driver: “You’re the boss.” With that, the cab driver starts the engine and starts the car down the longroad toward civilization. In the backseat, with his head rested safely on the back of the seat,Snow savours every breath, sight, and moment... THE END
