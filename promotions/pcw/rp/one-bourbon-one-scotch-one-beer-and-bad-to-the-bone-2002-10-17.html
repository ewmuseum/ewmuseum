One Bourbon, One Scotch, One Beer and Bad to the Bone

17 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10-17-02 RP 2 Fusion 18th RP 1 Turmoil 22nd Windows are rolled down on the midnight purple four-wheel drive pick up truck. A sledgehammer lies across the bench seat with the word ‘Buster’ engraved on the wooden handle. Over the truck speakers, Teresa sings aloud to George Thorogood. She presses the clutch, shift gears and racing on down the two-lane highway. ON THE DAY I WAS BORN…THE NURSES ALL GATHERED ‘ROUND…TO GAZE A THOUSAND WONDERS AT THE JOY THEY HAD FOUND..THE HEAD NURSE SPOKE UP SAID LEAVE THIS ONE ALONE. SHE KNOW RIGHT AWAY….THAT I WAS BAD TO THE BONE! With one hand on the wheel, Teresa picks up the sledgehammer and examines it. The scene unravels in her head when she attacked Cyclonica and costing her the Cruiserweight Title. She’ll be damned if she lets a Valet win her over. To Teresa, the blue haired bombshell got what she rightfully deserved; a good pop in the jaw. Now if Daddy Dearest keeps out of this, our Top Rope Temptress will be doing just fine. The last thing she needs is a father figure butting in on a daughter’s fight. The song changes the blues-type beat to ‘One Bourbon, One Scotch, One Beer’. No matter how far you take a person out of the country, you can’t take the country out of the person. In this case, it’s just good ass kicking Southern Rock. All this singing about liquor and beer gets Teresa thirsty. She looks to the empty seat beside her and places the sledgehammer back on the seat. Teresa now realizes this is the first time in years she hasn’t had a traveling partner, a drinking buddy, or even someone to argue with outside the ring. She shakes her head and turns the volume up louder on the stereo attempting to drown out any thoughts that would cause her to miss those of her past. Sorry guys, tonight I’m drinking alone. These words are said as if someone or some people were actually in the truck with her listening. As it has been said before, the past is come and gone. The present is now and what she does now affects the pages of the future. Right now, Teresa sees no man to fill the empty passenger seat in her future. Damn, I need a drink before I flip. Ah! There’s a store The Chevy step side pulls into the parking lot of a small liquor store. The building is wooden and painted black. An overhang shelters the entrance and neon signs decorate the front windows advertising various liquors. Teresa jumps out of the truck and strolls to the glass door then steps inside. A medium sized short black haired man stands behind the counter. He looks no older than 35 and takes a step back seeing who just walked in. Oh my gosh! You’re Teresa Tomas! Can I take your picture? Before Teresa can speak, the man had already taken a 35mm camera and snapped a picture of her as she first looks at him. Teresa raises an eyebrow to the man and picks up a bottle of Wild Turkey. If I see any part of that picture in a tabloid, I’m going to hunt you down and break your fingers. Catch my drift, sir? The man nods quickly putting his camera away under the counter and watches her place the bottle on the counter to be checked out. Yes, Miss Tomas. I couldn’t resist. In actuality, the man is not thinking about selling the picture. He had gotten nearly all of Teresa in the picture and he’s planning on enlarging it and tacking it to his wall. Teresa leans on the counter nodding slowly I’ll tell you what, let me take this fine bottle of Turkey with me and I’ll give you two free tickets to Turmoil next week. Just like Teresa to carry extra tickets around with her. As you can see, she uses them as bribes. She is known to give tickets as rewards to lucky fans that haven’t ticked her off. Upon hearing this, the man doesn’t hesitate to bag the booze in a paper sack and in return, Teresa hands him two ringside tickets for Turmoil. Back in her truck, Teresa glances at the time. Three hours until Mall Brawl. She knows she better stomp on the gas if she’s going to get there and get ready before it all begins. The thought lingers in her mind that there is the huge possibility that she will get banged and roughed around. She will need this Wild Turkey to calm her nerves after the show and relax her will be sore muscles. For now, she’s thirsty and decides to pop the cork. One drink tastes pretty fresh. Two drinks even better. One more and that’s enough. Three. Four. Five. The tall pick up pulls crookedly into the Mall Parking lot. Inside the cab, Teresa sings louder than she did earlier and doesn’t hear that she’s off key a bit. The door opens and she slides out holding an empty bottle of Wild Turkey. Whooohooo! Mall Brawl; here I come to kick your sweet ass! Teresa tilts the bottle for another drink but finds it empty. She also finds herself holding onto the side of the truck to keep from losing her balance. She peeks into the bottle with one eye and tosses the empty bottle in the bed of the truck. It shatters. Oh hell, I just killed the turkey and I’m so freaking drunk, I can’t stand straight. Oh well, let’s have a Mall Bang. I mean Brawl. Ready or not, here I come! Having yelled these last words, the drunken Diva staggers to the front doors of the Mall. She waves at the security camera looking at her from the entrance, and then she flips it off and makes an attempt to step inside but forgets to open the door and walks right into it. Now this will be fun, now where’s the bathroom..
