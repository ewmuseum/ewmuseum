Warcraft: Tides of Lexian [Part Three]

12 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 12, 2002 A war to end wars I licked my bottom lip, still unable to find the words that could describe my feelings at the moment. Fear was one of them, but I doubt it was the only. To be thrust into the everlasting light of battle seemed too momentous for me to bear, but it would not be a task that I could avoid. I didn’t know if they would make me fight, but something told me that the fight would be brought here, and there was nothing I could do to avoid it. It would be like a storm, seemingly chasing me wherever I went. No matter how fast I ran or how far I turned, it would be there. “A battle?” I asked, fear trickling from my lips. “But why?” Cho’Gor looked at me as if I was stupid. “We are war, human. Diplomacy was never an option with these humans. You do not conquer lands with words or false promises. You will not be involved in the battle, so fear not. Horal has other plans for you.” “And who the hell is Horal?” I snapped back, still confused on who this person was. I heard his name a thousand times, yet I still had no clue who they were referring to. Suddenly, Cero grabbed the back of my neck and placed his axe against my throat. I could feel the sharpness of the blade as it pricked my skin. He breathed heavily. “Never speak of Horal’s name in such a manner again. Do it, and I promise you that you will not live to see the morning.” “I would listen to him,” Cho’Gor warned. “Although I command these legions, even I do not hold an absolute power over them. You would be wise to watch your step.” “I’ll try…” Cero let me go, sending me toppling to the floor in a heap. He walked past me and into the building in front of us, which looked like a palace of sorts. Cho’Gor waited for me to get up, but I was in no hurry. As I wiped some of the blood away from my neck, Cho’Gor came over to examine me. “Are you hurt?” He asked, no concern at all sounding in his voice. “A cut, but nothing major,” I replied. “Are all of your warriors as maniacal as he is?” “There is nothing wrong with Cero,” He said. “He is still a part of the old school regime that still believes in toughness and willingness to sacrifice everything in the name of the Horde. I used to be like that, but even I know better.” “Maybe the Horde would be better served if they listened to you,” I suggested, watching the warriors continue to heckle the peons. “That is not for you or I to judge,” Cho’Gor warned. “Now, enter the palace. We’ll set up a room for you to stay while Horal figures out what to do with you.” I followed Cho’Gor into the palace where I was immediately met by two large grunts who would refuse to let me pass. Cho’Gor waved them off and they eyed me closely as I made my way into the main hall. The room was crude, with garbage lying everywhere and even a few human corpses hanging from the ceiling. I had to hold my breath from the stench as the bodies rotted above me. I suppose they were reminders of the spoils of victory and how strength and honor can bring untold riches. As we walked through, I saw more human weapons and clothing sprawled throughout the hall. That’s when I realized that this was a human palace – the Orcs must have taken it. The blood splashed against the wall still looked fresh, which meant that they hadn’t captured it a long time ago. This must have been recent. As we walked through a hallway, I noticed an orcish body lying in the way. There was a sword plunged in his chest, with dried blood covering the entire blade. His eyes were still open with his mouth in a gaping fashion. Cho’Gor took the blade out in a quick manner and threw it to the ground. He bowed his head slightly and continued to walk on. I stepped over the body and tried to keep my composure. I didn’t do well against the sight of dead bodies. Finally we reached a corridor with a large door in front of us. The door was decorated with several emblems of the sun and the moon, and there were two grunts standing guard at the door. “Is Horal ready to see me?” Cho’Gor demanded. The first grunt shook his head. “Horal gave us implicit orders not to let anyone into the chamber room. He is still meeting with his advisors.” Cho’Gor grunted and slammed his fist against the wall, creating a small hole where his fist used to be. “Horal and his advisors…I should be advising him, not those crack pot pretenders.” “Watch your tongue,” The grunt answered. “You come perilously close to treason.” “Treason?” Cho’Gor hissed. “I have served under Horal’s command for almost eight cycles now. I believe that I have earned some respect from him and the rest of the Horde.” “Respect is earned, not given.” Cho’Gor came up to this grunts face and snarled. The grunt snarled back, but it wasn’t as nearly as ferocious as the one Cho’Gor had just given. He grabbed the grunt by his neck and hurled him past me and to the other side of the corridor. The other grunt tried to make a move forward, but Cho’Gor unsheathed his axe and pointed it to the grunts head. He backed off. “I don’t think that was such a good idea,” I replied, watching the grunt get back to its feet. “If he wants a lesson in respect, then he shall have it,” Cho’Gor replied. The grunt hissed and took out his sword. As he rushed forward, an arrow pierced his back and he fell to the floor in a heap. My eyes became wide with disbelief as I watched as three elven warriors dropped from the ceiling and pointed their arrows at Cho’Gor. “ELVEN SCUM!” Cho’Gor shouted. “You will pay for your trickery!” “It is you who has tricked us,” The elf responded. “And it is you who shall pay the dearest price for defiling our world.” There was no chance that Cho’Gor had in stopping the elves. They had long range missile weapons, and he had an axe. Even though he had quite a size advantage, they just showed that a well placed arrow could even out the battle very quickly. I figured it was time I earned my keep. I rushed forward and tackled the elf closest to me. That sent all three of them tumbling to the ground, including myself, and we wrestled for a moment. I heard a loud grunt and realized that Cho’Gor was now on his feet and hurled one of the elves to the wall, crushing him against it. Another elf took out his small knife and stabbed Cho’Gor in the back with it. He yelled out in pain, which allowed the elf to slump against the wall. He was unconscious. The elf raised his knife in the air once more, but I grabbed his arm, spinning him around and landing a right cross to his face. He fell from the stiff blow, and I was going to follow it up with a hard kick, but the other elf threw me backwards. “What are you doing?” The elf shouted at me. “Why are you helping them?” “Let’s just say I don’t like to see backstabbers kill people when their backs are turned,” I replied, still well aware of the fact that he was standing over me. He pulled an arrow from his pack and raised it above his head. “For your treason against Azeroth, you will die…” “ROOOOOAR!” Cho’Gor grabbed the elf from behind and tossed him against the wall as well. This time, he pulled out his axe and with a swift move, removed the elf’s head from his body. Then all was silent. Cho’Gor sheathed his axe again and looked at the destruction that was caused. He laughed as he looked back at me. “I did not know you were a warrior,” He said with an almost sheepish grin. “Neither did I,” I replied. Suddenly, the doors to the chamber opened and three trolls emerged. A figure behind them burst through, armed with a large broadsword and a sour look on his face. He looked at me with a sinister stare and then back at Cho’Gor. “What has happened here Cho’Gor?” He demanded. “Lord Horal,” He replied, bowing slightly. “We were ambushed by three elves. They killed one of the guards. This human helped us repel the attack.” “This human?” He said, staring at me. “But why would he help us? What business does he have here?” “He is not on the side of the Azerothians,” Cho’Gor responded. “He is not from this world.” “So, he is the off worlder,” Horal replied. “So be it. Meet me in my room, Cho’Gor. We have much to discuss about the battle tomorrow, as well as the human. Come. Leave the human in a room in the lower quarters and make sure he stays there until we come for him.” Two trolls walked up to me and helped me back to my feet. As I passed Cho’Gor, he grabbed my arm and brought me close to him. “Your deeds today will not go unforgotten.” I nodded and then walked towards a spiral of stairs that I assumed lead to the room where I would be staying. Most likely it would be dark, dank, and an overall bad experience to stay at. Maybe there would be a hundred corpses there for me to stare at and wish were gone. Only time would tell how much longer I would endure in this world, but this much is certain – this isn’t a game anymore.
