On top of spaghetti, all covered with cheese, I lost my poor tempber, when somebody sneezed

30 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8-30-02 RP Fusion30 Dear Mom, How are things back home? I haven’t heard from you in a week or two and I’m getting worried. I will call you once I get back to the States, which shouldn’t be but a few days. I hope you’ve figured out how to use the computer I bought you. We can keep contact better that way. If you need anything don’t hesitate to ask. Canada is a nice country scenic wise, but I just don’t like hokey. I am anxious to get this show over with and come home. Jack and I are getting along all right. I know you love the fact that we are traveling together but don’t get any high hopes. We are not getting back together. Have you heard from Eric? I haven’t since I fired him during my early NTICW days. Blood may be thicker than water but business is business and if it means firing my own brother, then so be it. I love him Mama and I do miss him. I’m not going to ask if you heard from Scott because I know you haven’t. I messed up a while back and haven’t seen him since. Remember my temper? I’d rather not talk about what happened. I’m better now Mom. Jack says that a temper like mine just don’t go away without treatment. I think he wants me to go off on him just to laugh at me like he always have when I got mad at him. Anyway, Once I get free time, I’m going to look for Scott. I know you don’t agree, but I have to. I love him Mama. If all else, I just want to apologize. Only those I’m closest too have seen this side of me and for my career’s sake, I wish it to stay that way. I miss you much. Love, Teresa It’s mid-day in Montreal. An afternoon thunderstorm passes over bringing a chilled rain. Teresa clicks on ‘send’ and the e-mail to her mother arrives in Mama Tomas’s inbox. The old woman will not read till later in the evening. Though a lot is going through Teresa’s mind, she couldn’t think of much else to type to her mother. She closes the thin black laptop and slides it in a pocket inside her suitcase. She is in yet another hotel room. Like most wrestlers, their nights are spent in hotels. This hotel is basically simple with one king size bed, one dresser and a television sitting on top. A small bathroom is at the corner of the room with a sink dividing it and the small closet big enough for maybe 5 outfits. Most of Teresa’s luggage is on the floor of the closet. The walls are painted a simple beige color with the rest of the room kept the same earth tones. Just as Teresa zips the large suitcase back together, the door of the hotel room opens. In walks Jack with a bag of food from some food joint down the street. His other arm carries a brown paper sack. He sets everything on the small table near the door. A cigarette hangs loosely from the corner of his mouth. I hope you’re hungry. The food here is so expensive. Jack takes a seat in one of the chair beside the small table. There isn’t an ashtray so he uses an empty Coke can from out of the trash. Teresa rolls her eyes watching him before she slides off the bed and takes a seat at the other chair opposite of him. She opens the food bag and pulls out a white box that smells like spaghetti. I hope it tastes as good as it smells. What did you get to drink? Jack reaches in the brown paper sack and pulls out a box of powdered doughnuts and a case of Bud Ice. Teresa drops her forehead into her hands and releases a deep sigh. Jack, I hope you bought some Coke or something. We can’t just drink beer all night. Jack shrugs and reaches for a bottle and pops the top. He takes a swig and takes the second white box with the same order of spaghetti as Teresa’s. He takes off his black Stetson and tosses it on the bed, and then he takes off his white snake skin boots and leaves them under the table. There’s a Coke machine downstairs. Cans are 75 cents. Bottles are a buck ten. Bud’s better. Jake says these few words without hesitation, then sneezes before reaching for his fork.Teresa growls under her breath watching Jack with temper flaring eyes. She forgets what she wrote her mother about her controlling her temper better now. Her fists tighten so much, her knuckles turn white and she feels her ears burn. She doesn’t know why she gets so angry, she just knows that she does. Suddenly both of the white trays bounce off the small table and onto the floor as Teresa’s fists slam against the flimsy table. The bottle of beer rattle slightly and Jack’s open bottle nearly falls over but he catches it just in time. Jack looks up to Teresa with wide eyes. He finally sees this side of her he heard so much about. When they were together, she was never like this. Teresa stands staring Jack right through. Instead of getting angry as well, a slim smirk form across his face, then he laughs. His laughs are short lived when he sees climb onto the bed, and then leaps toward him causing his chair to fall back to the floor. Teresa sits on Jacks chest griping his throat with both her long fingered hands tightly causing Jack to fight for breath. Teresa speaks in a low growl. Bud’s better huh? You like bud? Do you? Then let me give you Bud. As Teresa reaches for a bottle from the table, she keeps her other hand on Jack’s throat. He takes this advantage and grabs her wrist forcing her hand off his throat. Teresa has already seized the unopened bottle and swings it toward Jack’s head but Jack grabs her other wrist. Using his weight advantage, Jack sits up forcing Teresa to the floor on her back pinning her wrists and sitting on her legs. He doesn’t appear mad at the least, keeping the same smart-ass smirk. If I remember correctly, you always enjoyed it best on the bottom. Nice little temper you got, but I still think you’re cute when you’re mad. Jack leans down and kisses Teresa’s forehead before standing to his feet and releasing her. Bad move on his part because as soon as he steps back, Teresa spring back to her feet and swings the full bottle of beer across his head and Jack falls to the floor in a pile of spaghetti that had fallen out of one of the white boxes. Teresa gives his ribs a couple of nudges. When he doesn’t move, she looks at the beer bottle still in her hand and opens it. The bottle has shaken so much that all the beer came spewing out all over Jack’s motionless body. She pours what little is left on him and tosses the bottle in the trash. If you like your Bud so much, I guess you’ll have to wear it. And that is what you call alcohol abuse. Teresa steps over Jack’s body and walks out the door. The afternoon thunderstorm has let up and the sun shines brightly over the city.
