They had thought that it was a prank. Nothing more than a harmless joke. That old, out-dated, but still offensive word scrawled across the locker-room again and again. Red crylon dripping down the blue walls like blood from a murder, and they had yukked it up. What a laff riot! Wait till I tell my girl about this one Georgie! That word scrawled on the wall over and over again. Nigger Nigger There were five of them in all. To Frank ‘Destrim’ Willis, it had seemed like a grand adventure. A sort of safari in the midst of the steamy San Jose spring. They were all from the upper-middle class, drove nice, new cars, made good grades, had ‘clean’ girl friends, and well-liked by most of their social group. He had thought it a grand idea at the time and even a week after and the ruckus from their escapade was just dying down it still felt great. Those spear-chuckers were righteously pissed, and the look of outrage on old man Andrews’s face had been too much. Frank and the boys were almost in tears by the time the principle slammed the door to his office shut. They had made off like bandits, with nothing more to show for it then a lot of laughs and a few red stains in their hands. Nigger Nigger That word scrawled all over the walls. Red paint dripping down, looking like blood, accusingly gaudy. Destrim loved the way it made him feel superior. It felt good, it felt great. Destrim moved across the street, heading over to Georgie’s house, Social Studies book under his arm, a no.2 pencil behind his ear. The Buddha beads around his neck bounced off of his white polo shirt. The legs of his khaki carpenter shorts snapped in the subtle breeze. The soles of his new Adidas shoes scuffed through the grime of the street. He was humming a snatch of a tune he had heard earlier in the day, “Down with the Sickness” by Disturbed. He liked the beat, the drive, and the harsh lyrics. Frank Willis fancied himself as of a kind of hybrid Goth. Not totally immersed in the dark urban sub-culture, but enough so that he dyed his hair black, slaughtering the roots of his naturally blonde hair, painted his nails black, and pierced his eyebrow several times. Yep, he was sort of a New-Age Goth. He kept up on all the latest anarchistic views and subscribed himself to the opinion that there was another dimension out there that human beings could enter if they just tapped into the right elemental forces. In the pale light of the evening the red krylon stains on his fingertips looked like bloodstains, and he liked that. Then a black Cadillac coup Deville cut onto the sidewalk in front of him. Gangster whitewalls, spinner caps, chrome bumpers, a Union Jack on the side with flames licking up the side, heavy bass tones emitting from the trunk. How had he missed that coming? He didn’t know; contemplating the validity of this reality could really take up a guy’s time. Then, two men jumped out of the car, slamming doors. “We’re going to fuck you up something good.” That would be the one to the left. Big, mean looking dressed rather shabbily; looking for the entire world like a man from “The wrong side of the tracks”. Tattoos were traced across his meaty arms, and Destrim recognized them from blurred pictures in the high-school newspaper. He played defensive end, whoever he was, and had made the All-state lineup. The other guy he didn’t know for shit but knew that he didn’t look all that intimidating. He was skinny and dressed similarly to his football buddy, save for that fact that he had a blue bandanna jutting from the right pocket of his baggy jeans. “You write that shit on the walls?” The skinny guy, baldhead gleaming. The set of his face was serious and non-chalant at the same time. His features were sharp and handsome, not dulled by fat like his counter-part. “What are you talking about? Who are you?” Destrim played it dumb. He didn’t want any trouble. It was all a joke. Why couldn’t some people see the humor in it? “Lemme see your hands, you fucking cunt.” The big ape. Destrim shot him a glare and made a note to remember his face. When he and the rest of the gang got together this evening, this fellow would definitely be brought up. Destrim turned towards the defensive end. “What do you want to see my hands for?” His voice had an edge that Frank rather liked. Very sharp, cutting, indignant. His focus was going to be running this guy right into the ground and then getting the hell out of here. He didn’t even think about the skinny guy until his nose was broken. Destrim caught two huge, looping haymakers. The first punch buried itself in the socket of his left eye, leaving it swollen and bruised for nearly four days. The second one connected solidly with his nose, crimping painlessly and sounding like Rice Crispies when you poured milk on them in the mourning. He reeled; the world suddenly tossed off its axis in a strident second. Destrim was dully aware that there was warm, sticky fluid splashing down into his mouth and down his shirt. He saw a blurred movement out of the corner of one eye, and then his world exploded into a galaxy of agony. The defensive end had doubled him over with a kick that would have sent Destrim’s testicles through the uprights had the situation been right. He collapsed to the floor in a heap, pain roaring through his crotch. He thought for a second that he would simultaneously throw up, explode, and scream all at the same time. “I see you around again, you die fucker.” Mr. Defensive end again. Destrim wanted to ask him why he hadn’t been a place-kicker instead but that thought was squashed when a heavy booted foot crunched his right hand. Willis felt the bones go in two fingers and then a horribly wet snap was heard. The world blurred, grayed, and spun crazily. That skinny guy. What a prick!
