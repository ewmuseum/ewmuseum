How a boy becomes a man living on the streets of NYC

8 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10-8-02 I was born and raised in New York City, the toughest city in the entire world. I was faced with many difficulties throughout my young childhood. My parents were both working parents; my father owned a meat market in the east side of town. My Mother ran a beauty parlor right next door to his shop. As they worked I would be by myself in my home where crimes took place very often and gangs were just around the corner. Growing in that environment you had to learn how to be tough on the inside and on the outside. When I was eight years old and walking home one day from school in central park, a gang came up to me looking for money. This gang of probably teenagers in there seventeen’s had a mix of minorities. There were some white guys, a couple African Americans and some Latin Americans. I just walked by pretending I didn’t here them. Little did I know that ignoring them would be a huge mistake. The minute I walked by them they jumped me from behind. Two of the men held me down while the others went through my backpack and took out anything with money value inside of it. They opened up my wallet and took out the money I would use for lunch in the school cafeteria for the week and stole it. I cried and cried waiting for someone to hear me. But the men covered my mouth not allowing me to scream for help. They ran off once they saw a jogger running my yelling at them to get off of me. They ran away with the money but I was happy that I was still alive. Being attacked was sometimes a daily occurrence growing up in New York City in the eighty’s. Drug hustlers were looking for money anywhere they could get it so they could buy more drugs so they would hunt the unexpected. You always had to watch behind your back because you would never know when someone would come out and an attack. It would usually be a gang of eight to ten men and they would usually hunt down the weak. I couldn’t take it anymore getting beat up and losing my money so some low lives could buy some more drugs. I need to find away to outwit these people and needed an idea as soon as possible. New York City can be compared to Charles Darwin’s theory of “Survival of the fittest” where the hungry lions a.k.a the gangs would prey on the unexpected a.k.a me and the only way I could stop this from happening is becoming mentally and physically stronger then these men. Now at the time I was thinking this I was twelve years old and most gang members in my area were in there early twenty’s so there wasn’t a chance of me being able to fight off ten guys at the age of twelve but I never really had no fear, then again the only way you can survive in New York City is not having any fear of anything or anyone. So I began my path to become more physically stronger so that I could hold my own ground and teach that anyone that would want to jump me a lesson. So I went into my wallet found twenty dollars and headed down to the local gold’s gym down the street from me. I paid for a six-month membership and I began my path on becoming stronger physically. Everyday after school I would workout for about a good three hours and I weekends I would spend six hours. I began to see a more physically fit body then I had before. By the end of the six months I was ready for anyone that would want to test me and try to take me down. I was thirteen years old and for my age I was very strong. Many older students in the school feared me because of my physicality so my friends and I were never bothered by some of the older students trying to steal our lunch money because we were younger. I began to think that I was unstoppable, a menacing force that no one could touch. It was my fault that I let that all go to my head because as I was walking home from school one day I was jump from a gang of men in there early twenty’s. I tried fighting them off giving a couple of them bloody lips and some black eyes. Sometimes I wish I never took a swing at them because then they saw me as a threat to them. So while one of them was fighting me one of the other gang members took the gun barrel of his gun and swatted in me in the temple. I feel to the ground and became unconscious. I later woke up in a hospital bed with my mother and father standing at the bed very upset. Thirteen stitches in my temple, a concussion, a bruised pelvis and a good amount of blood lost equaled the end result for someone trying to stick up for themselves and not being taken advantage of by a gang of lowlifes. This woke me up from the cloud nine I was standing on for a while, thinking that I was unstoppable and that could not have been beat by anyone or anything. I began to think that I was some sort of a mythological warrior of some sort that was not inflicted by any kind of pain and was the strongest human being on the planet. I became cocky, dropped my guard and paid for it. Being a thirteen year old and physically fit I began to think that I was the greatest thing since sliced bread. The attack this time was a rude awakening because I knew this time that I was not ready for survival on the streets and once again I bought a membership to the gold’s gym down the street from me, this time it was a one year membership. I trained and trained for a year, trying to get my body in the right shape so I wouldn’t have to put up with the shit that was handed down to me by these gangs. Now at the age of fourteen I began to get the mindset that I would not allow these punks to take me down again. For the age of fourteen I was very tough and didn’t take shit from anybody, which made me an open target for someone that wanted to make a name for themselves. I was a very popular in school, a good athlete and with those credentials I had many friends growing up. Little did I know that I had friends that could be backstabbers as well… Now I’m at the age of sixteen and having no problems what so ever with anyone. So I began to hang out with these kids from the other side of town, there basically known around the school as legit bad asses and not ones to mess with. They were all eighteen year olds and I was the only person that was not in their age group so I found it very rewarding that they chose me to take under there wing. We would spend the weekends hanging out outside a gas station early in the night and the cops would usually come. They would tell us to beat it and get out of there. So we would head down to one of the guys houses they would do drugs, drink alcohol and do crazy shit. I was not into that stuff because my Uncle died of drug overdose when he was very young and I didn’t want to do that to my parents. The guys would pressure me and pressure me into doing it and I would just say “no thanks, maybe later.” They would just shake their head and toke another joint. I would just sit there every weekend watching them get wasted; I would usually leave when they passed out which was a daily occurrence. I began to see that these guys were not the guys I would want to hang around with much longer, little did I know that they saw the same thing in me. It all began on Friday afternoon after school we all went to hang out, and then we went to one of the guy’s places. We all walked in, me being the last one and as I walked in I felt pain in the back of my head. I began to stumble and my vision became blurry, I began to see the guys laughing, saying “hit him again.” I felt that last strike in the back of my head and that was it, they knocked me out unconscious, I woke up tied to a park bench in central park and was being un-tied by a local police officer. That was the last straw I was humiliated and taken advantage of for the very last time. They thought they were hot shit, which they could use me and get away with it? Like I was going to hide into a corner and forget about what they did to me? Well the next time I saw them I let them know what I thought. It was that Monday in school, just a couple days ago they beat the shit out of me and left me tied to a park bench. I saw them at the end of the hallway, in there leather jackets like they were “The Fonz.” I could see them laughing as I made my way up the hallway with fire in my eyes. They were standing in a little huddle when I approached them; I taped one of the guys on the shoulder, Vinny. He was the undisputed leader of their group. Hey guys what’s up? Vinny: What do you want twerp? Another ass kicking! (He turned around to the other guys and they began laughing) C’mon guys, you know me I don’t want another trouble. I thought it was pretty funny actually. Vinny: I’m glad you found it funny, now beat it. Oh c’mon Vincent I’m not going to leave until I give you guys a piece of my mind. Vinny: A piece of your mind? How bouts I give you a piece of my fist! Hey Vincent c’mon lose the wannabe Italian mobster look and be a man. Vinny: What the hell does that supposes to mean? You want to know what I mean? Two simple words…Ass…Kicking. Vinny: Ass kicking? From who, I know it’s not you so is this a joke or something? This ass kicking will be courtesy of me. Vinny: Oh really? You dam right. Once I said that the next thing I knew was that my fist was is Vinny’s face. He fell to the ground covering his face the other guys then came to attack me and I began to swing at anything that was coming in my way. Then all of a sudden a couple of the teachers came out and broke up the fight, my science teacher Mr. Billings came out and slammed me up against a locker, holding me back. I was then walked to the Dean of Students office where I was told my fate and was suspended for a week. I told him my story and he basically understood why I did what I did. The gang of “legit bad asses” were suspended as well and the Dean of Students made shore that the police knew about what they did to me. That fight that morning would be the last time I would see those guys. They later had to go to court for assault and battery charges with additional charges that were pending on a robbery they did week before they beat me up, they also found drugs and alcohol in there rooms. They were sentenced to five to ten years in prison. I felt that I had received my revenge to all that has happened to me. But I never stopped getting physically stronger because I knew that I need to continue to be on the ball at all times when walking in the area that I lived or I would pay dearly. I grew up in a tough area and had a lot of tough times, but it taught me how to be stronger mentally and physically. That is something you can’t be taught in a textbook, you just pick it up, it’s like an instinct. An instinct that I will always have for the rest of my life and for that I am grateful.
