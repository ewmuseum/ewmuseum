The Bellzier Chronicles: Finale

3 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 3, 2002 OOC Note: This is my last rp for the PPV. No matter what happens, you guys were great sports and I look forward to working with you guys in the future. Undisclosed WhereaboutsApprox 7:50 am I felt a rush of excitement rush to my head as we drove towards the meeting place where I would be rejoined with Nat. Maybe it was just the thought that she was okay which was driving me to such a deliriously happy mood, but whatever it was, it didn’t allow me to sit still. The driver, the man I met in the Denny’s restaurant, obviously noticed. He looked over at me jittering and shook his head. “You’re acting like you haven’t seen her in years.” “It’s felt like it’s been that long,” I replied with an anxious grin. “Remember that story you told me before,” He said. “The one where you met that girl again in that supermarket?” “Yeah.” “It reminded me of something I experienced when I was a little younger,” He replied with a slight smile. “I used to be a normal individual too, you know? I wasn’t always the spy that you now know and despise.” “You’re not that old,” I added. “Not much older than I am, I would guess.” He laughed, tossing his head back. “The fact that you’re twenty-six years old and you have graying hair doesn’t mean that I’m still a young man. Many years ago, I was just a fresh intern out of USC, ready to make my fortune and ready to tackle a family. I had the girl all picked out and everything.” He was beginning to reach out to me, yet I wasn’t reeling him in. It probably had to do with the fact that I really didn’t know him yet. Hell, I didn’t even know his name. How are you supposed to have a serious conversation with someone who won’t even tell you his real name? “You know,” I began to say. “I still don’t even know your name.” He perked an eyebrow up, but kept his eyes on the road. He was still disciplined. “Who’s interrogating who now?” “I’m not interrogating,” I replied, crossing my arms. “I just thought it would be nice to know who I’m dealing with here. Name’s are usually the first thing you find out about a person.” “You’re not dealing with the Syndicate here, Tom. Besides, you don’t need to know my name.” He paused and looked over at me, only to find a disgruntled look. He sighed. “Cross. My name’s Cross.” I smirked. “And what the hell kind of name is Cross?” “I don’t know,” He replied. “What the hell kind of name is Lexian?” “Touche.” The car drove up into the parking lot of warehouse whose lights were on. The morning sun was just starting to break out of the clouds and a few rays could already be seen. They reflected off of the warehouses windows, blinding me momentarily. By the time I could see again, we were already parked and Cross was unbuckling his seatbelt. “C’mon,” He said, opening his car door. “They’re waiting for us.” We made our way into the warehouse, and immediately I was greeted by the warm smile of Nat. She was sitting in a metal chair and leapt out of it once she saw me. “Tom!” She shouted, as she threw her arms around my neck. “Are you alright?” “Isn’t that my line?” I joked. I looked over her shoulder and saw my friend, Sean O’Reilly, walking towards us. In his hands was my cactus, Bobby the Cactus, who was my pseudo manager. He could speak, which is something most cacti could do.” “Tom, good to see you,” Sean said, patting me on the back. “We heard a lot about your little car chase this morning.” “You did?” “It’s been all over the news,” Nat explained. “Apparently they’ve been searching for you and the other cars all morning. We were afraid you would be caught.” “No chance,” Bobby added. “Tom knows how to drive that Civic like he was Dale Earnhardt.” “Shut up, Bobby,” I remarked. “Where the hell were you when I needed you?” Bobby just gave a pssh. “I think the almighty Tom continues to forget that I’m only a plant. Do you see any legs? I can’t just get up and start driving around the city for you.” “Well, hopefully everything’s back to normal now,” I said, looking behind me back to Cross. He was standing with a few other men dressed in black suits. They all looked serious and seemed to be discussing something fairly important. Cross looked back at me and nodded. “Who’s that?” Sean asked me, pointing over to Cross. “The guy that helped me during this entire ordeal,” I replied, running my hand through my hair. “You wouldn’t believe where I met this guy. I was running through Denny’s after getting soaked in the rain, and I was sitting in the stall, right?” “This isn’t going to be gross is it?” Nat asked with a sour look on her face. Say what you will about her, but she’s still a lady. Guys don’t really care too much. We tell war stories about our endeavors in the can all the time. Don’t you? “No, of course not,” I said. “Now, I was sitting down taking a dump when…” “Tom!” Nat complained. “Fine, I was in the crapper right?” “Hmph!” I rolled my eyes and shrugged. “Okay, okay. I was in the bathroom doing my ‘business,’ when the guy in the next stall called over to me and started trying to have a conversation with me. It was pretty uncomfortable, actually. You ever have something like that happen to you?” “Not that I can remember,” Sean admitted. “Although sometimes the crapper is the best place to discuss things. I know I do most of my thinking there.” Well, doesn’t that explain a lot? “I don’t know,” Bobby said. “Going to the bathroom for me is letting off oxygen. As you know, I eat using photosynthesis.” “Oh shut the hell up, Bobby,” I said in an annoyed tone. “No one wants to know about how you eat.” Bobby relinquished a spine which flew past my head. I rushed towards Bobby angrily, but Nat held me back. Sean placed Bobby behind his back so he didn’t cheap shot me with one of his damn quills again. “Let me at him!” I shouted, still trying to rush past Nat. She was awfully strong for a news reporter. “I guess the big, bad science major doesn’t want to be reminded of how he failed as a student,” Bobby retorted. “I didn’t fail,” I shouted back. “Besides, why in god’s name does a cactus need imported mineral water to feed anyways? Water is water. It’s not like it tastes any different to you.” It might have been a low blow, but I didn’t care. Bobby was calling attention to the fact that I used to be a science major, which was still a passion to me. Hell, if I wasn’t wrestling, most likely I’d be in a lab somewhere mixing up reagents and spending the better part of my day messing with test tubes. “Shows how much you know!” Bobby replied. “I’ll have you know that it makes a big difference. Tap water gives me gas.” “Oh give me a break…” As I turned around, I watched Cross and his entourage walk towards us with a semi-amused look on his face. I was a bit embarrassed, but then it faded away. I had nothing to be ashamed of, except for the fact that I was talking to a plant. “I hope things are going well?” Cross inquired, nodding at everyone. “Just great,” I mumbled. “So, what’s the deal? Is everything fine now?” “It depends on what you mean by ‘fine,’” Cross said. “You see, we got Nat back, but that was more of a mistake on their part than our ingenuity. They’re still looking for you, Tom, and they won’t stop until they have you.” “They’re not getting me,” I said, looking intently back at Cross. “You can bet your life on that.” Cross sighed and took off his dark shades which he had been wearing the entire time. “You’re tough, Tom. But you can’t fight the entire syndicate by yourself. And no matter how smart you think you are, they’ll always be a step ahead of you. I guarantee it.” “Can you guarantee my safety?” I asked him, already knowing the answer. “What?” “You heard what I said,” I replied quickly, not wanting to go through the run around again. “Now answer the question.” “Of course I can’t guarantee your safety,” Cross replied. “We were lucky to even get to you before the syndicate did.” I just laughed. “I’ll take care of myself, Cross. Don’t worry about a thing. I’m a big boy now.” We all began to leave as Cross and his cronies eyed us towards the door. “You’re in way over your head this time, Lexian,” Cross warned. I looked back at him and shrugged. “Aren’t I always?”
