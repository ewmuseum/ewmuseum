The Bellzier Chronicles: Another Beginning [Part Two]

24 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 24, 2002 Denny’sSan Francisco, CA3:!5 am You never want to rush into any situation, but I decided to make an exception. I could feel it in the air tonight that something was amiss, and when my intuition comes calling, there isn’t much anyone can do to stop me. Why didn’t she pick up the phone?” I thought to myself. Could something be wrong? Maybe she was in an accident? God, I have to get back… I was still buttoning my jacket as I rushed through the Denny’s restaurant. One of the waitresses shouted back at me that my coffee was ready, but heeded no attention in her direction. A few of the patrons that were actually having a meal at three o’clock in the evening watched me run off in a dizzying hurry, perplexed at my anxious exit. Some parts of me wanted me to stop and reassure them that everything was okay, especially since anxiety only spreads amongst people. But there was no time. The door to the restaurant slammed open as I burst from it, and I quickly drew my keys from my pocket. I clicked the alarm off of the 1998 Honda Civic and the doors automatically unlocked. As I looked up for a moment, I could see what appeared to be a navy blue Honda Civic parked on the opposite street. A man was sitting in the passenger seat on the phone, while the driver was standing near the drivers door with his arms crossed and cigarette sticking from his mouth. I hesitated for a moment, but opened the door anyways and got into my car. The warm air that the car had insulated rushed into my face and I drew my keys into the ignition. Just as I was about to turn the key, I heard my passenger door open. I looked over with a stunned look on my face and a man with a gray overcoat was sitting next to me. “What the hell do you think you’re doing?” I asked angrily. I wanted to reach over and land a quick right cross on the intruder, but I didn’t want to risk making a scene, especially when I knew that two other strangers were watching me from a distance. “Whatever you do, don’t turn that key.” I recognized the voice immediately. “You’re that weirdo from the bathroom stall. What do you think you’re doing?” “Saving your life.” I shook my head and clenched my fists. Some people never learn, but eventually they get a shot in the arm that convinces them otherwise. I was about to give a very hard lesson in what it means to do as your told, but as I reached over, he drew a gun from the inside of his jacket and pointed it directly at my chest. I hesitated and drew back. “That’s good. At least you understand this.” I stared back at the gun whose barrel hand my life in its hands. I had never been so closely confronted by death, and if I were to say that I was not scared would have been a lie. A flat out lie. I had often believed that one’s own life was but a reflection of their future, and if that were true, what future did I have? “What do you want from me?” I asked with a hint of fear in my voice. I tried not to sound afraid, but when death stared you directly in the face, it was hard to say anything at all. “Just listen.” The man said. As my eyes adjusted to the light, I could see his figure a little more clearly. I could not readily tell his height, but I would have guessed that he was five foot eight. He had short black hair which was slicked back with what appeared to be far too much gel, and he had a light moustache. He looked as if he was Russian, although I’m not that great at telling ethnic backgrounds on the fly. He smelled of alcohol, which didn’t surprise me at all. “Fine,” I said, giving in. “You are in a dangerous situation, Tom Lexian,” He said. “Not only do you not have any idea what you are involved in, but you are running directly into their trap that they set up for you.” “Who? Who is trying to set me up” I demanded. “You have been selected to be added to a group known as the Bellzier Syndicate. They operate out of Paris, but they have contacts all around the world.” “Why would they want me? I’m a nobody.” It hurt to say, but sometimes the truth hurts. I was an average citizen, nothing incredibly special. If this so-called Bellzier Syndicate wanted a new top spy, why couldn’t they recruit a spy already working for the CIA or another top ranking organization. Why come to me when all I want to do if make an honest living and raise a family? Not exactly spy material. “That may be so, but they don’t exactly pick up your average joe off the street. They’ve established your physical capabilities, your mental prowess, and have decided that they want you.” “So what do I do now?” I asked, still not seeing the validity of his statements. “Do I just step out of this car right now and wave around my arms, screaming at the top of my lungs that I don’t want to be a spy?” He just shook his head. “Crack as many jokes as you want. Your wit won’t be as sharp as the knife they stab you in the back with when they know that you don’t wish to join them.” Tactfulness was obviously not his forte, and the mental image of myself being stabbed in the back was not very pleasant. I began to realize that this might be for real and that it was in my best interest to do as he said. “What exactly do I do then?” “You do as I say, and we hope that you survive long enough to allow me to get you out of this mess.” “And who are you?” I asked, perking an eyebrow up. It was a question I wanted to ask him for awhile now, but it didn’t pass my mind as an extremely important question when there was a gun leveled at my head. “A friend,” He stated flatly. “Don’t give me that…” He raised the gun a little higher so that it was at my face, and I abruptly stopped speaking in mid-sentence. I could see into his eyes now, which burned with a ferocity I have only seen in the battles of the squared circle. He had secrets to tell me, and I had a feeling that he would not immediately share them. “You know only what you need to know. Do you understand that? I will decide when it is time for you to have more information. In the meantime, I suggest you follow directions and allow me to do what I must.” I scoffed. “You’ll forgive me if my eternal gratefulness doesn’t immediately shine through. I have a hard time trusting anyone that gets their point across using a deadly weapon.” “You and I both know that this is the only way you would have responded positively,” He replied. “I did a preliminary analysis of you in that bathroom stall conversation. You weren’t exactly Mr. Shining Personality yourself.” I had to chuckle. It was damn near 3 a.m. in the morning and he wanted me to be able to hold a conversation in a public restroom. If expectations weren’t high enough, he also wanted me to just go along with his plan which he undoubtedly would unveil to me soon enough. The only real problem was, I knew what was coming – I just didn’t know what to do about it. “Now, look in the rear view mirror and tell me what you see,” He said. My eyes quickly darted to the rear view mirror, which showed the Mazda car which I saw before. Both men were sitting in the exact same places, staring directly at my car. I was beginning to get a bad feeling in the pit of my stomach. “What do they want?” I asked. “Let me guess…they’re part of the Bellzier Syndicate.” “Most likely. Did you know that they’ve been tailing you since you left your house this morning?” I didn’t. You don’t usually notice the cars moving by you, especially when you think that you’re just an average individual minding their own business. Unfortunately, my life was never this simple before. “No, I didn’t. I suppose you have been tailing me as well.” “I haven’t made my presence known to them quite yet, but I’m sure they’re wondering who I am now.” “So do you know these guys or what?” “The man in the passenger side of the car is Michel Grulen, the local contact for this part of the United States. That’s his brother standing outside of the car, Pierre. They’re kind of like the brain and muscle type of tandem.” “Pierre and Michel…” I began to say. “Something tells me I’ll be seeing much more of them in the next few days. “Not if we can help it.” “So what do we do now?” I asked, feeling out of the loop in the entire situation. “We’re going to get out of here.” He looked into the rear view mirror briefly and then licked his lips. “When I tell you to, I want you to turn the key slowly and start the ignition. The pull out as fast as you can, heading south on this street. Get to the freeway and we’ll lose them on the bridge.” “We’re in a Civic and they’re in a Mazda. It’s going to be a bit difficult to flat out lose them on an open road.” “It’s late at night. We’ll be able to pull some maneuvers.” “I hope you’re right,” I whispered to myself. I turned the key and the car began to start up. I switched the gear over to reverse, and hit the gas as hard as I could. As the car began to pull out from the parking lot, I glanced into the rear view mirror to see the two men piling into their Mazda, rushing to start their car too. “Get us out of here now!” I switched back to drive and bolted onto the main street. I overran the sidewalk, not even bothering to use the off ramp and spilled out onto the street. The mazda was quickly on my tail. My speedometer read eight miles per hour and rising, but it wouldn’t be fast enough. The mazda quickly made up the distance and was only a few feet away from my bumper. “Can’t this thing go any faster?” “It wasn’t made for racing,” I replied, trying to keep my cool. My eyes continued to dart from the rear view mirror and back to the road, which was surprisingly busy with cars. “Why the hell are there so many cars on the road right now?” I muttered to myself. I was on a two laned street that headed directly to the Bay bridge which would lead back to the Bay Area. I swerved from the left lane into the right, cutting off a white van that was going about as half as fast I was. As I passed the van, I looked over at the drivers side of the car and a man looked back at me. I had another bad feeling in the pit of my stomach. Suddenly, the side doors to the van burst open, and two figures dressed in black emerged. “Get down! Get down!” He grabbed me and ducked me head down as the black figures fired shots into the car, smashing the side windows. Glass littered the floor of the car now. “Get up and get us out of here!” I pressed down on the gas pedal as hard as I could and the car took off like a rocket. I could still hear the sound of a gun firing, but we were now pulling away. I stood back up and took control of the wheel, just to see a straightaway towards the bridge. Most of the cars on the road had pulled aside from all of the mayhem and now it was going to be a drag race to the finish line. He looked at me as I approached the bridge and smirked. “You believe in God?” I shook my head. “Start.”
