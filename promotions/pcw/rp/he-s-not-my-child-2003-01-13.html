He's Not My Child

13 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: January 13th, 2003 The clock chimes at midnight as I stir in my bed of sheets on the floor of Chris’ Storms living room. I sit up tossing the sheets to the side. Wrapped up in my pink silk pajamas I stand up and walk over to the couch not to far from me and sit down beside the sleeping child. Looking at him I can see the face of his mother. I have never met her but yet I can tell that the child has a remarkable resemblance of her. They both were innocent and they both did not deserve to be stricken down the way they were. His mother by a gun, and for him emotional wounds that would last a lifetime. I always thought at times that the childhood I had was a harsh one that I would never want anyone to have to relive for me, yet looking at this child I suddenly feel that the neglect I suffered as a child was nothing more than a skinned knee compared to the wound that had cut deep into the heart of this child. At least both of my parents were there. At least I was not torn between the two and I least I knew them. Little Evan would never see his mom again. The closest thing he had to a mother now was me. And his father, well he was in jail. Surely little Evan would want nothing to do with his father once he grew up. The closest thing he would have to a father would be Chris and yet he had not even met him yet and I still had yet to find out how Chris would react to the newest addition to the family that seemed to be falling apart. Looking over at Evan I noticed that the sheets covering him were falling off. I reached over to the sheets to tuck him back in but before I could I heard the front door open. Looking up a smile crossed my face as I saw Chris walk into the scene but my smile faded as I could tell he was not ready to share the same affection with me. Instead a frown crossed his face as he eyed the couch quickly and then looked back at me. “Bringing home another guy when I’m not around are you? I thought I could trust you Tessa!” He shouted in a disappointed tone. “Chris it’s not…” “Don’t even try to give me it’s not what I think. That’s what everyone always says.” He cut in as his face was mixed with emotions of both sorrow and anger and tears began to form in his eyes. Whether they were from fury or sadness I did not know. All I knew is that he was jumping to conclusions much too quickly. “But it’s not…” “I told you Tessa, don’t give me that!” He cut in once more, walking over in the direction of the sheets. “Chris…” I tried cutting in once more. “Jackson you can come out of there now!” He shouted at the sheets. “I know you are here to get more secrets about me before our match Tuesday, but it’s not going to happen if Tessa has not told you everything about me yet.” “Chris just listen to me for a second!” I shouted, trying to grasp onto his attention. “What is there to listen to?” He responded while keeping his focus on the sheets hiding the child on the couch. “After all you don’t see me taking Teresa Tomas home with me telling her all the secrets about you before your match.” “Chris, please!” I pleaded, just wanting him to listen for a second. “Tessa enough!” He shouted, reaching out to the sheets and grasping onto them tightly and ripping them violently from the couch, awaking the sleeping Evan. “Mommy!” Evan shouted awaking from his dream, grasping onto my arm tightly surely not awake enough to realize that he was not back at home and that I was not his real mother. “Mommy?” Chris questioned aloud. “You mean you had a child all along and you never told me! And let me guess, its Jackson’s child!” “No it’s not!” I fought back, becoming sick of being hollered at for no reason other than helping a child in need. “Then whose child is he?” Chris questioned, slightly narrowing his blue eyes at the child and then directed his eyes towards me, wanting an answer. “He’s a child whose friend I saved down at Central Park.” I said while rubbing the child’s back in my best attempts to comfort him. “Why is he here then? Shouldn’t he be back at his house? Do I look like I have enough room in here for one more person?” Chris snapped. “Chris he can’t go back home! His mother is dead and his fathers in jail.” I said, looking over at Chris with my blue eyes starting to sting with tears of sorrow for the kid who would now have to deal with Chris and me arguing on top of everything. “Tessa we can’t even handle our own problems, how do you expect us to handle another kid on top of this all?” He started to clam down slightly, obviously giving in to the innocence of the child, or maybe it was me. Getting sick of arguing with Chris in front of Evan, I sat up from the couch and stormed into the hallway next to the living room. Peaking back into the room I motioned for Chris to do the same. Walking away from the half asleep Evan Chris walked out of the room and stood across from me as I got back to addressing my point. “Chris listen, do you really want this kid to suffer through the same crap you did as a child?” I questioned his decision of not wanting the child around. “Tessa, that’s not what….forget it.” He said, dropping his voice with each word before becoming inaudible as he lowered his head before walking off to his bedroom, slamming the door shut behind him creating a loud bang that left a hole in my heart. He was hurt and it was because of me. I’d messed up again. Walking back over to my bed of sheets on the floor, I dug my face back in the sheets and began to cry while wondering why on earth I had to be such a failure at times, and if I would have the same unfortunate luck while going against Teresa Tomas this Tuesday.
