Sugar Coated Suicide

21 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10.21.02 The Navy Pier, downtown Chicago… -The sights and sounds of the Windy City never cease to vanish downtown, regardless the time of day. Even at well passed midnight, eager sigh-seers and tourists bustle around the dockside lots around the Pier, a brisk draft sweeping in from Lake Michigan forcing them to hold their jackets close. Thin clouds darken the tint of a half moon, but the dim light still manages to skate across the rough surface of the water, glittering like a billion white firework sparklers in its deep abyss. A few blinking red lights mark the location of sailing boats in the distance; the signal lights fade in and out with the motion of the lake, like tiny red eyes glaring from the fathoms of another world. All in all, it’s a beautiful October night in Chicago.- -Hefting his massive key chain to the sliding door lock, a well-built security officer clicks the dead bolts into place, closing off the front entrance to the Navy Pier. The thought of a savory Krispy Kreme doughnut puts a quick edge in his step, eager to close the place down in time for a shared dozen of the glistening morsels of sugary goodness with his partner. With the sweet taste making his mouth water, the guard reaches to twist the final deadbolt into place…- Renegade: Hey Jacob. Mind if I catch a little private jog before you close shop? -Standing with his thick arms tucked into the front pocket of a red hooded sweatshirt, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins meets Officer Jacob’s startled stare kindly.- Officer Jacob: Christ Rollins, you think you could announce yourself a little better? You’d give me an ulcer or two if I didn’t already have ‘em. -Chuckling, The Renegade nods.- Renegade: Too much Krispy Kreme will do that to you, pal. -The sudden bewildered look on Jacob’s face forces another fit of laughter from Rollins.- Officer Jacob: Was it that obvious? Renegade: Yup. I could see the hunger in your eyes. (looking beyond Jacob, into the main courtyard) Mind if I run a few laps? I’ve been away from the ring so long, I’d almost forgotten what “working out” meant. Officer Jacob: Sure, be my guest. Harry’s just making his final rounds, clearing out the couples and last minute types. You’ve got about thirty minutes. -Rich Rollins waves a grateful thanks as he jogs past Jacob and into the unlit courtyard, his shoulder-length brown hair bouncing in rhythm with his stride. The sounds of the waves crashing against the boom barrier waft in from over the inner courtyard wall, creating a peaceful atmosphere for The Renegade’s determined mindset. Moving at a steady pace, he quietly passes the first lane of shops and souvenir stands to his left, ignoring the bright signs and flashy advertising that is only half visible in the gloomy moonlight.- -Breathing steadily through his nose, Rollins watches the distant horizon of Lake Michigan, its thin separation between water and sky hard to distinguish at night. Fewer blinking red lights spatter the landscape than before, leaving The Renegade alone with his thoughts and the gentle chilling wind that bites at him through his red sweatshirt. His footfalls are soft in the open space, their usual heavy trod sucked away into the sky above with hardly a whisper. Light and steady, Rollins trots along peacefully…- -…until the faint outline of a single man, hunched over the edge of the dock, breaks Rollins’ concentration. Ignoring The Renegade’s presence, the shadowed figure sits on his knees facing the ocean-like lake, the dull glint of a knife sparking from one hand. As Rollins comes to a dead stop not ten paces away, the man silently draws the sharp blade toward his exposed wrist. A mixture of emotions shoots down The Renegade’s spine, but none so strong as alarm. Taking a deep breath, he shouts out.- Renegade: Stop! (the man freezes) Please… whatever it is, it can’t be worth this. Put the knife down. -Slowly turning a tear-streaked face toward Rollins, the man leans into the moonlight. Illuminated under that pale white glow, the face that stares back at The Renegade is hollow beyond words, completely devoid of any human emotion save despair. Even though the figure is careful not to turn fully around, Rich Rollins can still see the uniform adorning his emaciated body.- -Sitting hunched over with a knife in one hand, tears blurring his face, is Security Officer Harry Trenton.- -Recognition instantly blooms in Officer Trenton’s eyes as he spots Rollins, his hand trembling with the knife edge near the veins. Licking his lips, Trenton speaks.- Officer Trenton: D…don’t help me, Rollins. I don’t want help… not now… Renegade: Harry, I don’t want to have to force the blade from you, but I won’t watch while you kill yourself. You have a wife and kids, for the love of Christ! Don’t put them through… Officer Trenton: (forcibly) Don’t tell me how to feel! -A silent sob sticks in Harry’s throat as he cranes his neck around to see The Renegade more clearly. Slowly raising his hands into a gesture of peace, Rollins’ mind works in hyperdrive as he watches this man, a friend he had drank with at bars all around the city, blankly look back at him. Harry swallows hard, cutting Rollins off before he can speak.- Officer Trenton: Shelly… she left me, man. Packed the kids up and split, said I was “too unstable” for a “healthy relationship.” (he wipes his face with one sleeve) And you know what takes the cake? She didn’t even leave her mother’s fucking phone number… just 1-800-CHARTER. Renegade: (soothing) Maybe she’s trying to tell you something, Harry. Look, I’m here to listen, if that’s what you want. Jacob out there…(he motions toward gate)… he wants us to grab some doughnuts when you guys finish closing, all right? How does that sound? Officer Trenton: Don’t patronize me, Rich. I don’t need your pity. Don’t need your pity… the words ring hard in Rollins' mind, as it was the last phrase his brother spoke to him days earlier. The last words, he realizes, his brother would ever say to him before being sentenced to death for murder. -Shaking the thought from his mind, The Renegade inches ever so closer to his unstable friend, carefully offering his palms in a gesture of neutrality. Speaking softly, he desperately pleads with Officer Trenton’s sense of reason.- Renegade: Harry, I grew up without a father. He did his duty in ‘Nam, and took a bullet for his country. I wouldn’t wish that on anybody, especially such a sweet family as yours. I mean, just look at my brother Jason. He’s on trial for murder because he hates me; dad wasn’t around to take care of a family that needed fathering. Instead of a caring parent, we had a welfare check and food stamps to make a living off of. The government didn’t do shit, ‘cause nobody owes anybody else when you live like we did. Now please, for my sake, give me the knife… I’ve got a box of Krispy Kremes with your name on ‘em. -Blinking in surprise, Harry Trenton looks upon Rollins’ outstretched palm, his knife-welding hand twitching with indecision. The words visibly sink into his already sunken eyes, sparking a life within those dark orbs that hadn’t been there before. Slowly working his jaw as if using it for the first time, Officer Trenton responds.- Officer Trenton: Ju… just give me a few minutes, okay? I need… I need to collect myself. Just give me a few minutes. -Carefully studying his friend’s face, The Renegade gently lowers his hand back to one side. Officer Trenton, who had moments ago been breathing very heavily, now looks into Rollins’ eyes with an acute sense of composure, wiping a bead of sweat dripping down his brow. Letting out a weak laugh, Trenton continues.- Officer Trenton: You’re a smart man, Rich Rollins, very smart man indeed. Let me just wash my face a little, I’ll meet you back with Jacob… Renegade: I don’t trust you alone with that weapon, Harry. Give me the knife, and I’ll go wait for you by the front gate. -The security guard’s eyes dart from Rollins’ face to the glinting surface of the knife in his hand, one corner of his mouth turning upward in a failed attempt at a reassuring smile. Placing the knife on the cold concrete, Officer Trenton swiftly brushes it off the end of the Pier, watching it splashing into the dark folds of the waves nearly ten feet below. Satisfied, The Renegade stands erect, eyeing the officer warily.- Renegade: I’ll give you a few minutes, Harry. Jacob and I both will be waiting. -Absently nodding his reply, Harry Trenton continues to watch the ripples his intended suicide weapon created on the surface of the water, his voice quiet and distant.- Officer Trenton: Yeah, Rollins. Just a few minutes… Two hours later… “Pull that line in, I think I got a bite!” Alexander Renolds and his best friend, Earnest Gormond, steady themselves aboard the small gray fishing boat, each man grabbing a handful of the thick rope that dips down into the dark depths of Lake Michigan. The first rays of the early morning sun begin to skate across the calm waters, sparkling like yellow diamonds falling from the cloudless sky. Heaving with all the energy left in his overweight body, Alex pulls the last few feet of the fishing net into the thin boat, moving aside an empty beer can to make way for the moss-covered bulge that rises from the lake. “What the hell… this sure isn’t catch-of-the-day material,” Earnest observes, running one hand over the dark coils of netting, tangled and slick with seaweed. “N’fact, I don’t even think it’s a fish.” “Fish, mineral or vegetable, I don’t give a good Goddamn one way or the other,” Alex replies, winding the length of the rope around the crook of his elbow and wrist, “Unless it’s anything like the boot you caught last week, we ain’t…” “The boot, and a whole leg to go with it!” Earnest exclaims, jerking his hand back from the mass of seaweed and netting. Beneath his roving touch, the lake sewage had fallen away to reveal a dulled black shoe, the toe sticking through a space in the rope. Setting the folded fishing line down in a pile, Alex glances at the soaking bundle in wonder, too small for a human body. At least, for a whole human body, that is… “Aw Christ, another floater,” cries Alex, wiping his arm across his forehead. “I don’t wanna have to touch a dead man, Earn.” “You won’t have too,” his companion replies, motioning toward the bundle, “This unlucky fella ain’t exactly got a leg to stand on, if you take my meanin’.” Earnest laughs at his own joke while Alex shoots him a disgusted glare, turning around to face the small hand motor in the rear of the boat. “One way or the other, it’s time to call it quits.” “We’ve only been out here thirty minutes, Alex!” “And it’s a thirty minutes I’d just as soon forget,” Alex retorts, grabbing the throttle. Pulling back with a swift jerk, he steadies for the engine to rev… and nothing happens. Pulling back again, Alex curses at life in general as the motor stalls, refusing to turn over. “What’s the matter?” “Damn thing’s caught onto somethin’,” Alex says, his voice laden with frustration. Turning to Earnest, he flashes a sarcastic smile, motioning toward the rear of the boat. “I’m sure as hell not stickin’ my hand in that water ‘n findin’ the remains of our friend there.” “You want me to do it?” Earnest asks, his tone thick with amusement. “By all means, be my guest,” Alex replies, scooting aside to make room for his partner. Straddling one of the benches, Alex props one arm on top of a styrofoam cooler, watching warily as Earnest bends over the engine with his sleeve rolled up to the elbow. Slowly, Earnest dips his hand into the chilly water, reaching down into the propellers of the engine. Gripping the bulk of the motor for support, he leans farther over the edge, tilting the boat slightly with the shift in weight. “Be careful, Earn.” “Shaddup, Alex. I’m trying to concentrate without slicin’ my arm off.” Licking his lips with determination, Earnest gently feels the blades of the propeller under the surface, groping around the contours in search of the obtrusive element. Eyeing the ignition lever cautiously, he can’t help but imagine the sensation of getting his arm caught in the propeller, jerking back as it suddenly turned on… “Got it!” Tightening his grip on what feels like rough leather, Earnest unwraps something from behind the propeller, his arm so far under the boat that his cheek almost rests on the surface of the water. Gritting his teeth with strain, he holds the engine for leverage with his opposite arm as he yanks on the leather strip, pulling forth the mysterious hindrance from its dark depths. “Dear sweet Jesus,” Alex exclaims, instinctively putting his hand to his nose. Floating just behind the engine, a leather belt strung snug around his throat, is the corpse of Officer Harry Trenton. ~Fin~
