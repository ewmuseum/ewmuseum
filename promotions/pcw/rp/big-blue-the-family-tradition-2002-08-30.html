Big Blue, the Family Tradition

30 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8-30-02 Football in my house was like the un-official family function. My family is huge New York Giants fans and it would bring family members from all over the area to out house to watch the annual Giants game every Sunday and sometimes-even Monday. My grandfather was the first Giants fan and would watch the Giants every Sunday on his couch with a beer in one hand and a bag of potato chips in the other. He was the first Giant fan Then my father who is the oldest in his family was the first to be christened in the New York Giant lifestyle watching Frank Gifford run the ball down the field and scoring the touchdown at the old Polo Grounds. Then my Uncle Frank was the second child to be christened as a Giants fan, followed by my Aunt Linda and last but not least my grandmother as well became a giants fan when she married my grandfather. The location would be my father’s house and we recently bought a good sized television so it was a big thing for the family, they would all come over bringing their children so I could play with my cousins during the pre-game show and then during the game we would sit in front of the television watching big blue do what they do best which is bringing New York a championship. Then after the game I would be a kid again playing outside with my cousins. My father would make pepper and eggs every Sunday, while my Mother would do the hamburgers and hotdogs on the grill for everyone that didn’t feel like eating peeper and eggs. My grandparents would bring the beer, soda and chips. My Uncle Frank would bring the appetizers that would usually be mozzarella sticks or French fries. My Aunt Linda would bring her world her world famous chocolate cake with ice cream for desert. After the dinner we would go outside in our backyard and play touch football for a good hour. Then we would talk about the game for a good forty-five minutes and then we would call it a night. This took place every Sunday and when it came to Monday’s if the Giants played on Monday, we would just reduce everything, get the homework done and get a smaller dinner with no touch football. But no year was more amazing then the Giants run in 1986. Lawrence “L.T.” Taylor and Phil Simms were leading the Giants to be one of the best teams in the NFL; they took the Giants to the top of the heap in the NFL, winning their division. We were so excited cause we have been waiting for a championship for the longest time now and this was our opportunity to really show everyone that the Giants are the greatest franchise in NFL history. We made the made to “Super Bowl Sunday” one of the greatest, if not the best, sporting event in the United States. Our family was psyched, we had family from all over there and the regular crew was they’re as well chanting, hollering and wearing the Big Blue colors. I always remember watching Bill Parcels on the sideline pacing back and forth thinking of the next play to execute. My grandfather loved watching Parcels because he could relate to him, my grandfather always referred to him as his longest lost brother. That Sunday was like no other cause all of us had butterflies in our stomach because we wanted to see “Big Blue” win so bad. We had the gear on and we watched the game like nothing in our eyes before. I watched my grandfather who had a smile on his face throughout the whole game even when the Giants would have a bad play he would just sit there and smile enjoying the Giants in the Super Bowl. We sat at the edge of our seats when the countdown from ten seconds to zero with the Giants winning the game. When the whistle blew and Bill Parcels got drenched in Gatorade signaled that the Giants finally done it. Lawrence Taylor threw his helmet in the air, Phil Simms raised his fist in the air and us back in the living room were going nuts. Hugging, screaming, excitement ran throughout the house, as the Giants were champions of the National Football League in the year of 1986. Throughout the celebration I noticed my Grandfather sitting there looking at the Giants winning the championship with a tear in his eye. He had waiting all his life to witness the Giants winning a championship and now he finally saw it. My Grandfather is one of the toughest individuals in the world and seeing him cry out of happiness were made me think how badly he really loved the New York Giants. I walked over to him very slowly tapping him on the shoulder. “Grandpa you alright?” “Yeah I’m fine, I just can’t believe they did it.” “They deserve it.” “Man those bastards can play.” (Chuckle) “They sure can Grandpa, they sure can.” He then proceeded to stand straight up in his Frank Gifford jersey and look me right in straight in the eye. It seemed forever and I saw my Grandfather in a new light, a man that passionately loved everything. He then put his hand straight out and I shook his hand. “Enough with the tearful emotion get those dam beers out and let’s celebrate!” “You dam right we will Grandpa.” That moment always stands out in my mind because less then a month later he passed away due to a heart attack. He was ninety-one years old and he was dead in his favorite chair watching a replay of the Super Bowl game. That moment after that game always stands out in my mind as something really special to me. He was a great man, my grandfather and he passed away the way he wanted to, seeing the Giants win the Super Bowl. He would tell me as a kid that he could die easily once the Giants won the super bowl, they gave him his wish and he knew he could rest now knowing that he succeed a dream, to watch them win a Super Bowl. “Big Blue” is still a family tradition to this day.
