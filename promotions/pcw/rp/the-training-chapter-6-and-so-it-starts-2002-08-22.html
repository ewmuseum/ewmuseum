The Training - Chapter 6 - And so it starts

22 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 22nd August 2002 Chapter 6 – And so it starts Recap: Illiterate housewife Charlotte Lawrence wants a job, one that doesn’t involve reading and writing. In an odd choice of career for a fragile woman, she has fallen in love with wrestling. Boyfriend Joe Nero isn’t happy with it, but he supports her. Charlotte has left her hometown of Prairie Town for the weekend, and trekked across America to New York and ‘The Epitome’ hotel, where a PWA Training Camp is taking place. Last night a group of drunkards tried to advance on her, but she was saved by a young American-Italian named Beppe Langsome, who proceeded to kiss her hand. Charlotte, aware of her loyalty to Joe, freaked out and made her exit. As we enter the first day of training, how will the relationship between the two new acquaintances develop? Beppe Langsome sat down at the dinner table in his hotel room, sipping orange juice from the carton. He never drank from glasses, it was only something else to clean up later anyway. The microwave clock behind his head showed it was only 6:48am, yet he was already in his workout gear of grey tank top, royal blue shorts and white trainers. At the meeting the previous night, the trainer had warned them to be ready early. No roster places in bigger federations would be given to those who slacked. As Beppe’s eyes skipped over the black text of the newspaper unfolded before him, he tried to remember the name of the trainer. Lee something? It doesn’t matter anyway, he thought, trainers are always very up themselves and cock sure of what they’re doing. No doubt his name would be shoved down their throats throughout the weekend in the hope that if they one day became big, he would be awarded a shred of the credit. One name he did remember though was that of Charlotte Lawrence, the fine specimen of a woman he had engaged with in a brief conversation. She seemed pleasant enough, if perhaps a bit shy. Her figure most certainly lent itself favourably to the imagination, and her smile was enough to make anyone like her. “Annoying yobs,” murmured Beppe to himself, in reference to the posse that had tried to corner Charlotte last night. Beppe hated people like that. If you fancy a woman, treat her with respect, and fancy her for the right reasons. Fancying someone so you can show off to the lads isn’t the way to set about matters. Shovelling another mouthful of cereal into his mouth, his attention turned towards the television, which had been humming silently in the corner for a few minutes. Looking up, Beppe saw the mug shot of what appeared to be an escaped convict. The man donned a very untidy beard, a pony tail so ridiculous in it’s shortness that it should be deemed illegal, and threatening eyes. Sharp, piercing brown eyes. Beppe was slightly taken back, and even turned away from the screen at the sight of those eyes. If looks could kill, anyone watching the news report would have been dead on the spot. Sighing, Beppe reached for the remote control and turned the mute television off. Glancing down at his watch, he stood up and grabbed a bottle of water. “Ten to seven. Enough time for a quick jog.” Armed with his water bottle to keep energy levels up, he turned the knob on the door of the apartment, and shut it quietly behind him. *** Charlotte emerged from her bedroom in her training gear, consisting of a white strap top, and black jogging bottoms, as well as new white trainers she had bought just before making the journey to New York. Her back turned to the corridor, she locked the door, before slipping the key into the small rucksack she taking with her. Turning around, her head smashed into someone else’s chest. “Ooh, sorry!” she giggled. Looking up, she saw Beppe Langsome, the man she had been introduced to at the ‘meet and greet’ the previous night. He was also the man who’d kissed her hand and looked overly happy to be in her company. Blushing, Charlotte started to walk down the corridor. She never knew what to do when she got into a fluster like this. She passed the same wallpapered red walls, and the same pieces of art, hoping Beppe wouldn’t follow her, but he did. “How are you this morning?” It’s not that he didn’t seem like a nice person. In fact, he seemed like a very nice person. That’s what worried her the most. “I’m fine, and you?” mumbled Charlotte. She could at least find it in herself to be polite. “Yeah, never been better. I’ve just been out for an early morning jog; there is nothing better to awake the soul.” Both of them giggled, with Charlotte’s laugh coming out more nervous than joyful. Looking over her right shoulder at Beppe, it looked like he had indeed been jogging, as his tank top was soaked in sweat already, and little beads had formed on his forehead. Not to mention his neatly cut hair, which was drenched. Wait a second, Charlotte thought to herself, am I noting all the fine details of his body? Oh God, quick, look disinterested. Charlotte’s march down the corridor picked up speed, almost to the point of a jog, and Beppe struggled to keep up with her. Through the winding and twisting corridors of The Epitome hotel they walked in silence, with Charlotte anxious to not acknowledge Beppe’s presence. If she did, she would surely feel guilty and start questioning her loyalty to Joe. After a couple of minutes of walking past hotel maid’s with their cleaning trolleys, and normal holiday makers wrapped in towels hurrying to the deserted swimming pool, Charlotte and Beppe arrived at the Grand Hall once more. The previous night it had been decked out with 50 chairs, a podium and microphone at the front, and the mini bar at the back had been open, where sparkling trays had glasses of champagne on top of them. Now though, the chairs had been stacked down the sides, the podium had been taken down and locked in a cupboard, and the mini bar was shut, with a scratched piece of wood placed down over it. In the centre of the hall were two wrestling rings, and in any other spare space blue mats to protect the fine wooden floor were laid down. Charlotte and Beppe joined the other 48 students in the centre of the hall. Unlike last night, there was no rowdiness or stupid ness, although it had to be noted that a few of the more excitable young lads were speculating about how they would be able to do anything and everything right off the bat. “OK, CAN I HAVE SOME HUSH PLEASE?” The cry came from the indomitable figure of Lee Harper, the PWA trainer who had perched himself on the front of the stage. Everyone’s heads turns towards Lee. He couldn’t have been any older than 30. All his hair was still there, and for all intents and purposes he was in fine shape, but unlike last night he wasn’t in a suit. He was in a faded red PWA tracksuit, which was perhaps the worse for wear. Hanging menacingly around his neck was a whistle. Also unlike the last night, he didn’t need to ask twice for silence. Everyone wanted to get on with what they’d made the journey for. “OK, once more, welcome to the Primetime Wrestling Academy Training Camp, and for those of you pissed out of your skulls last night who might have a shoddy memory, I am Lee Harper, your mentor, trainer, and if you want to reach what you regard as the promised land, for the next weekend I am God. Time is not with us this weekend ladies and gentleman, so we will get straight into things in a minute, but please, may I remind you that you are responsible for each other’s safety. Contrary to the popular opinion of our nation’s youth, wrestling isn’t about kicking the living crap out of your opponent. It is about putting on a show, painting a picture, telling a story. You cannot do that if you’re in the ring on your own. You need an opponent. Therefore, if you break your opponent’s neck in training by making him take a reckless bump, you are screwed. No pay check for you. SO TAKE CARE OF YOUR CO-WORKERS!” Charlotte stood with her arms crossed, looking straight ahead at Lee. Beppe positioned himself next to her, and he wiped his forehead dry with a wristband. He glanced quickly at her, apparently pondering something in his head, but then he shook his skull and focused back on the stage, where Lee Harper had now stood up and jumped onto the floor with the rest of the students. “What you’ll learn first is undoubtedly the most important thing you will ever learn in wrestling. If you cannot do this properly, you will wreck your back, or break an elbow, or even worse, break your neck. If you injure yourself in wrestling, that’s time lost, and in wrestling, time lost is money lost. Perhaps now I’m speaking in terms you can understand right?” This dry attempt at humour provoked a few sarcastic laughs from the men at the front, but nothing more. “Of course, I am talking about bumps. In each wrestling match there are hundreds of bumps. If you take a clothesline, you need to bump. If you take a powerbomb, you need to bump. Hell, if you take a simple move like a punch you will more often than not need to bump. When bumping, fall straight onto your back, and spread your arms out horizontally. DON’T EVER THINK ABOUT PUTTING YOUR HANDS DOWN FIRST! You will break your elbows, the bone will snap in two.” At that point, Charlotte started to get scared. In the past, wrestling had just been an art form and something videos relayed to her. But now she was preparing to take her first bump, and put her body on the line. Could she do it?
