The Bellzier Chronicles: Another Beginning [Part Three]

25 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 25, 2002 Bay BridgeSan Francisco, CA3:30 am The wind bristled through my hair as the car sped on at over one hundred miles per hour. It was almost as if I was sitting in a convertible with the top down, only I wasn’t. The windows to my car were blown to hell, and the only thing keeping me from flying out of them was the thin seatbelt making sure that I stayed somewhat secure. I had never been shot at before, but as the cliché goes, there’s a first for everything. I really didn’t think anything of the white van that I was trying to pass, but I realize now that things are becoming much more serious than they really are. Amazingly, my passenger seemed to be incredibly patient throughout the entire ordeal. I guess it could be worse – a screaming, shouting, hysterical passenger wouldn’t do very much for my confidence in getting out of this mess. As I sat at the drivers seat, covered in shattered glass and trying to keep from not shaking, I realized that I still didn’t know my passengers name. While it was true that he basically forced himself upon me using a weapon, I still could have obtained a bit more information from him. Once again, I was left in the dark and at the will of this individual that was running me around the Bay Area like a madman. Now I’m involved in a high speed chase that could likely wind up killing either me or him, but most likely the two of us. The situation was beginning to become much more dangerous. Even though we were beginning to pull away from the main pack of pursuers, that didn’t mean that we had lost them. I looked into my rear view mirror, which had a bullet going directly through the center. From what I could see, there were four cars that were following us from behind. There was original Mazda, and white van that shot at us, and two black Lincoln towncars. Being realistic, three of the four cars would be able to total me in second. I looked over at my passenger. “Got any bright ideas?” “Survive,” He replied. He made it sound incredibly simple, although I knew differently. “That’s great,” I murmured. “You do realize that there are four cars behind us, right? I would also like to remind you that those Lincoln towncars, if they wanted to, would be able to rip into us like we were just pieces of paper.” “I thought you were a good driver,” He snapped back. “Spies don’t get pulled over just because they’re being chased by four cars. They find a way to survive.” I scoffed at his notion, failing to see the logic in his statement. “Do you remember who you’re talking to? I’m not a damn spy, remember? You and your buddies tailing us are spies.” “You have it in you as well. You’d better find it soon before we get caught.” His positive nature was absolutely uplifting. Never in my wildest dreams that I think that I would have to call upon so much self confidence from myself to get out of a situation, but now was one of those times. I began to think that he was testing me to find out if I really was as good as this Bellzier Syndicate thought I was. What if they were all working together and this is just a big test to see if I’m worth the time and effort to pursue. If so, I’m screwed either way. I’m just screwed more on the latter plan. I looked over my passenger who continued to just stare forward. No one else could be this calm in a predicament like the one that we were in now. Either he was working for them or he had been in a million predicaments like this. Either scenario scared me. I could see the metallic arches of the Bay Bridge coming up. The air blew into the car like crazed hurricane now without the windows to shield us, and my hair whipped in the wind as if we were riding in a convertible. I could feel my heart beating and my breathing was quickening. The ice cold chill of the Bay’s wind crest could probably light a fire under the most experienced Antarctic explorer, and suffice it to say that I wasn’t used to this weather. We were now driving near one hundred and thirty miles per hour, and halfway through the bridge. The engine of my car gave a loud thud which signaled to me that it didn’t like to go this fast. I just shrugged and patted the dashboard. Hold together, hon. We’ll get through this. I hope. Out of the corner of my eye, I could see a car coming out from my left side. It was one of the black Lincoln towncars, and it was coming up on me. I looked over my right shoulder to make sure that there wasn’t another car coming on my blind side, and unfortunately, there was. The other Lincoln towncar was coming up on my right side, and soon we would have a civic sandwich. “I think we have a problem,” My passenger said, looking over both of my shoulders. “The obvious never escapes you, does it?” I nervously replied, sarcasm drenching my words. “What should we do?” “What do you mean?” I got a stern look on my face. He was moving in and out of obliviousness on me. “In about two seconds, those two Lincoln cars are going to slam into us on both sides and smash us into a short pancake. What am I supposed to do about that?” “Get away from these cars. They’re faster and stronger. Outmaneuver them – you should have better handling than a towncar. “That’s easier said then done.” The towncar on my left came up right next to me, although I couldn’t see into their car. Their windows were tinted so the men that were chasing us masked their identities. I could hear their engines firing up and I gripped the wheel to brace for the impact. The towncar swerved into us, and the impact nearly knocked me into my passenger. I reclaimed my balance and realized that the car had come dangerously close to engaging the towncar on our right side. I pulled the civic so that it was in between the two towncars and tried to figure out my next move. “We’re not going to outrun them.” He was right. I hit the brakes immediately and as I did the towncars sped ahead of us and were at least forty feet ahead of us now. As I smiled to myself, we were rammed from behind by the Mazda. The white van tried to swerve in front of us and cut us off, but I hit the gas and rocketed past the white van before it could react. As we moved past the white van, I could see the two town cars already slowing down and trying to turn around. As they pulled the one hundred and eighty degree turn, I sped past them and obtained a much longer lead than before. I looked back in my cracked and damaged rear view mirror to see the Mazda pursuing but it began to slow down. It was giving up. I sped the civic away from the bridge and took the first off ramp I saw. As I looked over at my passenger, he had a brief smile on his face. “That was some nice handling there, Tom,” He said. “That was some nice luck we ran into,” I replied, shaking my head in doubt. “Luck had little to do with it, although I can’t necessarily that it didn’t have hand in our escape. But you did well.” I was going to tell him that luck had everything to do with it when the car phone rang. I looked down at it in surprise since I never got calls on it. Actually, no one even had the number except for Nat and myself. Nat… I picked up the phone in a hurry and brought it to my ear. “Hello? Nat?” The voice on the other line was gruff. “Not exactly. How are you, Tom?” “Who is this?” I demanded. I never think when I talk – he was on a phone, so he had the right to refuse my questions. Hopefully he didn’t know that. “You don’t know who I am, and I doubt you would have any clue even if I told you my name. Let’s just say I’m a part of the little get together that tried to corral you today.” “The Bellzier Syndicate….” I muttered under my breath. “So, you’ve been talking to your passenger friend. I should have known someone would have assigned a person to protect you from us. It doesn’t matter though. Eventually we’ll get to you and our plans together will commence.” “Let’s get something straight, friend. No one is using me for any plan. I’m not having any of it.” The voice on the other line chuckled. “Ah, you Americans are so funny. You try to sound tough, but I can hear your fear even through the phones. You don’t mask things very well.” “And you have a pretty disgusting accent for a frenchie,” I countered. “We’re not all French, Tom. It just so happens, I’m the American contact. But we’ll be introduced later, I suppose. I just wanted to let you in on a little secret. Something that’s been bothering you even before you got into this little mess.” “What’s that?” “Oh, come now, Tom. Can’t you even try to venture a guess?” “Stop playing games with me. I’m about to seconds from hanging up on you.” “But Nat wouldn’t want that…” I paused. “Now I have your attention, don’t I?” I gritted my teeth and my eyes began to shoot forward towards the bridge again. “Listen to me. If you hurt her, or even look her the wrong way, there will be hell to pay.” “Oh, I’m sure there will be,” he said with a mocking laugh. “Is that why you’re driving away from us? Tom, we will be meeting soon enough so I don’t even worry about tonight’s incident. If anything, it reaffirms our belief that you’re the right man for the job we have in mind for you. So good ahead and try to conjure up a plan with your friend over there. We’ll be right here waiting for you. Myself and Nat.” “I’ll be coming,” I whispered. “And when I am there, staring you in the face and holding your head in my hands, I will laugh. The tables are going to turn.” “Tom, the tables turned a long time ago. The ball is no longer in your court – in fact, it was never in your court to begin with. We’ll talk later. Abientot.” The phone hung up and I was left there knowing where Nat was, but not being able to get her back. This was turning out to be a really bad day.
