Mat Land, Part 8- Wheres Your Mind

15 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/15/03 The phones ring constantly, never quieting down as the employees move about the office in Langley, Virginia. The spring afternoon is hot, and nearly everyone is starting to sweat as they move around the home base of the CIA’s operations. People come and go, each blending in with those around them, their grey and black suits meshing to make no one stand out. Only one stands out and that is Matt Jackson, making his way across the glimmering tile floor. His white suit swishing as he walks and his black suits sparking from the sunlight that is glaring in from the window behind him. Quietly he makes his march past all of the data analysts and into the famed conference room of the three wise men. Sitting at an enormous oak round table is the DCI and his two deputy directors. All three wearing matching black suits and each having his grey hair combed over to the side. Their faces light up with a smile as the ghost walks into the conference room, taking a seat in the chair across from them. Cracking a smile of his own, his white teeth show through and lighten the mood in the room. Placing his elbows on the table, Matt’s hands are greeted by a folder sliding into them. He looks down at the vanilla folder and pushes it to the side, focusing his brown eyes back onto the men in front of him. “Matt, you aren’t producing the results that we need. You promised us information and we are still waiting for it,” the DCI begins, taking his pipe out of his mouth and setting it down on the table. “You see Matt, we have been talking and we are not sure if it is the PCW thing or something else, but it doesn’t dispute the fact that you aren’t producing.” Taking a deep breath Matt runs his hands through his short brown hair and focuses his eyes in on Peter Benson the DCI. He knows that he hasn’t been getting the information from Sarah Clarkson that he promised, and maybe Peter was right. Maybe it was the stress of the PCW and Jason Hilton trying to stand between him and Haley. “Look Peter, I promised you information and you are going to get information. She has been a little harder to break then I expected, but don’t worry. I will have your information shortly after Spring Fling and then we will take down her father once and for all.” “You have until Spring Fling and then if you haven’t gotten what I want. I will replace you, simple as that,” the DCI responds, rubbing his chin with his forefinger and thumb. “You might want to look in that folder though. Something was brought to my attention the other day and I thought it might be of some interest to you.” Pushing the folder back into his line of sight, he opens it and begins to flip through the contents inside. Nothing out of the ordinary, pictures and a bio of an agent he isn’t familiar with. Claire Lune, female British Intelligence and close friend to Sarah Clarkson. Proving the folder isn’t of interest to him, Matt quickly pushes it out of his way and places his elbows on the table again, letting his slender face fall rest into them. “What is the point of it? She is a close friend of Clarkson, big deal. Do you guys want me to kill her or something?” Matt asks, with a hint of anger in his voice. He is dealing with enough right now and knocking off some has been British agent doesn’t rank high on his things to do list, espially with Spring Fling just a dew days away. At least then, he is going to be able to get rid of Jade and Lacuna once and for all. They have caused nothing, but problems for him and this is his opportunity to finish the job he started against them a few weeks back. “No, we don’t want you to kill her. Just thought it might be nice to inform you that she is the one they sent in to find Clarkson. Her father trusts no one else then this girl and we can’t be sure why,” Peter begins, “Basically it is some white trash chick. She lived with them when her parents left and has been there for Sarah whenever she got into trouble, but she seems mostly amateur so you shouldn’t have any problems dealing with her.” “I thank you for all this Peter, I really do, but I have more important things to attend to. Such as Lacuna and Jade and Spring Fling. I can’t really be bothered with all this other side shit,” Matt bellows out, slamming his fist down on the table to get his point across. Each of the three men starts to take renewed interest in the conversation that is playing out for them. “When we gave you the PCW mission, you promised it wouldn’t affect any of your other work and now look at what is happening. That is the other thing we wanted to talk to you about. You have been using agency information and influence for things other then shutting down the PCW. That isn’t acceptable Matt. You need to get your head back in this fucking game,” on of the deputies starts. He is quickly silenced by the hand of Jackson’s that was just raised, crossing his arms across his barrel of a chest; Matt leans back in the leather chair. “My mind is on the game have no worries,” Matt tells them, his voice cold and precise. “Also, I think it is in your best interest never to question me again. I never used the CIA for my problems with Lacuna; I called in some old favours and took care of it on my own. I got the job done and I believe we can leave it at that.” His body tightens in anger and the smile leaves his face. Staring coldly at the deputy who questioned him, his eyes burning holes in the man, who just stares back. Matt knows that if they knew about the attempted fixing of the Continental Tournament and the killing of the man who failed him, they would blow their tops. “If we are done I will be on my way,” he says, kicking his chair out from behind him and nodding towards the wise men, before swaggering out of the conference room into the hallway. He fixes his white sport coat, checking to make sure his automatic is still firmly in his holster. *** “I have it narrowed down to a few names Mr. Clarkson,” Claire tells him, her voice barley more then a whisper. She has been up for three days, trying to figure out exactly who kidnapped her best friend and what they were doing with her. Not a lot has been discovered, except for proving Andrew’s theory about the Central Intelligence Agency being involved. Sitting across the huge desk from her is Andrew Clarkson, famed British Intelligence Agent. Leaning back in the leather chair, his black suit swishes causing thousands of small creases. Rubbing his black eyes with his enormous hands, he lets a tear fall from them, but quickly catches it, careful not to let Claire see this momentary weakness. His almost baldhead, gleams in the florescent light that is shining overhead in his study. Darting his eyes around his favorite room in the house, he notices nothing has changed. The rows and rows of books, one stacked above the other, a few painting of forgotten heroes hang so perfectly between each bookcase and the red carpet that just adds a little brightness to the normally dull room. Everything is too his liking, not as if it wouldn’t be in the first place. “You need to get her back Claire, I can only image what is going on with her,” Andrew squeaks back. He is having a hard time grasping the fact that his daughter has been taken and that there is a good chance she is being hurt. “I am doing everything I can Andrew,” she tells him, letting her jade eyes fall out of focus, as she runs her hands through he reddish-brown hair. She knows that if her hunch is right, then it is going to be hard to get Sarah back. The one name that stood out to her was none other then Matt Jackson. The famed ex-Ranger and CIA operative that no one dares to talk about. “I know you will Claire, because turn about is fair play. I am sure you remember when your cousin was hassling you and I took care of it,” Andrew firmly says. Trying to make her remember all the things he had done for her and make her remember the times when he didn’t do anything for her. “I know what you did and I will take care of this,” she replies. “Tomorrow I am going to follow up on a few leads and then go from there.”
