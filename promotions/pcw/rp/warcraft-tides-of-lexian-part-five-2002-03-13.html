Warcraft: Tides of Lexian [Part Five]

13 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 13, 2002 Where is Tom Lexian? Sean O’Reilly couldn’t believe what happened. One second he was in his apartment, enjoying a nice game of Warcraft II, and here he was, sucked into some parallel universe. This stuff was only supposed to happen to Tom, not him. Tom was always the one that came back with these wild stories of visiting strange new worlds and meeting all sorts of crazy aliens or creatures. Was it time that Sean could have one of these adventures as well? At least Sean wasn’t confronted with one of those strange beings. He was looking up at a human, or at least someone that resembled himself fairly closely. Definitely not someone from Never Never Land. The last thing Sean remembered was playing the game. He and Tom had just gotten ambushed behind from these two goons they were playing on Battle.net. It was all set up perfectly, but they had underestimated their opponents. They were much better than either of them had anticipated, and now they were paying the price for their overconfidence. Maybe it wasn’t entirely their fault. Sean always said that there was always someone better than you out there, and now it had been proven to him. Or maybe the true answer was that they had gotten old. No longer could they reign superior over all others in online play and beat up on unsuspecting fourteen year olds. Nope. They had to contend with the veterans, those wily vets, that are still sticking with the game to this day. Sean knew they were about to get creamed. Sean was ready to give Tom a harassing lecture on strategy and how he has gotten rusty in his game, but before that could happen, Sean found himself sucked into some kind of fantasy world. He was now sitting in a grassy knoll, surrounded by dead bodies, and now looking up at a stranger. At least it was a human looking stranger. “Who’s Tom Lexian?” The man asked Sean. He had heavy body armor on and his long sword continued to catch Sean’s eye. Sean just shook his head. “No one, never mind.” As Sean got back to his feet, he noticed that the man had a cut on his arm. It was bleeding profusely, enough so that blood was beginning to drop onto the ground. “Are you going to take care of that?” Sean asked. The man looked at the cut. “We are close to an Alliance camp. I’ll have the cleric heal it for me.” “Alliance camp?” Sean said. “I hope that’s the good guys you’re referring to.” “The good guys?” The man replied. “If you are referring to the forces of light, then you are exactly right. We are fighting to save our homelands.” “And what homeland would that be?” Sean asked, hoping that the man would reply Earth. Please god let it be Earth. “Azeroth, of course.” Dammit. Azeroth? As in the Warcraft II world, Azeroth? Now the irony was getting too much and Sean closed his eyes in despair. Somehow Tom got out of these strange places and managed to come back with some wild story. But what if Tom didn’t get sucked into the same world? What if Sean was stuck here for good? He wasn’t nearly as resourceful. “Are you alright?” The man asked Sean, with a compassionate look on his face. He placed his hand on Sean’s back, but Sean just brushed it away. “I’m fine, I’m fine,” Sean added hastily. “Who are you anyways?” “My name is Vanji,” He replied. “I am a noble warrior for Azeroth, fifth brigade to the king himself. Who are you, if I may ask?” “My name’s Sean O’Reilly,” He said, still looking around. “What happened here?” “It was an orc raid,” Vanji said with disgust. “They attacked without provocation, knowing that this day was one of the holiest on our calendar. Today marks the five year anniversary that the infernal portal opened and allowed the Horde to enter our world. I am saddened that we even celebrate this date, but we must never forget.” Sean nodded slowly, still taking in everything that was happening. Vanji looked at him with concern. “Are you sure you are alright? Are you on some sort of special mission?” “Mission?” “Well, I assumed you were teleported here. I didn’t see you at first on the battlefield, yet here you are.” Vanji reasoned. “You must have been sent here by the mages themselves.” “I don’t know how I got here,” Sean admitted. “Although I’m definitely looking to get out.” “That would probably be a smart move,” Vanji replied, wiping some sweat from his brow. “The orcs are bound to return, and should they come back, they would find us as the sole survivors of this battle. Come, we will venture back to home.” “And where is that…” Vanji looked back briefly. “Back to the Violet Citadel.” A Meeting of the Minds The meeting place of the Kirin Tor in the Violet Citadel was perhaps one of the castle's most intriguing rooms. The room was not an actual part of the citadel itself and was instead a floating structure situated twenty feet higher in the sky than the four great towers of the castle. There it ruled over the whole of the city and the whole of the nation itself. The meeting place was a true marvel in that it was not physically connected to the structure at all, and was instead bound to it by various spells set in place by the very same magi who built and used the mystical place. Like a moon in stationary orbit above the castle it floated ominously. It was the constant focus of all mystical energy in the whole of the nation of Dalaran, and the constant envy of ambitious young apprentices. It was the place that every mage strove to get to: the place reserved for only the best, brightest, most skilled, most powerful, and wisest of the whole of humanity; the Kirin Tor. The room itself was a massive sphere made of the same violet rock of the castle. Arcane bolts of energy constantly crackled and darted along the outer surface of the smooth orb. There were no windows to be seen and to any who looked upon it, the room looked like a perfectly smooth, gigantic purple boulder floating in the air above the citadel. The study and knowledge of the place was worth a volume of its own for the hundreds of spells of protection and defense placed upon the room and for the ingenious architecture of the forces needed to hold the orb at bay in a constant place above the citadel. The room was the only place in the whole of the citadel that could not be reached by a door or gate. Only one with knowledge of certain spells of teleportation, and the knowledge of the many passwords needed to enter through the magical, protective layers around the orb were granted access to its core. Only the Arch-magi of Dalaran knew the spells of teleportation, and only those Arch-magi who had proven themselves worthy enough of a seat on the Kirin Tor were given the passwords. That night, even the soft, purple glow of the room was swallowed entirely by the fury of the storm. The sphere seemed dark and black, blending into the ominous shadow that was the sky. Yet inside the strange room, the light was clear and crisp, emanating off the inner walls of the chamber and glowing inside like firelight. The inner walls were bare of windows or decorations. Instead the walls were lined in confusing and intricate golden letters, written in ancient runes of the High Elves, the same runes that the Elves used to teach the humans in the ways of magic in ages long passed by time. The runes were originally carved into the stone of the inner chamber and were then later filled with specially enchanted gold that shone with an inner fire and shimmering radiance. The runes provided the light inside the chamber while also serving as wards and enchantments of protection on the chamber itself. Eleven golden chairs floated inside the chamber, held aloft by the ever-flowing energies inside and around the room itself. Eleven chairs for the eleven arch-magi of the Kirin Tor that used the room for their secretive purposes. Three of the chairs floated light and empty, left without people to bear. The other eight were filled with the various magi that made of the Kirin Tor, human, elven, male and female alike were present, representing the very pinnacle of arcane power in the world. Suddenly a sparkling bluish light flashed near one of the seats of the chamber, signifying the coming of another mage to the chamber with the brilliance of the spell. Yet soon after the light of the teleportation spell subsided, there was a dull light, as if the golden light of the chamber was swallowed lightly around the chair of the newcomer. All in the chamber was brilliant and shining, but the chair of the newcomer seemed strangely flat and normal as if each of the chairs were lamps of their own and this one had gone out. The newcomer appeared instantly sitting in his chair with his arms rested lightly upon the rests of the golden chair. He was dressed entirely in a black cloak, the rest of his garments concealed from sight. About his feet were old and worn boots of black leather. The boots were layered with gray mud, as was the bottom of his cloak. The mage kept his face concealed with the black hood of his cloak despite the fact that the other magi of the council kept theirs down, as was the custom. 'You're late,' said Celwyn with a wise, old voice of an elven mage. The voice echoed in the chamber and seemed ethereal and distant, yet stern and resolute as the voices of the elves were. Celwyn was one of the oldest on the council yet her fair elven features hid the ages worn in her face. She had been elected to the council for her incredible understanding of the arcane as well her as her uncanny knack for knowing and feeling the balance of the world. She was a master historian and her knowledge of the doings of the world was renowned and respected on the council. 'We have been waiting patiently, Master Gurgoth. Perhaps you could try to improve your punctuality a bit, yes?' An old voice then replied. Old and ragged it was and it did not echo. Instead it seemed to creep and slither in the undertones of the room. Yet despite its seemingly weak presentation, a deep sense of awe and respect filled any who heard the voice, for its wielder was one of great power and control. 'I apologize profusely to the council for my tardiness. I had important matters to attend to that needed my attention. For the delay I am sorry. I pray you have not been waiting long?' 'Not long, no, but do remember that we all on this council have important matters to deal with as well,' replied Celwyn. There was a short pause before the meeting was picked up by a different mage, Burdred Tydul. Burdred had served the Alliance in the last year of the Second War as a battle mage, yet his actual combat experience had been short and bitter. He had served in various sorties involving orcish bands left in Azeroth after the war had shifted to the dreaded home world of the orcs. His last battle of the war had met with disaster as his band of troops was ambushed deep in the heart of the Orclands in Southern Azeroth. Tydul's troops had been brutally ambushed and Burdred had been one of the three survivors out of a whole company of troops. Memories of the failure had haunted the mage and what were what drove the mage to come back to Dalaran for complete mastery of battle-magic. Burdred was a middle-aged man in his late forties, yet despite his relative youth compared to others on the council, he was gravely respected for the terrible onslaught he was capable of upon the battlefield, especially against the orcs… 'Well, now that everyone is here I would like to start off the meeting. It has been two months since our last meeting and I'm sure we all have some news of some importance or another. I have been down in Azeroth surveying the recent events regarding the Horde. I'm sure you have all heard the news by now, that the orcs have escaped their camps and are now being reunited with one another under a new Warchief?' Burdred's comment was greeted with a slough of mumbled comments and guttural sounds regarding the news. The council had heard the news and it sounded as if the majority were displeased. Finally a female, nearly the same age as Burdred spoke in response. Her name was Jyra Mihri. Jyra had been elected to the council after the end of the second war. Although she herself had not served in combat, her incredible value as a healer and her care of the people of humanity gave her the credit and respect needed to serve on the council. Having studied the magics of healing, Jyra had taken the teachings of the Clerics of Northshire and perfected their arts into easier and more efficient spells that could be used far easier on the battlefield. Due to her studies, countless men had been saved. She said, 'Indeed Master Tydul, we have heard these tidings. Though this is undoubtedly bad news, this revolt took place nearly a month and a half ago and since then we have heard no reports of hostile orcish activity in or around any of their known habitations as of yet. And for some reason, I find myself unconcerned with this new uprising, if that is what you wish to call it. I have seen what war is capable of, and at the moment this new Horde is small and scattered among all the lands of the former Alliance. Until they have fully regrouped and shown clear signs of hostility and offensive aggression I cannot consent to any move against them. Let us pray that the fire of their hearts has died and that their interests lie in something more productive than war.' Tydul responded, 'I too wish for this to be so, Master Mihri, but at the same time we cannot let this development go unchecked. If this new Horde is left on their own it is very possible that they could fully regroup, and once that is accomplished, there is no telling what their intentions could be and what they would be capable of. I am not advocating an all out assault on this new orcish threat, but I am strongly pressing you members of the council that it is our duty to look out for the safety of humanity. My suggestion is that we at least look into the details of this uprising. We need details of their Warchief, we must strive to learn their plans and capabilities. And most importantly, we must hinder their ability to reunite.'
