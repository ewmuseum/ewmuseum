Title: Hallmark of a Hypocrite: The Equation of No Return Featuring: The H.I.T. Date: not entered Greed. Perhaps the easiest of all sins to fall into. Called averice in the official listing of such evils, the 'seven deadly sins', greed can easily destroy both the greedy and those around him. Case in point: Primetime Championship Wrestling. Using Tim Walden to collect, PCW Owner Xavier Kannon is taxing the roster to an early death. What for, however? To land more prestigious venues for pay per views? No. To put into massive advertising campaigns? No. To use to further secure over PCW, even? Surprisingly, the answer is again no. Then, one might ask, what DOES Kannon use his wealth for? Listen well, dear friends, and all shall be made apparent. ::As the bell to signal the beginning of first period at Brigham Young High School in Salt Lake City, Utah rings, the twenty-five students of the Hardcore Isosceles Trapezoid sit in their desks, in a perfect 5x5 square, awaiting the arrival of their teacher, who is on his way from the library with a new discovery…:: Jimmy: Mr. Trapezoid’s late! Susan: The two minute rule’s about to go into effect! Jimmy: Two minute rule? Susan: Yeah! Remember, Mr. Trapezoid told us that if he’s ever late, we should practice our F.U.C.K.ing! Jimmy: Oh yes, of course! Which letter were we up to last time? Susan: Fun! And, we certainly did have fun…meaning we’re now up to Unviolent… The HIT: Which last week’s F.U.C.K.ing between you and Jimmy certainly was not. But regardless, class has now begun. ::Trapezoid stands at the entrance to the room, carrying his research, papers, and a nice white piece of chalk for use on the board. He plops his things down on his desk, and greets his students.:: The HIT: Good morning class! Class: Good morning Mr. Trapezoid! The HIT: Yes, now class, I know we had planned on doing our lesson today on Pyramids: Not Just For Really Old Guys, but I’ve made an important discovery in the field of mathematics entertainment! Jimmy: Mathematics entertainment? The HIT: Mathematics plus sports entertainment equals Mathematics entertainment. Write that down, there’ll probably be a quiz on it when I get around to writing the forsaken thing. ::Trapezoid scribbles that particular statement down on the board.:: Susan: Okay, so, what is this great discovery? The HIT: I’ve managed to figure out where all of the PCW tax money goes! Class: WOW! The HIT: Yes, I know, it’s quite an achievement if I do say so myself. Anyway, this is how it goes. Figure that the average PCW superstar is taxed total of 20 percent of his income. The tax money Kannon recieves per superstar is equal to .20I, I standing for income. ::The children carefully take notes to follow HIT’s mad board scribbling.:: The HIT: Now, let’s just take an easy to work with example. Assume PCW only had five guys in it, and each had the same income, Kannon’s total take from them would be I. So, Kannon has now made the full salary of one PCW employee in taxes. Kannon’s salary, however, is naturally a bit larger than everyone else’s, sitting at 1.25I, again, I representing the income of the five men being paid the same. ::More frantic scribbling on the board occurs. Much of it is illegible, but that really doesn’t seem to make much of a difference to anyone.:: The HIT: Now, if we take Kannon’s salary and add it to the tax money, that means that Income XK (IXK) is equal to 1.25I + I, or 2.25I. However, Kannon does not use any of the tax money for anything to better PCW! And so, I had to figure out where the cash went. And this is the difficult part, where I had to determine the Prep Factor. Jimmy: Prep Factor? The HIT: Oh, you’ll seee… Susan: Yeah! Let him talk! The HIT: Thank you. So, anyway, I looked at the number 2.25, and realized that it was eqivalent to the fraction 9/4. I used this as a slope to try to find an equation that would possibly help me.. I took the original percentage of taxation, .20, and used that as the x coordiante of the line, and then took the I, with a coefficient of 1, as the y-coordinate. Thus, I used the point and slope to write out my equation in point-slope form, coming up with y minus one equalling nine-fourths times the quantity y minus point twenty, or, in slope intercept form, y= (9/4)x + .55. Jimmy: So? What does that line have to do with anything? The HIT: We don't graph it! We merely take the DERIVATIVE of the function, leaving us back with that nine-fourths. Now nine-fourths....is the Prep Factor. Susan: Then, why did we need to do all that work in between to get a number we already had? The HIT: Makes it look more complicated. Now, what this means is that of Kannon's 2.25I salary, Eleanor gets four-ninths of it....and.....and....and.....wait, this doesn't make sense does it? ::The class shakes their head no.:: The HIT: Didn't think so. Ah well, anyway, the point I was trying to make is that Xavier gives Ellie lots of money to further her fashion habits. And, of course, her own personal Snapples. Regarding Eleanor's assets, at the very least, the slogan IS true. They ARE made from the best stuff on earth! But still, Kannon, don't count on a nice victory fuck with your walking seminal recepticle. After I've dropped you on your head with Parallel Bases II, you won't exactly remember what an Ellie is to start with! You are just one more step on the path to the Cruiserweight Title.....a path marked with the blood of the Front Office! ::The bell rings to mercifully end the period, sending both student and teacher scurrying out of the classroom.:: ---- View The H.I.T.'s Biography
