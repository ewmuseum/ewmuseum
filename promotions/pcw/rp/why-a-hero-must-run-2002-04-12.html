Why A Hero Must Run

12 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-12-02 **OOC NOTE: Theme music is, in fact, essential for this RP. It is by my favorite band, and you must download it. I have spoken. :b **Theme Music: 'Get Set For Sound' by The Stereo** ----------------------------------------Dear Jenna, ... Love,Jason---------------------------------------- Hey [The sun rises over Harbid Terrace, the small college community on Pensacola Street in Tallahassee.] Get set for the brand new sound [The sky is gently turning to blue. The clouds are few and far between.] It’s perfect in every way [On the street, a few cars pass, but not too many on a Friday 6:00 AM in a college town.] Unlike any before you’ve found [A black Chevy Tahoe in the Harbid parking lot sits with a door ajar. Next to it, one of those little whirlwinds of leaves that's completely unexplainable whirls around.] At least that’s what we’re told to say... [As we get closer to the door, we see a man putting on a second heavy knee brace.] Hey [Pull strap, click lock, pull strap, click lock. Repeat.] We’re smarter than that don’t you know [He flexes his knee tentatively, the black rubber and metal of the brace offsetting the blue track pants. The knee and the brace both creak with age.] But you counted the heads so hit play [He stretches his arms as he slides out of the car, his pectoral muscles making the Core Wrestling School t-shirt dance.] And assume what you don’t really know [He takes one long look at the sun...and throws a wink to his wife.] Now there’s something that we’d like to say [Slam the door. Pick up the pace.] It’s easy to make up [And now we run.] It’s not hard to fake [The man runs through the parking lot towards Pensacola Street, and turns down the sidewalk.] Watch how they do it to me [And now we run.] This is serious... [The fluidity of muscle and man is something that's oft taken for granted. This man runs like a prized thoroughbred...all muscle, all finely trained.] It’s a big mistake [The legs stretch, the knees bend, and we run.] Hey [The arms pump, the chest opens, and we run.] I grew up with the sound around [He passes the occassional sorority girl looking for better legs or the pretty boy college dude looking to keep in shape and pick up girls. They're ignored for now.] Spent my summers just listening [Because now, we run.] With no reason to turn it down [He crosses the Doak Campbell bridge, which isn't it's official name, but it's what everyone calls it. To his left, road and the occassional moving car...to his left, the omnipresent construction work. An early-rising construction worker gives a friendly wave.] I would listen [Without thinking or a second look, he waves. His thoughts are fully internal. About breathing, and moving.] Now, I've grown up and I understand [He pulls to the left, and down towards Doak Campbell Stadium itself. A few students and administrators who've arrived early barely acknowledge his presence. His sneakers make a dull noise on the brick ground.] Target us with a marketing plan [The gate to the stadium stands wide open and unlocked. The man runs through the chain-linked gate. A security guard nods. He sees this man every day.] But they’ve seemed to forgotten why [And now we run faster.] The music’s a lie when the target’s tried [We run up stairs.] It’s easy to make up [Doctors told him 'Do not run up stairs. It is not healthy for your knees. Find some lower-impact cardiovascular exercise.' But stairs feel good, so we run.] It’s not hard to fake [There are exactly 733 stairs from the bottom of Doak Campbell stadium to the upper deck.] Watch how they do it to me [We run up 2932. And down 2932. And we count every single one.] This is serious... [Because running is good. And counting is good. And because the sunrise at the top of the stadium was so pretty the third time he saw it, he just HAD to go back up a fourth time.] It’s a big mistake [At the bottom, the last time. The man stops running. He reflects on his situation.] What have we done? [The challenges ahead of him.] What have we created? [The people who effect him.] What does this mean? [His friends.] And how are we gonna make it? [His enemies.] What have we done? [His responsibilities.] What have we created? [His students.] What does this mean? [It takes him approximately 40 seconds.] And how are we gonna make it... [Because as John Crichton says...a hero never thinks about the consequences of his actions.] Now? [And this man is a hero.] What have we done? [So he runs.] What have we created? [Back outside Doak Campbell Stadium, past the security guard, soles of his shoes making the same dull flap against the brick walkways and concrete.] What does this mean? [Back up the D.C. bridge, now with more cars passing him, most going in the opposite direction.] And how are we gonna make it... [A hero isn't afraid to make his own way.] Now? [Running isn't always a sign of cowardice.] Get set for the brand new sound [The sweat pours off him like a river. It soaks his shirt, it makes his hair fall about in unheroic tangles.] It’s perfect in every way [And we're back at the car. At Harbid Terrace. The day of the biggest match of the man's career. Tonight may be the most grueling contest he's ever endured. And on Sunday, he must stand up for his beliefs. Against people who not only want him silenced, but gone.] Unlike any before you’ve found [The man winks at the sun again, counts out loud, 'One.' And turns back to the street from where he came. He's got three more laps to do this morning. Three more times he'll make the trip. Pushed to the limit of stamina. Again, we run.] At least that’s what we’re told to say... [Because heroes have to last forever.] [Because heroes must find strenght to stand.] [Because heroes aren't afraid to run.]
