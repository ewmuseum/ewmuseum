Home Sweet Home...

22 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 22, 2003 {{{{Scene}}}}The scene opens up on the open road, no landmarks in site, just a road in the middle of nowhere with deep thickets of forest on each side of the road. The car, a red 2001 Dodge Viper, one of the cars that Semore Smoke has had for years and will never trade in for anything else, except maybe a ’76 Trans-Am T-top, the Burt Reynolds car known otherwise. But that’s the car driving down the road. The music blaring on the radio, “Lifestyles of the Rich and Famous” By Good Charlotte, the scene cuts inside to where you see Semore Smoke behind the wheel driving. He’s wearing a black t-shirt with “PCW” written across the front of it and a pair of khaki shorts, so the temperature must be warm for him to be wearing those. Smoke is also doing probably the worst thing that he can do, he’s singing along… Semore Smoke: …Celebrities want sympathy, all they do is piss and moan, inside the rolling stones, talking about how life can be, I’d like to see them spend a week, living life out on the street, I don’t think they would survive, if they could spend a day or two walking someone else shoes, I think they would stumble and they fall, they would fall…… fall, fall, fall…. Lifestyles of the rich and the famous, they’re always complainin’ always complainin’, ‘cause money is such a problem, well they got mansions, think we should rob them. Well, did you know if your famous you could kill you wife, there’s no such thing as twenty-five to life, as long as you’ve got the cash to pay for Cockren. And did you know if you were caught and you were smoking crack, McDonalds wouldn’t even take you back, you could always just run for the mayor of DC. , I’d like to see them spend a week, living life out on the street, I don’t think they would survive, if they could spend a day or two walking someone else shoes, I think they would stumble and they fall, they would fall. Lifestyles of the rich and the famous, they’re always complainin’ always complainin’, ‘cause money is such a problem, well they got mansions, think we should rob them, rob them, rob them….. {{{{There is a small instrumental part….}}}} Semore Smoke: Fall…. Fall… Fall, Fall… Lifestyles of the rich and the famous, they’re always complainin’ always complainin’, ‘cause money is such a problem, we’ve got so many problems, they think it’s all bad, Lifestyles of the rich and the famous, we’ll take your sports cars too, just stop complainin’, lifestyles of the rich and famous, lifestyles of the rich and famous, lifestyles of the rich and famous!!!!! {{{{The music then changes to another song, in fact, it’s Semore Smoke’s theme song, “Hash Pipe” By Weezer, which he also tries to sing after the Bass guitar revs…}}}} Semore Smoke: I can't help my feelings, I'll go out of my mind. These players come to get me 'cause they'd like my behind. I can't love my business if i can't get a trick. Down on Santa monica where tricks are for kid. Ooh, come on and kick me, Ooh, come on and kick me, (Wooaoo), come on and kick me, (Wooaoo), you've got your problems, (Wooaoo), I've got my eye swipe, (Wooaoo), you've got your big g's. I've got my hash pipe. I can't help my boogies they get out of control. I know that you don't care but i want you to know the knee-stocking flavour is a favourite treat of men that don't bother with the taste of a teat. Ooh, come on and kick me, Ooh, come on and kick me, (Wooaoo), come on and kick me, (Wooaoo), you've got your problems, (Wooaoo), I've got my eye swipe, (Wooaoo), you've got your big g's, I've got my hash pipe, (uh) I've got my hash pipe {{{{A great guitar solo takes over, playing the notes that were just sung in the first two verses…}}}} Semore Smoke: Ooh, come on and kick me, Ooh, come on and kick me, (Wooaoo), come on and kick me, I can't help my feelings, I'll go out of my mind. I’ve got my hash pipe, (Wooaoo), you've got your problems, (Wooaoo), I've got my eye swipe, (Wooaoo), you've got your big g's. I've got my hash pipe, (uh) I've got my hash pipe, (uh) I've got my hash pipe, I've got my hash pipe! {{{{As Smoke finishes this song, he turns and pulls into a driveway. He drives his Dodge up the driveway, which is more of a path, at the end of the path you can see what seems to be an old white house in the distance. Surrounding the house are several fields, which currently are supporting Tobacco, the Golden Leaf, a well-known site in Wilson, North Carolina, Semore Smoke’s hometown. Smoke pulls up in front of the house and then cuts his car off, including the radio, which had a new song playing, one that Smoke wasn’t about to try and sing, “Hotel California” By Eagles. Smoke walks forward up the concrete steps of the old house and then approaches the front door and opens the door. Inside the house you see an old couch in the corner, on the wall adjacent from the door you see two recliners, to the immediate left of Smoke as he enters you see a small entertainment system including a 24 inch TV, two VCRs, a Playstation, and a stereo system. In the far corner away from the couch you see a computer desk with a computer sitting on it. Smoke closes the door behind him and looks around the house with a smile on his face as he says these words…}}}} Semore Smoke: Home Sweet Home… Nice words to say after being driven out of Atlantic City by the FBI… but I can continue everything here, where I grew up… {{{{The scene fades on Smoke’s grinning face as he walks towards the couch and flops down on the dust-covered couch…}}}}
