Redemptions and Revelations : Episode 1; Chapter 1

7 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/7 The moonlight gleams down on the silent structure looming in the background, it’s inner walls incarcerated within a perimeter of razorwire-crowned steel fencing. A dilapidated sign of hoary and plain lettering is minutely illuminated under the light of the glistening sphere in the heavens above, set on a rickety timber post with letters tarnished from years of neglected maintenance. Passing briefly over the dreary entrance to Deerfield State Sanitarium, a silhouette of stocky proportion wavering slightly in the ashen lights hovering about the walls slides across the dew-laden earth. Slowly the notorious Boston thug lumbers into view with gaze cast toward the taciturn building with a pair of shades to veil his beady eyes, regardless of the budding darkness, casting a glint from the reflection of the moon’s pallid rays. He rests with feet apart and facing the looming asylum beyond the retaining walls, his scarred and burly arms crosses about his equally brawny chest. The glasses shrouding his eyes hide their icy glaze. Ph-nglui mglw’nafh Cthulhu R’lyeh wgah’nagl fhtagn The syllables reiterated over and again, always ending in an eerie reverb before permeating his thoughts once more. What exactly had drawn him to this spot he could but venture a guess; likewise went for how he had come to arrive at this foreboding destination. From beyond the walls of the asylum there was deathly silence. He passed without resistance through the secured gate, the guard station being curiously vacant as was the surrounding perimeter devoid of life or security. Still in an arcane trance he trudged almost mindlessly through the sprawling yard beyond that razorwire-laden fencing, passing empty benches and saplings planted as décor around the square lawn. A single light flickered in that desolate and madness-stricken structure, a lone tongue of radiance penetrating the darkness on the outer window of the third floor. Toward this Bruce walked without grace or visible passion, under the guise of a mindless automaton. He scaled the concrete stairs leading to the door, and the lock inexplicably released with a minute click upon his reaching for the door handle. Thrusting open the door, he entered the halls of the madhouse and the silence was instantly replaced by the screams of the deranged. His composure remained unwavering as he passed through the halls, the cries of the psychotic ringing in his ears yet producing no reaction in his still mind. At one point he paused and shifted his stoic gaze to the left as a strait-jacket bound inmate pressed his insanity-ravaged visage against the thick-paned glass of his rubber-enveloped cell, spouting off further insane ramblings. ”He is coming! Greath Cthulhu’s hour is near! Prepare the altars for his glorious arrival!….” His speech degenerated into a slew of blasphemous monosyllables as Shanahan turned and plodded forward, leaving the maniac to rant to whomever else would listen. More cries of praise for the tentacle-faced celestial saturated the air to intermix with the random screams of the mad, many coming to pound and batter on their doors as the jaded thug lumbered listlessly by. He crept down the winding corridors and past the confined lunatics as they ranted endlessly in their chambers in praise and worship of various other cosmic abominations that had taken hold over the frail mindsets of humankind. As he passed Section L of the first level he reached a staircase leading to the upper levels. As his footsteps clattered across the tiling of the floor and then directed upward toward the third floor, he failed to notice the scarlet hue of light emanating from the pinnacle of the stairs and bathing the entire area in a pale red tint. The unnatural phenomenon continued as he exited the silent stairwell and entered the third level of the sanitarium, its radiance brightening as he marches wordlessly past the row of cells to other side and thus again being exposed to the myriad of mad calls and ritualistic chants from the frenetic-minded inmates. At the end of the hall rested a doorway, the door hanging open with noxious mist billowing into the hallway and the red light pulsating as if to the steady beat of a human heart. A continual hum filled Shanahan’s ears as he crept further toward the yawning portal ahead, the hum slowly morphing into the monotonous and high-pitched piping of a flute. As he reached the doorsill the piping increased in pitch until it audibly bore the resemblance of an unrelenting shrieking. Almost instantaneously the mist wisped away and Bruce stepped forward over the threshold into the darkness, the door slamming shut in his wake. For nearly a minute he was encompassed in hybrid silence and inky blackness, the only other constant being the tedious whistling of the flute, the sound emitting from all around as if devoid of any individual source. He became only remotely aware as a swirl of color began to churn around him, the darkness slowly lifting to reveal his environment. Had it not been for his lack of will and entranced state, even the jaded Boston ruffian would have been driven to join those rambling psychotics in the bowels of Deerfield State. He rested in the midst of what can only be described as a morbid gallery of irreverent sights, a ghastly chamber encased on three sides by ‘walls’ reddish-brown in color veined with pallid yellow that pulsed erratically and pumped toxic fluids while intermittently spewing forth clouds of pernicious gases. A vile river of blood coursed the perimeter before passing from sight over the horizon, the walls of the grisly ‘room’ embellished with profane symbols recognizable by none but the most studied of occult scholars. Most hideous perhaps were the beasts inhabiting this foul land, the shambling fiends that haunt the night and the shadows and things which walked that ought to crawl. Bruce leveled his eyes forward, the final sight snapping him from his trance and dropping him to his knees while uttering a fervent prayer. Writhing in abyssal glory only several hundred yards beyond rested a colossal writhing mass of amorphous black matter, bubbling and oozing to the thin piping of the flutes that refused to die away. Dancing in a frenzy around this mass of pulpy flesh were a veritable army of alien beasts and lesser servitors, their chants echoing ceaselessly about the hellish chamber as they blasphemed to the tone of the accursed flute. Beyond this formless mass sat others ranked among those known as the Great Old Ones. Jack’s recent studies in the Mythos texts rendered him the recognition of the loathsome Chaugnar Faugn, the elephantine avatar called the ‘Horror of the Hills’, the twisted urchin-like form of sea-dwelling Glaaki and the burning mass of fiery plasma come to be known as Cthugha. Even these beings, demigods of such archaic root that mankind could not even comprehend, bowed before the revolting mass of flesh before them all. Bruce shivered in dread as his eyes fell on the vile blob, and only slightly detected the appearance of a familiar figure at his side, formed in the manifestation of an Egyptian pharaoh. He glared deep into the smoldering red eyes of he known as the Crawling Chaos as the arcane messenger protruded a bony finger ahead and just above the bubbling mass that was The Thing That Should Not Be, the blind idiot god Azathoth.
