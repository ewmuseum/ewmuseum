There is No Happiness Where There is No Wisdom

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Present Time “There is no happiness where there is no wisdom.” We always look for happiness but we can’t seem to find the factors that will make us obtain it. We listen and follow the human law, but whenever this one item is missing, happiness will never be obtained by simply obeying that edict. We listen and follow the divine law, but again, happiness will never be gained without this one attribute – wisdom. But how can we understand this citation if we don’t look at the words that composed them. Firstly, we must analyze the word “happiness”. Happiness by definition is an agreeable feeling or condition of the soul arising from good fortune or propitious happening of any kind; the possession of those circumstances or that state of being which is attended enjoyment; the state of being happy; contentment; joyful satisfaction; felicity; blessedness. In synopsis, being happy means you are content and satisfied. Now, we must explore the word “wisdom”. Wisdom by designation is the ability to discern or judge what is true, right, or lasting; insight; common sense; good judgment. So the question now is: how can you find contentment and satisfaction when wisdom is absent? There is absolutely no way. Just like a lady I know. She is seeking happiness but in the end, she will meet her demise. The equivalent goes for Dorn Tracer. But this girl is one in particular. She might not die along with the brave hearts that she loves, but without those priced beings, she will always have neither life nor substance. She might get the goals she is seeking and she might grow up with a treasure of accomplishments but how can she enjoy it when wisdom is not present? Just like in the magnificent and ostentatious play, Antigone, everyone was seeking happiness but in the end, everyone met his or her termination, including Creon, the great King of Thebes. And all of this came about because he was lacking wisdom. He could’ve used his common sense and good judgment but he stuck with his absurd laws instead of forbidding the burial of a man - a human just like he. Because of this, happiness was also lacking and Creon will never be able to acquire it until he founds that mandatory wisdom. The same goes for the other major characters: Haimon, Antigone, and Eurydice. But why am I talking about a play and not about this lady I’m about to face? The very same lady I know is seeking for happiness. Scattered Life as she said. Why? Because I say, sometimes, the works of literature is more precise and has a better understanding of life itself. This lady I’m about to face and her life revolves around a plot, just like in a movie or a play. She is seeking for contentment but it’s seems like it’s always misplaced. I’ve seen it many times. If she is listening to me right now, I would point her out to this very same line. “There is no happiness where there is no wisdom.” Agreement is in my mind. For one, it’s hard to get contentment if you can’t even judge what is right from what is wrong. King Creon tried to transcend the laws of the deities but enforcing his own law. If the decree is wrong, consequences will be attached into it. With that said, the end result of his misjudgment will lead to a more bigger misery than to begin with. For Creon’s case, it wasn’t just Polyneices and Eteocles that died, his own wife and son also perished because of his lack of wisdom. If Polyneices was buried in respect for the divine law, not for the human law, then everyone could have had a better existence. The second reason why I agree with this quote is because all it takes is common sense to separate right from wrong. Antigone knew that her deeds were right. And although she passed away, her wisdom will give her happiness in some form of life after her short time here on earth. I believe that good deeds are always awarded. And lastly, this citation is accurate because a wise man with the right knowledge and wisdom knows how to get happiness. Haimon knew in his heart that the only way he could reach satisfaction is by being with Antigone forever. When Antigone died, he knew what he must do. And although some people might say that it wasn’t the smartest thing to do, I believe that Haimon knew exactly what he was doing. The only thing that would make him happy is to be with Antigone. And that’s exactly what he did when he made her “his bride in the houses of the dead.” Haimon’s wisdom brought him to the realization that only in the afterlife will he be able to be beside Antigone, thus giving him the ultimate happiness. Now, I know you are lost. Do not agonize, my friend. You don’t even need to read and analyze the play – that’s my job as a dissimilar wrestler and an enthusiast of literature. The definitive understandings lies within our lives and our search for happiness, wisdom, and victory. In life, winning makes people happy, just like in the play, Antigone. Creon, just like the lady I’m about to face, wanted triumph but Antigone, just like I, wouldn’t accept defeat. In the end, it was wisdom that took over and made Antigone the victor and Creon as the defeated villain. Antigone reached the decisive happiness in the afterlife while Creon suffers greatly because of his lack of wisdom and insight in his present life. From these stories to our elderly history and all the way to the present world, it has always been the same theme. Wisdom leads to happiness. Happiness leads to victory. And victory goes back to wisdom to start the cycle all over again. All of this merged, creates the powerful line of “there is no happiness where there is no wisdom.” That is one fact of life that isn’t erratic. Now, Brianna ‘Gadget’ Washington, are you sure that’s wisdom?
