Bitchfest

Date: Sunday, for the ME tag match So he is an attention freak. He wants them to like them, but they won’t. Big deal; they’re supposed to like me and I’ll be the first one to say that they’re fickle as hell. He says that he doesn’t want to dwell in past. Incredible how he can self-contradict that way. The whole tone of his speech was dwelling on his past experiences. He was used to be something of a fan favorite. Whether in the Greatest Team Alive in the Bishop-era of NTICW or coming in as a Cruiserweight champion or being in Asylum… he was used to being cheered, whether he was supposed to or not. But he turned his back on them by turning on Jeremy Howard. And then they booed him. He didn’t expect that. Anything he did was supposed to be good and right. And with them behind him, he was supposed to be capable of anything. No, it didn’t work out that way. They booed him. They hated him. They saw his face and spat at him. Shouted profanities at the street. People were returning their merchandise by pelting him with it, people were wiping their fat asses with toilet paper with his face on every sheet. He had become one of them… One of the heels. He denied it at first, trying to prove to them that they were mistaken. He’d been in the right and they just weren’t seeing things properly. He had always sided with them before, and they him. Why should that change just because he exerted some independence from the then most beloved face in PCW and the world, for that matter? They weren’t buying it. They weren’t buying him. They weren’t buying his merchandise. The owner at the time, Tyler Nelson, hated anything that involved faces, and for that reason was all too happy to push him against Howard. Hell, he put Drake Maxwell against Howard just because he wanted Howard’s ass kicked. Was Maxwell anywhere near Howard’s league? Of course not, no one denied that. But he was a heel, and heels get their preferred booking no problem. That’s what he doesn’t understand. “Boo hoo, as a heel nobody likes me.” Fuck that. He still doesn’t understand how this business works despite somehow being a commissioner, CEO, and owner of the biggest and most popular wrestling federation ever. The fans, unfortunately for them and possibly for buyrates and for my own ratings and public approval, don’t mean shit. They have nothing to do with anything. Look at it like this. The fans like you. Hooray, you can walk around in public and get cheered and have people asking for your autograph and pictures to sell on E-Bay because they want to get something out of you. You owe them everything, you owe them more than you could possibly give back. You may not even be that nice of a guy, that pleasant to be around. But they designate you as a fan favorite and they own you. Apparently he likes to be owned. To be made the bitch of everyone around him. He should try prison sometime, but I digress. So what do you get for being a face, as it pertains to being within the industry? Nothing, really. The faces are there for fan reaction, but they get a positive fan reaction whether they’re jobbers or main eventers, so why give them the spotlight? If they manage to have skill, then all the better. Good for them. But odds are that it won’t matter. The people will pay to see you no matter where you are, so there’s no reason to just spontaneously put you in the main event, or even the upper card; even the upper mid-card. You’re just left behind, left to languish and stagnate while the people they hate are the ones getting all the big opportunities. He doesn’t realize that. It’s a principle of the business: HEELS GET PUSHED. If you’re a heel, that’s almost a guarantee for a title shot. You don’t even have to win, just stay a heel and you’ll float to the top of the ladder while everyone drowns from the flood of public support that actually holds them beneath their potential spot on the card. I’ve been a face in my career longer than I had been a heel, but I made all my biggest impacts mainly as a face, or at least just as a tweener. Why is that? The heels are supposed to be the bad guys. No one likes the heels, they’re supposed to be the ones that everyone pays to see lose, right? But deep down, they want the heels to win the big titles, to get to the top. That makes a face defeating them all the much sweeter. They’ll have more to lose than just a match. Hell, they won’t just be defeated, they’ll be dethroned. So based on that, the heels get pushed. There will never be a #1 Contendership match for any title anywhere ever that does not have a heel involved, even if they don’t win. It’s just a rule. The people don’t like you as a heel, but being a face didn’t help you climb the ladder. I’m fucking crying a river for you, man. I really am. It must be tough having to go through your professional life where everyone wants to see you fail, yet you’re pushed to succeed. It must be tough to purposefully isolate and alienate yourself from a normally accepting group of people because you’ve consistently gone out of your way to insult and anger them, and then bitch about them not kissing your feet and worshipping the ground you walk on. It must be tough not walking out of the house with an umbrella and raincoat even though you’re fully aware that you’ll be drenched with spit as soon as you enter public. I cry these tears for you. Because I pity your small mind. Don’t you ever fucking think that being a face is a walk in the park; that we don’t get our share of unnecessary adversity. It’s as much a cakewalk being a face as it is being a child molester trying to get a job in an elementary school. Harsh example, but it works. You’re a face, all the fans and marks and smarks and smartasses, reporters and interviewers and random Joe Blow, Joe Mama, Joe Bitchasscocksucker, and any other Average Joe and Just Joe—they all think you’re the greatest thing since sliced bread. So what when that means that everyone in the federation isn’t even indifferent to you, but they hate you and will do everything in their power to make sure that your professional life involves much more suffering, pain, and hardship than should ever be. You think that when I first signed into the business that there was a clause in my contract that said “Your girlfriend will be killed,” and I was all like ”Dude! Fuck yeah, I’ll take it!” and came along anyway? That’s all the extra shit that nobody tells you about when you’re in school or working out a contract. Rush Mason: Had nothing to do with him, but he tries to come and use me at my expense as a stepping stone or random point to make about how great he is. Could’ve picked anyone else, but I was there and between a heel and a face, he picked the face. They always do. Chef: I reached him first when the Continental Title was vacated since he’s a habit of doing such things (he vacated the World Title after beating Ivan Stanislav for it). I got to the title first, which were the rules of the match. But Chef decided that he, as a heel (which he apparently wanted to be as of whenever), it was necessary to screw over a face. He could’ve just walked away with it, but instead he denied me the title and gave it to Rush Mason. Totally Tubular Tommy: Has multiple wins against me, none of them being clean wins. Either interference from Harry or using the ropes or tricking the referee into thinking I hit him with a weapon when I didn’t. I’ve no actual way to address these grievances. Now, if he were a face and I a heel, fucking hell, there’d be an instant redress and the match would start up again or it would at least be erased from the records. But since I’m a face, the faces just have to accept that they were cheated and live with it. Violence Jack: Took away my home and family. Killed the woman I’d planned to marry. YOU THINK I WANTED THAT? “Sure, crack her skull open, it’ll do wonders for the complexion and it’s cheaper than Botox!” Sick ass freak. So wrapped up in your own self-pity that everything outside of you is just an abstract concept. There is no world other than you, is there? In your mind, the world began with you and it will end with you. You are hated by all, but have done more than most could ever aspire to. So what if you don’t get standing ovations whenever you walk out from behind that curtain? Don’t you fucking dare pretend as though you’ve a monopoly on human suffering. Don’t you dare act as those you don’t think about it everyday. Don’t you dare try to make another attempt as self-importance. Accept your station in life. Everyone else has. You’re Xavier Kannon. You’re an asshole. Deal with it.
