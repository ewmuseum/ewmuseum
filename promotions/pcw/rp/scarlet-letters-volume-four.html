Scarlet Letters...Volume Four

Date: Recent Past Highway to Heaven, Street To Hell 'Character Of A Happy Life' How happy he is born and taughtThat serveth not another's will;Whose armour is his honest thoughtAnd simple truth his utmost skill! Whose passions not his masters are,Whose soul is still prepared for death,Not tied unto the world with careOf public fame or private breath; Who envies none that chance doth raise,Of vice; who never understoodHow deepest wounds are given by praise;Nor rules of state but rules of good: Who hath his life from rumours freed,Whose conscience is his strong retreat;Whose state can neither flatters feed,Nor ruin make oppressors great; Who God doth late and early prayMore of his grace than gifts to lend;And entertains the harmless dayWith a well-chosen book or friend; -This man is freed from servile bandsOf hope to rise, or fear to fall;Lord of himself, though not of lands;And having nothing, yet hath all. -By Henry Wotton, 1568-1693. Dear Yuu, Our bokken flies true. Our mind is clear. Revenge should not be what we seek, we know it. We should be able to live in peace, without the need for any vengeance and destruction. We look back at our previous entries, and wonder at how we could have been filled with so much bitterness. Tyler Nelson and Chef, although a vile pair of men as ever, and not worthy to be masters to anyone, does not need to be the target of our wrath. This seems like a sudden change to us, though. Over the last few days, we wonder what it is that changed us so. We will try to write them down here. Although we have always thought of it, seeing it in words seems to give us a different perspective on our thoughts. That is why we write you letters. We want to see what our thoughts look like from a page. We want to read everything, to see what our past is like, what our thoughts are like, but most importantly, to learn from what we believed before. Our goal in our life is to improve. We work hard to hunt better, to practice harder, to focus stronger. Those are vigorous acts, yes, in both physical and mental ways, but we must also heal our mind of emotions that are never released. We must clean our thoughts, and keep them aside. It happened almost four days ago. When we were hunting with our Kendo sword, we was ready to strike at the deer in front of us when the deer suddenly ran away. We withheld our sword, upset at the loss, when a little girl tripped over a tree root and fell where the deer used to stand, crying. We put the bow away and stepped closer. We were horrified at what we saw; the girl was bruised beyond belief, having dark marks over her elbows, knees, shins, and shoulders. Her clothes were torn up and in tatters. She cried out to us as we stepped near, reaching for us as if we were a savior. We could not leave her there. Although we would have been happy chasing the deer down, we knew our conscience would not let us live had we left. We knelt over, withdrawing a cloth and our water skin so we could clean off her scrapes. To our dismay, her ankle was sprained and her leg was broken. Our past training was still fresh in our mind, though, so we made her a rough cast out of fallen branches. We picked her up in our arms. The girl was surprisingly light. She seemed about eight years or nine, perhaps. Our cave was not far from here, so we brought here there. With the little linen we had, we tried to clean off her open wounds and put pressure on the scrapes. She was still wailing when we managed to get her into our sleeping furs, but, thankfully for us, she was asleep by the time we got the fire started up to keep her warm. She seemed to have fallen down a cliff or something. There are many shallow hills and short ravines around our area, in fact, there was one very close to where she tripped over the root, and we reasoned that she must have fell down there, cutting and bruising herself along the way. We wondered where her parents were, but if they were not around by the time we reached her, then they would not have found her at all. At least, under our care, she would heal properly. It was morning then, and we although we did not have enough food to last two days; we withheld our hunt so we could stay by her side. She woke up that evening. We were still there, doing a sketch or writing a poem; we do not remember which one. When she saw our face under the firelight, her eyes grew wide with an emotion (terror perhaps?), but she did not shift from her spot. 'Don't try to move around,' we said to her. When we said those words, we was incredibly surprised at ourselves, for those were the first words that we spoke to another human in days. We were surprised at the sound of our own voice, but we tried not to relish in the thought of hearing it. The girl stopped. At that moment, we felt bad, so we reached into our pack for some jerky and offered it to her, as well as water from our canteen. She reached for it, cautious at first, but then accepted our gift and was eating madly. We then took the time to show her the makeshift cast that we made for her. Her leg was broken and she would not be unable to walk until she got a decent cast and a pair of crutches. 'How are you feeling?' we asked her. 'Fine,' she replied. She sipped at the water. We went to grab some of our pottery so we could make her a cup of herbal tea. As we did, she eyed us, then asked, 'Are you Akiyama?' We looked back at her, our eyes fixed on hers. She shrank back, we was staring at her so hard. 'Yes,' we eventually said to her. 'They said you were dead.' It was a true statement, we supposed. We did die, after all, and was in the Void. We could feel the pain in our gut, the heat of the burning negativity that overwhelmed a being submerged in darkness... we shudder at the thought of it. We always try to push it to the back of our mind. We didn't reply to her, and instead placed our jar of water over the fire. 'Do you want some tea?' we asked. She nodded at us. 'Thank you, Mister Akiyama,' she replied. We looked at her, then said, 'Call us Akiyama.' We both watched the water heat for a few minutes, before she asked, 'Akiyama, why are you alive, if everyone else said you were dead?' We had no response for her, actually. Normally, people who die stay dead, without ever rising from their afterlife. When we rose from death we were swept up onto the shore, wearing full clothing, our equipment, and our sword. We wondered why that was, but we never ever found an answer. So we told her, quite honestly, 'We don't know why we am still alive. It may have been a mistake.' She must have taken it as a mistake that society made, when we meant that it was a mistake that we was revived. She said, 'Why don't you go tell everyone, then?' We shook our head. 'We don't want them to know. Can you keep it a secret for us?' The girl nodded. We then moved the pot, added in the leaves, and poured her a cup. She seemed to enjoy it very much as she curled around the fire, sipping a warm cup. The tea would help heal her leg as well. As she finished, she curled up and fell asleep. We knew there was nothing outdoors to fear, so we put the fire out, covered the entrance up, and then fell asleep ourselves. When we woke, she was still sleeping, so we decided to go out and try a hunt. It was early in the morning, and she still seemed tired from last night. We decided to return in an hour. When the hour was up, we had not managed to catch much, only a rabbit. But the rabbit would certainly feed two people for a day to come. She was a very patient, very tolerant girl. When we brought the rabbit in and began to skin and gut it, she made no sound or protest when we thought she might have. We looked toward her, tempted to ask her if it annoyed her. But we reasoned that if it did, she would have to speak up against it. We checked her leg over that afternoon, and found that it was in the right position. It was a clean break, luckily for her, and although she was in pain, and crying when she thought we couldn't hear her, she took it very well. We ate well that evening. We suppose that she was a more tolerant guest than most. Instead of judging us by what Nelson and the other players of society said, she decided to see for herself, and apparently, she liked what she saw. She stayed by choice; once, a group of hunters came close to the cave and all she needed to do was call out had she wanted to leave. The girl was an artist, somewhat, and drew very excellent sketches. We lent her a charcoal and within minutes, she had a lifelike picture of the fire and the surrounding crockery. We used to think ourselves a fair artist before we met her, but she put our light sketches to shame. We discussed art for a long time that evening, about looking, drawing, and how lines were made. She was our teacher that day, and as she taught us some of the finer points of art, the sacrifice of our time to help her had already paid off. It had paid off in more ways than personal gain, however. She was a strong girl who carried herself well, knew what she was speaking about, and was able in mind and spirit. When we saved her, we didn't intend to get anything out of it. But now that we did rescue her and tend for her wounds, there was an extra bonus handed out to us, in addition to the warm feeling that serving good does. We made a friend. Her name was Grace. We found it a fitting name; her tolerance for us was a virtue. Despite the horrid stories that went about us, being a cold man, a harsh man, she sought to learn more of us. She found it strange that we did not ask for her name for almost three days. We had learned her name only because she told it to us. As we went into the third day, she was beginning to ask us about our letters, which she had seen on the ground. During her stay, we did not write our letters (which is why we are writing this now) so we thought it surprising that she would ask. So we went into the book and read her one of the days we had written in the book. When we were done, she seemed both awed and bored at the same time, or perhaps it was just confusion. Her eyes were wide but her mouth was set in a straight line, as if trying to digest the information that we had just presented to her. She didn't say anything about it after we read it to her, and then went back to eating her breakfast. The passage we read to her was about being your own entity, to control yourself and not to let anyone else control you. By that evening, she was able to walk (with assistance, of course, from both a pair of makeshift crutches and our shoulder), and we went on a short trip for fresh air when we saw flashlights and heard voices. From the loud, inconsiderate crunch of the grass under the boots of the new arrivals, we judged them to be city goers. The name 'Grace' was being called over and over again. She looked back at us, tilted her head ever so slightly toward the bushes, and then nodded at us, motioning for us to go. At that moment, we thought of her as more mature than she looked. If she is actually a nine-year-old girl, we would be surprised. Her mannerisms resemble someone who is fourteen. We never ever had a chance to learn her age. We nodded back at her, lifted our arm in slight salute, and then put her gently on the ground. We stalked off into the retreat of the black woods without a sound. Grace began to call out to the wandering patrollers. We did not look back, but we could hear the voices loudly; her parents and the police officers that were looking for her had fulfilled their objective. We could hear the happiness of the sobs and the tight, worried voices of the cops. The noise drowned out Grace, though, and we could not hear what she said. Apparently, she had not mentioned our name. We went back to our cave, feeling a bit of pain, but the dominant feeling in our heart was one of fulfillment. The bowls of soup and stew on the cave floor lay, as they usually did, but the difference was that there were two of them. We miss Grace. It would have been good for us if she had never left. But we're glad we met her. And we're not sad that she left. We have almost everything we need. We can live by ourselves. Our mind is free. Best of all, we can live with ourselves. Unhindered by the outside world, we have learned what the human soul can do if the heart is followed. As we write, we think of all of those that wish to do us harm, Nelson included. Perhaps it is foolish for us to seek him. What good would it do us? It would not enlighten us; it would not force a good deed on the people. Let them deal with their own problems. Be tolerant. Grace has taught us that. We have to tolerate the behavior, before we can go out and change it. We used to write about preparation. We used to think it was preparation for the destruction of the President, but now, we've learned that it was a lot more. It is the preparation of the self. We improve, not in body, so we can kill, nor in mind, so we can outwit. The sword is but a tool we use to practice. We do not need it. It serves only to kill. The shedding of blood is but a path to death. We do not need it. It serves only to kill. We improve the soul. So we can find inner peace. For, as long as we are content with ourselves, as long as we know we am good, we will be happy. But people like Grace, who come along and make our life brighter, turn a happy life into bliss. Happiness comes in many forms. But eternal bliss is really rare. ** Akiyama **
