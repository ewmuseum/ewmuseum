What Ever Happened To Ed?

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01.11.03 (#1 TT) After five months of pain and agony, Abner is back out on the road, and back in the PCW. Ready to kick ass. Shifting his rig into sixth, Abner floors it, jerking the economy-sized, gas-guzzling, tree-killing hog onto the interstate, smiling as he whips past other, lesser cars, peeling along the guardrails, barely able to maintain control. But he's got it, baby. Life in the fast-lane. Just the way he likes it. Upon hearing of his return to PCW, Abner was overcome by an overwhelming emotional cyclone of unthankful annoyingness, for having been forced to leave to begin with, anticipation, and relief. Nervousness was pushed aside by an all but confident brand of readiness, daringness, and determination. He prepared hard. Harder than ever before, and took lectual advice to heart. He trained daily, ate healthy, and lost weight. He now schedules his time appropriately and conservatively, allowing himself no more but a few hours of guilty pleasuring a day (save beer time, which, of course, is included as part of his training schedule). He is wise, reflective, and concise. And still a drunk. Instead of looking at the road ahead of him in such a wide-open view, he's smart in limiting his challenges and focusing his resources, which he now has many. Instead of veteran-esque nostalgia, he takes part in a learn-from-what-mistakes-I-made-in-the-past reflective operation of sorts, watches old videos, and takes notes. And downs whiskey. Sometimes, stepping back from the screen, Budweiser in hand, he shakes his head in disgust and whispers, 'I can't believe I did that,' or 'could've done better,' and such. And so, after coming back to the sport he loves, and after a quick, successful stint in the company he loves, Abner has once again been considered for Director of Redneck Affairs. He received a call earlier this morning from his old buddy Duke’s office, and things seem to be falling into place rather smoothly. But will he get the job? And if he does, can he handle it? We’ll see. But that’s not all. Next week, at Tuesday Turmoil, he makes his third bid for the Extreme Title, a charm he has yet to get his mitts on. But you know what they say. The third time’s a charm. And so it shall be. But let’s not get too far ahead of ourselves here. With Shaun Styles up next on his plate for Turmoil, Abner doesn’t want indigestion by taking on too much with too little stomach room. (I mean, there is the daily beer keg.) How do you eat and elephant, a man once asked. Small bites. But what of Jacques? And what of Cuzin Ed? And of course, what of the tortured souls of the gay trio who trounced on Abner’s dignity day in and day out since he’s come back to the company? No matter for them. For after Abner’s done chewing his food in the ring, he’ll have his desert, too. Yes, if he does in fact decide to go back to being Director of Redneck Affairs under the Duke Administration, Jacques and his homosexual cronies will get theirs… But is Cuzin Ed okay? Well, last Abner checked, he was being shipped to a minimal security prison, where he waits another trial, and possibly more indictments on money-laundering and drug charges. But so be it. He’s not Abner’s responsibility. And as for the drug man, well, Abner never made the initial delivery. As a matter of fact, he never even delivered the package. Instead, he simply called back, said he couldn’t do the job, and never looked back. The man was nice enough about. Though of course, a wee bit disappointed. But that’s okay. He said he “somewhat” understood, whereas Abner painfully took up the task of making him fully understand, to no avail. But with him becoming DRA again, Abner would certainly have the salary to at least lone his cousin the bail, if not pay the bill off completely. Of course, in some way, he’ll be expected to pay him back. Abner feels no monetary responsibility toward Ed, and also no moral responsibility, as he promptly refused to take up his grandmother’s suggestion to “talk him out of it,” though Abner, if asked, will certainly be honest with him. But then again, it would be great to have Cuzin Ed back in his corner… Abner slows down, putting on the breaks of the truck. Now, he’s caught in the traffic of the big city, and he takes this opportunity to think back to an earlier day, if he remembers, his last moment with Ed… ~~~The violent roar of the chainsaw erupts through the forest as Abner saws through the tree, bringing it to its knees, crumbling to the ground. Satisfied, he flips the protective eye-cover off his yellow hard hat, tossing it, along with his gloves, over his shoulder. Abner looks down at his fallen foe and smiles approvingly. He sets down his chainsaw and takes a seat on a log. It's muggy. He uses a red handkerchief, which he pulls out of the pocket of his hunting jacket, to wipe the sweat off his brow. He blinks. The evergreens of the deep pinewood forest are in a haze, the Christmasy branches dancing around him, distorted by the heat. He takes a bit of a sandwich and chases it down with a beer. A full beer. The sandwich, still half-wrapped in plastic, seems to be melting under the midday sun, too. The thin yellow American cheese has combined with the warming mayonnaise to form a sticky sauce of paste for which the thick slices of ham float upon. The sandwhich is cut diagonally forming two even triangles of fluffy white bread, just the way Abner likes it. Abner finishes off the sandwich. And pulls out another. And eats that too. And downs three beers. Satisfied, he wipes the crumbs off from his lips with a dirty, greasy hand, semi-politely, and stands up, grabbing his stomach, and belches. In the distance, a man laughs, and a bigger, heavier-set figure comes into view. It’s Cuzin ED. “Damn it, boy, you sure gots one helluva a gut on ya!” Ed marches up and playfully punches Abner in the ribs. Abner, reluctant to respond, stands there smirking, taking in the heat of the midday son. He’d usually be quick to retaliate, even competitive in play, not wanting his younger cousin to get the best of him, especially since Cuzin Ed has been getting tougher over the years, and is even considering joining and indy fed in the near future. “Yes sir,” Ed says, jokingly, “that’s one helluva beer-belly…and just think,” he goads his cousin, “if it weren’t for the fact that you’re such a lazy pussie, you could work it off in a real man’s job!” Abner, thoroughly offended, takes a step forward. He knows Ed’s only joking, logging being one of the most physically challenging of occupations, but still, it hurts. So half-playfully/half-angrily, he strikes out, catching Ed underneath the chin. “Ow!” Ed screams out, jumping a step back. “Man, that really hurt!” So the younger, heavier redneck lunges out, catching Abner unawares in the gut again, this time harder. And this time, it hurts. So Abner winds up, sidesteps another of Ed’s half-assed, blows, and pow, drives him square in the chest, knocking him on his ass, laughing all the while.~~~ And that’s when it fell out. A small, clear bag. The smile on Abner’s face disappeared, and as his cousin followed Abner’s vision, turning to look in the direction Abner did, “hey, what is it?”, he saw the now present bag, and he lost his grin as well. “Oh Well,” Abner sighs, shifting back into sixth, “that’s what that shit’ll do to ya.”
