Interview with the Devil: Crazy Taxi (Part 7) RP # 2 for Turmoil

25 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-25-03 Rargh! Sometimes things just make you wan to jump and down and scream! You can’t though, because you’re in the middle of the public and you’re trying to keep them from realizing you’re insane. I mean, well, not insane, but getting there. I’m sure Shane is asking himself right now if he is going crazy or if this is really happening to him, and I know that’s probably one of the reasons he is acting the way he is. The fact that he doesn’t know and it keeps happening, it almost reminds me of this movie I watched once, A Beautiful Mind? Yeah, that’s it. The things seemed so real in that movie, but they all ended up not being real, could this be the same thing that is happening to Shane? Or am I giving too much thought into this whole process of analysis? There are so many things you really must take into consideration before you come to a conclusion with Shane though. While what we’ve seen thus far can only lead us to conclude that he is a raving lunatic, he is quite the spiritual man and this has to be the work of Satan on his life. What better way than to make Shane think he is going crazy than to actually drive him over the edge? It’s a sad thing, and yet it is almost brilliant in a way as well. I guess the only real way to find out anything is to continue our story. Shane sits in the back of the taxi, eyes closed, and rubbing his hands together. Stressed almost completely out of his mind; the driver looks in the mirror and with a smile asks, “Where to bub?” “South Amboy,” Shane replies with a shot voice, “Just trying to go home.” Shane realizes he is okay now and just lies back in his place, but he can still be seen shaking from all that has happened. The driver continues taking a peek at Shane every now and again, until he finally speaks, “Looks like you’ve had a pretty rough day, bub.” Shane smiles a bit with that comment and places a hand upon his head, “You probably wouldn’t understand the half of it.” Shane releases a bit of a chuckle. “Well, I’ve heard a lot of stories before,” the driver says as he places his arm across his chair, “I am a New Jersey taxi cab driver, I’m sure there isn’t a story that has been told that I haven’t heard.” The driver chuckles a bit himself, “The only people that have it worse than me are the guys who drive in New York.” Shane laughs a bit with the driver at that comment, “You wouldn’t happen to be a religious man, would you?” The driver becomes a tad bit silent, as if to be thinking, “Well, I don’t really know what to say about that one.” The driver continues to think a little bit, “I attend church on Sundays, but I’ve never really viewed myself as someone who was really religious. Why do you ask?” “You see,” Shane straightens himself up in the backseat, “I am someone that you would deem to be a very religious man. I actually work for the Vatican somewhat.” “Oh, that place where the Pope works over in Italy or something?” The driver cuts Shane off to ask that question with a bit of enthusiasm. Shane shakes his head a bit in disbelief at the comment, “Yes, exactly where the Pope works, but you see my problem is that I’ve got too much pressure on me right now. If you were more of a religious man you might understand the battle I’m going through.” The driver looks in his mirror, at Shane, and with confused eyes asks, “But if you’re working for those people how can anyone battle you? Aren’t they like the mafia of the religious world?” Shane lies back in the chair once again, “Well, I guess you could say we’re like the good mafia fighting against an evil far worse than anyone other in this world. That evil is what I’m battling right now, as hard as that may sound to believe.” “Well, I don’t guess it’s so hard to believe when that evil is sitting right in front of you.” The driver says with a snicker much like the one Shane heard before. Shane’s face grows pale once again as he shakes his head in disbelief now, “What did you say?” The snickering grows louder as the driver turns and the face on the man is of sheer horror, a site that would cause any man to faint, “You’re personal ride to hell is here, Shane. Are you ready to pay the fee?” The driver turns around once more, rearing his head to the sky snickering louder and louder. “No!” Shane says as he grabs at the door handle and tries to open it frantically, “You’re not here! No! Leave me be!” “You’re not getting out, Shane. You’re stuck with me now!” The driver says yelling in an evil tone and laughing even more as he watches Shane try to escape. Then suddenly the driver looks back at the road and boom! The car comes to a crashing hault as it collides with a tree. For a few moments both Shane and the driver do not move. Shane’s head bleeding from the impact of hitting the mesh metal that was separating him and the driver. The once closed door is now open, and Shane realizes this once he comes to and begins to slow slide himself out. Shane begins to pull himself slowly up to his feet on another tree located not five feet from the crashed taxi. Shane begins rubbing his head to take away most of the blood flowing into his face. After regaining his composure, Shane slowly makes his way over to the taxi and looks in to see the driver staring right back out at him with an evil smile. “You’re time has come, Shane.” The driver says with an evil glare, “The master has come for you and you will not succeed us, you will fall!” Shane begins to step back gasping a bit, “No!” “Yes,” the driver says, “You are ours and you will not escape us next time. You have been warned.” With that being said the driver and the taxi both disappear from the area. Shane continues to panic as he reaches for his forehead and realizes he is still bleeding very badly. He then turns around and notices he is back in South Amboy now, but not one of his favorite places. Shane begins to walk by several tombstones as he tries to find his way out of the St. Mary’s Cemetery. Looking back down the path that the taxi traveled, he realizes that they had been coming through the cemetery for quite some time, most likely during the whole time that the driver went psycho on him. Shane takes one final breath and just flops down next to a tombstone and rests for a bit. Slowly we make our way around the tombstone that Shane is rested at and we see wrote on it, “Charlotte and Harold Robertson” and the scene fades to black. ~Series To Continue~
