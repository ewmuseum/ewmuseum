Date: .... The sun was about to rise over Dallas, and it was the time the real early risers woke up for a morning coffee and to get dressed. Not many were up because it was real early, but life was on the streets. Mostly drivers, occasionally walking pedestrians, but only one of them was running. A man with long black hair run into view, Eddy of course. He was breathless and he put his hands on his knees and sat down on a bus stop bench. He was in clothes that were open to the cold wind. A short sleeve shirt and jeans, in 20 degrees. Fog was coming out of his mouth as his panted. He looked, the sun was about to rise. It was a very cool occasion, having the feeling of coming back to your old home town. But, he was here by luck you could say. A trucker was driving to Atlanta, where his parents were supposedly at in a hospital. Of course, when the trucker fell asleep at a rest stop, he ran out into Dallas to see his sister. She could give him answers if his parents were dead/dying. Eddy then dashed off through downtown. He knew where his parents/brother/sister lived exactly, even after a year of isolation. He dashed off ahead, tears in his eyes, flying in wind, making his face colder then it was, as he recalled everything in this city. He was turning numb it was so cold. He arms were freezing. But he had to get to his sisters before she went to work, if she did. She, a year earlier, was a waitress for a Mexican food restaurant and a student. It was Saturday, and she went to work on Saturdays in the mornings. Things may be the same or changed. It didn’t matter. He recognized the Blockbuster, the Subway, the McDonalds, the movie theater. However, he remembered seeing a Wendy’s between the Blockbuster and McDonalds, but there was a Taco Bell there then. He remembered also seeing a cell phone company, now turned into a Chinese restaurant. The suburbs were ahead. His sister was close. 4000-something Silver Street. Eddy address out, but it was there. Then he remembered. When he called the house, nobody 'moved'. His parents didn’t move. They were dying, and his sister had disconnected the phone because she didn’t have the money to support paying or was too depressed to answer. Whichever, she probably only answered the door, because Skylar had said he was in town and came over and was told his parents were dying by Eddy’s sister. When Skylar came to pick Eddy up from his trashy home in Wyoming to take his back to PCW, he never mentioned it until he was at a truck stop. Then Eddy run off with a trucker to Atlanta, and stopped here. God Eddy was tired. But he was nearing Silver Street. Just minutes later, Silver Street was in front of him. In the 4000’s too. He looked, took one glance at a house, and knew it was his. The sun was up. Normal people were waking up for coffee and dressing, and it was a rather gray day. He looked around, and saw what was looking for. A house. 2 stories, wooden and brown. 4352. It was Melissa’s. He walked up to it, and closed his eyes, and knocked. Nothing happened. He knocked again. Nothing happened. 'Melissa, it’s me! Eddy!' Noises were going on in there, and finally, a very beautiful Hispanic girl about 16, who had long black hair with a burnt-orange/reddish tint to it opened, wearing a red blouse and a black skirt. She dropped her jaw. 'Eddy!' The girl fell into Eddy’s arms and Eddy gave her a hug. She was crying on his shirt, and he was smiling a smile. Both were joyous to see each other. After a second, she got off. She smiled. 'Eddy, it’s been…Over a year?' 'Yeah…' 'Wow…' In between them, the feeling of sibling love felt so true then. It was the greatest moment in the past year Eddy had ever had. 'Come in…' Eddy nodded and stepped in. It smelt good, maybe her perfume, or an air freshener, but it smelt good, like a garden. It was dark inside. She flipped the lights on. It was a little different. The first room you entered was the dining room, and the table was turned differently. The kitchen was right beside it. Behind the dining room, you could walk into the living room. The couches were in a different form and the TV was also different. He couldn’t quite remember before, but it was different. 'Let’s talk over a cup of coffee…' 'Okay.' He sat at the dining table. In front of him was a landscape painting of a lake, and on the other side there was houses. A boat was in the water, and the water was sparkly blue. He remembered it. He looked around the dining room, seeing paintings he remembered and didn’t remember. Quickly, his sister got two mugs of coffee and sat one in front of him and sat on the other side and stirred hers. He took a sip. It was mildly creamy, just like he liked it. He sister didn’t forget him. 'Well, why are you now in town?' 'To talk.' 'I know that…' 'About mom and dad. Are they still alive?' 'Why…yes, how’d you find out they were dying?' 'A good friend of mine named Skylar told me.' 'Ah…Skylar.' She paused and took a sip of her coffee. It was steaming hot, and so was Eddy’s, but it felt good on his cold throat and body. 'Listen, Melissa…Are you sure they are living still?' 'Yes. Sadly, they may die soon…' 'I have to get to Atlanta!' 'Listen, before we get off in this convo, where have you been?' 'Broke. In Wyoming. In nowhere city. No car, no money, and I couldn’t reach this house. I had assumed you guys had moved.' 'I disconnected myself. I didn’t want any phone calls, I only used my cell phone for my boyfriend, my best friends…ETC…' 'Yeah. Listen, I have to get to Atlanta to see my parents!' 'Eddy, calm down. Tell me the rest of the story.' Eddy sighed. 'Can you turn on the TV?' Melissa flipped it on. The TV was visible from the dining room. It was early morning news program time. Eddy hadn’t watched TV in a long time. 'Now, please finish.' 'My friend Skylar somewhere reached me, and told me PCW wanted me back. I had dropped out to strive for more, but I got flat broke and ended up in a slump until he pulled me out', right after saying this, Eddy took a sip of his coffee. 'So anyway, he picked me up. We drove to a truck stop, then he let me on the news my parents were dying. When he told me they were in Atlanta, I saw a trucker going to Atlanta. We drove until he stopped here and fell asleep, and I ran into town to find you.' 'Listen. When mom and dad got in a car wreck, my life sucked then and now. I had to take care of Hunter and I’m 16! I had school, I had work, and nobody supported me until I got my boyfriend’s help. He was a lifesaver. Both of us are in a lot of stress now…' Eddy sat his mug down. 'Melissa, I have to get to Atlanta!!!' 'Eddy, be calm…They won’t be dead for at least another month, if they do die. Check up then. Go wrestle until then Eddy, you’re making a comeback.' Eddy wasn’t making eye contact with her when she said this. He was staring at his mug, thinking. 'OK…I just hope they live…' 'Don’t we all? Eddy, you’ll be fine. You were always a proud, passionate person. You have self-faith, you have it all. Wrestle, I’ll be OK, mom and dad will be OK, and Hunter will be OK.' Hunter, if it wasn’t found out already, was Eddy’s little brother. 'Well, OK…Where is Hunter? Upstairs?' 'Yes.' 'Ah. Anyway, I’ll see you. I have to figure what to do now….' 'Wait.' '….Why?' 'I have to give you something…' Eddy got up along with his sister and followed her into the kitchen. She opened up one drawer, and pulled out a present wrapped in brown paper. He looks at it. To:EddyFrom: Mom and Dad. 'Wow…' 'They had this wrapped for your 19th birthday, before they got in the accident…' The present was big and rectangle. He stared at it. 'Open it!', said Melissa, smiling. Eddy dug his finger underneath a folder a rip it, ripping tape with it. He pull it apart from then, and what was there was a big white box. He lifted the top. There was numerous stuff in there, but the first thing his said was a big photo album, marked 'Photos' in marker on the cover. He lifted it, and looked inside. He flipped through, seeing pictures of him wrestling, seeing him young, seeing him old, seeing him in all sorts of situations. He glanced and smiled. 'Amazing…' Melissa smiled. Eddy look down in the box again, and saw a neatly folded black shirt. He glanced at this, and lifted it up. On the front was 'Young Tiger' in orange letters on the chest, and 'PCW' in blue letters on the stomach. In the middle, was a tiger’s face, with his eyes brightly showing and glimmering. He turned it over, and 'Eddy Ramirez' was written. He smiled. 'I didn’t even know I had a shirt on the market.' 'Yeah…' He down at the box again, and saw a slender jewelry box was there. He opened it and looks. There was a necklace, and he pulled it out. It was a gold necklace, and had a cross. He looked at it, and flipped it over. The other side had his name engraved. 'My name is engraved!' 'Wow! Can I see?' He handed the necklace to her and looked at the rest of the stuff inside. He saw a couple of books inside. He pulled them out. One of them was 'It' by Stephen King, the other was 'Shogun' by James Clavell. Eddy had mentioned wanting these books before. 'I’ve always walked these!' Eddy set them down and looked inside and saw the last gift. It was simple, but effected Eddy: A coffee mug with Young Tiger on it. Eddy smiled. 'Wow…These gifts are amazing.' 'I know. They’re pretty simple too.' 'But effective. I will cherish these forever.' 'I have a gift too…' Eddy smiled. 'Follow me.' Eddy followed her outside, and she pulled a pair of keys from her pocket. She pressed a button, and the brown garage opened, and out came his red Durango, his regular car. 'Wow! My car!' 'Yeah. I drove it a little, but I have my own car.' Eddy got the keys, and turned off the alarm. He walked straight up to it and opened to door. 'It’s as good as new! Thanks!' 'I knew you’d like it…' 'I do! Now, I must be going, I wish I could give you something…' 'No need to.' Eddy nodded and shut his door. He rolled down his windows and turned on the truck. He smiled brightly, and drove out, waving to his sister. 'Bye!' 'Bye…' Then Eddy was gone in the fog.
