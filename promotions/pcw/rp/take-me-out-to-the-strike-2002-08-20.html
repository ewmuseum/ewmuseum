Take me out to the....strike?

20 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8-20-02 The headline read: “Baseball players set strike date for Aug. 30th” and my heart sank to the ground. I was filled with all different kinds of emotion: Why are you doing this?What a bunch of assholes they are!Overpaid idiots!Did they forget who fills the stands? Did they forget where merchandising, ticket revenue, television revenue and some money in their contract come from? There have been nine work stoppages in Baseball since 1970. The most recent was in 1994. They are supposed to be “America’s Pastime” but where are they now? A year shy from the horrific day that was known as 9/11 and they cannot give their fans the right of entertainment? Giving the fans another great October spectacle? No we got to bitch and moan about the salary cap so we can get more money, raising ticket prices, food prices and merchandise prices through the roof because we don’t care about the average fan’s hard earn money. My grandfather and father are hardcore New York Yankee fans. My grandfather can remember Babe Ruth playing, Lou Gehrig’s farewell speech and Joe DiMaggio going fifty-six consecutive games with a hit. My father remembers the summer of sixty-one where Roger Maris and Mickey Mantle battled to sixty-one homeruns. He remembers Reggie Jackson hitting three homeruns on three at bats in one game in the World Series. Inside and outside there body is covered in pinstripes, the New York Yankee colors. As a kid I remember them taking me to a weekend game because my grandfather had season tickets in the lower level, eight rows back from third base, they were some of the greatest seats in the world. He would take my father and I too weekend games while on weekdays him and some of his poker buddies would go on the weekdays. It was great filled with loads of baseball fun and everything then was, get this cheap compared today. Hotdog: (Then) $1.00Hotdog: (Now) $4.50 – $5.00 Crackerjacks: (Then) .50 centsCrackerjacks: (Now) $3.50 Soda: (Then) 1.00Soda: (Now) $3.50 - $4.00 Who do you blame for these sudden raise in prices? The players. The first million dollar player was Texas Rangers pitcher Nolan Ryan and I believe that contract was signed in the eighty’s. Now let’s go to two decades later, in the year 2001. At the time shortstop of the Seattle Mariners was Alex Rodriguez and at the end of the year he would become a free agent, telling everyone that he was going to test the free agent waters. Rumors swirled on where he would land? Will he be a Met? Will he come back to the Mariners? Will he join Junior in Cincinnati? He finally landed in a new home, the Texas Rangers. The same team that paid Nolan Ryan the first ever one million dollar contract. They also became the very first sport franchise to pay a baseball player two hundred and fifty million dollars over nine years, making him have the biggest salary for any athlete in America. The only other person that makes more money then him in sports in Formula One driver, Michael Schumacher who makes thirty five million dollars a year. A-Rod’s salary made the Texas Rangers revenue skyrocket, jacking up prices in every field of their stadium and stores throughout the Texas area. His salary and many other Baseball salaries have caused stadiums across the country to raise prices to an all-time high which hurts the average hard working American baseball fan to not be able to see a baseball team live. Here is the run down of a trip to a local ballpark:(Depending where you park) Parking: $5.00 - $15.00(Depending on how many people go) Tickets: $25.00 - $65.00(Hotdog, Soda, Crackerjacks) Food: $11.50 plus tax.(Baseball cap, Baseball Shirt, Foam finger) Merchandising: $65.00 plus tax Estimated money spent: $131.50 plus tax that isn’t added on. Also depends if you have children or not. For the average working person that wants to go to a baseball game this makes your wallet look like it just went on a diet. Spending that much money for a nine inning game where the players don’t even go all out anymore, the traffic, gas money, listening to the drunks flap there gums on how there so much better then the baseball players and sitting next to a overweight person isn’t worth going to a Baseball game anymore. I’d rather watch it on my couch drinking a beverage that was a reasonable price, being able to go a clean bathroom and being able to see the whole field is much better then seeing it live now. It’s very sad for me to say this because I used to go to Yankee games at Yankee stadium all the time as a kid, but in the early ninety’s my grandfather couldn’t keep up with the price on the season tickets, we tried helping him out but it just wasn’t enough. They overpriced my grandfather out of the game he loved; bless him that he is still alive and healthy to watch the games still on television but I can tell it’s not the same for him. He loved being able to smell the hotdogs and now it’s all gone. He knows that a strike in the game he loves might be happening and ever since he heard it he just shakes his head when it is talked about, on the news or at the dinner table, he just sits there in a deep dark stare shaking his head, awestruck that the players would turn there back on the fans especially the devoted fans like my grandfather. How would they like it when they finally come back from strike (if it happens) and there are no fans in the stands that they went on strike? Then how would they feel? They should realize how the fans feel before they strike because if they strike again, a lot of fans will turn there back on the game…for good. They are making it sound like the fans don’t deserve to watch them play for the rest of the year, that there priorities come first over the people that put money in their pockets and that really pisses me off cause I’m a die hard baseball fan that feels like they stabbed me in the back and twisted the knife around for the last time. I guess you could call it the “final cut.” Coming from the wrestling profession you don’t see us striking do you? No we because our fans are important to us and realize that they put food on the plate. Cause unlike the professional baseball players today we are real people. It’s taken me a long time for the big time and I worked my ass off getting paid twenty-five dollars a show in the independent federation, trying to keep myself financially stable. But they have had everything fed to them and they just don’t care so they screw every one of their supporters along the way. Well this is the last straw, they will break the camels back if they strike and they will unleash a fury that will never be repaired ever again. The knife has been twisted for the last time and the fans will not put up with another strike. Just remember without any fans, sports athletes are nothing.
