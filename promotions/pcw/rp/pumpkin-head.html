Date: 06.06.03 You’ve all heard the legend of Pumpkinhead. Ya know, the mythological demon of rural folklore who rights the wrongdoings of victims, by seeking revenge and punishing the perpetrators in cruel and unusual ways. Well that’s all fine n’ dandy. Cause across small towns throughout the nation, young farmers, rednecks and hicks alike have feared the wrath of this mysterious creature, and it has kept them in their place. Mark my words, many a cold New England night, even the roughest and toughest of the types-why even Abner himself-have woken up a cold pool of sweat, shivering at the thought of meeting that creature. But few, if any, outside the community of Hicksville, Vermont have heard the legend of Pumpkin-Head. Yes, that’s right. No, not “Pumpkinhead”…”Pumpkin-Head”. (See, with a dash in the middle?) Now, now, I know what you’re all thinking? “What’s the difference”, right? Well, ha! I’ll tell ya what the difference is! So listen up: Now the series of events I’m about to relate to you are true. This tale-the story of a young, scarred-face, teenage porntstar, who just happened to be the teenage daughter of a senior town selectmen-is true. It’s all true. Every word of this story, every syllable, no matter how stressed or stretched it may seem, is as solid as a rock…and no, not the “Old Man on the Mountain” rock, but as solid and secure as a redneck’s love of the Cold One. Every character, living or dead now, is as real as the sunshine on a hot summer day…well, except for the ones that coincide with a solar eclipse, and so the day is not actually sunny, but anyway, much, much more real than Britney’s boobs. The house, unattended for nearly twenty years, was falling to pieces and was sold dirt cheap. Mr. O’Neil was a successful businessman from the automobile industry, and recently had moved up from some rich, yuppie town in central Massachusetts. At the ripe old age of 42, he was all but ready to retire having already made his fortune. And oh, yes, there’s another thing about Mr. O’Neil: He also happened to be a father. Yes, that’s right; he had a beautiful seventeen year-old daughter named Casandra. So beautiful, in fact, that one time she was the runner-up at Ms. Massachusetts pageant. So it goes that six or seven years ago, on the first day the O’Neils’s moved in, the family was quickly made to feel…unwelcome. Abner remembers the day vividly.
