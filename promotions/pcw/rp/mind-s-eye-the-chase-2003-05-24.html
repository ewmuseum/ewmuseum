Mind's Eye: The Chase

24 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/24/03 Guard: Hey! Get back here! Tania ran as fast as her legs could take her. She tried to sneak in wearing light blue shawls over her body, but the security guards are informed that in Iraq, women haven’t been required to cover every inch of their bodies like in other Middle Eastern countries. For her to be masked from head to toe was rather suspicious, but she didn’t know. She couldn’t have known. “Yo lo debo perder,” she said to herself. Tania got the gist of what the guard was saying, even though she didn’t speak English. The guard was calling out, not because he thought it would help, but because the noise would attract attention. She had to lose her current choice of wardrobe, and her pursuer, if she was to get to her target. Guard: Stop! The hotel was heavily guarded on the inside by Pinkerton security, brought in from the United States. They were effective at what they did, the company in business for well over 100 years. Pinkerton guards were personally called in by President Grover Cleveland to quell a railroad strike in the 1890’s. Turning the corner in front of her, Tania ran past a Victorian designed chair and yanked it in the way behind her. The guard leapt over it with ease and continued after it. Her footfall was soft against the exquisite (and authentic) maroon Persian rug that lined the floor from end to end along the winding hallway. She’d managed to get up to the fourth floor but was spotted by a Caucasian guard in his thirties, balding though still very physically fit, with broad shoulders and a brown beard that reached all the way up to his sideburns, and blended with his short, close-cropped hair. He’d been with Pinkerton for a long time and was good at his job. DING! An elevator door opened. Tania looked back through the eyeslit in her shawl. Nothing behind her. There was a four-way intersection to the hallway coming up. A very short hallway leading to a window right in front of her, and at either side were two rows of elevators. She slowed down gradually as she came hallway, though the guard had longer legs than she did and was already catching up to her. She slowed, looking to the right but not seeing anyone. To the left, she saw a tall Arab man dressed in a white suit, with a brown turban over his head and a gray beard boarding the elevator. The guard nearly caught up with her, and he was still running full speed. Tania knew that she’d be caught if she got on the elevator, and caught if she just stopped. “Hay sólo una posibilidad,” she said to herself as she dropped down to the floor, curling into a ball. The Pinkerton guard didn’t want to trample her, and thus jumped over her. He had to stop himself, he kept going on his feet and nearly hit the window. Meanwhile, Tania had gone ahead to the elevator and sat down in the chair in front of it. Quickly slipping off her shawl from over her head, she tossed it into the elevator and acted calmly, trying not to breath hard despite all the running she had done. The guard came back around the corner of that intersection to see a part of the flowing shawl go into the elevator as the door closed. The guard ran up to the elevator and pressed the button to open the door, but it had already started moving. Another elevator soon came along, and the guard would enter that one. As he pressed the button to go up a floor, he turned around and came to a rest stance at ease to catch his breath. As the doors closed, he took notice of the fair-skinned Hispanic girl sitting in the chair in front of the elevator he failed to catch, her chest heaving. Guard: Hey! Wait! Tania waved at the guard as the door closed on him and she ran on her way to room 409, which was just down the end of the hall and to the right from where Tania was. Without her disguise she was now just a 13-year-old girl with wavy black hair hanging down to her shoulders, coffee-colored skin; a slender body only 5’1” tall and 104 pounds. Skylar walked up to the door and opened it. He was only wearing navy Sean John sweat shorts. Approaching the door was the girl that he saw being chased in his mind’s eye. The guard caught up to her and grabbed her around the shoulders. Guard: Gotcha! Tania looked up at Skylar. “¡Skylar! ¡Me es! ¡Tania!” Skylar’s head goes down to the smaller girl. “¿Qué tal sus hermanas, Rafaela y Ines? ¿Dónde están ellos?” Skylar’s head went up to the level of the guard, who himself was starting to breathe heavily from the chase he had to go through to catch the trespasser. Don’t worry about it, she’s with me. Guard: She’s trespassing. She’ll have to be detained. She’s a kid; she’s my daughter. Let her stay. The guard seems confused for a moment, unaware that Skylar had any family. He releases Tania, though, who immediately steps toward Skylar and hugs him, her arms not quite reaching around his body, the side of her face pressed against his stomach. “Venga adentro y tome una bebida del minibar. Entonces iremos obtiene Rafaela y Ines. Usted no los debe estar saliendo sólo.” Skylar sends the guard away with a wave of his wrists, and steps aside to allow Tania to enter his room. He hasn’t been face-to-face with Tania since before Violence Jack burned down his home. This was a bit of a reunion, and an awkward one at that. He had the premonition of Tania’s chase, and “Sandy” had warned him that the girls were coming. What was Skylar seeing in his mind’s eye? What was going on with him? So far, one part of this emerging clairvoyance had proved correct, as the girls were in Iraq. They were in Baghdad. Skylar had to get the other two and take them home safely. No doubt they ran away from Florida State care to find him. He could give them lodging, and maybe backstage passes to Supremacy, but he had to get them back home. If it looked like he kidnapped them, he couldn’t get them back legally if he wanted to. Sadly, though, there was no time to immediately do the right thing. Supremacy was quickly approaching, and with it, Skylar’s World Title shot. He had to stay, had to hold off on turning them in. He would look bad for not immediately returning them to the United States, but it was something that he had to do. At the time being his priority was not with his image in the minds of the Florida State Department of Children and Families. He wanted to win the main event. And take care of the girls.
