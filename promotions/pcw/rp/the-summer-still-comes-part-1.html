The Summer Still Comes (Part 1)

undated

Date: not entered Eddy stared over the cliff of the rocky, jagged cliff upon the beautiful orange and pink sunset that lie before him. It cast a dim look upon his face, and a dim look at the mountain. Obviously twilight, Eddy stared over, below him a field of grass, some dying orange and some green grass. Whatever was the majority, which Eddy couldn’t tell, both had a beauty to him not equivalent to each other because both held the same change of seasons within them. That had nothing to do with Eddy’s feelings at the time, but he looked at how pure and clean this area was. Being an Indian reservation it held a true clean feeling because of how Indian’s viewed pollution as not good at all. He glanced at a teepee below him, and the chanting of general Indian music around a campfire. He was intrigued by the culture, very respectful and honest. He felt a little man and the American newcomers long time ago for driving them completely off their own land. Eddy, dazed in the sight, got completely side tracked to what was going on behind him. An Indian chief, over 6 feet tall with feathers in his head and animal skinned clothing stood above him, Eddy barely noticed however, until he said something. 'Very pretty sunset, isn’t it?', the chief commented. Startled, Eddy turned. The chief wasn’t looking at him, but straight forward at the sunset. 'Oh yes, very nice indeed', Eddy back. 'I noticed you quite alone up here upon this cliff; would you like to come down and view of tribal dancing below?' Eddy smiled. 'Sure.' A few minutes later Eddy was looking at the dance around the campfire, warm on a decently cold evening. The Indian’s circled the camp fire, chanting things Eddy had no understanding for but an enjoyment and interest in despite not understanding for it. 'Are you interested in our culture?', said the chief. 'Very much so.' 'Would you be interested in learning more?' 'Yes. How?' 'Books work, however being straight from the source always works better. I can divulge you better.' 'Really? That would be nice.' 'I have no time for tonight, however, tomorrow night you may come back and learn more. Would you enjoy you do that?' 'That’s fine…', Eddy said. 'Do you know the time?' The chief glanced up at the sun and clouds, then looked down. 'I’d predict 6:30.' Eddy nodded, but forgot he had a watch on. He looked at his wrist. 6:32 'Wow, you were on the bullet.' 'I wasn’t off by a minute or two?' 'Well, yeah, but it’s close enough. 'Ah. The sun tells more then your watch can however. Remember that.' Eddy was very confused, but strangely had some odd love for having that feeling. The next day… Eddy had seriously made his steak too well done, so badly it had very little flavor. He had forgotten about the steak on his grill and once he came back the steak was too well done for his taste of Medium Rare. Eddy enjoyed it but it took lot’s of A1, salt and pepper to make it have decent flavoring. The day was very sunny, a little on the warmish side and it had been predicted rain early morning but Eddy doubted that because the sky was so the nearest cloud was very far away. A friend of him named Fred Sparr was coming over with his girlfriend, Eva something. The last name was a long Russian-esque name Eddy couldn’t even pronounce let alone remember. Eddy at this time was staring down at a book, a red cover with golden letters labeled 'Native American Culture'. He rocked in the chair his grandfather gave him and flipped pages when he was done with them of course. He had spent quite some time on doing his hair this morning, even though all he had to do was comb it and shape it in the front on his preference. He had a glass of milk and a plate of carrots, celery and ranch dressing on the stand beside him which was a redwood oak. Eddy was flipping the page, when suddenly he heard a faint crackle. Then he realized it was going to rain, the predictions were right. Eddy looked at the window, sitting his book down and staring and the once blue skies now gray. Dumbfounded, Eddy heard a pitter-patter sound on his window as a couple of raindrops hit it, and in time, it was raining very hard. Eddy was shocked at this, and he heard the phone ring and decided to pick it up. 'Hello?' 'Eddy? This is Fred…' 'Yeah?' 'We can’t come.' 'Why?' 'I just got a call from my office…I had a good pay job offer and I have to take it this afternoon. Even though it’s Sunday, I can’t resist…' 'Oh, dang. Well, I’ll see you some other time.' 'Hopefully. I’m going out of town all next week tomorrow for Spring vacation.' 'Hmm….Oh well.' 'See ya.' *click* Eddy sighed at put the phone back up. Again with little to do, he decided to continue reading.
