Storytime...

17 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/17/02 Part One The blue sun burns brightly down upon the weary gathering of some 15,000 souls in the Land of Kannonia. A cool breeze only creates a tension among the men, taking the wind as an omen of their fate. Sensing the anxiety of his men, the taut complexions and expressions, and the general uneasiness, the morale-sensitive Captain Cualpin suggests to his commanding officer, the great General Juzion, that he say some words of encouragement to the men. A man of few words, Juzion is at first reluctant; however, with prodding from Cualpin, Juzion finally decides to speak. Kicking his crygmaph forward, Juzion falls back from his position at the front of the force and heads back through the parting crowd into the middle of his battalion. Everyone fills in the space around him but respect his presence to leave a four meter radius on all sides. The general, atop his crygmaph, takes a long, absorbing breath; with this Juzion seems to inhale every aspect of the men’s feelings, the atmosphere of the day; he gracefully dismounts his animal, slowly pacing around the edge of the circle to again take in the esprit de corps of the wearily anxious assemblage, gains a voice suppressed for years and with an eloquence never before exhibited begins to deliver a grandiose speech worthy of transcription. “Men,” booms General Juzion, “we are on the brink of victory. Before us stands the enemy capitol. We need only capture it, and the war shall soon be over. This is the last battle. After 70 years of negligence and oppression by the Overlords, we are here, fighting for our right to exist! They have set us aside, they have neglected us, they have even attempted to exterminate us; we are still here. No matter what the Overlords do to rid themselves of us, we survive. Yet, that is not enough.” Juzion takes a second to draw in the mood of the men before continuing. “Now is the time to end this long-standing feud. We must take the fight to the Overlords. We cannot simply escape; get away and survive until the next fight. We must attack, to ensure that we do not simply survive for the moment, but endure until the sun itself burns out. At first, we attacked cargo ships and ammunition stores. Then, we overtook small forts and outposts. Now, after 5 long, hard years of sneaking about in our own land, and utilizing guerrilla warfare, the “Neverlords” are staring down the barrels of their own rifles, in their own territory! “Their numbers are greater; their weapons are better; their training is far superior. Yet our sense of unity and justice; our tenacity; our hope for our families, our posterity, that they no longer will face the subjugation we have received from these outsiders. All these factors, these characteristics, stirred and combined within us to create such a force that we have come out of hiding, gathered together on this battlefield that was once our own, answered the call to arms and shouted to our oppressors, the world, the heavens themselves that it is our time and we will not be denied! Every one of Juzion’s men cheer and drum the ground with the butts of their rifles. A rounding chant, much like the sound of barking, comes from deep within the collective stomach of the army and is released in such unison as to be heard as if only one shouted. Juzion allows the men to rally their spirits, appreciating the last moment before the battle that the men can enjoy themselves to an extent. After half a minute, the proud general raises his hand, and the entire battalion falls to a silence within two seconds. “Look at what has been stolen from us. Our homes; domiciles we built from the ground up with our own bare hands, only to be robbed from us by belligerents. Our lands, lands that our ancestors called sacred; lands that we have lived upon since the sun ignited. Our culture, our way of life; one that remained intact for thousands of millennia. We lived as a prominent, technological society, not one of nomads and vagrants. Our tongues; this accursed language we are forced to speak. Not even the elders remember the Old Language. It exists only in writings that we can no longer read because of the lack of the ability to translate into the vernacular that our antecedents used most eloquently in the poems, ballads, and epics that are lost to time and the mind’s understanding. The list of grievances runs to the Magvidajir Constellation. “I believe in action. My good friend Trikalgo can tell you, since we were children, I always wanted to know why things were as they were. I always wanted to change those things I saw as wrong. Of course, a yearling cannot very well combat an invading force, but in time... in time, look what one can accomplish. More important, look what 15,000 ones can accomplish; see the power we individuals hold when we unite and stand together, fighting for what we believe in: our families, our lands, our cultural edification, effectively, our past, our present, and our future!” Juzion could say more, however the revolting army roars deafeningly in affirmation. The general has already rallied the men to a point where they are willing to die simply to liberate one yard of land from the Overlords, yet he wishes to continue. Understandably, Juzion has much on his chest that he wishes to voice before the contest, because one way or another, this will be the battle to end the war. Seeing General Juzion’s lack of elation, his battalion once again falls to a hush, his silence just as powerful as his words. Juzion begins to speak again, but thinks better of what he chooses to say. He stares into the eager faces of his men, those who he has fought alongside for many battles, experienced many victories and defeats, and instantaneously reflects back on their time together. When Juzion starts up again, his tone is quite different. “I thank you men for all your patience in fighting the enemy on all fronts; for fighting for your lost comrades; for maintaining the army despite a number of AWOL’s among us. You are the heroes, gentlemen. Let us win this last battle so we can return to our families. Let our governments learn from this war; let the history texts remember this war; let our children be spared another war; let us be assured that our fallen friends, cousins, brothers, sons, and fathers have not died in vain. I ask that we bow our heads in a moment of silence for those we have lost, for those we may very well lose today, and for those we pray to Wyx are never lost.” With that, the battalion drops to one knee, folds their arms across their raised legs, lowers their heads, and closes their eyes, thinking about their friends and families as Captain Trikalgo sings an especially slow, somber dirge to those who have died in previous battles. The mood is one of anxiety. The men hope for a quick siege; they miss their families after 5 years away from them. The atmosphere around the battalion is so pervaded with tension, the air so thick with fear and anxiety and anticipation it could be cut with a knife. Trikalgo finishes his requiem. An uneasy pause follows. Not a sound is made. Apprehension set in during the threnody. This time is peaceful; the army does savor the moment of troubled tranquillity, but is afraid to break it. Nature is calm now, and being People of the Land, they wish to treasure this time and ask forgiveness from the Wyx, Great Mother of the land, before they scar the earth with their guns and graffito it with the blood of men. Captain Diyleq cherishes this moment. He is spared of pre-battle anxiety and instead focuses on the complete placidity of the instant. Rather than concentrate on the imminent clash, Diyleq instead takes notice to the slow, relaxing zephyr, the best catalyst for relaxation; the chirping of the small xectenans nearby, their insectoid wings scratching together the most lovely sound anyone could hear; the innocent grass, dancing to the rhythm of the wind; the air, standing still as if attempting to delay the coming events; the other soldiers, all grasping for a sense of security in this calm before the storm. The general is the first to move. He mounts his steed and trots from the center of the revolutionary army of 15,000 to the front. Fixing his gaze across the deep, wide blue river, the anzaeps, rehtks, and other fish dancing innocently in their own clear, uncorrupted world; across the red tinted field, the jingoas and felmxes gaily masticating the crisp blades of grass; below the small hill upon, the veritable Mount Olympus upon which the soldiers look down upon the domain that they once called theirs, to the Overlord capitol. This city is New Washington. It is from here that the Overlords’ exercise their dominion over the once virgin soil. General Juzion slowly draws his sword from its sheath. He raises it with purpose to his face, in front of the bridge of his nose. He kisses the cross formed by the blade and the hilt. The general points the sword to the yellow sky, holding it at a right angle. With perfect fluidity, he drops his arm forward and raises the “Yell of Insurrection,” a high-pitched trill that makes every soldier join in with zeal, as he shouts at the top of his lungs, “Charge!” The army rushes forward to the capitol, General Juzion leading the charge. The “Yell of Insurrection” continues. Thundering upon their horses to the city of final conquest, each soldier draws his assault rifle in anticipation of the coming clash. Part Two Within Terra Firma, the defensive stronghold based in New Washington on the continent of Rush. Every protective measure and security protocol is based and operated from this facility. Each one of the four entry points into the city is connected to this stronghold. Deep inside the hexagonal structure, adorned with laser emplacements, and missile turrets, the Overlords are now learning of the rebel uprising. “Sir!” a defense monitor urgently calls from his station at CapCom, “We have a problem!” “What is it, soldier?” Defense Secretary Speed Urban responds in rigid military form. “Our proximity scanners indicate a large force of rebel soldiers storming the boundary gates.” “Where are they attacking from?” “All sides, Sir.” “Are the checkpoints holding?” “Not quite, Sir. Checkpoints Alpha and Delta report breaches. Only Checkpoints Bravo and Charlie are holding.” “Damned we had to send our main force to Outpost Jordan. Send Sigma Company to Checkpoint Alpha and Victor Company to Checkpoint Delta.” “We can’t, Sir!” “That’s not what I want to hear right now!” “We already stationed Sigma Company at Checkpoint Bravo, and Victor Company is currently en route to Outpost Gamma. I’m sorry, Sir.” “What the hell do we have?” “A small occupational force near the subterranean elevator to Checkpoint Charlie, but they’ve already been dispatched to that site.” Another defense monitor raises his voice. “Sir!” he shouts with exigency, “all checkpoints now compromised!” “Are you certain, soldier?” “Positive, Sir. Checkpoints Bravo and Charlie just confirmed breaches.” “Sir,” says the first monitor, “with all checkpoints breached, we could be overrun in ten minutes. The magnetic storm last week disabled our defense emplacements and we still have not reactivated them yet.” The secretary groans, “I know, I know.” The defense secretary rubs his chin, scratches his head, and sighs. He slowly releases the words, “Use the Trance.” “Are you sure, Sir?” “It’s still experimental,” says the second aide. “I’m aware of that, officer” the secretary retorts. “We’ll get our own men.” “Follow the order!” The first defense aide removes a chain from around his neck and palms the key attached to the end as he lifts a large yellow lid covering a keyhole and a smaller red lid under his raised computer monitor between the panels on either side and the keyboard across from the bottom of the screen. He holds the handle between his thumb and forefinger and inserts it into the keyhole underneath the yellow lid. With a 180-degree clockwise turn, the key raises the red lid and exposes a depressed red button with another keyhole in it. The aide inserts the key into this keyhole and turns it 360-degrees counter-clockwise. The button lifts up from its default position. The aide presses the button back down. A computer-generated voice chimes in. “Warning: Trance activated. Activate security protocols.” The aide types five keys from the twenty located on the left panel. The voice speaks again. “Security protocols activated. Electromagnetic shielding in place.” The aide types five more keys from the panel on the right. The computer once more speaks. “Target site and radius established. Trance Power Cells charging.” The aide leans up from his station, rubs his eyes, and jumps from his seat. Hie rushes out from behind the long computer terminal to the door directly adjacent to the vending machine. Out in the hall, the aide collapes against the wall and begins to cry. He plugs his ears and closes his eyes in a vain attempt to escape the moment as the computer voice says: “Trance power cells charged. Launching Trance Probe.” “God forgive us.” Part Three The open center of the hexagonal Terra Firma opens at ground level to reveal a massive missile silo. From it, a spherical probe five meters in diameter shoots 1 mile into the sky. Oblivious are the battling rebels and Overlord soldiers thousands of meters below. The four rebel units of 3,750 souls each, all intact, bravely fights past the Overlords’ defensive squads. Round after round is emptied from the antiquated M-16 assault rifles of the rebel soldiers. Although the bullets do not take complete effect against the state-of-the-art armor worn by the Overlord patrols, sheer numbers and the element of surprise give the advantage to the rebels. The probe overhead comes to a hover in midair at its peak of launch; the magnetic suspension system keeps the large sphere above the ground, despite the high gravitational pull. The battle rages on down below. The rebels are gaining ground against the Overlords. Remarkably, the rebels still have no casualties. The Overlords, on the other hand, are reporting 300 deaths and 1000 wounded soldiers. The battle seems all but lost now as the Overlord soldiers begin to desert their positions. Up above, the probe separates on its horizontal axis and rotates at 6000 rpm as it glows brighter than the sun with a strange white light. By now, a few soldiers are looking up at the strange sphere that looms ominously in the sky. Faster and faster the globe spins; brighter and brighter the light becomes, until no one can hold their head up or even keep their eyes open for more than two seconds. A loud, high-pitched, eardrum-popping screech emanates from the probe. The disoriented and subdued fighters down below can do nothing more than grope wildly about trying to regain their bearings; some are still firing blindly. “My eyes!” shouts Diyleq. “My eyes are wide open,” says Trikalgo, “but all I see is white!” “Hold your positions men!” commands the collected General Juzion. The light continues to grow. The screech grows ever louder. Captain Cualpin shrieks in pain, “It burns!” The soldiers on the ground become increasingly sluggish. Many have trouble staying on their feet. One by one, the men on both sides fall to their knees. The mens’ eyes roll into the back of their sockets. The light and sound overwhelms the fighters, the weaker of whom lose consciousness. A stray blue laser launched from the barrel of an Overlord’s rifle hits Diyleq in his head. The frontal lobe is penetrated and instantly erupts into a acrid verdant goo. He dies in very much pain. Cualpin, one of the few stout men left standing, is struck by another errant laser blast. The occursed blue light strikes him in his left femur. The leg explodes and Cualpin finally collapses to the ground, cringing in extreme pain. Trikalgo is epileptic, he is thrown into a wild seizure and the violent convulsions make him bludgeon his head on the ground. He beats himself to death. The probe looses a sonic boom that rips the air and the atmosphere is superheated to 150 degrees Celsius. Juzion’s eardrums pop. Oozing green blood gushes from every orifice on his body: his ears, nostrils, eyes, skins pores; Juzion feels pain over every square centimeter of his body. He can feel the water within his body boiling. His muscles tighten; his joints lock up. All this within one second of the sonic boom; Juzion blacks out. Part Four Juzion wakes, or so he feels; there is complete stagnation, no light, sound, or movement with which he can gauge his awareness. He can only sense his awakening, although it puts him in a very much anxious state of mind. The air does not move. In fact, Juzion cannot even feel his body. “Darkness....” he thinks to himself, the voice seemingly echoing throughout the emptiness. “Still air.... Isolation.... How long have I been here? Can’t feel my body.... Am I moving? I can’t even see myself.... Am I injured? Why am I here? How did I get here? Where am I?” And a voice comes back to him in the darkness, the source seemingly everywhere at once. “You are where all wayward souls come on the voyage to eternity...” From the darkness, a pale white skeleton, glowing silver, materializes. It is holding a tablet, upon which there are various names marked. It approaches Juzion. He backs away, but doesn’t move anywhere. Outstretching its bony appendage, the skeleton opens its mouth and speaks. “Welcome to Death.” End:“Rush Mason... he desires another thrashing? And he lobbied for the no-DQ stipulation, what are the bets that Playaz Inc and Blayze will be a part of it? I’m tempted to just give him a match at the PPV, so I won’t have to think about him anymore. “But that’s for another time. Goodnight.”
