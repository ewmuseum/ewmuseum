A Child’s Dreams: A Man’s Nightmare

19 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/19/03 A Child’s Dreams: A Man’s Nightmare It started when I went back… I went back to a place where I had not been for years. A place where my heart swallowed my body whole and where everything I could ever try and comprehend washed down the drain faster then a drop of water sliding down a sink. Everything was just a blur to me. And I wish I could have kept it that way, but unfortunately like all thing that are blurs, they become completely clear after you look at them long enough. I guess that’s what you could say about the artifact I held in my hands. It was just a simple journal for the first few second, but it soon became so much more… I open the dusty pages of a decrepit journal with a leather covering and gold text that displays the name Jose. Jose was the name they used to call me. I can remember the Signor Valez saying it as if it was as clear as day. “All boys must have a name. You don’t have to like the name, but a name tells the world who we are and it differentiates us from the rest.” I would always remember thinking to myself, “If everyone has a name, then aren’t we all similar. For I the one with no name, was actually the one differentiating myself.” Signor Valez was not a bad man though; he was just a man that was on history. If it happened that way one hundred years ago, why not have it stay that way. He gave me this journal so I’d never forget that there was a name in this world for me. I remember I used to think very little of it. I even remember the first thing I ever wrote on it. Romantico flips the cover to see if the words from long ago were still there. To his pleasure they still were as clear and as vibrant as ever. “What’s in a name?” Romeo and Juliet. As you can see, I didn’t think too much of names. I was a daydreamer. If I had my way I’d have the name of all great writers and poets of the eighteenth century. Of course that could never be, but dreams were dreams. As a boy my dream wasn’t to be named Jose, it was to discover the truth. So I look over at the first page. The date was October 6th, 1990, which was certainly odd seeing as I wasn’t one to remember dates very well. The title was one that grasped me and held me in its clutches for the next few moments. The Future I had not yet read a word, but I already knew that it would hurt me more than anything of physical pain could ever do to me. My past was a figment of my imagination I would like to block out. I figured the only way I’d be able to block it out, is if I coped with it. So I began reading the entry I had wrote over ten years ago where I had that script style writing where everything had to look immaculate. It read… Today was a day like all others. I rose to the sun, I rested to the moon, and I worked to the melody of the songs being sung in my head. I didn’t go to classes today. It just seemed as if they were trying to make me less wise then I had actually been. Sometimes I feel like they put us in these learning institutions to brainwash us into the way everyone else does things. Originality doesn’t exist, but I shall not follow the path of the meek. I’ll make my own path and see where it leads me. It may be good, it may be bad, but how would I know if I never tried? So I stood in my little room under the steps like usual. I wrote a poem that reminded me father. I saw an old knife rusted château, and it reminded me of how he’d always show me his knife collection he was so proud of. I’m starting to miss him more than ever. I just can’t wait until the day he takes me out of this place and we go to live together in Madrid. That will be a day I look forward to. For now, I’m stuck in what seems to be an eternal misery, hoping every day and every hour that it will draw closer to the end. I try so hard to not think about what will ever become of me, but it always comes back into my frame of mind. Sometimes I think it will live with me forever. Hopefully by the time forever rolls around my dreams will be answered. Well Signora Mattina is calling for supper. I’m not very hungry, but I’ve been losing a lot of weight and really need to begin eating. I’ll write again later, but for now I must depart. Like always, I thank you for another day my Lord. A shut the journal as I dropped my head with my emotions taking over every part of me. I stood there and noticed all the things I had then, that I’d never have again: HopeFaithGoals Now I’m just empty. Nothing is inside any longer that could make me want to care. It’s as if I’m just a body. My life ended before its end. I faded away with the sun one summer afternoon, and that sun will never rise again. So I stood there thinking, wondering where I could have ever gone wrong. Was it meant to be like this? Why did a boy’s dream have to be my nightmare? Why? A knock at the door came. I turned my shoulder and saw the lovely young Veronica who came with me on my little voyage to the past. “Romantico, are you almost done, I’m getting a little hungry?” “Yes, I’m ready.” I said as I threw the journal on the ground, leaving the memory to be taken away and disintegrated by the ages. Some memories are just not worth remembering.
