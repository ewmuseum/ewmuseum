Choosing A Sacrifice

3 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2 - 3 - 03 (Amidst the gloom and darkness of a large, darkened room, a fire burns brightly. The raging tongues of fiery hell reach towards the high ceiling, bellowing smoke and cackling rapidly. The area around the fire is illuminated, but it’s nothing but a gray cement floor and a brown ceiling. The fire itself rises from a circular pit on the floor, ranging about 5 feet in its diameter. Judging from the black smoke, various pieces of wooden and coal burn beneath the raging inferno. But what purpose does it serve?) Voice: Let’s see here… what would be suitable… (A voice emits from the darkness, close to the fire but hidden in the black air. The sound of footsteps is heard above the cackling of the flames, signaling that something is getting closer.) Voice: It must be a fitting sacrifice, or else I’m screwed this Friday. (The voice, accompanied by footsteps, gets closer and closer to the raging pit of fire until the figure comes into illuminated view. Wearing a long black trench coat and customary black shades, the figure has got to be only one person: Dorn Tracer. Carrying a small cardboard box in his heads, a troubled look is donned upon his face.) Dorn: I hope she is pleased with my selections. It’d be quite the red ass to have picked something inferior and then be smited by a barrage of meteorites. Dorn, with hope: But I digress… I think I’ve picked suitable things to sacrifice. (He drops the box in front of the fire and folds his arms across his chest, staring down at it. A sly grin crosses his clean-shaven face and he nods to himself, apparently happy.) Dorn: It should work perfectly. At least… (His face melts back into uncertainty just as quick as it came out of it.) Dorn, unsure: … I hope so. (Dorn continues to stare at the contents within the box for a moment before breaking his gaze and surveying the room. A cross look forms on his face and he smirks about something.) Dorn: A little dark, I’d say… might as well turn on a light. (He leaves the illuminated view around the fire and heads off into the darkness. For a moment, nothing happens. After a minute, though, an explosion of white light forms on the ceiling and the entire room becomes lit up. The room is HUGE, almost like a warehouse storage area. And it might as well be, because standing like great metal sentinels around the room are HUGE statues of various figures. One is a man-bull with 6 horns, gripping a strange looking spear and roaring in fury. Another is a stern looking man wearing a long cloak with a crystal embedded in his forehead.) (There are about 15 statues, standing against the four walls of the room. It’s an overwhelming sight, with the metal giants towering above the roaring fire. The biggest statue, though, is that of a golden dragon looking like it’s flying towards the heavens. Candles form a circle around it and a ten-pointed star sits at the base of it, like the statue is actually a shrine. Dorn walks back over to the fire and nods with a smug, satisfied grin on his face.) Dorn: Much better. Now, let’s see what we’ve got here… Dorn, looking in the box: Hmm… wow, I picked up some pretty shitty stuff… but I’m sure there’s something in there… (He reaches into the box and pulls out a brass picture frame. The Astronomer stares at it for a moment, puzzling and puzzling…) Dorn: I wonder… Naw, this won’t do at all! (Dorn chucks the picture frame into the pit of fire, which roars higher towards the ceiling in response. He reaches back into the box, digs around for a moment, and pulls out a teddy bear with an eye missing. The object of childhood still has most of its fur and that cute grin on its face… Dorn scowls in disgust.) Dorn, with apathy: Ah… Mr. Wuggles. How I hate you! EAT RAGING FLAMES, POO-BEAR! (The Astronomer raises the stuffed bear above his head and hurls it into the fire with a roar of apathy and anger. Some deep-seated hatred for his childhood seems to be the focus tonight, as he’s been pulling out various memories from the box. Dorn continues to sift through the box, looking through the odds and ends for something… appropriate. Suddenly, his face lights up and he rises from the box.) Dorn: AH HA! Perfect! This should do quite nicely as a sacrifice to the Goddess Of All, the Dragon Goddess En’Flarim! (From within the cardboard memory box, Dorn pulls out something rather obscure… a crayon drawing of what APPEARS to be a dog. Though from an artist’s perspective, they’d suspect shit with legs. But it’s all the same to Dorn.) Dorn: YES! My first kindergarten drawing! The very object that created my loathing for the delusional and unbelieving. My teacher claimed I needed to work on it… BAH! WHAT DOES THAT BITCH KNOW! (He raises an angry fist to the ceiling, gritting his teeth in anger and drawing his eyebrows inward. Dorn stuffs the drawing his jacket pocket and strolls around the fire towards the giant Dragon statue shrine. The 10-point star is a lot bigger up close, at least 7’ tall. In front of it sits a small podium, with a bronze faceplate on the front. Dorn kneels to one knee in front of it and edges his face close to the plate, reading the inscription and looking at his reflection.) Dorn: “By the decree of the Great Goddess En’Flarim, those born of flesh and bone shall leave their souls and entrails here for divine consideration. Those who have served unquestioningly and gave their life for their cause shall be rewarded with a place in the Universe. Heretics and blasphemers will be cast into a raging pit of eternal flame, never to see life again.” … wow, that’s harsh. (Dorn shakes his head and brings out the drawing. He flattens it out and lays it down in front of the podium, so it’s perfectly aligned with the faceplate. Dorn then bows his head before the titanic statue and begins his session of sacrifice…) Dorn, grimly: Oh great Goddess, Watcher of All and Creator of the Universe… allow my life to you. I offer you a chunk of my soul and past, a very piece of my being, to do with what you will. Give me your divine presence and bless me with the strength and courage to… HALT! (The Astronomer is cut off in mid-sentence and raises his head to the statue. It appears to be normal, so Dorn looks behind himself to see if anyone is there. And, surprisingly, no one…) Dorn, to himself: Strange… oh well… (He starts to continue his session of prayer before the mysterious voice rings out again.) Don’t you dare! Dorn: What IS that? It is I… (Suddenly, the giant, rounded eyes of the golden dragon statue flare to life. Dorn jumps to a standing position and backs up, getting a full glimpse of the living golden golem. The eyes burn with a maniacal fire, but the statue remains un-moving. Suddenly, the flames burst from the eyes in a steady stream of raging, spiraling towards the cement ground. Dorn dives out of the way with a yelp as the fire comes crashing down, setting the ground ablaze. The inferno rises from the floor and suddenly dissipates, leaving the Goddess En’Flarim standing in its departure.) En’Flarim, smugly: Rather disappointing… a foolish portrait of some beast to sacrifice to your Goddess. (Dorn falls to one knee before the mighty Dragon Goddess and speaks in a low, frightened tone.) Dorn: I’m sorry… but I consider it a piece of my soul, and… En’Flarim: No excuses! This doesn’t please me at all, Dorn. And I warned you against that. (Dorn just merely nods.) En’Flarim: Therefore… though it pains me greatly to do this, for it causes me grief as well, I will have to punish you in a harsh way. Dorn, grimly: I deserve it… En’Flarim: Instead of getting a chance to contend for that Rising Star trophy, you shall instead defend the honor of having that chance against the same person. (The Astronomer’s whips his head up in surprise and his jaw drops in horror. He leaps up from his kneeling position and shakes his head rapidly.) Dorn: WHAT?! That’s insane! We need that belt! En’Flarim, raising a hand: Silence, Lunis Superia. You disrespected me, and now you shall pay the price. (Dorn lowers his head in shame and sulks away, right past the blazing pit of fire. He stops, kicks the box of memories into the inferno, and walks off. En’Flarim folds her arms across her chest and smirks.) En’Flarim: He’s learning… [End.]
