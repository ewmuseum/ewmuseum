Interview with the Devil: The Flashback Past #1 (Part 8)

27 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-27-03 It seems as though times never become slow for Shane and they can only get worse from here. Satan works in mysterious and very evil ways, but in Shane’s case this seems to be doubled by a thousand times. Shane doesn’t see it, but this is definitely a sign that he is doing something right. If he wasn’t then Satan wouldn’t have interest in him and would leave him be, but the work he is doing is somewhat of a missionary, only Shane isn’t as passionate about his work as a missionary would be. The Lord knows Shane means well in his work and that is the only reason he has kept him here for this long. Last we left Shane, was right after the crash. The day couldn’t get any longer for him than it had already been; or could it? As Shane lay back on the tombstone we saw the names wrote on it, but we had no clue what was about to happen next. Shane leans his head back on the stone and finally notices that he is actually seated next to a tombstone. A cold chill runs up and down his spine as if he knows exactly where he is now, and a tear begins to fill his eyes, as he slowly begins to crawl his way around the stone. He stops momentarily though and takes in a last breath before poking his head around to the other side and he finds his parents’ names on the stone. Shane then covers himself over the graves and begins to weep as the scene begins to turn wavy. The scene swiftly changes from the cemetery to a local church where we find many flowers covering a wide area and two lone coffins sitting at the front of the church. A small child begins to make his walk to the front of the church where the opened coffins rest. The child couldn’t be over the age of fifteen as he steps himself up around the pulpit and takes the microphone. The crowd isn’t very large, but the emotion surrounding the crowd is devastating. The small child begins to weep a little bit, “I know I’m not very old, but I knew one thing, and that was my mother and father loved me dearly.” More tears begin to stream down his face, “I have faith in my Lord though and I know this wasn’t something that happened for no reason. My mother and father were the biggest inspirations in my life.” A person in the crowd breaks out in moaning, the child notices and begins to weep a little heavier than before, “We all have to realize though that they went to a better place. I know my parents were two of the most godly people I’ve ever known in my life, and I’m not saying that just because they are my parents; I’m saying it because it is the truth.” The boy turns and looks into the coffins, “Those two people, they were my family. They taught me everything I’ve ever known up until this point in time,” the tears begin to flow even more, “And Satan took them from my life!” “Now the only person I have in my life that could ever take their places is the Lord himself,” the child looks up to heaven as the tears continue to stream down his face, “I know my Lord would never leave my side in a time such as this and I vow from this day forward to always fight the good fight,” he looks back down at the crowd of people and he slams his fist against the pulpit, “I don’t care if it goes against our beliefs or not, the Lord is calling me right now for a greater purpose, and I’m not about to deny him of that.” The child steps down from the pulpit and begins to make his way through the crowd, “You as members of this church and as my family in Christ can either help me or shun me from the church because of this, and I would much rather stay here than anything, but I can not be a burden to this church.” The child stops next to a certain man and looks him in the eyes, tears flowing from both, “Pastor, do I have your support?” The pastor looks at the child and with a frown, more tears begins to flow from his face as he throws his face into his open arm on the back of the next pew. In the process throwing the other toward the door, motioning for the child to be gone. The child continues to stare for a few moments, before turning and making his way from the church with more tears flowing from within him. He begins to bolt through the church doors and onto the cemetery lot, where he continues to run away. The scene goes wavy once again as we fade back into Shane, still laid out on the two graves and “Through the Fire” by The Crabb Family begins to play in the background. ~Series To Continue~
