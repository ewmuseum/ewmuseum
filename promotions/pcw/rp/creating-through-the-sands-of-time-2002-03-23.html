...creating through the sands of time...

23 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3-23-02 The sands of time spill over the tales of different men, their voyages and odyssey's throughout the grains that time shall allow them. The pharaoh's of Egypt told the time of daylight and night with the hourglass, leading the way for the sundial, and the latest clock told the ways of what is and what was not by its hands. Now man tells its own time in the planes of life with their actions, and as a journalist, I photograph them with a passion, the look of which nothing to fear me by my eyes, by the grumbles in my stomach, or the twitch of my toes. 'Ernie! Get in here now!' the boss calls my name outloud from his offices. I could tell by the silhouette hanging on the outer rim of his door that something urgent has come upward, but I bite my lip in anticipation. Oh, well first I haven't introduced you have I? Excuse me for that, I've been known to rush to things a bit quickly ever since high school. My name's Ernest Jameson Silous, or Ernie for short. I received my middle name from a famous southern war hero who fought in World War II. I never knew him or whatever, but he isn't the very subject of the story at hand, I am. Well, what's left of my pride anyway. The story since I am a photojournalist for a very high budget newspaper published across this great country of ours began Monday Afternoon. I knew it sort of as sipping on a round fixture of coffee looking over shots from a bank robbery foiled by a superhero printed inside of one of those Spiderman comics, thinking to myself, Maybe one day I'll experience something like that.That day was sooner than I thought. The editor called my name without reckless abandonment, usually he would to just tag along with one of the field reporters, but I thought differently about this latest tour of duty. He had a smile on his face as I walked into the room, his white mustache twitches when he talks and this was no different. 'You see son, today is a report on that courtroom drama at City Hall and one of our regular cameraman, Bill has happened to come down with food poisoning.' I hesitated with my question, 'So you want me to fill in for him?' 'Well yes son, that's why we need fresh young blood on the case, not some old geezer who barely holds the camera up straight and we end up getting a shot of someone's brassiere.'the editor told me with a joking grin on his face. I nodded with excitement, for it being my very first assignment. He pulled open his drawer, filled with different items from past recluse moments of high fidelity in the photography department some of them were black and white from the sixties and their usual riots and protest. He pulled out one of himself back in the seventies, which caused me to start laughing under my breath, but still I paid some mediocre attention towards it. Moving towards another stack of photographs, I could see some of the biggest cases ever recorded by the police, murders, robberies, and sting operations. Looking over, I could see a picture of City Hall when architects were first building it. Looking at it today, it's kind of creepy walking through the arch and hearing the deep base of an organ going off. But, in my town, it wasn't even close to being something of the non-supernatural. On top of the City Hall building, something always struck a weird cord inside of me, the large statue on top of the arch. An angel looking over everyone that stands above it and looks deep into the eyes of its unsuspecting prey, waiting for a moment to let them feel comfortable. Then without warning, swoops down off of the arch and mauls whatever the prey is, from mouse to human. The editor snapped his fingers, taking me out of the daze causing me to look back at his face. 'So, are you ready to take this?' 'I dunno.' I told him, I couldn't come close to giving him my true answer, and then he'll tell me that I wasn't cut out for this job. 'You don't know? I didn't ask you for donuts, just a simple yes or no question. So are you in or out?' he told me, trying to use self-reassurance to get my hopes up. I slightly nodded to him, trying to not be scared about this job. 'Yeah, sure' I replied to him. I felt a slight lump grow in my throat, like a tied knot you had trouble trying to untangle. I gulped real loud, trying to knock it down my esophagus and into the pit of my stomach so I had some slight relief. Was I thinking clearly with what I said, I really don't know, but still I had to do something in order to make my small career mean something to some people, not just myself. I could have taken a smaller role in this, but I had to rise out of mediocrity and well, move on with my life. I walked out of the room with a smile on my face, not realizing the danger I could be in trying to find out about this. I hailed a cab, which came close to coming right on the spot but fell a few inches away, making me jog back to reach it. Hoping in, I could tell the cab driver had one of those 'one to make my day' trips with people. I didn't say a word to him, just directions and payment. Slamming the door behind me as I got into the house, I could hear the running of my little sister who's got a smart head on her shoulders for an eleven year old. As I turned around, she gave me a large hug around the waist. 'I heard about your new job.' She told me without hesitation and a smile on her face, her blonde hair swayed in the air as I let a sigh on relief go on my face. 'Yeah, you did your homework?' I asked her, an assumption that she usually did her work, being I had to take care of her until Mom got home. Yeah, I know its odd having a job and still living with your mother but I didn't make enough wages to even cover my own apartment. I didn't mind it though, she still fixed breakfast the way I like it. Breathing calmly, she went back into her room to work on one of her school projects while I took it upon myself to turn on my computer and try to research something around my newest obstacle of fear. A slight smile on my face played no worries as the computer booted up. Sitting down in my rolling lounge chair, I continued to recognize what could have happened to me if I become a victim to the angel in disguise. The search engine on my web browser popped up on my computer monitor as I typed in 'Alexandria Virginia' and punched in the enter key. Coming up weren't that many results among what I was looking for, just a labyrinth for different paths to places in which I need nothing except something for my own pleasure. Then, my eyes caught something a small article on the murders along Alexandria, dating back towards the nineteen sixties when a young girl and a man larger and older than her, let out a rampage among our fair city. Deaths along their heads and their heads were lacerated with the numbers of whom past they have slaughtered for their own pleasures. Thirteen, that's the original number the Bartlett kid did, before they caught him and fried him in the electric chair. His accomplice, Claire ended up becoming a recluse before she died herself, probably from withering and lonesome. I needed a better look, glancing for more information on the Bartlett case, I decided to divulge deeper into the mystery and looked at another page, looking at a string of murders along the sides of City Hall, people whom lived perfect lives without any known relative meaning of heart failure, ended up dead with their hearts crushed. Odd problem about it was; there wasn't a single person charged with the murders including when they had the numbers imprinted on their heads. My Atlanta Braves baseball cap rested along my brown hair as I continued looking at this, before there was a knocking on the front door. Neither my sister nor my mom happened to know who was out there, so it happened to be up to me to answer this strange house call. As I pulled the door open, a small man with greasy black hair and a pair of cultist looking eyes stared at me while his black trench coat hanged sharply off of his back. His forehead stood out sharply as it stuck forward, clean-shaven and . you sir?' he asked him with trembling fear. He looked like a pushover you'd find at a hardware store behind the counter whose only intake was water. 'I am, so what do you want?' I told him sharply, hastily he jumped when I said this so I happened to reply to him, 'Why do you jump when I yell?' His teeth chattered together loudly, so I beckoned him to come inside, not fearing just what could he do to me actually. He told me without some stop towards his voice, 'I just have a problem with people yelling towards me.' 'Could you tell me something?' he asked with a scared boyish look on his face. I couldn't help but notice the odd trembling feeling he felt inside of my house, maybe because it was just room temperature and maybe a cool sixty five degrees outside, ten degrees lower with the rain coming down anyway. 'What?' I asked him without much abandonment and fear to say what I wanted to. I could tell though that with fear, a person can be a little timid to say something happens to be very unlucky in life, I can notice that from many of my co-workers. He lifted up his shirt and all I saw was a group of different marking, no blood on his outside, but the inner workings on the inside of his body were trying to sew up those lacerations. All I could tell from it is that this man had been through something, and some of those things I didn't want to find out. 'I am a road map, of pain.' He told me, a sick distasteful smile on his face, but I didn't find it odd, nor grotesque but kind of normal for a guy looking like he did. Resolving his problem I just chuckled to myself, as if I were the coroner from the morgue and seeing things like this weren't unusual for my eyesight. 'I see that, mind I ask how did you get that?' I asked him without much of a grin to my face. He chuckle to himself and wheezed at the very end of it. 'Well, you see it's a very complicated story, one summer night's dream or course of examination.' 'It was a dream?' I asked him calmly as he took a seat in a nearby chair while I took the one right in front of my computer enjoying his story, since it did entertain me for a second. 'No, more like a requiem for a night of course walking. I could tell by the night's twinkle and the star's absolute gaze upon this heavenly earth upon that time in which I took a quick gaze along the riverside. An enchanting night this was, coming home with higher hopes than a child on Christmas morning with his gifts known and playing with them for absolute glee in his or her eyes. Barely could I cause for a stroll coming down from the office, on Enoch Avenue. You know the Lanier Building?' I nodded to him, thinking to myself as I picked myself up from the chair and went to take myself a glass of Coca Cola on the rocks. As he rocked in the chair, I could easily find some odd occurrence that it could have something to do with the Bartlett murders, but I didn't care for it a second. 'Yeah, the Lanier Building, isn't that where they produce those comic books from the Golden Age?' Quizzically, my curiosity was beckoning for more; all it would do was stare me in the face with reckless moments and enduring times in life. 'Yes, aren't you a large cartoon fanatic Mr. Silous? It's been known with you that with some of your photographs that you love the heroes of the pieces of apparel. Never mind it though, just idle chitchat. Anyway, you see withering away on this night as I took my hat down the river walk; the water splashed upward just a little, covering me in the dressing of it and soaking me. As I shook it off, I could feel a sweep of air rushing over me. I took my head; turning around trying to find whomever had tried their best to dust me off from the water storm that swarmed me only a few minutes before. Looking upward, I could see the red eye glaring deep into my soul, scaring me beyond belief.' Looking at him once he stated the words, I couldn't recognize just what was we talking about but anyway I could here inside of my head, a white bird ravenous in appearance peaking deep into the flesh and ripping out the inside. That doesn't scare me much after all. I could see a swell of tear coming from his eyes as he tried to continue with the story choked up on fear and whatever as it started to become more of some odd fixation towards the truth of the supernatural than what was physically possible and impossible to a sense where you lost the feeling of being a puppet, being inside somebody else's skin and the point of living inside of a world smaller than what is actually is. I can tell from his eyes, the tale of Eros the God of Love intoxicating his mind to try and figure out perplexing mysteries you'd find on Mysterious Mysteries of the Mysterious Kind. 'My eyes bulged, trying to keep the fear from not showing in front of this monster, quietly did I beckon my body backwards trying to keep myself from keeping quiet. His breath was icy cold, enough to chill the bone with the sick feeling that you were to project vomit. He blinked only once, before proceeding to give me, what you see right before your eyes.' It's true, I thought to myself, grasping my chin with the peculiar feeling that my theories on this could be correct. But also, they could be incorrect. It was only for me to think about. Looking upward, I could see a small incision of a blade or sharp point harpooning him, clawing away trying to reach something. 'After he was finished, I could barely breathe for it was so hard, so tight.' He told me before taking a slip of ice water. The tears streamed down his face, feeling for no reason to even live anymore. He could barely hold the glass in his palms, trembling and shaking with fear. Some truth to whatever he was saying caused me to gulp loudly, loud enough for him to hear me. He gazed at me, not sharply with the feeling that I had done something wrong, but that of which a young child would glance at the unwrapped, priceless piece of candy he wanted to devour so badly. 'Does there happen to be a problem with my story of pain?' he asked me with the common innocence. In essence, I would have responded to him with a no for maybe he has wasted some time with his story, but I gave it no common gesture of arrogance. 'I happen to be taking photographs at City Hall tomorrow, the feeling for which I think I just may take a sick day thanks to your story.' 'Don't feel it like that, for you will end up having the same repercussions that I have felt.' He told me honestly; I could have felt something different as he told me that, more of a sneaky thing when he felt so reassuring about that. It was like a colon, controlling the rest of its sentence when you barely even recognize it on paper. Clenching tightly to the glass and raising it up for another sip, his eyes began to twitch a little bit before his set it down and smiled in a relaxing tone. The exhilarating feel of overcoming your fear's would be a nice addition towards my repitore, but I couldn't find it to be either the truth for I am one of those kinds who happen to think when there is a prophecy, either you investigate, or you end up one of the victims sitting in fear. He nodded along with his saying as I thought of the moment when, just when in anticipation I could just happen to do what I've wanted and figure out the mystery. I was only time as the man, whom could have been a sucessful person at the Lanier Building was subjected to such gruesome tactics by the 'angel' had a solemn look on his face. I looked back up at him and blinked holding my breath. 'You see, conquering your fears Jameson, can bring you great health, so I presume.' he told me without heistation, or the choking essence as he had done previous. 'I forget, what is your name?' I forgot to ask him while he was taking steps into my hallway. The doorknob slightly turned and out he stepped, in an odd occurrence I just let him inside my home without identification. Stupid am I. 'Milton,' he said nodding his head. 'Milton Dammers.' Smiling, I kindly escorted him outside where the rain had stopped coming downward onto the Earth and the stars blankly stood out for all to see. It was a delight to happen to see them, finally after the pounding that the Earth had received thanks to the showers. I smiled deeply, and Mr. Dammers went on about his business, reminding me when he was on the sidewalk, 'Don't forget! He lives!' I didn't know if I wanted to believe him or not, but still something told me that tomorrow was going to be something I was not going to ever forget. As my head hit the pillow that night, I could blink for eternity and still not fall asleep. I continued to sit there on my side and continue to think before I closed my eyes and whispered to myself, 'Tomorrow will be something...' I dozed off sleep on the pillow for hours and then, the sunlight had crept into my eyes, causing my hands to ball into semi circle fist and wipe my eyes of the sleep. The alarm clock had gone off and a slammed my right palm on the off button. Tomorrow had come.
