What It Means To Be A Father: Starring Jeremy Howard and Troy Howard

18 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/18/02 “Higher, Dad, higher!” “Okay… not too high.” “Please?” “… You’re a Howard, you can take it.” Jeremy Howard pushes his son on a swing in Elkhart, Indiana. The sky is clear and blue, the trees rustle in the light breeze, the leaves soaking in the bright sun. The Elkhart natives are used to their hometown hero, so they leave him alone while he spends his quality time with his son. Any other town there would be a mob around JKH asking for his autograph. Young Troy, son of the already legendary PCW wrestler Jeremy “Krayzie” Howard is smiling and laughing, happy for the moments he gets to spend with his father such as this one, playing in the park playground. Troy loves his father very much, likewise does the father love the son. Times like these are precious, and Troy is very lucky to have a caring father like JKH that will do everything in his power to be there for his son. Too many wrestling families have the father away from the son so much that the father is but a mere distant benefactor. JKH has broken that mold, being there every step of the way as his son grows and experiences life. “It’s been quite a trip getting here,” JKH thinks to himself as he pushes Troy in the swing. “The trials I’ve had to endure…” “The Hinkle Family… all started wrestling that kid. Seems so long ago. Just ‘cause Hinkle was the governor’s son, that was supposed to make me job to him? Heh, I still remember how it felt to make him tap with that hook.” A slightly different smile having nothing to do with Troy comes across the face of JKH. “Then they come and cart my brother off. Bastards. The time it would take me to get back in the ring… but it was worth it, doing what I love to do. And soon I’d have a wife and child, but even this would be taken from me? To have Troy taken away, and Jessica ran off she felt things were so hopeless? No, we came back from that, didn’t we? Troy, Jessica, and the Krayzie One, all back together again.” Troy chimes into his father’s internal monologue. “Can we go on the twirly-round, Dad?” he says with happy eyes wide open. “Sure thing, son,” JKH replies with a smile. JKH stops the swing to help Troy off, and together they move hand-in-hand across the playground past the slide and the pole, the monkey bars and the jungle gym to the “twirly-round.” JKH sits his son upon the metal platform and begins to push the spinning plate, slowly at first, then gradually faster. Troy holds onto the bar above his head, smiling at his father as he runs alongside spinning the playground toy. “Around in circles, things always seem to come. My brother, Justin… he gets released only to be taken captive by… Maxwell…. “That NTICW punk thought he could make a name for himself at my expense. He thought he could waltz right into PCW and use me as a stepping-stone for his aspirations? And look what it’s accomplished: I’ve got my brother back, the Continental Title added to my Hall of Championships, and he’s so bad that he can’t even get booked.” JKH jumps onto the “twirly-round” with Troy, sitting right beside him. Troy says, “Hold on, Dad!” JKH grasps the bar as his son says, and meets Troy’s innocent, happy smile with another of his own. His eyes pierce through his sunglasses and catch Troy’s. The gleam in his son’s eyes makes JKH happy. “Troy… I do this for you,” Howard thinks to himself. “I get my own enjoyment out of wrestling… but I do it for you, son. I always have you in my head when I go out and wrestle. Anything it takes, I go and wrestle with all my heart, whatever would make you proud of me.” The sunlight shines onto the metal bars of the “twirly-round,” lighting up Troy’s rosy cheeks even more than their natural radiance. “I just hope…” he thinks, “I just hope that I don’t let you down with these issues with Kannon. Gold Patron Meritorious he wants to be called… heh, that’s the stupidest thing I’ve ever heard. But he’s getting to me even more than Maxwell is, because unlike Maxwell, at one time, Kannon had my trust… my support. I would back him up if he needed it. I take him under my wing and let some of my top-tier influence rub off on him, and he repays me like this?” Troy says, “Let’s go to the monkey bars!” the spinning playground apparatus nearly coming to a stop. Troy hops down off of the ‘twirly-round’ and runs ahead of his father over to the elevated monkey bars. JKH follows close behind. “I’ve dealt with too many upstarts like Kannon to count. All of them thought they were better than me. They’ve all wanted to use me to get to that brass ring… and how many have succeeded? None of them. I’ve made a career out of beating expectations and wet-behind-the-ears rookies like Kannon, Maxwell, and others. And so young yet, I’m not about to stop.” JKH meets Troy at the monkey bars and lifts him up to the bars, allowing Troy to grab on. JKH holds Troy underneath his arms and Troy moves along the monkey bars. “To think you’ve actually been able to get a match against me at Spring Fling, Kannon. To think you believe yourself worthy to challenge my Continental Championship when, as you’ve stated many times yourself, you only lasted 3 minutes and 8 seconds in the March Madness Rumble. It’s hardly believable. I’ve faced meter maids more threatening than you. Hell, I’d bet that Snapple Lady is more threatening than your dumb ass.” Troy breaks his father’s train of thought with a request. “I got it, Dad…” says Troy. “You can let go.” JKH complies with his son’s wishes. He watches as Troy moves along the monkey bars by himself. JKH stands proud, watching his son. “Troy’s growing up so fast… but I’ll always be there to watch him; I’ll always be there to help him when he needs it, and to give him his space when he needs it. But that’s because I CHOOSE to, Kannon. And that’s the way it will always be. You won’t put me out of the business, when I leave it’ll be because the ONLY thing I want to do is spend time with my family. And until that day comes, you can be damn sure that you’re going to be playing second fiddle to me. And it’s not about your faith, it’s not about that dumbass cocktease of a valet you hang around with, and it won’t be because of some perceived PCW glass ceiling. The only reason, plain and simple, is because when you get right down to it, I’m better than you, and that’s not going to change any time soon.” Troy has moved to the end of the monkey bars and climbed down onto the playset to which they are attached. Troy hops up and down with excitement. “Did you see me, Dad? You saw? I got all the way to the end by myself!” JKH walks over to Troy, smiling and laughing and clapping his hands. “I saw, son… I saw. You want to do it again?” Troy nods his head yes. “Uh-huh.” The bars being a little higher than Troy can reach, JKH lifts him to the bars again to let his son move back to the other end of the monkey bars. “But this time, Dad,” Troy says looking over at his father, “don’t let go….”
