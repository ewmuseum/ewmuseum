Why? Part 1RP 1 of 2 for Tourny Week 2

15 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/15/04 I spent the last two weeks wondering why. Deep down I knew the answer, but I kept denying it. After all, this is a unique business. In a lot of sports, you work within a team environment. You’ve got teammates that you call ‘family’. You look out for one another, you take care of one another. Because in most sports, it takes the whole of the team to make you champions. In most sports, you can’t get to the top of the mountain alone. You’ve got to work well with your teammates. You’ve got to take care of your friends, because they’ll be there for you when it matters. And then you got professional wrestling. A sport not based solely on the team as a whole. In this business, teams come second. In wrestling, the ultimate goal of each and every wrestler is to climb that mountain, to be the king of that mountain and to spend every waking day as the World Champion. In this business, you fight your entire career to make it up to the mountain top, a lot of the times, with out the help of friends or a team. I still remember the day I met Jon. We were both new to PCW. I had just come off a two month stint in the Indy fed South East Championship Wrestling out of Orlando. I rightly don’t remember where Jon was before his arrival here. But I do remember the night I met Jon. And I remember we hit it off right away. And Why not. After all, we were both still rather green. I had only finished training with Dory Funk Jr a little less than a year earlier. PCW was my first big break. And believe you me, being near 7 foot tall, and 350 pound, doesn’t make it any easier and less intimidating the first time you walk into a national federation like PCW. It was very overwhelming. And this is coming from me, a man that’s won the NCAA Final Four, that’s played a ton of arenas across the nation when I was in the NBA. My first night in PCW, I was scared. And I could tell Jon was too. And because of this, we had a common bond, and that led to the beginning of a great ‘out of character’ friendship. We traveled the long road together. We roomed together. The both of us, fresh faced rookies, tried to make our first few months in PCW as comfortable as we could. When we had time off, we headed home, whether it was to Orlando, to see my then wife Stacey, and my two girls, or to Las Vegas to see Kristin….. And of course do some gambling at the blackjack tables. And need I mention that my two little girls love Jon. ‘Uncle Jon’ as they called him. And I can remember Jon always smiling when he spent time with Kenzie and Carleigh. After all, Jon had no kids of his own. He sort of ‘adopted’ mine. Like I said, Jon was a true friend. My best friend, in a sport that doesn’t revolve around friendships and teamwork. Of course, that was all behind the scenes. As far as storylines went, we weren’t friends. We were just two rookies. We both had to learn the hard way, the Primetime Championship Way. I suffered loses, he suffered loses. It was standard in this business, to pay your dues. And we both had too. No questions asked, no ‘buts’, no ‘whys’. We did as we were told. As our ‘OOC’ friendship continued to blossom, so did, eventually, our careers. After about 6 months into our PCW tenure, I was given my first major title shot. A Continental Title shot against then champion Alan Kreigman. That evening, before the show started, Jon came in and wished me luck. I was touched. Here I was, getting a shot, that both of us deserved. Well, less than an hour later, I watched on the monitor as Jon came out, beat Kreigman in an impromptu match. He won the title I was slated to wrestle for that night. After that, it was almost a year before I got another shot at the Continental title. That’s right. I said almost a year. I didn’t receive a title shot from my friend. Jon didn’t tell the powers that be that ‘Hey, let’s give Boda a shot at the title, he deserves it.’. No, Jon went on to ‘bigger and better’ things with the higher ups of PCW at the time. He seemingly left me downstream while he swam with the big fish. ‘I.C’ Jon was starting to come into his own, while I was still looking for that niche, as far as the powers that be were concerned. I never asked why. I was happy for Jon. A short few months later, Jon and I had our first opportunity to do an ‘in character’ angle. We formed a dominant tag team within the powerful stable called Media. And of course, because of our ‘out of character’ friendship, we had great in the ring and on screen chemistry. We knew each other backwards and forwards. And we were having fun. We finally got that ‘main event level’ status. We tore it up too. Both in the ring, and out of the ring. We got into some problems, backstage and personally. We partied a lot, hell, we were young and in charge. Nothing could stop us. Well, nothing but the front office. Word got around that me and Jon got into a bar fight with a few hicks when PCW made a tour stop in Huntsville, Alabama. PCW settled it with me receiving a one month suspension, and having to pay a fine of $100, 000. PCW paid the medical bills of the two men also, as part of the settlement. Yeah, the fight was my fault. Jon got involved to break it up. As a result, I was ‘tossed’ out of Media, and spent some time at home. Of course, PCW never did know that it was Jon that started the fight. It was Jon that sent the poor bastard to the hospital, clinging to life. But Jon was in trouble of losing his Kano Enterprises, so I stepped up, and took the fall for him. After all…. That’s what friends do for one another. I never asked ‘why?’ or ‘what is in it for me?’. Jon was a friend in trouble, so I gladly accepted the blame, so Jon wouldn’t lose it all. And so I sat..... And I sat..... I played with my kids….. I went fishing..... I watched Jon on television, playing off my ‘firing’ as if it didn’t matter. I knew it was part of the deal. I knew he cared, he wondered what I was doing, how I was doing. He cared….. right? The truth is, he didn’t. I was serving time off, because of him, and he never called to thank me. I saved his business, I saved his money, and he didn’t call, or stop by to apologize. And as far as PCW still knew… I caused the fight that cost them a lot of money. Then my phone rang one day. I thought it was my friend Jon calling, finally, to thank me. It wasn’t, but it was PCW officials. They were offering me to come back, but not on Turmoil, or Fusion. Nope, they had a new product that needed a big, monster character on it. So I accepted, hoping that soon I would be back in PCW. I packed my bags, and headed to Primetime Championship Wrestling….2. And still no call from Jon. I guess being in one of the top spots in the big league was time consuming. God knows at that point, I had no clue what it meant to be a superstar. And then I revisited Shanghaied. In my head, in my mind, on my television. I replayed the ending of the show. I replayed the entire main event with Propaganda. I rewatched that match over, and over and over again, till it became embedded in my brain. Then for the last two weeks, I laid awake at night, watching this match unfold every time I closed my eyes. I stopped asking why, because I knew why. I knew the reason Jon turned his back on his longtime friend. I knew the answer to why Jon would throw away a strong bond that was over 4 years old. I woke up in cold sweats every time my mind revisited that chair shot from Kano, sending me crashing to the mat. I woke up screaming every time I was struck down by Kanton, by Arson, by Propaganda and finally, by Kano. Over and over, they brutally laid me out. In character, it is a perfect story. Two friends, now enemies. But the bitter truth is that out of character, two friends have also now become enemies. I didn’t ask why. I knew why. And Kano’s explanation at Shanghaied, didn’t even scratch the surface.
