Dilemma (Not featuring Kelly Rowland)

29 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Title: Dilemma (Not featuring Kelly Rowland) Featuring: Tim Walden Date: October 29, 2002 The taste of victory...So very near in potential and yet so far away from reality...What must be done to get the win in itself doesn't seem so difficult on the proverbial paper, and realistically isn't as tough to deal with to a certain point in the match as one might think...Work with Duke, keep the match going in our favour momentum-wise, if he gets the pin, don't break it, let him have his glory... If I get the opportunity to take the pinfall... I could go for it. I have to, it's my one shot at the big time, El Diablo's already got some big wins, Snow's had his World Titles, Duke's been a top-level name for the better part of a year now, but as with most rational people, they realize that everybody who makes a run at the belt gets a bigger piece of the spotlight, and they're willing to do whatever it takes to win, for the most part..Almost to the point where they're moving beyond the rational boundary of determination.... Duke's ultimatum still rings in my head... Give him the win or I get the boot. Could I betray my own career for another's satisfaction? I've never had the opportunity to go for the title seriously, the closest I've been is stealing it back in the day of the Deuces, and the first time I get a real chance to go after it, I am left with the choice between my personal success and the feelings of my teammate, and perhaps consequently my group's stability..The opportunity is there, but the repercussions of the decision that may be necessary go deeper than the mere fact of who wins and who loses..Going for it would be the fair way to go, I can't just throw the big match when I'm on the cusp of glory ...But to take the shot and try to make good with it could piss off my good friend and co-worker...Duke Williams, a man I've been both on the side of and against, depending on the state of PCW at the time, but who has always had my respect as a strong wrestler and a generally likable person..for him to suddenly ask so much of me is where I have to weigh out my options as a human being, and to honour his request could lead to even greater things on his part, keep the Front Office stable as ever, and give him the chance to prove he could win the big one..But it has its negative side for me as well. To give Duke the win, just let him take what I would try so hard for and lay down for him, would mean tarnishing the credibility of the event, for though interference often has an impact on the match, it at least takes the form of one person sending a message of their intent, their grit, their determination to be 'the man', to simply lay down shows a message of subservience, submission in fact, that I am content to be 'the lackey', 'the man should the real man step down', and simply 'not looking out for himself enough to be all he could be' ...'The lackey'. I've been 'DA LACKEY~!' for most of my career, at least in Jeff Marx's eyes..From the start, I always tried to make friends in high places, and I always seemed to get stuck doing their dirty work..I formed a brief bond with the White Mexican while we were in the PWA, and it carried into PCW when I finally got there, but he was always the one making a difference in the ring, while I just didn't accomplish that much..I left for the Thugs 4 Hire, which may have personified the lackeyhood of my career: 4 men, or at least a couple of men and some jobbers, who got themselves pummeled by insurmountable adversaries because Nelson was too gutless to take care of his own business or have the sense to not stir up enough trouble that it was too much for him to handle. I left eventually, but found myself stuck in what felt little better, with the Deuces calling the shots, I at least believed it was a group who'd help each other out when we needed it, but they didn't give me the backup I needed when I got ambushed by Flaymin Aymin's goons, and I realized the relationship was a one-way street..Eventually, the anger built up to the point where I just snapped, and I decided I wanted to be more than just a lackey, and this time Tyler gave me the chance... When I bought 10 percent of PCW, I wasn't the lackey any more.....I was the patsy. Nelson exercised his majority muscle to run me out with the intent to reign over the federation once again, but when he ended up taking the fall, I eventually made my way back into the fold with the Front Office...From the moment I made my return, it was no longer the adventures of the lackey Jeff Marx still considered me as...I was part of a group who provided mutual support now, everybody on the same page working toward a greater good for the federation and themselves. I wasn't just the tax collector, I was an integral part of the team..Not the halfback hired to come out and fake a new play, only to toss it to the same running back that always made the same tired charge up the middle to get the touchdown. This would send a message all right, but the message was that there was no real room for me to get the gold when Duke was still the big dog... But to refuse to lose, for the sake of one chance that I didn't want to miss, was it really worth it? There may be other opportunities, and while I remain in the Front Office I retain the perks of the executive life, not to mention the knowledge that I will probably be able to sweep through any title division I want outside of the big two on demand, and I could even potentially make it to the Continental hunt if Duke or Xavier either gets injured or has more pressing matters to deal with..What good would it do to try and take the shot, full knowing that it would infuriate Duke and may well lead to the breakup of the Front Office, and with it my cushy executive privileges, as well as the potential for the Front Office to dominate for months, even years to come, to win Alliances for PCW's sake, and to aid each other whenever enemies grew too great for one of us to handle... Was all this really so important to gamble with my future in PCW? ....Just to be something more than 'the lackey', and to hope that I could make it through to the next level without alliances? ...I know it's not something I can decide for now and hold firm to with certainty, when the heat of the moment takes its hold... ...We'll see what develops. ---- View Tim Walden's Biography
