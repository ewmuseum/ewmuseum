Pulling Strings, The Twelfth Part Of

2 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 2nd, 2003 '...It's a beautiful day in the neighborhood, a beautiful day in the neighborhood, for you and I, for you and I...' For those who have not grown up in America, you have not probably heard the voice of the man in the cardigan sweater and slacks who every single day for thirty some odd years decided to be like Jim Henson, and entertain children for a thirty minute time slot. That love, that feeling of warmth, and innoence was dashed away from us Thursday morning as Fred Rogers, host of Mr. Rogers Neighborhood for PBS, died of stomach cancer at the age of seventy-four. But, the quesiton is, what does Mr. Rogers hold in regards toward Dante Jackson, a spiteful, cruel and egoist with mind only upon making himself bigger and those around him smaller and more minute by the minute. As the scene fades in towards a small television, fuzzy with snow, the reclining chair a few inches in front of it can serve as reminder that in all of Dante's 'greatness', ineptude follows not very far. The shewed look upon his eyes tell the story of a man who's all cried out as he's wrapped himself in a red and black polka dotted blanket, caught with a thumb logged between his bottom and upper row of teeth. The remote, covered with ash from chip stuffing and grease is stuck firmly along side his hip as it digs deeper and deeper into the arm chair, without anyone truthfully noticing. Dante Jackson: Yes, and I'm being a little bitch now, but deal with it. Mr. Rogers died! Switching the television with another remote for another portion of the television equipment a stereo appears to be on, a obvious red light blares brightly from its obscure hiding place as major guitar rifts and screaming rings throughout the house of Jackson as in the words of a black man... Dante Jackson: DAMN YOU! NEVER LET TOMMY OR RUST OVER HERE AGAIN! THEY LEFT THE SLIPKNOT CD INSIDE! Alone as he might be, the company of his wife or his agent is totally in need right now as the red button on his telephone beeps inside of his office, Jason Cunningham grabs his things, before shouting in an uproar. Jason Cunningham: TIME TO SAVE THE DAY! A sharp turn towards the entrance of the home of Jackson as he begins rapping on the door, causing Dante to be more impaitent in waiting, and wondering who could it be. Dante Jackson: Did I order pizza? Because if I did... He cracks his knuckles and holds a Kung Fu stance at the entry way as he creaks the door slightly, allowing Jason Cunningham to take a few steps in before.. Jason Cunningham: OOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOW! Dante Jackson: Oops, sorry Jason, didn't mean to hurt ya there buddy. Cunningham shakes it off. Dante Jackson: What can I get you, a shake, a copy of Playboy, Jack Daniels? Jason Cunningham: No, what you can get me is someway to squash all of your demons inside one jointly covered box so that they won't come back, in, out, or around the corner and cause a spin off character. Dante Jackson: Say what? Cunningham sighs a long sigh, walking over towards the Lay Z Boy chair of Dante's and relaxing in it. Jason Cunningham: Fix me a Scotch and the Torrie Wilson cover, PRONTO! Dante Jackson: Um, the Torrie isn't coming out until April. Jason Cunningham: APRIL?! WHAT THE HELL AM I SUPPOSED TO WACK TO NOW!? Dante thinks for a moment, tapping his foot a few times before a small imaginary lightbulb pops above his head. A cunning look grows on his face as Jason stares at him, almost frightened at Dante's glances. Dante Jackson: The... Jason Cunningham: Don't say the Chyna issue, for the love of God, NOT THE CHYNA ISSUE! Dante Jackson: I wouldn't kill you Jase, you're my agent, my road dog! Jason Cunningham: You mean....I'm...your 'friend' now? Dante Jackson: Of course, and I meant Mr. Rogers, he died remember? Jason nods, remembering the moments of childhood when his mother used to park him right in front of the television whenever Fred Rogers' cardigan sweater would appear upon the box, Dante sniffles a bit before turning his attention towards a few magazines upon the rack where most of the drinks would be at. Dante Jackson: The heck is that? Jason Cunningham: Oh, new XXL issue, seems like the ROC is on the cover. Dante picks up the magazine from off the rack, viewing it from afar before flipping through the issue's various articles and nit picks on various men and women within the industry. Cunningham snatches the television remote, BET popping on from a previous set channel as Jason tries to continue changing the channel, but BET still stands there, untouched, not moving a bit. Jason Cunningham: Sup with BET man? Dante Jackson: Oh, I guess Tracy was trying to lower her white television standards and begin watching BET 24/7. Jason Cunningham: She could have cut on UPN during the week to do that. Dante sighs, his fingers still manuvering the pages of the magazine until he gets towards the review section. There, he looks up his name and the album title, checking the four point five record disc under the name. Dante Jackson: Ah, a near classic! Jason Cunningham: Don't get your hopes up, it could be a set up to a downfall. Only you and Nas dropped two albums in the same year, atleast yours weren't super commercial now were they? Dante Jackson: Of course they weren't! I put like five, six diss tracks on dudes that don't even rap! I took the trash talk game and put it in a new perspective! Jason Cunningham: Yeah, but its like your mortal enemy coming back from jail. Dante nods. Jason Cunningham: You know Reno Enders right? Dante Jackson: Don't remind me. Jason Cunningham: Well, he's seriously trying to bring you down in New York, trying to take over the business. You know how it goes with these cutthroat ideas. First Gotti gets an idea for a record company and boom, it spreads like cancer. Like the mixtape craze, you gotta hop in when the hoppin gets good. Dante Jackson: So...he's alive still? Jason could only nod along with him. Dante Jackson: Good, time to pay the old boss a visit.
