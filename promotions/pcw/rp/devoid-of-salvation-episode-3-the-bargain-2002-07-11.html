Devoid of Salvation: Episode 3 -- The Bargain

11 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 11 Death embraced Shanahan that night in the cool subterranean caverns of Baltic Europe. Her grip was enchanting and inviting, an escape from the feeling of acute helplessness plaguing his psyche as he knelt before the Crawling Chaos. It was his only evident liberation, the only means of escape from the baleful wrath of He who stood before him and the remaining legion of cosmic fiends roaming the realm of human existence, both dream and corporeal alike. He could feel the icy gaze of Nyarlathotep, the smug grin of the deity as he glared ominously into the back of his fallen enemy. His ire was perpetual and endless, and for the ‘crimes’ of his predecessors Bruce would pay the penalty inflicted by the reprehensible celestial. The Old Ones had nothing in common with the common God of modern belief; they were vengeful and loathsome beings and they made no attempt to hide such a fact. They yearned to cause suffering, and this thirst for torment was insatiable. Azathoth, Shub-Niggurath, Yog-Sothoth….they were but incarnate instinct, boiling eternally at the center of creation with no active will but acting on that of dark impulses. But the hideous Nyarlathotep and his subordinate kin were a different breed altogether. They took sincere glee in the suffering, and the Chaos most of all. Indeed, the only true escape was that final transcendence into eternity that only death could accord. A blessing he realized the sadistic demon would never grant as long as he remained entertained. “Insolence is a cruel joke played by your own primitive mind,” scoffed the Chaos as he strutted about the chamber in that pharaoh form so prevalently used in his appearances to the Boston native, Shanahan’s eyes still cast woefully to the blood and muck-soaked floor. “You choose to defy me and my Lords, carry on this fruitless crusade to bring about our exile and ultimate destruction. For thousands of years, since your kind were still picking fleas from their hides like the mindless dogs you still are, mankind has longed for a way to rid us from their nightmares and banish us from their domain. Your archaic origins, the Dark Ages, the Baroque period, and throughout the reigns of the glorious empires of Rome, Babylon and the Dynasties of China……your puny mongrel race has subsisted in this struggle, and made no progress. The Elders are all that keep us confined, incarcerated like common animals with the use of their damned magics.” The broad-shouldered avatar cast His eyes down on his wretched mortal foe. Those eyes gleamed with the fiery inferno of the abyss, glimmering with the sparkle of rubies. A grim smile danced awkwardly across his lips. “Shanahan……cease it all. Desist and halt this empty quest. I realize that your greatest wish right now is the capacity to leave this miserable life. I cannot say that I blame you. You endure an abject existence, Bruce. First you face persecution by the social elite, warring with both yourself and the hostilities of everyday street life. You’re held down, kicked and proverbially raped by a system that never gave a damn about you, and all the while watch as those closest to you fall by way of illicit worldly sin, gunned down without reason and victims of self-induced gluttony. Now your efforts to protect the innocent go unappreciated as you try to make amends and repent for your lifetime of hatred. I do not condemn you. Contrary to your faith, I can be an entity of divine mercy. I have remorse for the downtrodden and the outcast. And as such, I offer you this….” A serrated dagger appeared before the broken ruffian as he knelt on the floor, its tip already fouled with the blood of many before. Bruce blinked, closed his fist around the ebony hilt of the grandiose weapon. A metallic screech echoed across the chamber as the blade passed over the coarse cavern rock, Shanahan lifting the elegant blade to admire its beauty. It was a stark contrast of sorts to the repulsiveness he usually associated with Nyarlathotep and his brethren. “No one understands you, Bruce,” mused the monstrous deity as the mortal ran his calloused hand over the blade. “You fight for an underground organization with a futile goal, a glorified terrorist union who take the lives of the innocent that claim to protect. You fight for an employer that rewards greed and egotism, granting opportunity to the undeserving for sake of their own personal agendas. Bruce Shanahan, you live in a vile world of corruption, perversion and brutality that would as soon spit on you as offer you salvation. I now offer you that salvation. It will be swift, painless and honorable. You will be in charge of your own destiny, your own fate shall be in your hands. And you shall forever be released from the wrath of myself and my kin. Take the easy option, mortal. Follow your heart.” Shanahan’s heart was calloused and black, as rigid as stone and with the same warmth. The Crawling Chaos’ offer was enticing. In fact, it was possibly the greatest gospel Bruce had heard in many long, sleep-deprived months. Deliverance was at his doorstep, he need only cross the threshold. He realized all that his antagonist said was true. It was issued forth from a barbed tongue, yet nonetheless was accurate in every conceivable aspect. Though he attempted to atone for his sins, it seemed with every step forward that he fell two back again. The Blackhelm Coalition, what did they matter? They existed in the shadows, brought forth no glory and seemed to never make conspicuous progress toward meeting their objective. What ‘honor’ did his brotherhood in the organization bring? He had raided accursed tombs and barren, lurid dreamscapes only to have his aspirations clash in a web of broken promises and shattered hopes. He was part of an organization who almost routinely took the lives of the innocent as collateral damage for their actions. Father Craven, Bishop McCarver….what fools they were. And, ah yes….PCW. The promised land, the nirvana of his profession. What rewards did it have to offer? Shanahan’s ambitions weren’t motivated by greed or wickedness. He didn’t fight in the name of some false religion, or under the guise of something he was not. He fought for retribution, a sense of honor on behalf of all those who had been raised indigent and who nearly demolished their own bodies for love of what they did. He was a warrior with purpose, a paladin for the persecuted. For months he had waged war night-in and night-out in an organization where he watched a greedy, self-serving executive propel himself to the top to combat a self-professed gladiator who had been jammed down the populace’s throat so damn long how could he not be heralded as the Messiah he was being promoted as? There was that punk-ass ‘Scientologist’ fraud, Xavier Kannon. That delusional ape Akira Akiyama. The purposeless redneck on a perpetual alcohol binge, Duke Williams. The second-rate wannabe with a God complex, Jason Snow, and that raving, spandex-clad maniac Rent-A-Villain. Bruce didn’t have the outspoken persona those leeches did; he was notably outspoken, but it was in a way the brass despised. He told the candid truth, the painful truth, and was condemned and ridiculed for his readiness to do so. He didn’t follow the status quo and because of it was made a scapegoat, forced to ‘put over’ innumerable scores of ‘talent’ for corporate benefit. What did it matter? He had beaten nearly all in his way to within an inch of their pathetic lives, annihilated their bodies and crushed their morale….only to be forced to take phantom dives to destroy his own credibility and boost the stock of his inferior opposition. It was a travesty. But he was steadfast in his refusal to change. If that meant his undoing….so be it. He looked up at Nyarlathotep, stared directly into the burning eyes. Bruce saw past the fire and into the abyss beyond into fathomless eternity. “This world has forsaken me,” sighed Bruce, drawing blood from his right hand as he slid the notched edge over his palm. “The time is truly right, Old One. Thank you for this liberation….” Nyarlathotep grinned as Bruce fondled the dagger, placing the blemished tip over his left breast. * * * Slade and Stryker stumbled into the 2nd level, out of breatha nd fumbling for new magazines. In their wake, they heard the irritated cry of the Cthonian as it burrowed again to give chase. The vibrations emanated from beneath their feet, the chant-like hum accompanying their digging reverberating across the chamber and through the tunnels. “Dammit!” shouted Stryker before hurling his now defunct radio across the room to smash to pieces. “I should of known this f*cking operation would be fouled up! I don’t even know where everyone is! For all I know our entire squad could be wiped out!” “If we get outta this alive, remind me to destroy that damn GeoCorp,” snarled Slade as he slammed a new magazine into his rifle. “They have a knack for digging up things they shouldn’t.” The vibrations grew to an almost deafening volume as the ground before them exploded in a torrent of falling rock, one of the worm-like Cthonians bleated and echoed forth the ‘death hymn’ as it had become known. Slade and Stryker wasted no time, emptying their weapons into the scaled beast. It recoiled with a shudder as bullets ripped into its plated flesh, the chamber lighting up from a state of pseudo-darkness as the barrels of the weapons flashed and flickered. To the right, more of the alien chants sprung forth before giving way to an avalanche of falling rock…… * * * Vanessa brushed a tangle of hair from her eyes, gritting her teeth with determination as she sent bullet after bullet hurtling through the air to slam into her pursuer. The plated Cthonian swayed its head to and fro, its mouth-oriented tentacles flailing as bullet after bullet careened into its body and head. The thing scuttled from its burrow and raced after the fleeing Vanessa Wilder, who cast a doleful glance back at Shanahan. The Boston time-bomb was still kneeling on the ground, glazed over eyes cast to the ground in that horrifying trance-like state. Her green eyes flashed with sorrow for the bitter street thug, who was obviously unaware of the imminent threat that raced by him, seemingly ignoring his presence. “God speed, Bruce,” she thought to herself before opening up with another volley on her attacker. She knew his pain, grasped the adversity he had endured. He had spent years battling for survival, and now with those days seemingly gone he now engaged cosmic forces with power of he couldn’t even comprehend. Alone and used for years, his rage was logical and she could identify in a sense. Some people were just born to land hard.
