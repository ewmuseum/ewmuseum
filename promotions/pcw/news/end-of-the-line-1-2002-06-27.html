End of the Line #1 Posted by Sandy Wong on 27 Jun 2002 ---- Here it is, people. The End of the Line. Every now and then, I shall grace you with my presence on paper, and in this first edition of my semi-regular (whenever I can be bothered to sit down and do it), I shall speak about Pay-Per-View Etiquette. Such a thing is something that's never taught, but really needs to be. There are unwritten rules... laws that you just have to abide by if you're going to participate in a PCW pay-per-view. Some of you know them, some of you don't. Still, a refreshed course couldn't hurt. So, here it is; my words, dictated to... never caught her name... anyway. RULES FOR PAY-PER-VIEW BEHAVIOR 1) If you win, get out of the ring as quickly as possible. Now, I understand that you may believe that your win is of the utmost importance to your career; for the rest of us, however, we couldn't give any less of a damn about you or your win. So should you manage to beat your opponent(s), please just get out of the ring and lets us get on with the card. If there are any problems with this, you'll have much time to write a letter of grievance after I forcibly eject you from the ring. 2) If you win, don't thank God. It's pathetic. Don't thank God for your win. God doesn't care if you win or lose, God has His own plans. I'm sick and tired of it. Look, if you want to be a devout Christian or Muslim or Jew-whatever... go ahead. But STOP believing that this obligates ANY God to do a favor for you. Religion isn't a quid pro quo deal. You believe in whatever God because you choose to. Any achievements you make are not blessings, something your God has given you, but rather just what your God allows you to accomplish. Please do not confuse those two different concepts. 3) Don't complain about cheating, cheap shots, sneak attacks, or interference. Look, you're grown men and women. If you can't defend yourself, you're in the wrong business. 4) Don't whine about not performing well should you lose. Xavier Kannon did it after only lasting 3 minutes and 8 seconds in the March Madness Battle Royal. And that, more than anything else he's done, has made everyone in the back hate him. I like him, he's a good wrestler, but he just doesn't know when to shut up. Now, if you lose, take it with a grain of salt, improve, and don't lose next time. Anything else you do will be ignored, or even worse, draw the hostilities of everyone in the locker room. And you wouldn't want all those people against you, now would you? 5) If you win a title, it would help if you defended it within the first week of winning it. Not that you have to, but it certainly makes you look like a better champion to start defending it as soon as possible. 6) If you must attack people in a pay-per-view, please make sure that there's some point to it. I won't mention names, but a couple of PPV's ago, Xavier Kannon and Darkstar suffered an attack at the hands of one apparently irate wrestler. Most of the time, we in the back understand why one person does something. But in this case, we were all baffled by this particular course of action. Said wrestler was repaid in kind, and no more mention needs to be given to that. But for the rest of you, remember, senseless acts of violence only go unpunished in the case of the Dark Ages. 7) Cluster-matches are best avoided. Only one winner, and even then he's the worse for wear. Clusters ultimately end up doing no more than damage to a person's body, and sometimes, their career. 8) There is no sex in the Champagne Room. Well, apparently Tyler Nelson continues to break this rule. But anyway, there is supposed to be no sex in the Champagne Room. Sure, there's champagne in the Champagne Room, but you don't want champagne; you want sex. But just remember: no matter what the girls (skanks, all of them) may tell you, there is no sex in the Champagne Room. 9) Stay away from me. I usually keep a variety of sharp weapons on my person. Add to this that I get so focused on my match that I lash out at anything that disturbs me before I'm ready, and it's safe to say that for your own good, it's best to just leave me alone. 10) Do your best. That's all you can really do in a match. Give it your all and make sure that even in a losing effort you made a good enough showing to make us take notice of you. There are other rules, but those are more implied. These are the only ones you really have to follow. Other than that, just be sure to do your job and everything will be fine for you.
