The Doghouse Finale Posted by Sandy Wong on 24 Dec 2001 ---- You guys all know what this is, the last edition of the Doghouse. And as promised, it’s huge. I wasn’t able to pull off newbie ratings, because quite frankly, there are just too many of you. Anyway, I hope everyone enjoys this. Fedwars/PCW... no comment. The PCW low card is stacked like never before. For now, however, it doesn’t necessarily improve PCW, because the upper cards are all as strong/weak as they were previous to what happened with NTICW. However, in a month, when all of the former NTICW’ers drop their labels, things will get very interesting. And no one benefited more from his time in NTICW than Xavier Kannon. Instead of spending his early PCW days jobbing and scratching his way up the ladder, Xavier comes to PCW packing a reputation as a future Main Eventer. Simply put, everyone else (with the possible exclusion of Drake Maxwell) will have to make a name for themselves here, Xavier will not. And making a name for yourself in PCW is a goal that will take months to achieve. More on that in a second. To be completely honest, I’m not completely sold on Xavier Kannon. He’s a blue chip prospect to be sure, but climbing into the Main Event scene in PCW may be something a little out of Xavier’s reach. Then again, no one since Rent-a-Hero has looked so good coming into PCW. Some will say Kannon’s a surefire future champion, while others say that he’s very good, but not spectacular. I’m somewhere between those two opinions right now, waiting for Xavier to decide on which side of the fence I fall on. To be honest, I’m not sure if all of the newbies realize exactly how rough it can be trying to establish yourself in PCW. If you need examples, look no further than the upper midcard, where Snow, Big Alan Steele, and Vampir Nosferatu play. All of these guys have been pegged as Main Eventers since the day they entered the company. But that was over half a year ago, and still, there’s not one of them who can claim Main Event status. No doubt, each of them will. But do the newbies have that kind of dedication? They all have confidence, and they’ve been showing that since as far back as the interviews. I’m interested in seeing how well they handle tests to their egos. This isn’t NTICW, this is PCW, where the big boys play. Aside from Xavier Kannon, there’s also a guy named Drake Maxwell who comes to PCW packing some heat. Maxwell’s no Kannon, that’s for sure, but he has all the right tools to probably be better than Kannon someday. Maxwell’s a great writer, but I’ve heard at least three people, not even including myself, saying that the newbie has to cut the filler out of his RP’s. He really seems to fit the mold of someone who sets a length goal before writing an RP. It’s nice to see that his latest RP’s are a bit cleaned up from his earlier ones. Ken Kaze and Zurick need work. We just got rid of Syko, and now we get not one, but two reincarnations of him. Kaze, however, does show some promise, and made a good impression on the staff in his interview. I can see Ken Kaze as a future Television Champion, and maybe even Continental Champion. But reaching those goals will both take a lot of work, and a lot of improvement. Main Eventing, at this point, seems out of the question. But this is PCW, where the Backseat Boy can turn into Rent-a-Hero (so what if he fizzled out?). This might not be a popular statement, but Red Rock is showing more promise than a lot of the new talent. And he earned my respect by taking a demotion to PWA and working his way back. Dedication seems to be underrated in PCW, although it’s getting more attention now. The proof is in our last two champions. Red Rock needs some work, but all of the newbies, and half the PCW roster, need work as well. I don’t know if Red Rock can ever make it past the midcard, but he’s a guy who I’ll be watching closely. D-KAY CALYX IS IN PCW!!! This being my last column, I feel like a dying man who’s life long dream was fulfilled on his deathbed. D-Kay is the new Tim Walden, with a better workrate. But a lot of people have stereotyped D-Kay by his early work. I can tell you though, right now, D-Kay is probably one of the top three or four writers in PCW, and also the most underrated man in the federation right now. As good as his writing is, however, he’s going to be held down until he tweaks his gimmick, which is what, in my mind, has held him from a PCW contract for three quarters of a year. I don’t think any one has ever shown that kind of dedication. I don’t know how far D-Kay can go with his current gimmick, but his writing skills could one day see him much higher than most people think. Decimation went off rather well in my opinion. I don’t think it quite matched Red October, but it was probably a little better than Alliances. However, I almost want to say Decimation was better than both of them because Jeremy Howard was finally crowned PCW Champion. And look what he’s done since: Totally shattered the curse that has plagued every PCW Champion, with exception to Ivan for the most part, and continued to have a stellar workrate. And did I see a rumor saying NTICW’ers might form a stable? Please, I’m begging, don’t let that happen. I know it’s a quick way for guys like Xavier and Maxwell to get some real heat, but it could kill them in the long run. What each NTICW star needs to do is find their niche in PCW, so they can drop their NTICW tag. Besides, didn’t Wild Deuces just do that? And how successful was that? Is there any reason to think that repeating this angle would make it any better? Yes, Not at All, and a big Hell No! Big Alan Steele is officially not my favorite PCW’er anymore. To be honest, I hadn’t really followed him for a couple of weeks. And when I decided to take a look at his recent work, I found it above average. Some things, like the RP that I believe was titled “Amish Porn” seemed lazy. Will BAS find a spark and catch fire once again? I’m pretty sure of it. People with that much talent don’t just die off. Well, except Rent-a-Hero, but I guess the jury’s still out on him. Ivan Stanislav desperately needs to drop out of the Main Event scene for a while to refresh himself. He’s fought for the title in each of the last three Pay Per Views, and has been in the title scene for almost all of 2001. That’s just not healthy, and I’m starting to get the feeling that Ivan is being stuffed down my throat again. Ivan is one of PCW’s best, but that doesn’t mean he can’t drop out of the title scene for a month or two. If he went down to extreme for a while, and then came back to the world title division, I think he would be packing much more heat. Right now, sadly, I’m a little burnt on Ivan Stanislav. Will the same happen to Jeremy Howard when he eventually loses the championship? I’m not sure, really. Jeremy hasn’t been quite so wrapped in the title scene, and he hasn’t been there for quite so long either. All I know is when the Krazed Lunatic finally does begin to wear on me at the top of the card, the Extreme division wouldn’t be right for him. He’d be much more suited for a Main Event, or upper mid-card, feud. But we’re now talking about days that are probably months away. I think that’s it for news, so now it’s time to get into the real meat of this column. Categories and the monthly polls. What Card are YOU in? For the second time, I’m going to give everyone a run down on how I see each card in PCW and who fits there. I’ve done something a little different this time. You’ll notice there’s an “FC” after everyone’s name, and then a number. The “FC” stands for future champ, and the number is out of ten, based on how much of a chance I feel that person has of one day becoming PCW World Champion. Check out the legend...
