The Doghouse #5 Posted by Sandy Wong on 31 Oct 2001 ---- Hello again, and welcome back to the Doghouse. I hope you all read my “Special” article, an interview with the Amish Mad Man. I feel it was my very best work to date. If you think only NTICW have business reading that, then you’re dead wrong. Yoder’s a class act and always has interesting things to say about the way things are. Now, lets go on to this weeks column, shall we. I’ve got a couple of special things on tap, and I want to get right to them. First of all, it was a busy week for Jane Reagan, as most are. - Judas Steele is back. Does anyone have a concrete reaction? No. Because Steele hasn’t done anything concrete. Time ticks away and we see nothing from Judas Steele. That’s going to hurt Tuesday night’s ratings. Steele was an excellent addition to Red October, but it means nothing if Steele doesn’t show up and earn the spot he’s been given. If he doesn’t, then it will hurt our memory of red October over time. - Where’s Big Poppa Plump been? Sure, he’s injured now, but where was he when he wasn’t injured? I say the guy should be dumped back to PWA. There are people down there working for roster spots. This guy has one, and is doing nothing to keep it. - Finally PCW has terminated the Polish Punisher. If anyone wants to see a real Cinderella story, look at the Polish Punisher’s career. Maybe Cinderella’s not the right analogy. Does anyone know a story about a guy who was never really that good, but caught break after break? In his PWA days, PP was decent, but no better than the other guys. Still, he skipped PCW2 and landed a job with PCW. He saw early success, riding the like a hot air balloon on Ivan Stanislav’s heat. Finally, the balloon has been shot down. He’s a waste of roster space. -So both Jason Snow and Kid Wonder are injured. What does that mean? It means there’s a hell of a lot of open room in the mid-card. Guys like Unstoppable Eugene, Corey Williams, and especially Timo Bolamba, will have every opportunity to step up and fill the holes. - I just learned that Meanstreak has officially retired. I will leave it at that for now, but I’ll say that a lot of Main Event room is now open. Ivan Stanislav as champion? That’s some great news for PCW, as it seems the “glass ceiling” has finally been broken. I expect Ivan to be the best champion in months, and look forward with a lot of hope to PCW’s future. Watch for Boda to get a legit shot now, and Jeremy Howard should get some well-deserved attention. As my frequent readers know, I usually try to put some fun spots in my column that everyone will enjoy. No, I’m not talking about the polls yet. Those will come later. This time, I want to get to something that will come after every PPV from now on. I don’t want this to turn into a shoot column, but I guess ... ... ... ... It’s time to shoot. RED OCTOBER REPORT CARDS You want the grades? You can’t handle the grades! But you’re going to get them anyway, like it or not. In this section, I’ll grade the PPV performance of a few select PCW stars. This time? Who else? The eight finalists for the PCW Title! Meanstreak - Does it really matter what his grade was? The fact that he’s Meanstreak instantly raises it a full letter. Meanstreak won the world championship, in what I hope was a close decision between he and Ivan Stanislav. Meanstreak’s PPV work went from the excellence that he’s been so long noted for, to RP’s that were absolutely poor. Of course, I’m talking about these “journal” RP’s. Meanstreak should really consider putting a stop to them. He’s our marquee man. When people from other feds look at PCW, they judge us by our best. That’s Meanstreak. If they look at one of these “journal” RP’s, then they’ll wonder why the hell we’re supposed to be so great. With that said, I don’t want anyone to get the wrong idea about my opinion of Meanstreak. Before the finals, I was on the fence about Meanstreak. But his PPV performance was outstanding. Do I think he’s the best? I’m still on the fence about that. But no one can really dispute the decision to give him the championship. He made a lot of believers out of PCW’s younger generation at Red October. His workrate was good, and most of the quality was top-notch. Grade: A (journal RP’s brought it down.) Ivan Stanislav - I was most impressed with the Russian Bear, I think. His workrate was outstanding and he was definitely gunning for the championship. PCW management will be kicking themselves if Meanstreak follows the trend of the past several PCW champions and disappears. Whether you like Meanstreak or not, you have to admit that it’s a possibility. If Stanislav had been given the ball to run with, it wouldn’t have been. Ivan’s stuck through thick and thin with good work. Did he beat Meanstreak on the boards? I don’t really know the answer to this one. Meanstreak was great. So was Ivan. Ivan’s the more reliable of the two, but Meanstreak’s probably got more heat. Overall, no one should be disappointed with Ivan’s performance. I’m saying right here and now, Ivan Stanislav is the next champion of PCW, and I think he has a chance to be one of the greatest. Grade: A+ Kremmen: Started strong, didn’t finish at all. In my own humble opinion, Kremmen didn’t have the quality to compete. Maybe it was staff duties, I don’t know. Kremmen looked good going through the tournament, but maybe that’s because I was psyched to see him back in action. However, I believe he only RP’ed 5 times (all early) for the finals, and as I said, his quality was lacking. Maybe I get out of line in these columns sometimes with my personal opinions, but that may well be what keeps the readers coming back. This is another of those instances, because I’m going on record to say that I believe Kremmen will retire again. I think he came back to win the title. He didn’t do that, and I don’t think he’ll stay motivated now. He won at the PPV because of heat. That’s all. The quality wasn’t there, and the workrate wasn’t up with the big boys. I should fail Kremmen, but I’m feeling nice. Grade: D Tyler Nelson: I stated that I thought Nelson was the better between he and Kremmen. Now? I have no idea. Nelson had Kremmen beat in quality, but he simply didn’t show up. Again. Where was Nelson at Back to the Oldschool? Even after Nelson screwed up that high profile match with Jeremy Howard, I didn’t question the decision to put him in a high profile situation at Red October, especially since he looked decent going into it. But after he dropped the ball in two straight PPV’s, if he’s a big player at Alliances, I’m going to lose my kibble. This isn’t the Tyler Nelson of old and I think we have to get used to that. So how do I grade him? Again, I should fail him, but he did have good quality. Grade: D Kid Wonder: Probably the biggest surprise of the tournament was the excellent performance Kid Wonder put in. Excellent workrate matched with quality that’s usually on target will get high grades. A few of Wonder’s RP’s, I could have done without. Most, however, were very good. Wonder was so good, that I wonder why he was pinned cleanly by Snow in the Continental title match? Sure, it helps their feud, but Rent-a-Hero was far more deserving to have his face kicked off. I hope we see more of these great performances by Wonder. It seems that the entire federation is hoping to see a return to form by Kid Wonder. Will we get it? I hope so. It’d be great to see the teen idol headlining cards. As I said earlier, Kid Wonder had an excellent PPV. One of the best. Grade: A Snow: The Killin Villin took another step forward at Red October, putting in a great performance. It was nice to see him get the #1 spot in the Continental division, setting up a match between he and Vampir that PCW 2 fans will drool over. It would have been equally satisfying to see Kid Wonder win that match, but still, Snow’s stock couldn’t drop after his performance. His workrate was up there with the best. He started strong with quality, but it dwindled down the line with his final few RP or two being just average. Still, PCW management gave Snow the ball and he ran with it. Expect him to keep climbing the PCW ranks. Grade: A Rent-a-Hero: On par with Snow coming into this event, Rent-a-Hero dropped the proverbial ball. He gave his partner, Jeremy Howard, nothing until the very end. Even though he finished strong, two days worth of RP’s for the biggest night of his career doesn’t cut it. Expect to see Rent-a-Hero have to work hard for his next opportunity. With that said, expect him to make the best of it when that time comes. Everyone’s down on Rent-a-Hero right now, as they should be. I feel partly responsible for that (by the way, responsible doesn’t mean guilty), so allow me to speak on this for a moment. Rent-a-Hero has way too much talent for the lackluster performance he put on at Red October, and I think he’s learned his lesson. That’s not to say he should automatically get another huge shot like he had at Red October. He should have to earn that by production, not potential. With that said, those who think this PPV ruined Rent-a-Hero are wrong. He’s far too good. He’s one of the best. This is a set back of either minor or major proportions, but Rent-a-Hero still has one of the biggest upsides in PCW. He’s still a future champion. After all that praise, the fact remains, he dropped the ball. His grade was saved by a decent recovery. Grade: C Jeremy Howard: Possibly the best of Red October’s combatants, Jeremy Howard put on a stellar show of interwoven quality and an unheard of workrate. In short, he was absolutely amazing. His quality was on and off, but was “on” way more than he was off. Workrate wise, he was the best. Quality-wise is arguable, but you can’t argue that he was close. It’s tough to say if a better showing by Rent-a-Hero would have made a difference in the results, but it couldn’t have hurt. All in all, Howard and Ivan have been without a doubt the most consistent upper-carders in recent PCW history. Everyone else has come and gone. Ivan and Jeremy have been there week in, week out. Again, amazing showing from Jeremy Howard. We all should hope he gets rewarded for the hard work. Grade: A+ Now that I got that out of the way, lets move onto something else that I’m going to do from time to time. I call it, “Advice to PCW Management”. Catchy? I thought so... Advice to PCW Management: Cutting the Fat This is in regards to Pay Per Views, not the roster. Every time a PPV rolls around, PCW management tries to include everyone. Are the results an explosive PPV? No. The results are a great PPV that could have been better if it weren’t so watered down. What can be done? Why not do what PCW was supposed to do anyway? Have shows in tiers. TT and FF could be the major shows, with SS being for up-and-comers and jobbers. Furthermore, don’t cancel Sabotage on the night of the PPV. Instead, have a mediocre card devoted to jobbers and up-and-comers. How much better would those 20 man brawls be if they were 8 men brawls with pure quality RP’ers involved. I’m reminded of the Duke Williams/Boda feud. Vampir Nosferatu was thrown into the card when he had nothing to do with the feud. Now, I’m a Vampir Nosferatu mark, but his presence lessened the value of the Boda/Duke feud. Vampir would have better fit into a lower spot, which was occupied at the time by a jobber.
