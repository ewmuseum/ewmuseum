The Business #21 Posted by Sandy Wong on 27 Jan 2002 ---- The times, they are a-changin'... Today in The Business: Heading into the start of 2002 and PCW's first PPV of the year, Shanghai'ed, let's a take a step back to look ahead and gaze into the crystal ball for the upcoming year, and let's do it quickly, before TPB bites my head off. (You know, you'd think that rate limiting would be designed to stop things like having him AIM me repeatedly with nothing but the "angry smiley".) Before we get started, though, I'd like to be the first to congratulate Chris "Boda" Sloboda on his PCW Award for "Most Improved". That's right; me, Robbie Decker, wants to be the first to shake his hand... and remind him that when you're on the bottom of PCW, there's no place to go but up, huh? Now, to the Business: a rundown, piece by piece, of where PCW is headed for 2002. THE TALENT PCW's definitely stepped into the next generation over the course of 2001. The old guard is fading: Meanstreak is long gone, Alan Kriegman is retiring, Kremmen is gone and back and gone again, Kid Wonder has been farmed out... with the exception of a few holdovers (Miyagawa, Clyde and Tyler Nelson, most notably) PCW is full of new breed talent. The best wrestlers in the fed right now got their starts in 2001: Jeremy Howard, Ivan Stanislav, PWA's own Jason Snow, and others. I personally think that there's been a shift in PCW talent; in PCW's old guard, there was a more equal distribution of talent; there was a roster stacked with very talented wrestlers from top to bottom. In today's PCW, there's more of a rift; I think PCW's superstars are better than the superstars of old, but there's also a bigger gap between the talent level of the top stars and the midcarders. (Jobbers, as always, are jobbers). It's hard to tell if the talent level will rise or fall in the next few months. PWA has been working hard to prepare guys for the top-level federations, and NTICW will help them refine their in-ring skills and their ability to cut decent, coherent interviews; but with all of the feds working together in PTC there's going to be a lot more free agency, a lot more big contracts, and a lot of guys getting drawn away from their homes to follow the big bucks. When you also consider that NTICW talent is fair game for OSW as well, it could mean that PCW is going to have to look within to find the main eventers. Snow is a great example of a guy stepping up and becoming a main eventer of his own accord. Who steps up to fill the gap? If Darkstar survives this weekend, it could be him. It could be my personal mark, Big Alan Steele, when he gets back rested from his recent injuries and hiatus. Hell... it could even be Boda (when the temperature of hell drops a few degrees to match). PCW needs guys to elevate their games to prevent a glut of midcarders, many of whom struggle in mediocrity and end up falling like flies. OLD SCHOOL WRESTLING Though nobody knows who exactly is pulling the strings in OSW... it looks like the world will find out in a week... one thing is certain; there's a heavy amount of PCW's braintrust and backing behind Old School Wrestling. The Phantom Booker has put in a large amount of work behind the scenes (as has yours truly, who, with my top-access media credentials, is privy to many of the backstage areas and secrets of the federation). Though the two feds are independently owned, and even competing against each other in the ratings, OSW and PCW can be considered as close to brother federations in PTC as is possible. Interfeds? Talent trades? Free agents? Anything is possible. Next week on Saturday Night Slam, I'd expect to see some big names; I'd expect to see some surprises; I'd expect to see PCW's roster raided... and I'd expect it to be the start of a heated battle of one-upsmanship between the two feds. With heavy competition for both ratings and talent, I can foresee PCW and OSW becoming an interfed rivalry for the ages. Then again, maybe since I'm drawing contracts from both federations, I could be a little biased. :) TOO CRAZY FOR PRIMETIME? We've seen critical injuries and deaths on PCW's shows. We're about to see a "First Death Match" at PCW's Shanghaied 2. But with competing OSW promising "old school", will PCW get goofier to compensate? The general trend in all of professional wrestling over the past ten years has been a shift to a more violent, more sensational product. I think that for PCW to maintain respectability, they need to be willing to buck the trend. Watching shows from many of the rival feds in PTC, I see angles and ridiculousness that's just beyond any sort of normal belief. Don't get me wrong: I'm all for high-quality asskickings and guys going out there and doing what they have to in order to defend their pride and fight for glory. But if PCW develops any sort of reputation for outlandishness, there will be a twofold drop in talent: an increase in unskilled wrestlers who get attracted to that sort of garbage wrestling, and a decrease in current wrestlers and prospective talent scared away by an unsafe working environment. Nobody wants to miss time due to injuries, and if they become more commonplace in PCW, the wrestlers are going to start looking for federations where the management pays better attention to their well being. 10 QUICK PREDICTIONS FOR 2002 1) Duke Williams will be PCW's president after Shanghaied; but it will quickly become obvious that without the financial backing, he doesn't have enough strings to pull to gain total control over PCW. Still, he will make Tyler Nelson's life miserable enough until Nelson (my hero!) reforms Media (again) and comes down hard on the management. 2) One of PCW's names will cause a scandal by having a public tiff with management, and then bolt to another PTC fed. Popular opinion seems to say Ivan; especially with most of the management out to get him; but he's too loyal a worker from what I've seen hanging around backstage. 3) The PCW will attempt to get a women's division off the ground; it will fail, miserably, and just be another excuse for gratuitous T+A. Hey, no problems there. 4) The Duke Blue Devils will win another national championship in college basketball (which has nothing to do with PCW other than being enough to drive Shoot King up a wall, what with his Terps just being notquitegoodenough to deal with Duke.) 5) Alan Kriegman's retirement will last, oh, let's say, three months, at which point he'll come back and work a couple of weeks with Kremmen, Trashcan Man, and Howard Porter, who will all quickly disappear back into the murky netherworld of retirement for at least another month after that. 6) NTICW's "Stone Hot" Steve Baustin will work a two-week stint in PCW and win the World Title, just because. 7) PCW's Rising Star Title will be sent to the trash heap again around the middle of the year, when the roster is pared down to decrease bloat. With all of the other excellent but less competitive federations in PTC, PCW's curtain-jerkers and midcarders will start looking for greener pastures (that require less jobs), thus making PCW shows easier on our eyes in general. 8) JeW3 will open and get laughed at by... oh, let's say, everyone. (Kreller's note: JeW3 has already come and gone, and it was laughed at.) 9) PWA will expand, gaining much more staff, and servicing most of PTC. Graders will continue to be in short supply, however. 10) And lastly; look for Jeremy Howard and Ivan Stanislav to be the champions for most of 2002, running an epic feud in the early part of the year. By the time we hit June, I'm expecting these two to put up at least two nominations for Match of the Year, if not the best match in PCW's history. Phew! Now that I can turn this in and collect the paycheck, I'm off to get some sleep before Shanghaied explodes from the middle of China... and it'll be a busy week, as I expect to be on hand for both of PCW's shows before flying to Easton, PA to witness the grand opening of OSW from the front row... and I expect to see each and every one of you there! Hating you all, ---Robbie Decker
