End of the Line #2

29 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
End of the Line #2 Posted by Sandy Wong on 29 Jun 2002 ---- Yes, this is the second edition of End of the Line. In this one, I have interviewed Trigger backstage at a recent event. Tape recorded so I wouldn't have to bother remembering a transcriptionist's name. Erin! That's her name. ... Anyway, here's the interview. *Has to go change the name on the check he wrote* ---- The End: You! *Snaps fingers* Who are you? Trigger: Trigger, Infinite Warrior The End: I'll call you Tigger, Infinitely Boring. Problems with that? Trigger: Yes. But despite your attempts to break my spirit, it will not be broken. If you have a purpose, state it. If else, leave me be. The End: Well, I was about to interview you and give you publicity since you seem to be a wrestler here (unless you're an oddly dressed ring crew technician). But you're being a bad sport about this and you don't want your name highlighted in the news, so I'll leave you to your pointless anonymity. *Walks off* Trigger: I have nothing wrong with your interview. Return. But if you choose to make a mockery of my character, I SHALL crush you! The End: So you admit that you are nothing more than a gimmick? Trigger: Nothing more than a gimmick? Is this world nothing more than a creation? Is our life nothing more than a story? The End: And unnecessarily verbose as well. Majored in stupid rhetorical questions, didn't you? Besides, I'm interviewing you, unless you're so caught up in the mysteries of life that you forgot. Trigger: Continue with the interview. I will answer any and all questions honestly. The End: First, give me in about... ten sentences, your career before PCW. Trigger: At first, I began my career wrestling in independent federations. I was highly acclaimed, considered even a legend by some. But when I decided to retire, I realized that I couldn't, no, because something wasn't right. I had accomplished everything I wanted to in the minor leagues... but I never really got a chance to spread my ideas to the world. The only place I could do so was on the top, and so I set my sights on PCW. Three months later, after defeating competition in PWA and XWA, I arrived. I am now where I want to be, and therefore have peice of mind. The End: Is that to say you're content with your career at this very moment? Trigger: Yes I am... mostly The End: Don't be! You won't ascend Rising Star status if you remain content. Speaking of that, it seems that you're defending the belt at Gold & Glory against another new hire, Kris Burden. Speaking frankly about him, what do you find are his particular strengths and weaknesses? If you have a strategy against him, I won't ask you to reveal it. Trigger: Kris Burden has talent. He must. Afterall, he surpassed all the rigorous training and steps that I had to take to get where we both are today. But I feel him to be too arogant, even if he tries to avoid it. So far, nobody has offered him a real challenge. I think he was surprised at how much I had to offer when we fought last week... and he will surely see much more at Gold & Glory. The End: So, as far as how much of a challenge you expect Kris Burden to pose against you, how does he rank in terms of your most dangerous opponents? 1 being least dangerous and 10 being most dangerous. Trigger: 10. I view all my opponents on the same level, of having superhuman strengths. It is the only way to convince myself to put forth an effort greater than my wildest imagination. The End: In that case, from what you're telling me, if you ever underestimate an opponent, then you'd be screwed because you wouldn't give 100%. The End: Always give 100%. Even if you're wrestling Jeff Marx. Trigger: It's just like the saying, "Give 110%." Give more than possible, and you won't be disappointed. The End: Do the impossible. "Hey, it could happen." You sound like one of those old McDonald's kids commercials. Or at least some cheesy after-school special. Trigger: Is that so? I don't watch television. The End: Neither do I, in a manner of speaking... Trigger: How interesting. The End: Anyway, getting back on topic, I'm sure you feel as though you'll be a World Title contender soon. Now, putting aside all that crap about viewing all your opponents as superhuman, if there was any one person in PCW's past or present that you'd best like to beat for that strap, who would it be? Trigger: Big Alan Steele. Not because I like him. Not because I hate him. But because I respect him. The End: Okay, and now here's the part where we completely shift the focus from you... Trigger: Alright. The End: First, not including yourself, who do you think are the top 3 Rising Stars in this federation? Trigger: That's a tough question to answer. If I have to choose three though, I would say Pretty Cool Guys, Jordan X. Shabazz, and my own opponent, Kris Burden. Not because of their gimmicks or characters, but because of their talent alone. The End: Actually that's four people, but I'll let it pass since we all know PCG would never amount to anything in a singles career. Next ranking -- the top three innovative wrestlers in terms of their ring repertoires. Trigger: I must say, another difficult question. Afterall, who could get into PCW without talent? Xavier Kannon, Rent-A-Hero, and Tyler Nelson are just a few that come to mind. The End: In answer to your question -- Hank Gunyon, Hiro Saotome, Zurick, Avalanche, Drake Maxwell, Scott Kilborn, Lord Crom Griffin Dagoth, Token Weed, Stryfe... the list actually goes on for quite a while. The End: Last question, but this has nothing to do with wrestling. Trigger: Yes? The End: What are your favorite songs, books, movies, hobbies... whatever. Trigger: Occassionally, I listen to music in order to get psyched for a matchup. My favorite is the underground rapper X-Raided. Besides that, I recently haven't had much time for such types of media... being on trial for Attempted Murder and all.... but my main hobby would most likely be training my WITs, (Warriors In Training), to achieve greatness. The End: Talk to Clyde. He can set you up with a postal job. Exempt from murder charges when you work for them. Trigger: Luckily, the trial has passed, and as you can see I am not in prison. The End: Actually, I can't see that. I'll take your word for it, though. Trigger: I see you haven't lost that sharp wit of yours. The End: As for the Warriors in Training, might you want to achieve this "greatness" you speak of before training others to attain it? If you learn somewhere along the line that you've been giving them the wrong advice and lessons, you'd have to train them all over again. And that's only if they decide that they want to come back (and we know this is true because you can't force greatness upon anyone). Trigger: I have no doubts that I am leading them towards success. Being the warrior that I am, I cannot fail. The End: Are you God? Trigger: No. I am the Infinite Warrior. The End: But, you're not God? Trigger: No, no I am not. Trigger: At least in my opinion that is... Trigger: *breaks a slight smile* The End: Then the universal balance dictates that you win some, you lose some. Look at yourself saying "I cannot fail." Now who's being arrogant? The End: *Cracks smile of his own* Trigger: I simply cannot fail MYSELF. If I give it my all... If I keep my warrior ways... then I have succeeded in my attempts. As for how it relates to the wrestling world... I have lost two matches in my entire career. I do not feel it a stat to be ashamed of. Correction, three matches The End: Of course, the Infinite Warrior's mind is so narrow that he takes the phrase "you win some, you lose some," to only apply to wrestling... Trigger: Is there any other subject in my life to which it would apply? The End: Life itself is a game. That's the answer to the question you asked earlier. Life is nothing more than a game. You start off with nothing, work to attain something from there. Some people will help you to the finish, some will hinder your progress. You'll suceed sometimes, you'll fail other times. And ultimately, everyone gets to the same hollow victory at the end, the game being "over," and the whole thing put away and forgotten. Trigger: A bleak outlook... but a certain one. Thank you for taking this time to interview me. Unfortunately, I must be off. The WITs won't train themselves... *Walks off* The End: There he goes, Tigger, Infinitely Boring. He's too optimistic, really. Won't last long here with that attitude. What have we learned? He speaks too much, thinks about idiotic questions that only people with nothing else worthwhile to do with their lives think about, and apparently he works part-time at a day-care or something. That's it, this is the End of the Line. Go away.
