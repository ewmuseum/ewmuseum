Rembrandt finally has a say about CBF and his career(int.)

Hammerlock News — undated in the source
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Twenty Questions with Rembrandt Blast Reynolds takes his place at a table of the Knight's HQ, as CBF's big name Rembrandt is scheduled for an interview. Rembrandt, veteran of Cslam, enters, shakes Blast's hand, then takes a seat across from him as Blast lights up his cigarette, grabs the notepad, and cues the camera. Blast: "Hello folks, I am sitting here today in the Knight's headquarters with one of the founding members of the Knights and a 2-Time CBF World Champion, Rembrandt. Remmy, you've been around for a long time and this is the first interview you have ever given. I am honored to be the one to conduct it." Rembrandt: "Well I am honored that you would deem my opinions worthy of an interview. What do you have in mind?" Blast: "How about 20 Questions with Rembrandt!" Rembrandt: "Sounds good to me...fire away!" Blast: "Just how long have you been slamming?" Rembrandt: "Well, I started back in the old CIF in June of 1998. I was a first round draft pick by the Executive to join the CBF in July. I have been here ever since." Blast: "CIF?? Draft choice??" Rembrandt: "Oh yeah, most of you young guys have never heard of the CIF. It was the Grandfather of all Indy feds. At the time, it was the only minor league fed in Cyberslam. At the end of the month each major commish would pick 3 slammers from the CIF to promote to their fed. The Executive had an awesome eye for talent. Myself, Destroyer and Thrill were the CBF's picks that month. In fact, that July was probably the greatest group of newcomers ever to the CBF because it also saw Al Bradd make his first appearance as well." Blast: "Wow, thanks for the history lesson. You mentioned Thrill. Being new around here, I was wondering what you thought about the CBF Hall of Fame inductees for this month?" Rembrandt: "Well, for starters, they were all deserving. Chev is the greatest stable leader of the modern era and also probably the best on the boards I have ever seen. Al, well, when he has the time to devote he is nothing short of sensational. I have been running angles with him since day one in the CBF, and he never fails to amaze me. Thrill, well, I ran angles with him since day one of my career, and he never gave less than 100%. He had to fight for everything he got too, but he made it just like he said he would. He was also one of the founding members of the Knights, but he and Chev had a falling out and he went off on his own. He really developed into a true talent and soon became one of the best in the game. Of course, if he hadn't started hanging around with that Rubber Chicken, he never would have amounted to anything." Blast: "Thrill and Al Bradd. You mention them a lot. You three have been sort of tied together for some reason by everyone I talk to. What's the deal there?" Rembrandt: "Well, aside from starting together, we got involved in many of the same feuds. We all sort of banded together at one time to rid the CBF of the Proof stable. It was then I met my wife, Sarah Bradd, when she was pregnant with Thrill's child. You can see where this obviously led to some complications. But I feel that I have worked things out with both men since then." Blast: "Obviously, considering the way you and Al teamed together last month. I know many of the Knights weren't too happy with some of your actions. Is your relationship with them strained at all?" Rembrandt: "I sure hope not. I know that most of the non-CBF Knights didn't think anything of my actions, and I know that Destroyer didn't mind. Chev, well, he has been sort of a mentor to us and when we don't do as he would, well, I know he was a little disappointed. Justin Tyme, however, was truely not happy. In fact, he wasn't very happy with any of us. I hope that next month we can all pull together. It's really what the Knights are all about." Blast: "I know that Chevalier is your stable leader, and I know you respect him. But there was some feeling in the locker rooms that if he had lost to Dark Knight in the loser leaves fed match it would have been the best thing that could have happened to your career. He did not end up losing, but what do you think of the speculation?" Rembrandt: "Interesting. I think some said that because Thrill really made a name for himself after he stood up to Chev and got out from under his shadow. I mean, let's face it, when you mention the Knights, especially in the CBF, you think of Chev. Maybe the rest of us are in his shadow a bit, but that's just fine. There's a reason he is in the spotlight, just ask Dark Knight. However, I feel that I have made a pretty good name for myself during my time. I like to think I bring a lot to the table and I know that many of the opponents I have faced know what I can do to. And trust me, I have faced the best that have come through these parts." Blast: "Do you feel you were slighted in the CBF Hall Of Fame voting as some have suggested?" Rembrandt: "Not at all. Everyone in there is deserving. If it is meant to be, I will be grateful. If not, well, these things happen. The most important thing is that I have had a great career and had fun doing it." Blast: "Speaking of your career, I know you are a 2-Time World Champ. What other belts have you held?" Rembrandt: "All of them, except the Brass Knuckles title, but I have only fought for that once. I have held each of them multiple times as far as the others go, including the World Tag Titles on 7 seperate occasions." Blast: "7??? Wow, that's a pretty awesome record. Any favorite partners?" Rembrandt: "Well, I have been teaming with Destroyer in the DOD since about half way through our first month in the CIF!! We alone have held the tag titles on 4 occasions. The other title reigns were due to retirements or because of Blitzkriegs." Blast: "Word has it that you aren't a very big fan of the Blitz type card. Why not?" Rembrnadt: "Well, I guess they are OK for the singles titles, although there are always problems with these cards and someone always ends up getting screwed. But especially for tag teams. It usually leaves 2 slammers that aren't partners, usually 2 of the top guys, and keeps the real teams from competing with each other for the belts. You just don't see great tag team feuds anymore and I think that this is one of the reasons why." Blast_reynolds
