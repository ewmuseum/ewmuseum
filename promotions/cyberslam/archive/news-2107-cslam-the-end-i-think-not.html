CSlam: The end? I think not!

3 February 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The Glory past reporting today... You have your Reveres, your Adamses and your Washingtons... All marked heros by history. And why? Because they stood up to the oppression of a corrupt empire and fight for all that they knew as true. They knew they would be branded rebels, rejected from society if they failed, yet they continued on anyway. That, my Brothers, is heroism. That is pushing back that little voice that says "No"...putting everything else on hold and just going for the gusto. Slammers, It is time that we step up and take up our mantles as heros. Time we stood up for what we believe in and know to be true. Time that we took back CSlam. We can't let the failures of corporate commercialism get us down. We can't bow out now. This is the TRUE defining moment of a game that has captivated so many of us for so long. This, more than when the game was first opened, represents the true beginning of this game...the day we reject the opinions of the management and proceed to play as we know how. I have seen many rebels...many heros...already going to work on their feds. Many of the reporters here are helping, pushing, to show them what this all means to us. Ladies and gentlemen, this is our time to shine. This moment, brighter than any UT gold, is what we live for...the chance to save something we love. Do not go gentle into that good night, Old age should burn and rave at close of day; Rage, rage against the dying of the light. Though wise men at their end know dark is right, Because their words had forked no lightning they Do not go gentle into that good night. Good men, the last wave by, crying how bright Their frail deeds might have danced in a green bay, Rage, rage against the dying of the light. Wild men who caught and sang the sun in flight, And learn, too late, they grieved it on its way, Do not go gentle into that good night. Grave men, near death, who see with blinding sight Blind eyes could blaze like meteors and be gay, Rage, rage against the dying of the light. And you, my father, there on the sad height, Curse, bless, me now with your fierce tears, I pray. Do not go gentle into that good night. Rage, rage against the dying of the light. Let Dylan Thomas inspire you. Let Thoreau and Emerson supply you. Do not let this be the end, gentlemen. This time, it's a fight for our home. Let's do what we can to save it. Phant out. none
