iHEL: Interview With Commissioner Case

15 August 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
bs Commissioner Case is seated in his office smoking one of his trademark big cigars when the iHel reporter enters for the scheduled interview. "Sit down and make yourself comfortable. Lets hurry it up though I got things to do." "First, just give us a little bio of yourself in C-slam." "Well my Cslam bio is abit short. Most of my experience lies elsewhere in the ewrestling world and I dont think i'm allowed to talk about that here. Lets just say i've run organizations larger than an indy league and leave it at that." "When you origionally arrived in iHEL, what was your first impression?" "Um ... in all honesty i'd have to say I thought about telling them where they could stick iHel. I've seen the many problems of iHel in the past and felt like I was getting tossed to the wolves, but i've made the best of it. I think iHel is slowly making improvements and pulling itself out of the pit." "And how would you describe it now?" "I think it's better now. Most of the inactives are getting weeded out slowly so that those with talent have room to grow and make something of themsleves. From what i've seen so far there has been less garbage in flashes and more quality." "What do you think of the influx of newbies coming into the iHEL?" "Its the indies, newbies come and go. Either they [BEEP] up and I give them the boot or they get into the swing of things and take an active role in improving the league." Do you think Cyberslam's changed over the years? I already told you, I haven't been in Cyberslam that long! No more stupid questions! Okay, then how about this. If a tree falls in the woods, and no one's there to hear it, does it make a sound? Of course! Kappoosh! What's the sound of one hand clapping? I think this interview is over. Okay, but do you have any closing comments?" "Well people have seen me as a cold and somewhat harsh individual. I try to be as fair and unbiased as possible. If people do what they are supposed to do then I can be a really easy guy to get along with. My job here is to train the future stars of Cslam and that is exactly what i'm doing. I have to mold these guys and train them to be good flashers and talented guys in the ring. If I seem a bit harsh in doing that well it's because sometimes training people requires a stiff approach. People who work hard and take an active role get recognized and treated well. People who do nothing but take up space in the locker room get the stick. If that seems unfair and cold then so be it. Thats how I run things." none
