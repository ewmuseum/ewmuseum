A three-nights-a-week federation of original characters that ran from November 1999 to August 2000, settling its matches on a Zeus simulator weighted by the quality of each member's roleplay, and closing with a final roster of ninety-nine wrestlers, nine title ledgers and a tribute page to its retired champions. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Ultra Fantasy Wrestling ran three nights a week for nine months, from November 1999 to August 2000, under card names it kept the whole way: Monday Madness, Wednesday War and Friday Night Fight, with a pay-per-view a month above them.

Its members argued themselves into matches on a roleplay board and then filed a registration form; the result came out of a simulator, Zeus 4.0.2, which the federation named openly on its own facts page alongside the reason its president graded every posting — a good roleplay bought “push points” that raised a character’s strength, vitality, speed and charisma inside the machine, and the federation said in its own words that the generator, not the staff, decided who won.

Nine championships ran at once, and they moved constantly: a hundred and thirty-five recorded title changes in nine months, the TV Title alone changing hands twenty-three times. The house comedy was institutional rather than incidental — a federation-controlled jobber called Roody Poo lost week after week, got a night of his own, won the World Title by counting his own pinfall as the match’s referee, and was killed off interfering in every match of a pay-per-view.

The presidency changed hands repeatedly and ended in a committee: a rotation of vice-presidents each writing one night of the week. The federation closed in August 2000 with a Champions page that still named its last holders and a Stables Title whose holder it never disclosed.

Its own front page carries the closing notice, dated the run and signed “Rest In Peace”, telling applicants to stop applying and recording where its members had gone: many of them, it says, could be found at PCW.

Affiliations

Primetime Championship Wrestling (PCW, Primetime, 2000–2004)

Successor to UFW (Ultra)
Ultra Fantasy Wrestling closed in August 2000 and its membership carried on at Primetime Championship Wrestling, which had opened the month after. Ultra Fantasy Wrestling says so twice in its own voice — its front page's closing notice places its former members at PCW, and its links page says in the first person that many of them joined that federation. PCW's own shows then narrate the move from the other side for two years: an announcer bills a wrestler as a former three-time Ultra Fantasy Wrestling Television Champion, two stables reform under their old names, a departing World Champion's farewell traces his own path through the closed federation, and one biography of PCW's own records a ten-month run there under a different ring name. A diaspora rather than a handover of the same organisation: the two federations had different operators, and a month of overlap. Recorded on the same reading the GIWF-to-UWF line carries — one membership's continuous history under a second banner.

MWF

Shared continuity with UFW (Ultra)
A second stop on the same road. PCW's own shows place several of Ultra Fantasy Wrestling's members at MWF between the two federations — a departing champion's own farewell says "From UFW to MWF" and then "From MWF to PCW"; a commentator says MWF only had one of them because Ultra Fantasy Wrestling closed; and two wrestlers are billed as holding titles in both. MWF is not held.

IWO

Rivalry with UFW (Ultra)
An interfederation challenge Ultra Fantasy Wrestling ran from its own first months, staged against a neighbouring federation and carried on its own interfed pages, including interfed tag team titles contested in January 2000. PCW's own database names the same federation third in one wrestler's other-federations field, with a heavyweight championship there. IWO is not held.
