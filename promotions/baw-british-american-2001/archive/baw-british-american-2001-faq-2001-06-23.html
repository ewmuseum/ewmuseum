As the name suggests, it is an organisation that operates upon both sides of the Atlantic. It was formed in mid 2001 by a cross-Atlantic consortium, keen to exploit both the lack of mid-level wrestling federations in the United States, and the absence of a clear market leader on the British wrestling scene. While it does have a consortium behind it, they are fully aware of the number of wrestling start-ups that fold within a month, and as such, haven't put their full wealth behind it yet. The concept behind the touring and television schedules was to split amongst both countries. One cycle would be wrestled in the United States, whilst the following would be wrestled in the United Kingdom. As for television rights, a syndicated program will be put out on various regional channels in the US, and on Channel 4 in the United Kingdom. The consortium were keen to have a man at the helm of the company who knew the grappling scene on both sides of the Atlantic, and, as such, they chose former AWP, CSW, and SEWF star, and former darling of the UK indie scene, "The Answer" Jonny Troisi. Troisi was known for his big brawling style, but he was university educated (Lancaster University, English Literature), and this combination of intelligence and insight into wrestling on both sides of the pond saw him being given the opportunity to take the helm, an opportunity he grabbed with both hands. 1.2 Regular Explanation BAW is run by me, [name withheld]. Some of you may know me from my characters The Andrazoons, Obsessive Compulsive Disorder, "The Answer" Jonny Troisi, or The Boy Hedges. Anyway, this is my big jump into the world of fed-heading, one that I hope I can do well in. The reason behind me setting up this fed was me being disenfranchised with a lot of the e-feds out there at the moment. There seem to be two types of feds: ones run just for the sole enjoyment of a small clique of guys handling in the fed, and ones run on an impersonal level, where the handlers and fedhead have no degree of interaction whatsoever. There are a few feds out there that don't abide to these rules, but I believe there is room enough for another one. A fed that is fun for the handlers, and puts out a professional, interesting product. I also found the increasing number of British handlers in e-wrestling as something that needed addressing. US and UK cultures are still rather different, and, as stupid as it sounds, this can make RPing hard for UK handlers. With a cross-Atlantic assault going on, handlers from all over can feel at home. At this moment in time, I am the sole man in charge of the fed. I book the matches, control the backstage staff in character, and so on and so forth. BAW, eventually, should be a federation with a decently sized roster (around 30 I believe), regular cards, and opportunities for everyone who actually brings something to the table. . 2. What style does BAW use? I don't believe, as a fedhead, in stifling people's styles in RP/match-writing, and, as such, I will not be employing any guidelines as how to write. You are free to write in either the Action Bracket System style of writing, or in the play-by-play style. Both styles have their advantages and disadvantages, and I will leave it up to the matchwriter to decide which one he wants to use. 3. Where is BAW based? BAW's main headquarters operate out of Northampton, England. However, a secondary HQ operates out of Chapel Hill, North Carolina. We will tour medium sized venues, the sort of size that ECW (RIP) would have played to during its run. 4. How are matches decided? BAW is, as e-wrestling should be, a booked e-fed. As with most feds, there are four factors that decide if you'll win or lose, and they all have varying degrees of importance. They are: 1) RPs 2) In-Rings/On-Show Segments 3) Angles/Heat 4) Strats 4.1 RPs. This really is the key to e-wrestling, the thing that makes e-wrestling so. This is your chance to put your character across: what's he thinking, what's he doing, what's he feeling, what's he planning? The better you put him across here, the better everyone in the fed understands him, the more heat he gets, the more successful he becomes. As a rule, the best RPers are the ones who do best in e-wrestling, but three other factors also play a big role. 4.2 In-Rings/On-Show Segments A good way to get any extra points across from your character is to do an in-ring, or a backstage segment for the show. It shows extra effort, and it goes towards making the show more enjoyable and a better read for everyone. However, it is worth noting that these aren't necessary, and a pointless segment designed just to curry favour won't work. Sorry. 4.3 Angles/Heat Angles are the lifeblood of wrestling, both e and "real". A well worked angle can see a character shoot way up the card and into title contention. BAW actively encourages handlers to talk to other handlers in order to create angles, which add to the whole atmosphere of the show. 4.4 Strats The least fun part of e-wrestling, we know, but an important one. I'm not going to go overboard on strats, but they make the matchwriters job so much easier. A strat for BAW should really consist of the following: 1) Entrance (if different from one on your app) 2) Wrestler's recent history 3) Wrestler's mindset for the match 4) Moves or spots for the match 5) Any writer reminders for the wrestler's style. As stated before, the more you put into BAW, the more you get out of it. 5. What are the names of BAW's shows?: 5.1 BAW Bedlam The main show of BAW, our equivalent of Raw/Nitro. It will feature well-written matches, angle work, and anything else that you might expect to see on Monday/Thursday night. The shows will be run on just over a weekly basis, more likely every 8/9 days. Implementing a strict weekly schedule just serves to, when one card gets delayed, disrupt all of the forthcoming cards By being slightly more flexible, we are hoping that this won't happen. 5.2 The BAW Deal The "catch-up" send-out for handlers. Contains things such as rumours, rankings, upcoming line-ups, any OOC notes that need mentioning, and anything else that's fit to print. This will come out within a day maximum (hopefully a lot less) of BAW Bedlam being released. 5.3 BAW Words From The Wise The RP packet of BAW. This is, surprisingly, where your RPs go. Um. there' s not much else you can say about this, is there? 5.4 Supercards This is what all of your work builds up to, the big extravaganzas that occur every 4/5 times a year (depending on how things go along). BAW isn't big enough for PPVs (and the British PPV market isn't ready for wrestling) yet, so we will just be hosting supercards instead. Here, we are looking at amazing matches, explosive blow-offs to feuds, top-line clashes, and everything else you'd expect from a supercard. 6. Who's Who In BAW? 6.1 EXECUTIVES CEO: GEOFFREY DEAN - Nicknamed by some "Daddy", due to his liking of younger girls, Geoffrey Dean is a British businessman who mainly operates out of the USA nowadays. He is well known for a Midas touch when it comes to business, and the very fact that he's involved with the federations bodes well for its future. However, he has little to no interest in wrestling, and, as such, will rarely be seen on camera. PRESIDENT/COMMISSIONER: JONNY TROISI - It's rare for anyone in wrestling to be both a president and a commissioner, but then Jonny Troisi is no ordinary man. "The Answer" is a three-year veteran, but those three years have already seen him gain a major rep as a FMW style brawler with a brain. His decision to take a less strenuous job at BAW may have had something to do with him wanting to keep his career lengthened. He's pretty much a normal working class kid who made it through hard work, and he tries to keep everything in order. He won't appear on camera unless he needs to. HEAD OF SECURITY: BRYON HARRIDAN - Bryon Harridan is, as law states all e-wrestling heads of security must be, a former pro-wrestler. He never struck it big, due to a lack of. talent really. His only claim to fame was his bodyguard role for Brent Warner during his SWP days. Bryon is in security because he likes hitting people, really. What a nice man. 6.2 OFFICIALS SENIOR OFFICIAL: EDDIE WALKER - A true legend on the US indie circuit, the man has been in the squared circle for 30 years, and has seen it all. Along with having a major role in BAW's training camp, Eddie has also been appointed to be the senior official in BAW. He takes no nonsense from anyone on either side of the face/heel divide, and he's not adverse to getting a bit physical if the situation demands. OFFICIAL ONE: DICK HOLSTER- The classic Cockney wideboy, Dick got involved with wrestling after his boxing career failed to take off the ground. Finding that he was also unsuited to in-ring action in wrestling as well, he decided to take to the stripes and officiate. Dick tends to side with the bad guys, and rather enjoys "accidentally" missing any acts of cheating. OFFICIAL TWO: JACKSON CANNERY - The typical naïve newcomer in any sport. Jackson is, for want of a better word, a complete mark. He loves the spectacle of wrestling, and his age of 19 makes him very overexcitable. Sides with the fan favourite mainly, but just loves watching the sport. However, his enthusiasm sometimes leads to the action getting slightly carried away. 6.3 ON-AIR PERSONNEL RING ANNOUNCER: CASEY PARNELL: The classic, useless, yet rather attractive ring announcer, Casey was selected for the job due to her ability to memorise the words to both "Star Spangled Banner" and "God Save The Queen" PLAY BY PLAY COMMENTATOR: ALAN CHEVRON Englishman Alan has been involved, as both a booker, in the British wrestling scene for over 25 years now, and remembers with fondness Big Daddy, Giant Haystacks, and other useless dead one move wonders. He now works for BAW, but still hankers for the old days, and views with distrust anything "hardcore". He tries to treat all wrestlers equally, but he will tend to side with the faces, and those who don't break the rules COLOUR COMMENTATOR: "ONE MAN CAVALRY" DC MCLEAN: DC McLean was a big name in both Japanese junk feds and the indie extreme scene in America up until mid 1999, when a serious injury forced him to leave the grap game. He has done emergency cover commentary for a number of wrestling federations in the past years, but this is his first full0time position. DC is a massive fan of all things hardcore, and also isn't too big a fan of the UK itself. Inevitably, he tends to side with the heels. IN RING INTERVIEWER: DOUGLAS PUZO - Douglas is one of the mainstays of wrestling journalism. He has been involved with written and radio wrestling media for a good 30 years now, and has always been one to keep up to date with the latest developments. Now he's employed at BAW, and handles any in-ring interviews with great aplomb. BACKSTAGE INTERVIEWER: RICHARD VERNON- A former heel manager on the UK and German wrestling scene in the late 80s, Vernon has attempted to carry his act over into the US over the past decade, with only moderate success. Trying for a change of track, Richard has decided to switch to an interviewer role with BAW. A weasely little man, Richard has a permanent devious stance, and as such isn't usually too beloved by the wrestlers. FIELD REPORTER: FELICIA DINIZ- Every federation has to have its own personal piece of eye-candy representation, and Felicia is ours. Not amazingly skilled in the field of interviewing, but she has skills, so that's alright. An amazingly flirtatious persona, she knows how to use her assets (fnar) to get ahead. HEAD CAMERAMAN: EMILY SALTER - Emily would rather not be behind the camera, preferring instead to be in front of it. Despite having an Equity card, the only work Emily can find is behind the cameras, and her resentment at not being seen by the word usually ruins her work. 7. What titles/awards are available in BAW? 7.1 Heavyweight Title The major title in the federation, and one of the most important on both sides of the Atlantic. This is the biggest honour for any wrestler in the federation, usually gained by hard work and resilience. It must be defended at least every three weeks, but there is nothing to stop it being defended more often 7.2 Atlantic Title The federation's secondary title, not as prestigious as the Heavyweight title, but still a major accomplishment. This is what everyone on the mid-card in BAW is aiming for, and can propel many people up to the heavyweight title division, if defended well. This title has the same three week title defence ruling as the Heavyweight title. 7.3 Television Title The preliminary title of BAW, which will not exist until BAW's roster reaches a size that can accommodate it. As with most TV titles, this title has to be defended on *every* BAW televised show, be it Bedlam or PPV. A strong run of victories here can announce a wrestler's entry in BAW in strong fashion. If BAW develops a sufficiently strong tag team roster, than a tag title will be introduced as I see fit. 8. How much to I need to participate in BAW? I understand that, in some weeks, you may be unable to RP for whatever reason there is. However, if this is going to be so, I'd appreciate you telling me so in advance. Wrestlers who never RP just take up valuable roster space, so, if you fail to RP for three consecutive matches, without offering me any reason why, your BAW contract *will* be terminated, without exception. 9. What limits are there on RPs? The normal ones: a) No sexual swearwords. That includes f*ck, c*nt, tw*t, etc etc. If it is essential that you use them, please censor it with a [BLEEP]. Other swearwords I'm pretty relaxed on, but don't make it look like your wrestler has Tourette's. b) Anti-Semitism, racism, or homophobia are complete no-go areas. If you are not too sure if a word falls into these categories, just ask me, and I' ll be happy to advise. c) Don't use other people's characters without their prior permission. We have enough members of BAW's backstage personnel to use for whatever (sensible) purpose you may desire. 10. We _do not_ want an Excedrin headache because someone lost a match. This is the part about being a fedhead that we hate the most. Can't we all just get along? Just think of the federation as a big soap opera. The people who have the best story-line going on get the most airtime on the show. Those who don't, don't get very much time. So, the people who have the best angles, and role-plays, will get the airtime, in other words, win. So, write like (Add favorite author here), and you should be all right! 11. How do I join?
