*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-WORDPLAY*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*- Smash Rutherford *-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*- (A tennis court, somewhere sunny, probably Florida. Definitely in America somewhere. But Florida more than likely. A sweaty, red faced man in tennis gear stands on court, returning balls with quasi-lightning accuracy from a ball-chucking machine thing on the other side of the net. None of the returns are actually in, but he makes loud grunting noises while hitting the ball, which does make it all seem awfully impressive. The camera approaches him, slowly. However, just as it gets close, he draws a pistol from his shorts and spins round to face it. He walks slowly towards the camera.) MAN: WHAT IN THE (naughty word) DO YOU THINK YOU'RE DOING? CAMERA: Um... we're... M: THIS IS PRIVATE PROPERTY, PAL! YOU BEST GET YOUR SKINNY WHITE ASS OUT OF HERE NOW, UNLESS YOU ENJOY EATING ALL YOUR MEALS THROUGH A SIEVE! C: I don't think you quite understand, Mr Rutherford. We're from the BAW... M: (Lowers gun) What? C: The BAW. The wrestling federation you're signed up with. M: Oh. Right. The 'wrestling federation', that's a new one. First it's 'the Quakers', then it's 'the mailman', then it's 'the police', then it's 'the FBI'... C: No, no, we are actually from the BAW. Here are our cards. M: (reads) Felicia Diniz.... Ah, you must be here to clean the pool. C: Er, no.... we're here to do a profile of you for the BAW. M: Profile? C: Yes. For the fans at home. M: For the fans at home? Don't give me that 'for the fans' crap. The 'fans' are only 'fans' because they're either too dumb or too feeble to actually be wrestlers in the first place. Why do I have any obligation to the 'fans'? Why do they have any right to a profile of me? Why can't you go and do a profile of someone in a field in which they have a chance of succeeding in, like the guy who cleans the toilets at Disneyland? C: Er, well, because the fans don't know an awful lot about you, so we thought it might be nice if we could profile you so they get to know more about you. M: Really? C: Yes. Is that OK with you? M: Eh, whatever. Hey, can I use your towel? (The man grabs the boom mike and wipes his face with it, then tosses it back to the camera person.) M: Too soft. Don't use so much fabric conditioner next time. (Inside the man's house. The camera pans round. It's a bit like Through the Keyhole, really, but without the rubbish bits.) FELICIA DINIZ: (always off camera) Smash Rutherford is a twenty-five-year-old from the Tampa Bay area of Florida. Most of his life has been spent on the international tennis circuit, where he certainly made quite a name for himself. (Video montage. Smash [who, in case you didn't guess the first time, is the man on the tennis court at the beginning] throwing a racket to the ground at the French Open. Smash throwing a racket to the ground at the Australian Open. Smash kicking over the umpire's tower at the US Open. Smash attempting to choke out a line judge at Wimbledon.) (Interior of Smash's house. Smash reclines on a tasteful leather sofa.) FD: Smash, many would say that you are one of the more colourful characters in tennis today... SMASH: Really? Like who? FD: Er... SR: Don't 'er' me, bitch! I want names! FD: Um... SR: And addresses! My lawyers are gonna be all over their asses... FD: O-kay, I'll move on then; some might say that the switch from the pro tennis tour to professional wrestling is a slightly odd choice. Why did you choose to make the switch. SR: Well, really, it wasn't that difficult a switch to make. Tennis is so fricking restraining, all these people telling you what you can and can't do... FD: You mean umpires? SR: Umpires. Should've called them um-pissants, if you ask me. Little bitches sit up there in their little bitch-ass chairs, and they mumble into their bitch-ass microphones all that shit, like 'let', 'second service', '30-love'... FD: So you found the environment SR: I mean, '30-love'? 'Love'? I ain't being propositioned by no tiny-ass bald dude in a frickin' high chair! FD: I see... SR: And they're such frickin Nazis, man! You even try standing up for yourself, they give you some 'code violation' bullshit - FD: Yes - SR: Lemme finish here - some frickin' code violation bullshit, man, and I cannot frickin well work like that, man! I try frickin' tell him that, he throws me off the frickin court! FD: Right - SR: And then those asshole 'journalists' call me a frickin psycho cos I pull him out of his bitch-ass chair and start smashing his head on the frickin wall! I mean, hey, buddy, you try keeping your cool when some shrivelled up little dipwad tells you that you're out just cos you don't hit his suck-ass little ball shit in between his skank-ho white lines! FD: Hmm. I see. So what you're trying to say is that you found the environment of tennis too constraining for your individualistic flair? SR: Er, yeah, I guess. FD: Right. SR: That and the fact that I've been banned from tennis for three years. FD: Right. SR: Umpire my ass... FD: So, you make your debut next week against Tequila Mockingbird - do you have any words for your opponent? SR: Yeah. You just better pray they got an umpire in that ring, cos that's the only way they can save your ass from me - FD: I think you'll find they do, actually. SR: What? FD: There is actually a referee officiating during the match. SR: Ah shit. Guess I'll just have to use this then. (Smash pulls out a tennis racket from behind the sofa.) FD: A tennis racket? SR: Not just any tennis racket. I've taken out the strings and replaced them with barbed wire. (Smash pulls out a leg of lamb from off camera. He then smashes the racket into it. It sticks. Blood starts trickling out.) SR: See? Neat, huh? FD: Er, you could say that... SR: I know. And it'll be like that next week, except instead of this meat here it'll be Tequila Mockingbird's head. You best get your plastic surgeon on hand boy, cos next week, you'll be asking for 'new balls please!' Heh - new balls - d'you get it? FD: Er, yes - SR: I came up with that myself, man. FD: Right. Ha ha. SR: Ah, it's hilarious... FD: Well, Smash, thank you for your time... SR: Indeed. Now get off my property before I set the dogs on you. Mockingbird, who in April of this year was convicted of "impersonating a police officer", was released less than a month ago, and it is evident that the wrestler had indeed been "working out" during his time inside. Mockingbird, who's birth name was Ted Kazinski, was found to be "recklessly impersonating a undercover cop impersonating a prostitute", on the streets of Delaware's Central Business District, a crime more common than speeding in the state. Having served his non-parole period of two months, Mockingbird was released in early June, and he soon moved interstate, never to be heard of in the realms of Delaware again. It is believed that Mockingbird is seeking the resources needed to aid his drug habit, that being to Vitamin C, which began in the late 80s, with Mockingbird following the catch-cries of some his wrestling followers. While rehabiliting in prison, Mockingbird put his addiction down to the citric flavouring of the Vitamin C tablets, admitting to being a "lemon junkie". Mockingbird before his conviction shared several relationships with homosexual women, convincing them that due to his cross-dressing nature, he could offer them a "different angle". What he didn't know that this twisting tale of fast love would end up in being locked up in a medium security prison, as if Delaware weren't one in itself already. This reporter must confess, BAW has made a terrible decision in signing the cross-dressing, Vitamin-addicted ex-con. While this could offer Mockingbird, a "new start", one must hope that the shameful actions of the past do not occur in this new territory that he shall occupy. It's certain that in BAW, no cross will be left undressed, no vitamin container will be not sucked dry and Tequila Mockingbird will have added to the growing list of shocking pun names in wrestling. Delaware's gain is definitely the rest of the world's loss. 2001 BAW Productions
