Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

BAW, British American Wrestling, announced itself in June 2001 as a promotion that would work both sides of the Atlantic, its headquarters in Northampton, England, and a second office in Chapel Hill, North Carolina. Its founding FAQ imagined touring cycles alternating between the two countries, syndicated television in the United States and a slot on British terrestrial television at home.

The fiction behind it was a cross-Atlantic business consortium that installed a university-educated brawler, "The Answer" Jonny Troisi, as president and commissioner at once, backed by a chief executive who had little interest in wrestling. The FAQ filled out a full broadcast crew, two referees of opposite temperaments and a head of security, and set out three titles: a Heavyweight belt, an Atlantic title and a Television title held back until the roster was large enough.

It was a booked promotion, deciding matches on roleplays, in-ring segments, angles and strategies, with a main show called Bedlam on a deliberately loose eight-to-nine-day cycle and a separate roleplay packet, Words from the Wise, which reached the newsgroup under the name Wordplay.

The first Wordplay, of 12 July 2001, is the fullest surviving picture of the roster: a banned tennis star with a barbed-wire racket, a trailer-park teenager wrestling to rescue his sister, a self-flagellating mystic called Despair, a returning former champion and a newspaper report on a paroled ex-convict signing with the promotion.

Whether BAW's first Bedlam card was ever run is not in the surviving record, which ends with that packet.
