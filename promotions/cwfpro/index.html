An eleven-year-plus GeoCities e-fed run by one continuous owner-promoter, folding predecessor efeds CSW and XXX and roughly a dozen in-house developmental brands into one continuous CWF-Pro hierarchy of 44 title lineages and 1,441 recorded reigns across a roster of 292 wrestlers. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

CWF-Pro ran continuously from April 1997 to at least April 2009 under one promoter, billed on its own served ledger as "Pres Burns" — a chairman-owner character whose own bio card claims a start as a nameless CSW reporter in 1997, "so tired of all the crap" that he built a fed of his own. Its title history names eight further Commissioners across the run (R@ven, Miguel Sanchez, Amber Burns, Sgt. USA, Beastman, Mike Powers, Dawg, JL Black), each holding the same office in succession rather than a rival authority. Its own roster page, its own roleplay board, and its own Hall of Fame and Awards ledgers cover the whole run as one continuous institution, never splitting by brand or era.

That continuity absorbed rivals rather than merely outlasting them. CSW — its own title history says outright that Pres Burns "Acquired the CSW Efed" — and XXX, sold to him by its own operator in 2005, both survive in CWF-Pro's own records only as folded-in predecessor lineages: their belts are carried under a section of the fed's own title-history page headed "CWF-Pro's Developmental Leagues," and every one of them ends the same way, unified into a CWF-Pro-branded title by the same wrestlers who held the fed's own top belts. CWE, DTW, EWF, TDW, UBH, UEH, WWW and a backyard-wrestling-styled brand round out that same in-house farm system — nine additional labels, one promotion, one roster moving freely between them.

The record still names real stars of its own: Miguel Sanchez, billed "the Icon," carried the CWF-Pro Title and CWF-Pro World Title across more recorded reigns than anyone else on file; Mike Powers, Beastman, Jackson and Hero cycle through the same two belts for most of the 2000s; the Double Dragons, the Moonlight Express and Destruction Inc anchor a tag division that ran under four different names of its own across the run. The fed's own home page closes its last surviving update, dated 20 April 2009, mid-storyline — new champions crowned, a "Perfect Generation" stable gaining a third member, a returning Moonlight Express cutting a shoot interview — with no announcement that anything had ended, on a GeoCities host that stopped being anyone's to update within months.

Storylines

CWF-Pro holds no served match-card documents at all — every title fact on this wing comes from the fed's own numbered title-history ledgers, never a results page. The arcs below are drawn from the one document that narrates the fed's own history in its own promoter's voice — a December 2001 board post, “Cwf.pro Efed timeline(updated),” posted by Pres Burns himself — cross-read against the characters' own served bio pages wherever their own prose corroborates or extends a beat. No show page exists to link any of it to; the citation is the board post and the bios themselves.

Draven's death, the Factor, and Alex Draven's return Written by AI, 2026

Draven's own served bio narrates a run that made him, briefly, the only man in the fed's history to hold three titles at once, before Pres Burns turned him into “Corporate” Draven — a heel run backed by the office itself, restoring his World Title with Burns' own help. The turn broke the other way when Draven finally got his match against the Icon Miguel Sanchez: Burns cost him the match and revealed Sanchez as his own real family, and Draven, stripped and broken, formed a stable of his own — the Factor, with Beastman and Hammerman among its members — against Burns' own Elite team, losing the loser-leaves match that ended it. What followed is the fed's own best-known angle: Draven asked Burns for a retirement match against Sanchez; Burns gave the match to Capt. Sexy instead, at a Tuesday Night Fight, and Draven was “killed by being superkicked off of the top of a cage” in a Hell in a Cell — the board's own 2001 timeline recording the real fallout: “the cwf.pro gets a bad rap from the fans, news, wrestlers and everyone.” Capt. Sexy's own bio says only he, Burns and Draven knew the fall was a work, and that the heat it earned him is what some backstage rumor blamed for his own exit to the TDW three weeks later. Alex Draven, Draven's own brother, signed in 1999 for exactly one purpose per his own bio — payback on Pres Burns for his brother's death — and feuded with the Burns family before the whole angle resolved the way a real-world dispute would: an in-fiction lawsuit, settled out of court, Pres Burns paying the Draven family $11 million. Draven's own bio closes the account years later: “the Draven family and the Burns family spouted the blame of it at each other for years. Until recently both families have finally made amends.”

The Jock/Furnace ultimatum and the CSW #2 war Written by AI, 2026

Furnace won the Royal Title early in the fed's first year and, per his own bio, “got mad at Sanchez about something he said in an interview and then he started with Pres Burns” — fired on the spot and stripped of the belt. Jock, the reigning World Champion and Furnace's own friend, threatened to walk out unless Furnace was rehired; Burns fired Jock too, stripping the World Title from him in turn — this despite Jock's own bio recording that Burns and Beastman had already quietly agreed Beastman would beat him for it at the next PPV anyway, before the ultimatum forced the office's hand. Legend quit the same stretch, throwing the Intercontinental Title on Burns' own desk. All three vacancies were settled the same way, per both the board's own timeline and the title ledgers themselves: a battle royal crowned Beastman World Champion for a second time, White Zombie Royal Champion, and Jackson Intercontinental Champion, all in one night. Jock and Furnace then defected together to a rival promotion, CSW #2, run by JL Black — Furnace's own bio remembers the two of them turning up there “and acted like they had the cwf.pro titles and threw them at a fake Pres Burns.” CSW #2 folded within a few months; the board's own timeline adds one more beat neither bio carries — a week after it closed, GeoCities pulled the promotion's own page outright, for slander against Pres Burns by name.

The Insyders' in-ring sabotage Written by AI, 2026

The Insyders (Scott Nash & Kevin Hall), founding tag champions, splinter when Beastman breaks away from the Icon Miguel Sanchez's own UBH faction to lead a rival “UBH Blood Brothers” — Scott Nash goes with him, leaving Kevin Hall behind, per both the board's own timeline and the Insyders' own later bio, which records the breakup as short-lived: “the angle only lasted 2 months” before the pair reunited. Draven's own bio remembers a title cost from the same rift: with the Insyders “turned on each other,” Draven and Ticking Time Bomb challenged for their tag gold, and Scott Nash attacked his own partner mid-match, laying Kevin Hall out to be pinned — one of the fed's own quickest title changes on record.

Affiliations

Cyber Space Wrestling (CSW, Cyber Space, 1996–2004)

Acquired by CWF-Pro (1997) (2004-02)
All four of CSW’s own title lineages were stripped and unified into CWF-Pro-branded belts at the acquisition; CSW’s roster carried forward into CWF-Pro almost entirely intact, with most of its independent-era champions continuing on as CWF-Pro characters.

XXX

Acquired by CWF-Pro (1997) — sources disagree on the exact year (2003 per one served bio, 2005 per another); neither is picked.
Sources disagree on the year: the-flash-brothers-don-and-jerry-flash’s own bio dates the buyout to 2003 (“he bought it in 2003 after it had shut down”); bobby-williams’s own bio dates it to 2005 (“He did however sell Pres Burns the XXX efed in 2005”). Both are recorded; neither is preferred. Burns reopened the acquired promotion as CWF-Pro’s own farm efed, which ran 2006–2007.

Extreme Internet Wrestling Zone (EIWZ)

Acquired by CWF-Pro (1997) (undated (after December 1998))
CWF-Pro’s own already-served title histories record all five of EIWZ’s own titles unified into CWF-Pro’s: Jason Star defeats Pain to unify the EIWZ Extreme Title with the CWF-Pro Ultra Extreme Hardcore Title; Brad Bruns defeats Havok to unify the EIWZ European Title with the CWF-Pro Canadian Title; Hero defeats Necromancer to unify the EIWZ Lightweight Title with the CWF-Pro Royal Title; Beastman defeats Flint to unify the EIWZ North American Title with the CWF-Pro Intercontinental Title; and the Hot Guys (Barney and PK) defeat Cool Moe D & Pimpster to unify the EIWZ World Tag Team Titles with the CWF-Pro World Tag Team Titles. Every defeated EIWZ-side name matches a champion crowned or defending at EIWZ’s own held Christmas Bash 1998 or December 17, 1998 broadcasts. CWF-Pro’s own reign histories carry no exact date for any of the five unification matches; one CWF-Pro bio page places its own subject’s EIWZ tenure as an early-career stop years before a 2006 CWF-Pro contract, so the unification reads as a later event than EIWZ’s own December 1998 run, not a contemporaneous one.
