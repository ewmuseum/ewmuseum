The NeCW is looking for new talent - be in quick!!!!! Are you keen on e-Wrestling, either with experience from other feds or new to it all? Do you like the excitement and controversy of wrestler interviews and the passion of feuds in the WWF, but also like the hard-nosed grappling of the old NWA and Japan? If you answered yes to those two questions, then the NeCW is the place for you. This month-old federation, which is now taking on all-comers, combines the best of both worlds. If you have the time to put the effort into your wrestlers, through flashes, strategies and challenges, then sign up now!!!! If you're interested go to the official NeCW website at: http://www.angelfire.com/ma/Clunk/NeCW.html and fill out an application form. If you still need more convincing, read the official NeCW FAQ (Frequently Asked Questions) which follows (there's an application form in the FAQ as well). Thanks for your time, Fletch NeCW creator/president *********************************************************** OFFICIAL NECW FAQ (FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS) VERSION 1.0 *********************************************************** Contents: 1.1 Introduction (What is NeCW?) 1.2 How does e-wrestling work? 1.3 What are the rules of NeCW? 1.4 What do you look for in participation? 1.4.1 What is a good flash/roleplay? 1.4.2 What is a good strategy? 1.4.3 How do I develop angles and storylines? 1.5 What titles are there in NeCW? 1.6 Who's who in NeCW? 1.7 Application form ****In preparing this FAQ I have looked at FAQ's of other fed's to see what they include (though I've never copied verbatim). So I hereby acknowledge CCW, CWF and IIWF, who I suggest you check out as well. ******************************************************* 1.1: INTRODUCTION (WHAT IS NECW) NeCW (National e-mail Championship Wrestling) is a fairly new e-wrestling federation, having opened in late March 1997. It was started by Fletch, who had handled various wrestlers in other e-feds but felt he needed more from e-wrestling. A writer by trade, a keen wrestling fan for a number of years and with enough spare time to properly run a fed, Fletch decided to open NeCW and become president. The philosophy behind NeCW is that it provides a way for wrestling fans to feel like they are really part of the sport. Most wrestling fans have thought at one time or another 'Hey, I'd love to be a manager or a wrestler', but obviously this has only been a pipe-dream. But with e-wrestling, and NeCW, an ordinary person can invent a wrestler (or up to four in NeCW's case) and control their career. NeCW is modelled on a mix of several different existing and folded real federations. It is not riding on the "extreme" or "hardcore" bandwagon, although there is always the chance of chairs and other foreign objects and crazy stuff like falls-count-anywhere matches, all-in-brawls and blood. Fletch grew up watching the old NWA and Bill Watts' UWF, and it is this sort of hard-nosed grappling which forms the basis of NeCW. Add to that flamboyant characters like the present WWF and WCW, plus technical wrestling like in Japan and Mexico.....the result is NeCW. Now, NeCW is not a huge international promotion like WWF or WCW, but rather a good-sized independent regional fed, like USWA or the now-defunct Smoky Mountain. It is based in Detroit Michigan, at the NeCW arena, but does quite often venture out to towns and cities around the Great Lakes area and further south into the middle of the US. But most of all the NeCW is about fun. Handlers should have fun with their wrestlers, and ABOVE ALL BE CREATIVE! ***************************************************** 1.2: HOW DOES E-WRESTLING WORK? E-wrestling is as close to real wrestling as the average person can get. It has all the drama, flair and passion of real wrestling, but with words instead of pictures. You simply create your wrestler(s) using a creation sheet, and set about getting those wrestlers involved in feuds, winning matches, getting into contention for titles - everything that goes with real wrestling. There are a number of ways for fed-heads to decide the outcome of matches: * By "booking" - fed-head or a committee decide who should win, usually by who is the best roleplayer (ie who conducts the best interviews and comes up with the best angles). * By "simming" - a simulator (TNM, WLS or dice-based are the most popular) is used to find the results. This can lead to unlikely outcomes though. * A mix of simmed and booked - the fed-head takes into account standard of flashing, match strategies and sometimes uses a simulator to decide matches. This method is used by NeCW. Here is how NeCW matches are decided: A wrestler is given a score for the standard of his flashes (interviews) - this score goes up and down depending on each flash sent in (ie its accumulative). When Wrestler A is put up against Wrestler B, Fletch looks at the flash scores for both, then decides whose match strategy is the better (ie better thought out, more realistic etc). If Wrestler A has the higher flash score, and the better strat, he will win (match done on TNM simulator, but Wrestler A chosen to win). Same if Wrestler B has the higher flash score and better strat. If Wrestler A has the higher flash score, but Wrestler B has the better strat, or vice versa, or the differences are very close......Fletch leaves it to the TNM simulator to decide the outcome. Sometimes in cases of close decisions, Fletch will take into account the effort being put in by one of the handlers (ie developing lots of angles, coming up with good ideas etc) and give the win to that person's wrestler. BUT, if either wrestler has no strategy, his opponent wins automatically. This method was chosen because Fletch believes booked feds can be construed as biased, whereas straight simmed feds are unreliable (a jobber can win on a simulator when he normally wouldn't) A big part of e-wrestling is setting up angles, feuds and storylines (see later section for detail on these). Fletch sends out detailed match results to all handlers, as well as magazine shows featuring the flashes of all wrestlers. ********************************************************** 1.3: WHAT ARE THE RULES OF NECW? Cards: All NeCW handlers are sent out match results three times per week: * Superstars on Wednesday (delayed coverage) * Friday Fracas (delayed coverage) * Weekend Wrestling Wrap-up (highlights of three cards held over the weekend - sent out on Monday). Magazine shows: Three magazine shows are sent out per week: * Midweek Mat Chat (Wednesday) * Friday Frenzy * Weekend Wrestling Wrap-up (combined with card highlights on Monday). These shows all feature flashes sent in by handlers. Basic rules of NeCW: * Moderate swearing is allowed (ie "kick your butt", "ass-whuppin") but nothing too crass, as wrestling is a family sport. * Each handler is allowed four wrestlers (four singles, two singles and a tagteam), either in a stable or separate. But remember - only do as many wrestlers as you can handle - I'd rather see one good wrestler than four dumb ones. * Every handler must flash at least once a week (more if you plan to get anywhere) or you will be suspended then fired. * Unrealistic characters will not be accepted. Types of matches: Any type of match you can think of (although you won't see barbwire, exploding baseball bat thumb-tack matches in NeCW). Since we use TNM regular, the most popular matches will be: * Regular one-fall singles and tagteam matches. * Cage matches * Falls-count-anywhere matches * Submission matches * No countout, no-DQ matches * Best of 3 Falls matches * Iron Man marathon matches * Triangle matches * Sixman tag matches * Battle royals * Survivor series type matches **************************************************** 1.4: WHAT DO YOU LOOK FOR IN PARTICIPATION? The ones who succeed in NeCW are the ones who put in the time and effort, and have a good knowledge of wrestling and the way it works. I don't want to make e-wrestling a writing contest (I won't be picky about every spelling mistake), although I hate to see flashes that are barely readable because of spelling and grammar mistakes. To succeed you need to challenge for matches, send good flashes and strategies in regularly, and be willing to discuss angles and story ideas with Fletch. ..................................................... 1.4.1: What do you look for in a flash/roleplay? All flashes are written differently, and two flashes may be totally different but similarly good. The one thing which all flashes must be is.......INTERESTING. The sole purpose of flashing is to develop characters and add heat to federations. Without them, wrestling would be boring. Here's what I look for in a good flash: * Sets the scene - Flashes are interviews which can't be seen, so you must paint a picture for the reader. This includes saying where it's taking place, what the people are wearing (not right down to colour of socks though) and what their appearance is (smiling, angry, pacing, flexing etc). * Develops character - Readers don't necessarily know the background or psyche of your wrestler(s), you have to weave them into your flashes. Also don't have them saying the same things all the time - develop character. * Sets up feuds/angles - Flashes are where you make snide remarks about other characters, talk about a current feud, hint at future feuds, set up angles etc. * Something different - Having wrestlers say the same thing over and over, having them interviewed at the same site......repetition means boring, so spice it up a bit. So, while there are no set rules to flashing, the more inventive people will always succeed before the boring ones. ..................................................... 1.4.2: What do you look for in a match strategy? A good strategy tells me what sort of game plan your wrestler is taking into a certain match. This is not what moves he will do - these are determined by TNM, which is in turn determined by the type of wrestler your guy is. Instead you should tell me: * If he is going to attack before the bell... * What pace he will try to wrestle, and what type of match i.e. concentrating on one body part, or going for power moves etc. * Will there be any interference? * Any surprises in store. I rate strategies like follows: * excellent - has a well thought-out game plan, which fits the wrestler and comes up with what is needed to beat the opponent. * good - sensible game plan, using suitable tactics to try to win * poor strat - little thought has gone into it. An example of a poor strat is like this: My wrestler will attack him before the bell, and when he's worn him down he'll give him the [finishing move] and pin him. An example of an excellent strat is like this [this is one I used with one of my wrestlers, Iceman Ikura, in another fed]: Iceman is going to try to control the pace of the match, using his martial arts skills to try and wear down Inferno, who is much bigger than him. He will try and stay out of his reach, to nullify the strength advantage Inferno has. He will of course resort to underhand tactics as well. If Ikura looks like losing at any stage, Tokyo Terror [Ikura's manager] will interfere with his bamboo sword, and both of them will beat Inferno after the bell. You see, it covers pretty much everything. Now, sneak attacks are also a kind of strategy. Here's one I used with Iceman Ikura: During Inferno's match, when Inferno is being dominated by his opponent, Tokyo Terror, dressed in wrestling tights and a mask with the US flag on it will come down and interfere on behalf of Inferno. He will hit his opponent with a chair, causing Inferno to be disqualified. Inferno will be mystified as to what's going on, and Terror will come over to congratulate him. When both men are talking/arguing, Terror will pull off the mask to reveal himself. Inferno will be shocked, and then Iceman will come out from under the ring, and hit Inferno from behind with a chair. Iceman and Terror will beat Inferno with chairs/bamboo sword until he is out of it, and then Iceman will grab a bucket from under the ring. He will empty this, full of raw fish, all over Inferno and he and Terror will then leave. You see - it's all about theatrics! Now, I don't expect stuff like this all the time, but wrestling is not just flash and glamour, it's about outthinking your opponent. ............................................................ 1.4.3: How do I develop storylines/angles? The first thing you must do to get your wrestler in matches is challenge. For every upcoming card you will be required to challenge if you want your wrestler to get matches. A challenge is a simple message to Fletch like this: .......... challenges ...........to a ........match at the next card. Once you're in matches, it's good to get in feuds to get noticed. To get in a feud you can either attack another wrestler or team (tell Fletch what you want to happen in the attack, don't do it in a flash), start bad-mouthing them or gang up on them during a match. Anything that happens in real-life wrestling can happen in e-wrestling. If you want your men to viciously ambush a wrestler (eg when the Four Horsemen attacked Lex Luger as he arrived in a limousine in the old NWA) it can happen, as can almost anything. To keep the heat in a feud you'll need to discuss storyline ideas with Fletch, who always has suggestions and angle ideas of his own. Above all wrestling is about heat and angles, so keep it interesting. If you see something on an old wrestling tape you want to try (don't try to copy WWF or WCW too much though) suggest it to Fletch. ******************************************************** 1.5: WHAT TITLES ARE THERE IN NECW? There are currently three singles titles and one tagteam title in NeCW, though more may be added later. The titles are: * National Heavyweight Championship - the top title in NeCW, which everyone is vying for. Only the top-five contenders get title matches. * Television Championship - the second-string title for those not quite at National title level. Anyone can challenge for this one. * Submission Ring - a gold ring which is held by the top submission wrestler in the federation, and if someone beats him by submission they win the ring. * Unified Tagteam Championship - this title was formed when a top Mexican promoter donated the Mexican International Tagteam belts to the NeCW, these belts were altered slightly to become the NeCW Unified belts. This is what all tagteams want. The National title must be defended once a month, the TV and Tagteam titles once a week, and the Submission Ring has no minimum requirement. ********************************************************* 1.6: WHO'S WHO IN NECW? NeCW staff: Fletch - president/creator Bracks - vice-president/writer [name withheld] - writer NeCW broadcast team: * Bob Frankhauser - the main commentator. He calls all the matches, and is as straight as an arrow. He hardly ever sides with anyone, but has been known to lean towards the good guys on occasion. * "Living Legend" Al Rich - Rulebreaker-aligned colour commentary man. He's an ex wrestler who retired from the ring early to take up commentary. However, recently he returned to the ring to feud with the tagteam Canadian Rockers, and is also managing. By doing this he was fired from the NeCW broadcast team. * Sir William Hoffmeyer - Rich's replacement as colour comments man. Is not actually knighted, he just thinks the title gives him prestige. He's a smarmy Englishmen who does not like American wrestlers. He usually sides with the rulebreakers, but has been known to side with the good guys if they are technically sound. He basically likes technical wrestling above brawling. * Larry Van Keel - Does not call matches, instead presents a show called "Friday Frenzy", which features interviews with wrestlers. He's firmly on the side of the good guys. * "Diamond" Dave Casanova - Another former wrestler, who tends to side with the rulebreakers but is not as biased as Al Rich. * Kevin Kash - NeCW's man with the microphone. If there's an in-ring or lockerroom interview to be done, Kevin will be there to do it. He's unbiased, and his questions are totally neutral. * Lefty Goldblatt - presents the magazine show "Midweek Mat Chat". He's very uncolourful. * Downtown Brown - a colourful-speaking New Orleans African-American, who likes to "jive". He currently has no role with NeCW, having been replaced by Goldblatt on Midweek Mat Chat, but NeCW bosses are looking for a role for him. ************************************************************** Here's the application sheet (cut and paste for more than one): Your name: Your e-mail address: Stable name (if applicable): Manager(s) (if applicable): Wrestler name: Hometown: Height: Weight: Background (be specific): Entrance music: Ring attire: Alignment: (face/heel/neutral): Which of the following best describes your wrestler (choose one only): 1.
