The AIPW stands for All International Pro Wrestling, and it is one of the newest fantasy e-fed's on the internet. The AIPW is a place where handlers can let out their creativity through their e-wrestlers. To be able to lead your wrestler towards the heavyweight title and more.
The AIPW's main goal is too have active involvement from all the handlers. Meaning, that you role play once a week, write strategies for your wrestler's matches, write in-rings every once and a while, and start up angle's with other handlers. Since angle's are definitely the most important guideline in AIPW, it is very important that you talk to other handlers and get involved in angles with each other. Getting involved in angles is definitely the best way to get recognized and pushed.
The AIPW is ran by Robert Winters, and hopefully soon to be assisted by a dedicated group for writers. We aim AIPW towards a fantasy world where the handler's creations inhabit it. And since it is a fantasy world, it is very important to get familiar with the employee's in the AIPW. To get familiar with the employee's, check out the employee's section in this player's guide.

Technically, the AIPW makes itself known as a roleplaying league; that is to say that the success of the wrestler depends on the quality of the handler's roleplaying for that character. Good roleplaying doesn't just mean submitting the mandatory one flash per week and hoping for the best. Good roleplaying means not only submitting a flash which is imaginative, develops the character of the wrestler, and advances current storylines, but also submitting tactics for matches, and angle ideas, ways to improve the character and so on. The more you put into a character, the more you will get back from the league.
In addition to the general premise of deciding match results on the basis of roleplaying quality, the AIPW is also "booked". Match results are not simply determined with that single match in mind. Generally, the bigger picture is taken into account, and in the AIPW, that bigger picture means angles, storylines and so on. For example, if two good roleplayers are feuding, it becomes entirely arbitrary to decide the result of the match on the basis of roleplaying alone, so the President will take it upon himself to decide the result of the match with a coherent storyline in mind.
Equally, upon entering the AIPW, even the best roleplayer may find his character loses a match or two in the first few weeks of his tenure. Every new wrestler to the league must pay his dues, and that could mean doing jobs for a few of the more established stars. It is impossible to enter the AIPW and be challenging for a title within the space of a week or two. As such, even wrestlers who are successful in other leagues may find that their reputations elsewhere mean absolutely nothing in the AIPW. The reasoning behind this is that not every handler can be familiar with every other league, and so the measuring stick for a wrestler's achievements must be the league which is shared in common; in other words, the AIPW. If a character comes to the league bragging about his eighteen world titles in a number of feds which folded overnight, he won't find himself pushed into the title picture. In fact, sometimes such antics can have quite the opposite effect.

#AIPW. VIII. Roleplaying Rules (Taken from the IIWF, and a couple things added.) Nobody wants to stop handlers having fun in the AIPW, but it's important that the following ground rules are understood and taken into consideration out of respect both for the wishes of the fed head and other handlers:
Don't: -Ask for your wrestler to hospitalise another wrestler. Sneak attacks are fine, but attacks with barbed wire baseball bats or double-barrelled shotguns will not be accepted. It's unfair on the handler of the victim to have his wrestler laid up in hospital and miss out on action in the ring without his consent.
-Attack announcers in your flashes. Unless you have the express permission of the fedhead, don't have your wrestler brutalise any of the AIPW's staff, with the exception of the numerous jobbers, although you should still respect the above rule about hospitalisation.
-Use excessive profanity. Often it is realistic and within reason for wrestlers to use obscene language in moments of extreme emotion. However, words stronger than "ass", "bastard" or "bloody" will usually be edited out of flashes, because such language is contrary to what is acceptable in everyday speech and on the majority of television programmes. It is still possible to express anger or discontent without recourse to a string of four-letter words.
-Submit more then 2 RP's a week. RP's should be no longer then 9 k, as they will get repetitive and it will lose the viewer's attention. All roleplay's should fulfil one or both of these two criteria: firstly, they should build heat for current storylines, and not simply babble on badmouthing every other wrestler in the league; and secondly, they should relate to the wrestler's match on that card or angle involved with the card.
-Take things too seriously. Every once in a while, some handlers seem to forget that fantasy wrestling is only a game, and heat generated between characters can rub off into real life. If you are unhappy with the way things are going, send a message to the fedhead explaining your concerns. You will always be met with a prompt and reasonable response.
Do: -Take an active interest in your wrestler. Your wrestler is more likely to succeed if you follow his progress and not only submit flashes, but also keep the fed head apprised of developments in his character, successes elsewhere and so on.
-Send in strategies for individual matches. One of the most effective and useful forms of roleplaying can be sending in a strategy for a particular opponent. You might be surprised at how much of an effect this can have on the match; strategies give the writers a more detailed impression of the character, and lead to a more "realistic" depiction in the ring.
-Ask for matches. As the rosters become more and more crowded, the only way to ensure that your character is noticed is to ask the fedhead to book matches for you. If you sit back and expect to be given title shots or a push, you'll be disappointed. Activity is essential.
IX. What makes a good flash? (Taken from IIWF also.) There are no rules about what makes a good flash, but several guidelines should be considered:
-Keep the angle interesting to all members of the AIPW and develop your character(s). Remember that many people read the reports. The best way to ensure future feuds is by making sure that others know about and are interested in your wrestler(s).
-Be creative. Experiment with new settings to add to the personality of your wrestler(s). -Longer flashes are not necessarily better flashes. Make sure your wrestler(s) get his/their point across and do not repeat it endlessly. Overkill forces readers to skip flashes. (Heavily important!)
-Flash regularly to maintain interest in angles and your wrestler(s). Handlers will be required to submit at least one item, be it a promo, interview or news item, for each of their wrestlers at least once a week. Consistent roleplay is essential.
-Keep flashes free of profanity and swearing. Think of the AIPW as sports entertainment and bear in mind the limits under which actual wrestlers must operate on television. Strong language, if submitted to the report, will be edited.
X. AIPW Employee's The Broadcasters- Brett Harding: One of the most knowledgeable wrestling historian's in the world today. Harding know's every move, inside and out, and is a key factor in the AIPW broadcasting team. He isn't the type to judge wrestler's based on character, instead he judge's based on the factor's in the match. He has been known to be very stubborn and stick to his word.
Rex Trenton: Grew up a rich kid, will probably die a rich kid. Rex Trenton believes that he can get away with any harsh word or action he commit's. Rex has been known to be very critical of wrestler's in general and will let out his opinion's often.
Interviewers- Lisa Lexington: The perfect, cute, girl next door, she follow's the rules, is polite, and always dressed in an appropriate fashion. And sometime's her kindness will get her hurt in the AIPW, as it is easy to step all over her.
Arthur Conning: A serious, straight forward, to the point, kinda guy. Arthur doesn't diddle and dolly, he gets straight to the point. He often appears very straight forward in his question's during his interviews, which often are viewed as being blunt.
Officials- Paul Jerome: The AIPW's premiere referee, Paul Gerome call's everything straight down the middle and never shows any favoritism.
Grady Anderson: A bit of an oaf, Grady is known to be in wrestler's way's during their matches. Often in matches he is knocked down or unconscious.
Jeff Johnson: A rookie referee, straight out of training. Jeff is often very overwhelmed by the wrestler's in the ring. He is known sometime's to have a bias towards one of the wrestler's based on how much he looks up to them.
Other important figures- Michael Edwards: The AIPW's enthusiastic ring announcer, he enjoy's every moment he is in the ring, with the mic to his lips.
Robert Winters: The most imporant man that you never see, the president of the
AIPW. Rob Winter's is known to rarely make appearances on television, the time's he does make appearances are usually only to announce very important things.
