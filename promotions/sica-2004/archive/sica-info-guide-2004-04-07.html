Registration / \ / \ / \____________________/ Introduction ------------ Let me first start off by saying, though this packet will answer all of your questions about Shootfighting International, this is not an FAQ. Let's just call it an informative brochure of sorts. Something that will map out what the SICA is all about, but without a little Q&A section. Now, I'm sure a lot of you are wondering what exactly the SICA is all about, while some of you might remember it from a couple of years back with a somewhat similar name known then only as SI. The SICA is an idea I had awhile back that took the concept of creating a UFC/PrideFC/K-1 mixture and turning it into a fantasy fighting promotion. A lot of people seemed to really like the idea when I first started it up, so now I'm bringing it back to see if everyone still does. A little in-character background information on the promotion itself: The SICA is located in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil. The former owner of the promotion has recently sold the rights off to Rio native and businessman, Carlos Argenta. Though he's a wealthy man, Argenta views this as a project. Only willing to put forth a certain amount of money to risk on this project, he hopes to build the company into another profitable business venture. Out of character, this promotion is run by myself, [name withheld]. I ran this promotion before under a slightly different name and with a slightly different concept. On top of that, this is by no means my first attempt at something along these lines. I've run other e-w promotions, but I'm not going to give you a long list of my credentials because this is something completely different and I want to make sure it comes across as such. The SICA will also be run off of Yahoo Groups and the location for it can be found at http://groups.yahoo.com/group/S-I-C-A Basically, the SICA is about providing something new and entertaining that you perhaps haven't seen before. I'm hoping to try and make this concept interesting enough to where we can all have a little fun. Whether your fighter wins or loses, I want to try and make the entire environment enjoyable no matter what. If this sounds interesting to you, continue on with the rest of the brochure. Also, if you have any questions regarding the fed, feel free to contact me at [detail withheld] Concept ------- For those of you who are familiar with and enjoy other shootfighting promotions such as the UFC and K-1, then the SICA should be a lot of fun. The concept isn't too complex, but I recommend that you read everything to help you understand what it's all about. First, you create a fighter custom to your own desires and then the SICA signs him to a contract for a certain amount of time depending on how impressive his resume is. So, for example, if you're new to this whole concept and you're not too sure how to fill out the application properly, the SICA will sign that fighter for a minimal period of time (one or two fights/events) on a trial basis. Then, after the contract expires, you will have the option to negotiate a contract renewal for more money and a longer term, depending on how well you did during your tenure. Now, some of you might be wondering "Why does it matter?" regarding the contracts and monetary issues, right? Well, the more money that your fighter makes, the more 'demand' there is for that fighter. Those fighters who make more money are more likely to get big matches right off the bat to get them notoriety amongst the promotion. However, the starting budget of the SICA will be $2 million dollars, so we can't fork out top dollar to everyone. For most, you're going to have to earn your way to a higher salary by getting wins and showing that you're worth it. Okay, so now that the basic concept is out of the way, I'm sure everyone wants to know how to make a successful fighter in the SICA. I'll go over this in more detail later on, but for right now here are the basics. It's really very simple ... all you have to do is be active in some way or another. So, if you want to write an interview with your fighter, write an interview. If you want to write a strat, write a strat. If you want to work with your opponent and put together a promo for your upcoming fight, do it. Anything you can think of to have your fighter active in the promotion will count towards how successful he is. Nothing is going to be regarded any higher than another. And just so you know ... grammar, spelling, and punctuation will not be a factor in deciding, as I will format everything sent in to me. I only look for creativity. It's really that easy. Rules & Regulations ------------------- - Once inside the octagon, both fighters must remain in their corners until the signal is given to fight. - The only way to win the fight is to make your opponent tap out or if your opponent is knocked unconscious. Very rarely will the fight be stopped, but there will be the occasion where if the official decides the opponent has lost too much blood or has taken too bad of a beating that the match will be called. - There are a few ways that a fighter can get disqualified. There is to be no fish-hooking (fingers in the mouth or nose), no eye gouging, no attacking a fighter after they have tapped out or have been declared to be unconscious, and finally, no low-blows. Some organizations allow this, but the SICA does _not_ condone hitting someone below the belt. - To tap out, the fighter must hit his hand against the mat at least three times consecutively. Any less is considered an attempt to break free of a hold or get back onto their feet unless otherwise deemed a tap out by the official. - The only restriction on fighting styles in the SICA is that there can be no more than two of the same fighting styles used amongst the fighters. Basically, I don't want to see more than two fighters who use wrestling, kenpo, jiu-jitsu, kung-fu, capoeira, etc. as their style of fighting. If an application is submitted that uses a style that two people have already applied for, then it will be rejected and I will ask them to find another style for their fighter. (An easy way around this is to either check the roster or send me an e-mail asking what if your preferred style is already taken by two people.) - NO WEAPONS! If a fighter is caught using a weapon of any kind against their opponent, they not only will be disqualified, but they will be expelled from the SICA indefinitely. We're trying to find out who the best fighter is, we're not trying to kill the opposition. (However, this is always an interesting way to get rid of a fighter you don't want to use anymore.) - There will be a fifteen minute time limit for every fight. The time will run down during the matches and if nobody has won the match, then it will be declared a draw. The only exceptions to this will be tournaments, in which case there will be no time limit. There must always be a winner when it comes to a tournament match. - Other than that, anything goes. Events ------ There will be one bi-weekly event that holds about four matches on it, which will simply be called the SICA's Black & Blue. The cost for a seat at a Black & Blue event is $20. All of these matches will be written in summary style and the show will be hosted by two commentators, who will recap all of the action and give their thoughts on each fight that took place. They will give insight on each fighter, which will provide subtle hints as to what they need to improve on for the future so that the handler of the fighter might be able to add to or correct their application and become a better, more solid fighter. The ring announcer for all live, televised events will be ARISA SONADA. The appointed Enforcer of the Octagon and official referee for all fights in the SICA will be GARY JACOBSON, who was acting enforcer and referee in SI as well. He's an experienced fighter and he's not easily pushed around. He makes the perfect enforcer because of his size, strength, and intimidating demeanor. You're not going to be breaking any rules with this guy in there with you. The hosts of the show will be Daniel Omens and Carl Masterson. Masterson has commentated for SI in the past, while Omens is a newcomer to the commentating scene altogether. Below is a little bit of information on each of them, since they will both be available for interviewing the fighters if you so choose to write one out and send it in. Hopefully this will help everyone get an understanding as to what kind of personalities they are. CARL MASTERSON - Masterson is the most knowledgeable person when it comes to shootfighting. He knows every fighter's flaws and weaknesses and can break down each fighter into the bare essentials of what they need to work on to improve. Though he is in no way a fighter himself, he is the head analyst of the SICA and knows just about every style of fighting known to man, including everything about each style that can play into the effects of what goes on inside the octagon once the doors are locked. Masterson is, however, a very nice person. He won't offend anyone directly and he keeps his comments and questions strictly professional. (OOC Note: The things this person says about your fighter will help you flesh out his style of fighting as well as his techniques if you take the time to make the corrections on your application.) DANIEL OMENS - Omens, being a newcomer to commentating, isn't the most knowledgeable when it comes to fighting styles. However, he's very skilled at understanding people's personalities and analyzing what they might need to do to become a better fighter. He's quick to recognize that a fighter gets frustrated easily or that they run out of energy quickly. Small things that this guy says may make or break your fighter, should you choose to follow up on any of it. He's not the nicest guy in the world, but he _is_ very professional. He's not afraid to tell a fighter right to his face that he has problems or that he's doing great. This guy could possibly get under some fighters' skins and be respected by others. (OOC Note: The things this person says about your fighter will help you decide where to place your points for the fighter's attributes. Pay close attention and you can really help your fighter advance in the future.) As I said before, you will be free to use either of these guys for interviews if you want to, but if you plan on doing so, please note that you cannot harm them in any way. They are professional journalists, after all. Not punching bags. ;) Success in the SICA ------------------- Okay, now I've gone over the fact that to be a winning fighter, you have to have an active fighter. However, that isn't all there is to it. There are a few details that I have yet to mention. Things that will give you incentive to keep updating your applications with new information and make it so that you want to be a top contender in the SICA. After all, without at least a _little_ competition, it wouldn't be much fun, now would it? The following are things that you can do to help your fighter be successful inside of the octagon: 1. On your application there will be a part where you can distribute points to different attributes that your fighter has. Things like strength, stamina, agility, etc. Every time your fighter wins a match, you will be rewarded with points to help your fighter. However, how many points you earn will be based upon your performance in the fight. (Whether you wrote an RP or not, whether you sent in a strategy for your match, and how well your fighter did overall.) Strategies and roleplays won't help a lot, but they will contribute to the point factor a little bit. The more points you get, the better your chances at getting a contract renewal for more money. Also, even if you lose, you will still be rewarded points for your character, even if you didn't send in a strategy or a roleplay. The points will be less than if you had won, but they will be points nonetheless. And if you lost, but you sent in either a strategy or a roleplay, you will be rewarded points for doing so, as well. 2. Strategies. These will help you in your fights _incredibly_ ... a good strategy can give your fighter that edge in your match that he might need to take out the opponent. What I want to see in your strategies, if you decide to write one for your match, are things like how your fighter plans to attack the opponent, what parts of the body they are going to focus on, what kind of defense they want to use against the opponent, and maybe a couple of moves that they plan to use against them. A good strategy will earn your fighter a lot of points, win or lose. 3. Roleplays. Like I said, roleplays will _not_ help you very much in your matches. What they _will_ do, however, is make your match a hell of a lot more interesting. The more heated the matches are, the better they're going to be, even if they are in summary form. And the better your matches are, the better your fighter will perform, and the more points you will receive in the long run. It won't guarantee you a victory if you write one, but it certainly can help your chances. Roleplays aren't to be longer than 2-4K. They're just supposed to be brief comments from your fighter before their match regarding their opponent. I don't want to see big production roleplays in the SICA. We aren't about that. Getting Signed -------------- Now that I've gone over all of the basics, it's time to explain how your fighter will get signed with the SICA. Your application, as you know, will be the most important aspect of your fighter. By filling out a detailed and informative application, your fighter will be taken more seriously and will have a better chance of getting signed to the SICA. If you have sent in an application, but you have not yet received a contract offer, then that means that your fighter will be placed into a Free Agent pool of fighters waiting for their chance to get signed. We will be taking special exception to Rookie fighters or inexperienced fighters in the hopes that they will become the next generation of champions. Though, that doesn't mean we aren't going to be signing the more experienced fighters. However, if you are to try and make an experienced fighter, your application needs to be something special. You must know your fighting style, be able to list his moves, finishers, attacks, etc. More experienced fighters will not get more points just because of that fact, but he will earn more money which will help him earn more points later on. If the Free Agent pool becomes very large, then applicants will be selected based on several things. Detail of the application, the amount of money he is worth compared to what the promotion can afford, style of fighting, and probability of success in the SICA. All of these things will be determined by the owner, Carlos Argenta, at the time of contract offerings. As for re-signing and renegotiating contracts, it's really very simple. Once your contact has expired, you will not be able to participate in any more events until your contract has been renegotiated. You will begin receiving contract offers before your contract officially expires, but until that time, you have the option to try and ask for more money if you think the offer is unreasonable. Every counter offer will be considered, but not all will be accepted. If the SICA chooses not to accept the counter offer and you don't want to re-sign for less, then you will be placed into the Free Agent pool until contract negotiations can be settled. Also, normally you will only be allowed to re-work your applications after the cycle has ended (every two months after PPVs), but for those whose contracts expire before that time, they will have the opportunity to add any points that they have earned to their abilities before sending a counter offer. Note that all points earned will still be taken into consideration before the initial offer is made, but this gives every re-signed fighter a chance to do even better than before and in some cases give them an extra edge before each tournament. Tournaments ----------- This is the real deal. An SICA tournament is something that will take place once every other month. Out of all of the competitors in the SICA, eight fighters will be selected to take part in the big pay-per-view tournament that will be held. These fighters will be picked based qualifying matches before the time of the PPV. The participants of these qualifying matches will be determined based upon many things. The most important is their win-loss record, though that isn't the only deciding factor. Effort also plays a big part in being picked for a qualifying match. The more effort you show in the SICA, the more likely it will be that you get selected to be in the tournament, even if your win-loss record isn't the greatest in the promotion. How well the fighters perform in their matches before the PPV also comes into play when deciding the fighters to be selected for the qualifying matches. If a fighter gives it their all in every match, but doesn't win every match, they are more than likely to be selected over someone who just wins more often but doesn't put on as good of a performance. After all, everyone loves watching the underdog give it his best shot. Once the eight fighters are selected to take part in the tournament, they will all fight against each other in elimination matches on a PPV event that will be completely live and televised, meaning the matches are all play by play, not summarized. Also, every match in the tournament will take place _that_ night and it will continue all night long until someone is declared the winner and the best fighter in SICA. The winner of the tournament will receive a $20,000 cash prize and a trophy to take home with them to symbolize that they are the best fighter in the promotion. However, every other month a new tournament will begin and eventually a new champion of the octagon will be crowned, at which point the previous holder of the trophy will present the new winner with the same trophy in a post-tournament ceremony. Not to mention that they will also receive the $20,000 cash prize. After winning the trophy once, it is extremely unlikely that the same fighter will win it in the next tournament, but the chance is still there. This is mainly to keep one person from holding onto the trophy for a very long time without someone else at least getting a shot at it. Registration ------------ ATTENTION ALL FIGHTERS! This is the application section. Below you will find a very detailed application that is the key to creating a great fighter here in the Shootfighters International Combat Association. Before venturing on into this section, make sure that you have read through this entire brochure for the SICA and that you understand exactly what you're getting into. Really put a lot of thought into this application and show me what kind of fighter you can come up with. Remember to try and make your fighter as unique as possible. I don't want to see any clones from any of the professional shootfighting promotions that exist today. No Shamrocks, Gracies, Bas Ruttens, Severns, etc. MAKE YOUR FIGHTER AS ORIGINAL AS POSSIBLE! I can't stress this enough. Also, make sure your fighter seems like he has a personality. I want to be able to relate with the fighter even though I don't have an interview to base his attitude on. Your application should just speak for itself. Now, if you think that you understand all of this and are ready to start your application, scroll down and start working on it. When you're finished, send your application to [detail withheld] and I will get back to you as soon as possible with a contract offer. Thanks for your interest and I hope you enjoy the league!
