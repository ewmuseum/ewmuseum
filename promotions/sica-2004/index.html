A fantasy shoot-fighting/MMA-style promotion, hosted on its own Yahoo Group and explicitly not styling itself e-wrestling, that ran its own "Black & Blue" show under an in-fiction Rio de Janeiro ownership. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Shootfighters International Combat Association introduced itself on 7 April 2004 as a revival of an earlier concept its own founder had run under a similar name, and drew a hard line around what it wasn't: “not e-wrestling... more reminiscent of UFC or PrideFC.” In its own staged fiction, the promotion was based in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, recently bought from a prior owner by wealthy local businessman Carlos Argenta, who treated it as a project rather than a passion.

Its own concept ran on a contract economy: fighters were signed against a stated $2 million starting budget, earned points through wins, strategies and short pre-fight roleplays capped at 2-4K, and negotiated bigger contracts as their record and their promotion's money grew. The federation ran one biweekly show, called Black & Blue, priced at $20 a seat and told in summary form by two hosts, veteran commentator Carl Masterson and newcomer Daniel Omens, with Arisa Sonada announcing and Gary Jacobson, held over from the earlier “SI” incarnation, serving as the Octagon's enforcer and referee.

Every other month the promotion planned to run a live, play-by-play pay-per-view tournament: eight fighters, selected as much for effort and performance as win-loss record, fighting single-elimination in one night for a $20,000 prize and a traveling trophy passed from champion to champion, deliberately built so no one fighter was expected to hold it twice running.

No further document survives. Whether Black & Blue or the tournament ever ran isn't in the surviving record.
