Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Downtown Championship Wrestling introduced itself as “a completely scripted E-Fed based in Queens, New York” in a Rules and Regulations document posted 30 October 1999, favoring short match write-ups built around angles over long blow-by-blow description, with results decided by submitted roleplay rather than match strategy.

Four titles ran its card: the DCW Heavyweight and Tag Team Titles, each defended twice a month; the Television Title, defended on every card; and the Brass Knux Title, contested only in gimmick matches. One weekly show, Retribution, ran ten to twelve matches each Sunday from Lost Battalion Hall in Queens, alongside a monthly pay-per-view.

Its 24 October 1999 Retribution opened a first-round Brass Knux Title tournament: Steve Shaw, having bounced through four other, since-closed promotions, submitted Ice with his finisher after a rival trio jumped him three-on-one; a brawl between Johnny Dangerously and “Psycho Freak” Jonathan Durst was ruled a no-contest when Brian Payne interfered with a lead pipe, advancing Shaw by default; and Harry Shilton, “the Hooligan,” beat Eric Draven with help from ringside allies, drawing a post-match beatdown from a still-feuding Beavis Jones. Payne himself paid for the interference in the tournament's main event, sent into a handicap match against the promotion's full eight-member wrestling school; its own president led the group to the ring, had them jump Payne before the bell, and watched one of them, Loco Crazy, pin him in a rout.

The card also introduced Tim Russo, “The Millennium Man,” who told the crowd the president had personally begged him to “save the DCW,” and reshuffled its Television Title picture when challenger Seminole walked away from his own title shot to align with a debuting ally, leaving Shawn Dyer as the champion Enigma's sole contender.

No further document survives; the card itself breaks off promising a sequel that was never collected.
