Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

This circuit staged a real-roster simulation of the World Wrestling Federation, its one surviving broadcast a nine-match Raw is War card for 18 September 2000, hosted by Jim Ross and Jerry Lawler. Its own writer describes having run the circuit since that May, offering to send a fuller history to anyone who asked.

The card opened on the McMahon-Helmsley Regime's collapsing grip on the promotion: Vince McMahon blamed Chris Jericho for costing the Regime a clean sweep of every title the night before at Clash of the Champions, Chris Benoit called out Triple H for the Intercontinental Championship, and Cactus Jack proposed his and Benoit's own tag titles go on the line against the whole Regime at once — a fight that grew, through Shane Douglas's own demand for a shot at Triple H, into a four-way main event.

The undercard turned over two vacated titles: Lance Storm beat Dean Malenko to open the Cruiserweight Championship tournament Chris Benoit had vacated, and Super Crazy upset Essa Rios in the same bracket. Elsewhere, the Great Muta and Shane McMahon beat Raven with an assist from three of the Four Horsemen, and Kurt Angle beat Chris Jericho for the second title change of the night after Muta and McMahon interfered again at ringside.

The main event pitted the reigning tag champions, Benoit and Cactus Jack, against the Regime's Triple H and Rob Van Dam, a rival tandem of McMahon's bodyguards, and a Triple Threat pairing of Shane Douglas and Perry Saturn, all four corners in at once. Ric Flair and the Four Horsemen ran in to take Cactus Jack out of the match, and Triple H and Van Dam won the WWF World Tag Team Championship as the show closed on the Horsemen beating Jack down in the ring.

No document earlier or later than this one card survives. Whether the circuit's writer kept posting cards beyond it, and what became of any of the night's outcomes afterward, are not known.
