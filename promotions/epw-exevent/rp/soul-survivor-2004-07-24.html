Soul Survivor

24 July 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
(The opens once again, this time with KJ arriving back home. cay sees him pull into the driveway and comes out to meet him. Still sitting in his truck, cay moves to his truck window to talk with him.) Cay: Do you feel better baby? KJ: Yeah, I really do. He told me some things I really needed to hear. One of these days I’m going to bring you to meet him. He really is a great man. Cay: I would love to. You hungry? KJ: Actually I’m starving. Cay: Well good, because I made some spaghetti that I know you will love. Why don’t you come in the house and eat and let’s just relax tonight. You can get ready for your match this sunday. (KJ smiles at cay and gets out of his truck. They embrace one another and share a moment or two holding each other. Cay takes him by the hand and leads him into the house. The camera fades out.) [“The Plot Thickens”] “So let me get this straight buddy,” Dex said for possibly the millionth time as he paced his tiny living room. “You hop a plane halfway across the country, kick some people’s ass on national TV, and come back to give me five grand?” My head, which felt as heavy as a bowling ball, fell down and back up again in a vain attempt at a nodding motion. He got it though. “And since you’ve got about the strength of a cockroach,” He continued furrowing his brow. “You take some magic drug that you haven’t tested and is made out of the shit that you stole from the lab which got you FIRED in the first place?” “That about covers it my friend,” I said with a weak smile as my aching head rested on the back of his comfy recliner. “Nobody knows I’m KJ, and nobody knows that KJ is me. I had the mask on the entire time I was in Washington...” Dex stopped pacing for a second and turned to me. “And how you get five G’s for wrestlin’ twice?” I smirked. “Apparently I impressed the talent scout,” I said still smiling. “And when I asked them for an advance on my salary, they were more than happy to oblige. I have no clue what I did after I took that serum last week, but I’m guessing I laid waste to those guys because ... well, like I said, they were impressed enough to give me a twenty-five hundred dollar cash advance.” Dex looked at the money-filled envelope on the coffee table blankly. “Which is enough to keep the building out of the hands of Uncle Sam,” I said smiling more broadly than ever even though it made my head hurt even more. “And plus I got my weekly check too. One thousand and five-hundred dollars a week is a pretty sweet deal for someone who despises professional wrestling but hey Dex hated wrestling i was just goin along with this prick.” Dex laughed. “You is such a whore, yo,” He said softly. “But you said you keep blackin’ out from this shit, and now you look like you got the does hangover eva’ ... you sure you should be messin’ with this Prometheus shit? It sounds like the kind of shit that can fuck a dude up, yo.” My smile flickered. “I can handle it Dex,” I said closing my eyes. “Plus, I don’t even know if this is from the serum. Maybe I just got tossed around more than I thought during my match last week. I really need to look at that tape...” “Heh,” was all he could say. “I guess the plot is startin’ to thicken young buck.” For a second neither of us spoke. “Dex...” I said weakly. “What?” “You can’t tell anyone about this man,” I said with a sigh. “If any of this got out, I’d be in some serious shit. Maxwell Labs would have proof that I stole the Prometheite, every pharmacy in the country would be trying to steal the formula from me, and if EPW finds out that I’m using this stuff to compete, they’d fire my ass in a second.” “Dude, it’s professional wrestling.” “Yeah but... don’t they do steroid and drug tests like the other sports? It’s just like baseball right?” Dex sighed. “Dude, it’s professional wrestling.” We laughed for a second. It made my head hurt a little bit more, but after what I’d been through in the past week, from loosing my job to getting paid a thousand dollars a week for doing something that I hated, to almost killing myself with the Prometheus Serum ... it was all just so overwhelming. I knew I had to get up and get on a plane in a few hours to get to the next EPW taping, but for the moment I was home, and I was safe. For the moment, the Prometheus Serum didn’t exist to me. I could still feel that Dex was worried about me though. “I just need to save up a few grand to pay some bills,” I sighed. “And I need to get a new job at a new pharmacy or something. Then I’ll stop. I swear.” Dex stopped laughing so suddenly that it scared me, and his gaze was drawn to the window. Into the perfectly blue sky. “Before I left Washington,” I sighed. “ I took off my mask, changed my clothes, and got an interview with some people who know some people, and got a job as an interviewer for EPW. That way, I’ll be able to get into the arena without having to take the serum while I’m on the street, and nobody’ll get suspicious about me being in every city of every Agression taping...” The radiation of Dex’s worry became more eminent. “Don’t do this for me Dex,” i sighed. “Because if I find out you’re fucking up your body to pay my bills, I’m going to have to fuck you up.” He looked over at me. “I done spent a lifetime of loosing people,” he continued. “My wife, my kids, my father, my mother, ... all to dumb shit. Drug wars, drive-bys, suicide, and shit. You the only friend I really got left man, and if I loose you... if I loose you...” I wanted to say something to comfort him. I wanted to tell him that it was going to be okay, and that maybe I’d be able to quit my wrestling job and just become a full-time interviewer at EPW so that I’d never have to touch the blue-stuff again... I wanted to tell my friend so much... But my brain was exhausted even more than my body. The weight of the world was just too much for my shoulders. And so I drifted off into a dream... It was still raining. I was cursing to myself as I dragged my suitcase down the uneven sidewalks of the street “Okay,” I muttered. “After I give him this money, the next thing I’m doing is buying an umbrella. This is ridiculous.” It had literally taken me a week to get back home after my match with EPW, and for the first time since I’d been living in the city, I was glad to hit my feet on the uneven concrete as I dragged not only my luggage but my body through the darkened streets. “And I’m going to get a car too,” I sighed. “A nice big red convertible with vanity plates and everything...” I’d felt unusually groggy and sick ever since my two time with EPW, but I suppose a lifetime of physical inactivity can do that to you when you’re mixing it up with two professional wrestlers and a referee. My white sneakers hit the sidewalk one after another in a pattern so disturbingly rhythmic that my sleepy mind even danced around the idea of simply snatching off the shoes and chucking them into the empty street. Before I could accurately decide if that’s what I wanted to do or not, however, I was there. One-Five-Eight Mifflin Street. “Home,” was the word that snuck up into my vocal cords and pushed itself out of my pressed lips. “I’m home...” I realized exactly how absurd the time was when a peak of sunlight hit me in the face seemingly out of utter darkness. “There’s probably some stupid metaphor in this” I muttered. “But I’m far too tired to think of it...” Up the stairs I went with my annoyingly on-beat sneakers followed by the loud banging on my dangling suitcase hitting the railing. My body was giving out. I never heard the front door open. Didn’t remember ever knocking on the door or pressing the doorbell. Then came a voice in front of me, but I was already falling forward with my eyes being pulled into the back of my dead – plunged straight into darkness. Maybe he caught me, or maybe I hit the ground. I’m not really quite sure. I don’t really remember hitting the ground though, so I guess it’s the former. When my eyes righted themselves and I blinked a few times I saw him standing there, just as sad and pitiful as he had looked when I left. “Yo you tipsy or sum’n?” is what I think I heard come out of his mouth, but I didn’t care. My hands fell into the pockets of my beat up raincoat and pulled out an envelope. A thick envelope filled with hundred dollar bills. Fifty of them to be exact. “Woah!” he exclaimed. “You rob a bank or sum’n?” “No,” I breathed groggily, and I knew that he was going to ask me questions, and I knew that I needed to give him answers. Someone had to know, and at the moment, he was the only friend I had. “Dex... when I come to... can I tell you a secret?” “Sure but what do you mean when you come-“ That time I definetely hit the ground. well you ever danced with the devil in the pail moonlight Dex's eyes lit up with fright as i pulled out a gun and blew his brains out all over the wall and floor, now i have no friends i thought to myself as i threw his money onto his limp carcass as blood soaked into the dollar bills, thinking to myself pay backs a bitch, i walked away hearing the odd scream of fright in the distance..... but i then woke up to reaslise all this shit was just a dream. I wiped the sweat from my head and sighed a sigh of relief as i headed for the shower thinkin to myself fuck that was some dream, hope it dont become a de ja vu in the future of my life.
