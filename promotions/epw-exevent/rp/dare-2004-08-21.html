Dare.

21 August 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Opportunity. The choice, destined instance in which one can seize or attain that which he most desires. To take advantage of it is the optimal decision, to take it for granted is least desirable, because opportunity only knocks but so long. Questions. What is Hudman's involvement with Chapel? What brought him back? How long will Khaos hold that title? Can he fend off two focused opponents at once, not having to even be pinned to lose his strap? Will The Hunter send Shawn over the edge? Who was that man at Aggression? Does Hudman have Shawn's number? Does Shawn even care? Who's going to leave Breaking Point with the Evolution of Professional Wrestling's Intercontinental Title? The man with the least to lose always wins. ***** [The screen is pitch black, and slowly fades to the "EPW" logo in the center. The logo disenegrates into obscurity, and the name "Hudman" appears in its place. A woman's voice, almost robotic, narrates.] "The returning legend," the voice dictates, the word "legend" echoing in her metalic-sounding resonance. [We fade from the "Hudman" namesake, cutting straight to a match from the July 30th, 2004 edition of Friday Night Aggression. The match is already underway. The words "The Re-Emergence" appear on the screen before the match continues. Chapel grabs the top turnbuckle, and begins to ascend to the top. Shawn Singleton attempts to pull him back down, only to receive a stiff back kick to the head, sending him to the mat. As Chapel sits atop the third turnbuckle, turning around, Shawn leaps from the mat, and runs towards the corner. Chapel quickly counters with a kick to the chest, wraps his head around Shawn's neck in a standing front facelock, and takes him to the mat sharply with a tornado ddt. As Chapel hooks the leg, laying across Shawn's abdomen for the pin, Hudman leaps the barricade on the near side of the ring, and, unbeknowst to the referee, holds Singleton's legs as Chapel secures the victory.] "Former Intercontinental Champion," the voice says, re-appearing. [We switch from this scene, now moving forward in time to the August 6th, 2004 edition of Aggression. The words "No Coincidence" descend from the top of the screen, stopping in the middle as the images from the match slowly form together into a fluid motion. Chapel and Shawn Singleton sit atop the cage, exchanging sloppy punches, teetering atop the massive structure. They simultaneously land stiff right hands to one another, knocking both men over the edge of the cage. Chapel instinctively reaches for the metal bars, reaching the top one, allowing himself to hang higher than Singleton, who is a few bars below him. He uses this advantage, kicked Shawn in the head while pulling himself back up. Suddenly, the camera catches a man leaping the ring barricade, quickly approaching the ring. The camera picks up that it is none other than Hudman, who reaches up with both hands, grabbing Singleton's left leg. At the same time, Chapel reaches down with a low swinging punch, hitting Singleton in the temple, causing him to lose his grip along with Hudman's tugs at his legs, sending him falling from the cage. The bell rings, and Chapel retains his title.] "Former EPW World Heavyweight Champion," the voice says, allowing "champion" to echo. [The screen goes black again, this time with the words "Title Defense" in the center. We are at the Friday the 13th edition of Aggression, the Double Jeopardy match. Both, Shawn Singleton and Ace, are down in the center of the ring. As Ace crawls towards Chapel to make the tag, placing Chapel in a position to lose his title, Hudman makes his way down to ringside. As Singleton gets to his feet, ready to go one-on-one with Chapel, Hudman yanks Chapel from the apron. Moments later, the two of them rush from the ringside area, disappearing into the back.] ***** Three times. Three title opportunities taken from Shawn Singleton by Hudman. Opportunity is knocking for the fourth time. Take it from him, now, Hudman. I dare you.
