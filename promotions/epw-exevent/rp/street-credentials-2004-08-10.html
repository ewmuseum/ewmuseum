Street Credentials

10 August 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
If that’s all his past has to offer then he is fucked. A fake drug dealer and a soft ass crack addict were the roughest things to ever come out of his life. To me, that is almost like you had everything I ever wished for. Seeing you made me remember the reasons why I hated to wake up the next morning, because there was always somebody else that had it better then me. But like you said, there’s no reason to talk about my past, I will show you first hand the way that I grew up. From there you can take your own outlook on the way that I was living. [The scene opens up on a dimly lit road considering that it is midday, it’s very dirty with trash everywhere. Almost all of the houses on the street have either boarded up or broken windows. There is a playground with a bunch of little kids playing on what is left of the usable equipment. Then further up ahead there is a big circle of kids dressed in black. They jeering and heckling can be heard even at the very end of the street. This is where Kyle Pearl and Molly make their entrance onto the road. They are on foot, and they way that they are dressed make them look very out of place. Kyle has on a pair of jeans that are designed to look like they have holes in them, with a white shirt, and a backwards Yankees hat on. Molly has on a pair of low cut jeans with a white tank top on, and a very disgruntled look on her face. The two continue to walk further down the street, and Kyle continues to look around like he is taking a tour of some foreign country. Molly on the other hand keeps shooting looks of fear at Kyle, and continues to turn around every five seconds. Becoming more worried and worried, as the two show no sign of turning back Molly breaks the silence.] Molly: I think you have proved your point. Why are you doing this? There is nothing here for me, and there is definitely nothing here for you except for a past that you swore you would never go back too. I don’t know why you must insist on always being the tough guy. And it’s funny how you are never thinking of anyone, but yourself. Maybe, you aren’t the right one for me after all. Kyle: Everyone needed to see this. You needed to see it, Maize needs to see it, and the rest of EPW needed to see it. This is how I grew up everyday of my life. Do you understand how hard that must be for a kid in his teens to grow up and seeing shit like this everyday? Apparently not, because if you did then you wouldn’t be bitching right now. The truly funny thing about it is that you haven’t even seen half of it. You are just being introduced. Molly: Kyle, I don’t want to be here anymore. Please, let’s leave we have to start heading over to the airport. You have to be in Iowa City in a few days, and I know how you like to examine the training facilities before you have to get into the ring. Please Kyle, I’m begging you let’s just leave. There is only trouble up there. Kyle: We can leave as soon as we get to the car. It’s just a little further ahead, right past that big group of kids up there. [The thought of walking past those kids enters Molly’s head, and she shoots Kyle another look of uncertainty. Kyle catches this one, and replies to it.] Kyle: Molly, don’t worry about it, I promise that we will both be fine. Nothing will happen to either of us. [The two continue on towards the kids. As they begin to get really close Molly’s steps seem to get shorter, and shorter, and pretty soon she stops dead in her tracks. Kyle pretends not to notice and Molly quickly rushes forward to keep up in fear of being alone on these streets. When the two get within 20 feet of the kids there focus is no longer on what it was on before. Their focus turns over to Molly. Kyle Pearl, and Molly walk past the kids as if the aren’t even there. The kids take offense to this and start taunting Molly.] Thug #1: Yo fellas, check out this piece right here. She think she can walk by us without payin’ us no mind. We might have to teach her a lesson if ya know what I mean. [The groups of kids begin to follow very closely behind Molly. Molly is now becoming more and more worried, and begins to start digging her nails deeper and deeper into Kyle’s arms. Kyle is still taking no notice to the kids at all.] Thug #2: Aw look, she gots her self a big bad man too. We may have to do a little something about that won’t we fellas? [The groups of kids continue to jeer at Kyle, and Molly before Kyle turns around and delivers a right haymaker to one of the kids. All of the other boys lurch forward as if they were going to do something to Kyle, but then they all get a good look at the mans face.] Thug #2: Oh shit, my apologies Mr. Pearl. We ain’t mean anything by it sir. We was just leaving you guys though weren’t we fellas? Have a good day Mr. Pearl. [All of the kids take a couple of quick steps back, and then break out into a sprint in all-different directions. Molly looks at Kyle surprised, and Kyle just breaks out into a little smirk. They continue to walk around the corner, and sure enough the little red Civic is sitting right where Kyle said it would be. They get in and start driving before Molly could bring herself to talk.] Molly: How come you didn’t tell me that these kids knew who you were? Not only that how come they seemed like they were scared of you? I all of a sudden feel like there is a side of you that I don’t even know. I don’t like that feeling at all Kyle. Kyle: Those kids did know me Molly. They knew not to fuck with me. Growing up around here I ran things by the time I was fourteen. Every kid for three blocks was scared of me. But like you said before, that was the life that I left. A life that I was promising never to look back on. Oh, and sometimes there are certain things about a person that nobody should know. [45 Minutes later on the plane ride to Iowa City] Molly: So is that why you have been acting so strangely lately? Because your match coming up is a streetfight. So what happens if you lose this? Are you going to go into seclusion and never show your face on the wrestling scene for the rest of your life? Kyle: There is no what if I lose. I’m not going to lose. In my eyes Maize is just another kid that wishes he grew up on the streets. This match is so important for me, because it gives me the chance to show you, and the rest of the World that I mean business. When I first thought about coming back to the wrestling world it was to see if I still had what it takes to compete. Now I know I can compete with anybody, so now it’s all about winning [After saying these words Kyle relaxes back into his seat, and watches as the clouds start to get smaller and smaller.] I hope you are ready Maize
