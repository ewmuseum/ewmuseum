Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

EPW — Evolution Pro Wrestling — was a website federation on the exevent.net host, and its own account of itself is the account of a name that kept changing under the same enterprise. It opened in May 2001 as Xtreme E-Wrestling, a real-wrestler federation; closed that October; came back in July 2002 open to both created and real wrestlers, with Coleman Dowell, Johnny Newmonic, Hudman, Chapel and others rising during that run; and closed again that October. An August 2003 return, created wrestlers only, brought up The Destroyer, John Adams and Dio before a deal to buy the Xtreme E-Wrestling name outright fell through and forced a rename.

The company returned that December under its present name, Evolution Pro Wrestling, and its own Last Resort pay-per-view billed itself as the first show under the new name. That run folded in March 2004; a further run from June to late August 2004, which the fed's own history calls its biggest yet, staged EPW High Stakes from the MGM Grand Garden Arena in Las Vegas on 20 June 2004 before closing again for financial reasons. Its Aggression, Riot and Warfare television shows ran to weekly roleplay deadlines, and its own Rules page reserved one championship, the EPW World Title, for the roster's top hand.

In April 2005 a company called Exevent bought full ownership and announced a reopening for that June; how the story ended isn’t settled in the surviving record. The fed’s own account says Exevent went on to sell Evolution Pro Wrestling’s rights, roster and championships to a company called Core Wrestling, which is reported, in the same breath, to have folded on 16 March 2006.

Six named pay-per-views survive as a slate — Anarchy, High Stakes, Last Resort, Overthrone, Reforge and Total Chaos — but only two carry any history of their own on the fed's own site, and both are a sentence long. Its roster, its website staff and its match results all lived in the site's database, and none of its records survived: no card, no title reign and no wrestler's own record.
