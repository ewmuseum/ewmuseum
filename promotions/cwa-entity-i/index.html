The CWA, founded 4 March 1997 with three weekly cards, two interview shows and a no-real-wrestlers policy, attested only by its own founding post and one respondent's original-character application. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

This CWA announced itself on 4 March 1997 with a full weekly schedule already laid out: three commentated cards — Monday Night Mayhem, Thursday Night Fights, and the Saturday Night at Midnight Fights — plus two interview programmes, Wednesday Interview Wars and The Sunday Show, where accepted roleplays would run. Its own rules capped a roster submission at three singles wrestlers and one tag team, barred “real wrestlers or real stables” outright, and tied a wrestler's winnings, matches, and title shots directly to how good and how long the roleplays sent in for them were.

Its only surviving response is one respondent's own application, five days later, for an original character called Owen Masters: a 235-pound technical heel out of Hartford, Connecticut, billed with a Piledriver, Side Suplex, Superplex, Stun-Gun, and Neckbreaker among his five main moves and a Figure Four Master-Lock as his finisher, described in the application itself as “a younger and tougher version of the great Ric Flair.”

No card, roster, or result document survives, and nothing later than 9 March 1997 does either — whether Owen Masters or anyone else ever wrestled for it isn't in the surviving record.
