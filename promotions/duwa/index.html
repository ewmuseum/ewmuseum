Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Down Under Wrestling Association circulated its first rulebook on 22 June 1997, run out of Adelaide, Australia by a president who introduced himself with prior credits in “the now defunct UWWA” and “the ever popular AEWF.” He said he had owned a league before, the WRL, that fell apart when his co-owner “quit on me, without notice,” taking every wrestler's name and the whole roster with him — the reason he gave for spacing this promotion's own schedule out to one weekly card and two flash updates rather than the three shows a week he had run before.

It was booked rather than purely simmed, match results decided by a wrestling council weighing storyline and how active a member had been, with a simulator brought in only to settle an otherwise-even, poorly suited pairing. Membership was capped at 25 singles wrestlers and 8 tag teams, one entry per handler, building toward three titles — Heavyweight, a 210-pound Lightweight limit, and Tag Team — each contender decided by a running win-point ranking, under house rules that limited profanity to the word “ass,” twice per interview, per member.

A revised rulebook a week later, 29 June, filled in the calendar the first had left open: six named bi-monthly pay-per-views running February through December, built toward an outdoor October supercard, “DUWA Starslam,” projected at over 12,000 in attendance, plus two further weekly flash shows alongside the original Tuesday broadcast. The same revision pictured all three championship belts as finished designs — each one stamped, in the same breath, “CURRENTLY VACANT.”

No document later than that 29 June revision survives. The promotion's own tentative start date, then set for late August with a chance of slipping to October, is the last thing known about it — whether it ever crowned a champion of its own isn't in the surviving record.
