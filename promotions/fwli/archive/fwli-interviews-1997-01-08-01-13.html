Taped Thursday 01/09/97: [The scene opens in the study of Lord Byron's Louisiana mansion. Byron is sat behind the desk, watching footage of Hardcore '97 on a small television screen.] Byron: If there's one thing I hate, in the ring, or out of it, it's unprofessionalism. [Byron picks up a remote control, and presses fast forward.] Byron: I have been asked several times since Hardcore '97, 'What happened between you and Jean-Pierre Vachon? What was said?' [Byron sneers] The truth be known, I was simply offering the new 'Living Legend' some practical wrestling advice. Take a look at what I mean. Heart: [on tape] Nancett gets to his feet, and Vachon locks on a headlock! Smart move. [Byron smirks] Slow the match down, as much as possible! Bowie: [on tape] But Nancett was ready, and sends Vachon to the mat with a back suplex! Nancett leaps up in the air, and delivers a snap legdrop! What a move by "Gambit"! Byron: No, what a mistake by Vachon. All he had to do was shift his weight, and he would never been in such a position. There's inexperience for you. Watch this next one. Bowie: [on tape] Nancett grabs Vachon by the hair, remember it's no disqualification, and begins slamming his head into the turnbuckle! 1....2....3. [Byron yawns and presses fast forward]...29....30! Nancett slammed Vachon's head into the turnbuckle 30 times, and suddenly Vachon's face is covered by a crimson mask! Byron: Countless opportunities for the block, the reversal, the counter. Can you really believe that this man is an FWLI champion? This slow, unprepared, little-boy-lost? What sort of a way to defend your title is this? What sort of way to defend your CAREER is this? [Byron sneers] And yet when I offer some simple advice, point out some dangerous mistakes, what happens? He gets touchy. He doesn't want to know. Bowie: [on tape] Oh, this is wrong! This is completely unfair! The referee has no choice in the matter! One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten!!!!!! Ring the bell, this match is over, and so is the career of Jean-Luc Nancett! [Byron stands up and walks across to the window.] Byron: And one again, Vachon is victorious, if not by conventional means. How long is it since you last wrestled, Vachon? No, I don't mean defended your title, since you seem to prefer to only fight opponents once they have one arm tied behind behind their back, I mean really wrestled? Without stipulations protecting your worthless hide? Without having someone finish your opponent off for you? [Byron smirks, and mocks] And you still have your dignity! And you still have your pride!' [Byron sneers] The Dirt Dog's standards were higher than yours. At least he had the guts to face an opponent on an equal basis. If not the ability to match, of course. Bowie: [on tape] Vachon turns away, and goes to get his title, but Byron grabs him, and spins him around... Vachon nails him with the belt! Heart: [on tape] What the hell is Vachon's problem? [Byron snarls, and turns the TV off.] Byron: [ripping off his gloves] Vachon's PROBLEM is that he has forgotten how to wrestle. You would think that advice from the world's greatest ring technician would be warmly welcomed, especially after that pathetically poor showing. But no. Intead, Vachon lashes out in anger and frustration, and insults a man who was only trying to help. [Byron hurls his gloves to the floor.] Byron: I DEMAND satisfaction! Let us see, Vachon, just how much real courage you have left in your useless carcass. Either face me, one on one, and prove that there's more to your title reign than cheap shots and stipulations, or hide behind the petticoats of your pretty wife once again, and let the whole of the FWLI know just how much of a coward you really are. [Byron sneers, and bows] I'll be looking forward to your response. Ciao. [Byron turns towards the window, and away from the camera. Taped Friday 01/10/97: [FADE IN to a hotel room table. VACHON, wearing a closed bathrobe, is seated with Daniel WEST. An antiseptic bandage is taped to his head.] WEST: Ladies and gentlemen, welcome. It's a few hours after HARDCORE, and I'm here now with Jean-Pierre Vachon, who successfully defended his title against Jean-Luc Nancett of the Player's Club in a grueling battle that cost Gambit his FWLI career. Um, Jean-Pierre, even though you won, you don't look very happy this evening. VACHON: You know what, Daniel? I'm not. It's not because of this [indicates bandage] or because I'm feeling remorse over Nancett, it's because... well, because of the Iceman. You see, feuding with the Player's Club is one thing. Carrying on the feud into another man's fight, especially when the other man is ME, that's a whole 'nother picture, brother. I never asked the Iceman to stick his nose in my business... hell, I didn't even know that he and Speece were in the crowd. And now, when I go to sign autographs, I gotta hear the fans tell me that my latest victory was a tainted one - tainted because of Brian Knight and his Alliance. And that's not my style. Knight... I don't know if you or any of your Alliance ever thought much about the consequences of Hardcore, but you all just got put on the "Living Legend's" hit list. And if Danny Dynamite and the Player's Club don't wipe you out of FWLI... rest assured, the "Legend" will. WEST: Speaking of the Player's Club, the way Gambit lost the match... some of the fans are saying Dynamite and Kaufman may be after you for revenge. VACHON: Maybe, but I doubt it. I doubt it very much. Remember - it was Nancett who wanted the match to be Crossroads rules, and it was Nancett who upped the ante so that it'd be instant retirement for the loser. Hearing him talk about how he'd stacked the deck against me, how he'd restore honor to the belt when he was champion... I'd say he had the worst case of chicken-counting in FWLI during the summer of '96. And you know what the thing is about "crossroads" rules? Winning doesn't depend on the punishment you inflict... it depends on the punishment you absorb. When I picked up Nancett and carried him into the ring, I was thinking how easily that could've been me taking the shots that he took. And when I put him in the middle of the ring, I was wondering: can he get up? WILL he get up? Because I didn't want the match to end that way either. But Nancett took too much. He overestimated himself, the amount he could take. I didn't win that match, Nancett lost it. But don't shed any tears for him either. He went in with both eyes open. He knew the consequences if he lost, and he still insisted he could win. If people want to remember Jean-Luc Nancett, let them remember him as a good wrestler who took a gamble, the biggest gamble of his career, and Luck handed him a busted flush. WEST: Stepping aside from the Player's Club, I'd like to talk to you about Lord Byron. He went into the ring after your match, obviously insistent on something- VACHON: Oh, I know what he was after. He knows he's the number 5 contender for the TV belt. You know what he said in the ring? He said because I won in such a disgraceful way, I should just hand the championship over to him. Naturally I ignored it, but when he started getting loud about it... You know, Daniel, I think there's a switch inside me that goes off whenever someone starts shooting off about their superiority. Lord Byron's a lot more articulate than Haron Atkinson, but a blowhard's still a blowhard. Lord Byron... you want this belt, you're going to have to get it the old fashioned way, in the squared circle. You're gonna have to get your hands dirty, you're gonna have to sweat, you're gonna have to go all out. Because I assure you, I will not go gently into that good night... I will defend this belt as I've always defended it, with all the skill and power in my possession, and perhaps even a bit more. Step into the ring with me, Byron... you'll learn why my matches are always the stuff... of legends! [Camera fades.] --------------------------------------------------------------------------- Interview with Players Club member Dan "Flash" Kauffman. Taped Saturday 01/11/97: [A press conference has been called at FWLI headquarters, and media personnel, several wrestlers, and fans of all ages pack the press room. A small podium has been erected, the FWLI logo on the front. From the doble doors behind the podium emerges Dan Kauffman, a lack of color in his face. He wears black and gold colors, and is clearly in a somber mood.] KAUFFMAN: Before I begin, let me demand that there be no questions or interuptions as I speak... I may or may not answer questions at the end... and if I've thought about what I'm about to say correctly, there will be no need for questions anyway. In the eighteen months I have existed in the world of wrestling, I have grown from a smallish rookie with some technical skill to build on to a respected and rewarded athlete who in many ways has reached the pinnacle of the sport itself. I have been awarded several paper awards, including the best "Face" of the year. I have won... though now I will admit, from questionable means... the IIWF World Heavyweight Championship, a prize that means a lot to me. Most of all, I have won the respect and admiration of most of my fans and fellow wrestlers. The respect is what I started wrestling for. I wanted to be looked up to, to be considered a legend and a hero. Those were my dreams. I have accomplished so much... But for all the accomplishments and awards I have been given or have won, for all the respect and praise that has been heaped upon my shoulders elsewhere, I still have yet to get off the ground in the federation of which I have been involved with the longest... the FWLI. I have often called the IIWF my home, but the truth is, the FWLI came first. And I feel that, while in the FWLI, I have dissapointed my fans, my fellow wrestlers... and most importantly, myself... with the losses and constant excuses that I have made. If my career were to end now... and I considered ending my career very strongly... I would consider my career a failure. Such is the esteem I hold for the FWLI. I have taken for granted many facts about the FWLI... the fact that it is a tough, established league which no wrestler can win over with other accomplishments. The fact that it takes more than a running mouth and a way with logic to succeed. To succeed in the FWLI, you must do your best work inside of the ring. And as the recent house shows indicate, and as my performance at Hardcore proves, I have not done my best work inside the ring. I find it hard... almost suicidal... to admit that I submitted to another wrestler. I couldn't find an excape. And that one fact nearly drove me out of the sport I love. No, the FWLI is not a place to show off other champinships. It is not a place to claim you are the best and hope someone accepts that statement. The FWLI was created to seperate the good from the great. I have found out that I can only be discribed as good. For myself, that isn't good enough... but I've been saying that for six months, and I'm still only good. I have decided to stay around the FWLI for the time being, and I have wiped my slate clean. I have no plans for battle, no more goals for success in the FWLI. I will do exactly what I did when I first wrestled in August 1995... I will step into the ring, I will give my all, and I'll move on. If I am still not successful, so what? I gave my all. But I need to start from square one again... I must reprove my skill and talent to the entire world. One more time. This will be the last you all hear from me until after my next match, whoever it may be against. I know I have contracts to fulfill, and I will do just that. But it's time to start over. No Verhoeven on the slate, no Knight on the slate, no Vachon on the slate. Besides my alliegance to the PC, I am restarting the engines. Let's see how well things go this time...
