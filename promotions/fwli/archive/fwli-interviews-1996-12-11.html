("Mad Dog" Eddy Ramos & "Ace" Alex Porteaux), along with manager Jeandra. Taped Wednesday 12/04/96: [Scene is in the locker room during an FWLI house show. Damage Inc have just returned and are sweaty and are seated in front of a monitor. Jeandra stands in front of the camera posturing and primping in her brand new Utah Jazz jersey, personalized (Jeandra "69"). Suddenly, both Ramos and Porteaux rise from their bench and head towards the camera. Porteaux has his head lowered.] PORTEAUX: [pops head up comically] OH? *NOW* ya respect us?? Ain't that some stuff? Ya know, I've been hearing this for a while now. Player's Club... all summer long it was, "We gonna whup your a*s... we gonna beat your a*s... we gonna do somethin to our ass" and then on national radio, I get this whole drivel about how y'all RESPECT us! JEANDRA: And now... PORTEAUX: The Rages talk about how they were gonna run over... how they were gonna thrash us... RAMOS: Naw, Ace... they said they were gonna KILL us... PORTEAUX: [looks at Ramos and smile] ... oh yeah... they DID say that, didn't they? They got up and said they were gonna KILL OUR A*SES! And *NOW* we get this whole respect speech again! Let me tell y'all somethin. STICK TO YA GAME! If ya start out threatenin' then that's how you better end it! If ya start out fierce you better END UP fierce, because that's how *WE* do things 'round here! The both of you had some strong words at first, but when pushing is FINALLY coming to shove, the words just got SO much nicer, eh? We ain't changing a DAMN thing! Come hard, come fierce, come like bad a*ses... we don't care HOW ya come... just come correct!! Now I got more motivation because it's stuff like THIS that rubs da Ace the wrong way!! JEANDRA: Hold true to your words, gentlemen... never forget it... ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- Interview with The Dangerous Alliance (The Genius & Mr. Arrogant & The Perfect One), along with leader Mr. Destruction. Taped Friday 12/06/96: [Scene opens with The Outlaw at a podium sometime after FWLI BLITZ.] Reporter: Outlaw, any comments on your Cage Match with Winston Major? Outlaw: Yes, I'd just like to say it was a pleasure kicking his sorry @ss all over that cage. Reporter: Are you worried about a fine or suspension of some sort? Outlaw: No, they should suspend me... I'm a dangerous man. Don't you get it? I cracked Duke losers head open in the center of the ring with that superplex from the top of the cage... then I bashed his carcass in with multiple hits from a chair. Duke "I suck" Major is finished... he will never wrestle the same again... his career is over. When anybody gets in the ring with me... it's a crossroads match... nobody I fight is ever the same when the fight is over. I'm the reason half the wrestlers go bald and I'm the reason wrestlers retire early. Reporter: Outlaw looking at your record I can't help but notice that you are UNDEFEATED?? Surely this is means for a higher ranking than 35? Outlaw: You're damn right... nobody can put my shoulders to the mat for the 3 count and our faithful president knows it. I'm being shoved with the crap wrestlers for fear I'll cause upsets in the FWLI, and believe me... given the chance... I'll beat anybody they stick me in the ring with. Reporter: Do you have your sights set on anyone right now? Outlaw: Anybody the FWLI throws at me, it doesn't matter... I'll whip Engel's @ss, Quigley, Desirable, Hitman, you name it... I've got all their numbers. Reporter: What makes you so sure you can compete with the big guns of the FWLI? Outlaw: Because I've been raised to survive... all my life I've been fighting and fighting... knowing that the next fight could be my last but always coming out on top... and why you ask?? Because i'm not predictable... I never wrestle the same match twice. Every strategy every gameplan is different... I've got so many different ways to beat a man it's insane. Reporter: What are your thoughts on the Champion right now - Todd Newkirk? Outlaw: Engel's got him... Newkirk can't beat Engel fair and square and he never will. I'd expect Engel to be champion in the near future... and then maybe if he's man enough... he'll give me a shot at his sorry @ss. Engel is much more of a challenge than Newkirk would be... fighting Newkirk would more than likely to turn into a gang fight... and that's my specialty... that's all I ever did back home in that bar. 3 on 1's, 5 on 1's... I'll bounce them all out of the FWLI on stretchers. Reporter: You sound primed and ready for anybody that comes your way. Outlaw: I'm unstoppable... [pulls out an open contract] ... I dare ANYBODY to prove me wrong ... [slams contract down on podium stand] ... you know where to find me. Taped Saturday 12/07/96: [The setting is a boardroom, with a large mahogany table in the centre, and a video screen on the wall at the head of the table. The members of the Devious Dangers (Greg "The Barber" Roberts, Storm, "Modern Day Romeo" Ryan Rankin, and "Fabulous" Freddy Blair) are sitting around the table, chatting and drinking coffee. The are all casually but expensively dressed. "Devious" Dan Davis enters the room, wearing a Versace suit. He places a briefcase on the table, opens it, and pulls out a laptop computer, a videotape, and a cellular phone. He inserts the tape into a wall-mounted VCR, and pulls out a laser pointer.] DEVIOUS DAN: Okay guys, cut the chatter [they turn to face the video screen]. The subject of this week's meeting is the five stages of terminal illness [he starts the tape, showing footage of the Bandwidth Mountie in action]. STORM: [shocked] You mean the Mountie's terminally ill? DEVIOUS DAN: No, he's in perfect health. It's his TV Title reign that is on its last legs. When someone realizes that they have a terminal illness, they go through five stages in the process of coping. The first stage is denial. The Mountie claims never to have lost to any of the Devious Dangers, but the facts suggest otherwise [rolls the tape, showing the end of the Blair/Mountie match from BattleCry, with the Mountie being counted out; cut to the referee raising Blair's hand in victory]. FAB FRED: Funny that the Mountie would forget this match; I remember it like it were only yesterday. RANKIN: Yeah, and I had a countout victory over the Mountie too, don't forget. BARBER: I had a victory over the Mountie a while back, but he doesn't seem to remember that one either. DEVIOUS DAN: That's right. Now, the Mountie would technically be correct if he said that no member of the Dangers has beaten him *for the title*, but that's not what he said. The truth of the matter is, we've beaten him on more than one occasion, and he knows it. Now, the second stage is anger [rolls tape, showing the Mountie superkicking a mannequin of Jean-Luc Nancett, shattering it; cut to the Mountie bursting a balloon with a picture of Brody Thunder taped to it]. Here we see the Mountie taking out his anger and frustration -- on a dummy! STORM: What's the deal with the balloon? BARBER: [in his best "Bruce Lee" voice] Balloons don't hit back. [laughter] DEVIOUS DAN: Yes, and mannequins don't beat you for the TV Title. So he takes out his anger on harmless inanimate objects. RANKIN: [sarcastically] Probably weren't any schoolchildren around to bully. DEVIOUS DAN: The third stage is fear [rolls tape of a Mountie interview, where the Mountie discusses how he's going to beat Jean-Luc Nancett, "Living Legend" Haron Atkinson, and Brody Thunder.] FAB FRED: Who was that? Jean-Luc Picard? [laughter] STORM: "Living Legend" Rowan Atkinson? [more laughter; Barber and MDR look at each other and smile] BARBER & RANKIN: [in sync] Blunder in Paradise!! [riotous laughter] DEVIOUS DAN: You see my point? He's afraid to put up the TV Title against a *top* contender, because he knows that he would lose! So he chooses to focus on these ham-n-eggers, rather than to accept Fab Fred's challenge of a tiebreaker match. STORM: I was wondering why all of a sudden the Mountie was getting all worked into a lather over... who were those guys again? RANKIN: It doesn't matter. So what next? DEVIOUS DAN: Well, there are two more stages the Mountie can expect to go through. The fourth is bargaining, so he'll probably start pleading with the FWLI bookers for a series of non-title matches, in order to buy himself a little more time as champion. BARBER: Whatever happened to *fighting* champions? [shakes his head] Oh well, it sounds like we won't have to worry about it for much longer. What's next? DEVIOUS DAN: The fifth and final stage is acceptance. At this point, the Mountie will finally realize that his days as TV champion are numbered, and he will sign for a title match against a top contender like Fab Fred here, and he will lose both the match *and* the title. FAB FRED: Yeah, that's be sweet. I don't know how he can say that he's the better wrestler, because we've each won one match. DEVIOUS DAN: But there's a lesson here, guys. Like him or not, you have to admit that anyone who can hold a title for as long as the Mountie has must be doing something right. But nothing lasts forever. All of you have won and lost titles, if not in the FWLI then eleswhere. It's all part of the game. And should anyone need any more evidence of the impermanent nature of our existence, look at this: [rolls tape of the Tyson/Holyfield fight, showing Holyfield knocking out Tyson; cut to the referee raising Holyfield's hand in victory]. NOBODY has dominated heavyweight boxing like Mike Tyson, but he's no longer the champion. Like a great wrestler once said, "being the man and staying the man are two different things". Any questions? STORM: Yeah, I've got a question. Who did you bet on in the Tyson fight? DEVIOUS DAN: Holyfield, of course. [Fade to black.] ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- Interview with Mark "The Executioner" Engel & "Maniac" Melanie Engel. Because anything less wouldn't be the FWLI! ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- Interview with FWLI Television Champion "Bandwidth Mountie" Jean-Pierre Vachon. Taped Monday 12/09/96: [FADE IN to the front of the pharmaceutical department of Eaton's, Rideau Centre, Ottawa, Canada. FWLI reporter DANIEL WEST is facing the camera.] WEST: Hello fans! Well, it's Christmas time again, and most of us are right now doing our gift shopping, and I'm about to join FWLI's Television Champion, Jean-Pierre Vachon the Bandwidth Mountie, as he goes to buy gifts for his fellow wrestlers in the hottest E-mail wrestling league. [PAN TO an aisle where BANDWIDTH MOUNTIE is in front of a laden shopping cart, examining two boxes. DW steps up to him.] WEST: Good to see you, Mountie. MOUNTIE: Always a pleasure, Dan. Unlike some people, I don't have to firebomb an office to get the right attention. Now then, right now I think I've got the perfect gift for the Barber, Greg Roberts of the Devious Dangers. They're both priced the same, so the question is which brand to use. What do you think? [BM shows both boxes to DW.] WEST: Um--Mountie, this is ear wax remover. MOUNTIE: That's right - because listening to him lately, I think maybe his ears are plugged up or something, because I don't believe he heard a word I said. [TO CAMERA] I never said I never lost to the Devious Dangers. What I DID say was I've BEATEN each of the Devious Dangers. It's there in black-and-white: the last time I faced Ryan Rankin, I beat him. The last time I faced Freddy Blair, I beat him. And the last time I faced Greg Roberts in the ring, I beat him too. WEST: Now that last one - that's kinda controversial, because apparently that match was never televised - and the shots were from the video room where FWLI is preparing its new video releases for the New Year- MOUNTIE: I know that, Dan - I've said before that the Barber would deny it, and true to form, he did. But the problem can be easily rectified. [PAN with BM and DW as they walk away from the pharmaceuticals towards the cashiers.] MOUNTIE: Roberts, it's obvious you're itching for a title match. I wasn't going to offer this, since I know you and your fellow Dangers to traffic are focused on the 6-man belts, but I'll have a contract ready - just for you. The Television title on the line. No DQ or countout. No one else in the ring or at ringside. And when the match is said and done - I WILL have beaten the three Devious Dangers. THAT'S the claim I'm making - NOT that I'm unbeatable, but that I've beaten the three of YOU.You know Dan, Roberts has said that I'm in a state of denial, but you've never heard any of the Dangers admit that I've beaten them, have you? WEST: I think Ryan Rankin came close- MOUNTIE: He's thinking of the time when he was still posing as Minstrel Boy Romeo, and when my wife showed him her TRUE feelings about him. He says he's new and improved since then? I've MET the new and improved Ryan Rankin in the ring - summer of 1996 - AND I've beaten him! It's right there in the House Report, you know? And he won't admit it! Now Freddy Blair - you saw his reaction after my last match with him, he simply could NOT bring himself to say, "I was beaten by the Bandwidth Mountie!" All he was saying was, "There's NO WAY he could have beaten me!" And all the while he was forgetting - he's had TWO chances for the TV title, and he blew it both times. The first time - I admit it, I was out cold, but he could have put me back into the ring and pinned me for the belt. He didn't, and it's history. And of course you all saw the second time. WEST: Yes, I remember. But you're challenging one of the best wrestlers in FWLI in Greg Roberts, and- MOUNTIE: That's EXACTLY the point I'm making, Dan. To be the best, you have to beat the best. You know, one of the problems with the FWLI rankings list is that current champions aren't ranked. By beating Greg Roberts, I'll prove I have a higher ranking than some of the best tag teams in FWLI. WEST: Well, um, leaving aside the Dangers for a moment, I see in your cart you've already gotten some stuff for the other wrestlers in FWLI- MOUNTIE: Sure, Dan. [pulls out an object from the cart] This one's gonna be a little tricky to wrap. It's for Haron Atkinson. WEST: A hand mirror? MOUNTIE: Yep. Atkinson looks into this, he'll see the ONLY PERSON in FWLI who thinks of him as a "living legend." WEST: Now that brings up a point. Haron Atkinson is a newcomer to FWLI, and you've agreed to offer him a title shot. Greg Roberts has already been critical- MOUNTIE: Not to worry Dan - this won't affect Greg's chances for the TV title. Remember, Atkinson said he could beat me in five minutes. With this match, that's EXACTLY how long he has. WEST: A five-minute match - that sounds like quite a handicap. MOUNTIE: Not at all. Remember, Atkinson himself made the claim. I'm merely holding him to it. Which brings up a point - you know, Atkinson, you're very lucky. I think only Kerry Von Erich got a title shot so quickly after entering a wrestling organization. I strongly suggest you prepare yourself, because pretty talk doesn't count in the ring. With that in mind though, I think it's time for you to put something on the line here. You claim to be a "living legend." You can't have a "legend" without the time to back it up. Here in FWLI, lots of people can claim to be "living legends" - Joe Melillo, the two Chrises, Thomas and Taylor, the Engels, Worthington's circus - and me. And when you lose this match, Atkinson, you'll surrender to ME the right to be known as a "living legend." Pure and simple. A match really isn't worth anything unless you're prepared to sacrifice something, and the "living legend" tag is probably the one thing about you that's worth anything. WEST: But back to my point, Mountie: Roberts is critical because Atkinson, along with Brody Thunder, doesn't have a track record- MOUNTIE: Just because someone's new doesn't mean you can be com- placent. New people can get lucky. I've had to relearn that lesson a dozen times, and I don't think Roberts OR his crew will ever learn it. I may not respect Atkinson - no, wait - I CERTAINLY don't respect Atkinson or Brody Thunder, but I won't underestimate them. WEST: I see. Well, um - what else do you have in here? MOUNTIE: Ah. Well, this one's for Jean-Luc Nancett- [BM holds up a small package.] WEST: What is it? MOUNTIE: A watch. The traditional retirement present. WEST: Implying that Gambit's going to have time on his hands, because he'll have to retire after his "crossroads" match with you. MOUNTIE: You know, I hadn't really thought of it that way, but now that you mention it - yeah, that's it exactly. [A female voice suddenly calls.] FEMALE VOICE: JEAN-PIERRE! JEAN-PIERRE! [JULIET VACHON, in civilian clothes, suddenly rushes up to BM and DW.] JULIET: I think I've found something to get a point across. [She holds up a small package to BM.] What do you think? MOUNTIE: Suspenders, Juliet? JULIET: Is't not the perfect gift for Dalbello Rage? MOUNTIE: [pause] Why... yes! Yes it is, indeed! Chuck it in, Juliet, we're almost done here! [JV tosses the suspenders into the cart, kisses BM on the cheek.] WEST: Um - I think I've missed something. Why should Dalbello Rage get suspenders for Christmas? JULIET: Because, milord West, you only need suspenders if you don't have a belt! [to camera] Dalbello Rage - be forewarned that marriage has not dulled my talent in the ring! If the Maniac takes not the FWLI Women's title from you - rest assured that I WILL! MOUNTIE: Would you excuse us, Dan? Christmas shopping takes a while. WEST: Sure, Mountie, but before you go - any last words on this holiday season? MOUNTIE: Just this. [to camera] Folks, this is the time of year when people start to talk of "peace on earth, good will to all men." Well, if they talk so much of it that you get sick of it [grins] tune in to FWLI! Trust me, "peace on earth and good will to all men" is the LAST thing on our minds! WEST: Thank you, "Bandwidth Mountie!" [to camera] We'll be right back! [Fade out.] ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
