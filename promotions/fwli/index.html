Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Fantasy Wrestling League International took its name from an existing federation that announced, on its own October 1995 broadcast, that it had “transformed itself” from RSPCW into the FWLI. The new name did not stay uncontested: in March 1996 its own commissioner issued a public statement denouncing an unrelated operator who had picked the abandoned old name back up to stage FWLI's own characters without the permission of the writers who created them, calling it “a feeble attempt” at revenge on two federations that had turned the man away.

Its January 1997 pay-per-view, Hardcore '97, put five championships on the line in one night — the World, Tag Team, European, North American and Television titles — and closed its card with a Crossroads match whose loser would retire from the promotion outright. The following month, its own editorial board refused to air a tag title match a writer had submitted with what the league judged an inappropriate finish, awarding the titles by decision instead; a member who leaked the unaired match's closing minutes anyway, risking expulsion to do it, described a run-in so chaotic it ended with a surprise appearance from a wrestler the promotion had not booked that night.

Not every applicant who wanted in got an answer. A rejected would-be member wrote publicly that October asking “Was FWLI Afraid?” after his and his friends' debut roleplays — including an elaborately narcissistic character billing himself the greatest wrestler alive — went unread and unanswered, even as newer names kept appearing on the roster page. No response from the league itself survives.

The FWLI renamed itself again in spring 1997, continuing under the new name into at least December of that year with a freshly created International Tag Team Championship changing hands under its own house rules.

No closure document of its own survives past that point; the promotion's own record simply stops.
