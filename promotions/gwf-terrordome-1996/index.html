Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

This promotion rose on 8 December 1996 directly out of a predecessor's collapse: “The GFWF has folded, as the guy who was running it has bailed. I am picking up for him and rechristening the group the GWF... just like the old GWF out of Dallas” — a homage by name only, run on an old pen-and-paper wrestling game until its two commissioners could find a simulator. Members were capped at two singles wrestlers and two tag teams each, fighting for a World Heavyweight, International Jr Heavyweight, World Tag Team, United States, United States Tag, and King of Pain title (defended only in Danger and Death matches), with a Women's title added on interest.

Its first card, “Welcome to the Terrordome” (12 December 1996), sold out its own arena and opened with the Women's Title tournament: Amber Jane, entering with manager John Schisz to a chorus of boos, lost to Persephone at 8:27 after a nearly flawless shooting star press went wrong; Laura Wineinger beat Amber's tag partner, Kathy Lynn, with the Chest Basher; Nadia Williams beat Helen with a guillotine legdrop; and Banshee beat Mary Williams with the Banshee Slap.

Its World Title tournament qualifiers ran alongside: the Holy Executioner beat Stormtrooper of Death with the Wrath of God Powerbomb, Iceman beat Nightmare with the Icefreez, Heavy Metal Joe chokeslammed the smaller El Jeffe in 3:24, and Entropy and Lunatic Louie — the latter aided repeatedly by his manager, Scarface — fought a 19-plus-minute battle the commissioner called good enough to send both men into the tournament regardless of the finish.

Its first King of Pain was crowned in a three-way dance that spilled outside the ring again and again — a chair, a purse, a sock of silver dollars, and two broken tables all saw use — before Graphic Violence piledrove an interloper, Submarine, through the wreckage of one for the pin at 17:23, after already powerbombing Cyborg through the other.

The next card was set to crown a first Women's Champion and carry the tag and World Title tournaments forward. No further document survives, and how either tournament finished, or how the promotion ended, is not in the surviving record.
