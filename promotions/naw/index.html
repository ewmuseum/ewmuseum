Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

New Age Wrestling opened in late March 2003 and picked the Cow Palace to do it in, because its own president came from San Francisco and said so into the microphone on the way to the ring. The calendar it published ran six weeks through the Joe Louis Arena, the American Airlines Arena, the Staples Center, the Pepsi Center and ARCO Arena, with a pay-per-view called Fury in the middle of it.

Two of those shows survive. The first is the whole of its opening night. The second, a week later, is the night it crowned champions: Mystic J took the Rising Star Championship from Weaponary, billed at seven feet ten and 410 pounds with a finisher called The 747, and gave a backstage interview about it with the belt over his shoulder; The Redeemer beat Xtra Ordinary for the Xtreme Championship by what the commentary called half an inch. The same script then opens a two-out-of-three-falls match for the Heavyweight Championship and stops partway through it, with a chair under one man’s neck. Five days later the fed’s own title histories page still read Vacant.

After that the record is cards with nothing behind them. Anarchy was pushed to a Saturday because two of the fed’s own writers had not sent their matches in, and the president wrote the rest of the show himself. The pay-per-view card went up with no matches announced at all. How it ended isn’t in the surviving record: the last thing the fed wrote was captured in January 2004, and by that May the address was serving a parking page.

Storylines

The arcs below are recovered from a full read of all thirteen held documents, including both surviving Thursday Night Anarchy scripts and the card posted for the Saturday after them.

The Violators and the vacant Heavyweight strap Written by AI, 2026

Cereal opens the fed's first show issuing a live open challenge from the ring, billing himself the next Heavyweight Champion. Renegade and Nightmare, the Hitmen, answer it for him instead: Darkside no-shows their own scheduled opponent, Cereal is awarded the win and jumped from behind by a debuting TraV, and the three reveal themselves on camera as a new trio calling itself the Violators. Cereal wins the show's own Over The Top Rumble anyway, over four other finalists including E-Dogg himself, to become the #1 contender for the vacant Heavyweight Championship. A week later TraV and Cereal meet one-on-one for the belt in a three-match series billed a Street Fight, then a Cage match, then a Ladder match — the only copy of the show that survives stops partway through the first fall, Cereal stomping TraV with a chair, no finish recorded anywhere in the source. Five days later the fed's own title histories page still read the belt Vacant, and the card it posted for the following Saturday billed a fresh #1 contendership match entirely — the Redeemer against Chris Daniels — without the TraV/Cereal series ever producing a champion at all.

The Rising Star Championship's false start Written by AI, 2026

New Age Wrestling's very first match is billed for the Rising Star Championship — Weaponary against Johnny Blaze — and Johnny Blaze wins it clean off the Blaze Bomb. E-Dogg cuts his own entrance music to come to the ring and void the result on the spot, telling both men neither had earned it. The belt sits vacant for a week. On the second Anarchy, Mystic J, billed at seven feet ten and 410 pounds, beats Weaponary to become the fed's first Rising Star Champion for real, and gives its own interviewer Julia Roberts a backstage interview about it with the belt still over his shoulder. Johnny Blaze never gets a rematch: later the same night he no-shows a separate booking against Darkside and is fired on the air.
