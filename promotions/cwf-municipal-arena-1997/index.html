Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

This CWF opened its registration on 17 January 1997 as a stats-driven fantasy wrestling game: a wrestler's owner picked a fighting style, a cheat factor, and a moveset, and matches ran twice a week on hidden health totals the rules never fully explained, “done as realistically as possible.” A win-loss record fed an experience system — every 1,000 points bought a new hold and five points of health — and the first week's action was announced from the start as a tournament to crown a champion, who would then defend weekly against the number-one contender.

That tournament ran 28 January 1997 out of Municipal Arena as a 32-man bracket, broadcast with the arena's own announce team introducing its stables in vignette form: the militaristic Officer Corps, led by a five-star Commander over Major Destruction, General Mayhem, and Captain Carnage; twin brothers Maynard and Gaynard Gerkle, trained by their mother, a self-billed “13 time World Womens Wrestling Champion”; and one half of the Cyberwarriors, Zoker, who beat The Awesome Aero with his tag partner Nezbar's own ringside distraction. Major Destruction won his opening match with a finisher called Heavy Artillery and went on to take the tournament itself.

By the Week One results, 3 February 1997, Major Destruction was defending that title as champion of record against Nezbar in the main event — and lost it, pinned by a single powerbomb in under two minutes after out-wrestling him all tournament, a result the broadcast called finding “a Squirrel” after being “loaded for bear.” The same card carried the promotion's stable rivalries into the ring: Blood of the Gang Lordz was disqualified against Captain Carnage for pulling a gun at ringside, and Emerson Wildside beat fellow tournament semifinalist Todd “The Bodd” Thomas.

Week Two's rankings, posted 5 February, welcomed the Gerkle twins back to action and a new wrestler, Hellstorm, and set up a run of stable feuds for the week after — the Gang Lordz and the Officer Corps both separately challenging the Iron Fists to a tag match, Red Demon calling out General Mayhem for a cage match. No document later than that rankings post survives. How Nezbar's title reign fared, or whether the promotion ran a third week at all, isn't in the surviving record.
