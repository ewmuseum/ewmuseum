Countdown to the I-Crown, Part One

28 July 1998
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
. ___ _ _ / __|___ _ _ _ _| |_ __| |_____ __ ___ _ | (__/ _ \ || | ' \ _/ _` / _ \ V V / ' \ \___\___/\_,_|_||_\__\__,_\___/\_/\_/|_||_| _ | |_ ___ | _/ _ \ \__\___/ ___ ___ |_ _|__ / __|_ _ _____ __ ___ _ | |___| (__| '_/ _ \ V V / ' \ |___| \___|_| \___/\_/\_/|_||_| [The screen stays black for a couple of seconds, before the World Cup of Wrestling logo fades up on the black background. Then "Final Countdown" by Europe starts playing, and the logo and background start fading out. Taking its place is a television studio, with the usual desk, on this occasion occupied by two men. Behind them is the WCoW logo, and a series of TV screens. Both men are wearing green suit jackets - the one one the left looks in his 40s, while the one on the right is much younger.] [As the music fades out, words appear at the bottom of the screen. Below the presenter on the left are the words "Bryan Nucci", and under the one on the right is "Lance Hart".] BN: Hello everyone, and welcome to the Countdown to the I-Crown Finals! That’s right, in just a few hours the first ever I-Crown champion will be "crowned". I’m Bryan Nucci, and I’m here once again with former wrestler Lance Hart. LH: Thanks Bryan. In the next hour or so we’ll be taking a look at the eight men who will fight it out in Chicago, Illinois later tonight, with their common goal of course being the coveted 1998 I-Crown title. BN: We’ll also hear comments from I-Crown organiser Fletch, as he speaks about the success of the tournament and his thoughts on the past two months of action. Right now though, we’re going to recap the schedule for tonight’s matches. LH: The eight finalists are of course US pool winner King Apples, Canada pool winner Derek Mota, UK pool winner Marty Warnett, South Pacific pool winner "Bullywug" Glenn Hudson, Eastern Europe pool winner Wuzho, Asia pool winner Kashan Akuma, Americas pool winner La Pulga Gigante, and Mediterranean pool winner Fabrice Duvalle. BN: Those eight competitors will begin tonight’s megaevent by fighting it out in an over-the-top-rope battle royal. The order of elimination will decided seedings for the tournament, and who fights who in the first round. LH: This will be followed by four first-round matches - with the top seed fighting the bottom seed and so on. From there the four winners will battle in two matches, while the four losers will battle in two matches. BN: The losers of the two loser bracket matches will be eliminated from the tournament, while the winners of those matches will advance to a three-way semifinal. The third competitor in that match will be the winner of a match between the losers of the winner’s bracket matches. LH: And meanwhile the two wrestlers who won their winner’s bracket matches go straight into a semifinal to fight each other. BN: The winners of the semfinals then meet in the grand final, to find the I-Crown 1998 champion. If all that sounds a bit confusing folks, it’ll be clearer as we progress tonight. The main thing to know is that the two wrestlers who meet in the grand final will be taking part in at least their fifth match of the night, not counting the battle royal, and possibly even their sixth match of the night. LH: That’s a big ask Bryan. Obviously stamina is going to become a big factor, as is pace. A wrestler who goes all out in the early matches will likely not have anything left in the final stages, so it’s a matter of pacing yourself. BN: Yes, but that said, a win in the first round would make it much easier, so winning early on is also vitally important. Remember that those coming through the loser’s bracket have to fight a three-way semifinal, whereas those coming through the other bracket have a normal semifinal. I know which one I’d rather be in. LH: True. Well, the United Center in Chicago sold out for this event weeks ago, as the wrestling world watches with interest this event which began as a simple idea put forward by wrestling personality Fletch. Since its inception, this tournament has stolen headlines left right and center, and has put a lot of top wrestlers to the test. Earlier this week we caught up with Fletch, and here’s what he had to say about his "baby"... [The camera pulls away from the commentators, and zooms in on a TV screen behind them. Suddenly the TV takes up the whole screen, as we see the tape of the interview roll. The first thing we see is Lance sitting in a black leather lounge chair.] LH: Hi, this is Lance Hart reporting for the World Cup of Wrestling. I’m here with WCoW co-organiser Fletch, who has been the driving force behind this unique concept known worldwide as the I-Crown tournament. Thanks for coming along Fletch. Fletch: Your welcome Lance. It’s great to be here. LH: First of all, how did you come up with the idea of the I-Crown? Fletch: You know Lance, a lot of people ask me that question. It happened while I was running my old league, the NECW. Being originally from New Zealand, I have always looked at wrestling on the global scene, and the NECW contained wrestlers from all over the world - Australia, Scotland, Brazil, Japan.....all sorts of nations. In fact, we used to get called the United Nations of Wrestling as a joke. One day I thought it would be cool to have a tournament to find the NECW’s top international wrestler, with eight wrestlers all representing a different country. Unfortunately NECW had to close because of various reasons, but I still wanted to go through with the tournament idea. My friend Karol Zielinski was running a series of independent events, so I told him about the idea, and he loved it. I sat down and refined the idea, and when I went looking for international wrestlers to take part, I found that choosing eight was going to be way too restrictive. Instead I decided to split the world into eight regions, and choose four wrestlers from region. The I-Crown was born. LH: Were you expecting the response you got? Fletch: Well, when you try something new like this, you’re always a bit wary about how it’s going to be taken. I had a fear that not many wrestlers would like the idea enough to take up their spare time in a tournament, but luckily it didn’t turn out that way. When the responses started coming in, I was overwhelmed to say the least. LH: By the number of big-name stars that signed? Fletch: Yeah. I mean, we were never going to be another IIeW, and I didn’t even ask any of the guys who fought in the Deschenes Cup, because I didn’t want to be seen as copying. But I found that there were some big names which hadn’t taken part in Ron Deschene’s event, and they were eager to take part. I’m talking about guys like Derek Mota, Glenn Hudson, Eddie Jacks, King Apples, Super Tengu....those guys who have made it in the "big feds", or who have been ranked among the elite in the sport. LH: Not all of those big names performed though did they? Fletch: No they didn’t. One thing I’ve learnt from the I-Crown is that reputation means nothing. Sure, some of the guys like Hudson, Mota and Marty Warnett have proven their class, but others were nothing but a big flop. On the other hand, guys who are not that well known outside their home leagues, wrestlers like Wuzho and Kashan Akuma, have really proved they can foot it with the big boys, which is great to see. LH: The finals night is of course coming up this weekend. What are you expecting from this event? Fletch: Well honestly I couldn’t pick a winner. Any of the eight finalists could win, so it’s going to be interesting to see who does emerge victorious. The only thing I’m certain of is that the matches will be intense, because all of the wrestlers have talked to me about their desire to win this event. With twelve matches plus a battle royal, the fans in Chicago, and around the world via cable tv, are in for a night unlike anything they’ve ever seen before. LH: There’s no doubt about that Fletch. Well, thanks for agreeing to this interview, and here’s looking forward to a great I-Crown finals night. Fletch: Thanks. [The scene fades out, and suddenly we’re back in the studio.] BN: Comments there from World Cup of Wrestling co-organiser Fletch. Right, well next up we’re going to start our look at the eight men in tonight’s match. Let’s go back to June 13 in Montreal, Canada. The first ever I-Crown match - the Eastern Europe pool match. Two men are left in the ring - Sweden’s Kristof Johansen and Romanian Wuzho....... [The screen goes blank as we cut to the video, and then snaps back into life, as the following words appear at the bottom:] VIDEO FOOTAGE: I-CROWN NIGHT ONE [JUNE 13 1998] ============================================== [As Wuzho picks up his opponent, Johanssan manages to roll him up, surprising him totally, 1.....2..... kick out. Johanssan gets up and continues the match with a legdrop. He picks up his opponent, whips him into the ropes and executes a clothesline, but Wuzho ducks down. As he returns, Kristof goes for a back body drop, but Wuzho kicks him to the face and executes yet another DDT. He goes for the cover, 1...2.... kickout.] BF: Once again, he uses the DDT. That’s quite a powerful move! Not many wrestlers could get up from two DDTs, but Kristof Johannsan isn’t most wrestlers! [Wuzho goes for a vertical suplex, but Kristof counters it into his own back suplex and continues with a flying elbow drop. He continues the match with some kicks to the chest and he climbs yet again to the top rope, this time executing a shooting star press.] LVK: WOW!!! I can’t believe all this action is happening in the match! One moment, Wuzho is on the top, the next it’s Kristof dealing out the pain! [Johanssan, thinking that Wuzho is still on the mat, climbs to the top rope, not seeing what’s happening behind him. Wuzho climbs to the second top rope and puts his arms around Johanssan’s body, executing a super-German suplex, going for the cover, 1....2..... kick out again!] SW JOLLY GOOD SHOW!! That was an astounding move! Both men are certainly going to their limits, even if they are not great wrestlers like Nick Leshenn or Robet d’Artois. [Wuzho is signaling his finishing move. He picks up his opponent, puts his head between his legs and goes for the jack-knife powerbomb, but Johanssan counters with a frankensteiner, into a cover, 1...2.... kick out. Johanssan runs into the ropes and executes a sunset flip, covering him again, 1...2..... kick out. He Irish-whips him into the corner and goes for a Hand-Spring Elbow Drop, but Wuzho goes out of the way and Kristof lands on the mat. Wuzho picks him up and easily powerbombs him with his finishing move!] LVK: It’s over! Cover him! [He goes for the cover.....the official jumps on the mat......he puts his hand once on the mat.......the crowd is silent.......he puts it on the mat a second time.....the fans start cheering.......the referee puts his hand on the mat a third time as the fans cheer like crazy and Johanssan kicks out.] SW: It’s OVER! He kicked out _AFTER_ the three count! BF: YESSS!!!! The ref is signaling the end of the match! DING!!! DING!!! DING!!!! [The Romanian flag drops from the rafters,as the referee goes to the corner and hands the Eastern Europe title belt to Wuzho, who raises his arms in victory and still can’t believe that he has won the match.] RC: Ladies and Gentlemen, Kristof Johanssan has been eliminated after 22 minutes and 11 seconds! The winner of the match and I-Crown champion of the Eastern Europe Pool for 1998, here is.......WWUUUZHHHOOOO!!!! [The fans are still cheering and cheer even more, as Wuzho puts the belt high into the air and manages to battle his fatigue and get another "ARIMA!" out of his lungs. He looks over to Johanssan and they shake hands, as the fans give them a standing ovation. Wuzho puts Kristof’s left hand in the air as both celebrate in the ring. Wuzho leaves the ring and and continues the celebrating in the aisle with the fans.] <<video footage ends>> BN: The honour of winning the first ever I-Crown pool match went to Wuzho, the Romanian superstar who while reaching star status in organisations such as the WWO and the IWF/WOW, would not have been picked by many to win that match. LH: You’re right. When you’re up against names like former IIWF cruiserweight champion Icehawk and SCRA regular Johansen, it’s easy to be overlooked. BN: Wuzho, who can best be described as very serious and intense, proved the doubters wrong in Montreal though. Since then many of the more famous wrestlers in this tournament have professed respect for the son of a gypsy. This week we caught up with the big Romanian - this is what he had to say....... [cut to tape] ______________________________________________________________ _ _/ __| I-CROWN | | | (__ THE Wuzho (Est Europe champ) | |___\___| FINALS______________________________________________| [ An abandoned alley in a big city; it is night. A grey haze covers the entire area in a blanket of humidity; a man is in the middle of the alley which is lit by orange lights, hanging from the concrete walls of the surrounding buildings. That man is Wuzho. He is lifting a large block of cement over and over again. He is shirtless and his body is covered in sweat. His hair is untied and sopping wet. He is wearing old work boots and shorts; his face is painted the usual black with two thin red crosses going over his eyes ] [ The camera zooms in front of him to see him work; his face is twisted in pain, but still he keeps lifting and releasing, lifting and releasing, over and over again. With a loud shout, he drops the cement block to the ground and pants, bending over, resting his hands on his knees. He takes a deep breath and looks back up at the camera; he swings around and picks up his grey wool sweater; he wipes the sweat off his body and tosses it aside. He licks his lips before beginning to talk ] It seems now that we have entered the last leg of the buildup to the final round of the I-Crown that many are beginning to feel the pressure. The air is tense and desperation has taken over many of the competitors, who now see that there is very little time left to prepare for this great event. The last moments are filled with anxious competitors trying to pull off whatever they can to make these last as memorable as they can. But not only do we see the last thoughts and comments of all these great wrestlers who have made it this far, we are "treated" to a sudden surge of interest by one Derek Mota, who felt it amusing to go out and make light of most others cultural backgrounds and homelands. You know, Mota, I expected this much from you, and frankly, I'm not surprised. But when it comes down to a event of national pride of international proportions, do you think it is wise, let alone tasteful, to insult others by attacking what is most precious to them? But then again, when I think it through more clearly, perhaps it is something deeper that motivates you, Mota. Perhaps you do not have any ties to your homeland. Perhaps you do not feel the pride of your Canadian brethren... perhaps they have long forsaken you and you seek only to better yourself by filling your head with lies about the uselessness of other cultures and countries. Perhaps you are simply a little man in quest for an identity, lost and confused, who should be pitied and not reviled. But one thing is for certain, is that your sarcastic smirk will be gladly wiped off your face by any one of us left in the competition. And if it is me, it will be all the better. Many others in the competition have not been as vocal or boisterous as others, and to those, all I can do is wish them the best of luck. Perhaps their confidence will lead them down the way, and hopefully for them, they will. To Glenn Hudson, the man who calls himself "The Bullywug", all I can extend to you are heartfelt and sincere wishes of luck in the tournament. You are a man who has shone through the tournament with grace, style and intelligence. Your ways have not only gained my respect, which isn't that important an event, but you have gained the respect of thousands of fans across the world. If I was to meet you in the tournament one-on-one, I would be more than honoured. You are a man worthy of the I-Crown and I hope fortune shines upon your face. As for the man who calls himself the "Ikon", I hardly have anything to add of which I haven't said before. You arrogance is revolting, your words are loathsome, and your thoughts are repugnant and juvenile. You place yourself in a position which only you and the people you pay seem to believe about you. You live a life filled with so many lies that you have begun to believe them yourself. Where is your dominance in this sport? If you are so dominant, why do you even consider yourself an underdog? Yes, we may be two wrestlers whose careers are rising, yes, we are both craving for the spotlight and attention that an event of this magnitude has created, we both know what we can achieve by winning the I-Crown, but the question is not how bad do we want it, but at what cost? No matter how big the prize, or how important the event, I will never sacrifice my name or my integrity to walk away with an honour. If you are so soulless as to sell yourself, your honour, and your country's honour for the sake of your endorsements and for the size of a pay check, if you will as much lower yourself to disgraceful lows simply to have the chance to place a piece of metal around your waist which you know, deep down inside, that you did not win as the better man, then it's all yours. Because when I step into that ring in the battle royal, and we meet face-to-face, I will make you understand that no amount of money, fame or attention can save you from the man who is all business. I do not flaunt myself in front of thousands in each of my interviews because saying that I am better than you is cheap wind. I let my actions talk for me. You do not scare me, nobody in that ring scares me, and I know for sure that I am not scared to wrestle. Can you say the same? You can put on whatever show of acrobatics you want to, but I will slap you down like the dirty fly you are. You have question my integrity and my beliefs long enough, and it's time that someone shows you just how it is real men, authentic human beings settle their wars in the ring. And I hope that fate has it that I will face you in the ring, one-on-one at the I-Crown because frankly, I would have no more pleasure than to hang you over the ring post in victory as your limp body swells under the punishment which I have delivered. And the only thing left in your mind will be the picture of two scowling eyes, fixated on you, with flames of green hate ablaze within them. And once we leave the I-Crown and you return to your posh hotel rooms, you will have realised, that you have feared my arima. [ a bright white flash and everything goes black ] [cut back to studio] BN: You could never accuse Wuzho of being frivalous or not concentrating on the task ahead. He would definitely have to be one of the most intense characters in wrestling. LH: Yeah, but the fact that he’s hardly ever seen in daylight and that he’s from Romania doesn’t exactly help combat jokes about vampires does it? BN: No it doesn’t. We’ve just heard what Wuzho had to say earlier in the week, and right now we’re crossing LIVE to the backstage area of the United Center, where the Romanian is standing by...... =================================== +++ LIVE from the United Center +++ =================================== [ A locker room; the area is not well lit; only a few neons in the showers area provide the necessary light to see anything in the room. Sitting on a bench is Wuzho. He is slumped forward, his elbows resting on his knees. He is in his wrestling attire, shirtless, and seems quite focused ] [ A moment passes with only the sound of Wuzho's breathing ] Now, the time for talk has ended. [ another pause ] Everything we have worked for comes to fruition tonight. [ another pause; he breathes in deeply ] There is nothing more to do or to say, but to reflect on what has been, and let those words, let those actions guide us into the light of the I-Crown's final moments. [ he pauses again, takes a deep breath and stands to face the camera; his face is painted the usual black with two thin red crosses going over his eyes ] There is nothing more to add about those who I am going to face tonight. There is nothing more to say. I stand here before you now with my soul bare, my persona completely naked, just as it had been week after week leading up to this event. What I gave you was nothing short of my very essence, my inner being; I was as authentic to myself as I could. I did not try to be comical, presenting myself in a light to try and amuse you. I did not flaunt myself in ways to convince you of my greatness or my superitority. I did not hide behind any acts or lies or false pretenses. What I showed you week in and week out was who I was, and what the I-Crown meant to me. All I spoke about was me, and what I intended to do, because that will be exactly what I'll be doing tonight. Who cares about endorsements, merchandising, large contracts, or what-have-you; when we all step into the wrestling ring, it becomes a level playing field, and none of those things can help anyone down the line. What I give you tonight is what I gave you over the past weeks: I give you Wuzho. The man who grew up on the roads of foreign lands, who faced prejudice and resentment, who was kept from ever achieving his dreams, who lived with rage and anger breeding within him, who has everything to gain, and absolutely nothing to lose. This is a moment which I have sought for a long, long time and I have no intention to mess any of this up. [ He takes a pause ] I am prepared. [ he breathes in deeply, closes his eyes and relaxes his face muscles ] [ more assertively ] I am prepared. [ Wuzho stares into the camera a few instants; his eyes quiver a little as his breath becomes more pronounced and regular; he licks his lips and expires heavily through his nose before saying one last word, almost under his breath ] Arima. [ A bright white flash and everything goes black ] [cut back to studio] BN: Wow. What can you possibly say about that, other than Wuzho looks as ready for battle as any wrestler has ever been. LH: I’m glad I’m not stepping in that ring with Wuzho tonight. I pity the men who have to face him, I truly do. BN: Well folks, we’ll take a short break now. See you after this........ [cut to commercials]
