A Usenet interfed exhibition, hosted from a GeoCities site by two co-organisers, that ran a one-off crossover pay-per-view drawing invented wrestlers from federations across the newsgroup, then continued as a periodic tournament series. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The World Cup of Wrestling described its own reason for existing plainly, in its co-organiser's own words, the week its first show aired: “this non-fed PPV isn't, and never was, meant to be another IIeW or anything of that magnitude. It came about when people started talking about dream matches on RSPWF, and has served its purpose of providing a platform for those type of matches.” It drew its own card from wrestlers across other federations rather than fielding a roster of its own.

Its first event, A Night to Remember, ran 9 May 1998 and mixed, by its own description, “some big names…some semi-big names…and…some up-and-comers from small feds.” Its own after-thoughts, posted the same day, weigh what worked and what didn't with unusual candour: matches between wrestlers who had never shared a ring before sometimes drew little heat, and handlers who didn't roleplay were written short matches on purpose, so that those who did could earn the better spots later on the card.

That same afterword rules out a second event of the same kind — “will there be another event like A Night to Remember in the World Cup of Wrestling? The answer is probably not” — and redirects the promotion's own future toward a tournament series instead: the I-Crown, and a planned Zielinski Cup after it.

The I-Crown ran that July, drawing regional qualifiers into a global field that included Kashan Akuma of Tokyo, Marty Warnett, Pulga, Wug, and a masked competitor billed only as Wuzho. Its own organiser closed the finished bracket out asking readers directly for feedback, “for once,” on whether the format had worked.

How the I-Crown itself concluded, and whether the promised Zielinski Cup ever followed, isn't recorded.
