JIWWA Results, 11/19/01, Korakuen Hall

19 November 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The J.I.W.W.A. returned to Korakuen Hall today for the second time in 7 days. The fans were packed (2105 &#8211; Sell Out) into Korakuen as the league builds to the big Yokohama Bunka Gym show on 12/15. Several key match-ups filled out the card including The Goddesses of Beauty meeting the team of Keiko Sakura and DARIA Wakizawa. Wakizawa and Sakura met on 12/15 to crown the new Nihon National Junior titleholder. The Main Event reignited some old tensions and further developed some new ones as Rekka Sakura teamed with &#8220;Thunder Jet&#8221; Yuriko Tanaka against 3WI Champion Yori &#8220;DIAMOND&#8221; Kenyuki and &#8220;DANGEROUS QUEEN&#8221; Sachie Yokoyama. Rekka has yet to avenge her 1998 KO loss to Kenyuki. Kenyuki&#8217;s Bloody Knuckle Uraken dropped her en route to her most surprising defeat. Tanaka, on the other hand, is gearing up for her 12/15 match with Yokoyama. The two women have already been involved in a pull-apart fight backstage following the 11/12 Korakuen Hall Main Event. Things are building in a way that can make both the wrestlers and the fans happy. O = Win X = Loss First Match CRUSH Yamada (30&#8217;00 DRAW) Bonnie Bender Two girls were making debuts to open the show. Kazuya Asakino scouted Bender during his last trip to the United States. Yamada, on the other hand, is Yori Kenyuki&#8217;s prize pupil from the EDO Academy. Bender seemed to have good interaction with the Korakuen crowd, getting many fans behind her in the portions of the match she controlled. Yamada wrestled a smart match, relying on a heavy power attack of kicks and strikes. Bender seemed to be working Yamada&#8217;s right arm for most of the match and nearly had the win at 23:13 when she drove Yamada&#8217;s arm down with a Fujiwara Armbar. Yamada was trapped for about 45 seconds before making the ropes. The closing moments saw Yamada lay into Bender with kicks and Bender replying with hard chops, both women were knocked down with a minute left. Bender was on her feet first but slightly dazed. Yamada pulled herself up using the ropes for support. Yamada used her last bit of strength to hit a lariat on Bender for a nearfall. Even with time running out, Yamada tried for her Thunderfire Powerbomb. Bender reacted quickly and cradled Yamada leading to a pinfall attempt as the time limit expired. The fans were sucked into this match as both girls&#8217; styles reacted with fans. Bender has guts and impressed everyone with her mat technique. It is obvious Yamada was trained by Yori Kenyuki by the brutal kicks and shoot elements blended into her offense. Both girls received a round of cheers after the match. There is already talk of a rematch on the next Korakuen show on 11/25. Second Match Haruko Itzuki (7&#8217;06 Northern Lights Suplex) Yumi Kudo (EDO Academy) A very quick paced basic match. Kudo is another Kenyuki disciple but seems very green. She stuck to trading mat holds with Itzuki and executing the basic bodyslam and dropkick. Itzuki is just as good as ever, her timing crisp and execution flawless. End of the match saw Itzuki hit a missile dropkick and follow up with a Northern Lights Suplex for the 3 count. Itzuki has made it clear to the press that she wants to wrestle the winner of Sakura/Wakizawa on 12/15. Third Match SPARROW Yokota (27:37 La Magistral Cradle Counter) Misae Suzuki A great match between 2 fan favorites. The crowd reaction for Yokota was strong but the schoolgirls went nuts for Suzuki. A great lucha flavored match with lots of aerial spots by both women. This was a hot match from the start. Suzuki attacked Yokota before the bell and hit a dropkick sending Yokota out of the ring. Suzuki followed up with a perfect Tope Suicida to the outside ring area. The match shifted momentum several times leading to several nearfalls. Move of the match goes to Yokota with an incredible Cannonball Senton from the top rope to the arena floor on Suzuki. Crowd was hot for the finish as Suzuki hit Reverse DDT followed by a Release Throw German Suplex Suzuki then went for the pin with a La Magistral Cradle. Yokota countered the move however, by trapping Suzuki in a Mat Crucifix scoring a surprising 3 count. Yokota keeps her momentum going into the 12/15-title challenge with Kenyuki. Match 4 Keiko Sakura (X) Angel Saito (22&#8217;02 Avalanche Style Destiny Hammer) D. Wakizawa Mina Yamaguchi (O) There were lots of problems here as Wakizawa and Sakura had trouble communicating. DARIA Wakizawa did show why she is the pick for Rookie Of The Year honors as she carried the weight for her team. It all started going wrong when Yamaguchi ducked a Wakizawa lariat at 17:27. The lariat instead hit Keiko Sakura, leading to a shoving incident between Sakura and Wakizawa. It seemed at crucial moments following that that Sakura refused to tag in to save her partner. The heat segment on Wakizawa was incredible as both Yamaguchi and Saito unleashed every head-dropping move they knew in order to try and end the match. Saito was downright furious when Wakizawa kicked out of a Gory Special Bomb. Finally at 28:12 Wakizawa tagged out to a reluctant Sakura. Sakura was double teamed by the Goddesses of Beauty relentlessly while a drained Wakizawa collapsed on the ring apron. The end came when Yamaguchi planted Sakura into the mat with a Michinoku Driver. This set-up the Crucifix/Chokseslam combo that finally scored the 3 count. Keiko Sakura was not happy post match yelling at an exhausted DARIA Wakizawa. Sakura even went as far as slapping Wakizawa before both girls had a very intense staredown. Wakizawa, to her credit, walked away. Final Match Rekka Sakura (X) Yori Kenyuki (38&#8217;06 KO &#8220;Blinding Queen&#8221;) Yuriko Tanaka Sachie Yokoyama (O) There is only one word that can describe this match&#8230; violence! Rekka Sakura seemed very out of place in this match as Kenyuki, Yokoyama and Tanaka threw stiff kicks and punches at each other between brutal head-dropping power moves. Sakura took 3 Uraken&#8217;s from Kenyuki and 4 &#8220;Blinding Queen&#8221; knee strikes from Yokoyama in the final 5 minutes of the match. The crowd went wild when after the 3rd Uraken Kenyuki front mounted Sakura and threw down punch after punch to Rekka&#8217;s exposed face. Rekka made no attempt to cover up and was saved by the ref pulling Kenyuki off her. Credit has to be given to Yuriko Tanaka, the youngster hung with the big guns of the promotion and gave as good as she got. Most impressive was her knocking down Kenyuki with Kenyuki&#8217;s own &#8220;Bloody Knuckle&#8221; Uraken. The segments between Yokoyama and Tanaka seemed like a pit fight with the punches being especially brutal. The end saw Rekka Sakura unable to answer the referee&#8217;s 10 count following Yokoyama&#8217;s 5th &#8220;Blinding Queen&#8221; of the match. This marks the second time this tour Rekka Sakura has had to be carried back to the locker area by attendants. A great card overall. The fans were thrilled to see Sachie Yokoyama again and really were impressed with the EDO Academy girls and Bonnie Bender. There is genuine concern from fans over Rekka Sakura&#8217;s health. Her last 2 performances have been lackluster and the blows to the head she has suffered over these last 2 matches have everyone worried. On the flipside, Yuriko Tanaka seems to be breakout star everyone thought she was 18 months ago in blaze. Always recognized for her tag teamwork, Tanaka has been standing out lately. The fact she almost KO&#8217;d Yori Kenyuki has many people talking about a possible 3WI title match in the near future. The J.I.W.W.A. returns to Korakuen Hall on 11/25.
