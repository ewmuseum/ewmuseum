A federation of Galveston Island, Texas, that staged Thursday Night Revolt on the island's beaches, pier and hotels from June 2010, toured the Caribbean, Louisiana and Texas, ran to October 2015 and came back for three Sundays in August 2019. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Galveston Island Wrestling opened in a hotel pavilion. Its first Revolt, on 3 June 2010, played to 650 people under fireworks at the San Luis Hotel and Resort, with three commentators, interviewers at the hotel pool and on the beach, and a motto: “Dare to Defy.” The night crowned Aspen Chaud its first champion, in a three-way match with Joshua Acquin as the guest referee, and its owner, Blake Beckett, stripped her of the belt on the air before the show was out.

Revolt was the weekly, Thursday nights at first, 141 of its 279 cards, and the rest of the schedule grew around it: Monday Night Affliction, Extreme TV, Fusion, Rebellion, and pre-shows that give a bout of local talent a paragraph and a WINNER line. In the autumn of 2010 it was written against a rival promotion, EPW, whose Tuesday Night Evolve cards are kept among its own, and the two sides met on the Unscripted card at Pier 21 on 26 September. The Undisputed Championship began in February 2011 with Eric Sparrow; the Television Championship, the busiest of the sixteen title lines on its record, ran to twenty-eight reigns.

The cards named their rooms and took them on the road. Galveston supplied the hotels, the beaches, the Seawall Boulevard Entertainment Complex, Moody Gardens, the Kauffman Arena and the Pleasure Pier. The tours supplied Grand Cayman on 22 September 2011 and Nassau on 13 October, then Louisiana and Arkansas in the spring of 2012, a Wembley Arena card in London for International X on 29 April 2012, billed as an iPPV, and Aggressive Expansion at the Chesapeake Energy Arena in Oklahoma City on 3 June. A cry of “BOOM! BOOM! BOOM!” runs through 160 of the cards, and 245 of them close their matches on WINNER lines.

The schedule was never steady. Nothing survives between 19 December 2012 and 23 May 2013, and the card of 23 June 2013 says the company was “reopened one month ago.” Another stretch runs from 4 August 2013 to 7 March 2014, and another from 25 November 2014 to 15 March 2015. The last card of the first run, Monday Night Revolt on 5 October 2015 from the Ferrell Center in Waco, played to a hundred fans after a night of exhibition matches and looked a week ahead to Thunder in Paradise, which the card before had set aboard a cruise ship. No write-up of that show or of any later night of 2015 survives.

It came back for three Sundays in August 2019. On the 11th it was “LIVE from the Pleasure Pier” for the first time “in a long time,” with Bronx Valescence back in a GIW ring and a five-way ladder match that made Riley Savell “the first North Beach Champion of the new GIW era.” Valescence held the Undisputed Championship by the 18th, and the last card, on the 25th, ended with Agustin Gates winning the North Beach belt by putting a challenger through a table. Nothing later is dated.
