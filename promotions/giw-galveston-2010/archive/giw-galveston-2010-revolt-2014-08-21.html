GIW — Card

Revolt, 21 August 2014

21 August 2014
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
GALVESTON ISLAND WRESTLING PRESENTS: REVOLT Live from the Texas City Arena – August 21st, 2014 The show fades into a shot of Tony Miranda standing in the ring with a microphone in hand. His eyes glare out toward the cameras, then the staging. The audience can sense the tension emanating from him due the the erroneous results of his match last week. Tony looks to be fuming, but he soon reveals why when he speaks. TONY MIRANDA: "I am livid! My match last week did not go according to plan. I am pissed that all of you paid to see a street fight between myself and Jack Waters, but didn't get to see that match. Last week, in my locker room I found a note and in it was a statement by the new administrator of operations here in GIW that said the match would be made into a singles competition and that our rivalry needed to be toned down." It was obvious by the tone of his voice that he was heated inside, and his eyes burned with resolve to get to the bottom of the matter, as well as get what he bargained for; A street fight with Jack Waters. The crowd chanted behind him, but silenced when he spoke again. TONY MIRANDA: "I'd like to think that in all the years I've spent with this company, and in the company of some of the greatest individuals to walk through here, not only that, but being a former Undisputed Champion of Galveston Island Wrestling, that I would be given what was promised to me. See, Alexis Terry, the schemer that she is, booked me and Jack for this series of five matches, the first being a Street Fight. Yet, when Alexis is run out of town by some anonymous administrator, my match has to be toned down? I'm sorry, but am I not the man that took down Scar in the Tai Pei match? Am I not the first guy to step in line when a new match type is suggested? I am an innovator because these fans deserve a great show from great wrestlers! I want, no, I demand that tonight's match be made into the Street Fight that it should have been last week!" Jack Waters suddenly appeared on the big screen and the camera quickly cut to him. He was close up, his face the only thing in view from the neck up. He, too, was fuming, but perhaps for a few different reasons than Tony. JACK WATERS: "I understand the frustration, Tony. Our match should have been brutal. We were both completely, and utterly cheated out of it. I feel like I'm being left out of some loop that you're in on!" TONY MIRANDA: "What!?" JACK WATERS: "You heard me! You were the one that got contacted by the anonymous administrator, Tony! Where was my notice? Oh, right, at the guerrilla position being told that at the last minute our match was a singles competition and that it had to be light on the violence. I think this is all a set up! If it weren't for those changes, I would have wiped this entire state with your blood, Tony! I was going to win that street fight that you had changed!" The heated argument sparked chanting from the crowds. They backed Tony Miranda whole heartedly, but when he spoke, they still roared for him until he reached out to silence them. It took a moment, but his fans were dissolved vocally. When he had the moment, he spoke. TONY MIRANDA: "This is ridiculous, Jack! Why would I have changed the stipulation? Do you really believe that I'm scared of you? I've held the Undisputed Championship, you've yet to hold a winning streak! I will make damn certain that tonight's match between us is what it should have been because I am the better man and I hold up to my end of the bargain. When I say I'm fighting you in a street fight, then I'm doing just that. As for this new administrator, I don't care who are what she is, but this match his happening. I rallied for this rivalry to finally come to and I'm not letting it fall in shambles to politics. Stay out of my matches, and we're solid. As for you, Jack, I'll see you tonight, in a proper street fight." With that message made loud and clear, Jack's face on the screen returned to the show's view of Tony Miranda in the ring. The crowd rallied behind him as the show cut to a break. QUOTE Upon return from break, Astro's theme by Mumford & Sons, "Awake My Soul" played into the arena as he came out to the stage. Antonio Zeroni had already made his entrance during the break. OLIVER THIBODAUX: "Zeroni left a mark when he came into the company, I believe he could be the next big thing, like Wake Walker." RILEY STEVENS: "Before he walked out utterly defeated? No, see, Astro has a record, not a great one, but a record that is getting better with each event. My money is on him tonight, because I happen to know that with his victory comes a shot at contendership." OLIVER THIBODAUX: "Where do you get that from?" RILEY STEVENS: "It said so in the card description. Don't you read these thing?" OLIVER THIBODAUX: "In all honesty, I just go home and lay about until the show itself." RILEY STEVENS: "No wonder I have the better name. So, yes, Astro has entered the ring and these two competitors are staring one another down. Zeroni looks impressive matched against Kevin Astro, but we will see once the bell rings who gets the win." Zeroni reached out to lock up his opponent, but Astro ducked under and contorted the muscular arm of his opponent into a wrist lock from behind. He pushed off, and as Zeroni turned, Astro smacked him cold with a super kick. Whether applied as a normal strike, or something special, as long as it hit its mark it always dropped its target. Zeroni crashed to the ground and Astro seized the moment to pin him, even if it wasn't to be. The referee struck the canvas once, then twice, then suddenly an uproar from the crowd filled the arena as he struck for three. Zeroni was beaten on his second week, and Astro has quelled the newcomer before he could get a streak. WINNER via Pinfall: KEVIN ASTRO! The show returned with a view of Tony Miranda in the corridor by the unlabeled office of the new administrator. He waited outside, arms folded, and eyes forward. When the camera panned, it showed Alexis Terry standing opposite of him. ALEXIS TERRY: “I will not stand idly by while this person steals my job and fires my allies. I am the one that wanted your match, isn't that right Tony?" TONY MIRANDA: "That may be so, but you are corrupt, Terry. I don't know how to handle management, and I'm not going to try. I'm seeing the administrator before you, and I don't care what you say, or do.." These words did not go well with Alexis Terry, but as soon as the door opened, he brushed passed her and into the room, the door slamming behind him. The camera quickly cut to a wide shot of the stage. The song 'Vicarious Devil' by Tool echoed throughout the arena and the crowds went wild. Beck Ramsey appeared before them upon the stage with a hand held high, the hand with his title displayed for all to see. In his other hand was his pipe bomb and he was ready to drop it. Beck Ramsey: “So, it looks like Harlequin has decided to single herself out and become part of the match in ten days at Blackout! You might wonder if I'm trembling in my boots about it? Not at all. Remember, even though I don't need to be pinned to lose my title, I also don't need to pin Madison Seton. With Harlequin in this match, I now can let them work each other into a black sweat and then seize the moment when opportunity arises." The crowd backed him, mostly. There seemed be some divide, but he held them in the palm of his belt-holding hand. Beck Ramsey: “When both of my opponents have had their exposure to the Blackout, then I will reign as champion undisputed." The show then cut to a break. QUOTE The two competitors were both in the ring as the show returned. Mo Chronic was alone, save for his grill. The Captain struck first, but Mo deflected. He kneed his opponent in the gut, a strike over the ribs and then a snap DDT to seal the deal. He pinned Captain, but refused to let him go out that way. Mo was playing with him. Captain was tired of it, and Mo knew, so he pulled him up to his feet and took the time to knead Captain into a withered mess before setting up for this 'Wrestling Game Al Bundy', the crowd screamed in unison the familiar Peggy cry of 'Al' as he performed it, undoubtedly driving the Captain out of his mind. With a victory under his belt after the pin, Mo lifted up, his partner then joined him and they celebrated as they always do, and will. WINNER via Pinfall: MO CHRONIC! The camera panned over to the stage as the match contenders began to drift out of the arena. Mo' Love first, then slowly Captain with the referee, but all of this unseen as the camera kept on the man at the stage. Christopher Darren Erns. In his hand was a blood stained kendo stick. The same one he had used to brutalize Michael Lynch with. He began to make his way toward the ring, The Captain still at ring side with the referee. As Mo' Love was about to pass CDE, he raised the kendo stick and readied to strike. RILEY STEVENS: "Why do this Erns?" OLIVER THIBODAUX: "I heard from the back that he wasn't allowed a match with Lynch last week. Instead, he was chosen as the voice of the new administrator." RILEY STEVENS: "Michael Lynch was destroyed. Moment of Glory was clearly not his night, but that doesn't give CDE the right to take out his frustrations on the other talents." OLIVER THIBODAUX: "Since Moment of Glory, and the rejection last week of his match, CDE's brain has just tossed reason into the air like Yahtzee." During the commentary, the camera showed Mo' Love back away from the strike. They tried to get around CDE, but the vitriol came again with another swing. The referee tried to put distance between the three, but just as CDE came in to strike the official, the lights blackened instantaneously. A dull roar changed to faint cheers that grew only louder and louder as those whom could see the dim figure emerge from the entry curtain from flashing cameras and bright lighters. Fog filled the stage as the figure stepped forth. A voice broke through the darkness, CDE's. His voice was muffled by the roar the crowd now louder than he expected. A dim light shown on the scene so they could see him with the raised kendo stick in one hand, a microphone in the other. Mo' Love looked on, but off-center. Lynch was slowly approaching from behind, but CDE did not notice. His speech faded into the crowd's audio distraction, but his last words were louder, even than them. CHRISTOPHER DARREN ERNS: "The treachery of the demon is nothing compared to the betrayal of the angel." Finally, Christopher Darren Erns got a strike in. The referee ducked, not wanting to get hit, and the mark was made on Mo Chronic's jaw. He swung around as the strike knocked him senseless. His partner tried to lunge at CDE, but the man behind the attacker stopped him. Instead, he pulled his partner from the fray and the two began to back up toward the ring, and into the darkness. Noticing this, CDE turned. The camera closed in on the two with a mid-shot. Michael Lynch and CDE glared into one another's eyes. The crowd roared even louder. Some jeered Lynch, others booed CDE. They also had their fans as loud as they could be. Some were disgusted with CDE, but the moment was too much for anyone not to be excited for. They knew an explosion was bound to happen. When he turned, he didn't know what to expect. Probably the administrator. When he finally could see in the dim light and darkness that shrouded them that it was Michael Lynch, his expression changed to surprise. Instead to of the explosion the crowd expected, CDE handed Lynch the kendo stick stained in his own blood, and like an apprentice to the dark lord, he took to one knee and lowered his head. To this action, Michael Lynch began to strike hard and repeatedly with the stick against CDE. His rival merely smiled and winced in sickly joy as the pain shot through him like sharp needles as the stick hit him fast thwack after thwack, which echoed through the arena. When he could take no more, and Lynch made his last strike, Erns knelt lower and then offered the microphone to the masked demon. MICHAEL LYNCH: "With Christopher Darren Erns under my control, you will be the force driving me to victory to obtain the Undisputed Championship title. I will become a champion whose reign will defy even that of Alioth Starre and Aspen Chaud. I will be the champion that will make this company buckle to its knees. In return, CDE, you will be a better Deep South Champion for it. You, my degenerate, will climb this jagged mountain and claim it as I claim the championship I have earned. Together we will rule Galveston Island Wrestling." The arena reverted to pitch darkness. RILEY STEVENS: "What in the Fox Channel's graveyard did we just see?" OLIVER THIBODAUX: "I think CDE just joined forces with Michael Lynch." RILEY STEVENS: "After this night, I may not believe in monsters under the bed, but I'll be damned if there aren't any under the ring." As the light returned, the arena was clear, save for the crowd, and the contenders of the previous match. Mo' Love and Captain, with the referee of the bout quickly retreated to the back. The show then cut to a break. QUOTE Christopher Darren Erns was back. He made his entrance to the ring, but this time with Michael Lynch leading the way for him. This was CDE's match against Beck Ramsey, but with the new alliance between the two, who knew what would happen. When they were settled in the ring, "Vicarious Devil" brought the crowds to their feet. Beck stood once again in a victorious pose at the edge of the stage. His belt held high for all to see that he was the true Undisputed Champion. He descended the aisle with his eyes on the two at the ring, showing them that he was not some person to be trifled with when he lifted the title to them and barked remarks at them. Lynch and CDE stayed almost calm and motionless in the ring until Beck finally entered. Instead of that moment where the referee would take the title from Beck temporarily, then they'd circle one another until the bell rang out, CDE lunged at his opponent just as Beck slid into the ring. The belt fell to the matting outside and Beck was trampled by several mud hole stomps. The referee had no choice but to get the bell to ring quickly and the match to begin immediately. Michael Lynch descended from the edge of the ring and into the solitude of the outside as he watched CDE take control of Beck Ramsey within. First was an undisputed kick to start with flare, to show Beck who was the true champion in the ring. CDE was the Deep South champion, but he didn't feel any less than the Undisputed one, considering that title actually was being disputed. Then he performed a stalling vertical suplex with a cover after. Though Beck pulled his shoulder up and broke the pin at the count of two, CDE was not concerned with it. It simply meant, as a sick smile etched across his face, that more pain would be inflicted on his opponent. He yanked Beck from the ground, peeling him lick a sticker, then performed several hard, repeating headbutts until Ramsey staggered back into the ropes. There, Beck made his stand. CDE was coming in for some European uppercuts, but he dodged it. Instead, Beck used the ropes to perform a springboard clothesline that dropped CDE light a fat sack of bricks. The impact echoed throughout the arena. He then expertly applied a Fujiwara arm bar, but CDE rolled away just in time. As expertly as it was applied, CDE was on top of his game. The two rolled themselves up to their feet and collided, but Beck won out on that one with a belly-to-belly suplex. CDE jumped to his feet and Ramsey just performed another, then another, then a cover. Michael came to the edge of the ring, not to interrupt, but to [/i]watch[/i]. This still distracted Beck and it let CDE get out of the cover at a two-count. He glared over at Lynch, who wanted the title Beck held, or Madison. At the moment, his eyes were set on Beck. Beck Ramsey dragged up his opponent set him back down with a hard backbreaker. This was just a show for Michael Lynch now. He made sure to face-front these maneuvers so Lynch could see his new apprentice being destroyed before him. A poor choice in allies, perhaps? Beck grinned. He wasn't grinning too much longer after CDE countered a back suplex and used the maneuver on Beck Ramsey. After that, a spine buster sealed the deal for him. Beck was laid out flat like a pancake. For victory, and for Michael Lynch, CDE was determined to perform the Malice Acrimony. This was his signature maneuver. He presented Beck Ramsey to the crowd, fallen and broken. Soon, he backed toward the corner where Lynch resided and waited until Beck turned onto his stomach. When Ramsey did, and tried to lift up off the canvas, CDE came and performed a hard punt that struck Beck and caused him to reel back with the severity of it. Beck Ramsey was not coming up. The referee came to him to lift an arm, but it fell back. At this point, Michael Lynch joined CDE in the ring as they watched Beck Ramsey go through the procedure of losing via knock out. By the time he was stirring again, the bell had rung and the victory claimed. Christopher Darren Erns glared out to the crowd, arm raised, then he and Lynch departed the ring, the damage had been done and the war had been won. This, to them, was a message clearly sent. As it was, the shot cut to a break. WINNER via Knockout: CDE! QUOTE The cameras cut to a two-shot of the commentary team upon return of the show. RILEY STEVENS: "This just in! Tonight, I'm being told by the administrator herself that we have a special surprise for this match!" OLIVER THIBODAUX: "Thank you, boss, I'll let them know. Sorry, that was her in our earpiece and this match is definitely a go! We have–" RILEY STEVENS: "Let me announce it! I want to tell them the that we'll be seeing Ma–." Vixtrola cuts the commentary off with "Gunboat" as the Undisputed Champion, or at least one of them, Madison Seton, breaks through the entry curtain. The crowd jeers and sneers as she raises the title high. Not an hour earlier Beck Ramsey made the same gesture, but Madison, in this moment, was the true champion. After she had made her way to the ring, her opponent and number one contender for the title came out to the ring. Harlequin was ready to show how much she earned the title, and had deserved it over the petty squabbling of Ramsey and Seton. The ring was now full. A referee between the two and the the match set. The bell rang and they were off! Seton and Harlequin both locked up in a grapple hold that seemed eternal as neither wanted to give the other control, but soon it was forced in Harlequin's favor. She pulled Madison into a classic headlock and tightened when the other female opponent tried to break free. She pandered to the crowd as Seton struggled to follow instead, forced actually. It took only so much, and she waited only so long to make her move before Seton reared her elbow and then stabbed into Harlequin's ribs. She ignored it at first, but Seton was relentless. Once broken free, Madison threw a toe kick to the gut of her opponent and performed a DDT to smack the skull of her painted contender into the canvas, leaving green smudges where a face had impacted pure white matting. She rolled Harlequin over, but the victory would be worked for, not given. Harlequin rolled onto her side, into Seton's body to break the pin. Madison was not pleased and glared at the referee as Harlequin rolled away, but Seton didn't let her go too far. She pulled her back and gave her opponent a quick one-two punch to the face to keep her in check. Harlequin was now Seton's little voodoo doll. A stab of the knuckles to flesh here, a knee there and pain shot through her like the branches of a tree extending throughout her body. After a swift clothesline, Seton looked down at her opponent and laughed. She mouthed something, but the jeer of the crowd blended it with their audio disruption. Perhaps something about Harlequin never becoming champion. When Madison reached down to pull her number one contender up, Harlequin transitioned it into an arm drag, rolled onto her feet, and then dropped down with an elbow. The chain set of moves caught Seton by surprise. Quickly, Harlequin applied the Boston crab and dragged, as Seton writhed, her opponent toward the center of the ring. Seton knew this move well, it was one she expertly applied on many of her opponents as well. Because of her extensive knowledge of the submission hold, she knew not to fight it. It was too much effort to reach for the ropes and crawl like a victim to safety. Instead, she used her understanding of its mechanics to twist around and, yes, pull Harlequin's hair. This was foul play, but it forced Harlequin to think of something other than inflicting pain, and it forced the referee to get involved. He counted, she let go. Then she did it again and once again the scene played out. Next, she writhed and managed to find some way to break one leg free. It now became a single-legged Boston crab, but that freed leg allowed her to kick and jerk around like a shark out of water. It was too much for Harlequin to control so she let go. Instead of just dropping Madison to the canvas, though, she climbed to the side to roll her opponent face up and kept the leg she held under her control. Madison recognized the contortion she was now being forced into. Her body turned back around as if it were the same submission hold, but it wasn't. Harlequin was going for a sharpshooter, and this can not happen! Madison kicked and propelled Harlequin's rear-end from her and then rolled backward onto her feet. When Harlequin turned and rushed her, she performed a Russian legsweep to down the painted woman. The match was carded, but the surprise wasn't that these two were going head to head, no, everyone knew that. The surprise was a distraction, as Beck Ramsey rushed to the ring. His eyes were on Madison Seton, and hers on his. He slid in like an expert baseball player and in all the confusion, the referee was bumped and knocked aside. He collapsed by one of the ring's corners as the two powers collided. It was Madison and Beck at it again at the center of the ring. Harlequin recovered, slowly, but surely. When she turned her head from the blank and green canvas spots, she saw her opponents for the next super show. Beck and Madison fighting, ignoring her once again. It was time something was done about that. She climbed to her feet, used the ropes and propelled herself forward with a great lunge. When she met them with the full intensity of force, they turned toward her in surprise, she grappled their heads and leapt up to bring them down with some Clowning Around. The impact caused Beck to be smacked hard against the canvas, fall backward and roll out of the ring. All of this just in time for the referee to get his head together. He saw a cover up happen in his clearing daze, then began the count. One, two, and three! She threw herself from the canvas in victory. Her paint sweated and peeled into little blotches on the canvas, and exhausted from the struggles, but she had won! Not only had she won, but she defeated one of the individuals considered to be the Undisputed Champion. Could this be a sign for the triple threat? That's what she hoped. WINNER via Pinfall: THE HARLEQUIN! The arena was clear when the show returned. The crowd were amped and ready for the main event between Tony Miranda and Jack Waters. Before that, however, a soft, feminine voice broke the audience into silence. VOICE: “This is your new administrator speaking. This week, I've decided to give Alexis Terry the night off. A visit from her has proven that she's incapable of letting go, so I'll deal with you, my dear, at Blackout!" The crowd wasn't certain how to react to this. Cheers for the new era, but jeers as well as it seems this new woman had only been making mistakes since she forced Alexis to step down. VOICE: “It is understandable that you all have mixed reactions to my arrival. I have a checkered past with other federations, but Alexis Terry signed me on as a part-time manager. Someone to help her schedule, write, and get things done in the back, but nothing more. However, Alexis Terry couldn't handle the flames you all burnt under her dainty little ass and because of that fact, I've decided to step in and do something about it. I may not be the administrator you wanted, but I'm the one you need. I will make Galveston Island Wrestling character driven, which means the wrestlers will have their say and have their matches to shine and define their careers with. I am not a contender, never was, and so I don't have the cloud of judgement like Alexis Terry does. No, I will not be your heel, nor your face, but someone to handle things with a calm, collected, and neutral set of eyes. That is why I chose CDE last week as my voice, and why I had even disrupted Tony and Jack's match. Even letting Beck Ramsey intercept the last match. A voice is nothing until force is added. At Blackout, you will see the woman behind the new order around here. Next week, I take full control of GIW!" QUOTE Tony Miranda was in the ring, ready for Jack Waters, but there was something wrong with this picture. The big screen at the stage revealed Waters to not even be in the arena. The camera panned back to a wide shot to show that Jack Waters was, not only in his street gear, but also out in the actual streets of Texas City. JACK WATERS: "I'm right outside, Tony. You wanted a street fight? Well, guess what, I got some time with the administrator myself after you had to shovel in. You wanted this match, I wanted this match, but I'm upping the ante. Meet me outside and let's have this fight in the actual streets, not in some tamed wrestling arena. See you soon!" The camera followed Tony as he quickly descended from the ring to find his opponent. He leapt over the barricade, ascended up into the crowds and out to the hallway. Outside Waters waited with a referee at his side. He was just outside the arena, actually, and watched the glass door entrance for his opponent. When Tony finally emerged and lunged forth from the double doors, the match was officially set. Tony didn't come alone, however, no, he had a friend by the name of el Ladder. Taken probably in the hall, from some vendor, it didn't matter. He was loaded and ready to shoot. He took a hard, but slow swing with the edge of the ladder coming at Water's head, but his opponent dodged to the side. Jack came in close, knowing Tony had a long range weapon, he needed to get inside his measure to strike. He jabbed at the ribs of his opponent. When Tony was forced to drop the large metal structure, Waters continued with an uppercut that knocked his opponent onto the street below them. A car beeped as it tried to pass, swerving to not hit them, but Waters didn't mind. He used an Irish whip to slam Tony Miranda into the side of the moving vehicle. His back slapped hard against metal and Tony crumbled to the street below once again. Waters was in his prime now. He pulled Tony close and performed a devastating German suplex. Even though he performed it, the pain of his bare back slamming against rough pavement nearly took him out as well. He recovered more quickly than Tony, however. Tony rolled to the side to evade a stomp, then swiped Jack's feet to trip him, but he only staggered back. Tony managed to pull himself back up and ducked under a right hook, jabbed is opponent with a knee strike, then propelled himself against Jack with a running dropkick. The force struck Jack hard enough to send him reeling into the metal pole of a stop sign. His back seized up and he winced under the pain. The marking of the metal now etched temporarily into his flesh. Jack Waters fell to his knees. His head limp and shoulders pulled down as he grasped the harsh earth below him. When he looked up, he saw Tony running toward him again. He knew this would be the end if Tony could connect with the lariat, but he couldn't allow it. Jack ducked out of the way and off to the side as Tony's body collided with the metal pole. His knees crunched and buckled, his body collapsed and Waters seized the moment to take a chance at a cover. That was all it took for him to reach victory. Now they were even. Each had a victory, but this one was Jack's. Jack Waters stood over Tony, hand held high by the referee, not that he needed help to celebrate this victory over his rival. The show faded to the GIW logo, as Revolt! ended. WINNER via Pinfall: JACK WATERS!
