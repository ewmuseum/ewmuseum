Whatever 12 1st Mariner Arena — Baltimore, Maryland ---- WHATEVER! WHATEVER! Introduction Edward Olmstead once again sat behind his desk, which, for those keeping score at home, was much neater than Reed Young's and didn't contain nearly as many discarded candy wrappers. Smiling, he looked at the camera and began his monologue. "Welcome to the twelfth edition of Whatever, which is going to highlight one of my personal favorite matches from Evo's relatively short history. However, in all the years I was a part of the unified LCW, this match stands up there with the Lethal Combination match as one of the best gimmick matches in the company's history." "What was the Lethal Combination, you may ask?" Olmstead paused, and his face became serious once more. "Duel is going to talk about that during his part of the show, and I don't want to take away from that commentary in the least, as I think it's best that someone who actually participated in the match should be the one to talk about it." "With that said, I'll tell you a bit about the match that you're going to see tonight. It originally took aired on Evo's first supercard, Dawning, which aired on the fX Network on March 27th, 2002." "As one of the co-main events, it featured a cage match between Duel, then the Madhouse Champion, and Frank Heimdal, and this match was anything but ordinary. Leading up to, Alex had started acting strangely, stiffing his opponents and looking to legitimately injure them. It was by luck more than anything that we managed to prevent more serious injuries, and when Frank came to me about the match, I had my reservations." "While I didn't doubt his ability to put a stop to Duel's reign of terror, a part of me still didn't want to risk arguably our hottest commodity, but he was insistent and seemed entirely too driven for me to *not* give him the match. Going in, I think everyone knew it wouldn't be planned out at all, and that's exactly what we got." "In spite of that, though, the emotion put on display by both guys during the match proved to be enough to make it a show-stealer, and I hope you'll enjoy here tonight in its entirety." ---- Showcase Match Thoughts: Frank Heimdal Showcase Match Thoughts: Frank Heimdal "Hello. My name is Frank Heimdal. I've been asked to speak at length in regards to Madhouse Title Cage Match where I decided to pit myself against Duel, A.K.A. Alex Lawson." Frank pulled his shirt cuff down awkwardly. "I'm not real comfortable discussing this for a lot of reasons. The motivation behind my actions wasn't what I'd call 'right' despite the fact that I did it, essentially, to stand up for my sister. From what I understand, this was THE match of my career. I worked stiff in the match, which I know isn't right, but I think it was justifiable. And finally…" Frank looked down at his knees, then back at the camera. "…I don't remember much of the match." Frank stopped for a moment and then went on. "I know that sounds really strange, but the circumstances that led up to that night were stressful to say the least. I was operating on almost no sleep, I was forgetting what day it was, I probably wasn't eating right, and a million other little things that are brought on when someone who you love very much stands to be hurt, and when you're obsessed with making someone pay for what they've done wrong." Frank looked nervous now. "Duel was doing some pretty bad shit. I was pissed. And I wanted him to pay. It was all-consuming. I didn't care about Alex's disease. I didn't care about myself. I just wanted it all to be done. Nobody else seemed all that interested in stopping things, and I guess I can understand that; how easy is it to stand up for yourself when you have your hands up like this," Frank demonstrated by putting his forearms up over his face like a boxer protecting himself, " saying 'Please don't kill me. I'll do whatever you want." Frank brought his hands down. "I knew it wasn't the Alex I knew doing what he was doing. That was the killer. I didn't want to believe he was capable of what he'd done. I wanted it to be an accident, a mistake, some playing around that had gone too far. But after a while, after putting up with more and more abuse, not doing anything because I wanted to make sure I wasn't going to do something rash or stupid, seeing Livvie miserable 24-7, seeing scared people everywhere I turned, every time I saw Rook and his hand…I stopped caring about the fact that Alex may have still been in there somewhere. "So, I wasn't doing this for myself, which is really the reason one should do anything like that. I look at the belt that I got out of it, and I'm disgusted with the whole situation all over again. It's a reminder of what quite possibly was one of the worst times of my life. I guess it's good that I was a zombie during the match. The next day, Livvie was telling me about it, and I must have said 'I did what?' twenty times. However, I'd like to develop that move where I pass out on my opponent some more. I think that'd be real effective in future matches and would be nothing but a boon to me in so many other ways..." Frank laughed mirthlessly. "So now, now Rook and Duel have this…Jesus, what could this possibly be called? Is it a feud? Not really. Maybe more like an ongoing playground fight where no one involved is going to win in the end and everyone involved goes home crying. They're so busy loving to hate each other that they really don't realize how fucking stupid they look. "It occurred to me a while ago why I approached Duel. I did it to see for myself how he'd changed, why he'd changed. And he's changed. And it appears that he changed for the right reasons. But not as much as I'd hoped. He's still caught up in the ego trip of it all, winning just for the sake of winning, and against someone he's crippled for life no less. Very compassionate, Lex. I have a feeling that, had he really TRIED, had he really WANTED to try, because he's the one who should have, he and Rook could have talked this through a long time ago. I have a feeling that, due to some unresolved issues or matches or what have you; both of them are still vying for that illusive win. It's like they're saying without saying 'Just one more, just one more so I can prove that I can one up you once and for all.' Or that's the impression I get from it. Not that my opinion seems to matter all that much these days. "As for Rook, I know that he needs to make up for the ways he was shafted as he was growing up, all the unfair shit he was subjected to over and over just because of the kind of man he is, the inner flagellation puts himself through on a daily basis. He's a great person. He has the potential to be a way better guy than I could ever hope to be. I know what he's capable of. And it's a LOT. But I see this, this, and I ask myself why I ever bothered with him in the first place. Not only that he's taken the easy way out, but that he's better at being a fucking shithead, no…he's not good at it at all. Rather, that's how he's chosen to act and he's more than okay with compromising all that he's learned to be, and, more importantly, how he's learned NOT to be, just to settle a grudge. Rook's the one who taught me to let go. And now he can't do that himself." "If I'm coming off as disgusted, it's because I am. I knew both of these guys very well. I loved how Alex treated my sister back in the day, and he's the best friend anyone could ask for. He's got a way about him that just makes you comfortable with how you are and it's so nice to NOT have to apologize for your idiosyncrasies because he knows that those are what make you who you are. He just accepts. Or I think he does. I think he might be there enough to be himself again. I haven't really talked to him all that much lately. "Rook kicks ass because he doesn't just let people in, he's picky. He's discerning. And he has enough respect for the people he lets in because he's so brutally honest and that is so fucking refreshing. And when you finally do befriend him, he's…" Frank stopped for a second, seemingly amazed at what occurred to him, "…he's the same way Alex is, come to think of it. They both know they're fucked in the head, they both know pain better than anyone else so they're good to have around because of this. And instead of comparing what they've been through and trying to diminish what's going on with you, they just look at you and nod, because they fucking get it. " Frank looked just a touch moved. He composed himself in a split second and went on. "So, here are two people that I hold…that I USED to hold in high regard acting like a couple of spoiled only children and I'm outraged." Frank chuckled again. "And this brings us to our challenge. I have challenged them both to a triple threat ladder match with the stupid fucking goddamn Madhouse Title on the line. Now, after all the preaching I've done, you may be asking yourselves why I'm doing this. And that's a legit question. And I'm more than happy to answer that. "It's because the only way I think I could possibly let them know how mad I am is if I beat the ever loving shit out of them. I've tried just about everything else. This is the last resort. AND, maybe if I get through this alive, which I really and truly doubt that I will because one's big, one's mean and they're BOTH better at this than I am, but if I do, the title will finally mean something to me. Even if I lose it. I did this; I did this for MY reasons, because I wanted to." Frank leaned back. He looked purged, relieved, finally at ease in front of the camera. "I used to get mad at myself; how could I possibly keep a promise to anyone if I can't keep a promise to myself? I'm getting better at that lately. I promised that I wouldn't let shit get ridiculous like it has in the past, but I've blown it yet again. It's gotten dramatic and crazy and over the top. So I'm fixing it." Frank crossed his arms across his chest and looked down. "Are we done?" A voice off camera said that they could be done. "Okay, good." ---- Showcase Match: Madhouse Title Cage Match: Duel (c) v Frank Heimdal Showcase Match: Madhouse Title Cage Match: Duel (c) v Frank Heimdal System of a Down's "ATWA" filled the first floor speakers as a shot of the Madhouse title appeared on the monitor wall. As Serg Tankanian sang, the Madhouse title was replaced by a close-up of Frank Heimdal's face on the left, Duel's face on the right, and in the background, lighter than the two men who would be competing in the match; there was a shot of Olivia Heimdal, the look on her face a mixture of fear and anger. The video package began with Impulse 1.1, where Frank Heimdal had inadvertently dumped Duel to the outside with a back body drop. It then cut to a shot of the closing moments of the Rook Black/Duel Evo Title match from Impulse 1.3, with Duel choking out the Texan to win the championship. This was followed by clips of the various confrontations the two had endured since then, including: their encounter as part of a six-man match on Impulse 1.4, their confrontation and Duel's challenge on Impulse 1.6, Frank dumping Duel over the front of the announce position on Impulse 1.7, Duel's attempt to get out of the Dawning match on 1.8, which was followed by a split screen of Frank's hitting Cranial Smoke on Lost and Duel tapping out in pain to Tommy G, and then, finally, the confrontation on Impulse 1.9, which had begun with Duel attempting to get out of this match and had ended with Duel attacking Frank with a crutch. As the vocals died down, the music continued to play and the steel cage was lowered into position. It wasn't one of the newer wire mesh versions but rather one of the old-school blue-steel cages that looked more like a set of prison bars descending than anything two men should've been wrestling in. Livvie Heimdal: Well here we are again. Legendary Joe Black: Hello friends and neighbors. It's time to journey back to LCW evo for more pain and suffering. LH: Knock that shit off, Joe. While the video package was playing, the ring crew had quickly assembled the steel cage that would hold the Madhouse Title match. Rather than the more current chain-link and aluminum version of the wrestling cage, this structure was a throwback to the older days of wrestling, its thick blue steel bars intersecting to create one foot by one foot squares. The thick walls of the cage began at the floor and reached nearly to the lighting rig that hung suspended above the ring, putting its height at around eighteen feet, with close to fifteen of that above the canvas. The crowd roared in anticipation of the match, but as the lights in the club died down and a familiar bass line began, their reaction quickly changed. Frank's black silhouette went into the door at the ground floor as seconds later, his assailant, another black silhouette, fell out the window and hit the ground, shattering into a million pieces. The most likely candidate for summing Frank's current mindset up in one word would've been determined, and everything about his walk to the ring, from the look in his eyes, to the staunchness of his posture, to the grim set of his mouth - it all served to indicate that he was prepared for this match, both mentally and physically. Frank backed away a step, but Duel had merely extended his hand for a handshake.
