H.Q. Hendrix Biography Nicknames His Ratings Highness Hometown Los Angeles, CA Birthday 8-17-1976 Height 5'11' Weight 220 Biography Look, I’m not different than any of the other people that go out in that ring every night to perform. I go in that ring, I do my stuff, and I wake up the next morning with a pain that is almost unimaginable. We do it because we love it. We love every aspect of this sport. Hell, we even love the pain. Even if I didn’t love this atmosphere though, I’ve got no way out. If I were to hang up my boots right now, wrestling would never be the same again. Ticket sales would drop, merchandise would stammer, and the fans would cheer my name during every event anyways. My name, as it happens to be, is H.Q. Hendrix. I’m a pro-wrestler...hell...I’m THE pro-wrestler. I’m the reason fans are fans. I’m what makes them watch the events every week, and do you know why? Because I’m the best. It’s just that simple. I’m not going to drone on about crap that has nothing to do with how I got into this sport. I see too many wrestlers digging up some bullshit about how their parents were murdered when they were babies and they grew up as orphans on the street...and hot damn...they got discovered in an alleyway by some local promoter. They want to bullshit themselves with stories like that, I could really care less. I didn’t have a hard life. I was born August 17th, 1976 in Los Angeles, California. My mother has told me about the scenario time and time again. The nurse cleaned me up, wrapped me in blanket and handed me to my mother. My mother knew even then that I was destined to be the greatest at whatever it was I chose to do with my life. My father worked full-time as a sports agent. He’s worked with many of the greats of every sport. I got to meet most of the guys he worked with too. It was like a dream come true for a kid like me. A kid who was as into sports as could be. The job brought in a lot of money, and many people will tell you I had a lot of things handed to me on a silver platter. I’m not going to argue. I was a spoiled little brat, I’ll admit it. But so what? Those are the breaks...some kids get to be spoiled, some don’t. I deserved everything I got anyway. I started playing football when I was 11 years old. By the time I was 14, I had high schools from all over the state begging me to come to their school and play for them. I ended up at Lawndale High. I was the greatest wide receiver to ever set foot in that school. I set many records, and I shattered many records. Records weren’t the only things to get shattered in my football career though. I excelled in school, and graduated with a 3.4 GPA. I had a my views set on a College close to home, so I turned down all the offers I got from other colleges until they came. They being UCLA. They offered me full scholarship provided I play football for them. I agreed, but I had one condition. I wanted to be on their wrestling team as well. One of my father’s clients happened to be Randy Savage. I met the guy once, and he explained the career to me and I started watching it on television. I immediately fell in love with it. UCLA’s athletic board was skeptical at first. I had never participated in any amateur-wrestling event in my life. But I told them: “anything I put my mind to, I will achieve and surpass.” With a smile, they shook my hand, and I was guaranteed my place in UCLA. Now, before you get on your high horse about the major differences between pro-wrestling and amateur wrestling, let me just say, I knew the differences. There are similarities as well however, and major wrestling promotions do look at sports backgrounds. Having wrestling in my sports background would bring a smile to the faces of Federation officials. The sport was fairly easy for me to learn. Some of you might struggle with it, but it was a lot like football for me. They both revolved around knowing what your opponent is going to do next...where he’s going to move, and knowing how you’re going to counter that particular movement. Strategy, it’s all about strategy. In football, I was doing great. First string my freshmen year, and it only got better. By junior year I was the big man on campus, people rolled out the red carpet for me. Nothing I hadn’t expected upon entering UCLA. I was also participating in matches by my junior year, and I won the majority of them. The coach wouldn’t admit it, but I was the best guy on the wrestling team. My senior year was when things went bad though. It seems like bad things always happen to great people. It happened during one of our football practices. We were running a play, and I had the ball. We were going full-speed, it was a Wednesday, and we always pushed full throttle on Wednesdays. It happened fast. I was going for the touchdown, and then I saw a guy coming at me. I went to jump over him, but my kneecap hit his helmet at a bizarre position and bent backwards. The doctor called in a spiral break. I don’t remember his exclamation of the break, after he had told me my football career was over, my ears automatically shut everything else out. There was only 3 months left of the school year, and I got to finish my education at UCLA. I wasn’t the main man on campus anymore. It’s hard to be much of anything when you’re hobbling around on crutches. After I graduated, I stayed with my parents until my leg was healed up. I asked my father to contact Randy for me, and my father did. I talked to Randy for a long time and explained to him my interest in becoming a wrestler. He gave me the number of a few trainers. When my leg was finished mending itself, I called some of the trainers. I ended up making my way into once of Dory Funk Jr.’s classes. My background in wrestling did help me excel, but I focused on combining a repertoire of technical moves with high-flying moves. I find it to be a deadly combination. Some of the well-known feds started scheduling me in dark matches. The fans loved my moves, I knew they would. They were stuck on every step I took in a match, and their jaws were always hanging at the degree of perfection that my execution of moves withheld. These federations told me I would need to slow down my ring-work though, so as to not outshine their other performers. I wasn’t going to water down my work for anyone. So, I’ve been wrestling in the indys. Here I am now though, enjoying my time in A!W. Watching fans in arena’s around the world gape at my awesomeness. God, I love being me...you would too.
