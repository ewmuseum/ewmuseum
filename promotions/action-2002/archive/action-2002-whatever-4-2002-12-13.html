Whatever 4 1st Mariner Arena — Baltimore, Maryland ---- WHATEVER! WHATEVER! Introduction. (It was that time again... Whatever. The scene flashed over from the Action Wrestling Signature Introduction to the Whatever startup video, which showed different wrestler’s shots for over two minutes, as “Breathe” by Nickelback played throughout it. The scene then switched, as Derik Shaw smiled, sitting behind an Action! Screen with the Whatever logo jumping randomly upon it. He sat in front of a black desk, with red writing that said “Whatever” across the side. “Breathe” then started to fade out, as Derik Shaw readjusted his tie, smirked, and then spoke.) Derik Shaw: Hello everyone and welcome to Whatever! I’m Derik Shaw... and for this show, we’ll see High Flyer in literal action against Action! Man! As well we have some reports on some following wrestlers, and the Match and Segment of the Week as always too. Our one-on-one interview with Coral Avalon, which, was meant to be this week... will now follow this coming week. Sadly, Coral’s been tied up in other matters at this time, so Whatever will have to do without him. Yay for that, dammit! This past week on Pressure Point proved to be another big event. LLB will battle Reed Young in a Write Off Match come ‘Players and Pawns’... and this coming week, we have a big six man Battle Royal for the rights to face Conrad Ramsey directly after! The Ringer, Sweet Aroma, Bobby Minio, Airborn Tyler, Max Danger and Neko Case. As well, a Number One Contendership Match is said to be booked for Pressure Point. Whomever it’s between, I’m not exactly sure yet... yet if Joey Malone has his way, I think Kent Anthason will be in the mix. He has to. With the addition of ‘Bodybreaker’ Alex Brody, and the giant return to the ring that saw High Flyer walk into the Bell Centre and take on Joey Malone for the Action Championship... things look to really get interesting now. I’ve also forgotten about the push that Noah Hawkins received during that show, when he took on Chris Fox. Despite the loss, this has to add heat to the fire with Invalid and Hawkins. That’s all for the news. After this we will bring you the Segment and Match of the week. If you’re new to watching Action Wrestling, the soul purpose of this is to highlight some of our best stuff for you to check out. And best, this stuff is. Stay with us. (“Breathe” plays again as the scene goes to a commercial.) ---- Segment of the Week: The Gift That Gives. Segment of the Week: The Gift That Gives. Starring: Simon Seaman (C). Within the confines of the Arena situated a familiar face. Simon Seaman, decked in a blue, pastel, leisure suit sat there in silence. He didn’t seem to be doing much at all. He just sat in his chair and stared at the wall for a good while. Sure, some could say that he was doing absolutely nothing at all... or was the rest of the world just doing too much? Nope. The former was correct. You could hear a pin drop in that locker room, though it’d probably disturb the beautiful silence that surrounded him. If you put that sound of silence on a CD, it wouldn’t top the Billboard charts, but it seemed to fit in this situation. ‘S2’ felt calm. A serene scene it was. Was he thinking about his life? Was he thinking about the future? How about potential opponents for that Championship he currently was in possession was? “All right. Here we go. This is going to be sweet.” He stated casually as he held the Carnage Title up in front of his face. Simon glared at it and ever so slowly lifted a toothbrush to his mouth. If this moment had a sponsor, it would be Colgate. “Thssis an ashummm... ashom...” Only a dentist could comprehend those words. Unfortunately, there wasn’t one in sight. So, he paused for a second and repeated himself. “This is awesome. Who else could have guessed that this Title wasn’t only for wearing and hitting people over the head with? I’ve got to write this down.” He set the brush aside and retrieved a pen and a pad of paper. “Use number two... mirror.” He proclaimed with glee until it was time to proceed. The Champion... of Carnage by the way... brushed in the most appropriate manner. Not back and forth, but in picture perfect circles. It was like ballet. A sad and mind numbingly boring ballet to the extreme. It is no wonder why he has that Title. Then, things got interesting. Things referred to in the previous sentence... basically... things. In the corner of the room sat a fairly large present, adorned with elaborate wrapping paper and a bow to match. “Whashtha...” Was all he could mutter. Simon dropped everything on the floor and walked around, attempting to find a place to spit the contents of his mouth into. In a rush, he swallowed it all down in an uncomfortable fashion instead. “Mmm. Toothpasty.” He said sarcastically. He made his way to the gift and picked it up. S2 shook it a little and then shook it again. “Do I dare?” Simon asked himself. Seconds later, the answer was as clear as crystal Pepsi. The tag attached to it stated it was meant for him. In addition to that, it was from anonymous. Definitely not suspicious in any way, shape, or form. “Oh, I double dare. Mark Sommers-Nickelodeon-game-show-style, baby.” Just to make sure, he threw the gift against the wall. A Seaman tradition if there ever was one. It was go time and Simon did not disappoint as he carefully opened the early Christmas gift. “Do not open till Pressure Point 41?” He stated with confusion. “Crazy Christmas gift, Pressure Point 41 is happening right now! Stop being so stupid... oh, you.” It was his mission to keep the ribbon and gift wrap in tact for the following holiday season, or die trying. He popped the top open and rummaged through the contents of the box. Chicken dance Elmo this gift was not. Simon was speechless as he caught a simple glimpse inside. Placing the cover back on, he did as best he could to restore the packaging to its original condition. He set it back where it was and then took a seat. “La la la... la la dee da...” He mumbled in order to distract himself from what he just saw. Did it work? In a union labor sort of way, yes. With the Carnage Belt in hand, he left the room to get himself some fresh air. “It always has to be a surprise. I asked for a bike when I was seven and I got a surprise. I asked for wall-to-wall shag carpeting in our bathroom when I was eleven and I got a surprise. Now this. I’m better off with gift certificates.” ---- Match of the Week: Action Championship. Match of the Week: Action Championship. These men traveled the world together, competing around the globe for numerous promotions. It seemed wherever the one went, the other was soon behind. Flyer and Malone first met in the summer of 2000. Joey was getting noticed through his tag team the Winds of Change with partner Daniel Phillips. High Flyer was one half of the current IWO World Tag Team Champions with partner Tony Davis. Being in the same division, you’re bound to run into one another, but shortly after the Winds of Change entered the IWO, both teams split and Flyer was put on the shelf. Following Flyer’s return, he and Malone hardly were in the ring together, and when they were, it was mostly together against an opposing force. Never had they had to square off against one another. But here, in Action!, all that would change. All their past between IWO and jOlt would never prepare them for the fight of their lives with one another. Their road trips and their partnership and the backstage friendship they held with one another wouldn’t matter for the next twenty minutes or however long this match will go until the WB kicks them off the air. The only thing that will matter is the World Heavyweight Strap. And that changes things considerably. A light snowfall fell over the entranceway as the tune to “It’s Beginning to look a lot like Christmas” by Johnny Mathis played. Shortly after the first verse, the track scratched and the lights went out completely. That’s when “Loco” by Coal Chamber started, a white blaze shooting up from the top of the entrance and re-illuminating the crowd in attendance. Standing in front of where the blaze was happened to be High Flyer, his right arm extended to the sky and his fingers in the shape of devil horns, or his index, pinky, and thumb fingers extended. He quickly made his walk down to the ring still holding his hand up, pointing to his sternum each way and slapping a few of the fans hands as he went. Sliding underneath the bottom rope, Flyer quickly sprung to his feet and raised his hands to each side of the ring once more with his devil horn posture, before turning his attention back towards the entrance and making the universal motion for the belt to go around his own waist. “WHAT!?!?” “Denial” by Sevendust rang out through the arena as the fans rose to their feet in cheers. Actually, a lot of them had already risen, so the ones who hadn’t quite yet did so now. That’s when another shot of pryo shot out from the back, and Joey Malone came out from the backstage area. He wore the belt around his waist, a respect to the tradition that was made throughout the history of professional wrestling, and simply made the universal belt sign around his own strap. Flyer smiled from the ring as Joey wandered his way down, each knowing why they were getting into what they were getting into. But not sure exactly what would happen. Malone climbed into the ring, never taking his gaze off of Flyer before they squared off in the center of the ring. They stared each other down, never casting an eye away as Malone unstrapped the hooks of his championship belt from around his waist. The referee was then handed the title, and he held it high up for the crowd in a time honored tradition to showcase that it was on the line. With beautiful camera work, we could see both men's stern gazes looking into one another, the World Heavyweight title the only thing between them. The bell sounded, and Flyer was the first one to make a move, however not offensively. He extended his hand to Malone in a sign of respect, sportsmanship, and without a doubt the most important factor above all else, their friendship. These next twenty minutes would be brutal without a doubt, but no matter the outcome, they'd still have their friendship between them. At least, that's what it was supposed to mean until Flyer's hand was slapped away by the current Action! Champion. Flyer looked down to his hand and narrowed his eyes, before sympathetically tilting his head to his side and looking up at the usually jovial Malone. He must have lost his smiles. Flyer extended his hand once again, still in disbelief from Malone's previous action, and awaited a response he can believe. And that's when Malone slapped the taste out of his mouth, sending Flyer stumbling backwards in shock and clutching at his jaw. Before long, Flyer turned around and reached up, locking the champion in a side headlock. Malone tried to shove Flyer's face away with his hands and pulled at his hair until the referee reprimanded him. Malone decided to push the challenger into the ropes, and then shot him off the other side. On the rebound, Malone reached over sideways and went for some sort of tilt-a-whirl maneuver, but being awfully quick, Flyer's able to go for his own move, an attempted tilt-a-whirl head scissor. Malone is just as quick though, reacted in mid-stream using his pure strength advantage to stop the head scissor takeover and keep the challenger dangling in his arms. He lifted the 200 pound lightweight onto his shoulder for what looked to be an electric drop. Malone stalled for a bit to try to grab his arms in the position for a Japanese Ocean Suplex and to make sure that Flyer was positioned correctly, his tray table locked in it's upright position and his seat belt fastened. Thing is, Flyer decided to throw a few elbows to Malone's skull to disorient him because he wasn't exactly happy with the stewardess' service. He never really cared for those early morning flights anyway. Flyer twisted his body around to try a victory roll into hurracanrana, but Malone was able to shove Flyer's legs off his shoulder just as Flyer flipped backwards for the 'rana motion. This let Flyer land on his feet and he stumbled a bit from the fall. He was still getting used to the ring and his agility since he hadn't had a professional match since July, and could count the amount of matches he's had all year on one hand. This gave Malone an opening, and a definite advantage. Joey charged with a clothesline, but Flyer saw him coming and ducked his head to the side. In an urangi position, Flyer tried to lift the champion up for his patented Lunatic Bomb. Malone shrugged it off with a couple of well placed elbows to the side of Flyer's face before following that up with a swift kick to the midsection. With Flyer's wind knocked out, Malone picked up the lunatic for a scoop slam, but was quickly reversed into a head scissor. Bested, Malone slid out underneath the bottom rope to regain himself. The fans let out a few cheers to the show they just witnessed as Malone clutched his forehead on the outside to regain himself. Malone walked around the corner of the ring and went to get in, but Flyer was there, sitting on the middle rope to open the ropes in a sign of respect. Malone looked up to Flyer before him, and simply slid back in. Test of strength to restart the series, which Flyer is hesitant to enter. He does, much to his dismay, as Flyer's arms are wrenched to the point that he’s is on his knees in front of the champion. Malone shrugged off the right side and started to focus on Flyer's left elbow, hammering it with a few of his own forearms before lifting the challenger up to his feet. That's when Malone gave Flyer a knife edge chop that would make Ric Flair smile, and caused the crowd in attendance to show their support to the nature boy with a 'Woo!' Flyer clutched his chest with his free hand and could feel the blood rising to his surface. He let out a groan and a visual look of displeasure as Malone swatted his second hand away and let loose with another chop that resounded out in the echoing arena. Malone went right back to the left elbow joint, hammering with two forearm shots before lifting the arm up and wrenching it back down. This motion sent Flyer to the canvas back first, and Malone desperately dropped down to try locking in a fujiwara arm bar. Flyer fought back though, tucking the arm closer to his shoulder so Malone didn't have the grasp to lock the hold in. Flyer then rolled onto his back to get away from Malone, but Joey just took this as an opportunity for a cover. One. Flyer quickly got his right shoulder up and continued rolling away, before knipping up to his feet. Malone regained himself and waited for Flyer to charge him, which he did. Malone went for a hip toss, but Flyer once again landed on his feet. Malone was waiting on this and attempted a swift boot to Flyer's midsection. This was caught by Flyer, who then decided to rake Malone's eyes while still holding onto his left leg. Flyer looked at Malone and gave him a sympathetic shrug before swiftly kicking Joey's left knee. He followed it up by another shot, and then dragon screw leg whipped Malone to the canvas. Malone grimaced as Flyer kept his hold on Malone's leg. Flyer slowly got to his feet and drug Malone up with him. A few more kicks to Malone's knee, and then another dragon screw, Malone was down on the canvas clutching his chronically injured knee and trying to fight showing his pain. Flyer shrugged off Malone's work on his left elbow before reaching down to pick the champion up to his feet. Flyer's chest burned red from Joey's chops, and Flyer had the urgent desire to pay him back. Malone limped to his feet before Flyer threw his arms away from his chest and chopped him across his pecs. Malone doubled over, dropping to his knees from the shot and the pain he felt in his joints. With Malone kneeling, Flyer tilted his head to his side before extending his hand and telling Malone to 'Rock on.' He did a little head bang before knife edge chopping Malone once again, this time to a groan from the champion. Malone fought through the pain to the delight of the crowd. He stood up on his own will power, and overhand chopped Flyer square in the chest. Flyer collapsed to his knees this time, his chest as bloodshot as a stoner's eyes. Malone grabbed Flyer's left arm and locked in a standing arm bar, having the definitive advantage with height and positioning. With his free arm, Malone let loose with another vile chop to Flyer's chest, causing his skin to rupture just a small bit. Flyer's face contorted with every drop of blood that escaped his chest that felt like it had been stabbed by a porcupine. His face twisted and mangled itself in pain for every moment that Malone held that arm bar in, and wrenched the arm even further in the wrong direction. Malone hooked the arm bar completely straight overtop his head, and then came down onto it with tomahawk chops. Each blow sent the lunatic down to his knees in pain. Flyer fired back with a couple of right hands to Malone’s gut, but Malone shrugged them off quickly. Joey lifted Flyer up by his injured arm and pushed him into the center of the ropes. With the bounce, Joey looked to send Flyer off to the other side, but Malone held on and used the velocity to wrench the elbow and shoulder joint on the rebound. Flyer slammed down to the mat face first and grimaced in pain, grabbing his left elbow with his free right hand. Malone sympathetically tilted his own head to the side, before raising his right boot to stomp on Flyer’s now exposed elbow joint. Flyer had other ideas in mind, as when Malone raised up his right boot for the stomp, he rolled to his side to free his right hand and downward chopped on Malone’s left knee. Malone caved quickly, landing on his back and went right to support the leg. Joey felt his knee burning, searing with every passing moment. And yet, he wasn’t going to get any rest. The lunatic got to his feet and stared down at his prey like a vulture. A couple of swift soccer kicks strike their intended target as Malone tries to block them with his hands to no avail. Malone cries in pain, before Flyer turns his back to Malone and hits a standing moonsault. On Malone’s legs. Flyer stayed on top for a cover but couldn’t hook the leg due to Malone’s work on his left elbow joint. One. Two. Malone got a shoulder up at the last moment. Flyer slammed his right hand onto the mat three times before looking up towards the referee with wide hopeful eyes. Receiving nothing but an indignant “TWO!” from the ref, Flyer turned his attention back to Malone. High Flyer lifted Joey up off the mat and threw him into the corner, which was all for well since Malone couldn’t exactly stand very well without support. Flyer measured Malone and delivered two side kicks to his mid section, before spinning his body and catching Malone with a leaping thrust kick that sent him almost toppling out of the ring. Flyer then drove his shoulder in Malone’s gut to make sure that he’d stay inside, and lifted him up onto the top turnbuckle. Flyer fired away with a chop to the throat, which sent Malone gasping for breath as Flyer set up Malone’s legs how he planned. In a shattered dreams position, Flyer turned to Malone and yelled “Sorry Joey,” before kicking him square in the left knee. Malone reached down and screamed in pain, as Flyer raised his hands in devils horns once again. Quickly, Flyer came back to the offensive, springing up to Malone’s shoulders in two fluid motions. Flyer twisted his body for a hurracanrana, but Malone still had his upper body strength and the capacity to catch the two hundred pound lightweight in his arms. Looking all around to the standing crowd, Malone tossed Flyer and jackknife powerbombed him off the top rope and to the center of the ring. Malone still sat perched on the top rope, and began to pull his legs back into the ring as to make his climb down effortless. Painless was another story, as each movement of his left leg sent sharp stabbing pain signals to his brain. At least in a no disqualification environment, the adrenaline takes over and you’re brain can’t think about the pain until it’s over. Here we’re not so lucky. “But while we’re here,” could be the only thing that went through Joey Malone’s mind, as he climbed all the way to the top rope, even with his bum knee. Staring out at the crowd around him that were on their feet, he extended his arms in wing like fashion before leaping off the top onto the prone High Flyer. ‘Bad Moon Rising’. But what was normally a head butt landed more to Flyer’s left elbow. Flyer cuddled up like a small child in his mother’s womb, crying out in pain and desperately trying to support his elbow. Malone did the same, clutching his left knee which took impact on the fall from the swan dive. Not a smart idea Joey. Not a smart idea at all. Both men rolled around in pain as if they were on fire, following the video tape’s clear instructions on what one should do when caught ablaze. Thing was, the referee didn’t care if they were burning alive or having a seizure, it was his job to make the count, and that’s exactly what he did for every moment both men were down. Flyer was the first man to move, but not quickly. He was the man more likely to get to his feet since his knee, while chronically injured, has had the last nine months off to repair itself. It’s also not been the focus point of a full on assault by the Action! World Heavyweight Champion. Joey wasn’t so lucky, as he heard the referee’s count break at five. Realizing he was still just on his knees and that his opponent must be the one up, he quickly rolled forward, just barely dogging a kick that was aimed at his left knee. He groaned as he kneeled while twisting his body back around to face Flyer in a sitting position. Joey took this opportunity to charge Flyer, looking for a flying forearm. Flyer however was able to duck underneath and hook Malone up, tilt-a-whirling him up into a tombstone position. Malone tried to fight out of the hold, shoving his body away from Flyer’s and looking to turn it into a head scissor. Thing was, Malone didn’t have the strength in his legs to take Flyer over, and this left him caught in Flyer’s arms. Flyer looked out to the crowd before yelling “Come on baby!” He leapt off the canvas and drove Malone to the canvas with a tilt-a-whirl inverted DDT bomb. He stayed on top for the cover. One. Two. Malone barely got a shoulder up at the last moment, as Flyer hit the mat in frustration with his bad left arm. Immediately he grabbed his elbow in pain, but this may have actually been a reminder more than anything else. Flyer was quick to his feet and looked to focus back on Malone’s injured left knee. He lifted Joey up off the canvas by his left leg, and when he was standing, quickly dragon screwed him back down to the canvas. Flyer held onto the hold, picking Malone once again off the mat just like before, and delivered a few swift kicks to Malone’s left leg. Malone let out a cry of pain, and Flyer shook his head in sorrow. It was at this point where Malone enzeguried Flyer with his good right leg. Flyer landed face first to the canvas like a ton of bricks, holding the back of his head and slamming his leg into the mat. Malone wasn’t in any better of a situation, clutching his knee in pain while desperately trying to get to his feet. He was using the ropes behind him as a crutch, climbing up the ladder to a standing position. Malone limped over to the fallen Flyer and picked him up to his feet. Once there, Malone let loose a knife edge chop that sent Flyer flying into the nearest corner. Flyer clutched his left arm to his chest, supporting both pains at once, as Malone took this as a way to kick Flyer’s elbow joint. Flyer remained standing by holding the top rope with his left hand even though he practically fell forward and was just a foot away from being face first on the canvas. Malone threw Flyer’s arms away once more and delivered another knife edge chop that made the crowd chant “Woo!” and Flyer to cry out in pain. Malone turned around and charged Flyer, who took left his leg and kicked the charging Malone in his face. Malone stumbled around, as Flyer waited for him to return to the corner. When he did, Flyer hooked his head in a ¾ face lock, turning his back to the ring. With his feet still on the second rope, Flyer climbed the ropes to the top and then backfliped over Malone, attempting to nail the move more commonly referred to as Sliced Bread #2, a moonsaulted acid drop. Malone shoved Flyer off on his descent, which caused Malone to reach to the corner for support. Flyer was also able to land on his feet, and quickly charged the recovering Malone. Flyer leapt up, attempting what could only be described as a monkey flip, but Malone used his upper body strength and positioning to simply lifting Flyer up and tossed him over the top rope. Malone fell to the mat trying to work the kinks out of his left leg, as the referee reprimanded him. On the outside, Flyer was down clutching his left elbow, as a reply showed that Flyer had landed elbow first on the floor. Malone quickly rolled out of the ring, and shook his left leg cancel out the pain. This is where Flyer chopped Malone in the knee once again, and then slammed Malone’s face into the apron. Flyer quickly rolled into the ring, and looked down at the fallen Malone. Before long, Flyer sprung up to the top rope and looked for a shooting star piscada, but Malone was smart enough to roll into the ring, and Flyer hit nothing but concrete. Malone quickly rolled back out of the ring, never getting to his feet and just rolling in before to both break up the count and avoid the piscada. Malone reached down and picked Flyer up by his hair before throwing him back into the ring. Malone followed suit, quickly using the ropes to return to his feet as Flyer slowly backed up, clutching his left arm close to his blood red chest. Malone limped over to the fallen Flyer, who decided to rack Malone’s eyes once more. Malone blindly limped around, as Flyer charged forward for a hurracanrana. Once on Malone’s shoulders, Joey realized what was going on, and threw Flyer off forward in a flapjack position and hooked Flyer’s head in a headlock on the way down. ‘Arizona Death Drop’. And Flyer bounced off the ring from the impact, rolling all the way to the ropes. He showed great veteran instinct to make sure that he was close enough that he didn’t have to kick out, or maybe he was just incredibly lucky. No matter the case, Joey crawled over and draped an arm onto Flyer’s shoulder. One. Two. Foot on the bottom rope. Malone groaned before pulling Flyer away from the ropes and closer to the center of the ring. He was on top again looking for the pin. One. Two. Flyer barely got a shoulder up, and Malone couldn’t believe it. Instead of jawing with the referee though, Malone simply leaned forward and hooked Flyer’s legs for another attempt. One. Two. And another kickout just barely at the count of two and four fifths. Malone let out a sigh before lifting the fallen Flyer up to a sitting position and locking in an arm lock, focusing pressure on Flyer’s left elbow. Flyer tried to roll through but only let Malone lock the arm into an arm wringer instead, which sent even more pressure on Flyer’s arm. Flyer got to his feet, and Malone tried to trip Flyer back down to the canvas for a fujiwara, but Flyer kept leaping up off the mat with ever sweep. On the third try, Flyer leap up and front rolled, mule kicking Malone square in his left knee. Malone immediately broke the hold and crumpled to the mat face first. Flyer landed on his back and began to pull himself up with his good right arm. Quickly, he sprung to the top rope and turned himself around to see Joey Malone lying in the center of the ring. Flyer looked from side to side before leaping. Five and a half star frog splash, directly on Joey Malone’s injured knee. Flyer tumbled forward and clutched his left elbow in pain. It seemed to be a common occurrence, these two men risking their own injuries to further hurt their opponent. Sometimes you wonder if it’s truly worth it. But then you remember the Action! Heavyweight Championship is on the line. Flyer slowly turned back around and dives on top of Malone. He can’t quite hook the leg though due to his injury, as the referee began his count. One. Two. Malone barely got a shoulder up. Flyer lowered his head and raised his eyebrows at the referee, before swinging his bright green hair out of his face. Flyer grabbed Malone by his hair and lifted him to his feet, much to the referee’s dismay. Malone hobbled next to Flyer, still dazed from the pain in his knee as Flyer immediately double under hooked him to his side. Flyer let out a cry of “OH YEAH!” before trying to lift Malone up off the mat for what couldn’t only be presumed as Hypothermia, his double underhook brain buster. Thing is, Flyer’s left arm gave out and Malone twisted out of the underhook and landed to the right of Flyer. Immediately, Malone went behind Flyer and placed his head underneath Flyer’s arm pit with a waist lock. Malone tried to lift Flyer up twice but Flyer blocked it each time, before Malone caught Flyer with a shot to his left elbow. Flyer forgot all about blocking the move, and Malone used this to his advantage, German suplexing High Flyer down to the mat. Flyer landed with a thud and relief that the pain would be over, but that wouldn’t be the case. He looked down and saw that Malone’s hands were still hooked, and let out a sigh as he was picked back up off the mat. Another German from Malone and Flyer landed hard on the back of his neck, immediately using his healthy hand to check the damage. Malone wasn’t finished, still locking his hands and delivered the third stop on his locomotive German Suplex Train. He couldn’t hold on for the bridge though. He tried to keep his hands locked and bridge for the three count, but Flyer’s work on his knee made Malone unable to bridge anything, and they both collapsed on the canvas. Malone favored his leg still as Flyer did the same with his elbow, and his most recent concussion from the locomotive suplexes. It may not be a concussion in actuality, but you try telling that to Flyer at this stage of the match up? I didn’t think so. Neither man moved much except for Flyer throwing his leg up into the air and slamming it down into the mat repeatedly. Whether he knew he was doing this was truly anyone’s guess. At the count of six, Malone began to stir. He hobbled to his feet, breaking the count at eight. Flyer wasn’t so lucky, as when he began to stir; Malone already grasped him by his hair and pulled at the roots. Flyer was pulled to his feet, and Malone swatted his arms away from his chest once more before delivering another shot of Canadian Violence to Flyer’s already blood red chest. Flyer let out a cry, crossing his arms across his chest to brace the pain. Malone reared back for one more chop, but wound up hitting nothing but bouncing into the top rope. When he realized that Flyer had ducked and was now in the center of the ring, it was too late. A Superkick delivered square in the jaw, sending Malone over the top rope and landing on the apron. Flyer turned around to the crowd, raising his hands once again in devil horns before he charged towards the now recovered Malone. Malone ducked a flying forearm, and then proceeded to rake Flyer’s eyes. Flyer stumbled around blindly, as Malone sprang up to the top rope. Thing was, his knee gave out, effectively crotching himself in the process of trying for a missile dropkick. Flyer rubbed his eyes one final time before turning his attention back to Malone. He tilted his head to his side, before rubbing his eyes once again. Was this for real? This was better than Christmas! Flyer walked over and Malone fired back with a right hand. Malone went for another, but it was blocked, and Flyer choose to slap the taste out of Malone’s mouth to the shock of the audience. Flyer raised his hands in his devil horn taunt once again before turning back to Malone, whose face was a blushed inferno. Malone knife edge chopped the challenger once again, which sent Flyer down to his knees. Malone then hooked Flyer’s head in a side headlock, and used the ropes to twist his body into what would have been a Tornado DDT. Flyer shoved him off his body, causing Malone to land awkwardly on his feet. Malone fell to his knees, and Flyer immediately took advantage of this. Flyer grabbed Malone by his throat, picking him up and driving him into the mat with an elevated choke DDT. Better known as ‘Cold Snow’. Flyer rolled Malone over and immediately went for the cover. Weakly, he grabbed Malone by the tights to hook his leg. One. Two. Thr- Malone barely got a shoulder up, much to the dismay of Flyer and to the shock of the crowd. Flyer slapped the canvas three times with his right hand and then showed the devil horns, three fingers, once again to the referee, who waved his hands in a cross before yelling “two!” Flyer stood to his feet before lightly pulling on Malone’s hair to get him to a standing position. As Malone struggled to his feet, Flyer slapped Malone once again across the face, which only proceeded to anger Malone even more than he already was. To motivate him to the point of an explosion. Malone rose to his feet and within mere moments, High Flyer was on top of Joey Malone’s shoulders, and Malone was looking to hit the ‘Malonestrom’. Malone tossed Flyer off his back for the michinoku driver part of the maneuver, but Flyer reversed the move into a sit down ace crusher. Malone bounced off the mat, clutching his chin in pain as his blood slowly began to boil. Flyer immediately rolled out of the ring to avoid Malone’s outrage, as Malone charged and reached over the ring and to the outside. Malone barely grasped Flyer by his hair, pulling the reluctant and wide eyed Flyer back up onto the apron. Flyer tried for a right hand, but it was blocked, and Malone let loose with another vicious knife edge chop that sent Flyer almost entirely off the apron. Malone hooked the dazed Flyer in a side headlock before trying for a vertical suplex inside the ring. Flyer landed on his feet behind Malone, and kneed Malone in the back. Flyer hooked Malone in an inverted headlock, raising his free right hand in the air in devil horns once again. Flyer went for the inverted face cut, but Malone shoved him off. Flyer used this velocity and came off the ropes, returning and ducking underneath a clothesline. Flyer however used this to grab hold of Malone’s arms, and toss him off the other side. Malone hobbled off the return, before Flyer dropped down and rolled through into a Boston crab. Flyer was going for the ‘Peaceful Slumber’, and with the work he had done on Joey Malone’s knee, there’s no way Malone could fight this hold forever. He had to fight it now. Malone tried to reach out to the bottom rope, while fighting Flyer’s valiant attempts to lock Malone’s skull with his shin. This was the same style maneuver Kent Anthason made Joey Malone tap out to just a month ago. Would history repeat itself? Malone desperately outreached to the bottom rope, the pain welling up in his eyes. Every moment that passed was excruciating for Joey. Just imagine having your knee bent into an uncomfortable position for a long period of time. And then now imagine that the knee should have had surgery long ago. Now imagine that the knee feels as if Jell-O is the only thing holding the bones together, and that’s the feeling Joey currently has. Malone took one last dive to the bottom rope, but Flyer was smart enough to pull Malone closer to the center of the ring. The fans let out a gasp as Flyer leaned back even further. He may not be able to lock Peaceful Slumber in fully, but God dammit was he going to use what he had to the best of his ability, and maybe, just maybe Joey’s hands would hit the mat three times. Joey began to flail his legs, whether in spastic pain or in an actual attempt to get out of the hold will never be known. Whatever the case, this caused Flyer’s left elbow to wrench even further, and Flyer broke the hold on that side, turning the move simply into a single legged crab. This Joey could reverse, and after a few moments, Malone had pushed his body up and tucked his head and shoulders underneath his legs to roll through and break the hold. Big mistake. Flyer dropped down on Malone’s chest, keeping Joey’s left leg hooked and wedging him on the mat like an accordion. One. Two. Thr-NO! Malone was able to free his right shoulder by kicking out his free good leg. Had Flyer only hooked Malone’s good leg and leg Malone to flail about with his left leg in the air, we could have very well had a new Action Champion. Flyer muttered to himself, saying the word “Almost” three times before he looked over to the top rope. His face lit up, a smile a mile wide. This was his chance. Joey Malone was down on the mat, desperately clutching his knee and screaming like a child, and Flyer was up on his feet, ready to fly. He climbed up to the top rope quickly, looking between his legs at the still prone Malone on the mat. That’s when he looked to the standing audience, and leapt off the top rope. ‘Flying Moon Shot’. Missed its mark. Flyer landed hard on the canvas as Malone had rolled out of the way just in time. Malone slowly climbed to his feet using the ropes, as Flyer stumbled to his feet, still dazed from the missed blow. Flyer wobbled for a bit, before walking right into Malone. And that’s when Malone lifted Flyer up into a fireman’s carry position to the cheers of the crowd. Some were deflated; they knew that the match was over. Malone had Flyer in position. But that’s when Joey’s knee gave out. Joey landed hard on the canvas, dropping the challenger out of the hold. Flyer landed on his feet, a little stunned from what just happened but eager to take advantage of Malone’s missed opportunity. Malone limped back toward Flyer, who was waiting, and hooked the recovered Malone in an Urangi position. Flyer tried to lift Malone off the canvas, but Malone wasn’t buying it. He may have lost his shot at the ‘Malonestrom’, but he wasn’t going to get hit with the Lunatic Bomb if he could help it. Flyer desperately tried one more lift and toss, but this just played into Malone’s hands. Just as Flyer went with the upward motion, Malone ducked his head underneath Flyer’s mid-section and lifted him up to his feet. Malone didn’t showboat, quickly tossing Flyer down in the center of the ring. ‘Malonestrom’. Center of the ring. Malone landed with Flyer in front of him, and hooked his legs, wrapping the lunatic into a small little ball. And the count was academic. One. Two. Three. And with the final slam of the hand down on the mat, High Flyer’s dream of becoming Champion left the field of reality and once again returned to fantasy.
