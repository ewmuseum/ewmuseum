JordanX Shabazz Biography Nicknames The Black superman, the superblackman, The Racist, the Extremist., Jordan X Shabazz Hometown Albany, New York Birthday March 8, 1976 Height 5'11 Weight 228 Fed Titles None as of yet Fed Awards Advanced to the quarter finals of the first annual King of the Submissions tournament. Biography The man who goes by the name of Jordan X Shabazz was actually born Jordan Andrew Copeland. his middle name is taken from his older brother Andrew Copeland, who wrestled in the OSW under the name of Drew Hostyle. Jordan was born in Brooklyn, New York, on March 8th, 1976. Jordan was born to Travis Copeland and Stephanie Copeland. Travis was a truck driver by trade, and Stephanie was a stay at home mother who was the person that took care of Jordan and his older brother, Andrew. Travis was on the road a lot, and he didn’t usually have enough money so Jordan, Andrew, and their mother, Stephanie, could live happily. So Travis took to any means that he possibly could to put money on the table; he became a drug dealer. Being the son of a drug dealer is never easy, as Jordan and Andrew found out. At constant times of the night people would bang on the door, and during the day people would bang on the door. One particularly scary incident for Jordan was when he was home alone while his mother, father, and brother, had left to do the grocery shopping, a cocaine addict burst through the door and held a knife to this throat, asking him where the drugs were. When Jordan told him that he didn’t know, the cocaine addict threw Jordan into the wall and disappeared into the muggy Brooklyn afternoon. At this time, Jordan was aged 7. After a few weeks of his father selling drugs, his mother began to think that the drug life was way too dangerous for her liking. So she went out to try and make money for the family while trying to convince Travis, who by now was obsessed with the adrenaline rush of selling the dope, to come clean and go back to truck driving. She became a waitress. She worked hard, nights and afternoons, while the kids took care of themselves at home. Andrew was only 9 when he was cooking, cleaning, and babysitting his little brother. One day, while Andrew was in the kitchen making some macaroni and cheese for Jordan and himself, the door barged open. Only this time it wasn’t a drug dealer. Oh no, instead it was social workers accompanied with police officers. They snatched Jordan and Andrew up and took them to the Children’s Aid Society. Both were put in separate homes. Both never saw their parents alive again. Jordan was moved to a white family in another country: Toronto, Ontario Canada. New York’s Children Aids Society was very close to Toronto’s in terms of business, not in terms of geography. They realized that there was not enough homes in the state of New York for Jordan to move to. Jordan lived in a predominately white neighborhood of Toronto called ‘Scarborough.’ It was here that he lived with his two foster parents, Sarah Cunningham, and David Cunningham. They were very nice people who both worked hard. Sarah as a nurse, and David worked as head of construction at a Nestle Chocolate factory warehouse. While they were at work, Jordan spent his time inside with the babysitter, Maria, watching soap operas for lack of a better thing to do. But when Jordan discovered that he was allowed to go outside, that’s where he went. He spent everyday he could outside riding a bicycle that Sarah and David had bought him on his birthday. When Jordan began to interact with other children, he was made fun of because he lived in a majority white area of upper middle class Canada. Several comments were made towards Jordan, all racial, for the simple fact that the his ’parents’ where white, and he was black. Some people called him “Monkey Boy,” and “Darkie” and even the dreaded ’N’ word. These things made Jordan depressed and often caused him to cry and become very angry at the world. Angry that he was taken away from his parents home and forced to live without his older brother, and live with white people. With all this anger, Jordan began to focus on school. He went to the elementary school called ‘Queen Victoria’, and he focused hard on excelling. He spent hours and hours everyday after school not watching television, but doing homework, and EXTRA work on top of that, just so he could impress his teachers and stand out. While in school, Jordan did his best to avoid contact with everybody that he could, and he did it well. When Jordan entered “Birchmount” High School in Scarborough, he was still very much a loner. He took all advanced classes, and got good grades. He coasted through high school, and was even selected to be valedictorian, but he denied it. He didn’t want to go up and make a speech in front of all the people he had grown to hate, not because they had tormented him, but because they were white. The white people were the reason he was in Canada in the first place, the white people were the reason that he hadn’t seen his parents in more than six years, the white people were the reason that he probably would never see his older brother again. When Jordan graduated, Sarah and David sent him to the University of Toronto, where Jordan studied Broadcast Journalism in the hopes that he could someday get into radio, perhaps working behind the scenes. University was easy for Jordan, he had a few friends, but nothing to write home to mom about. After four years of hard work and studying, Jordan graduated the University of Toronto with his degree in broadcast journalism, and managing to make it onto the Dean’s List. Immediately after leaving University, Jordan moved out of the dorm that he slept in, and went into his own one bedroom apartment. He was still being funded by his foster parents, who he had grown to be rather fond of, but couldn’t stand living with. With Jordan now living be himself, he applied for an intern position at a local radio station called Kiss 92.5 FM (www.kiss92.fm) and began working there. He enjoyed the job, he worked overnights and kept to himself for the most part. After spending a few months there, Jordan realized he wasn't liked by some of the people working there. After getting into an altercation with one of the DJs Jordan physically assaulted him. They tried Jordan for assault with a deadly weapon. That's where he met his only white friend today, Jake Jackson. Jordan was facing a life sentence in prison, but he convinced his manager that it was Self-Defense, and Jake Jackson, used his brain, and he made sure Jordan got off by paying some of the jury members. Jordan walked out of court a free man, and that began the bond that those two men share to this very day. After getting out of court, and starting over again with a clean slate, Jordan decided to move back to New York. This time not in Brooklyn, but in Queens. On a chance encounter, Jordan saw his brother, Andrew, for the first time in 15 years while walking the streets of Queens. The two shared a long catching up conversation, and then Jordan asked what Andrew was doing for a living. Andrew told him that he was now a professional wrestler, working the local Indy circuits, and being scouted by some major federations. He encouraged Jordan, who was rather athletic at this point from working out in prison, to join the profession. Jordan had watched wrestling on television before, but he wasn’t really enamored with it. But after seeing that his brother had at least enough money to feed himself without the support of his foster parents, he decided to get into the game. Jordan used the money that his foster parents gave him monthly, to pay a wrestling trainer named Johnny Rodz, who had wrestled in the WWF(E) under the guise of “Unpredictable” Johnny Rodz. Months and months of hard work with the hard trainer paid off for Jordan. The cold, grimy mats prepared him for the future. After a year of solid training and saving money from his foster parents, Jordan graduated Johnny Rodz’ wrestling school, and he went out on the Indy Circuit, teaming with his older brother under the tag team name of “The Original Brooklyn Gangstas,” or the OBG’s for short. After wrestling with his brother for a few months, Andrew went to a wrestling academy called the PWA, The Primetime Wrestling Academy. It was a place where wrestlers who were already trained, trained some more to become better and eventually went to one of many ‘big’ federations. Primetime Championship Wrestling (PCW) , New True Intense Championship Wrestling (NTICW), and/or Old School Wrestling (OSW). Jordan saw a federation on television called PCW, one of the ‘big‘ three. They had applications open and were accepting tapes from all over the planet for wrestlers who wanted to wrestle for the promotion. Jordan knew that big money was involved, and he immediately sent a tape in of himself. They reviewed the tape, and told Jordan to head down the academy where he experienced harsh training from some of the already established stars. After spending a month and a half in the Academy, Jordan got promoted to the NTICW. The NTICW was changed at this time from a ‘big’ federation to a ‘feeder’ federation. It was the ‘in between’ between the minor leagues of PWA, and the big leagues of OSW and PCW. In the NTICW, Jordan started off as a nobody, he wasn't known, and he didn’t have much character depth. But one day, Jordan was sitting at home watching MTV and watching a rap video. He couldn’t help but think that his people were being exploited. MTV was portraying African Americans as if they were all ’pimps’ who slapped ’ho’s’ and sold drugs. He hated this. He was an educated person who was working hard making money and trying to survive. Jordan called all the local Civil Rights offices that he could while he lived in Queens, and he soon found one in near by Albany, New York. That’s where Jordan met the Reverend Jesse Kintei. Jesse convinced Jordan that if he became a Civil Rights Activist while wrestling, he could spread the word that to all African Americans across the world to get educated, and to not do drugs or get in trouble with the law. Jordan did it. He became a Civil Rights Activist while a wrestler in the NTICW. Then, one day, he was listening to his radio, and he heard The Phantom Booker on PTC Malice. Jordan called in, and the spent an hour on the show. Jordan screamed, ranted, and raved, then he used his radio show popularity to ride his way through the NTICW. Jordan is a regular guest of the Phantom Booker and Brain Chapman on the PTC's Sunday radio show. This was the beginning of Jordan’s popularity as a character and professional wrestler. While in the NTICW, Jordan feuded originally with Mark Makonie, a neo nazi. The two men engaged in an intense feud which included such gimmick matches as a Billy Club match, a no DQ match, and burn the flag match. The latter match was the last match Jordan ever had in the NTICW, he was defending his Extreme Championship against Makonie. In a brutal 30 minute classic, Jordan prevailed, defeating Mark Makonie for a second time, while losing zero times to Makonie. That ended their feud, and Jordan's career in the NTICW. A few weeks later, Jordan called into the PTC Malice show like normal, and he begged Xavier Kannon (PCW commissioner at the time) for a job on air. He couldn’t stand the low payoffs of NTICW any longer, he had stopped accepting money from his foster parents, and his bigger brother Andrew, was now wrestling as Drew Hostyle in OSW, making big money. Jordan smothered Xavier with compliments, and after making Jordan look like an ass. He was given a chance to become Xavier Kannon's lackey. Jordan was supposed to lick somebody's ass for the PCW contract as something to amuse Xavier. Jordan was willing to do it, until he realized that the person who's ass he was supposed to kiss was Neo Nazi, and black man hater, Mark Makonie. Jordan immediately refused, which was going to induce a beating he wouldn't soon forget...But, thankfully, Duke Williams came out to make the save. After Duke and Jordan cleaned house, Duke offered Jordan a chance to become a member of the Malice Crew, Jordan immediately accepted and joined Duke Williams, Rush Mason, Jason Core, and Legend Inc. in that stable. At this time, Jordan suppressed his feelings of hatred for the white man just so he could get a check bi-weekly, and be on national TV. Jordan eventually startedthe Negroes International Growing Group Association (N.I.G.G.A), in May of 2001. It was exactly like the NAACP, except under a different name, and Jordan didn't want to work with white people or any other race, for that matter. He just wanted his race, African Americans, to rule the United States. Jordan even convinced “The Reverend” Jesse Kintei, to serve as his Vice President. After months of financial turbulence, it closed, sadly. “The Reverend” was without a job, and he turned to Jordan for help. Jordan made Jesse his permanent manager. Jesse was a fan of wrestling, and did this well. Jordan X Shabazz was the leader of an all black stable in PCW called 'The Uprising', but they broke up after Rush Mason turned his back on Jordan, and Thug RoC left PCW after financial squabbles with the owners. Jordan was set to debut in Old School Wrestling, before he had a falling out with the owner, Jane Reagan. She had him fired on a radio show that he wasn’t even listening to. Jordan then left the sport of professional wrestling, living at him, not doing anything. Jordan briefly wrestled for ACW before leaving when he realized his heart wasn't in wrestling anymore. That didn't continue for long. “The Reverend” Jesse Kintei was without work too, and he needed to find one. When he saw applications for SE open, he sent them one of Jordan’s tapes, they were impressed, and signed the Civil Rights Extremist to a contract. After two uneventful months in SE, which included two lackluster feuds, Jordan X Shabazz left the promotion after the promotion demand that he wrestle on at least three of it's house shows a week and still appear at Anarchy. After that, Jordan X Shabazz actively sent out his highlight reel video tapes to a few feds, one of them was Action!, luckily for the Civil Rights Activist, Reed Young was impressed by him, and Jordan immediately signed the contract, bringing him to Action! wrestling.
