Starring: Conrad Ramsey, Max Danger & Two Special Guests Writers: Michael D. End opening credits. Cue SR-71's "Tomorrow". Enter Conrad Ramsey. The fans jumped onto their feet. The former Bantam Champion looked somber. As a man in his position should. He walked out and lifted a hand to the crowd. Letting them know he appreciated their cheers. Conrad then headed down the ramp. The reaction was just tremendous. Not a single individual sat in their seat. Even that wheel-chair bound kid was on his father's shoulders. The smarks, notorious for their hating of the old man, stood. Applauding. "CON-RAD! CON-RAD!" filled the arena. Ramsey walked up the ring steps and stopped on the apron. He looked out at the fans. He was getting emotional. Tears? No. He could no longer bring forth those. The events of the past few weeks had drained him of that ability. The cheers continued as Conrad stepped through the ropes. Pat Hoskins had gone over and handed him a microphone. Conrad stepped to the center of the ring and looked back at the sold out building. Without speaking into the microphone he said, "Thank you." He held up another hand, thanking them for the response. "CON-RAD! CON-RAD!" grew louder. The Cumberland County Civic Center had better hope they have insurance for the roof, because it was about to be blown off. Ramsey then lifted the microphone to his mouth, "Thank you." They weren't going to quiet for a while. Held said it again, "Thank you." All in hopes of getting them to calm down so that he could address them. But they just kept cheering. "Thank you," he said again. "Please, this isn't necessary." It was all very touching. After a few more minutes, the crowd finally began to die down. Conrad said it one final time, "Thank you." He then lowered his head for a second, trying to re-gather his thoughts. He took a deep breath and admitted, "I really don't know what to say." He had a slight chuckle. "I asked for this time and now... well, I don't know if I can do what I intended." He shook his head slightly, upset at himself. The fans, however, chanted, "CON-RAD! CON-RAD!" All in hopes of getting his spirits back up. Postive motivation, as it were. Conrad smiled. And he mouthed, "Thank you." That's gotta be a record. As the crowd quieted back down, Conrad took another deep breath. "As you all know," he started. "The world lost a very special person three weeks ago. A person so full of love, so full of life." He shook his head, "Someone I loved more than you could possibly imagine." Emotions were running high again. The crowd then started another chant. A new one. "SA-MAN-THA! SA-MAN-THA!" Conrad looked out at them, chanting his late wife's name. Tears were beginning to well up in his eyes. The first tears since the day of her death. He was touched. Overwhelmed even. He wasn't going to thank them. It wasn't going to be enough. He just watched. Listened. Was moved. Slowly it started to calm down again. Very slowly. "Words cannot describe how that makes me feel," he told them. He then took another deep breath and pushed back the tears. "There is a reason I'm here. And it's something that's really been a long time coming," he said. The crowd began to murmur. Spontaneously they began chanting, "DON'T GO! DON'T GO!" Retirement? Ramsey shook his head with a slight smile, "Oh, no, I'm not going anywhere." The roars of the audience echoed off the walls of the Civic Center. Once they settled, Conrad continued, "There is someone here tonight, that I have some beef with. Someone who I've had problems with for quite a long while. I think you all know who that is." The smarks instantly started to chant, "DAN-GER! DAN-GER!" Conrad nodded, "That would be him. So, if Max would be so kind as to make his presence known..." He was only a short drive from his hometown in Augusta. The Carnage Champion made his way through the curtains. The smarks cheered loud but were having a bit of a war with the marks. But don't mistake all the love. There were still several people booing. The Danger Man had left his title belt in the back. Jessica, too, had not come out with him. This was between two men. Two men connected through one thing. Two men who had a long standing rivalry. One that had seemingly ended at Juggernaut II when the King of Submission beat Conrad. Max walked up the ring steps and onto the apron. He eyed the former Bantam Champion and then entered the ring. He walked around the ring and took a microphone from Pat Hoskins. He then turned and looked upon his stepfather, though he would never call Conrad as such, he still, technically was. Conrad spoke to Max, "Thank you, Max, for coming out here." Danger just nodded. "So much has happened. So much hate between the two of us. So much pain dished out," he continued. Max chimed in with a slight smile, "Though I dished out a bit more than you did." Because Duel ducked the attempt, hooked the waist, and proceeded to fling the young Kleptomaniac backwards with such height and velocity that Avalon landed brutally on the back of his head and neck, with a lot of his body weight landing in such a way that Avalon quite simply was folded up like an accordian. The fans gasped in shock, and Duel quickly crawled over and laid all two hundred and eighty-six pounds of body weight on top of the Kleptomaniac. One. Two. Shoulder. Duel blinked. Duel had been aware of the fact that Coral Avalon was a tad resiliant, but this was a kid that was coming off of a concussion, and he was still coming back. Duel grabbed Coral Avalon by his long black hair and pulled up his right leg, intent on doing more harm to it. At least, up until Coral Avalon blasted Duel in the side of his head with a enzugiri that struck with enough impact to be a loud cracking noise heard throughout the Cumberland County Civic Center. The fans were right out of their seats for that one, as Duel crumbled down onto the mat in a heap after Avalon connected with that move. The fans had dueling "Duel" and "A-va-lon" chants as both men slowly tried to come back. Duel was the first to his feet, but Avalon caught him with a surprise forearm shiver to the head to stun him. As Duel stumbled backwards, Avalon limped toward him, kicked him in the gut with his good leg, and caught him with a quick DDT, bouncing Duel's face off of the mat. Avalon paused, and held his knee. He was still trying to get that wheel turning again, and it was costing him momentum against the former LCW World Champion. Avalon slowly stood up, and limped around, trying to get his leg mobile again. He saw that Duel was getting up, so he limped over to him and attacked him with his best offensive option at the moment. Avalon then tried to whip Duel into the opposite corner, but Duel easilly reversed it and sent the Kleptomaniac crashing into the opposite set of turnbuckles. Duel, himself, tried to charge in, but Avalon managed to slip away before Duel could make impact. Duel bounced chest-first into the turnbuckles, and was met with a kick in the gut and the attempt at a Curveball. Usually, when Coral Avalon sets up the Curveball, it hits. But not this time, as Duel picked Avalon straight up and snapped him over with a spinebuster so hard that the fans simply gasped in shock. It was even worse for the Kleptomaniac to know that Duel was back in the driver's seat of the match, and the veteran was just a few short moments away from applying... ...Salvation. Avalon wasn't familiar with the inner workings of the ankle lock submission hold, and he wasn't particularly keen on being locked in the hold, which was why as soon as Duel grabbed Avalon's right leg for the move, Avalon rolled over as best as he could and kicked Duel in the head. Duel backed away, leaving Coral Avalon to try to get to his feet. Duel didn't want this and charged in on the Kleptomaniac with a lariat. It missed. The Curveball did not. The fans, as well as Allison Lindum, cheered as Coral Avalon landed the swinging double underhook DDT made famous by Scott Slugger. Avalon paused to check on the condition of his knee, before he pinned Duel. One. Two. THRE-NO. Foot on the bottom rope. Avalon was surprised, but he also knew that Duel had a hell of a lot more expierence than he did, and ring awareness came with that. Coral Avalon struggled to his feet, and waited for Duel to get up. Duel would make him pay for that, though. DRAGON SUPLEX. One. Two! THRE-NO! Avalon has once again kicked out of one of Duel's stronger attacks, but the Kleptomaniac was literally on dream street off of the Dragon suplex, and chances were that he had no clue what Duel was planning next, up until he pulled the Kleptomaniac to his feet and grabbed him in a half nelson. Then Duel, once again, pitched the Kleptomaniac across the ring, this time with a half nelson suplex. Avalon landed on the back of his head, but the momentum that went into the suplex had rolled the rest of Avalon's body with him after impact. This, of course, meant that Coral Avalon was left on his stomach, and Duel was in firm control. With Avalon holding both his neck and his leg in pain, Duel had quite a few options in how to go about pounding on him further. However, Duel decided to go for the option that was more painful, which Coral was sure that he wouldn't like. Duel picked up Avalon, hooked him up in a front facelock, grasped his leg, and tossed him down to the canvas with a fisherman's suplex. However, Duel wasn't done, as he let go of Avalon's leg upon impact, swivelled around, pulled Avalon up to a standing position again, grabbed the leg again, and hit the fisherman's buster. Satisfied, Duel rolled over and covered Avalon, hoping... no, knowing that this was enough to finish the Kleptomaniac and give him the Bantam title. One. Two. THREE. NO. He was looking to hit the watermill suplex, but Avalon managed to throw his legs behind Lawson before he could get a good enough grip on the Kleptomaniac to hit him with it, and landed behind Lawson to execute... THE DYING BREATH. The fans erupted, again, as Coral Avalon somehow lifted up and dropped Duel through his legs with the reverse DDT. "Av-a-lon" chants roared throughout the arena, as the Kleptomaniac tried to come back. He rolled to the outside and waited for Duel to return to his feet, and when he did, Avalon managed to get off a springboard, into a hurricanrana, despite his poor leg. Avalon couldn't hold on for a pin, though, as he held his leg in pain again. Avalon was tenacious in getting up, though, as was Duel. With Duel suitably stunned, Coral Avalon had all but set Duel up for his three main finishing maneuvers. With that, Coral pulled Duel to his feet and applied the inverted facelock necessary for the Ratings Spike. However, Duel wasn't THAT stunned, and he quickly countered, pivoting around until Avalon only had a front facelock. Avalon had no way of avoiding this move a third time. WATERMILL SUPLEX! One. Two. However, Avalon, in the interest of saving his sorry, beaten ass, moved out of the corner and charged into the ropes before him. As it stood, Duel was simply stunned and leaning against the corner. Avalon regained his bearings, and crawled to the other end of the ring. He needed just one last shot to put this match away, or else he was gonna get dropped on his head again, and god knows Coral didn't need another concussion. Duel turned, and Avalon charged. ANOTHER ERRONEOUS CONCLUSION. This time, Avalon got all of it, and five hundred pounds of humanity slammed into the canvas thanks to a spear. The Kleptomaniac laid on top of Duel and had managed to hook his leg on the way down. One. TWO. THREE! The bell rang. The match was over, and the fans cheered.
