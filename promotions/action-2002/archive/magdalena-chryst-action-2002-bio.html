Biography Have you ever seen a horrible melodrama? You'd know one if you saw one. In a melodrama, the plot isn't just content with playing out naturally and developing into a great story. Instead, event after un-expected event plagues the plotline for the sake of being dramatic. Once the viewer struggles and successfully finds a place where they're comfortable, and hope that all things are cleared, their hopes are shot down by another happening that upsets the story. Margaret Madrigal's life seems to be one such story. Written by some higher power, it is the tale of a girl born to a young and single mother in the small, goody-goody town of Mulberry, Ohio. This was a town in which only a few sheep have been black-listed, and only for petty sins such as shoplifting. It was apparent from the start that Maggie did not belong in Mulberry. However, In order to get a better grasp of Maggie, you must first meet her supporting cast. Namely, that refers to her mother. Meghan Madrigal was a young and single mother from Cincinnati, Ohio. The father of her child, Joshua Russum, skipped out on her saying that he wouldn’t be trapped by some one night stand. Meghan told him to fuck off and then she cried because she didn’t know what to do. She wanted a different life for her daughter from what she lived. Meghan, as a youth, encountered enough drugs, sex, and loud music to make Keith Richards look like a choir boy. The nearby town of Mulberry looked like the perfect place to get out of the fast lane and to slow things down to start a new, calm life. However, you can't hide from genetics. Meghan got to Mulberry in April of 1979 already 3 months pregnant. She rented a small one-bedroom apartment and got a job as a waitress making minimum wage in a small diner. It was called the “Hub City Diner” and it was modeled after the 50s (complete with a Jukebox that played Doo-Wop!). It wasn’t paying much, but hell, it was money that she could use. She saved up around half of it, cutting corners because she knew she would need the money soon. The employees at the diner pitched in for a baby shower that helped Meghan get everything together for the welcoming of little Maggie Madrigal. By the time the seven pound eight ounce baby girl popped out, Meghan had amounted clothes, bottles, a crib, and other baby paraphernalia. Meghan resumed work shortly after Maggie’s birth, and brought her to work with her as her co-workers watched after the little girl. The next few years were uneventful, and all was looking up, but the false sense of hope would soon be shattered. The toddler stage is a stage of unknown rebellion so to speak. At the age of three, unbeknownst to her mother, Maggie decided her new favorite word was 'Shit.' Maggie waited for the perfect time to inform her mother. Of course, that time turned out to be Sunday morning church service (even at three, Maggie wasn’t a fan of organized religion). Little Margaret ran up and down the aisles screaming 'Shit! Shit! Shit!' This was much to the embarrassment of her mother of course, who promptly grabbed her daughter by the arm, and ran outside. She threw Maggie into their beat-up car and sped home at around 90 miles per hour. Once she reached home, Meghan immediately found the soap and proceeded to wash out Maggie’s mouth. Innocence was lost, and a war had started between the mother and daughter. The war was going to have many, many battles. One such battle took place two years after the church incident. After a hard day’s work, Meghan came home and treated herself to a nice and long shower. This left Margaret “The Five Year Old Terrorist” Madrigal all alone to do whatever she pleased. She cautiously stepped into the kitchen, used a chair to climb up onto the counter and open the cabinet revealing what she was looking for: the glasses. Glass after glass was flung onto the floor, the same result each time. Glass shattered and the shards scattered across the floor, a smile widening on Margaret’s face after each one. She lost interest after the eighth one, so she climbed down (cautiously, who’d want to step in that?) and went to her room. Her mother was enraged, and for punishment she made Maggie kneel in the remains of the glasses for one hour. Maggie just wished she had a dog to blame it on. Fast forward six years to 1990. An eleven year old Margaret (she was an adult, dammit!) walked into the house smoking a cigarette as an outright protest against her mother. This escalated into an argument, just as much every action that one performed within a one-mile radius resulted in. However, this one was destined for something more. At the threat of being kicked out of the house, Margaret snapped and threw a punch at her mother. Meghan never bothered to dodge the blow; yet, she just laughed, informed her daughter she had just screwed herself, and dialed the police. Margaret was given a list of choices to consider as an alternative to juvenile hall. It was a new program in the Cincinnati area dedicated to keeping children out of trouble, a compilation of various amounts of programs good at keeping kids distracted, all the while venting anger. It was chocked full of martial arts programs, and Margaret would gladly have taken up kickboxing if her mother hadn’t interfered. Meghan thought it would be odd to put Margaret in a class to learn how to beat her mother up. She figured wrestling was nice, fake, and safe, so why not choose that? Even though Margaret was only 11 (11½ if you asked Margaret), her trainer, Carlos Valdez, was used to working with children her age. Approaching sixty, Carlos was a very wise man who had trained wrestlers in Mexico to become notorious high-flying luchadores. He saw Margaret as different, and tried something else. Carlos wanted to let Margaret develop her own unique little style, with guidance from Carlos on not only wrestling matters, but life issues as well. Margaret wasn’t like a Mexican luchador, and he wasn’t going to treat her like she was one. At first, Margaret resisted. Her mother had put her in this damned place with this old man after all. She soon realized, though, that it was the best thing that could happen to her. Carlos was a father figure to her, the one she never wanted, but the one she always needed. The presence of Carlos in her life calmed her down. Wrestling took time away from seeing her mother, greatly reducing the chance for conflict on the home front. Things were finally looking up again. Within three years, Margaret had honed her wrestling technique. It was a unique combination of instinct street-fighting and high-flying action. She toured the tri-county area with various independent feds including the KWP, OCW, and the CAW. She played the part of Magdalena Chryst, a cruel and cold-hearted bitch. She played the part all so well. The small audiences reacted loudly to the part Margaret played. We’ve all known a bitch or two. Your ex, your mother, that girl you wanted to fuck and dreamed about but spat on your existence. We’ve all known a few. At sixteen, Margaret was touring with the Cincinnati Association of Wrestling, performing to a crowd of about one thousand. Margaret’s character had become a sort of semi-star in the area, and the CAW had prospered because of it. After what many die-hard fans claimed as the best CAW show in a good while, Margaret the 16 year old champion showered and got ready to head to the hotel. Margaret liked the exercise, so she always walked. In this case it was only a ten minute stroll to her bed. She stepped out to the dark and cool Cincinnati night and walked along the deserted sidewalks. She was excited for she was sure she was going places. When she thought that, however; she never thought of the hospital. Five minutes into her walk, she was struck to the ground. Her training did nothing to help her 5’7” stature against a giant of a shadow. She lay there, unconscious and bleeding for hours. She awoke in a hospital, and she was told the attack had caused internal bleeding and she needed stitches in her face. The mental damage caused, however, was much greater than the physical. Margaret Madrigal was once a scared little girl that used a mask of cruelty to hide her fear. She was scared of her mother as a small girl. Carlos Valdez had taught her there was nothing to be scared of… but Carlos had lied. There was something to be scared of; something more terrifying than her mother. That shadow that attacked her, that was to be feared. Once again, she put up her mask of cruelty; this time she was a woman. Margaret returned to wrestling a few months after the attack. She played the part of Magdalena with a new intensity, and she played the part in the ring and in the real world. She adopted the name Magdalena Chryst for every day use because she had become her in every way, why not be called what she truly was? Magdalena acquired a cult following all around the country in the next four years. She would travel from major city to major city and do a few shows showcasing her talent on both the mat and the microphone. The likes of Houston, New York, Los Angeles, Miami, Chicago, and countless other cities had become targets of Maggie, where she made her mark. It was only a matter of time, she knew, that someone would pick her up for national success. Other Feds A bunch of indy shitters, like CAW Other Fed Awards CAW World Champ among others.
