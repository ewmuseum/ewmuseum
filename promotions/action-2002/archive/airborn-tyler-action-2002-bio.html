Fed Awards Won $50,000 on Pressure Point 29 Biography For Airborn Tyler, violence was a bad demon, a bad demon that was trying take away his soul. Throughout school he was picked on for being different, for daring to vary from the set trend that most schoolboys followed like a tight rope. ‘Differ from the track and your demise is inevitable,’ seemed to be the message for the nation’s youth, but then Airborn wasn’t part of any particular crowd. So his outlook on life was hardly surprising really. ‘Violence was not to be encouraged, appreciated or participated in’ had been the message constantly drilled into him by his parents. So much so in fact, that he believed it. Boxing, judo, wrestling, all sports at which the TV was turned off if Airborn had the remote control. That would be encouraging violence. Any cartoons with playful explosions? They were given the hard shoulder. Even soccer was a no no. All that physicality charging after a leather ball? The nerve of it all! Being an athletic kid though, Airborn persisted with sports, and from the timid age of just 10 he became one of the nation’s best in his chosen activities. Synchronised swimming and rhythmic gymnastics. He could dive under water parallel with the best of them, and wave what looked reminiscent of toilet paper around in time with the finest classical music, but if street credibility was his ultimate goal then he may as well have just pulled the toilet chain, for any hope of that was long gone. At school he was now the walking talking equivalent of a ping pong ball. In the playground at break times he would be pushed around amongst groups of rowdy boys, all of them hurling insults as he ran, trying desperately to escape their grasp. His goofy cheeks, buck teeth and untidy blonde locks never helped the situation, and his uncanny ability to get every maths equation possible correct proved to be the final straw. With alarming regularity, he started to get beaten up. Head held low he would often walk home with a black eye, or a cut on his arm, or a bruise on his shins. Why? Because whenever the gang closed him down, he refused to fight back, his anti-violence side shining as bright as the sun in the peak of summertime. He was the proverbial one legged man in an ass kicking contest. As time dragged on though, and he emerged out of puberty into his mid teens, he started to see the light, that this was no lifestyle to live. Fed up with the rocky and dangerous path he was heading down, he paid a visit to his tutorial teacher (and best friend according to some) Mr.Browning for some advice. The reply? “Go and join a boxing club, toughen yourself up. If you show these ignorant wankers that you aren’t going to stand for no bullshit then they will leave you the fuck alone. Now piss off.” As you can tell, the teacher didn’t have that many moral values, but God bless him, he taught Airborn an important lesson. Fuck moral values, and fuck anyone who gets in your way. It was hard to get over his initial trepidation towards violence, but Mr.Browning had done enough to convince Airborn. He joined a boxing club. The predictable conclusion came to pass, as week after week Airborn’s fragile frame took another beating. Every fight he competed in ended in a TKO, first round. He had no problem getting back up; he was very persistent; but getting knocked down three times in one round suggested that perhaps a boxing career wasn’t quite in the wings for him. Strength wasn’t engraved on the stone carrying his qualities list, so he had a word with his boxing coach. “Get involved in something that can utilise your speed, persistence and flexibility.” And so Airborn Tyler entered the world of wrestling, and after just one independent tournament (he reached the quarter finals), he was invited into PCW for a one month trial. However, they were not impressed with what they saw, and he was released, which is where Action! comes in. He took to the coporation like water off a duck’s back, and the rest as they say, is history.
