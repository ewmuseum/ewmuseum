The Great Awakening Biography Nicknames Grimey, Quint, Resurgence Hometown Boston, Massachusetts Birthday May 11th, 1978 Height 6'6 Weight 269 Biography My father dedicated his life to pleasing my mother. Likewise, things got really rough when she passed away. I was just an infant at the time; I have no recollection of her appearance, or personality, without looking at old photos and/or home videos. For that I am forever saddened. My father was rising in fame in our region as a popular comedian, but he wasn't the same after that catastrophe. The sorrow killed his act: He stopped being happy, He stopped telling his jokes, and he gave up on his childhood dream of making people laugh. We moved into a rough neighborhood afterwards, where my father opened an art supply store. He tried to show me humor in the world despite our poor position, but it became clear to me it was painful for him to try and convey any form of happiness or humor. I wasn't a fan of organized academics, and my father really didn't mind when I dropped out of high school after my sophomore year. After all, I was more inclined to do art; something I had been doing ever since childhood. I loved to paint and see my father flash me a proud smile with every completed product. Since schoolwork was no longer a priority to me, I sought an artistic profession. I was told that my art revitalized our corrupt neighborhood, but I wouldn't reach out to make such a boastful claim. Sure, my art funneled money into our city programs but money is paper and it left my hands quicker than it had entered. As a teenager, I began to gain a favorable art reputation locally. I applied to several art colleges, and received a full scholarship to attend K.C.A.I (Kansas City Art Institute). It started to look like everything was going to be all right, and I was genuinely excited about what was happening. But of course my life could never be 'all right'. Troublesome tribulations could not go away. They knocked at my door once more. I don't remember the events surrounding the tragedy, but it definately changed my life forever. All I remember is cleaning out a paint brush in the sink, only to wake up in a hospital as a victim of electrocution. But the real shock came with what my eyes saw, or didn't see for that matter. I was told that I had developed color-blindness in the aftermath of the incident. The symptoms were commonly associated with shock patients, but the impediment never went away as once expected. To this day my eyes see the world much like an old-fashioned movie. My eyes can only register blacks and grays, with soft hues of blue. This deprivation of my senses promptly destroyed what little budding art career I had. I took my skills and vision for granted and I paid dearly for it. I closed myself off to the world because I could no longer find beauty in it, let alone capture it on a canvas. I frantically searched for something to do with my life. I finally came out with the decision to walk in my fathers footsteps and become an entertainer. Of course I couldn't totally place my feet in his tracks because I was, and still am, not one to joke around. My father was a large man, and I used this inherited genetic trait to my advantage. I began to be a regular at the gym, training myself to become a pro wrestler. To this day I don't know why I chose wrestling as an entertaining medium. It had simply grown on me, and it looked as if it was my only option at the time because I couldn't sing, dance, or act for that matter. I would let nothing stand in my way until I was out there pleasing myself, my father, and the general public. I joined wrestling school and picked up the trade quicker -- and easier -- than I had expected. The rest was history, and I was rushed to my first federation. I felt like an annoying weed in a garden of roses. At first I didn't fit in with everyone else. But after observing our differences, I eventually found out that we were all the same. I was pleased that people were enjoying my work, and I was being paid an honest wage for it. It seemed as if this was my calling. But another speed bump made me think again. I've always been told that 'When life hands you lemons, make lemonade.' However, I could no longer stand the taste after my dad grew ill in the Fall. His final months really proved to be devastating. Little did he know that they were hard on me as well. I had to put wrestling on hold to take care of him. I was eventually fired for taking too much time off, and, God forbid, caring about my own family. My fame, money, and confidence went up in smoke. I was dead on a theoretical level, and if I didn't get a positive spark in my life, I probably would have been physically dead too. I needed a great awakening to revive my old life. The old potential I posessed before my life turned into a ridiculous charade. My life and mentality had been shattered too many times, and I felt too tired to pick up the pieces. Then I met Wendal. I saw him at my father's funeral. He was concealed in the back, distant from those in attendance. I could relate from the get-go, as I was very uncomfortable with the whole situation. But for some reason he placated my anxiety when the crowds dispersed. I discovered he had just been released from prison for a failed robbery attempt. I didn't really think, at the time, that he fitted the mold of a hardened criminal. He was pale, frail, and non-intimidating. He told me he was a big fan of my father's work, and he was sincere about his respects. He was a comedian too before adopting his short-lived life of crime. He tried to make a comeback, but failed miserably. We found common ground early, as we were both down on our luck, absolute failures in our own eyes. We were tired of failing, and perhaps, together we could succeed. For some odd reason, I couldn't help but relate him to my late father. Just everything about him reminded me of my late father. He shared his good qualities, as well as bad. It was like I had known him a long time. As our odd relationship grew, I had the crazy thought he could be a faint commodity in my wrestling return. We decided he would be the clever brains, and I would be the brawn. Hopefully, as time went on, I could pick up on some of his wit. I would be the visibly-menacing wizard, while he was the mind behind the curtains. We felt like an indestructable duo. With an influx of new concepts and practices, we quickly rushed a resume out to federation heads across the country. We tested the water by indifferently casting our lines, but were pleasantly surprised with what we caught. Surely, we had a keeper: Action Wrestling.
