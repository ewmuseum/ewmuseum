Reflecting On: Part Nine.

16 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
They were no longer rushing. No longer in a big hurry to get to where they were going. No, instead, they slowly walked through the airport. Heading for the shuttle train to take them to baggage claim where they could also grab a cab. Slots machines were going off all over the airport. People winning money before they catch a flight out of Sin City. Some having just gotten there, already emptying their pockets. Las Vegas was a very unique place. Xander and Jessica ignored it all. Xand had his right around across his wife's shoulder and the right across her stomach. She was just... in pain. It hurt so much. Samantha Dox was dead and she just really didn't know what to do. She was confused. Lost. Unable to think straight. Xander had been there. Still was in fact. But he had coped with it seeing his wife in pain. She had felt like an outsider when Xander wasn't telling her anything. She had just went along, oblivious to the situation. Once she had found out what was going on, she just crumbled. She couldn't be the brave one. Xander had to take up the call. He had to comfort his wife. And he was. He had learned how through his mother and then later through the years with Jessica. Okay, so it was nearly just three years. But you can learn a lot in three years, and Marcus Alexander Dox had. The two stood, embraced tightly, as the shuttle came to a close. The passsengers got off on one side and then the sliding doors opened up to allow the oncoming passengers to board. Standing next to a pole, Xander held it as the shuttle took off. Only about fifteen seconds went by before they were at baggage claim. Everyone exited the shuttle and headed for their luggage, a cab, or a hotel shuttle bus. A cab is what the couple wanted. A trip to the hospital, where Xander hoped his mother still was. In the morgue at least. He really didn't know what to do. Which was odd. He had been in the situation four times before. He was very young for his father's and sister's deaths. His uncle came during the years of adolescence. And his brother, well, he had been there and witnessed his brother's death. That wasn't the easiest thing to see. He had not even attended the funeral then. He couldn't. Wouldn't. Now, though, it was different. He was older. More mature. But, still, he was lost. He didn't know how you did things. How you went about seeing your dead mother. He felt strangely like Anya in 'The Body'. They had made their way through the crowd and two sliding doors opened up. The warm night air hit them. Jessica's tears were starting to dry up. Xander had long since stopped crying. He couldn't do it anymore. It just wasn't going to happen. A cab was waiting to take someone somewhere. They weren't picky about their passengers, just as long as they got 'em. Xander opened up the back door and let his wife inside. She slid across to the middle of the seat. When her husband sat down beside her, she quickly grasped his hand and held it with both of hers. Xander said to the driver, 'To the hospital, please.' The Asian driver replied, 'Hoss-pee-tail?' 'Yes,' was the hollow reply of the last remaining member of the Dox family. The driver just nodded as he turned on the ticker before pulling out. In the backseat, Jessica lay her head on Xander's shoulder as he stroked her hair.
