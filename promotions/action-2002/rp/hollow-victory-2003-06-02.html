Hollow Victory

2 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/02/03 Coral Avalon was feeling light-headed as he watched his trainer and best friend, Joey Malone, wrestle Daniel Phillips. He couldn't explain the feeling he was going through, but he wouldn't really know what was wrong with him until after this little exercise. He held his Bantam championship unconsciously in his left hand, not knowing that he hadn't actually let go of it since the Bantam title match. He simply sat there, less like watching his trainer beat the living hell out of the man that had put him out of action two months ago, and more like staring off into some fantasy land. Was he euphoric over his victory a few minutes ago? Or was he just lost in his own thoughts? Or maybe he was debating on what would happen if Joseph Tyler Malone would lose tonight, like he's done so many times in this particular arena? And if so, what would become of Coral? On the other side of the coin, you had Allison Lindum. Allison's not as nervous or as unsure of herself as Coral was. In fact, she was the yin to Coral's yang. Coral was shy to a fault, had difficulty making friends, and couldn't speak more than a couple of words in front of a crowd of screaming people. Allison, on the other hand, was friends with most of the people in Action by now(except Garvin. She could not STAND Garvin.), outgoing to a fault, and oftentimes seemed less like the manager of a now-Bantam champion, and more like a magical dancing faerie princess who didn't seem like the badass aikido blackbelt that she actually was. Allison loved Coral, because once you get through the layers of shyness and nervousness, you'd find a butterfly of crazy 'Coral-tude' just waiting to flutter out of that coccoon. Plus, you can't get the silly bastard to shut up once you get through all of that. Coral loved Allison, because before he met her, he had exactly two friends: Joey Malone and Kent Anthason. He often played 'middle brother' between the two. Which is to say that Coral often was the one who relayed the messages between the two, since they couldn't get along after Gold and Glory and they would continue not to. The only women he had ever talked to was his mother, whom he couldn't stand; his little sister, who couldn't stand him; and his elder sister, who couldn't stand anyone (without backing away in fear). Yet, Allison knew there was something wrong with Coral at the moment in time. 'Coral?' she asked, confused as to what was with Coral's staring. Not to mention the fact that Coral hadn't even gotten out of his wrestling gear yet. 'Yeah?' Coral asked, distant and far away from the realm in which a beautiful black-haired woman sat next to him in all of her silky-dressed goodness. 'Is something wrong?' Allison asked, trying to pry herself into whatever form of reality that Coral was in. 'I'm... I'm not sure... I feel kinda dizzy...' Coral said, again distant. Now, Allison was a bit tomboyish, if it wasn't hinted at already by her occasionally stubborn nature and her aikido background. Now, hanging around her brother, Marcus, has given her a few hard knocks, and while she only recently elected to simply lay off of the physicality, become pretty, and concentrate on academics(at least until the whole wrestling thing happened), she's also been a victim of certain injuries. One of which was, of course, the concussion. She's had two in her life, one when she was fifteen, when Marcus had tackled her a bit too hard in a game of football, and again when she was seventeen, when she tackled Marcus too hard with her head. In a game of baseball. The point is, Allison stood up, and didn't bother smoothing her dress out as she did so, as she put both of her hands on both sides of Coral's cheeks and stared intently into his blue eyes. The weary, spacey eyes that greeted her back gave her the suspicion that he had been concussed. 'Coral. I think you have a concussion. You think you can just sit there while I go find an EMT?' Allison asked him. 'Yeah,' Coral said, dreamilly, 'Sure.' The concerned girlfriend of Coral Avalon quickly left the room, though Coral couldn't really register the clicking of Allison's sandals on the hard, cement floor of Madison Square Garden's hallways. He glanced up to watch Daniel Phillips hit his shooting star legdrop, and he passed out before the three count could be registered. Welcome to the Bantam title, Coral Dylan Avalon. It's all downhill from here.
