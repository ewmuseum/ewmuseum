Action! — Tales

callow man defeated. . .morbid violence redeemed

30 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Thick must had made the air almost unbearable to breath. It was a terrible smell of body odor and sweat soaked clothing. The sweat dripped profusely from every pour in Maverick’s body. Each one hit the floor with a loud splash. They were the only noise in the cold locker room. Maverick leaned back in disappointment and regret. His muscles were tense and contracted from the earlier bout. Perhaps, more than they should have been. This was the first real workout Maverick had had in a long while. It was almost grueling in a sense. There was the same exhilarating rush from the crowd as he approached the ring. Not as much as it used to be, but it was more than he deserved. The work with the Mafia was eating at him and constantly calling him no matter where he was. Maverick was not only the Mafia’s newest hired muscle, he was also their newest hired hit man. The work was different than what he had ever done before. It was work, and it was making him rich. Still, the disappointment from his first match in his newest promotion was a failure. The opponent was a five-foot nothing shrimp among whales, and Maverick failed to gain victory over him. It was sorry indeed. Even if the victory Maverick suffered had been tainted, it was still a black mark on his record. There was nothing for Maverick to do to correct his mistake and work hard at becoming stronger and everything he used to be that he was now not. There was once a fighter in Maverick, and Maverick sought to find him once again. There were other duties on Maverick’s mind. He needed to fly home to New York and take care of family business. Soon Maverick was showered and dressed in his black dress pants and a thick black over coat over a gray turtleneck sweater supplied by Don Salvatore. The day was dark outside. Little light pierced the thick clouds above the earth. Whether it was moonlight or starlight, nothing shone through. No cars were left in the carport of the stadium. There was only one black Hummer. It belonged to Maverick. The shiny black corners reflected the buzzing lights hanging from the floor above. Maverick stepped into the Hummer tossing his duffle bag in the back seat. He sped away off into the night with steam putting out of the exhaust. Eventually, Maverick arrived at his destination. It was a quite large white house with a gigantically gated front yard. Cameras guarded the front gait. There were guards on duty twenty-four hours a day guarding each corner of the house. Maverick was granted admittance on first glance. His face was well known around the house, and his name more known around New York. Maverick was impeccably dressed. His nervousness caused eager paranoia. Important people always deserve such behavior. This person was probably one of the most important people in Maverick’s young life, so far. This man had made Maverick rich. He was wealthier than he ever would have expected after expulsion from Harvard. It was the Don after all. The front door was magnificent. It was two gigantic doors with gold trimming and pure gold lever door handles. It wouldn’t be completely absurd to believe that the glass was not glass but completely crystal. Though, it would be a little farfetched. Maverick’s knock at the door was answered almost in immediately. The inside of the mansion was even more glamorous than the outside. Wonderful masterpieces decorated the walls. The floor was nearly lethal from the pure Italian imported marble. Maverick was greeted. “Hello, my boy!” a man with shorter stature of Maverick and rounder, as well, greeted him. His hair turning silver with hints of the color it once was, black. He was neatly tailored in an expensive Italian suit. There was nothing but the finest. “Hello, Don Leone,” Maverick replied less than excited. The failure of his first match was wearing him down. “I have work for you, Kid. We gots a snitch downtown. The boys just figured him out, and we need to find out who else he told. When you figure that out, dispose of the piece of shit. If you can do this for me, then there will be nothing but good things for you.” “I can do it, Don Leone.” “Atta’ Boy! Nevuh lettin’ me down.” With that, the two men continued their conversation with Don Leone’s hand on Maverick’s back as he showed him to the door. Maverick had a new mission of the sorts, and he saw it as a way for him to exercise his fighting abilities. Maverick drove downtown to a small little no place in the heart of the Red Light District. Maverick knocked on the door and a piece of wood slid open and two beady eyes stared through it. Maverick was quickly recognized and granted entrance. The inside of the room was foggy. Clouds of smoke surrounded the men playing poker and blocked the lights. A man was strapped against the wall watching. He was in little pain. Little had been done to unlock the secrets to which his mind held, as they were waiting for Maverick to do the dirty work. Maverick knew his job immediately as they were previously outlined by Don Leone. “Hello, my name is Montresor,” Maverick approached the gentleman strapped to the wall. “I’ll be your pain maker for today.” Spit splashed upon Maverick’s face. It was retaliated with a violent blow to the ribs that sent the man in agony. Maverick prodded the man with a hot poker to his throat. It was simmering in a fire and burning red with fright. The man’s skin boiled as he burst with screams and his muscles tensed. “Don’t speak, I’m going to enjoy killing you whether I get the information I want or not.” Knowing his already predetermined fate, the man didn’t speak, but continued his screams of pain. Maverick threw one fist directly into his jaw breaking it into pieces. One more fist to the man’s throat, and it was his last breath. The other men in the room were shocked that Maverick didn’t get the information as he was instructed. Maverick left the body to the men to clean up. This little practice helped Maverick win the next two matches. Not as honorably as he had hoped, but victories nonetheless. The match following it was one of his specialties; yet, he seemed not to be back in the game quite yet. Though he lost that match, he was down but not out. When Resentment rolls around, Mr. Midget will resent the day he screwed with former Maverick Manson. . .
