The Family Circus: Cleo

12 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/12/2002 Now, Cleo Avalon was never the most conventional human being on the planet. Thankfully, she wasn't named after anyone major. She wasn't named for Miss Cleo, or anyone that was particularly famous. This was because Cleo had the fortune of not being one of the children of Irene and John Avalon. As a matter of fact, she was actually John Avalon's younger sister, putting her in the late forties range. Now, Cleo Avalon is unmarried after these last couple of decades, and it might have something to do with the same eccentric behaviour that the rest of the Avalon family exhibits. She has, in her life, run quite a few telethons and infomercials in her storied career. For what, you might ask? Nothing in particular, indeed. You see, Cleo Avalon believed not in the reasons why such hideously long things exist in the bowels of human television. She believed in the strictest conduct and the utmost respect as they took place. She believed in the beautiful acting of the people doing business with you. After all, if you're going to sit for an hour and a half or longer and listen to people rant about a CD collection of songs you really hate and are sick of hearing about, then dammit, put some feeling into it! The point is that Cleo Avalon is New Orleans' first and only infomerical and telethon critic. It was a dirty job that nobody wanted to do, and it was Cleo's job to do it. Well, at some point, she started to love her job just a tad too much, and right now, she was doing that job. And yelling at the television as she did it, much in the same way that the average American male yelled at the television as their favorite team get their asses pounded by their rivals. 'Oh, for the love of Pete, Jerry! Put some EFFORT into this one!' she yelled, which was heard by Coral Avalon, Allison Lindum, and Alan Rosefeld as they approached the door into one of the three television areas of the Avalon household. 'What the hell was that?' Allison asked. 'That would be Madam Cleo, dear Miss Allison,' Rosefeld said, with a bit of a frown on his face. He knew about Cleo Avalon's tendency to scream a lot, as well. 'Infomercial critics are like that to begin with.' Coral cut in, bringing about another look of horror on Allison's face that was much similar to the one she had given Coral when he had explained Elvis Avalon's silence. 'You're kidding.' was Allison's response, turning Coral's expression serious. He got in front of Rosefeld and Allison, stopping them in their tracks. 'No, I'm not kidding,' Coral said with a sigh, 'Of all of my relatives, Aunt Cleo can be pretty strange, and THAT says something when you've got a family full of weirdos like mine,' Coral explained, hold his hands out as if he's explaining this to a six-year-old, 'So, if Jerry Lewis starts to really suck today, then try to avoid the flying objects, because Aunt Cleo has a tendency to do more damage to this place than Elvy ever could.' Rosefeld said nothing. Allison merely shrugged. They walked into the room, to find a blonde-haired woman laying on a couch, watching Jerry Lewis bring out the Jerry's kids. She had a bucket of stale popcorn laying on her stomach, the remote control in his left hand, and generally looked disshelved and lazy. Except for the bits where she screamed at the television, which is when she dropped the popcorn on the floor and leapt to her feet. Luckilly for Coral and Allison, and especially Rosefeld, she wasn't that pissed any more. In fact, she had noticed her visitors and quickly hit pause on the tape she was watching. She spoke in a light British accent with a hint of aristocracy to it, 'Oh, hello!' Coral was taken aback. Mainly because Cleo never used to turn off or even pause the tapes she watched when she watched them. Maybe she had improved with age. 'You'll have to forgive me,' Cleo said, 'I had no idea that company was coming.' 'Um, right.' Coral said. 'Oh, is that you, Coral?' Cleo asked, 'Where is that cup of coffee I asked you for, anyway? I can't even get any good service around here any more...' 'Um, Auntie Cleo, I haven't been in this house for three years, now, and...' 'Excuses! You young people are just full of excuses!' Cleo snapped, interrupting Coral and tossing popcorn in his direction as she did so. Coral was about to explain to her that Cleo never asked him for coffee in the first place, and Coral didn't really know how to make coffee since he hated coffee anyway. But Coral didn't really want to have larger objects thrown at him, so he had to lie to his aunt. 'Um, okay, I'll get you your coffee...' Coral said, staring at Rosefeld the whole time as if to say 'get this woman coffee'. He could do this since Cleo wasn't even looking in their direction anyway. 'Good good. Now, go on. Chop chop. Don't keep me waiting this time.' Coral sighed and allowed for Cleo to turn on her telethon again. He turned to Rosefeld and whispered to him, 'Rosey, think you could take care of it for me? We'll go meet up with Lill in the meantime.' 'Certainly.' Rosefeld whispered back, before he walked off to the kitchen. Coral turned to Allison and motioned for her to leave the room with him. Once they were out of the room, Coral turned to Allison. 'We're about to meet my little sister, again. Lily. As I've said before, she's thankfully not as bizarre as the rest of my family, but she's kind of depressing to talk to. Not to mention that her room can be pretty freaky. Shall we?' Coral asked. 'Sure,' Allison said with a smile, 'I think your family is actually kind of nice. Why do you resent them so much?' Coral frowned and fidgeted a bit as they walked down the hall, in the direction of a staircase, 'I'll, uh, let you know later.'
