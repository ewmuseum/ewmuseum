Kasperov, Cats, and the Great White Dope

14 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
This is a [name withheld] / [name withheld] joint... This is a story about Coral Allison and Rook. Rook has waved goodbye to Alex and Livvie. Frank still wasn’t back from wherever he was. He had the whole house to himself, both halves of the duplex. Rook went outside and walked around the house, surveying his yard. Oh yes, his yard. He was the yard’s caretaker. The yard was his self appointed responsibility. The yard was his pride and joy. So as far as Rook was concerned, the yard belonged to him. First Rook surveyed the garden and flowerbeds for any embarrassments, smashed flowers, litter, or obvious weeds that couldn’t wait for proper weeding time. Then Rook looked up and down the street, carefully watching the change in yard plans among the neighbors. He had to stay one step ahead of them. They were originally shocked and pleased that the yard of the Heimdal home was suddenly going to be up to standards. Then they were just shocked as the professional wrestler’s work had every intention of surpassing them. While the grass of the yard had been neglected for far too long for Rook to work a miracle, the flowers and the hedges were absolutely to die for. Also, Rook’s yard didn’t have any trees. So the neighbors were working their pristine lawns if they had them, or they were working the presence of their oaks and poplars. Rook had to give them credit for working their strengths. But he was certain without a doubt that he had the best arrangement of lawn on the street, and if any of those people opened a book and realized where he stole the garden layout from, he’d be quite upset. But the real secret was in the back yard. It was a project that might take more than a year. And it was all thanks to Carl Linnaeus. He innovated the floral clock. Confused? Whoa ho ho. At approximately 6 am, on cloudy or sunny days, the spotted cats ear opens its petals. At approximately 7 am, the African marigold opens. You get the idea? Rook had the knowledge, and he had the tools. He had the flowers in the back yard, carefully observed. Because of minute changes in local weather and climate, each flower had to be observed for its performance in relation to the hour. As far as Rook was concerned, he wanted to isolate the particular strain of flower most accurate to this region. The only shitty thing was the fact that it also happened to be daylight savings time in this area, so that he’d have to replant every spring, and clear them out every summer. But that was okay. He had a goal. It was just a matter of finding more time to work on this secret project. So Rook made his notes about any changes in the neighbors’ yards, and just before he was about to return to the house to change into super gardener, a car slowly drove down the street. Coral Avalon and Allison Lindum stared out the window at Rook, who stared back. They pulled into the Heimdal driveway. After sitting in the car for a few moments, they got out. Allison said, “Hi.” Coral said, “Hi. Rook said, “Hi.” … Coral said, “I’m actually here to see Frank.” “Oh. Well, Frank isn’t here at the moment,” replied Rook. The three stood there a moment looking around uncomfortably. “Would you liked to come in?” the Great White Dope asked. Allison’s hand clamped firmly on Coral’s arm, and before he could politely decline, she said, “That’d be nice!” So Rook led them into his side of the house because at least that part was clean. Rook brought them up the steps into the living room, and asked if they’d like ginger ale, orange juice, coke, diet coke, bottled water, or iced tea. Coral asked for some ginger ale and Allison decided she’d like some bottled water. While Rook was fussing in the kitchen (he had to get them nice glasses, you know, ones for company that didn’t have foul language on the side, and they had to be three matching glasses, and of course he had to get ice, and there also had to be an equal amount of ice in each glass. Rook was horrified to remember that there was only one coaster in the house, and it also had his name on it too, but Rook somehow wasn’t panicking more than usual.) Coral and Allison looked around the living room. On the coffee table was a pile of books and comics. Allison picked up a copy of Scott Keith’s Tonight In This Very Ring but fortunately, Coral told her that it sucked ass just in time for her to put it back down without opening. So she picked up a copy of Oz: The Memoirs Of Augustus Hill instead. Coral didn’t see anything on the table that caught his eye, so his gaze wandered around the room before zeroing in. A chessboard was on a small pedestal (a little table made to look like a Grecian column, classy!) near the window. Coral rose and looked it over. A game was in progress. What the hell was it? Kasperov obviously, but against who? Rook came into the room with a drink tray (that’s how you cope without coasters! Bring a tray!) and glanced at Coral looking over Rook’s simulation. Allison smiled at him warmly when he put the drinks down. Rook casually said, “Do you play?” Coral nodded, “Oh yeah. I’m just trying to figure out what you’ve got going here. It’s Kasperov against who?” Rook slowly stood up, “Well it’s just a fragment of my imagination, but I’m trying to simulate Kasperov v Luzhin. Kasperov is kicking his ass of course, but still, it’s going pretty good.” Coral nodded, “Oh, okay, I see it. It’s too bad you have this set up, I’d love to-“ Rook walked over and picked the board up off the little table and brought to where their drinks were. He grabbed the rooks, one white one black. Rook held the pieces behind him, “Coral Avalon, I challenge you to duel.” Coral said, “Well then. Left.” Rook revealed the white rook, then handed it to Coral. They set their pieces up and Coral began with kp - kp4, the traditional opening. Coral said, “En guard.” Rook smirked, “Never fence with a gunslinger.” Rook definitely played chess like a gunslinger. He didn’t mess around, didn’t smirk at Coral while taking a piece which was something that always really ticked Coral off, and wouldn’t hesitate to relentlessly trade pieces back and forth. Coral noted that Rook took about one minute for each move, the ideal friendly match thinking time. He wasn’t greedy, nor quick to act, each of Coral sacrifice traps were undertaken with full consideration of the consequences. Oddly enough, Rook didn’t delight in using the pieces of his namesake. It was Rook’s wild imagination with the board that really caught Coral’s fancy. Though Coral grasped that his next few choices would lose him the game, he wanted to watch how Rook played. After a few minutes, Coral said, “You’ve got a good game.” And Rook smiled, saying, “Thanks.” After that, they began to trade pieces almost carelessly, openly discussing the finishing sequences Rook was considering. Checkmate was a mere formality. They shook hands. Rook said, “I’m sorry that Frank isn’t home. He’s … got something going on and he’s been pretty secretive about it.” Allison looked up from the book, 'Hey, Rook... I've been wondering this for a while, but... is there something wrong with your hand?' Rook held up his right hand palm facing out next to his left hand. Strangely enough, his right hand was pale, and smaller by minute increments. 'Well, a beer keg kind of smashed it. But on the bright side, as bad as it looks now, it was much worse. I've got it in pretty decent working condition now, but I still need to rehab it a lot. I've got most of the mobility back, but the bones will never be quite the same. What's worse is that it's `funny'. It sustained nerve damage which can sometimes get pretty weird.' 'Oh, dear.' Allison said, disturbed by the look of Rook's hand, before she spoke again, 'I'm sorry to hear about that.' Rook shrugged, 'I appreciate that. But that's life I guess. I'm still working for my bread and considering that, I haven't much to complain about.' 'What's up with your dad, anyway? He's said some really terrible things about you, and I didn't even have to bring your name up.' Allison asked. Rook leaned back in his chair, 'What can I say that would explain my dad? Well, my dad is a sociopath, not unlike me. But the difference is that he's got amazing social skills but no sense of self discipline. He acts on his whims and desires regardless of the consequences, hesitates to take responsibility for his choices, and in general can switch from being considerate to being really cruel at a moment's notice. We have a rivalry that's been on for the last 5 years or so. It's fluctuated in severity between a truce to all out war, and it doesn't look like it's every going to end. He'll be chasing me around with his walker when he's 80 I'm sure.' Coral Avalon sighed to himself. He wanted to tell Rook about his problems regarding his own family. How they don't accept him for his choice in occupation and life, or how they don't accept him for the incident where Coral's older brother was thrown out of an arena when Daniel Phillips spat upon him. He wanted to tell him about how his mother was very judgmental and too worried about her own social situation to care about her own son. He wanted to tell him that his little sister hated him, was incredibly violent-minded, and expressed it by drawing a few less-than-flattering pictures of herself stabbing him in the eyes. He wanted to tell him about his older brother and his lack of a tongue, and therefore his lack of communication skills. He wanted to tell him about his incredibly xenophobic botanist older sister. He wanted to tell him that if he had followed the destiny his parents wanted him to follow, he'd be a fencer. Instead, Allison Lindum spoke before he had gotten halfway into his own mental rant. 'Oh, I know how that is,' she started, 'My father's really overprotective of myself and my sister, Keri. He's like this Vietnam veteran, so when Joey Malone got engaged to my sister, he actually tried to stab him with a fish knife that he collected from a Vietkong soldier. Oh, and there was this time that a high school football player made out with me and my dad so verbally thrashed him that he confessed that he was a chronic bed-wetter.' Coral blinked, 'I so didn't need to hear about that. Seriously.' Rook nodded, 'Well it looks like you came out of it all right.' 'Oh, my dad's funny like that. I think he's just full of rage `cause his first name's 'Muffin'. He doesn't really bother me.' Allison explained. Rook blinked, his face displaying utter shock; in St. Joe, Texas, a boy growing up with that name would have killed himself, and once he did so, it'd be looked upon as an act of public service to the community. Then again, Rook didn't grow up in Sacramento, California like Allison did for thirteen years of her life, and Muffin did for all of his life. A name as original as 'Muffin' probably would've been accepted in crazy `ol Northern California. Rook said, 'Wow. Just ... wow. Muffin. I don't think I was ready for that.' 'Neither was he.' Allison said. Coral simply stood in the background. He, himself, had a really odd first name, which also happened to be a birthname given to him when he was born during an Avalon yachting trip off the coast of Australia. However, he didn't really want to bring THAT bit of information up. Nor did he want to bring up that his older sister was named after the Emmy Awards and his older brother was named after Elvis Presley. Rook shrugged, 'Well, I don't feel nearly so bad about `Randall' then.' Allison returned Rook's shrug, 'I think you should feel more sympathy for him,' she said, gesturing toward Coral, 'I mean, his actual name IS Coral.' 'Eh, I thought `Coral' was kind of cool. Unique. Interesting. That sort of thing,' said the guy who thought Rook was an improvement over Randall. 'We-well,' Coral said, nervously breaking his silence, 'I was going to change my first name, but, well, it's hard to come up with a name that goes with 'Avalon'.' Rook smiled, “My Uncle Ron would’ve tried to get you to go with an Arthurian gimmick, `Percival Avalon’ or something like that. He’s a bit old school that way.” 'And suddenly, 'Coral' doesn't seem so bad.' Avalon said with a nod. Just then Frank's cat Towtruck leapt up on Rook's lap and said 'Mrroww!' which translates to, 'Who are these people and why did you let them in my house? They had better be nice to me or give me presents.' 'Aw, how cute!' Allison said, scratching that cat behind its ears with her fingernails. Towtruck purred, translation read, 'She can stay. And the other one can too if they're a package deal.' 'That's Frank's cat `Towtruck',' explained the Feeder-of-Cats. Allison continued to scratch the cat by its ears, and smiled at Rook, 'I like him.' Rook said, 'She's a good kitty.' Rook turned to Coral, 'So what do you plan to do next?' 'You know, I have no idea,' Coral said, 'I'm kind of... I dunno. Directionless.' This was cause for Rook to smirk, 'There's a lot of that particular disease going around. You should hang out with us then. We make directionlessness look like apathy and laziness.' 'As opposed to the nepotism and laziness I got accused of for hanging out with Joey Malone?' “Why not? We’ve got no real stroke anyway, so it certainly wouldn’t hurt you. Also, wankers one hundred and one seems to be growing big for the britches as it were.” 'Oh, don't I know it?' Coral said. Rook's eyes opened wide, the delight on his face clearly showing, 'Of course. As a result of my beating after putting Jeff over, I got to be spirited away by secret Ninja powers. It was amazing.' '...Ninja powers.' Coral said, flatly. Rook was undaunted, his hands gesticulating his excitement, 'Well, what with the lights out and all. He used a Ninja hold on my hair and dragged me down the ramp. I tried to ask him about smoke bombs, but he ran away instead of answering. I plan to corner him first chance I get to ask him a bunch more questions.' 'I don't think he'd really enjoy that,' Allison said, 'I mean, he's a ninja. He's not gonna just reveal to you all of his secret abilities to you just `cause you'll ask.' Rook nodded thoughtfully, “You’re probably right. But I think I have to try.” Rook continued, 'In any case, Kenshiro isn't likely going to become an ally. And I think it'd be fantastic if you'd join up with the three of us, Coral.' 'You think?' 'Oh yeah. The Aqua Teen Hunger Force would be thrilled.' 'The Aqua Teen Hunger Force,' Avalon repeated, 'You, my friend, have too much free time on your hands.' Rook smirked, 'Actually, with more free time on my hands, I'm much much worse. But that's my particular kryptonite.' 'I think you should just join already, Coral.' Allison said, 'You have, like, no friends.' 'Hush, Allison.' Coral said, waving her off, 'Nobody's to know about THAT particular bit of information!' Rook smiled, it was a sharks grin, going for the kill, 'Besides, no one wants you to become the next Daniel Phillips.' 'HEY!' Coral shouted. Rook squinted, and said to Allison, 'On the misanthrope scale, I'd give him a 6.5, with a likely tendency to climb further. What do you think?' '7.0!' Allison said with a smile. Rook thought that over and said, 'I'd have to side with your lady friend, Coral. She knows you better than me.' Rook traced the self inflicted straight razor scars on his face, 'Being a sociopath doesn't lead to good things.' 'I'm not a sociopath!' Avalon shot back, 'I just find it hard to make friends.' Rook laughed and held up a hand, 'Okay, I'm picking on you too much. In all seriousness, I understand how hard it is to make friends. Sleep on it, think about it, and come visit sometime. We enjoy good company. I hear we're even fun to be around.' Coral calmed down a bit, 'Alright. I will.” Rook nodded, 'Good. Are you guys hungry? I bet I could fix something up for us.' 'Actually, now that I think about it... yes, I'm quite hungry.' Allison said before he turned to Coral, 'Aren't you?' 'Yeah...' Rook rose and said, 'Follow me to the kitchen, while I'm not a cullinary master like Frank is, I assure that you both will not die from food poisoning. Coral, do you have a carb restriction on your diet? If you do it's okay because I do myself so we can ...' And into the kitchen they went. A landmark event for everyone: Rook had successfully reached out to strangers for the first time in his life. Coral might have actually made a new friend. Allison had a newfound respect for Frank Heimdal for his cat. The Moral of the Story? Towtruck ownz.
