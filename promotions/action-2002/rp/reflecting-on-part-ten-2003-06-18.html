Reflecting On: Part Ten.

18 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
'On November 6th, 1997, Charles Abraham Dox was tragically killed during a wrestling match in Las Vegas, Nevada. Charles, wrestling under the guise of Max Hunter, was competing against heated rival 'Superstar' Brian Robinson. During the bout, which had drawn nearly 3,000 people to a local high school gym, Charles took what is called a tombstone piledriver. Unfortunately, injuries and accidents happen in the wrestling professional. And this had been the case for the 29 year old, Charles Dox, had been dropped viciously, albeit accidently, on his head, breaking his head on impact. He was dead on scene.' The small paragraph on the front page of the local newspaper read. The story was continued somewhere in the middle of the paper, but Marcus Dox, the younger brother of Charles, wasn't going to read it. He was dead and he had witnessed it first-hand. His dreams of becoming a professional wrestler were in serious doubt. He was unsure of his future. Unsure of what to do next. Charles' body had been flown back to Augusta for the proper burial next to his father, sister, and uncle. Many other deceased relatives were laid to rest there. The open-casket funeral had taken place the night before. Marcus had stayed home. He didn't want to see his brother's dead body. He didn't want to be around all the saddness. He had even told his girlfriend and long-time friend, Sarah Haplan not to come around. Not to speak to him. He was distraught. Just unable to move. He was incapable of forming the words to express his feelings. How much he hurt. He just couldn't. A lot of the family had arrived at the Dox home that morning. All of them were heading to the funeral home, where they would then follow the hearse to the cemetery. Marcus wasn't one of those people. He just sat in his room, on the floor, leaning up against his bed. With his legs bent up against his chest, he rested his chin on his knees. He was just staring blankly at the hardwood floor. There was a knock at the door, but he did not answer. His ears picked up the noise, but that had been his only movement. His eyes stayed forward with a blankness. Into the room walked his mother. Samantha Dox. She was dressed in a elogant lilac dress. She, too, was hurt. She had no tears though. Not yet. They would come when she saw her oldest son being put into the earth. 'Marcus,' she said softly. 'I understand you're not wanting to come. And I'm not going to force you to, nor am I going to tell you that you need to. Ever person grieves in their own way.' She put a hand on his hand and began to stroke his short hair. 'You've endured a lot over the years. More than any child should ever have to,' she continued. 'We've both lost a lot since you were born. Both of us have lost a part of ourselves, even, with those loses.' She then slid down onto the floor with her son and gave him a smile. She didn't turn his face to look at hers. He just did. Samantha went on, 'Do you know what keeps me from crying right now? Keeps me from freaking out and just throwing things around? Because it hurts so much. And I would very much like to break things.' That caused a slight smile from her son. That was warming to the woman's heart. Marcus then answered her, 'What is it?' She smiled again and ran a hand down the side of his face. 'You,' she said with all the love a person could. 'Knowing I still have you. Knowing I'm going to have you as my son for the rest of my life. That's what keeps me from doing those things. Keeps me sane.' She then leaned forward on her knees and kissed him on the forehead. 'I love you, Marcus,' she had said. Marcus looked back at her. 'I love you, too, mom.' There was a silence between them. A good silence though. After a few moments there was another knock at the door. A cousin or something. Marcus really didn't know. It was time to go. Samantha looked back at her son, smiling. 'When I get back, we'll do something together, okay?' He only nodded. Samantha then stood up and graefully walked from the room. It was time for her to be the mother to Charles. Pour every last ounce of love she had for him out as he ascended to whereever a person ascended after they had finished their life on Earth. Marcus sat there, thinking. Would he be her son for the rest of his life? How did she know she'd have him for the rest of her's? Why wouldn't he end up the others and be dead before they had lived the full extent of their lives? The thoughts puzzled him. But he dismissed them. Because he couldn't answer them. No one could. But that wasn't the point. His mother believed that they would have long lives ahead of them. That they wouldn't have to suffer through anymore losses of this nature. She believed it, with all her heart, and so did Marcus. She was going to be around forever.
