The Defining Moment: Part 2

11 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05/11/03 May 6th, 2003; 5:40 PM. 19 Days until Juggernaut II. Jeff watched out the window, following the bright yellow markings of the safety rail that traced the outside of the highway. The glass was speckled with mist from the afternoon rain that had since cleared up. Julie was in the driver's seat, glancing over at him every little while. She worried, after his match last night against Max Danger he hadn't been the same. He could barely walk, it just seemed as though he was in alot more pain then he was letting on. 'Are you sure you're alright? I can always cancel,' said Julie, turning her attention back to the baron road ahead of her. Destination; an upper-middle class community that only contained a hand full of homes. What Julie dubbed as 'the perfect place to start a new life'. The apartment they had been living in was okay, it was not as if they needed a whole lot of extra room, but Jeff had been expressing his annoyance with 'renting'. The fact that for the last three months she had been talking about how nice it would be to own their own house, always keeping it in the back of Jeff's mind, most likely helped the situation, too. Lately she had been taking it easy, staying off-camera as much as possible while urging Jeff to try and get out of his Asylum contract. If there was one thing she was sure about, it was that her husband, no matter how much of a coward he plays on television, does not like to quit something he has started. He also didn't like sharing his troubles with her. The news he had been given from his doctor was still shared by only him, and as long as he could keep it from her, that is how it would stay. Garvin reached for the bottle of mineral water sitting atop the glove compartment and took a clean swig to chase another pill that would help to ease the pain in his lower back, then nodded half-heartedly, 'I'm fine, Jewels. Just a little shaken up after last night.' Then grinned and took another drink. 'Alright, just...' She wanted to tell him her feelings, that he needed to take time off and heal, but she did not. For whatever reason, she couldn't get the words out. 'Just drive, Jewels, okay? I'm tired.' *** 'Five bed rooms, three bathrooms...' 'And a partridge in a pear tree?' A giggle escaped a delighted Julie Garvin. At five minutes after six o'clock they arrived at the first home they had circled in the newspaper; quiet, secluded, a fresh-water lake in the back. In alot of ways, as Jeff walked aimlessly around while his beauty of a wife continued to be 'wow'ed by the shmuck in the suite and tie, he saw a lot of similarities to the home he grew up in. The Garvin Estate; his uncle Ron's house that he once shared with him after the adoption. It was built in the 70's, a joint production between him and his father. The stories he had been told, clearly detailing how much enjoyment the two brothers got out of working together. They had always been close, and for the longest time Jeff felt guilty for shunning his uncle all these years- walking out of his life, his shadow, nearly one year ago. Then he remembered it had to be done. There was no way around it. It wasn't something the 21st Century Wrestling booking team came up with to help him gain 'heat' from an otherwise uncaring and silent crowd. Everything that transpired, it was as real as the floorboards onwhich he currently stood. Julie dashed over and grasped his arm, waking him from thought. 'The price is set, hun.' 'Oh yeah?' 'Two hundred thousand,' she said with her cat-like grip tightening. Garvin's eyes focused, though his head was still a hundred miles away, on the doorstep of his only living family member- begging for forgiveness. Whether it was the injury that humbled him, or if time had caused a change of heart, he couldn't be sure. He just felt like all the demons of the past needed to be put to rest. Then, with a gentle shake of his head, those thoughts were lost. And he smiled, though he didn't feel like smiling. And he hugged his wife close, those he could not feel her warm embrace. 'Let's do this.' 'Let's bury the past, and create the future.' If only he knew, that unlike the dead... untied issues do not rest in peace. Not for him. Not ever.
