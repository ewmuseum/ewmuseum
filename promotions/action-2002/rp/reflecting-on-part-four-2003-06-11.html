Reflecting On: Part Four

11 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06.11.2003 Butterflies filled his stomach. He was definitely not ready for this. He had had no time to prepare. Not time to think it through. It was all thrust upon him. What was he going to do? So many people were counting on him. He couldn't screw it up. He had to be alert, be ready. Fear flooded his insides. It was going to go horribly wrong. He just knew it. Everything was going to crumble. His life was going to be destroyed. He couldn't do it. He wouldn't do it. That's why he was screaming, 'NOOOOOOOO!' at the top of his lungs. He being Marcus Dox. It was the first day of kindergarten. 'NOOOOOOOO!' he screamed again as his mother tried forcing him into the room. All the other children, those who weren't big pussies, and their parents were looking at the doorway. The teacher, Ms. Helen, was trying to shush him just as Samantha was. The six year old Marcus Dox just didn't want to fuckin' go to school And who could blame him? He very much enjoyed playing with his toys and watching cartoons at home. With his mom. His father had past two years previous. It had been rough. But this was a new form of pain. At this point, more so to the young'un. Tears were streaming down his cheeks as Samantha tried calming him. She had that way about her, 'Marcus, honey, please quiet yourself. It's going to be okay.' Fuck no it wasn't! Didn't she remember her first day of school? It was brutal. An utter chaotic experience. Nearly traumatizing for many kids. She wrapped her arms around her son, hugging him. She had gone through this twice before. First with Charles and then with Amanda. Both of them had gone on to be real good about heading to school. Charles was nervous again, heading into high school, but still cool with it. Amanda just LOVED school. Marcus, though, wasn't enjoying it at first. Samantha said to her son, 'Marcus, this is going to be good. It's going to be fun. We've been over this already. You're going to be meeting new people. Making friends.' Fuck friends! He wanted TV, toys, and... COOKIES! Motherfuckin' cookies! 'No care!' screamed Marcus. Okay, yeah, he needed school and he needed it bad. What kind of way of speaking was that? 'No care'? Jesus. 'What did I tell you about poor grammar, son?' she scolded in her ever-soothing voice. 'Now, please, Marcus, just stop your crying and go inside. Everything is going to be fine.' A little girl, with curly blonde hair and bright blue eyes walked over. She had a cookie with her. A chocolate chip one. First off, why does she have a cookie in the morning? What's up with her parents? Anyway, she tapped Marcus on the shoulder. Slowly, with prodding from his mother('Look, Marcus, this young girl wants to talk to you.'), he turned around. Eyes red and watery with all the crying. 'Hi. My name's Sarah,' she said in her sweet, too-cute voice. 'What's your's?' Marcus looked the girl up and down. 'Yoink!' he said, as he took the cookie from her grasp and began to chomp down on it. The young Sarah looked at Marcus, horrified. Bottom lip quivering. Soon, she was in hysterics. Crying like a mad woman. Her mother came over. Samantha turned her son around, who was still biting on the cookie. 'What did you do that for?' Marcus shrugged. Samantha just shook her head and then said, 'Apologize to her.' Hell no. Stupid icky girl! No telling what he got just from breathing the same air as her much less eating a cookie she had been holding in her hands. Cooties were the most likely disease. After several LONG minutes of Samantha and Sarah's mother arguing with the six-year-old Marcus, he walked up to Sarah. 'I'm sowwy,' he said. 'It's okay,' she said with a smile. And then she kissed him on the cheek and ran off giggling. Marcus just stood there, face going red. Samantha kissed him on the forehead and said, 'I'll see you at three.' And she was gone. First day of class and the boy already had a girlfriend. Smooth operator that boy was. Marcus had turned around and saw his mother leaving. He simply waved. 'Bye,' he said to himself. 'I love you.'
