Drink Minimum

22 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 22, 2002 Television gives the illusion that any type of problem can be solved in a time period of less than an hour or in some cases, thirty minutes. You take a handful of popular television shows and something odd you will notice is that there is almost always an answer to an issue. Whether the problem involves teen pregnancy, teen violence, teen drinking, teen smoking or anything remotely related to a teenager, it's usually solved by the end of the program and is accompanied by a public service announcement with some quasi celebrity no one knows. Though for Simon Seaman, this problem didn't involve a teen nor did it include yet another adult telling teens what to do. It was a matter between friends that couldn't be solved in a hour. Not even if 'To Be Continued' was shown towards the end before the credits rolled. Simon had drove himself around and around for what seemed to be several hours until finally stopping at a bar to clear his mind. He wasn't in his usual positive mood, but he wasn't sulking either. 'Are you all right?' asked the female bartender across from him with a hint of sincerity in her voice. Simon stared up from the counter beyond several empty glasses situated in front of him. It wasn't like him to be in a such a mood, but this time was an exception. 'Me?' Simon answered. 'No, the other guy that drinks lemonade in a bar. Yes...you.' He took a drink from the glass and lazily replied, slightly uncertain of his own condition. 'I guess I'm fine.' The slender woman, dressed in a black tank top and leather skirt to match didn't buy into Simon's response. Simon too couldn't really believe himself. For in front of him was a female that, on any other night, would have been barraged with every lame pick up line and joke in the book of lame pick up lines and jokes from the master of lame pick up lines and jokes. Although, Simon wasn't up for it this time around. Probably for the first time in a long time. 'You guess? Game show contestants guess. Test takers guess too, but I'm sensing this doesn't involve a multiple choice question. Listen, there are only two kinds of people that come around here. People that drink to get drunk and people that drink to rid them of their troubles. Now which one are you?' she questioned. 'B?' Simon uttered nonchalantly before burying his face into his hands. His head quickly dropped to the counter as he body went limp. Suspicious as to what was happening, the bartender attempted to revive him and sought to get his attention by yelling into his ear. By doing so, she caught Simon off guard in a way that he almost fell off his seat. 'Seriously, what's up? I don't mind hearing you out. In fact, it's a prerequisite for bartenders. We listen to people's problems and try to give them advice. The kind of advice that can prevent a drunk individual from falling over and drowning in a pool of his or her own vomit.' 'That's comforting. Very comforting.' Simon remarked. 'I try my best. So what's the story, morning glory?' The blank look on Simon's face was priceless. It was a combination of shock and disgust with a dash of 'What in the hell?' thrown in for good measure. 'Here's the deal. If I tell you this, promise me that you'll never say that again.' he said before offering a handshake. The bartender shrugged her shoulders and replied. 'Depends on how good the story is.' With that said, Simon gathered his things and tried to leave, but his plans came to a halt as she called him back. 'Fine, fine, fine. I promise. There, are you happy?' In response to her question, he would down a whole glass of lemonade before letting that same glass drop to the floor, shattering into several pieces in the process. From there, he glared at her and answered with now practically everyone in the bar looking at him in a strange matter. 'Super...' Simon proclaimed. 'Observe as I give you an example. Let's call the man in question 'Person A' and the other 'Person B'.' She leaned over the counter and rested her hand on her cheek.. 'Definitely original. Move on.' 'So here is Person A. Some say he's a space cowboy and some claim he's the gangster of love. You know, the usual. Then there's Person B. He's not the man they think is at home. Oh no, no, no. If anything, he's a rocketman.' 'Elton John fan?' she inquired 'And Elton John would be...? Please, let me continue.' he stated. As she tried to say something, Simon interrupted and proceeded. 'Anyway, Person A is the kind of individual who would do anything for Person B. If Person B was ever in need, Person A would be there to help. Though, as of late, Person B has sort of been taking advantage that privilege doesn't even know it. Not only as of late, but when further investigated, it has been pretty much the whole time.' He paused for a moment before leaning in and whispering the remainder of his story. 'To present day, Person A wants to tear Person B a new 'A' and Person B looks as though he has Person A as his personal 'B'. You get what I'm saying?' Not knowing what to say or do, the bartender stuttered and said the first thing that came to mind. 'Oh, totally.' 'So what do you think Person A should do?' Simon asked. Checking if the coast was clear, she coaxed him closer to her until they saw eye to eye. 'I think Person A should stop hanging around here and face the problem head on.' replied the bartender. She pointed to the long line of glasses on the edge of the counter that garnered Simon's immediate attention. 'Person A should also pay for his drink.' About to reach for his wallet, he decided to grab his jacket and head on out. 'Um, yes. He should. Could you hold on one minute? Let me get him.' were words that came from his mouth before he sat up from his bar stool and calmly backed away. With every step, he glanced back at the bartender periodically while he slowly, but surely attempted to exit. 'Hey, Person A. Pay for your damn drink already! I'm sick of this.' he said out loud, appearing perplexed and in a state of panic trying to find the individual. On his way out, he backpedaled towards a group of people situated in a fairly large booth. As they stared at him, he stared right back and then pointed to the exit shaking his head in disgust. 'Can you believe that guy?!'
