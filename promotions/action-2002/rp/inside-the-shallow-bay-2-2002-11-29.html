Inside The Shallow Bay: 2

29 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
You see, James and Elayna Clark had children for most of a decade at roughly two years a clip. Thus, they produced three boys and one girl, all before they were married. Brandon was eleven when that happened, I was nine, my sister Alyson was seven, and the youngest kid of the coral, Skylar, was four years old. My parents never explained to me why they went off and had kids, a home, and all this other stuff before they were married. They had been seeing each other for thirteen years. If that isn’t a buffer for a relationship, then I don’t know what is. Let’s note that my parents were in their early thirties when they were married, and you have an idea. My father graduated high school, while my mother dropped out. Needless to say, my family is extremely different then any I have come across. Anyway, yeah, so there’s the background on my family. I was looking into the eyes of my sister for the first time since I left to go into military service. She was a doe eyed love of the age of sixteen at that time. It’s been over a decade, sadly. She was the only one that cried when I left that house for the last time. She was wearing a pair of low cut slightly faded jeans, a black tank top and sandals. Man, is she ever crazy to go outside in that garb in this cold. She reminded me of Christa Miller in appearance, but to this day I still remember that sweet little girl. Her hair was tied into a ponytail, and I could see the streaks of black. She was a natural redhead, and that is a plus, I guess. For what seemed like forever we stared at each other, losing ourselves in the sands of time. Would she remember me? Does she want me to come into her life right now? I don’t really know how she was going to react. That’s when she hugged me. I hugged back my leather trench coat making noises as I did. She rested her head on shoulder, and I could see a warm smile over her face. I looked through my glasses at her, and I could have died there. All that anxiety went away. “You’ve come back to us, haven’t you Rashard.” She said, playing with long white hair that protruded from my beanie. I couldn’t disappoint her. I couldn’t lie to her either though. So I remained silent. We stood there in that hug, letting the years melt away in a span of a minute. Nobody understands though. I would do anything for my kid sister. I was closer to her then anyone else. That’s why this hurt so much. “Come in, we were just watching the game.” She said, that smile beaming. “Mom and dad here?” “No. They’re still living in Arizona. They just hate the snow here.” So, after we exchanged words, she opened the door into the apartment, and I walked inside. I saw my older brother, Brandon, sitting on the couch watching the game. “JESUS CHRIST! You would think that they could get a better fucking coach! Mornhinweg is such a useless pile of…” “BRANDON! THE CHILDREN!” I would suppose that that was his wife. She had just turned around from stirring something on the stove to yell at Brandon. I found it quiet funny, my younger brother being that much smaller then me now. When I brought my foot inside the apartment, the people turned and looked at me. Brandon and his wife just stared a hole through me.
