Money in the Bank

18 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
With Pressure Point over and done with for another week, thank God, the Bantam Champion was left with not much to do... no more asses to be kicked, no more struts to be, uh, strutted, it was quite boring. Julie continued to bitch as she always did about cooking and how they needed to buy a new oven. Apparently it was the appliance’s fault she couldn't cook and she wanted him to shell out the dough. Ha! Yeah right. Garvin knew exactly how to fix this situation… he took her out for dinner. Not an expensive dinner, just... dinner. April 17th, 2003 Corello's Place 'Why did you take me here? All they serve is fish and something called 'Velt'... is that even a word?' Julie said as she skimmed the menu over in disgust. The restaurant Jeff had chosen was owned by Michael Corello; one of the few people that actually enjoyed Jeff's presence. He walked out from the back with a plate of glasses, one containing Coca Cola and the other mineral water. Corello had a thick, husky appearance. He wasn't clean-shaven, his hair was quite greasy, and he smelled of tuna fish. Jeff looked up, 'Ah! Mike!' 'JEFFERY! How's the wrasstlin'?' Garvin smiled and patted the silver championship belt that rested on his shoulder. He took it everywhere in fear that someone might break into his apartment and steal it. 'Ohhhhh, I see you gots' yourself a purdy belt there, huh?' 'Yessir, wasn't difficult, just walked in, chucked a couple douché bags around and walked back out... she's mine for life now, I love it,' he said, staring down lovingly at the dent that Sylo's skull had left. He had been meaning to get that fixed, but when you're a big shot like the Original, it's hard to find time to take care of such small things. Besides, he kind of liked it. It reminded him of his victories, as tainted as they may or may not have been. Julie scowled and cleared her throat. 'Excuse me?' 'Wha? WHA? YOU FART?' '...Noooo,' she spoke, rolling her eyes at the owner, 'I wanted to order.' 'Hey, hey, Jewels, we'll get to all that, I'm catchin' up with old pals here. I use to come in here every day after training with my uncle Ron and Mike would whip me up a plate of shrimp.' Corello laughed, blushing a little for he knew what a reputation he had for his fried shrimp. 'Eh, the kid loved the shrimp, what can a say?' 'I don't care. I just want to order. Can we ORDER?' '...Fine, I do have to get back, can't stay too late. I have a few phone calls to make to my boss, Reed Young. About new ‘Original’ merchandise I thought up while polishing the Bantam last night... Get this... And it'll blow whatever Keith has in store... THE GARVIN SHIRT *The shirt that rules all other shirts, making it so you don't need to buy anymore shirts 'cause you already are wearing the best shirt known to human kind as your stupid non-shirt wearing friends are reading this. Oh, and Garvin rules!* 'Wow. Makes me wanna' buy one... how much?' Garvin squinted, deep in thought. There were several things one had to weigh on when cementing a price for a new t-shirt. First: the expenses. Second: the demand. Third: desired profit. And finally, the most important... the buyers IQ. If it was low, Jeff could easily price it at WHATEVER he wanted. 'For you, Mikey? For you?' Garvin looked at the many food and liquor stains in his friend's white muscle shirt, noticing that it probably hadn't been washed in days. The business hadn't been doing well lately, but all Jeff saw was money... Money in the bank. Cha-CHING! And with that, his usual prick-like nature kicked in. '...You couldn't afford it. High-rollers only.' Corello swallowed hard. Then forced a response. 'I feel ill, if you don't mind, I'd like you both to leave. I'm closing for the night...' 'Oh, sorry, what was that? Saw a smudge on my belt, decided to give it a quick rub.' He glanced down to his watch and snapped his fingers, 'Shoot! We gotta' go, sorry... things to do, people to see. Come on, Jewels.' Garvin stood up from the table, pulling out a wad of bills that he kept deep in his pocket so to come off as a bigger man. He pulled out a twenty-dollar bill and Corello's face lit up. He thought this was Jeff's way of apologizing for the rude comment he made earlier. Had it REALLY been that long since he last saw him? '...A cola and water- that should be, what? Two bucks?' He placed the bills back and tossed his old friend the exact change. Tips were for suckers. 'Have a good one, Mike!' Corello watched as Jeff and his wife left his restaurant. 'Asshole.'
