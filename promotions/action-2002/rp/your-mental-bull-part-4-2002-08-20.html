Action! — Tales

Your mental bull - Part 4

20 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10th August 2002 31st July 2002, Pressure Point 23 Airborn Tyler vs Jonny Yankee If Airborn loses, he’s fired The fans slapping his back, hurling abuse at him, nothing seemed clear anymore. However, all of this paled into insignificance when his sight became clear as a crystal. In the distance, he could see something which he now held dear to his heart. His own person ratings hobby horse. The balcony. A renewed sense of purpose and enthusiasm burnt inside his heart, as Airborn grabbed Yankee by his tights and threw him over the security rail and onto the concrete floor which the fans viewed Pressure Point from. The security staff did a fine job of creating an alleyway for the wrestlers to fight through, and Airborn took full advantage by pummelling Yankee in the head with successive right and left jabs. When they finally arrived in front of the balcony, Airborn lifted his foot into the air and pointed to it. This was the set up move he had used on Bobby Minio to subdue him last week. Crescent kick! The kick connected with Jonny Yankee’s head, and he collapsed on the ground ala a sack of potatoes. ONE! TWO! Inside the ring, the referee had started his count, his hands raised high. Last time Airborn performed his stunt, a double count out was the result. Tonight, that wouldn’t be enough for him. He needed a decision. He needed a win. THREE! FOUR! Airborn scaled the steps of the balcony, and finally reached the top. As he stepped over the blue metal gate that stopped fans from falling, a familiar sight became apparent to him. The sea of fans with their arms held high in anticipation. The ring and the referee shouting at him to come down. The opponent, a sitting duck waiting to be shot down. FIVE! SIX! Airborn pushed off through his legs, and started the front flip. The height he got was unbelievable, and as he flew through the air, he couldn’t hear the crazed cries of the fans, or see the camera bulbs flashing once more. Everything was silent. To most his body appeared to be in perfect synchronisation, as it did to Jonny Yankee who was back on his feet. As he looked up, he saw the cruiserweight dawning on him. “SHIT!” “SHIT!” The first expletive came from Jonny Yankee, as he flung his prone body desperately out of the way. The second came from Airborn Tyler, a second before HIS prone body smacked the concrete ground. “HOLY SHIT! HOLY SHIT! HOLY SHIT!” Just like last week the audience’s chant ran for a good forty seconds. Unlike last week, Airborn Tyler hadn’t hit his shooting star press off the 15ft balcony. This week, he had missed it, and fallen hopelessly onto the unforgiving and painful concrete. SEVEN! EIGHT! Jonny Yankee sprinted through the crowd, scuttling as fast as his legs would carry him, trying to beat the referee’s ten count to gain a win. Airborn tried to move, but he couldn’t. His body was frozen, wrapped in a wall of pain and suffering. He couldn’t feel anything had broken, but the smack as he’d hit the ground was something indescribable. Tears running out of his eyes, Airborn lay on the ground; to the idle onlooker he was dead. NINE! Jonny Yankee hurdled the security guard, and dove at the ring, his arms stretched out in front of him. Slipping all over the place, he grabbed the ring apron... And pulled himself back onto the canvas. TEN! DING DING! The referee completed his ten count, and the time keeper ran the bell. As Airborn Tyler lay face up in the crowd, crying after jumping fifteen onto solid concrete floor, his worst nightmares were confirmed. He couldn’t move, he was in the most pain he’d ever imagined, and the ring announcer’s voice pierced the frantic atmosphere like the sharpest needle. “The winner of this Match... JONNY YANKEE!” EMT’s rushed to Airborn’s aid. He was in serious trouble, and urgently required medical help. Another hospital visit loomed large, and it might be longer than one night this time. “THIS CANNOT BE HAPPENING TO ME! FOR THE LOVE OF GOD, WHY ME!?!?” 8th August 2002, McDonalds: Chicago Branch Benjamin Tyler stumbled out of the taxi, handing the driver a ten dollar bill as he did so. Looking up at the building, it certainly looked daunting. Thirty floors of offices with people slaving away over minuscule desks, humans studying new recipes like programmed robots. These workers performed the same dull tasks day after day of their pathetic lives, and it wasn’t a task Benjamin was looking forward to. He hadn’t had an honest day’s work in his life. Sure, the wrestling world was taxing, driving up and down the country, training in the gym, but he’d never had to concentrate on one task for eight hours before. That’s if he got the job of course. With a steep intake of oxygen, Ben stepped through the revolving doors and into the front hall, which was more than impressive. Vast in it’s gigantism, magnificent with it’s marble pillars, spectacularly busy with suits dashing around, piles of folders in their hands. Adjusting his tie, Ben walked up to the front desk. Slightly over-awed by the surroundings, he could only cough gently to attract the receptionist’s attention. As she raised her head and moved her glasses slightly down her nose to look at the young man in front of her, the brown eyes engraved on her face struck Ben as piercing, and daunting. In wrestling he’d fought many big men, often a whole foot taller than he, but the trepidation he now felt was different. A whole new world was unfolding before him. “Hello, welcome to the Chicago HQ for McDonalds Incorporated, how may I help you?” enquired the receptionist in a friendly tone. Ben just gawped though. To him the words were sarcastic and mocking. For a second he thought that perhaps these emotions had surfaced to his face. Did his eyes look scared? Apparently not, as the brunette woman carried on smiling, waiting for his reply. Removing a piece of paper from his insider blazer pocket, Ben tried to re-gather his bearings. “Ah, yes, I’m here to see a Mr.Bedford.” “Do you have an appointment?” The receptionist leant forward as she spoke, and Ben took a step back. The question seemed very accusing to him, as if trust was not something held towards him. “I do.” “When for?” Slightly hot under the collar, Ben tugged at his shirt. Communication skills were never really something he’d encountered in wrestling, at least not when the balance of power was with the opposed. Looking down at his paper, he replied. “9:45am.” “OK, please take a seat and we’ll send you up in a minute.” “Thank you,” mumbled Ben, before sitting down in the seats pointed out to him. His first ever job interview. One had never been needed for wrestling, but now he was going to embark on a whole new adventure. Or was he? Damn that Jonny Yankee. All Ben had been trying to do was produce ratings and keep his job, but Yankee had to be smart and move out the way. Ben might have suffered cracked ribs, but he’d suffered most from losing his job. If only he could get revenge. But he was out of Action!. No way to get revenge was forthcoming. Now to concentrate on the darned job interview.
