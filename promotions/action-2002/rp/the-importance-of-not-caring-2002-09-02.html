The importance of not caring.

2 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09.02.02 Dear Journal, I don't care. It's not like I didn't want to win. I got my shots in, I grabbed a few cards, I put forth a certain amount of effort. But in the end, win or lose... I'm still Steven Denton. I've lost nothing and only gained more attention and TV time. It also helps that he beat me at his game, rather than him beating me at mine. I lost a 'Card Scramble Match'. To me, this means nothing. If it had been a straight up, standard wrestling match, I am very sure I would've won. If he pinned me in a wrestling match, if he beat me at my game, then I would care. But he didn't beat me at my game, he beat me at his... and that only makes sense, seeing as it's his game and all. It would've been nice to crush him at his own game and stand tall at the end. But even then, beating Christopher Fox is no big accomplishment, and in a Card Scramble Match no less. I'm glad I lost, in fact. I'd hate to have to admit one day that I'm better than someone else at finding cards. How embarrassing. All in all, it helps not to care. I've lost no self confidence. I still know I'm better than him at anything that doesn't involve Pokemon. He's no real competitor. He's no 'wrestler'. He has a gimmick that strangely gets over with the audience. His absurdly high voice, boyish antics, and downright idiotic sense of humor have somehow helped him get to the top. But he has no real ability in the business he competes in. He'll never win a match that isn't stipulated, and no one will ever pop for his wrestling. So, really, I don't care. I used him to get on TV more and push myself up the card. I'm happy with that, so the loss is no big deal. The day I care about losing a Card Scramble Match is the day I masturbate in someone else's car. In other words, never. --- Steven stood up from his desk. He reached his hands above his head, hooked them together, and stretched his back out while yawning rather loudly. He then moved back to his couch, and sat down, using the remote to unpause the tape he had in the VCR. As the screen came back to life, it was that of Joey Malone in mid-match, wrestling whoever. Steven watched rather closely, until I knock came on his door. 'Yeah?' Steven called, opting not to move. 'You still leaving in the same shitty apartment?' Steven smiled. The voice of his sister was like that of a god's to him after the week he'd had. He rose from his seat, quickly moving to and unlocking the door. He swung it open and was greeted with a smile. 'How've you been?' she asked, moving past him and dropping her bag onto the ground next to his couch. She then removed his jacket, placed in on the arm, and sat. Steven moved towards the kitchen. 'Eh, I've been better,' he responded, 'You?' 'Oh, I'm good. Trey and I just moved into a new townhouse in Springfield. It's real nice, a lot better than our old apartment.' 'So, why aren't you there instead of here in my shitty place?' 'Well, I saw that last episode of, uhm... god, what's it called?' 'Pressure Point.' 'Yeah, that one. Well, I saw it last yesterday. I know it's not really, well... I mean, you know what I mean. But when you got caught in the support cable in midair I could tell that wasn't supposed to happen. So I figured I'd take the few hour drive and see how you were.' Steven, meanwhile, had poured them both a little wine. He always had a bottle or two for special occasion. 'Wine?' 'Sure.' Steven sat down next to Sarah, handing her a glass. They each took a sip, enjoying it's taste. '...so, are you alright?' The younger sibling touched the long gash that spread across his neck. 'I've had worse. Just the gash and a minor concussion. Nothing you should worry about,' Steven spoke rather quietly. The pain in his head sharpened for a moment. 'If you say so...' she noticed the uncomfortable look on his face, and quickly changed the subject. 'Hmm... whatcha watchin'?' 'Just some wrestling tapes. No one you know,' he took this opportunity to direct his attention back to the screen. 'Well, I do recognize that guy with the blonde hair. He's in Pressure Point Action too, right?' 'Something like that,' he smiled. The pain in his head lightened a bit. They watched the tape together, and it wasn't long before Steven fell fast asleep. The combination of pain and not caring had tired him out a good deal, and he was happy to get a little rest. Sarah stayed the night, watching the tape that played with a bit of interest. She noticed that every match on the tape featured the same man. Placed on top of the VCR, she noticed, were two other tapes, both with the man's name printed on the label. She was curious to say the least, and couldn't help but wonder why Steven was paying such close attention to... Joey Malone.
