The Inevitable Return Of The Great White Dope

16 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Joe Black sat on his couch, a beer in hand. He was watching `Friends’ which was perhaps his favorite television show. Joe could tell you everything you wanted to know about Rachel and Pheobe, but he couldn’t really remember the names of the rest of the cast so well. It was his favorite show, but he honestly had to admit that he didn’t really like it that much. Joe continued to watch the show because he really hoped to catch an episode where Rachell and Pheobe have sex accidentally. He thought that would be great television. He also didn’t really like Buffy the Vampire Slayer that much, but he watched anyway because he loved to see Sarah Michelle Geller get her ass kicked. It happened rarely. He was often disappointed. But anyway. His phone rang. Joe groaned and got to his feet and walked over to the phone. He turned on speakerphone because the handset to his cordless phone needed a new battery. The damn thing would power out after two minutes regardless of how long he left it on the charger. It’d been this way for about three months. Joe said, “Hello?” It was Joe’s brother Ron. Ron was the head trainer at Black and Black Institude of Technical Wrestling. Ron said, “Joe. You need to turn on the television.” Joe walked back to the couch and sat down before shouting, “No Ron, I’m not turning the television on because it’s already on. And I’m not changing the channel either so don’t ask.” Ron said, “Will you get a new damn phone?” Joe said, “Sure. Later.” Ron sighed, “God, anyway, you need to turn on Action.” Joe said, “The one with the exclamation mark at the end?” Ron said, “Yeah that one. Turn it on.” Joe said, “No. I hate that show.” Ron said, “You hate every show. Now turn it on. One of ours is on tv.” Joe said, “Oooh!” and picked up the remote and flipped over to Action! Joe tuned into the last third of the Rana Venenosa & Duel. Joe said, “Ronnie, you idiot! That’s not one of ours! That’s the crazy chump Alex from evo. Who cares about him?” Ron said, “No, Joe, I meant the one in the mask.” Joe squinted as he watched Rana Venenosa wrestle. There was something familiar there. Joe watched as Rana Venenosa executed a double arm belly to belly overhead suplex. Joe said, “That’s my move.” Joe groaned as Duel bridged out of the pin and into the cross face chickenwing which was apparently enough for the submission. Joe watched as the mask was removed to reveal Rook Black. His son. Ron’s voice echoed inside of Joe’s living room, “Now you see?” Joe ignored Ronnie, he leaned toward the television as Rook started to headbutt Duel repeatedly. Joe said, “So, it’s you.” == The Sisters of the Incarnate Word are impelled to lead a life which fosters an imitation of the life of the Incarnate Word. This is especially seen as we live the virtues of the Incarnate Word: humility, simplicity, charity. The Incarnate Word of course, being the Messiah of the Roman Catholic Faith, Jesus Christ, Son of God, “The Word Made Flesh”. Sister Rebecca Grace thought this over briefly as she glanced around the living room of the Sister’s Residency in attachment to Incarnate Word Academy. Right now her and 5 other of her sisters were watching professional wrestling. And it was all Sister Rebecca’s fault. Sister Rebecca considered herself a mark. But these girls took the cake. She loved professional wrestling and regularly confessed that she sometimes felt a little guilty about it. She had been met with some opposition with regard to watching in the common room initially. But she had made some converts. Jesus, forgive her for the irony. But they loved the television show. Sister Rebecca reluctantly admitted that a few of the girls might possibly love to watch grown men running around in their underwear misbehaving. But that was between them God, and their confessor. The major difference was the Sister Rebecca loved the wrestling. And somehow, while it felt wonderful to share her obsession with her sisters, they just didn’t seem to be watching the same show as her. And this eventually drew away some of the enjoyment. So this was why Sister Rebecca wasn’t really paying attention to Action!’s Pressure Point (which the other sisters who had become fans called “PeePee” with giggling delight that tended to infuriate her) and was staring into space more or less in contemplation. Sister Marie Ambrose asked, “Sister Rebecca, who is Rook Black?” Sister Rebecca smiled and said, “He’s one of my favorites. He’s a penitent soul coping with his atheism. His style was a brilliant technical display that was fantastic to watch. But more importantly, his character reminded me of the struggle of Immanuel Kant. The idea that you are not to attempt please God by desiring a reward, but to Act as your conscience dictates without seeking a reward in heaven. That way, if you act well you are more likely to be worthy of eternal reward. He’s a troubling character; he worries me, which I suppose is why I liked him so much. Unfortunately, his hand was crushed and he hasn’t been seen since.” Sister Mary Taylor said, “Well, you’ll like this then. It turns out that Rana Venenosa was Rook Black, and he’s apparently hitting Duel over and over again with his head.” Sister Rebecca’s head snapped around to regard the television, watching Rook unload headbutts onto Duel before Frank Heimdal dragged him away. Sister Rebecca quietly thanked Jesus Christ, she was sure he found her wrestling obsession amusing, and He had perhaps smiled on her just a little. Sister Rebecca leaned forward in her chair, watching the screen intently as it focused on Rook’s bloody face. She said, “So, it’s you.” == Sickness walked along the hallway in the building she liked to call, “her complex”. She was wearing a black slinky dress, and her long red hair trailed behind her. Her little empire of followers was growing steadily. It was nearly an institution now, and it was growing under its own head of steam without much further prodding by her will. The building housed her dance club, “Shivering”, which was continuing to pour in money hand over fist. She was now rotating among her attendants the more talented and powerful men and women who wanted her, wanted to be her, and wanted to be her favorite. It amused her. Sickness entered the actual club premises of Shivering, and lifted a cigarette to her lips. A feisty looking Goth chick with pink hair in pigtails had lit her cigarette before any of the others could. The pink one must’ve been waiting for this opportunity. Sickness gave the pink goth a smile, which might rocket her upward in social status before being struck down by the glass ceiling or Sickness’ more regular groupies. Sick approached her area of the club, and the throne was hastily vacated for her to sit in. Tonight Sickness was going to dance, have a little wine, and take two young and pretty things to bed. She wasn’t certain about gender, but she could easily begin by process of elimination determining who wasn’t going to be sleeping with her. Not that there was much that Sickness was actually going to do. But by bringing two, it allowed her a little bit of fun, and when it came down to the dirty end of things, she’d merely watch the two please each other. It was safer that way, in more ways than just the physical. She regarded the television monitors in the club. For some reason she still found herself watching the wrestling shows. Since her last dalliance with that particular business, she had decided she never wanted to be part of it again. But that particular enigma of entertainment always had a way of bringing you back around for more, not unlike a lover that you absolutely hate. Sickness watched, her attention wavering through several shows before something caught her eye on Action!. Sickness’s mouth fell open and her breathing quickened as she watched Rana Venenosa wrestle Duel. She knew. She remained transfixed as her cigarette slowly burned down to the filter. The unmasking confirmed it. It was Rook, her former lover back in a day when they both were younger and less than they were now. She smirked considering how much her life had accelerated towards her goals and desires, while he still seemed to be doing the same exact thing. Of course, getting his hand crushed was one hell of a set back. But on the other hand, her nose had been smashed and she had received a concussion that last time she had been in the ring. She absently touched her face, where surgery had not only restored her look by augmented it. She watched Rook as he made a singular final effort to run back to the ring before his friend stopped him. She regarded his scarred face, a delight to the eye. It angered her. One of her favorite things was lightly touching Rook’s scarred body while he slept. She had never touched the newer scars that spilled out of his eyes like tears. Her mind reminisced over the delights of the flesh that she had shared with his virgin’s body. She was his first, and she smirked, feeling certain that her talent and experience had probably made a second liaison with anyone disastrously disappointing. She looked at him as he faded from view, and thought to herself, that not once when the mask had come off did he make an expression of anger, just determination. She found herself regarding the feelings of change in her mood, it looks like tonight she’d prefer to go to bed alone. Sickness left the dance club by an interior door, disappointing the crowd that her departure was premature. She entered an elevator and went upstairs into her private rooms. She entered a single room that seemed to just be for storage purposes. She opened a cardboard box and began looking through old pictures. She found ones of Rook and herself, sometimes they were explicit photos, them playing a variety of the many games of her devising, sometimes casual photos. Sickness discovered a photo of herself and Rook that she had almost forgotten. She was leaning against his shoulder crying. As the moment came back to her, the flood of feelings carried her away from the present to that moment in time, and she regretted that sometimes she just wanted someone that would hold her as opposed to offer her obedience, worship, and tribute. She started to cry a little, and immediately grew furious with herself for feeling this way. She dropped the photos back into the box, dropping the one that had moved her last. She looked at that photo once more before closing the lid. As she turned away seeking desperately seeking something to distract her, she muttered under her breath, “So. It’s you.” == Frank Heimdal was driving Rook’s Mustang GT back home. Rook was in the passenger seat, icing both his hand and his busted eyebrow. Frank said, “How do you think it went?” Rook was staring out the windshield at the oncoming road, “I don’t know. Good I guess. I’m still feeling like I’m still there in a way.” Frank said, “In the ring still?” Rook nodded, “Yeah.” Frank said, “Well, you’ve left a part of yourself in that ring tonight.” Rook smiled, “Yeah I suppose I did. And I’ll tell you what, Frylock, I don’t think I miss it now that it’s gone.” Frank laughed as they pulled into the drive way, “We’re home, Shake-zula,” he said as he popped the trunk open. They exited the vehicle, took their bags, and walked up the driveway to the Heimdal House for Wayward Wrestlers (it's not actually called that, they just call it that). Livvie Heimdal answered the door and greeted her older brother with a big hug. Frank entered the house and was followed by Rook. Olivia smiled at Rook, “So, it’s you.” Rook smiled back, happy to see her, “Yeah. It’s me.”
