Date: 08.05.02 misanthropy A calm, surprisingly cool Chicago summer morning. A few cars whizzed by on the road, most heading to their places of work. Next to no people strode the sidewalks, but that was mostly due to it being 7 AM. A few shop and store owners turned their lights on, and flipped the open/close sign so that it read they were prepared for business in this commercial area of town. A few more cars whizzed by, all of their drivers taking the time to glance quickly at the only pedestrian on the sidewalks they had seen all morning. This particular pedestrian wore sweat pants and a wife-beater style undershirt, with his sweat shirt tied neatly around his waist. His eyes were a very odd shade of blue, standing out brightly against the still dim Chicago morning. He was jogging healthily, rather than walking like the ordinary person at this time of morning would. He turned a corner, nearly breaking into a run as the street and sidewalks sloped downward and bit. After he reached the bottom, he continued his pace, heading towards a YMCA that was now only a few blocks away from him. After a couple more minutes, the semi-well built man jogged through the automatic front-doors of the YMCA. Panting rather loudly, he nodded to the lady at the front desk, who waved back conspicuously. Finally he stopped jogging, slowing down briefly to produce his card from his pants pocket and swipe it through the slot which opened the next door. He entered through it, now in a slow jog. After a short time, he reached the weight room, where the door was already propped open. 'Stevie!' yelled a different man, who smiled wide for the younger person entering the room. 'I'm Steven,' he fired back. He didn't let many people get away with calling him Stevie, and this guy was no different. 'Yeah, yeah. Hey I saw that last Pressure Point show, pretty entertaining stuff...' Steven could feel it coming, '...especially when you reached into that duffle bag! HAHAHAHAHA!' This particular fellow-weight lifter liked to joke around with Steven. He didn't let many people get away with that, either, and this guy was no different. 'Remember what I said last week?' Steven eyed the other man. 'No.' 'Let me refresh your obviously feeble memory: shut the fuck up,' Steven said, almost letting his mouth curl into a smile. 'Hey, I'm just joking with you-' 'That's the problem.' 'Hey, man, maybe if you just loosened up a little bit. I don't like that little annoying Fox kid either, but I bet if you just put up with him a little bit you wouldn't have... uh... 'ice cream' in your duffle bag,' he suggested to Steven. 'Put up with him? I'm having enough trouble putting up with you,' Steven undid his sweat shirt from his waist, and tossed it onto a nearby table. First things first, he began to stretch. 'I'm just making conversation-' 'Exactly.' Following this, the other man sighed and returned to his weightlifting. Steven, meanwhile, performed a few more stretches, before walking across the room, and setting the weights to what he wanted on the bench press. He quickly lied down, confident in himself enough that he didn't need someone to spot him. At first rather slowly, Steven began pumping iron. He increased his pace slightly every few seconds, working his muscles nicely. As he worked, Steven let his mind dance around, thinking about The Risk, Christopher Fox, the stove he might've left on at home, and his headache, which he'd seemingly had for months now... ever since he began wrestling in Action! 'Mister Denton?' Steven's eyes shot to the left. The voice, while welcoming, shot his concentration, causing him to juggle the bar for a moment. Finally, he was able to lift upwards and set it down. He quickly sat up on the bench, eyeing the lady who stood in front of him. She wore a YMCA t-shirt indicating that she probably worked there. 'Um... you're the guy who wrestles, right?' 'Yeah, what is it to you?' 'Well, my little brother's a big fan, and he just wanted me to get you to sign this,' she handed him a picture, which was obviously produced by Action! On the picture, Denton was seen dumping Joey Malone with a Tiger Driver '91. He smirked, admiring the picture for a moment. 'Well... do you think you could?' 'Maybe later. I'm busy,' Steven said, a semi-commanding tone in his voice. He lay back, and reached up to grab the bar again, when she spoke up once more. 'But, it's just a quick signature. I don't want to have to bother you again later,' she smiled, though it was a smile Steven would never see. He stared directly upwards, his hands firmly wrapped around the bar. After an instant, he lifted the weight from its place, and began lifting once more. The young girl sighed with dismay, and turned to walk away. A lot of people just assume Steven Denton is an evil, bothered man with no sense of humor or a bit of kindness in him. But he's not. He's a gentle man, filled with guilt over many things that have happened throughout his life. Normally he would put these things behind him. He is a man, and he has a future. But now, however, Steven Denton had one problem: A headache.
