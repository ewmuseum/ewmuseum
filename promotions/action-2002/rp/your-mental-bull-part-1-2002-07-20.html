Your mental bull - Part 1

20 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 20th July 2002 Airborn Tyler was walking around backstage at the 17th July edition of Pressure Point. Gloomily, he stared up at the white board, taking in the details, or rather, the lack of them. He had been booked for no segments and no matches, a stark contrast to the previous week where he had two segments with some little kids, and a match where he defeated Angelica Dawson. Had the focus group not found his input impressive enough? Throughout his life people hadn't found him impressive. 'Not anything new' I here you say, and I guess it isn't. Every nook and cranny of wrestling these days has a warrior with a sob story to tell, and inevitably the warrior fights back and conquers his demons, claiming his past inspired him to glory. Well, Airborn Tyler isn't like that. As Airborn admired the mosaic floor of the mass locker room, he wasn't thinking about ambushing someone to make a point. He wasn't about hurtle down to the ring, crack someone's skull with a steel chair then make a five minute long damning of the audience. He wasn't even about to go and moan to management. Instead, he did something that wrestlers are almost immune from these days. He cried. This 18 year old kid, just four days after his birthday, was standing in the middle of a locker room with grown men around him, and salt water dripped off his face and onto a puddle on the tiled floor. His black 'I'M AIRBORN!' t-shirt showed off the few muscles he had, and demonstrated his fragile physique. The t-shirt contained his conceited smile just below the text, and his black jeans had a silver belt around the waste. Adjusting the belt slightly, Airborn wiped the tears off his face with his forearm, before picking his belongings up off the oak bench. Face held low, he just walked out, leaving everyone else in the locker room to look on in amazement. This kid wanted to be a wrestler? Boy, did he have a lot to learn!
