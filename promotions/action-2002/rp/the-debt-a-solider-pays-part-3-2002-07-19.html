The debt a solider pays (part 3)

19 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 19, 2002 The battle had gone on for an amount of days that wasn’t unheard of, but an amount of days that was still rough on even the strongest man’s spirit. Sure enough, it had taken its toll on the General. His troops had won the battle, but he was nearly paralyzed by the raging sore that would ejaculate from his joints every time he moved. The simplest motion would send a myriad of pain pulsing through his body. He couldn’t let this pain show though. His soldiers relied on him to set an example. The General stood up from the slight comfort of his cot, grimaced at the pain, and walked out of his tent into the lively atmosphere of the Union Campgrounds. “Hello sir!” A young man greeted the General. “Good evening soldier,” the General replied, “I do very much think we fought a great battle these past few days, don’t you?” “Yessir.” The soldier responded with a smile. The General nodded with a slight smile, and continued walking. He came across one of his youngest troops, also one of his favorites. “Jonny…er…General!” “Jonny is fine young’un.” The General grinned. The soldier moved quickly behind the General and got him in a headlock. The General could have easily screamed in pain, but he had to keep himself from doing so for two reasons. He liked this kid too much to yell at him, this was the son he would never have. Also, if he screaming due to the agony he was in, his soldiers would know that he gave into pain…then they would do the same thing sometime down the line. Besides, this was just another challenge to the General. He used to be known for wrestling bears and coming out alive. His soldiers always grappled with him to see if they could come out on top. They very rarely did. The General waited for an opening, he caught a glance of the soldier’s leg out of the corner of the eye and he accepted the opportunity. He grabbed the young man’s leg, and fell backwards landing on the soldier. The boy relinquished his hold, and the men shared laughter. The harmony of good cheer and laughter reminded the General why he enjoyed this life so much, and why it was the only way he was willing to spend his life. Nothing would ever take this away from him.
