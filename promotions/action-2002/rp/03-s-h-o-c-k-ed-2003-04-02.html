Action! — Tales

03: S.H.O.C.K.'ed

2 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The Offering Quinton eventually returned to the hotel room, and found repose by reading the newspaper on his bed. He had calmed down a great deal since coming back, and Wendal received a slight apology with Quint's return. But since his return, Wendal had found himself victim of horrific boredom. He began to shift positions on the bed in an attempt to achieve comfort, but comfort was something he felt he could never find. He sat up and fidgeted uncontrollably. The bed squeaked in the process, which made Quinton quite annoyed. He let it slide at first and focussed more intensely on the paper; hoping he could vanquish the annoyance. 'In your lifetime, have you ever sat still for more than a minute?' 'Not often,' was Wendal's response as he stopped moving. 'Maybe it's because my stomach is growling. I'm very hungry--' 'I packed some food in my bag, feel free to take some,' Quinton offered politely. 'Really? Thanks a lot, Quinton,' Wendal replied, quite surprised at the gesture. He got up from the bed and walked to where Quinton's bag laid. He unzipped the bag and shoved a probing hand into it. A short time later, he produced a bag of chips. He returned to his bed, and noisily opened the top. Quinton instantly realized his bad decision. He just gave Wendal justification for opening a pandora's box of noise: bag rustling, chip crunching, finger sucking. The mayhem was almost too much for him to bear. As he listened to Wendal crunch away, he realized that he, too, was quite famished. For the first time in ten minutes, Quinton lowered his paper and tossed it aside. He looked longingly at the bag of chips. Wendal noticed. He held up the bag and asked: '---Hungry?' Was he ever. As Quinton got up and snatched the bag, he realized something. Sure, he was physically hungry...but his hunger couldn't be confined to just physical nourishment. He was hungry for something more; He craved a distant something he couldn't place a finger on. Nevertheless, he devoured a handful a chips quickly, as Wendal rubbed his worried brow. 'You know, I've been thinking...' 'When aren't you thinking?' Quinton murmured from a mouth stuffed with food. 'Don't get angry for what I am about to say. I don't mean anything bad by this, but what do you really have to offer Action? Sure, you got called back for an interview...but where's your gimmick? All I see is a person who's gimmick and real personality are the exact same. No offence, but I think that's pretty weak...and boring.' On an irritable night like this, Quinton could have blown up with the criticism. But he didn't. He started to act the way he always would, before this demanding day of unexcitement reared its ugly head at him. He nodded, but his countenance bore a very blank response. 'Maybe you're right, Wendal. But what can I do about it?' 'That's what I've been thinking about all day in the car. I wasn't just counting license plates and annoying you to death.' Wendal explained to the smirking face of Quinton. 'The only thing I can really conclude is that you have no passion. No passion for life, or wrestling.' Wendal was right. He had struck the proverbial nail right on the head. At the end of the day, Wendal was starting to make sense. Quinton lost passion for life after he lost one of his dearest sight devices: his perception of color. He didn't expect to wrestle, but enjoyed it once he got involved. The fame and money was extremely good to him, but he recalled getting discouraged after his oAw release. After all, it wasn't his fault. Quinton was exasperated, and plopped down onto the edge of his bed. 'You're right. Thanks alot, Wendal. Now you have me scared,' Quinton mumbled uneasily. 'Maybe some fear would do you some good, Quint. Go, take a shower... we both know you need it,' Wendal spouted good-naturedly as he fanned the air in front of his nose. He became serious once more. 'Just think about it. We'll talk in a bit, and sort out this mess.' [S.H.O.'ed is complete.]
