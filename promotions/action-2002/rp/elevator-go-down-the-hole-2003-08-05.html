Elevator Go Down The Hole.

5 August 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Jessica Michelle Dox had gotten to the elevators first. She pressed the up button, just as her husband, Marcus Alexander Dox, had said, 'Congrats again, man. We'll see you around,' and pulled his head from an open doorway. He walked the short distance to his wife, and the two locked hands as they waited for one of the lifts to arrive. They, like most of the Action! Superstars were staying at the Ambassador Hotel, Detroit for Monday's Pressure Point taping. As the two waited there, Xander put his free hand into his khaki pant's pocket. He began to tetter on his heels and toes attempting to whistle. For the entirety of his life, he had been unable to whistle. He just wasn't sure why that was. And then Jessica began to whistle the Hawaii Five-O theme. Seriously. Xander looked straight ahead as he said, 'Show off.' She laughed. She always enjoyed making her hubby jealous. All in good fun, of course. She then tip-toed and kissed him on the cheek, 'Oh, don't worry, hun, I'm sure, one day, you'll be able to whistle better than the lot of 'em.' 'Darn skippy, I will,' he remarked. He was slightly on the competitive side when there was something he just couldn't do. It bugged him. More than he would like. And when he couldn't do something, he tried and tired until he could. So he stood there, trying to whistle. What was the deal? Why could he not do it? Was the technique all wrong? Was he supposed to twirl is tongue or something? Ugh. Thankfully Jessica was there to distract himself from breaking stuff over not being able to whistle. 'Do you think we've been neglecting Bradley?' Wow! So not want Xander wanted to talk about. Especially since he had skipped out on the Lousiana trip and one of Jessica's shirts came up missing. He wasn't liking what he was feeling when the subject of his former tag team partner was brought up. Almost made him sick to think of what Duncan was doing. Xander didn't say anything. He just looked over at his wife, who's face was hidden by her hair. She then looked up, pulling hair behind her left ear with her free right hand. 'I think that may be what his problem is, you know. I mean, just like now, we were in there talking with Conrad, right?' Xander just nodded. They had been. The old man had won a nice, shiny piece of gold from Spinebuster Wrestling. The World Championship, in fact. And it was something Bradley was supposed to have been competing for, but he had opted to no-show. And it was Jessica's idea to spend so much time with Conrad. She thought it would help him. Would be nice for him to have some friends, especially those close to Samantha. And what was closer than her son and his wife? Exactly. And, turned out, the old bastard wasn't such a bad guy. Okay, sure, major generation gap, but he seemed to be one of the few elder people who weren't pissing their pants and talking incoherently about 'in his day'. So... it was nice. 'After your mom died, we started to spend more and more time with Conrad. Which as been good, I think, for all of us. But Bradley was never asked to be apart of all that. He was pushed aside. Out into the cold, like. And I think, that's what's been his problem.' Jessica looked into her husband's light green eyes (they turned green in darker areas and blue in lighter, but sometimes, just gray), and he stared back into her baby blues. He shrugged, 'So, basically, he's just feeling left out?' She nodded, 'That's exactly what I'm thinking. So, maybe, should we try and hang out with him more? Maybe you could finally learn sign language. It would mean a great deal to him, I'd think.' Notice how pointedly she said 'finally'? Yeah, Xander had brushed off every attempt on her part to get him to learn. Even back in Toronto, when he was working at the wrestling school that he graduated from... where he met Bradley and trained him, he didn't learn. They just were able to communicate... in a special way. Just natural chemistry together. You can see why they became a team then. But he never learned. Not when they teamed up on the big scene in PIW. Never. Didn't even give it much thought except for when Jess would bring it up. But he never did. Just, over time, forgot all about Duncan's issues. Fact of life, people grow apart. And now, with Jessica taking the position of Duncan feeling left out, he was having mixed emotions. Oh, he cared for Bradley. He was a very good friend to him. All he could imagine was Duncan standing out in the Canadian tunrda, with a sign, 'Need friends... oh, and food and shelter and stuff.' And he didn't want that for Bradley. Then again... this seemingly weird fixation with his wife. Just kind of made Marcus want to hit him in the nose. With a shovel. He then took a slightly deeper breath than he had expected, 'I suppose.' There was something about that answer that Jessica didn't like. There was something else behind the exterior that she didn't know, and it was something her husband was keeping from her. She could sense it, in that deep breath or sigh or whatever. She looked at him with narrowed eyes, trying to pick up on it, but Xander was staring straight ahead. What was it he was keeping from her, she wondered. It was obviously about Bradley. He tended to clam up at his mention lately. Not that Duncan was mentioned much. And that was the point. He had been cast aside. Replaced, almost, with Conrad. Had to be hard on him. He had no real family outside of his mother. And no friends outside of her and Xander. She felt sorry for him. But she didn't linger on it too long. And she didn't want to bring it up to her husband. He had already kept the condition of his back, which now, was stupid, because anyone could see the bad shape it was in. So she didn't need a full medical diagnosis on it. But this Duncan thing was different. It was like her husband didn't want her to know something. Like he had been doing something bad. She pulled her gaze from her husband and let her hair fall back over her ear. She, too, stared at the elevator. It had still not come and it was just on the seventh floor. 'What's the deal with the elevator?'
