A Day in the Drawers

3 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/03/03 A Day in the Drawers Intertwined with ‘The Domino Effect Series’ Previous Parts: Prologue Hunter sat alone on a leather sofa, fidgeting with a black pocketknife. Behind him was a lit fire burning heavily and in front of him was the television accompanied by a stereo with large speakers. ‘Player and Pawns’ kept him from hitting the sack early on New Years Eve – he had the enjoyment of watching the entire show. From ‘Ace of Spades’ Morg wrecking ‘Body Breaker’ Alex Brody in an ‘Alaskan Chain Match’ to Joey Malone losing his Action! Heavyweight Title, Hunter witnessed every last minute of the action taken place on Action’s! PPV Event. Unfortunately Hunter had no place on the event. He watched the show on television, along with the rest of the world. ‘Wow, this is boring.’ Hunter enlightened himself. ‘I should call Reed.’ Hunter grabbed the phone from beside him, knocking an empty beer bottle to the floor. Sticking on the back of the cordless phone was a torn piece of paper. On it was the phone numbers of important employees and friends. Reed Young’s name fell under ‘Chief Employees’, of course. ‘I hope he picks up this time.’ Hunter said, as he dialed. He put the phone to his ear and waited. ‘Hello…’ ‘Reed, you picked up!-’ ‘You’ve reached the whereabouts of Action! Owner Reed Young. Unfortunately, Reed is currently busy and/or away and cannot attend your call. Please leave a message after the - ’ *Beep, Beep, Beep* ‘Stupid answering machine!’ Hunter threw the phone to the opposite end of the couch. ‘What was I thinking anyway? He won’t want to talk to me. I can’t even beat The Ringer, why bother asking Reed for re-acceptance.’ He felt a combination of distress, anger and frustration, and lugged himself up to his feet. ‘I have to prove myself worthy to Reed, only then will I be re-allowed back into Action Wrestling. Young isn’t up for anymore of my bullshit. Despite his idiotic expressions, annoying actions, and lack of intelligence… he knows how to run a stable wrestling federation.’ Note: Action! Wrestling had been run over forty live events, which meant it had been televised for the past forty weeks (+). ‘I’ve jerked myself up once already… I don’t want that to happen again. If I could just show Reed I have what it takes, then he’ll see the real Hunter Levy Mackenzie.” Hunter approached a dust filed cupboard. On top were old records and videotapes, unclear to identify because of the abnormal amount of debris. It seemed as if he was looking for something. ‘If I could just find those videos.’ He searched more but had no luck. ‘I need some sort-of guideline. Where could those tapes of gone?’ He smudged his chin, and caught sight of a ‘DWF: SUNDAY NIGHT’ poser hung up on his wall. ‘Mick would know, he has to know. I could use them as a source of aid, they’d definitely help me out.’ Hunter strutted towards the sofa and once again, picked up the phone. He turned it around, drew Mick’s name, and dialed the number. Mick, also known as Slick’ Mick, was Hunter’s business manager in the Dream Wrestling Federation. He taught him most of what he needed to know about the *game*, despite his easygoing personality and sly lifestyle. Slick Mick knew how to train and had all the equipment to make an indomitable superstar. No wonder Hunter was giving him a ring, as he stood, waiting to hear his voice. ‘Slick’ Mick, how may I help you?’ His old rusty voice sounded exactly like it used too. ‘Mick, is that you? Its Hunter Mackenzie, your old employee.’ ‘Hunter? Disciple X! Whoa, isn’t this a big surprise!’ He meant it, that’s for sure. ‘I know. It has been a very, very long time.’ Hunter smiled. ‘What have you been up to lately?’ ‘Lately? Oh, not much. Hold on a minute, would ‘ya?’ It sounded as if Mick was in the middle of a meeting, Hunter could hear yelling between two individuals. ‘Sorry about that.’ He got back onto the phone. ‘Like I said before, I haven’t been ‘to’ busy. I’m in Holland doing small business. Amsterdam, to be exact…what about you?’ ‘Me? Well, actually, I haven’t been up to much. I’m kind of regrouping myself, and I caught you right in the process of it all. You see Mick, I needed a favor.’ ‘A favor, is that all? It can’t be too hard if its just a favor.’ Mick gave out a hardy laugh. ‘Shoot little guy’ Note: The term ‘little guy’ was what Mick used to address Hunter as, back in his novice years. ‘Its kind of hard to explain Mick.’ Hunter sighed. ‘Listen…’ his voice grew louder. ‘Is there anyway we can meet face to face? It’s funny, I’m actually in Amsterdam myself, spending a short amount of quality time in my hefty abode.’ ‘Its definitely possible. Same cabin that you purchased two years ago, right?’ Mick questioned. ‘Right. How did you kno- , oh right, you were there when I bought it.’ ‘Yes, yes I was. I’ll come by tomorrow at around…oh say sevenish in the morning, sound good? ‘You know it. Thanks Mick, I can’t wait to see your face again.’ Hunter’s confidence had already sky-rocketed. His former manager/best friend was coming to visit him after so long. The downfall was – he still hadn’t asked confronted Mick with his favor. Mick still had no idea WHY he was coming over. ‘Same here bud, take care of yourself. I’ll see you tomorrow. Peace.’ *Beep, Beep, Beep* Mick hung up the phone, and Hunter brought it down from his ear. ‘I’ll have to broaden my physical and mental skills to new heights. It’ll take a lot of time and work, but I’m up for it.’ He said to himself, clutching the phone even tighter. ‘I’m up for it alright.’ Player and Pawns wasn’t only a kick-ass Pay-Per-View Event, it was a trigger to motivation as well.
