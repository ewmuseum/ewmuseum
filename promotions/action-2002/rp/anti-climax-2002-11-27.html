Action! — Tales

Anti-climax

27 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Lee sat in the middle of the restaurant, eating slowly from the plate of jacket potato and baked beans in front of him. Using his knife and fork he scooped up some beans and fed them into his mouth. “Today weapon’s inspectors moved into Iraq in search of any evidence that they are developing chemical weapons.” Lee looked up at the television humming brightly in the corner, and his eyes widened. Just the sort of thing he needed to detract from any hard feelings he had towards himself: other people in the world making complete prats of themselves. Why did people do all this ‘touchy touchy’ stuff when they could either leave them totally alone or bomb them without warning? Philosophically, he started stirring the baked beans around on his plate. He wasn’t in the mood for this. Another glance at the television, another news story. “In England, the firemen are still on a national strike, with a spokesman claiming they wanted a 40% pay rise before they called future strikes off.” Baked beans were spat out onto the plate and Lee slammed his knife down in disgust, causing a clash which turned a few heads. A man at 11pm in the evening sitting on his own with a plate of baked beans causing a disturbance was unusual, and a waiter calmly asked him to relax out of respect for their fellow customers. “Fine, I’ll leave!” stated Lee, leaving a $60 bill on the table and storming out without even waiting for his change. As his trainers made contact with the slippery wet street, Lee hunched over and groaned as the rain gently tapped on his leather jacket. Just what he needed. “Stupid Kayden Paulton,” mumbled Lee to no one in particular, while also noticing that the night was a murky one. “Kayden Paulton just had to dampen our parade. We had him right there, then he…” Lee shuddered as he recalled the moment when Kayden had pinned him for a three count, thereby eliminating one half of the Dyad Champions. Some pride could be salvaged from brother David pinning Kayden a mere 2 minutes later, but that was team pride. What had been damaged was Lee’s personal pride. Throughout the match Lee had been preaching to the masses about how he was a supreme competitor, and he believed he’d shown to all that this was true. But the fans were fickle, they would see the result and couldn’t look past it. ‘Look, that loud mouth got pinned, what a self centred asshole.’ Lee could hear it all now. Unfortunately, an opportunity for revenge would be a long time in coming. After the Pay-Per-View the management (those that had some sense at least, so Reed Young and Jeremy Bold obviously played no part in this decision) had said that neither the A-Star Stable nor The Soldiers were to clash. The mileage in the feud was clocked up, and their issues were to be locked into a cupboard, laid to rest until another day. This didn’t suit Lee, but he couldn’t exactly do much about it. “Sorry,” he grumbled to a man he nearly walked into, before dancing swiftly around a lamp post and down a back alley. Kicking a soggy cardboard box, it was clear that his frustration was brewing. “I’ll just have to preserve my reputation on Pressure Point.” Banging his fists on random objects, Lee continued his advancement down the alley. Airborn Tyler might have been thrust into the spotlight, but for The Elements, and Lee in particular, the night of Sunday 24th 2002 couldn’t be over quick enough. And why had Airborn shaken hands with Jonny Yankee? That had just undermined Lee’s authority, something needed to be done.
