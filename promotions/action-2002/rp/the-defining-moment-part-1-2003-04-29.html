The Defining Moment: Part 1

29 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/29/03 April 29th, 2003; 4:30 PM; 26 Days until Juggernaut St. George Medical Facility, a familiarity to the many professional athletes who's physical health had begun to plummet. The five thousand square foot brick structure was located just over ten miles outside of Memphis, making regular trips to and from, somewhat bothersome. The conditions of this visit to Dr. Leon Felt's office wasn't a pleasant one as it had been in the past to catch up on old times. Leon was a long time friend of his now deceased father who had died when he was only nine. Lately, the upper-midcard of Action! Wrestling's roster had been plagued with for-coming injuries. It was Marcus Dox, known as Max Danger to the media, who first mentioned his on-going back troubles; a common injury for professional wrestlers. Then, low and behold, Jeff Garvin's time had come to experience the darker side of the business... the ramifications of a 'stiff' working style and a heavy calendar. No sooner than one week had past and Jeff had begun feeling a troublesome numbness in his right arm and an acute pain in his lower back. Perfect, just when he started to believe that his career was taking shape while in the middle of his first Bantam championship run, a back injury rears it's ugly head and he's left with no other choice but to seek medical attention. The possible outcomes of this visit frightened him. He worried what Leon might have to say. Jeff sat anxiously in the cushioned chair, watching intently while his doctor looked over a few charts and the x-ray that had been taken a little over a week ago. It was impossible to read Leon, as he never once changed facial expressions. “Well, Jeff, I guess you could say I have some good news and some bad news. The good news is, you’re back isn’t in the same shape as Mr. Dox’s, you’re medically cleared to wrestle by the looks of things,” he said with a dry smile and tipped his reading glasses up. “However, there is some wear and tear, heavy wear and tear that you should in fact be concerned about…” Garvin replied with a simple nod, and asked, “So what does this mean for me? Am I, like, fucked for later, or what?” Having grown up in the business, he knew what ‘wear and tear’ spelt for a lot of good men. Short careers. He worked a hard style, it was to be expected, but what did this all mean for the future? Was he basically counting the days, the months, the years, until his health finally crumbles and he’s held up in bed for nine months? He couldn’t live with that. “Not necessarily, it does mean though that you need to evaluate your priorities. Do you want to continue wrestling twenty-five minute matches each week, or cut down your work load so you’ll still be in the business for another ten years. “Nothings for certain, you may be able to get by the way you are now and last just as long, but one slip up could cost everything.” “Thanks, Leon, I guess I have some thinking to do, huh?” responded Jeff, forcing a smile to try and reassure his old friend that the news hadn’t gutted him too much. Though it had. Leon nodded in reply and left him alone to allow the news to sink in. It was then that he pictured himself many years from this day, sitting in their comfortably furnished den with his beautiful wife, a free Sunday morning without a show to work. He saw himself smiling while their son scampered about. The future he envisioned needed to be backed by a hefty paycheck. He wanted to be able to buy his family a home in a nice area and put his son through college… but could he do this if he was to get injured? Soon the money would stop rolling in and he would be left without a leg to stand on. The choices were set; two paths given… Now it was time to decide.
