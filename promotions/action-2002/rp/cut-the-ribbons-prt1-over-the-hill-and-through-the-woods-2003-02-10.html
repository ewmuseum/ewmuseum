Cut the Ribbons prt1 - Over the Hill and Through the Woods

10 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2.10.03 Public Transportation. The scum of our era. Bobby sat staring forward on the bus, watching the city chase the windows in the corner of his eyes. The stench forced tears almost. A homeless man sat a few roads ahead and the air circulation on the bus was sending the rotting smell of un cared for flesh and age old unwashed clothing towards the back. Bobby was down wind. He sneered and looked at the letter in his hands. He had it neatly folded, the writing in a formiliar cursive scribble. ----- Bobby, its been a long time. I'm sorry that I shut you out. I know you need me. I need you too. Come to the address on the envelop. I miss you. Signed, Mom. ----- He smiled whole heartily. Through all of the woman, the tag team partners, the false alliances, the friendships, none of the people in the business were his mother. He did miss her. From the PIW days when Jim fucking Johnson sent Prisoner 109 after Bobby's mother, he hadn't even heard her voice over the phone. She went into hiding from her OWN son. Her own flesh and blood. He could do nothing. It was ALL his fault. It was ALWAYS his fault. He was just trying his hardest to make things right now. God was unfair to lay this kind of responsibility on Bobby, unfair to lay this conscious on him. God was unfair for making Wrestling the only thing Bobby was good at and unfair for making Bobby such a sucker for all of his mistakes. Bobby clenched his fists and muttered under his breath. 'So unfucking fair.' The woman beside him across the isle heard him, and in her own state of anger and depression, replied with the reply that had probably been said to her in situations resembling this. 'Who said life was fair?' And Bobby could only stare forward and whisper his words before he would have had an unstable outburst that would have surely gotten him kicked off of the bus. 'My mother did.' Its been a while since Bobby had even said that word. Mother. And he felt like it would be a while before he could forget saying it again. But on top of those issues, he was really angry because of one simple situation. His Blue Mustang had been stolen and he was sitting on this stinky godforsaken bus with a bunch of assholes who don't know how to mind their own goddamned business.
