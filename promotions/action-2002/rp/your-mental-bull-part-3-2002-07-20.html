Action! — Tales

Your mental bull - Part 3

20 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 27th July 2002 24th July 2002, Pressure Point 21, Airborn Tyler vs Bobby Minio Incensed, Airborn Tyler sprinted across the ring, but Minio saw him coming and pulled down the top rope, allowing Airborn to live up his name and crash into the Crowd barricade out on the hard floor. The protective mats really didn’t do a very good job of protecting. Airborn stumbled to his feet, dazed, but when he looked up his eyes opened wide. HMPH! Bobby Minio leapt over the top rope with a no hands somersault plancha, and his graceful flight came to an end when he connected with Airborn’s chest, flattening him. A small “Ac-ti-on” chant started up, although it sounded too awkward to catch on. Minio was straight back up to his feet, as he threw Airborn into the Crowd. His Fans beckoning him, Bobby Minio followed Airborn into the masses. Security Guards cleared Fans out of the way as the Referee started his count. ONE! TWO! Minio punched Airborn a couple of times on the cheek, sending him stumbling towards the fire exit. However, he didn’t go through it, and turned around with a retaliatory crescent kick. Bobby was flattened. THREE! FOUR! The crescent kick had knocked Minio into the proverbial next century, and a lightbulb flashed inside Airborn’s small sized brain. He started to ascend the stairs, being jeered all the way by the Crowd. Finally, he reached the top, and saw the body of Minio, prone on the concrete floor. FIVE! SIX! Airborn climbed over the security rail over the top of the fifteen foot high fire exit doors. Bobby Minio was climbing to his feet, gasping for oxygen, and gingerly feeling his cheek after that crescent kick. “YOU WANT RATINGS? THEN WATCH THIS REED YOUNG!” Screamed Airborn. Dumbfounded, the audience looked on in amazement. Surely he wouldn’t do it? SEVEN! EIGHT! Airborn Tyler thrust himself off the balcony, and from a height of fifteen feet flew through the air with a shooting star press. His body was synchronized, his flight perfect, and camera flashes temporarily lit up the Arena in an attempt to catch this picturesque moment. After what seemed like an eternity, Airborn connected with the upright Minio, but he wasn’t upright much longer, as he was flattened. Both men lay on the floor, not aware of their surroundings. NINE! Airborn Tyler had just done a shooting star press onto another human being from a fifteen foot high balcony! Later that night... Action! interviewer James Attwood was standing backstage in his black polo shirt, with trusty microphone in hand. “A couple of minutes ago we witnessed a truly amazing sight, when Airborn Tyler delivered a fifteen foot high shooting star press to Bobby Minio. However, Bobby Minio seems to have come out better, with just a few bruises, a couple of mumbled ‘Where am I?’s. However, Airborn Tyler seems to have taken it worse. His neck hit the concrete at an awkward angle, and the EMT’s have asked that he be taken straight to hospital for an X-Ray. However, as he was carted away from the Arena, he had a few words for us.” In the bottom right hand of the screen a graphic appears, with the Action! logo, and small white text saying “A Few Minutes Ago”. Airborn Tyler appears, on a stretcher, and with a neck brace. “Reed Young, I hope you’re happy. Out there, I nearly gave my damn life to give you ratings. You wanted, I delivered. If that wasn’t good enough for your sorry ass company, I don’t know what is. I’ll be back next week, and if I haven’t got a job, I’ll be sorely pissed off.” The doors to the ambulance were slammed shut, before the red and blue lights flashed, the sirens sounded, and the tires screeched as Airborn Tyler was rushed off to hospital. With the ambulance disappearing into the city, James Attwood’s voice was added over the top. “Will Airborn Tyler still have a job next week?” 26th July 2002, The Hospital Airborn tossed the magazine from his hands, and watched it fly onto the floor, skidding across the slippery surface and disappearing under one of the opposite beds. Sniggering, Airborn sat up with a wince and took a sip from his bedside water. “Bloody Reed Young, making me take such liberties with my life. I should be at home, enjoying the high life.” Seemingly very bitter, Airborn rose from his bed, and for the first time we could see his bandaged right elbow, which had smacked into the concrete ground upon impact of the death defying leap. It had since materialised that the neck brace was a mere precaution, no damage had been done barring minor whiplash. In his blue and white hospital robes, Airborn strode down the corridors. Stuck in a foreign country, cuts and bruises engulfing his body, and an insecure job. What was he doing in America? Why not just go back home to England with his parents; they’d at least appreciate him for what he is. Rather than these fans of course, who don’t seem to realise his extreme talent. Sure, there had been a mighty round of applause when he was wheeled out that arena on a stretcher, but the applause wasn’t just for him was it? It was for Bobby Minio as well. There was no individual chant of ‘AIRBORN! AIRBORN!’. No, the second he set foot back in that arena they’d be jeering his socks off as per bloody usual. “Sir, can you return to your bed please?” asked a pretty nurse, with her blonde her tied back in a bunch. She put her arms around Airborn’s shoulder, turned him around and gestured towards his bed. Not in the mood to oblige, Airborn shoved her arm away and turned back towards where he had been walking. “I’m not going back to my ruddy bed. I’m discharging myself. I’m 18 now, I can do what I want, and I say I want to get out of his hellhole. I have places to visit.” Airborn grabbed his normal clothes from the bedside cabinet, and went into a toiler cubicle to quickly changed. Back in jeans, t-shirts and trainers he felt much more comfortable. His black sports bag was slung over his shoulder, and after signing for his own discharge he walked straight out of the revolving doors and flagged down a black taxi. Hopping into the back seat, he leant forward over the seats. “The Air Canada Centre.” The driver waved a hand of acknowledgment before pulling back out onto the road. Smiling contently, Airborn stroked his bandaged right elbow. He was going to Pressure Point 22 this Saturday. He had a job to secure.
