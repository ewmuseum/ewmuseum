Down and Out. (Part V)

27 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
BEEPBEEP! 'Step back through the metal detector please, sir.' Without hesitation, I produced a lamented card from right pocket. 'Here. I've got a bullet lodged in my brain. It means every time I step through the detector, it goes off. Card was given to me to show police whenever this happened,' I grumbled, looking halfheartedly at the officer. 'Ah. Thank you Mr. Noulin... uhm, Patrick. Go ahead.' I had a friend who'd been shot in the head during a car-jacking earlier in his life. Luckily (more or less), the shot wasn't fatal, it just messed up his brain. He was declared inoperable by his doctor, and given this signed card that the cop now handed back to me. 'I've gotta slip something important in to someone inside,' I'd told him earlier that week. 'I can't talk about it now, but it would mean so much to me if I could use the card just this one, quick time.' I practically begged him, and he gave in, assuming it was something meaningless. He acted as though he didn't mind in the least when he handed the card over, as if he'd loaned it out hundreds of times. I paced slowly towards a chair. The chair sat directly in front of a window, which divided you from the prisoner you wished to speak with on the opposite side (if I knew what these were called, I'd call them by name). I took my seat and looked up, seeing that no one was in front of me at the moment. Thank God, too, because I needed a few minutes to get ready. What would come next would be the worst of the worst. Having to look a man in the eye while you shattered his skull with a single trigger-pull, and then having to turn the revolver back on yourself. This was my plan, because the alternative was life in prison, or the death-penalty, which only meant a fifteen year long wait for death instead of a fifteen second one. I had decided on the shorter route, regardless of where I might find myself afterwards. I glanced around for the first time, noticing no guards on either side were really paying any attention to the conversations had by prisoners and there visitors. This almost made me smile... And then he came. I saw the prisoner door on the opposite side of the glass, to my left, slide open with a buzz. An officer closed the door after the man I intended to pay visit to entered. I did not look up, but I felt his eyes on me. I felt his surprise as he realized who it was that sat at the booth he was to sit at as well. I felt his happiness as he edged closer to the seat. I felt his love. My father took his seat in front of me. I almost broke into tears as I looked up into his face. The man I'd pictures in my head for some thirteen years had changed drastically since I'd last seen him. His hair was now near shoulder length, contrary its being cut very short in the past. A think, full beard covered his blackened face. Wrinkles had formed under his eyes and across his face, and his long hair had turned from black into a hybrid of gray and darker gray. I could not believe that the man that sat in front of me had been the same man who'd committed the sinful deed on that faithful day in April, 1990. His face no longer brought fear to my heart, but rather sorrow, pity, and regret. This was worse than I could ever have imagined. Across from me, my father brought his receiver to his ear. He leaned in close, and without picking up my end I could still hear him. 'Steven... I, I thought you'd never...' he trailed off, words were nothing now. Words could not express what either of us felt. For me, it was pain, anger, sadness, happiness, and guilt all at once. I could not sort out my feelings. I knew what I wanted to do. What I had to do. What those strange thoughts and dreams had told me to do. My right hand grasped the pocket of mine which held the weapon. 'Well I have,' I finally said back, choking back tears and emotions with all my strength. The look my father saw was that of pain, of regret of even showing up. 'I... I...' he began, 'I can't believe you're here! There is so much we have to catch up on... I don't know where to began.' Tears ran down his face as he spoke, but he put on a smile nonetheless. It sickened me to see him smile in such a moment as this. To try and make light of the situation. The anger inside me swelled up quickly, and I soon was back in control of the situation. I knew what I had to do. 'You... you took my life from me. You gave me life in the beginning but one day you decided to take it back. Mom meant everything to me, and you pulled her and my life away as though they meant nothing to you...' I sobbed, thoughts of mom popped into my head. 'Steven, son, listen,' he was choking back tears as well. His eyes were blood red as he tried to reassure me. 'I... I made a mistake, but...' 'A mistake?' I shot back, my bloodshot eyes shooting a hole through his. 'No, dad. Dropping a pencil is a mistake. Taking your wife's life ISN'T. But, you know what dad, if that's a mistake...' I pulled the revolver from my pocket, and pointed it through the glass and directly at my father's head. '...then so is this.' BAM! I heard the noise through my skull, through my head. I shut my eyes as it resounded around my brain like a thousand bombs exploding across a nation all at once. My whole body trembled as the deafening blast echoed. Oh no, I thought, I've done it. I killed him. I hate myself... but then I heard nothing. No glass shattering. No screams. No yells of panic. No crying. No blood spattering. Nothing. Was I deaf? I opened an eye, then a second. My mind returned to me as I glanced up. Revolver still against the glass. Father still across from me. I hadn't pulled the trigger. Amazingly, in the least likely event that could have happened, I later discovered that I'd actually lost consciousness from a nanosecond, and my finger had not slipped and pulled the trigger. So I laughed. My hand placed the gun back in its rightful place. No one had seen, my father had not cried out. He still sat there, tears running down his cheeks, horror painted across his face as if it was canvas itself. I rose from my seat, a little smile still on my face. I could not believe the irony of the whole situation, and I doubt my father could either. I nodded to my him, he who had now broken into a cold sweat, and quickly headed to the exit. I nodded to the same guard who'd checked me through the metal detector when I had first entered. BEEPBEEP!
