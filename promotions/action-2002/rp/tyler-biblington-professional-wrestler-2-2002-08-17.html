Action! — Tales

Tyler Biblington : Professional Wrestler (2)

17 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
“Are you ready?” Lunacy had pretty much been Tyler Biblington’s mentor for the past three months and it was time for the last minute encouragement that was going to help Tyler through his first big match. Tyler had been wrestling at the weekly house show event and was getting well-received ovations from the crowd. It was time for his debut on television and sweat was already dripping down from his forehead. He nodded and watched as Lunacy walked away. Biblington leaned against a wall backstage with his eyes closed. He waited for his music to play before he would step out and make his way to the ring. I don’t think I’m ready for this. I’m going to cave under all this pressure; my legs feel like jello, my whole body is shaking uncontrollably, and the worst thing is I’m scared out of my mind right now. I know what I’m doing out there though. We’ve gone through most of the big parts of the match. They told us to finish the match off based on the fans reaction. Whoever was getting the cheers was going to get the win. My ribs are still tender. I’m never going to hit my frogsplash. It’s my trademark move and I can’t even hit it properly. The house show wasn’t what I would call a success. They tried to make me a giant killer or something. Dark Blade is only like two feet bigger than me and he’s got to have at least two hundred pounds on me. If I ever screw up on him again, he’s going to kick the shit out of me. I can’t do this. It’s too early for me to be out there. It’s too late for me to leave. What am I going to do when they give me the mic on front of all these people? What happens to me if I screw up? Will they end my contract as quickly as they picked it up? I wonder if Lindsay came? She hates Wrestling but she said she might show up, just to watch me. I got her tickets. I can’t even remember where she’s sitting. “TYLER! GET YOUR ASS OUT THERE NOW!” The backstage worker screamed as Breathe by Prodigy was nearly over and I was still propped against the wall. I was frightened at first and wasn’t sure what was going on. It soon became clear that I nearly missed my first match. I bolted straight to the ring, praying that I wouldn’t fall down the ramp. I hustled my ass off and slid under the bottom rope and the match was about to begin. *** Dewey Jefferson was sent to the outside by a springboard dropkick from Tyler Biblington. He crawled around on the outside, looking like he had enough for the night. Tyler was ready for his moment now. His adrenaline fuelled him and his debut was going smooth. Now was the move that would give him the victory. He would slingshot out of the ring, flip and than hit the unsuspecting Jefferson on the outside. He rushed to the opposite ropes to give him that extra boost. He went over the ropes and at the last second, Jefferson rolled out of the way and uncovered the steel chair he was laying on. Biblington went headfirst into the chair. He couldn’t get his hands up in time. He broke his nose and blood poured uncontrollably from his face and he lay there motionless. Jefferson climbed back into the ring, not sure of the damage he had just caused. He played to the crowd who couldn’t see what happened to Biblington and listen to the ref’s mandatory ten count. Babylon was unconscious on the outside and the referee had made it to five before he called for the bell. He motioned for some medics to get to ringside because a wrestler was hurt. Jefferson had now turned around and watched in horror as he finally saw what he did to Biblington. He couldn’t believe it and he fell to the mat and sat himself in the corner. He knew he had just wrestled his last match…ever. Biblington was taken to the local hospital. Other than his nose and a concussion, Biblington would be fine. Nathan Mann, the owner of OCW, saw a future in Biblington but saw how much a risk he was. The first thing Mann did was talk to Jefferson about what happened. The two talked by themselves, in the office of Mann for the night, for roughly half an hour. He had no explanation for what he had done and left quietly. Mann would be back in the morning, he had a few things that he need to discus with Biblington. It seemed Biblington’s career was already over, thanks to one man’s careless actions.
