Dreams were meant to be broken: Chapter 5

9 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Fiend and his father stood in the meadow. His father had three quarters of a head left. Smoke began to filter from his father's head. Lots of smoke. The meadow was growing dark and the smoke was beginning to taste awful. That taste he was being mocked with earlier. The grass began to shrivel into rocks. The rocks began to coat with red and were soon soaked in it. The day had turned to night and building began to rise from the ground. They burst through the dirt and shot high into the sky. Buildings of grey and black shadowing him. His father's body dropped to the asphalt. The asphalt? He was back where he started. Except the body of his father was at his feet. Was at his feet. Now all that remained was blood soaked asphalt. He looked down onto the asphalt of the road and he knew that this red was blood. Familiar blood. He knew this blood, he'd been covered in it before. He'd had it sprayed across his face and all over his hands. This was the blood of his past. He stared down at the blood with his heart skipping beats. Ba-doomp. Ba-doomp. Ba-doomp. Ba-doomp. It's rate increased as his mouth dried. His tongue turned to sand paper as all the moisture in his mouth disappeared into worry. Worry that his past was coming back to haunt him, and it was. It was plaguing the back of his mind as it had always done. It was torturing him. It stabbed him in the back like he'd done so many times to so many before. He couldn't shut his eyes without seeing his past. The people from his past. The victims from his past. 'WHY ARE YOU DOING THIS TO ME?' he screamed loudly into the night. A door pushed open and a man was thrown into the alleyway. He stumbled backward and ran right into Fiend's chest. He spun in fright and dropped to his knees, begging. Begging. They always begged.
