The Second Person: Obscured by Familiarity

9 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I still think about you sometimes. I don’t find any consistency in my feelings toward you. It all depends on which you that I’ll be dealing with. I’m sure that while I only wish to repair and resume my friendship with that you that operates on the surface, what I need is closure with that you that is hiding from me. And it’s unfortunate that I’m going to have to seriously hurt that part of you that I truly do not find guilty of the other’s crimes. You can call it tough love. But to be perfectly honest I’m not doing this for you. It’s for me. This feels selfish. But it also feels necessary. I have many other choices to make. But this is the only choice that will allow me to live again. Since the incident, I’ve felt a lot of shame. Every time I’m freely given honest praise for who I am and what I do, I can only think about what happened and feel humiliation. I could pretty easily conceive of continuing to live in this zombie state, with only the god’s face bringing life back into me. But it isn’t my life. It isn’t me. And I want my life back. So. I’m going to take it from you. This will be something that we’re both going to regret. But at the conclusion of this, we’ll both share an equal amount of guilt. But I worry. And I wait. I think that perhaps I’m becoming something that I don’t really want to be. The more I let the god’s face bring this unique life substitute back into me, the harder it is for me to keep the special space open for the life that I want. So I’m motivated to act soon. I’m compelled to be a little more merciless than I’d like to be. Not very different from you.
