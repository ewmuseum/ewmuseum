Action! — Tales

Job interview

20 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 13th August 2002 8th August 2002, McDonalds: Chicago Branch Ben continued to sit patiently in the seat, uncomfortable and twiddling his thumbs. Not only was this his first ever job interview, it was a job interview for something he didn’t have the faintest idea about. If you asked me what a Big Mac was, he’d probably tell you it was an obese Scottish man. And how you turn an oven on, well, that’s rocket science. Of course, sitting down in the chair didn’t help matters. Not only was it wooden and awkward to sit in, the whole concept of staying still was foreign to Ben. He wanted to be running through the corridors of the arena, sorting out ‘spots’ for his next match, or even better, soaring through the air, taking the breath away from all those watching him. But he wouldn’t be doing that any time soon. Reed Young had fired him, and it was time to move on, shove the past aside. It would only block his path. “Mr.Tyler? Mr.Bedford will see you now.” Rising from his seat and walking down the corridor, Ben nodded at the receptionist as he went by. He wasn’t used to being called ‘Mr.Tyler’. In the wrestling locker room he was more commonly referred to as ‘bastard’, ‘wanker’ and ‘asshole’. Common courtesy hadn’t exactly been introduced into his culture. The elevator doors opened up in front of Ben, and he stepped in, smiling meekly at the two women already standing inside, wearing their blouses and skirts. The doors closed again, and only the fluorescent lights embedded in the ceiling prevented nothingness from setting in. What am I doing here? I’m a performer, a modern artist. In that ring I have my own brush, paint and paper, and I tell a story, yet here, I’m nothing. All I am is another tiny cog in the mighty works. That’s if I’m even enough to get the job. The woman behind Ben started shuffling around frantically with papers. That’s her life. Always worrying. A never ending flow of meetings and engagements. Trying to shove his thoughts to the back of his mind for now, Ben stood with his arms behind his back, attempting to appear amicable. Eventually, the elevator shuddered to a halt, and the doors slid open, letting the bright objects and colours flow into the human lifting device. Across the hall was a prominent object. An oak door, with a golden handle, a golden frame, and a big golden name plate. This was the office of Mr.Bedford, the employment officer for McDonald’s Chicago. Straightening his blazer and fidgeting with his tie once more, Ben took a big breath. It’s now or never. Ben rapped his cold knuckles against the door, and the echo rang loud and true throughout the hallway. Too loud for comfort. Ben scanned his eyes over the area to see if any unpleasant glances were being cast his way, but none were forthcoming. There was only person in sight, a man photocopying some documents. “Come in.” Why am I doing this? I’m better than this! Ben pushed down the gold door lever, being careful not to leave any smear marks. He walked through the door, and shut it behind him. The 18yr old didn’t walk into the middle of the room as is normal, he stood shrivelled up in the corner, smiling weakly at his perspective employer. Mr.Bedford sat forward in his chair, elbows on the mahogany desk before him. Smiling, he was Jewish mid-40s man, with a skull cap, and neatly brushed black hair which contained a few strands of grey. His brown suit with purple tie combination looked odd, and this didn’t put Ben at his ease in the least. Mr.Bedford waved his freely at the leather seat which faced him, inviting Ben to sit down. What kind of kid is this? So nervous and timid. Mr.Bedford had obviously not seen any editions of Pressure Point from the past two months. He may have appeared so before him, but Ben under his guise of ‘Airborn Tyler’ had been anything other than timid. His mouthy mannerisms and ignorant observations were now famous the world over. Say something then. Ben sat down in the seat, crossing his arms and legs, leaning back and away from Mr.Bedford. This was going to be a hard interview. “Hello, you are Benjamin Tyler?” asked Mr.Bedford. “I am.” Mr.Bedford ticked off a box on a piece of paper that rested on the table. “OK, I’m Mr.Bedford, and hopefully I will be one of your supervisors in the future. I am delighted to say that you are the only person who applied for the vacancy at our Surram Branch, which was advertised under the title ‘Property Janitor’.” Oh God, how pathetic does that sound? He’s going to have me cleaning toilets and scrubbing the floors on my hands and knees. I knew it. Why didn’t I just go home and become a spoilt brat like any other un-decent teenager would do? Mr.Bedford scratched his forehead pretentiously, not knowing quite what to say. He’d already decided before Ben had entered that he would be given the job, as he was the only applicant. Perhaps one scenario would suffice for now. “To be brutally honest, I don’t see any point in interviewing properly. You have the job and a three month contract will be in the post, with an extra six month job should we be satisfied with the job you do. However, I would like to test you on one scenario, a bit of fun.” A bit of fun? Last time I did something for ‘a bit of fun’ I ended up tied up at the bottom of a swimming pool in my underwear. Those gangsters obviously didn’t appreciate me shooting at them with a water pistol. Some people just have no sense of humour. “If the toilet overflows with sewage, what would you do?” I’m just being easy on the boy. No point in doing anything he could mess up, it could effect his confidence. Oh crap, I feel my confidence leaving my body as if a pin had been prodded through he skull. I’m way out of my depth, and that’s as a bloody McDonald’s janitor. Reed Young, come back, all is forgiven. “Get some of the mugs from the kitchen, scoop it back into the toilet and keep flushing until it goes away.” Mr.Bedford shook his head disbelievingly. Ben grinned like a hyena. I guess that means it wasn’t right. Kids these days. Who were the parents of this complete loser? Crap. Oh well, may as well make light of a bad situation. “I was only kidding with you. You get a knife and put it in the hamburgers as spread.”
