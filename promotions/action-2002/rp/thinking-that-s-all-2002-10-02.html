Thinking, That's All.

2 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 2nd, 2002 Noah leant back inside his easy chair, a cold ice pack resting against his lower back as he felt the chill of condensation enter through the tunnel of wounds. A few small shards of glass had entered through his back after being hip tossed onto the roof of the Grand AM at Retaliation, a constant reminder of being so close to glory but leaving empty handed. He still felt a slight twinge when turning his neck too far to his right. The Erroneous Conclusion hadn't had as much effect on his ribs as he'd thought. Taking the move on the cold concrete and having his neck whiplash from the manuver was what led him into a small chair, watching day time soap operas to keep his mind off of the pain. Laughing at other people's emotions somehow made hurt all but drift away. Hawkins reached into his front pocket, groaning with each motion before pulling out a pack of Malboro cigarettes and a black lighter. Hawkins tapped them against the palm of his hand a few times, for God knows what reason, before taking one out and lighting it. He let out a sigh before blowing the smoke to the nearest window. The window's frame was opened just a crack. Enough to cool down the sweltering apartment but yet drown out the noise from the outside traffic. Small white curtain drapes hung to the sides, revealing the sun glaring into the home and bouncing off of the window like mirrors. It reflected into every room in the house, considering there were only three. A small bathroom in the far corner, adjacent to a bed room that Noah slept in. The door was slightly open, showcasing a bed that hadn't been made in a while, and a simple dresser with another pack of smokes. Inside the bathroom, deoderant, toothpaste, and an electric shavor decorated the otherwise baren room. Inside, there was a small kitchen along the far wall, ingrown into the room, along with a fridge and an oven that probably hadn't been used since Hawkins had bought the place. If you opened the fridge, you'd be lucky to find a bottle of water and some cheese. Inside, Hawkins stared at his small thirteen inch television screen, rabbit ear antenna's trying desperatly not to crackle and rattle the picture before him. The room was quite small, so even from the furthest end of his chair and the television on the other side, Hawkins just was a step or so away from changing the channel. And that step might be something he'd have to take, as the soap opera Passions quietly changed to what seemed like a News program. 'Great' Hawkins muttered. 'Just what I need. The perfect cure for the insanely happy.' Hawkins sat up a small bit, trying to reach the television from his seat. What he would give for a simple stick or a branch. He gave up rather quickly, leaning back into his easy chair, with the television blasting about gun shootings and robberies. How your favorite food can give you cancer if eaten on the second tuesday of every month. How your doctor might be video recording your prostate exams for private exhibition. How your local car dealership are putting a-bombs inside of car engines. And yet, no one asks what the hell is going on in the world. What possesses numerous people to shoot and stab others for a wallet or because they looked at them cross eyes. What makes people take out their cocks inside restraunts and stir your margarita, even when you ask it shaken. Why does the world have enough evil in it to fill a full hour daily. Noah asked this questions. He asked why there wasn't as much good to report as there was bad. Were these people looking in the wrong places? Or is the evil in the world overwhelming to the point where Good will eventually drown out. And when it does, will evil become good? Or will evil even exist. Will anything exist?
