Seven Years: IV

12 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/12/03 Ryan Duncan Maxwell has been working for Joe Campbell for quite some time now. While he might have first appeared in September, he had really been in contact with the corrupt Asylum owner for more than a year, dating back even before his first appearance. When he made the decision to try and get his ban lifted, he finally got the chance to meet the Asylum owner and work in the Asylum. Joe Campbell granted him the right to leave the Asylum for Action! Wrestling, but in return, Duncan was asked to keep an eye on the fighters that worked in the Smilthy's restaurants. Well, unfortunately for Ryan, today wasn't a very good day, what with being held at gunpoint by a man that had been waiting for him in the dimly-lit basement of the Smilthy's restaurant. 'Ryan Maxwell,' the man said with a smirk, 'It's been a long time.' Ryan looked bewildered, and he examined the man carefully. Like Ryan, he was clad in a trenchcoat, but this one was closed so as not to reveal what he wore underneath. He also wore a beret, though Ryan couldn't see the insignia that was embedded on the front thanks to the lack of lighting. 'Who are you?' Ryan asked. 'I'm not surprised that you don't remember me. The program you were a part of has that way with you people,' the man said, 'I'll admit that you did well in keeping away from us despite the fact that you're a professional wrestler. You definitely have your wits about you.' 'I'm not gonna ask this again,' Ryan seethed, not at all amused by this interference from his job, 'Who are you?' 'And why should I tell you? Am I not the one who has the gun pointed at you?' the man asked. 'You sure you know how to handle that, old man?' Maxwell asked, having gotten a close examination of the handgun that the man was wielding, 'That's a Desert Eagle, you know. You must be a big Matrix fan. How do you carry that thing, anyway? You're as bad as those old-timers carrying the Colt .45s, you know. Always wanting the Hollywood guns rather than the ones that'll get the job done more effectively. And why are you even pointing a weapon like that in my direction, anyway? If you shoot at me with it, the noise'll just alert the guys upstairs, and I doubt very seriously that you've got enough bullets to take `em all out before someone calls the police, what with the seven bullet magazine. You probably don't have a license to kill, so, why don't you stop insulting my intelligence and point the Beretta that I know you have stuffed in your coat pocket at me, and get on with this.' The man fidgetted. He hadn't expected this. So, while keeping the Desert Eagle aimed at Fletcher, he opened his trenchcoat, and pulled out another gun. This was a custom made Beretta 92FS, gold plated. The man known to wrestling fans as Duncan Fletcher shook his head. 'Jee-sus, where'd you get the money for that job?' Fletcher asked. 'Quit your talking, Maxwell. You're out of line, and I am the superior officer here.' the man said. Fletcher tilted his head oddly at the man who had just now stuffed his Desert Eagle into his trenchcoat. It was then that he saw it. The badge. He was from the US Navy. What the hell did he want with Fletcher? 'Superior officer?' Fletcher inquired. 'Lieutenant Commander Alec Rivera, US Navy. We've been anxious to make contact with you for a while... Lieutenant Maxwell.' It took Duncan a full ten seconds to process the fact that he was just called a Lieutenant. 'Wait, did you just call me Lieutenant?' Rivera sighed, 'Perhaps I should start at the beginning.'
