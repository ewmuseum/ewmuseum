Dreams were meant to be broken: Prologue I

1 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
'Just give me the bottle, take my money, and save the chit chat for some other poor fool. Coz quite frankly,' he read the tag on the clerk, 'Enrique, I couldn't give a shit about the small talk, capische?' he spat at the clerk. The clerk put the bottle into a brown paper bag and handed it across the counter to the man before him. He exchanged a fifty dollar note for the bottle of Johnny Walker blue label. The clerk returned some notes and some coins to him. 'Thank-you, Enrique. Have yourself a fucking lovely night,' he suggested to the shop clerk. He turned and began to head out of the liquor store, but he noticed a fraile, old lady was giving him the death stare. He stopped in place and began to stare back at her. He could feel her hate staring him deep in the eyes. It almost felt like she was reading his inner most thoughts inside his head. Reading everything he had ever done in his life and it made him feel dirty. Made him feel worthless. Made him feel. Her reaction to his stare was quite unusual for someone so meek and fragile. It was almost intimidating for him, but he wasn't easily intimidated. In fact, it was a freak occasion that he might be. He stepped ever so slowly across to the old lady. Enrique, the shop clerk, began to watch as the friendly customer approached the old lady. 'Hey, buddy, nevermind Agnes. She's just an old lady, let it go,' he called from behind his counter. He turned to look at Enrique. He shot him a glance that would put a cat out in moments. 'What the Hell were we just talking about, Miguel?' 'Enrique.' 'Does it fucking matter who you think you are, Santo? I don't fucking think so. Save the small talk for somebody else, capische?' he warned the clerk. The warning was enough. He approached the old lady, bending down as he got closer, so he might look her in the eye and get on her level. 'Hello, Agnes. I am the devil, but you can call me Tobi if you like,' he quoted Rowan Atkinson to the old woman. 'I know what you are and I don't like it. Get out of this shop, you scum,' she replied. He was taken back. The gall of something so breakable amazed him. It shocked him severely. It even stung a little that something like that could try and be so powerful to something she would never understand. NEVER! 'You think I know what I am? You really think that you can fully comprehend what is inside of this shell you so despicably stare at? Huh? You think you know, Agnes? You'll never know. And you'll die not knowing. And if you ever did know. You'd take it to your grave. Making sense, am I, Agnes?' He put it all on the table, because that's who he was. He never beat around the bush, he just pulled that sumbitch right from the ground. Roots and all. 'What you see here, Agnes, is red. I am red.'
