Inside The Shallow Bay: 1

24 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I was having a hard time deciding whether to come to Merrill on this day. I was told the last time I saw them, before we went our separate ways that I was welcome to their homes. Like I needed the reassurance from my own family. It’s a cold, gray day here in mid Michigan, like always. I’m just miles away from my birthplace, but I have honestly never felt more distant in my entire life. The Explorer is rather low on gas, and after a fast pit stop and saying hellos and good byes to the family for fifteen minutes, I’ll go top it off. It shouldn’t take long, should it? The place is located in some remote apartment complex within the city. It would be considered the slums of this fairly small town, if I remember right. It was attached to the same main street strip as the local businesses such as the tavern, the print shop, and that kind of stuff. There was a little parking lot behind the tavern that I could get in from the main road, where I saw a couple of cars parked. I followed suit, parking right next to the white Shell Station building with my Ford Explorer. I exited soon after, and looked around. I saw an aged wooden flight of stairs that led to three apartments, each at a fair distance from the other. The one I was looking for had a black shingled roof walk way to it, a door a good two feet from the edge of the ceiling, now fifteen feet in the air. Instead of entering that second, I decided to listen. I could hear the Lions/Patriots game on the television, and I could hear my older brother wondering rather loudly whether or not Joey Harrington’s eyes were attached to his ass. He was always angry about the Lions, and I wouldn’t blame him. The Lions are the Ken War of the NFL, after all. A bunch of comedic fluff. I went down the roof and lit a cigarette, inhaling the cancer as if it was a passing trend. I was getting second thoughts, of course. I hadn’t seen these people in five years, if I do remember correctly. Maybe six. Sat on that edge, my feet dangling of the side of the roof, puffing down on my smoke. I watched the traffic go by at a slower pace. I could see every couple people giving me glances as they went by, off on their own little family trips and such. It was all family related. I wonder what kind of a family a man like I could have. Would I have a bunch of social degenerates, or something else? Would I have the ability to be a good father? Could I play catch with my kid? Could I tell him stories that would actually get a smile out of him? How would I react if it were a girl? Twins? I don’t know what made me think about settling down. Maybe it was the lack of affection I was receiving. It wasn’t like I was the same man I was once I got out of the Army, where I would sleep with a random woman on a random night. In the IWO, Joey and I were totally against the whole groupies’ thing that I saw people like Kell get sucked into. Me and Joey still had dreams of starting families, living in nice places with white picket fences, a nine to five job, and dinner on the table with my wife smiling, our two kids coming in from playing football outside. Call it 1950’s ideology, but that’s the kind of life a vagabond like I would want. This business gets demanding, and it comes down to making a decision at times. One problem, Joey tried to balance the two dreams. In the end, both have toppled down. For the first time, Joey’s going to be eating dinner alone tonight. I wasn’t going to allow myself to hurt someone like that. I am not a man of empty promises, of unaffection. I would give all this up just to be with Quinn again, when we were first seeing each other. As I played with these thoughts of Joey’s life and my own, I puffed down on the cigarette, flicking it out of my hands, watching the embers fall. The reason I think about Joseph so much is because he was my only family for a long period of time. He was that kind face on the road. Him and I were kin. We took different paths, but we believed the same things and created the same ideals. We were like neighbors. Or closer. I often wonder about him. I would call him, but I haven’t the guts to speak with him. Especially now. You don’t mess with a man with nothing to lose. I was so deep in thought that I became startled when a tap hit my shoulder. “Rashard?” I looked her in the eyes. My god, she was beautiful. My sister was all grown up now. “Alyson?”
