Face-Off: Extended.

23 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05.23.2003 What you didn't hear during the Conrad Ramsey/Max Danger face-off following their match: The two men stared each other down. All eyes were on them. Referee Benny Doyle had retrieved the Carnage Championship. Before he handed it to its rightful owner, he lifted Conrad Ramsey's arm high into the air, signifying that he was the winner of the match. The young Irish Referee then handed the title over to Max. Danger smirked. Oh, sure, he wasn't happy with the outcome of the match. But he still had the title. The title that Conrad so desperately wanted, and yet, didn't. It wasn't the title he was after. It was the redemption. The final word. Just getting the very last edge on Max Danger. It was about humiliation. It was about humbling 'the Danger Man'. He had already taken his mother. Now he wanted the one thing, besides his own wife, that he cherished more than anything: the Carnage Championship. Behind all this, Frank Heimdal had his arm raised in victory as well. That wasn't the focus, though. He, like everyone else, was watching the two rivals peer deeply into one another's eyes. There was hope, desperation, fire, anger, and even a hint of jealousy in the eyes of Conrad Ramsey. He had never beaten Max Danger. Not really. He wasn't going to accept a Disqualification at as a 'win'. He hadn't looked like a winner afterward. He had just one more shot. He knew that. It was now or never in his mind, and in everyone elses. If you couldn't win, then so be it. 'The King of Submission' would just be the better man. Simple as that. In Danger's eyes rage a passion unlike any other, but a small sprinkle of doubt. Every so slightly, it was there in his now-blue eyes. He had never lost to Conrad. Sure, this was techincally going to go down as a defeat, but he hadn't been pinned. He still had the old man's number. He honestly thought that. But there was something. Something in the back of his mind that was telling him that Juggernaut was going to be different. That it wasn't going to be simple. That he was going to reach deeper into himself than ever before. The New Jersey crowd continued to take pictures of Max Danger and Conrad Ramsey, just staring each other down. The two men's mouths were moving, but none of it could be heard. Only they knew what was being said. 'I know what that title represents, Max. But we don't have to do it that way,' Conrad was saying. 'We aren't gimmick wrestlers, Max. You know that.' 'What exactly are you trying to get at, old man?' Max replied. 'What I'm getting at, little one, is that we don't have to resort to weapons and putting each other through tables. Look at the show, Max. Ladder match. Cage of Death. We are above all that. And we both know that no one else is going to do a better job than us. No one will be able to out-wrestle us.' 'This is all true, yes. But I'm still not getting the point?' 'Max, I'm proposing that we do a simple match. No Gimmicks Needed. Straight-up wrestling. Pinfall or submission. Rope breaks.' Max chimed in, 'What about countouts? We don't want that.' Conrad nodded, 'I agree. There will be no countouts. Your belt, it can change hands on a pinfall. You agree?' Max thought about it a moment. Neither man was taking their eyes off the other. He blinked and slightly nodded, 'I agree.' 'Straight up wrestling match,' Max continued. 'I like the sounds of that. No Gimmicks Needed.' He started to smirk. 'I guess we have ourselves a deal.' Conrad said, 'Excellent. See you Sunday.' 'Yes you will,' Max said as he slowly began to walk away. Bradley Duncan had crawled from between the two men and was standing outisde the ring with Jessica. They waited for 'the Danger Man' to join them, and then they all started to head to the back. Just as Frank Heimdal was coming up to join the old man, watching him watch the old man's greatest advisary exit. Juggernaut II. That was where it was all going to end. Either Conrad Ramsey was going to walk away victorious and with the Carnage Championship, or Max Danger would retain his title and prove his complete dominance of the former Bantam Champion. The questions were easy to come by. It was the answers that were difficult.
