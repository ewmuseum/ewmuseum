Ho-Hum Personified

21 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Sept. 21, 2002 People who knew Simon well were fully aware that he was the kind of person who needed something to do. Whether it was meaningful or meaningless, he just wanted to keep himself busy. Busy as in occupied. Busy as in...working. Yet, spending time in a mall wasn't really working. Unless you think it to be so. 'You know what I realized? There's a difference between these two articles of clothing that several individuals just might not get. You see this jacket on my left? Actually, my other left? On this jacket, there are eight buttons, ten including the pockets. Are you following me because what I'm about to say is important? You need to pay attention. Are you paying attention?' On a lazy Saturday afternoon, Simon Seaman had found himself in, out of all places, a strip mall chatting with some random stranger. Now it wasn't just any strip mall, but...nevermind. It was an average strip mall and that was it. In reality, he'd never even imagine spending time in there in his whole life, yet, he was content. At least this gave him something to do for the time being. Apparently, he would need to keep himself for more than just a few hours. More like a whole weekend. 'Okay, good. That jacket has ten buttons, while this one, practically identical to the other, has not only ten buttons, but eleven. Twelve if you throw in the spare one you get in that little bag that comes with some clothes you get. You know that little bag? Man, that's a great feature. Back in my parents' day, they didn't have those. You remember that time? You look like you do. Oh, were the 1800's great or what? I couldn't tell you honestly, but I bet you could. Anywho, when my mother or father lost a button, you'd never hear the end of it. Truly, they would never stop yack, yack, yacking about it. They'd talk until their mouths went numb. Have you ever met those kind of people? Have you? The people that never shut up?' asked Simon energetically. The woman, who had the privilege of encountering this man, didn't know what to do. She tried to avoid further conversation with him and made her way down another aisle of the outlet store, but was met by Simon once again. 'Yeah. I can now say that I have.' she responded, with regret. Simon nodded his head in sympathy. 'Met one today, right? That's too bad. These kind of malls are filled with them though. Take if from experience...people around here are a little nuts if you ask me. An ordinary guy might get fed up with all this, but not me. I have just enough patience to cope. It's just a trait I have. I can't really explain it. Like the invention of the Rubix's cube.' The tangent he had been going on was clearly going nowhere, so he kept the conversation intriguing in any way he could. 'So, back to the clothing. One has ten buttons and the other has twelve. That's crazy like what.' The lady snatched the two jackets from Simon's possession and threw them aside in disgust, but he was right there to pick them up. She then attempted to storm out of the store, though Simon had caught up to block the doorway. 'Why are you still talking to me? I don't even know you.' the woman inquired. 'No offense, but I don't think you're argument is valid and I'll tell you why. For example, I don't know Simon Le Bon from Duran Duran. You'd think if given the chance, I wouldn't talk to him? That would be a lie, ma'am. Not only would I speak with him, but I would also discuss the significant effect of 'Hungry like a Wolf' on my interest in hungriness and wolf related music during my teenage years.' Before there was any time to proceed with this interesting talk of his, she scooted to the other side of the store and sought refuge as far away from Simon as humanly possible. 'Um, I'll be going over here now.' Suddenly, Simon was left by himself. With jackets in hand, he tried to get her attention from afar. 'If you can't see these good enough, I'll move back for you. No worries.' he mentioned. 'No, I'm fine.' replied a voice from behind a shelf. Absolutely bored, Simon walked out of the store and sulked. Moments later, he noticed he was still in possession of the store's merchandise and chucked both of them hastily and whistled his way down the sidewalk like nothing happened. Next stop would be the local 7-Eleven across the street. This truly eventful day seemed like it was never going to end. It was really one of those days where you wished you never existed, but had to for the sake of a Kool and the Gang revival world tour. 'What am I supposed to do? I want a match, but Reed won't get me one. I wish to contribute, but I can't. I try to do what I can, but they won't let me. I've got a job, Rodney. While I'm trying to do it well, Reed prevents me from doing anything remotely close to well. In fact, he probably doesn't want me to do anything at all. He can sit behind his desk all day watching those shows of his, though let me tell you this. that won't stop me from going to Retaliation. I will be there and that is no lie. Realistically, it's not a bald faced lie or a hairy faced lie either. This is Simon Seaman talking and I for one won't stand for some guy stopping me from getting my freak on, willy-nilly style, in that arena. Do you hear me? I said do you hear me?!' Simon proclaimed as he stood in front of a check-out counter as he dipped his hot dog in a big gulp and mixed it around. The cashier calmly glanced up from his Maxim magazine. He took one good look at Simon and pondered. 'Excuse me. What?' he inquired while Simon took the first thing he saw to cover his face. 'Oh, are you talking to me? he asked back as the cashier wouldn't stop staring at him. 'What are you doing?' Simon continued to hide behind the magazine as the cashier leaned over and snatched it away from him. 'You always read from Cosmopolitan, man?' the cashier said. 'You mean you don't? Keep up with the times, buddy. There's only so many tips you can get from 'O' without wanting something more.' Simon answered, in a weird combination of sincerity and sarcasm. An awkward silence filled the store for a good minute, until the cashier spoke up. 'And that'll be three dollars and twenty cents.' Simon noticed that he forgot to pay for what he had bought and nonchalantly took out his wallet. 'Three dollars and twenty cents?' he asked for reassurance. He supposedly didn't want an answer though as he begun to sift through his piles of coins. Simon checked his watch and figured out that he wasn't going anywhere else for a while. From there, it was his decision of where 'fun' would lead him to. 'Let me see here. I've got this many hours to spare, so...' He took a handful of quarters, nickels, dimes and pennies and used a pair of tweezers to attain what he needed. 'One penny, two penny, three penny, four penny...' The cashier grew with impatience as Simon proceeded. 'Sir?' 'Shhh! I'm trying to count. Now look what you did. For goodness sake, man. Look what happened. Do you have any manners?' Simon stated with disappointment. By now, he was becoming extremely moody, but was able to catch himself before his temper got out of hand. With a few deep breaths, Simon was ready. 'I'm cool. It's cool. Don't worry. It's all good. I'll just start over again.' The cashier reached for his magazine while Simon willingly went back to square one. 'One penny, two penny, three penny...' Surely, this is how every Saturday should be spent. That is, if you don't want to live past forty.
