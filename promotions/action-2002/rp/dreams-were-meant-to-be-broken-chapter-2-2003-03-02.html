Dreams were meant to be broken: Chapter 2

2 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Clip. Clop. Clip. Clop. He slowly stepped across the bloody asphalt toward the man in the chair. He knew he'd have to follow by the dreams rules. He could make his own rules inside as he went, but if he broke the dreams rules something darker would arise from the trouble. He always abided by its rules. Always abided. He came closer to the man, or body, tied into the chair. As he came nearer it slowly began to turn, as if it were greeting him. But it swivelled without help from the body, as it's legs couldn’t reach the ground. He came closer to the body, but stopped being a metre away from the chair. It still turned to meet him. Whether he was breaking the rules or not, one metre was good enough for him. This was close enough. He knew how these dreams worked. The chair had turned to face him, but the head was hanging down into its chest. It dropped there lifeless, but it couldn't be lifeless, because the chest heaved as it breathed. It was alive. Whatever it was, it was flesh and blood, alive and kicking. It was taking breaths more easily than Fiend himself. Yet he just watched the body, unable to see who it was in the chair. Waiting for it to raise its head up and stare at him. There was a bang and the factory came to light. It was as if had become midday, everything had become blue. Night had become day. Asphalt had become grass. Roof had become sky. Asphalt had become grass? Roof had become sky? I no less then a blink of an eye, a deserted warehouse had become a grassy meadow. Cows and all. And the man tied in the chair now stood before him. 'Oh fuck, it's standing, what do I do?' Fiend asked himself as the body stood before him. It's arms outstretched into a hug, it's head tilted to the side and it smiled. It smiled in a familiar kind of way. And it beckoned. 'I don't wanna go to you, capische?' he told the body. And yes, it was a body. It was rotting and decaying and smelling like worm shit. It was a body, more of a corpse than anything. More worm food than anything. 'CoMe To YoUr fAtHeR, bOy! CoMe HeRe!' wailed the body. He stood there staring at the body before him. Could it be? Could it really be him? 'I don't know if this is a dream or a nightmare,' he thought to himself. Nightmare? Dream? Reality?
