Date: 01-04-03 Thursday, June 4th, 1998 Jordan Copeland had been interning at the fairly new Kiss 92 FM radio station in downtown Toronto for just a few weeks. The station had opened just a few months earlier, and they were hiring kids fresh out of college to do some internship. They would listen and watch the hosts up close, pass out mail, sweep the floors, basically pay their radio dues, until they were given a midnight time slot where they would get to host to the lowest amount of listeners the station would get. Jordan wanted desperately to host a radio show, it had only been a few months since he had graduated the University of Toronto with his degree in broadcasting, and he wanted to get started. He knew that he had the charisma, and the talent to be ten times more entertaining than the people that were on the air. Shabazz entered the studio cautiously with the fan mail for the shock jock radio show host: Tarzan Dan. He acted immature, stupid, and horribly unfunny on the air, and that wasn’t too far away from his real personality. Jordan knew, from firsthand experience, that Dan liked to make fun of the interns on air; it gave him the feeling of being better than everybody. Jordan went into the mail cart that he was pushing as he came around towards Dan. Dan was on the air, which was a rarity, only one other time had Jordan delivered the mail to Dan while he was on the air, and Jordan got made fun of while he did it. He wasn’t in the mood for any type of BS today, and he knew that Dan would give it to him. As Jordan dropped a box full of mail on the desk in front of Dan, Dan began to laugh. “Ladies and gentlemen, the world’s ugliest intern just entered my studio, and for some queer reason, he’s avoiding eye contact with me. I think he’s trying not to fall in love with me. Because ladies and gentlemen, not only is this intern ugly, but he’s a queer. He’s an ugly queer!” Dan began to laugh at his own horrid joke. Jordan Copeland didn’t want to hear it, he shoved the mail cart out of his way and he grabbed Dan by his cheap flannel shirt. “Don’t talk to me like that,” Jordan said as calmly as an angry man possibly could. Dan looked around and laughed nervously, “Ladies and gentlemen, we’ll be back right after this brief commercial break.” Dan pressed the button that led to the commercial. He saw security coming around the corner at an intense pace. “You’ll never work in radio in your lifetime, buddy,” Dan said like an evil movie director with an insubordinate actor. Jordan knew he was going to be fired. Out of frustration, Jordan shoved Dan back into his seat. Dan fell backwards and rolled, head over heels. He came to a violent stop when his head slammed violently against the glass wall that allowed people to look in from the hallway into the studio. Tarzan Dan’s eyes rolled into the back of his head as security barged through the door and tackled Jordan Copeland. One of them radioed the call into the police station and requested an ambulance and two officers be sent to the Kiss 92 headquarters, which was just a few minutes away from both the St. Joseph’s hospital, and the police station. Jordan Copeland was in a world of trouble.
