Stupid Is, As Stupid Does (Part 1)

28 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 28th, 2002 Static filled the entire television screen in some random suburban home as a pimply-faced, squeaky-voiced teenager sat a few feet in front of it. “Is it on?” Oh dear. The teenager cringed and sat up from the rug before disappearing up a small flight of stairs to his room. What could deter a young boy so much as to actually pry himself from his most prized procession? A thing he loved and spent more time with then his on family? Jeff Garvin. Complete with his two-dollar hair-cut and look of utter stupidity. It was time, once again, to enter a deep, dark pit that not even the legendary Indian Jones would dare attempt to leap across in fear of what it might contain. That being the mind of ‘the Original’, of course. He tapped at the camera’s lense, his tongue stuck out and twisted to the side. “So you really are the Joey Malone that my son is always talking about? He sure would be happy to get an actual autographed picture of his hero.” A man, with a Japanese accent is heard in the background, most likely being the one holding the camcorder which was pointed at Jeff’s ugly mug. Garvin’s eyes shifted from side to side, “That’s what I said wasn’t it, chink? I said I was Joey Malone and that’s who I am! Get it?” “Okay.” The Japanese man replied sounding a bit scared of the unstable professional wrestler in front of him. Was this who his son deemed a role model? Let’s go back about ten minutes… Jeff had been wandering the streets with very little to do, having already finished his daily work out and downed his protein shake and cereal bar, that was until he spotted a short tourist from Tokyo, Japan with an expensive, Sony video camera in hand. He had over heard him asking a young brunet if there was any seats left for Pressure Point 36 because he needed to buy one of those autographed Joey Malone t-shirts (that could only be purchased at a live event) his son had been asking about for his birthday. Then, being the crafty, slimy devil that Jeff is, he pulled him aside and proclaimed that he was indeed Joey Malone and if he did him a favour, that he would sign whatever he wanted. “So… What exactly do you want me to do, Mr. Malone?” Jeff took a deep breath, looking around, “I want you to follow me around as I do my daily routine, you know, so the people can get to know Jeff Gar-“ He stopped, seeing a look of confusion beginning to form on the his camera man’s face, “Erm… Ah… JOEY MALONE… A little better.” “Oh…Kay… So… What now?” Garvin tented his fingers. “We make magic… That’s what.” Magic that would turn out to look a whole lot like a even more offensive version of the show Jack Ass… The fun was about to begin.
