Action! — Tales

One in a very large number

20 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 29th November 2002 “MUM! For christ’s sake, SLOW DOWN! You realise how close you were to going over that embankment with a twenty metre fall?” screamed Airborn Tyler as his Mother careered around another corner, narrowly missing an oncoming lorry. “No, how close?” asked Jane Tyler whilst trying to juggle between the steering wheel and her glasses. “TOO FUCKING CLOSE!” shouted Airborn, before ducking down and shielding his eyes when his Mother decided to cut up a red Porsche. It’s a wonder she had insurance to drive the car. “GET OUT OF MY WAY RAT FACE!” shouted Jane, waving two fingers out the window at the car she’d just hit on the bumper while overtaking. Airborn started whining to himself. “Dear lord,” he started as he bowed his head, “let me come out of this car alive and I swear I will never make fun of Christopher Fox again, even if he is a two faced Pokemon loving bastard.” As if his prayer had been answered, the car stopped for a set of red traffic lights. Silently thanking the big man in the sky, Airborn opened the car door and leapt onto the sidewalk. “AND WHERE DO YOU THINK YOU’RE GOING?!!” screamed Jane. “GET BACK IN THIS CAR THIS INSTANT BEFORE I GROUND YOU!” “I’LL SEE YOU AT HOME THEN!” announced Airborn before diving into the nearest public toilet he could find, and slouching down on the seat. Man, was he glad to have escaped from that death trap alive. “And Christopher Fox is still an asshole. I swear that if he says ‘Big Time’ one more time I will kill him, ‘BEAUCOUP GRANDE TEMPS!’ Heh, yeah.” Thinking that he was smart for turning ‘Kid’s catchphrase into French (he’d actually done it wrongly, he’d basically said ‘very big time’ whilst committing the cardinal sin of turning ‘grand’ into feminine when it should be masculine. Mme.Montlibert would roll in her grave, or at least on her desk chair), Airborn danced out the cubicle and into the hustle and bustle of a rush hour of Montreal. His oversized black shoes (the same shoes he’d had since leaving school a year earlier, he was too lazy to replace them) clipped and clopped on the pavement, while his long blonde hair stood out on a street where neatly groomed brown hair seemed to be the fashion. Airborn shivered at the thought of being a businessman. Having to wear horribly horrendous suits, lumping around a large awkward briefcase, and actually having to CLEAN his shoes once in a while did not appeal to Airborn. A piece of advice from his Year 9 textiles teacher Miss.Ellard stood out in his memory. “You, Benjamin Tyler, are a waste of space. As a result the world will do absolutely nothing for you, so just be as lazy as you like, as long as you stay out of my way.” Airborn had taken that advice literally. He figured that since Miss.Ellard now resided in Milton Keynes, England, it was okay for him to be lazy. As long as her female moustache didn’t come sniffing him out. Realising that he had precisely twenty seven minutes to get to the arena for a pre-Pressure Point meeting (no, Airborn can’t read a watch, he asked a stranger), Airborn decided the best policy to run. Rocking back on his left foot, Airborn looked for a gap in the eight lane road which confronted him, everyone with traffic moving at 30kmh. Of course, Airborn didn’t realise that. Determined to make it, he pushed off as fast he could go. Past the first lane. *crash* Airborn had crashed and burned before even getting a quarter of the way across the road. Luckily for him he knew how to fall (the only thing he did know how to do), and he rolled through. All the traffic in surrounding lanes stopped, and horns soon started honking. Slowly, Airborn climbed to his feet and dusted off his clothes, although it didn’t make any difference such was their natural state. Time to cuss out the asshole who’d tried their best to run him over… “You wan…CORAL AVALON! What a pleasant surprise!” Inside the Ford Fiesta, Coral Avalon sat behind the steering wheel, and his jaw almost hit the floor. What were the chances of knocking over Airborn Tyler at 6 o’clock in the evening in Montreal when traffic levels were higher than a Kylie Minogue skirt. Wait, that’d make it low. Hmm, give this rookie narrator a break, it’s my first day on the job. Haven’t you noticed how this story is already longer than most of the previous ones combined? I’m trying to get a work rate here! Anyway, Coral Avalon’s jaw hit the floor. For a few seconds, he clutched the top of the steering wheel with two hands, and looked up at his co-worker. Shame it wasn’t Jeff Garvin. Oh well, you can’t have everything your own way. “Hey, Boral Tagalong! The chances of you…a wanker…hitting me…an A Star Student…what are they? Like, really? Sure, the other way around, because I have good aim, but like, you need contact lenses and you STILL hit me? You know, if you lived in this hellhole of a city I would sue you. Wait, that’s physically impossible seeing as I have nothing to sue them for. Hmm.” Airborn pondered this point for a few seconds, whilst flipping off all the drivers impatiently waiting for him to move out of the middle of the road. “Anyway, wanker, any chance of a lift?” Coral Avalon had wound down the window to try and argue the toss, but it was too late as Airborn had already skipped around to the other side and hopped into the passengers seat. “You don’t mind taking me? Great! Come on then, lets get to, um, that capital of this country, what’s it called, Washington?” “We’re in CANADA.” “Yeah, exactly, the capital of this country, Washington, HIT IT BORAL!” Sighing, and knowing he couldn’t get out of this, Coral put his foot down gently on the accelerator and the car became mobile. Airborn started to fidget, and turned on the CD player. “This car is SO small! I guess people like you who have no friends don’t need room though. And what do you call this music Boral? It’s like, Our Lady Peace. Come on, give me some classical music, any Andrew Lloyd Webber? Now he is the MAN!” Coral sighed, as Airborn scrimmaged through the glove compartment for some music more to his taste. Perhaps sharing a lift with Jeff Garvin would be preferable. At least they could argue to pass the time, but arguing with Airborn Tyler was like arguing with a child: no matter who had the better point of view, the child would always win because he was too young to understand otherwise.
