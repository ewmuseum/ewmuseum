Your mental bull - Part 2

20 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 21st July 2002 The city of London is a big place. Within it is many different boroughs, attractions and cultures. The centre of London is normally just one long woven blanket of noise, hustle, bustle, and frustration. On this fine Sunday evening, as Airborn Tyler walked around the city with his hands in the pockets of his leather jacket, nothing appeared to have changed. He had been out of the country for a month, working on the road with Action! Wrestling, mingling with the boys and picking up the trade. It had been half a year since he’d left EYE, so picking up the routine hadn’t been easy. However, Airborn had been given the weekend off, and he chose to return to his roots and generally chill out. Fat chance of that. The London Eye is basically a giant ferris wheel, which gives all those on it a sky high view of scenic London. As the Eye came to a hault, the final people piled out, and those still queuing (the average wait is half an hour) were told by the security that the Eye had closed for the day and to return in the morning. Some unsatisfied parents left grudgingly with words of contempt, and timid, hot and bothered children poured their eyes out. Shaking his head at the scene, Airborn walked across the park and onto the pathway that ran along the River Thames. In the distance he could see the Tower of London, Tower Bridge and Canary Wharf, three sites synonymously associated with the capital city of England. He missed all this while away travelling. Broadway had nothing on the Globe Theatre, and there wasn’t a clock in the world as impressive as Big Ben. Nonetheless, he couldn’t help but feel slightly ashamed at the way his fellow countrymen were being treated. They’d been queuing for half an hour and then they’re brushed off like insignificant shrubbery? That is totally uncalled for. Still, Airborn put his nationalistic pride on the backburner and tried to relax. Tonight he was dressed to impress with his designer black sweater, black leather jacket and black jeans. The white Nike baseball cap prevented anyone from interpreting him as a goth, and his slicked back long blond hair couldn’t help but impress any woman who laid eyes on him. Right? Wrong. Airborn might have thought he was God’s gift to the female species, but as he leant against the metal rail stopping him from falling into the River Thames, he looked decidedly mediocre. Not exactly butt ugly, but certainly no Ricky Martin or Mel Gibson. His nose was slightly contorted from a year’s worth of botched moves in training, and a small scar above his right eye certainly wasn’t a love bite. And deep down Airborn knew it. Hurtfully, he recollected another moment from his childhood when he was laughed at, sneered at. Mocked. 5 years ago, when Airborn was 13 year old, his school had a Year 8 Ball for all the students to celebrate getting through the year successfully. Airborn dressed up in fashionable suit, rented from the local clothes shop, and strode into that school hall with as much gusto as he could muster up. Not that he could muster much at all. Years of bullying, torment and friendless summers had shrivelled him up into a wrinkly ball. He sat down at the table with seven people whom he knew hated his very guts, and managed to eat the meal that accompanied it. Melon, lasagne and cheese cake didn’t mix well with his stomach, placing him in a worse mood than ever. After the meal though came the coup de grace, or in Airborn’s case, the coup de misery. The cabaret. Airborn was a half decent singer in his youth, and it was in a blind moment of madness when he volunteered to join the other nine acts and sing a slow ballad. He found a group of girls to sit next to, and he watched the other acts with them. One girl in particular he got friendly with, and they even managed a conversation. For the first time Airborn was actually starting to like a girl! Perhaps this would be his lucky night? “Hey, um, Fiona, would you like to go and see a film some time?” For a few seconds time seemed to stand still, as Airborn looked lovingly into her eyes, and Fiona looked mystified into his. Quickly, she made her excuses and hurried off the toilet, leaving Airborn to ponder over his miscarriage of justice. He wasn’t a bully, a womaniser, he was just a decent self respecting young male, but the first time he plucked up the courage to ask someone out, they blanked him. How could God let this happen? Emotionally distraught, Airborn still got up and sung his song. Badly. From the second the piano till the second the lights shone on him till the second he hit his last note, he was a complete and utter nervous wreck that served for nothing other than comical relief amongst the audience. He received polite conservative applause at the end, but Airborn knew what they really thought about him. And as Airborn looked down into the River Thames Sunday evening, preparing to go clubbing, he couldn’t bring himself to drag his feet towards the club. Mentally he was still scarred from that experience when it came to women. He’d never been out with a girl, and he was certain no girl had ever fancied him. Suddenly not in the mood for partying, Airborn pulled himself away from the railings and strolled back to his hotel room. Why was the world so cruel to him?
