Taking Stock (The Way Things Were -OR- Duel 101)

11 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Put yourself for just one moment in the shoes of Alex Lawson. After taking nearly a year off from your chosen profession of professional wrestling, you chose to return to the ring, this time in Action Wrestling. Having spent the time off alternately honing your craft and pining over your lost love, you returned the ring more focused, more powerful, and more than ready to move on with your life. However, two old faces have returned to wrestling, also choosing Action as their new stomping grounds, and they’ve forced you to take stock of your life and make a decision. The first is a former friend and training partner who showed up in a one-shot appearance to answer a challenge that you somewhat foolishly laid out, only to parlay that into a full-time job in Action. The second is ostensibly not who you believe him to be, but every fiber in your being is telling you that the body language that you’re reading isn’t wrong, and that he is in fact who you believe him to be, in spite of all the histrionics to the contrary. Add in the scars and the hand, and if he unmasked to be anyone else, the moment would be so surreal that you’d swear you were dreaming. If you were Alex Lawson, you’d realize that in order to plan your future, you need to face your past. ***** For the first 7 years of your life, you’d lived a normal, happy life, almost like something out of a television show. The only thing missing was a white picket fence. Shortly before your 7th birthday, your father, a dedicated police officer in Philadelphia, was shot and killed during a routine traffic stop. After his funeral, you spent two weeks in the hospital after a fit of rage caused you to lose consciousness, your head hanging in your bloody and battered hands. The following two decades were more or less spent living a lie, as you repressed a major part of your personality and allowed a fractured, simplified version of yourself pretend to be you, an actor in a role that never ended. All that changed in December of 1999, when severe head trauma allowed all those repressed feelings and emotions to manifest themselves as a split personality. Horrified at becoming a hostage in your own body, you could only watch helplessly as the monster that you had unknowingly created did everything in its power to destroy all that you had made for yourself. And then the fun came. More head trauma set you free, but also forced you to fight a war with both of the other occupants of your head for control. Fortunately, a friend and fellow wrestler was able to use his background in psychology to help you mend your wounded mind, and you found yourself in love, happier than you could ever remember being. But the return of a familiar face, the man who had originally driven you to the brink of insanity and then sent you crashing over with a ladder wrapped around your skull, forced a regression in your therapy, and you were once again relegated to the role of the watcher, staring blindly as everything that had so meticulously been cultivated was either destroyed or forced to wither on the vine long before its natural course could be run. Further therapy with a certified professional and a much-needed and long-overdue asskicking brought you to where you were today – attempting to not only pick up the pieces of your shattered life, but also to set right the wrongs that had been done in your name by the entity that had now become a part of you, back where it should’ve always been. ***** As odd as it had been at first, you had to admit that the night spent at the bar with Frank Heimdal had been strangely euphoric, even as it had been painful to bear. The odd silences where once there had been laughter and joy, the stares that lasted just a little too long and questioned just how truly healed you actually were, and the restraint of full trust, they had all been oddly cathartic and yet still somehow promising. In retrospect, “catharsis” was an ideal term to your way of thinking. That single evening, beginning with the match and ending with the session at the bar, had staunched the bleeding, which, while it had slowed to a trickle, was still enough to leave you dizzy at times. And now that the scar had formed, it should have only been a matter of time before the healing could actually occur, and new growth along with it. However, as was so often the case, there was significant reason to believe that things wouldn’t work nearly so smoothly. And the constant in your thoughts recently was all the more reason to believe that there was no simple resolution to where you found yourself. And after admittedly limited interactions with the individual, you were only left with several unanswered questions, ranging from mere curiosity to outright thirst for knowledge: “Why the charade?” “Why go to all the trouble when your more easily recognized name could’ve surely garnered you a spot?” “Why frogs?” (Aside from the obvious connection.) “Did you do what I’ve heard you did simply to hurt me?” And it was the last question that weighed most heavily on your mind. Though she had never been your possession, and though she was most surely now more remote than she had ever been, there was still a part of you that clung stubbornly to her, and if that had been his sole reasoning, there was most certainly a desire to make him pay.
