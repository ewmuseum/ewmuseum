Two, In which a new Hope is found.

27 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The funeral had few people. Jackie’s mother and two sisters had flown out, but her sisters had left their families behind. Julius’ younger brother Tyrell had come from San Diego to be there for his brother and for the kids. The hall was nicely decorated, with pictures and achievements being the heavy decorates. The urn sat nicely at the top of the podium behind the Priest, who opened the ceremony beautifully with readings from the Bible. Though Julius was a Muslim, his wife’s Christianity was never in question when it came to their marriage. He never liked to think about things like that. On the left side was Jackie’s mother, sisters, friends, and co-workers. On the right sat the Arcades. Julius sat in the front row with Daisy and Donell both on each side. Julius was crying, but he tried his hardest to hold the tears back. Daisy was on the verge of kicking and screaming, but Donell was calm. He just stared at the floor ahead of them, blankly. As the Priest finished, he announced that Tyrell was going to make the first eulogy. Tyrell, who sat next to Donell, patted his nephew on the shoulder and stood from his seat. He hugged leant over and hugged his brother, then made his way to the podium. “Jacqueline Moiré Barter-Arcade was a peaceful woman. When I met her over twenty years ago, she was calm, collected. She always had that look in her eyes as if she was always observing everyone, but taking a back seat. “She was a watcher, in a good way. Being just a few years older than me, she treated me as if I was new to the world, and I was. Her boyfriend at the time, Julius, did the same. But where as Julius was the middle-child in are some-what deranged family, always rough-housing and sarcastic, Jackie was the tender-loving older sister. “She would always take me aside to help me out, and whenever I was down, she would take all those problems away with those re-assuring eyes.” As Tyrell recalled all of his past memories with her in his mind, he smiled. But something turned that smile cold, Julius noticed that. But before he could make any note of it, Daisy broke in to a rage of moans and tears. Julius tried to silence her, but it wouldn’t be so easy. He scooped his daughter up in his arms and took her down the hall. Jackie’s mother watched on, the somewhat odd display seemed to ease the tears. Tyrell decided to continue on with the eulogy, so that the celebration would not turn in to a carnival. Donell looked down the hall, and all he could see was the silent conversation taking place between his father and sister. Julius tried all he could to calm her, but nothing worked. Her face scrunched up and red, but the pain in her eyes was so much more. Her mother was never coming back. Ever. Donell slowly rose to his seat and walked past, almost undetected. Everyone in the funeral home knew what Donell was doing, but they chose to ignore it for the sake of the ceremony. He slowly sneaked behind his father and came in to the point of view of her sister. “Listen, honey, you’re right. Mommy is gone. There is nothing we can do. She isn’t coming back to us. But we can’t be staying sad over this.” She looked at him with those glossy eyes. “Well, we can be sad, now. We all miss her, very much. But if we stay sad, we won’t have time to remember the good times. Like when she put the bananas in chocolate syrup and put it in the microwave before putting it on the ice cream?” The comment brought a warm smile to her face. “And when she would cook us Hamburger Casserole?” Well, Daisy, I know what you are thinking. But don’t think about it. Think about the good.” Julius kissed his daughter on the fore-head and returned to his seat. Donell approached his sister, who was still smiling. “Why are you smiling?” “I don’t know, I guess... I miss mommy...” her smile slowly faded back to the tears, and she made an impacting embrace with her brother than almost knocked the wind out of him. “Who will protect us now? Who will keep all the bad people away from us when daddy is gone?” “I don’know, Uncle Ty?” But then he remembered that Tyrell was gone almost as much as Julius was. Donell did what he thought was necessary at the time. He returned the embrace to his sister, but tighter, and softly spoke to her ear. “I’ll protect you, Daze... “I’ll protect you.”
