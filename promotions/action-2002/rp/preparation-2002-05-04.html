Preparation

4 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 4, 2002 Subjected to staring at a black screen for what seemed to be an eternity, a voice is heard speaking to someone on a telephone. 'I'm going to do what I need to. You think I can do it? What do you mean I shouldn't be asking you? Oh, that's right. I should be asking me, but you need to give me some advice here.' A slight pause occured as the speaker awaited a response. 'No, I'm not smoking the hippie lettuce or whatever you heck you call it. You need to do me a favour and give me some suggestions here. Calisthenics isn't going to do this time, you know. I need something else.' The speaker is noticed coughing before continuing the conversation, talking in somewhat of an anxious tone of voice. 'Forget it, alright? I'm not doing Tae-Bo again. Next time I see that Billy Blanks doing Tae-Bo, I'm seriously going to hurt someone. I know that's the kind of spirit I need, but I already have that. How am I supposed to make a statement when I'm exercising karate style against my opponent? I'm trying to open serious can of whoop tushy on a Michael Myers type of character, not Mike Myers. Yes, they are two different people. Let's just drop the whole Tae-Bo thing, okay?' A large splash is heard as the conversion stalls for a moment before proceeding. 'No, I didn't drop the phone in the toilet. Where am I now? Well the bathroom, but that's not the point. Where is your head at these days?' With the toilet flushed and a door abruptlly closed, we hear from the person yet again. 'Look, just give me something to work with here. A thought, perhaps an idea I could use to gain the upper hand. At this point, I could go for anything.' Soon enough, we are taken to the inside of a busy butcher shop. In the background, cars, trucks, and other vehicles pass by at a steady speed. With a few customers being waited on, Simon Seaman is seen in the back speaking with the manager. 'Now it's not that hard of a job if you know what you're doing.' said the burly man in a filthy apron which appeared to have been last cleaned in 1927. 'If you follow the instructions that I just gave you, you should be perfectly fine. You think you can handle all this? As you can see, this isn't the slowest of days and we need 'It won't be a problem. That's for sure' responded Simon, decked out in black with a red, leather apron over his eccentric clothing. The manager continued to stare at the employee in this get-up for a good minute before Simon could explain himself. 'Oh, you might be wondering why I'm wearing this? It matches the blood and I'm really not in the mood to wash it anytime soon.' 'Sure thing.' answered the man as he walked out of the room with a suspicious look on his face. With Simon left by himself, he rolled up his sleeves and decided to get down to business. He then hauled a CD player closer to him and immediately pressed play to get him geared up for work. As Simon increased the volume of what was sure to be an up-tempo, hard rock sounding tune, it was instead the musical renditioning of Latin pop recording artist Shakira. He rhythmically bopped his head to the song, grabbed a slab of meat and placed it on the counter. For a moment, he was missing something and checked around him in search of a meat tenderizer. 'There you go, ma'am. Have a nice day' was heard just outside of the room Simon was situated in. Still in search of it, one thing was clear. He still hasn't found what he was looking for...or so proclaimed U2 on the best-selling album 1987 release entitled 'The Joshua Tree'. Still in stores by the way. 'Where in the name of Punky Brewster's career is that thing?' Simon stated as he began to worry. He examinated every nook and cranny until a sigh of relief eased Simon's mind. Simon then crouched down to pick up the object. While in the store, the manager was busy serving the other customers and assisted several different, reliable employees. One employee though decided to interrupt the manager. 'Excuse me, sir, but who is that new guy?' he inquired. 'He has credentials, that's what. As long as he's capable, you and everyone else should treat him with respect.' Seconds later, Simon could be seen tenderizing the meat in front of him with a trusty steel chair. He quickly wiped the sweat of his brow and continued to work as back in the store, the employee continued to ask questions. 'Though don't you think you should check up on him. It is his first day.' 'Are you mad? The best thing you could give a person is trust and he has mine. Don't worry for goodness sake. He will be just fine.' Next thing we know, large thuds could be heard in the back as Simon began to strike a large slab of beef hanging from a hook with a baseball bat. 'If you want me to check, I'll check, but I assure you that nothing is going to go wrong.' the manager exclaimed. Following that, Simon sat on a chair absolutely quiet staring at the table in front of him. Appearing unbelievably bored, he rested his face against his hand and glanced at his watch. Then from out of nowhere, a several pounds of chicken came crashing down onto the table, breaking it in two. From there, we could see him take notes on what had just occurred. 'I can't believe you don't trust him.' responded the manager as he walked over to where Simon was busy working. As he opened the door, an expression of pure rage was seen over his face as he noticed Simon lighting a 2x4 on fire about to attack yet another object. 'Can you give me some pointers on this? Should I attack Franks like this or is this too obvious?' questioned the manager as Simon held the weapon in his hand. Can you saw fired? Before we knew it, Seaman was seen in the gym. While he used the stationary bicycle, he held a cellular phone to his face and spoke. 'That's the last time I take advice from you.' He'd most likely have to find other means to practice for the inevitable match-up and would be setting out to do just that. 'What do you mean it was $4.99 a minute? I thought this trial was free.' Simon screamed as he instantaneously ended the conversation and threw the phone against the ground in frustration.
