The Defining Moment: Part 3

15 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05/15/03 May 13th, 2003; 11:09 AM. 12 Days until Juggernaut II. Nearly a week had passed, the check had been written and the house declared theirs. Though he rarely returned home after a show, due to his former-apartment being out of the way of most arenas that shows were hosted in, he decided that with the three or four days he would have to himself, why not spend it with his wife? Jeff walked in through the doorway, a beaming smile on his face for a change. He took one deep breath and exhaled. They were the first couple to ever walk over the threshold. The house was only built a few weeks ago; they were very lucky to get it at the price they were looking for. Peeking in the living room, his duffel bag hung over his shoulder, he saw his wife sitting cross-legged on their new coach, her laptop positioned on the edge of her knees. For some reason she got the idea stuck in her head that she wanted to write a screen play. Whether it was trash or not remained to be seen, he hadn't been home enough lately to sit down and give it a read. 'Hey baby,' he said in a gentle tone, dropping his gym bag to the floor and spreading his arms wide. Julie perked right up and hurried over to him. They exchanged a kiss and she led him into the kitchen where a pot of noodles were boiling. Taking the orange juice from the refrigerator, she poured herself a glass, took a sip, then handed it off to her husband who downed the rest of it. 'It's good to have you back, the upstairs sink is clogged, or broken, er... whatever.' Garvin groaned; his head drooped over and bobbed from side to side. 'Just what I look forward to, fixing things.' His said half-smiling. Unlike their on-air counterparts, they didn't despise each other. Though it was a continual game they played on one another; for some reason, they just like to pester each other. 'Ha,' she replied with a laugh, grinning as she spoke, 'like you ever fixed a thing in your life. You're handy work with the dishwasher was the reason we didn't get our damage deposit back when we moved out.' *** Twenty minutes later, he found himself being suckered in to doing the tedious job of taking the sink apart, and as he found out when he went to turn on the lights, the fuse box would also need tending to. Did the two hundred grand he forked over for this place, not include that everything is expected to be in working condition? Okay, no, but he was in the right mind to find the salesman and slap him in the teeth. He bent over, searching in an unmarked moving box for a flashlight. 'Christ,' he whined, giving the cardboard box a swift, hard kick. Nothing- not even that damned pocket-light Julie had given him for his birthday. Which, by the way, was the 'greatest' gift you can give to a twenty-four year old male. Hint the sarcasm? Then, out of the corner of his eye, Jeff caught a glimpse of the window. He looked out to find a man, in his mid-twenties, walking about on the deck in knee-length shorts and a fleece sweater with the sleeves pulled up to his elbow- an odd combination. He watched as he ran a hand through his short jet black hair, then it hit him... Go on over and ask this guy for a flash light. No, he wasn't exactly the most sociable person... but after abusing the cardboard box with his foot a couple more times, stress over took him and he headed downstairs and out to his own deck. At first Jeff didn't say anything, placing a hand on his barbecue. 'Hey!' His neighbour shouted, holding his lemonade up and giving a friendly nod. 'So you bought 'er, huh?' He said smiling; walking over to the side closest to Jeff's house and leaning against the boards. Garvin nodded, he, too, walking over to the side of his deck so to be in speaking distance. 'Yeah, bought it almost a week ago today, always on the road though... makes it tough to really sit down and enjoy anything.' 'I hear that. I do business all over the country; own my own carpentry business... Use to do a lot of the work myself 'till it got off the ground. Now I basically sit back and meet with potential clients,' he replied, 'I'm Dustin, by the way... You?' 'Jeff... Jeff Garvin… Say, you wouldn't happen to have a flash light laying around anywhere would ya?' 'Sure do, just come around and meet me in the garage, I'll dig one out of you.' 'Thanks.'
