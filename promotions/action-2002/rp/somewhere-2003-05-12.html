Action! — Tales

Somewhere

12 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Somewhere… Olivia Heimdal was getting to see her former broadcast partner, Joe Black, for the first time in months. Joe was eyeing Olivia up and down and admiring the beauty that nature and genetics had bestowed upon her. The two were overjoyed to see one another, and they were going out for dinner and drinks. In Philadelphia, Alex Lawson had no idea that that particular chain of events was even occurring. Instead, he was using the few days off he had from Action to indulge in a few of his favorite things: cooking, which had always been a hobby, but had become something of an unhealthy obsession after he began dating a chef’s sister; a bottle of merlot, aged to perfection; and, perhaps most importantly to Alex, finally listening to that Herbie Hancock boxed set he had bought over a month ago. The clear cube did make an attractive paperweight, but it was the music that he had bought the set for, not the fact that it came in attractive packaging. As he settled into the Italian leather sofa that he had spent entirely too much money on back when the paychecks were last regular, a plate of steaming calamari sitting in front of him, the man better known as Duel found that perhaps his choice of music wasn’t the best. And while he loved the music, the melancholy jazz did nothing to help his mood, and he found his thoughts drifting backwards to that fateful moment when it had all truly set in. The slap. He had made his living in one of the most brutal professions in the world and had a body that most professional bodybuilders would envy. He’d taken some of the most extreme bumps that the profession could ever call for and had come out relatively unscathed. In a line of work that left only the strongest surviving, Duel had lasted nearly six years, with only minimal breaks for the injuries he had sustained, which included several concussions and severe head trauma that had put him into a coma. He’d been told on more than one occasion that he’d never wrestle again, but each time, he’d trained just a little bit harder so that he could return to the profession that he loved. And yet the merest wisp of a woman had quite nearly brought him to his knees, and when he thought about her, the first thought that crossed his mind was giving it all up. Not because he thought he could get her back if he did so, but because it was his passion for the business and his unwillingness to take the time off that he needed to get help that had driven her away in the first place. Somewhere… Olivia Heimdal was enjoying her dinner with Joe Black, catching up on old times. He may have even been mentioned in the course of their conversation, but he had no way of knowing, and they may as well have been a world away. In Philadelphia, Alex Lawson was on the second disc of the four-disc boxed set and well on his way through the second bottle of merlot. It had seemed like a hundred times he had picked up the phone, wanting to call her and try to set things straight, but the still-rational part of him, which was decreasing by the glass, somehow managed to get it through to the rest of him that it was over, and no amount of apologies would ever make things right. Resigned to his fate, Alex slumped deeper into the couch and tried to let the music wash over him, and he was mildly successful; however, every time he began to get into a song, Olivia would return to his mind and it would all seem so pointless once more. It was a shame really. Such good music and he couldn’t even enjoy it. He may as well have been listening to whatever crap the marketing department had saddled him with. Two years ago, he probably would’ve enjoyed the music, even relished in coming to the ring to it, but that was a different man. In the past twenty-four months, he’d grown tremendously, and it was no secret that he owed a lot of his newfound maturity to the younger and more beauteous Heimdal sibling. Alex had realized before they had even officially become a couple that Olivia was the most special person he’d ever met, and the changes in his demeanor had begun almost immediately. In their discussions, Alex had realized that Olivia was through with the juvenile and self-possessed suitors that she’d had the misfortune of running across in her history of relationships, and it was his conscious effort to provide her with a viable alternative, in a sensitive and caring older man. And it would’ve worked, had it not been for his repressed identity once again coming to the surface in an attempt to ruin his life. And while that part of him was now gone, absorbed in the wholly healthy psyche that he hadn’t been able to enjoy in over two decades until a few months ago, the doubt still lingered in Alex’s mind. He hadn’t really been truly tested in a match aside from the mask match with Rook, and in that contest, he had displayed more of his dark side than he cared to admit to. Somewhere… Livvie and Joe were discussing how ugly women could revolutionize the wrestling industry, and an in-depth discussions of Joe’s unwillingness to commit his school to training the next generation of women wrestlers had commenced. In Philadelphia, Alex was opening his third bottle of merlot, and this time, when he had gone to get the bottle from the wine rack built into the island in the center of his kitchen, he had left the glass in the sink, along with the leftover calamari, which had been good, but not good enough to free his mind from the burden of the sins of his past. Drinking straight from the neck in gulps that were motivated by a thirst that wasn’t physical, Alex found himself slipping closer and closer to unconsciousness, the first time he had drank himself into oblivion since the night Frank had shown up in Evo. He toyed with the idea of calling the elder Heimdal, but the possibility of Rook Black answering the phone was completely unacceptable, and thus the notion was quickly scrapped. With his options thus limited, he focused once more upon the music, hoping the melodic and melancholy notes could help speed along his trip to oblivion. And with each passing drink, it became a more sound strategy. By the time the fourth disc began to play, Alex found himself pleasantly zoned out, feeling nothing but the music and thinking about nothing but how wonderful it sounded. Somewhere… Livvie and Joe had parted ways, perhaps permanently, over a man that was in both of their lives. Livvie’s tryst-turned-friend and Joe’s son, Randall “Rook” Black. And while Duel had no true confirmation that Olivia and Rook had intimate carnal knowledge of one another, the mere possibility weighed heavily upon his mind. In Philadelphia though, there was only the music, now accompanied by times gone by. All the years spent busting his ass in LCW, only to have Evo go under due to a downturn, both in the economy and in the demand for wrestling product. The friends and enemies he’d made along the way, both in front of the camera and behind the scenes. Laughing to himself, Alex toasted Bob Yobbo, Jr., his first real nemesis outside of the Indies. And moments later, it was with a bitter grimace that he thought of Mike Static, the man who had sent him crashing through the top of a cell with a ladder wrapped around his head. There were more, oh so many more, but only a very select few of them stood out as more than momentary glances into a past that wasn’t fully remembered. Harbinger, Gol, Dean Wallace, David Chambers…they were little more than names now, beside which there’d be two or even three numbers, separated by hypens, serving to indicate his record against each man. And blame it on the alcohol, but when Alex looked at things in that perspective, it was a horrifying account of how he’d spent the better part of his adult life. He was approaching thirty and had no love life to speak of, only a few associates, and no real friends, thanks in large part to the mental disorder that had plagued him since early 2000. The only thing he had going for him was a stable job, and in this business, stable was about as relative a term as they come. It was depressing in a way, but Alex knew that he should be celebrating the fact that he was still able to find work in spite of his history. With a chuckle, he supposed he was lucky that his new boss was an idiot. Somewhere… A young man was struggling with demons of his own, as things in his own life weren’t proceeding as he had hoped. Surprisingly not all that far away, a young woman was considering the decisions and choices she had made in the past year, and because of a combination of emotions and alcohol, she found herself wavering. In Philadelphia, Alex Lawson polished off his third bottle of wine and stretched out on the welcoming leather of the sofa. Just as he felt himself drifting off to sleep, the phone rang…
