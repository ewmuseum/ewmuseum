Who Is Jamal Wilson? -2

27 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/27/02 For the first time ever I had actually said something that I felt. It was no longer being fake and acting as if everything was fine and dandy. I told her straight up, I didn't like what she was wearing, and I didn't want to be anywhere near her on the dance floor. ICKY! How embarrassing would that have been? Me dressed as nice as I was, dancing with her, looking like she just jumped out of a Jennifer Lopez video. What a hoochie. That was the start of it. I didn't know that it was a turning point in my life, but it turns out that it was. The fact that I verbally bitch slapped a girl because I didn't like what she wearing made me feel good inside. It made me feel better than her, because I knew what the trends were and because I knew how to dress properly. The next day I awoke from a deep sleep, I was excited. Usually when I woke up in the morning the only thing on my mind was fashion. But this time, on my mind was something completely different. It wasn't even one thing; there were two things that were on my mind. First off, for some reason I really wanted to watch Ricki Lake and Jenny Jones this morning. But the problem was I had two classes. My entire life I had been a straight A student. When I say 'Straight A Student' I mean it. From elementary all the way up until the point that I was in now, I had gotten straight A's. School was easy for me and I enjoyed getting the knowledge that I did everyday. It made me happy. But for some reason that day, I decided not to go to English Literature and to French advanced. No, instead, I curled up in front of my television with Ben and Jerry's Rocky Road ice cream, and I watched Jenny Jones and Ricki Lake, who conveniently enough, were placed back to back by my local broadcaster of Fox. That day I was intrigued by some of the arguments that went on these shows. The first one was Jenny Jones; the subject title of the day was 'I Went From Geek To Chic'. That sounded like my life story. I grew up in a good neighborhood, I got straight A's in school, and I had very few friends. My entire childhood, I was a geek. But the night before, it was if I had changed. I had gained confidence, and an ego of sorts. But the confidence and ego came from one thing; the clothing that I wore. I knew for once that I was looking cool. I was looking nice and sexy. And I saw people in that club the night before, that didn't deserve to get in on the merit of what they were wearing. It was so... Ugh. After the Jenny Jones show, Ricki Lake went on. It was essentially the same show as Jenny Jones before it, but this one was different for one reason. That reason that made this show different from Jenny Jones' show, is that the people who grew up to be different/cool, they were allowed to confront their tormentors. As I watched these people confront those who had called them names in high school and the like, I realized, these people weren't all that good looking... In fact, they weren't even dressed properly! All they had were their high paying jobs, and stable home lives. This brought me to the question, who would I rather be? Would I rather be the chic looking person, with nice clothes and looks that could kill? Or would I rather be the blue-collar type guy who works hard, and is successful in life? So many questions, so little answers... for now. The answers will come; just stay tuned.
