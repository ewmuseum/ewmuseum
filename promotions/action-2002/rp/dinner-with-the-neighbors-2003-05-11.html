Dinner with the Neighbors.

11 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05.11.2003 Jessica always thought they should get to know their neighbors, become friends with them. Xander didn't exactly prescribe to that line of thinking. The less social you are with the neighbors, the less shit they borrow, he always said. Okay, he never said it, but thought it. But, with Xander's back in the way it was, and with his refusal to tell his wife what the doctor's diagnosis was, she had used it to her advantage. He had been forced into a dinner with Meredith and Albus Penworth, the newly married couple from down the street. Dinner was going well-enough. As in, not at all. Jessica had soon found the reasons behind her husband's hatred of being social. The Penworth's were a bit weird. Albus was a taxidermist and felt the need to explain, in detail, everything that went into stuffing an animal. Yeah, during dinner. He went into great detail on stuffing a chicken. Why someone would want to stuff a chicken was anyone's guess. But he did it. Guess what? They were having chicken for dinner. Jessica desperately wanted her husband to get the people out of their home, but Xander just smiled at her. She had wanted it, and he wasn't going to do anything to stop it. Oh, how she wanted to kick him so. She did stomp on his foot once. Putting on a big grin when the guests looked at her strangely. Oh, there was a wife, wasn't there? Meredith. She seemed normal enough. Except for the whole 'I'm-a-stripper-on-weekends-and-a-bristel-adder-at-a-toothbrush-factory-during-the-week' thing. Um, yeah. She, too, had to go into detail on both her jobs. Man, that was boring. Then it was time for her toothbrush factory details. Jessica was ready to just commit suicide. Xander, however, had begun to drift. His mind floated from the dinner table and into a back alley. He was kneeled down and leaning up against the wall. A man in front of him, with a mohawk and lots and lots of gold, turned around. MR. FUCKIN' T, BABY! 'I pity the fool!' Xander smiled. That's all Mr. T had to say. Of course, Xander was a smartass. Once Mr. T had turned around, he tapped the big man on the shoulder. Mr. T turned back around, 'Um, T, can I call you T?' 'I pity the fool who calls me T.' 'Yeah, um, sorry. But, I was wondering, do you just say that for the money, or is there a reason you,' he used finger quotations, 'quote, un-quote,' pity the fools?' Mr. T quickly quipped, 'I pity the fool who steals my gimmick.' 'Right,' Xander said. 'I'm sorry about that. Just asking a question.' Mr. T, then slapped Mr. Dox on the arm, as if he had just saw him for the first time. 'Hey, you wanna ride in the van? The A-Team van?' Xander's face lit up. But then he stopped. He waited. Mr. T looked around for a moment and then let out, 'I pity the fool who don't take me up on my offer to ride in the kickass A-Team van! Do you want me to pity you, fool?' 'Oh, no sir,' Xander remarked as he shook his head. 'I'm not no fool, that's for sure.' 'Alright then, let's RIDE! 'Cause, that's what I do when I don't have to pity any fools!' Xander was all like, 'ooookay, this is getting to be a bit much'. But all the while, at the dinner table, Angus and Meredith continued to tell their amazingly dull stories to Jessica, who had now put her elbow up on the table and was leaning her face against it. You know the way, where the fist is pushing your cheek up into you eye. And she was saying, 'Oh, that's great,' 'Uh-huh,' and the like. Xander, meanwhile, was grinning from ear to ear, whcih didn't help his wife any, because the dumbfuck Penworth's thought he was enjoying their stories. Oh, she was going to let him have it after they left. Back in Marcus' head, he and Mr. T had rushed to the beautiful black A-Team van. No, the rest of the team wasn't there. That upset Xander slightly. He wanted to meet Murdoch in the worst way. Instead, he got to cruise around with Mr. T, at fifteen miles an hour. That's as fast as it would go. Xander was bummed. He looked over at Mr. T, who was laughing like a madman(if you've seen him on Conan O'Brien picking apples, you'll know what I'm talking about). He was like a kid... well, a frickin' retarded kid with a shiny new toy, or a piece of string. Xander said to Mr. T, 'You know, Mr. T, this sucks. I should have dreamt about David Hasseloff and Kit. Now, that would have kick all sorts of ass!' Mr. T shouted, 'I pity the fool you picks David Hasseloff over me! He's the devil you know!' 'I'm sure he is,' Xander said, as he exited the moving van. Luckly, he just came to at the dinner table. Jessica was about to have a nervous breakdown. Xander looked around the table. Meredith was showing some of her new dances. Xander stood up. He rubbed his eyes and then said, 'You've got to leave.' It had stopped Meredith from removing her panties. She looked up at him and then to her husband. Angus then asked, 'Why? Is there something wrong?' 'Besides everything, no,' Xander replied. 'So, yeah, bye.' He then walked down the hallway and out of sight. Jessica was smiling. She then turned to the Penworth's and said, 'That was... interesting. We'll have to not do that again some time. I think you no where the door is.' And she joined her husband in disappearing. Meredith and Angus stood there a moment. They were about to say, 'We should!' anticipating a 'we should do this again sometime', but then realized what was said. Then the left. Never again, would Jessica think about socializing with the neighbors. And never again, Xander would dream about Mr. T and his damned A-Team van.
