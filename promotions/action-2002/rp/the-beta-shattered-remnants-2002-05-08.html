The Beta: Shattered Remnants

8 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/8/02 If those walls could talk… The old mansion in Port St. Lucie. Once it was an architects dream. The exterior is in great condition. The interior… That’s a different story. The thing is dark and dingy. Glass is all over the floor. Furniture is broken and ripped. Fine china is spread across the floor, in a broken heap. The walls that were once filled with pictures are bare, with cracks in the drywall along with holes. These things would cry of dreams, now shattered. The basement, once full of wrestling related accolades is now bare. Empty. The practice ring is gone. The neon lights now broken. Those accolades all in a heap in the corner. They cry for a lost pride. A picture, once hung in a place where he would always remember. It depicted a happy group of seven people, seven friends. They stood right in front of an arena in Newark, just a few hours before the IWO's Real Deal Tournament began, which Joey Malone would ultimately win. Seven people... Joey Malone, Keri Lindum, Syphon Fission, Simon Seaman, Cyanide, Quinn Morgan, and Kent Anthason. It now lay in pieces on the floor. It cried of friendships lost. A notebook, full of poems, creative writings, letters, all things that once were the real thoughts behind a man now fallen. It cries of an identity lost. A shattered bathroom mirror. It’s shards spread across the floor. It cried of pain. It cried of torment. If you were able to get inside, you would see this. The emptiness that fills this place. The change its owner went through. It was like a memento, forever flung away from its owner. That man was Rashard Clark. That man was once a icon of wrestling. That man was once a human being. Now… What remains, are these little mementos. Clark would never walk into this place again. Fission owned that body. Fission owned that mind. Or did he? To be honest, that man did not have an identity as it is. He was a lost being. Who knows what that man is? He doesn’t. Nobody does. Maybe this house knows?
