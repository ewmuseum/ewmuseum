This is a [name withheld] / [name withheld] joint. Coral and Rook were left alone. Alison and Livvie did some shopping action, Livvie accompanied by Alex, and Frank nowhere to be found. It was kind of funny, Alex felt that men were obligated to go, so he had offered. Heh heh heh. Never volunteer, meatwad. So, Rook turned to Coral and said, 'Want to do something stupid?' And Coral shrugged and said, 'Sure.' Bwahahahahahaha. Never accept anything Rook Black offers to do, Carl. Never. MetaScape features the work of four artists who approach the traditional premise of the landscape in radical ways. Julie Mehretu, Benjamin Edwards, Torben Giehler and Yutaka Sone represent a new generation of artists whose collective vision has been informed by revolutions in digital technology, mass media, and commodity culture. The exhibition inaugurates Project 244; a new gallery space devoted to the installation of works by today's most adventurous contemporary artists. Coral Avalon tilted his head at one such piece of artwork. Being from a rich family exposed him to a lot of art, and having an artist in his family in his little sister. However, he was a bit confused about this piece he was staring at, and without turning towards Rook Black, he spoke. 'Well. That's... different.' Rook couldn't keep himself from pontificating, 'It's the idea of the changing way of life has changed our minds in a lot of ways. Architecture being a reflection of the lifestyle of living, it's an imposing and significant presence of the popular status in functionality and taste, it's like fashion shows.' Coral stood in silence for a few seconds. He wasn't the most terribly educated person in the world, especially for someone who grew up in a rich family like he did. He tried to register what Rook had actually said, but only came up with a few silent nods and a blink of the eyes, before he actually said something. 'Yeah.' There's something that's just a little strange about hearing intellectual jargon laced rhetoric from someone who had that hint of the Texas drawl. It'd be like listening to the President say something that made sense. Rook continued, 'The artists push the boundaries to such extremes so that the idea drags the consensus by majority in the means in thier chosen direction by minute incriments. We won't ever see architecture like these designs, but these ideas slowly drag the state of architecture kicking and screaming along.' 'Also,' Coral said, trying to break away from Rook smartness compared to his own stupidity and lack of vocabulary necessary to maintain that kind of intelligence, 'Um. I think there's a ninja hiding behind one of those curvy, wavy thingies.' Rook stopped walking, his eyes wide, and his attention rivited, 'Holy crap. Well, Torben Geihler is an anime freak. The Ninja strikes again. Fantastic coincidence.' 'Ninjas have trulty captured the spirit of the young american male growing up in the 1980s.' 'Silent effecient killers, engaging in supernatural powers, doing amazing acrobatics, wearing all black, and just being so damn cool.' Coral smiled, he knew Rook well enough to act on his dorkiness. Still, he sulked to himself. He had been on this little trip with Rook Black for about thirty minutes now, and somehow he was wondering if he should've taken Alex's place on the unmanly act of helping Allison and Livvie shop around. He felt very out of place being in a museum and looking at artwork, especially with someone like Rook Black, who didn't seem like he belonged here, himself. Still, he couldn't keep a charade of ninjadom up any more, and he spoke softly. 'You know, Rook, maybe I was mistaken,' he said, 'I think it's just another curvy wavy line thingie that just happens to be in the shape of a ninja.' Rook removed his glasses, leaned forward and squinted, 'Oh. Maybe you're right.' It's possible that Rook understood that the ninja was a joke. Slightly possible. Rook looked around a bit more, then turned to look at Coral, 'Do you ... like looking at these sorts of things?' 'Not really,' Coral was quick to answer, 'I kinda got force-fed artwork for most of my life, and my understanding of art in general isn't particularly the best in the world.' Coral sighed to himself. His discomfort on the topic of art was evident, and he silently cursed himself for being here. There were only two people that he trusted with the knowledge of his family and his background, one of which was probably resting up in Phoenix, fresh off of retirement and preparing himself to be a father. The other was probably laughing with Livvie Heimdal about how silly Duel looked if he wore the dress that he may or may not have been holding for Allison. Neverthless, Rook felt it necessary to change the topic just a touch. 'Ahhhhhh, is there something that you'd like to do? Maybe something that Allison doesn't care for that you miss?' 'Well, I'm, uh... actually a trained fencer.' Coral said, somewhat nervously, 'Though I doubt you'd let me stab you with an epee.' Rook cocked an eyebrow, 'What's an epee?' Now it was Coral's turn to be smart. 'It's a kind of a sword that fencers use. It's got a bowl-shaped guard, about two or three feet in length. It's long and narrow, but the tip is blunt to prevent injury. It's a little heavier than, say, a foil, which is about as thin, but has a circular guard, has a four-sided blade, and a button on the tip that similarly prevents injury.' Oh yeah, Coral was the MAN at swords. Bow down. 'How does each type make the contest different?' Rook asks as they began to stroll along. 'That,' Coral said, with a shaky smile, 'I do not know. I assume that it'd be the same regardless of which type of foil or epee you use, but I didn't exactly act on a passion of fencing. Wrestling seemed much more interesting.' He held his head. His poor, abused, pounded, previously-concussed head. 'I think my splittin' migraine says otherwise, though.' he added a moment later. Nodding, Rook said, 'Concussion. Bad.' Coral pointed at a random picture to continue to sudden drop of IQ in this conversation, 'Picture pretty.' Taking the level even lower, Rook went with ignorant as well as unintelligent, 'I could do that.' And that was about as low as Coral Avalon was willing to let his IQ sink before he started questioning this dialogue. 'What?' 'That painting. I could do that. It's a play on the people who fail to grasp the quality of work and it's execution, and assume that their limited perception implies that they're looking at limited art. Then they apply their greed thinking that they could earn ficticious sums of money for uninspired work. To uh, continue the joke as it were,' as Rook began to understand that if you had to explain the joke then it obviously failed, he began slowly down his explanation, as if looking for a way out of it. Coral blinked. 'You know, I was seriously hoping I could lead you into a conversation where I didn't have to look like more of an idiot than I already am.' Coral said with a sigh. Rook sighed, 'You know, to be perfectly honest. I've been struggling here. I mean, I somehow get the idea that people like this stuff, and even more absurd, that they would enjoy hearing me talk about it. Even though I know that no one likes to listen to that crap. They want to talk about movies, or funny things, or how monkeys behave, or wrestling.' 'Or swords.' Coral added, helpfully. 'Yeah,' Rook remarked, 'It's rough. I'm usually worse than this. In fact, I used to be a lot worse at these things.' 'I'm sorry.' Coral apologized. Rook rubbed the back of his head, 'There's a way of fixing this. There's a way to communicate properly. A way of explaining things so that people can understand each other. This isn't something that's mysterious or difficult, it's not easy. It's like trying to hard, or something.' Coral had to be the idiot on this one, 'Ice cream?' 'Ice cream good. Let's go,' sayeth the Great White Dope. 'What's your favorite movie?' 'Bridge on the River Kwai.' Avalon answered after a moment's recollection. LegendaryRook: Rook said, 'I've never seen it. Would I know the director?' 'It was made in 1957.' Coral replied. Rook said, 'Ahhhh. Hmm. Sounds interesting. I think I'd have to go with Seven Samurai myself.' 'Tell you what,' Rook said, 'Give me your best impression on what makes your favorite your favorite and I'll give you mine.' 'Well, see,' Coral said, 'I can't really EXPLAIN why I like that movie. I just like it.' 'Well that's actually kind of interesting itself.' 'What do you mean?' 'Well, the film resonates with you. So you like the film. But the precise effect it has on you escapes you. It's a sense of feelings that don't carry over well into expression.' Rook looked up the ceiling, 'Seven Samurai. Well, every time I see it I pick up nuances that I've missed. But what really made me interested in the film is the fact that it's about people trying to cope with something terrible. In some circumstances, there's a relatively simple but difficult solution to the problems, and in other circumstances, the problems were either too complex, or simply insurmountable, or too late for anything to be done. In the fact of all that success and failure, somehow everyone who survives has to find a way to continue.' 'Sounds like me.' Coral said, after a pause. The two of them slowly walked toward the exit, finding nothing more interesting out of this little exercise, though Rook asked one last question before they slipped through the doors. 'Now is it life imitating art or vice versa?' Coral couldn't answer.
