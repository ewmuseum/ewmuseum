Cut the Ribbons prt4 - Patience, Presence, Presents

16 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
'Happy Valentines Day, Ma.' She smiled politely, it was the only thing she could do, she had no clue what else to do to make him happy. They had been together for two minutes and she forgot why she ever ran. He had grown into a fine young man. He took care of business, a specimen of extreme physical perfection. Its ashame he's just so fucked up on the inside. On the other side of the coin, Bobby felt a change in his Mother. She used to be Brave, defiant. When Prisoner 109 burst into their house in Las Vegas, and assaulted his Mom, he had a general idea how it played out. Prisoner 109 made some threats, and Delores Minio started mouthing off. She probably even took a swing at the big bastard. That was the mistake. Growing up Bobby's Mom was always a shit starter, as a teen she beat up bullies for Bobby's Uncle, she beat up boyfriends who did her wrong, she was a real bitch when you got on her bad side. Thats what Bobby admired about her. Her mean streak. It was one to fear, and one to respect. Bobby would have to wait for her to chill out when she would flip out, and he knew just being around her when it happened was for the worse, but he still loved it when someone screwed her over and she exacted revenge. Everything Bobby know's about Actions speaking louder then Words, he learned from Momma Minio. Everything he knew about punctuating himself with a punch, he learned from his Mother. Mother knows Best! Bobby could sense that she was timid now, toned down from the Dee Minio she was before. He could tell in her stance, in her tone, in her eyes. He learned to judge aggressiveness like that, after almost a decade of fighting. She sat down on the couch and he sat down on the recliner positioned next to her, smiling as she reached behind the recliner. 'I know they're alittle late but... I got your birthday gifts here.' She handed him three red boxes with white ribbons for the three years of his life he missed. He didn't know what to say. He remembered that his Mom was always an awesome gift wrapper. He couldn't even see the seems. 'How do I open them?' 'Cut the ribbons, Bobby.' She handed him a small pair of scissors. He looked at the ribbons, they were silk. The wrapping paper glistened in the light, it was expensive paper for such light gifts. She really had gone overboard with the boxes. He almost felt remorse to cut the ribbons, they were gorgeous against the red backdrop. But, he knew it was for the better. He thought opening the gifts was for the better. The seems were hidden under the ribbon. He picked at the paper with the tips of his fingers, then ripped it back, to see a white box. Square, probably geometrically perfect. Bobby crumpled the paper and placed it on the coffee table all to neat for garbage. He lifted the box, anxious at what she could have got a son that she barely knew. The actions of the gift spoke louder then words. His jaw dropped, and he looked at his Mom. Tears beaded in her eyes. He grew furious and tossed the box aside, grabbing the next, ripping the ribbon off, then the paper. He opened it to find the same gift. He threw it on the table in front of him with a soft paper thud and grabbed the third, hoping, praying, it was different. He ripped the ribbon and the paper in one swoop of his clawed hands and pulled the top of the box off with a physical exclamation point. It was the same. It was the same as the others. He wished he hadn't opened the gifts. He wished the beauty of the ribbons kept him from looking. The One Armed Scissor wished he had never cut open anything in his life. Actions spoke louder then Words.
