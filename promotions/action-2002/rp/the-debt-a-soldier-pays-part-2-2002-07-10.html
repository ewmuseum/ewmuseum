The debt a soldier pays (part 2)

10 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 10 The bellows of war rang out. The grunts were neither smothered nor amplified by the atmosphere of the battlefield. The screams and the agonized voices were just there, just part of what this campaign was all about. They were more or less reminders of the fate you would meet if luck was not on your side. While war might involve skill and strategy, it mostly consists of luck. A person doesn’t have the skill to dodge a bullet, nor the strategy to escape cavalry. However, if luck is on your side, you might live through both situations. The General had a lot of luck; he’d had luck on his side for as long as he could remember. Luck, strategy, skill, bravery...the makings of a soldier was what shaped him together. Of course, the General had taken his fair share of bumps and bruises. There was no denying that. He kept going though, and wasn’t that what really mattered? In his mind it was. CRACK! His pistol gave off a round in the direction of a charging Confederate Private. The opposing soldier went down in a heap. Was his heart stone? He killed without a shred of remorse. He killed with no regard to his victim’s life outside of war. To the General, there was no life outside of war. He respected war. He expected war. His opposites should expect war as well. Death is as much a part of war as luck.
