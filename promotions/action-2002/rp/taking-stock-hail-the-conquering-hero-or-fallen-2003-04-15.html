Taking Stock (Hail the Conquering Hero -Or- Fallen)

15 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
So, once again, you’re Alex Lawson, and you’re coming off of the biggest win of your admittedly young Action career. What’s more than that, you’ve just validated your sanity by unmasking Rana Venenosa as none other than the man you always knew him to be, Randall “Rook” Black. So, y’know, with all that going through your head, you’d almost have to be happy, right? Hail the conquering hero, and all that jazz? But, if you were Alex Lawson, that perception couldn’t have been further from the truth. In reality, you were sore, both physically and mentally. Your neck, in spite of being thick with tendon and muscle, had been brutally abused by a wide variety of offense from your opponent, and you’d also suffered a series of debilitating headbutts after the match that had left you dizzy and unsure of your surroundings. And even the blood-stained mask that currently lay on your bare chest couldn’t help you in finding the solace that you so desperately sought. Sure, you’d won the match and proven your suspicions to be correct, but in light of the path that you had been forced to take to reach that point, it left you with only one question: had it all been worth it? After the match, after getting the blood stopped by one of Action’s medical staff, and after the ringing in your ears had subsided, the details of the match began to flood back into your mind – the brutality, the relentlessness, and, most of all, the unwilling pursuit of the destruction of your opponent. It all seemed so hauntingly familiar that you couldn’t immediately place where or why. And then the thoughts of the past year came floating back into the stream of your consciousness like so many tiny fish, the thoughts of the chaos and destruction that you had left in your wake, and, most of all, the thoughts of him. Even after all the therapy and the clean bill of mental health, even after finally convincing yourself that he was gone, you were forced to come to the grips with the fact that you resembled your dark alter ego much more closely than you’d have ever cared to admit. And that was terrifying. You’d based your entire existence since coming back into consciousness nearly two years ago on the fact that Alex Lawson wasn’t Lex Laww, that Alex Lawson wasn’t Felony, and that Alex Lawson wasn’t Duel. And in truth, Alex Lawson wasn’t any of those personas – he was merely a man with a mental problem who was looking for help. But in therapy, you’d also learned that Alex Lawson was the sum of the parts, but it had never occurred to you that perhaps the balance wasn’t as even as you’d wanted it to be. And it was that realization that left you staring alternately at the drab hotel ceiling or at the mask resting upon your chest, unable to sleep. It was that realization that made you wonder about Rook, and about Frank, and most of all, about her. About Livvie. And seeing her earlier in the evening had been pure hell. Your heart had raced, your breath had quickened, and you’d found yourself stammering over all the words that you’d wanted to say to her for so long. And that was before the questions of your inherent goodness or evil had come into play. Once that particular floodgate had opened, the worries had been magnified, the doubts had doubled and trebled in your head, and the ramifications had become infinitely more complex. For there was no doubt that you were still in love with her, perhaps now more than ever, but the nagging question that had plagued you since you’d come to grips with your actions had been about whether or not you deserved her. And it was your love for her that made the answer to that question so hard to come by. It was your love for her that made you want her back in your life, but it was also your love for her that made you realize that she deserved nothing but the best, and if you were really not so far removed from the monster who had broken her heart, then how could you risk putting her through that whole ordeal again? Especially if you loved her as deeply as you claimed to. And the infidelity with Rook that may or may not have happened…no, infidelity wasn’t the right word, because she had come to her senses and left you long before that particular incident had happened…the tryst with Rook – well, that was a lot easier to get over than you had expected it to be. Sure, it still hurt, and badly at that, but seeing her there, so beautiful, so frail and yet so strong and determined, well, it was so much easier to forgive her than it would be to forgive Rook. But it was the past, and regardless of how close she and Rook were now, you could still remember how your heart had skipped a beat with elation and more than a bit of hope when Frank had assured you that they were nothing more than friends in the bar all those weeks ago. After all, it was for Livvie that you had nearly made yourself whole all those months ago, and if you could nearly overcome a mental illness with minimal treatment for the sake of love, surely you could make yourself a better man as well. Couldn’t you?
