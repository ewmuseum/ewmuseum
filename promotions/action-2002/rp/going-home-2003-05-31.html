Going Home

31 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Tuesday, May 27th, 2003. 0817 Hours. The house hadn’t been visited in over a month. Dust-bunnies collected on the mantle, while picture frames had been covered in cob-webs. Water spots dried on to the windows, with a sun-tattered porch’s paint peeling off the hand-guards. This all-too still setting was just about to become the home, once again, for the new Dyad Champions, the Arcades. They had ridden themselves of the Kia, and were now driving a loaned Blazer, since they were now on the West Coast. California and New York are worlds away, and they had the whole week to relax, then fly back for Pressure Point on Monday, where they would begin their new program. The wind-chimes began to blow ahead of them, as their view was distorted by dust from the rocky drive-way. The headlights gave two great beams that made that obvious. Julius slipped out of the Passenger seat, leaving his bags and Title Belt in the truck, while Donell grabbed his perfectly conditioned Dyad Strap and slung it over his shoulder as he pulled the key from the ignition, then stumbled up the porch to unlock the door. As he had always done before, in his kinder-years, he tapped the chimes, causing a ruckus that would make a dog go crazy. He slowly flung the door open, pushing back to hold it for his father, who just brushed the gesture off. The road had been long, they were tired. The flight from New York to Chicago was hardly comfortable, being that the two were succumb to the non-stop annoyance of a 12-year-old wrestling fan. But they happened to get about four hours of sleep in the AM flight from Chi-to-LA. Still, at 8 in the morning, that isn’t enough. Donell immediately collapsed on the two-seater couch, grabbing one of the decorative pillows his mother purchased for the set. He clutched it as he comforted his head. His father sat opposite, on the Love Seat, slamming his head back on the rest and shutting his eyes. Despite how tired he was, Donell had a nagging feeling in the pit of his stomach. It wasn’t their upcoming feud, with a Legend soon-to-be-named. It wasn’t how they’ll play Donell’s good Title as opposed to Julius’ cursed Title. And it wasn’t how Donell would treat his newly aggravated back. “You had an awful lot of ring-time, there,” Donell stayed in his position, as his father lifted his head back up to stare at his son. “Pardon?” Donell had to get his point across, knowing that his father only hears what he wants. “In that match. You had a lot of time in the ring.” “Yeah, well, you know my style. Time’s change, speaking in a literal term,” Julius quickly retorted. Donell nodded, “MmmHmm.” “Matches are shorter, even on big cards. I ain’t used to this shit, things were different when I was with Atlas. More coherent.” Donell turned his head away from his father, “Well, this is the now so adapt.” The cold response struck Julius. He looked at his son with wide-eyes, and felt the need to further explain his actions. “Listen, I’m sorry about the hogging sight. But you have to admit, it must’a felt good getting the pin. Would you rathered gettin’ more time and me makin’ the pin?” Donell shook his head, and his father smiled. Things had begun to get different. The father/son bond had already began to fade between the two, and was slowly beginning to be replaced with mistrust. But it was a one-way street. Donell tried to sit up, but a quick pain shot down his spine. “Ach, damn. I need some meds.” “I told you not to take that Table bump. With the shit that they build those, it could’a ruptured your C’s.” Donell sighed as Julius leaned forward, getting a closer look on his back. “Nope, they don’t make ‘em like they used to. I’m tellin’ ya’, if they began to-” Julius continued with his regular rants on how the business has changed for the worse, until he had some struggle in getting up without using anything to relieve some pressure on his knees. “Yeah, well, I still am baffled as to why you stayed in that crab for so long,” Donell quickly chimed in, taking his father off guard. After a deep breath, Julius examined the question, and asked again. “Excuse me?” “Well, you know, your knees can’t take as much as they used too-” Julius cut in the middle of his son’s sentence, taking offense to the past comment. “I know my limits, unlike you. My knees handled as much as they could in that match. No more, no less. When you-” Donell slowly turned his head, drifting to sleep, unbeknownst to Julius, with the soothing sound of his rough voice, until one particular comment caught him off guard. “-and with better training, people will watch you wrestle and know that you are an Arcade, and that you are my son. “Speaking of which, I’d better call Rook,” Julius slowly got up, as Donell was struck by his father’s last statement. “Son?” Donell questioned himself, but thought that he should maybe try to get more out of the situation, but do it more subtly. “So, ugh, dad,” he called out, “Russa wants to work more, and Pleh has a good idea for a set-up at Pressure Point.” From the other room, Julius shouted back, “C’mon, Don, it’s 8:30 and I’ve had 5 hours of sleep. Just let me call Rook and go to bed, I’ll be finished in the morning...” Before he could give a comment back, he heard the slamming of the door to the Master Bedroom. Donell just clutched the pillow even tighter as he rolled back to his stomach, slowly trying not to piss his back off. Drifting to sleep, he let his emotions go. 'Swallow your pride,' is what his father always told him. So he did.
