The Building Blocks of Sanity pt. 1

13 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 13th, 2002 August 20th 2002 – Tuesday – 1:30 AM An odour filled the air, the stench of week old garbage; of fish guts and raw hamburger, decaying in trash bags behind the old butcher shop on the corner by the old gynasium. It drifted across the walk way and into the thick wooded area of the park located in the heart of the city, right into the direction of a collection of benches where half a dozen homeless people gathered to sleep each night, with one taking watch at a time. The youngest of the group, Tim, sat indian style by the fire contained in an old oil drum that they found down by docks. Upon catching the thick, disgusting scent, Tim snatched up his book bag and hurried away, muttering, “See you poor fucks later,” under his breath as he left. It had been quite some time since any of them had had a full meal, a nice warm bed, or a shower for that matter, for some it had been years, while others not more than a week. Over the last two months things had taken a turn for the worst in the life of Jeff Garvin, first Kellen Kinkade and his antics drove him from 21st Century Wrestling, and then the moronic OSW fans caused him to get fired. Shortly as weeks went by, still unemployed, the money began to dissapear until there was nothing left and he was forced from his home. Truth be told, he could have very well went crawling back to his uncle who he had betrayed and left bloodied in the middle of a 21w ring last June, only to bring in Jenny Jiggles. As naïve and as stupid as Jeff Garvin really is you’d still think that he would have enough moral fibre in his body not to sell out his own uncle, a man who raised him when both his parents were six feet under, for some slut valet named Jenny Jiggles. But those who assume this, don’t known the Original that well. It goes without saying that Jeff only cares about one person, and that one person is himself. Shortly after he left 21st Century Wrestling, Jenny found a job with Pinnacle of Insane Wrestling, saying that Jeff just wasn’t profitable anymore. What goes around comes around, and just like Jeff dropped his uncle, Jenny dropped Jeff. A man who was once on top of the world, beating the hard core legend Ninja K in a thirty minute submissions match at Titania, a man who could be seen on television every Thursday night, could now only be seen as he was now… A loser. Without a place to call home, without a friend or ally of his own. He had nothing, nothing but a potatoe sack half full of pop cans. Five cents each, his only source of income. Sure, Garvin could have found some job around town, but that would have to be followed up with work, something that Jeff Garvin was not familiar with when it wasn’t in a wrestling ring. Heck, the boy was even too lazy to find his own cans, what he did indeed have he had to bum from other bums. And when that didn’t work, he stole them and ran, and ran, and sometimes ran some more… But usually gives up and hurls a rock towards them. No matter if they’re young, old, male, female. They weren’t taking those god damn cans away from the Garvinator. So there he laid, his body clothed in rags, the front page of a local newspaper covering his curled up body as he snored away. His brown hair longer than usual and his beard thick and scruffy. His potato sack and cans between his legs, safe and sound as he dreamed about naked females sucking on pop sickles as he pleasured their womanly places. Suddenly, in the dead of night, a can silently dropped from the opening and clanged on the ground below. … Only to be snatched up by Bill, the oldest and wisest of all the Memphis hobos. He clutched it to his chest, a single tear rolling down his cheek as this was the final can he needed to buy a shot of whisky at the local pub. Oh how he fantasized about taisting the sweet nectar on his old callused lips. Bill brought the dented Coca Cola can up to his mouth, puckered up, and kissed the steel object. Silence. “MOTHER FUCKER!” Garvin sprung from his sleep, tackling the old man to the ground and locking in a basic headlock. “You think your going to steal the Garvinator’s cans? I DON’T THINK SO! TAP, BITCH, TAP OUT!” Now for some reason, and I don’t know why, Jeff Garvin gets some strange pleasure from making people tap out. Some think it even turns him on, but that’s just speculation. The old man dropped the can and did as he was ordered to do. Three thuds followed and Jeff cackled, grabbing the can and shoving it in his sack. Though before he could relingquish the hold, a light flashed in his eyes blinding him. Like a deer frozen by the headlights of a half ton truck, Jeff stopped, unable to think as he continued to wrench away at Bill’s old, fragile neck. Wait, is that a buldge in the crotch of his pants? Erm… Rrrriiiighhhttt. “Sir, please let go, get down on your stomach, and put your hands behind your head.” A voice came. Soon after a police offer came walking from the shadows, dressed in full uniform, brandishing his firearm. Bill simply laughed as Jeff let him go, struck with fear. “Haha! You’ve done it now, sonny. You bitch ass motha fucka!” The old man grunted, choking and coughing; a hint of laughter in his voice. “Oh fuck, not you again!” The officer said, beginning to run, only stopping when he reached Jeff. “I thought I told you to stay out of trouble? I mean, when I caught you peeping in on the mayor’s wife with your pants down, did I bring you in? No. When I witnessed you urinating on the town monument, did I bring you in? No… But this is the fifth time I’m caught you harressing elderly people.” Garvin quickly raises his hand and replied, “Actually, one of them was a nine year old boy who called me a name. He had to be taught a lesson… Boy did that little bastard tap.” The Original began to zone off, remembering the event in its entirity and how he put that kid in the Hammer Jammer. Jeff cocked a smile and tented his fingers. “Excellent.” In the meantime, Sergeant David Breau looked on in shock that someone would take pride in such a deed like that, especially in the presents of law enforcement. Maybe he was retarded, or slow… That would make a lot more sense. David shrugged it off and dug out his handcuffs, ready to read Garvin his rights. “Jeff, I’m sorry, but I’m going to have to take you down town, you’re a menace to society…” “If I was a menace to society I could be like on BET!” David rolled his eyes. “Just shut the hell up and let me cuff your stupid ass.” Not wanting to wake the other people, the officer clicked off his flashlight and turned Jeff around, placing his hands behind his back and locking each wrist in to the steel constraints. “Hey, did you know there is actually two hundred and sixty seven ways that I could take you down right now, and four hundred and two submission holds that could follow? Pretty neat, huh?” “Hey, did you know that I have a gun and its loaded? Pretty neat, huh?” David shot back, mocking him, getting tired of hearing Jeff’s irritating comments. The Original gulped and stayed quiet the rest of the way as he was lead to the awaiting police cruiser. But Jeff still wasn’t finished pleading his case… “You do know that that old fucker was trying to steal my cans, right? I mean, do you know how many little kids and blind men I needed to scam to get those… A LOT. And not only-“ Click. The sound of a gun being cocked and readied. “…I’ll shut up now.”
