Action! — Tales

Misplace trust - Part 2

18 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It was 10:13pm, and even though there was nearly an hour of Action’s Blood, Sweat and Tears left to run, Siobhan was already leaving. Not by the conventional means, but through the crowd. With tears welling up in her eyes, she walked as fast as she could, hands daintily held in the air as she moved. Security officers in luminous orange jackets stopped enthused fans from blocking her way, and before she knew it, she had walked under a balcony and through a fire exit. The security officers turned around and struggled their way back through the crowd to their original positions. Looking up, Siobhan found herself in an empty corridor, with only one stage hand sitting on a chair, disinterested and reading a glossy programme which held a picture of Christopher Fox on the cover. No one else was around, no one manning the normal positions where they slogged their hearts out to get everything technical tip top. They were all behind the curtain at the top of the stage, gathered around a monitor, watching the last few matches. Siobhan squatted down in front of a television screen, and with her normally shoulder length ginger hair straightened down to her shoulders, tears welled up in her blue eyes. As he looked in the 12 inch monitor, there was a close up of her older brother Lee, struggling to his feet with a massive laceration across his forehead. She’d caused that, she’d hit him with a chair. She hit her own brother. Feeling extremely confused, her knees gave way and she collapsed on the floor. Sitting on the side of her thighs and her knees pointing at a ninety degree angle, she dropped her head into her forearms which rested on the concrete floor, and cried. Salt tears trickled slowly down her cheek and dripped onto her shiny purple shirt. She felt so dirty, so evil. How could she have hit her own sibling? “Be strong,” she whimpered to herself, lifting her head and staring straight at a wooden table leg. Pushing off the floor, she stood up and tried to wipe her eyes clear, causing the skin around them to turn red with irritation. “They had it coming, they turned on Airborn first, I had to stand up for him.” But no matter how much she reasoned with herself, she still felt bad. Across the corridor, the security guard looked up when he heard this young woman crying to herself. With a sigh, he folded up the programme and stuck it in the back pocket of his jeans, before walking slowly towards Siobhan. “Are you okay?” he asked, putting his arm around the young Irishmen and peeping his around the corner of her face. It was normal for people to get emotional, he’d seen it many times, and he didn’t normally do anything to comfort them. Would you go up to a 6’10” man crying his eyes out? However, something about this was different. This woman was but a young and vulnerable teenager who needed some support. “Get away from me!” shouted Siobhan, swiping his supporting hand off his shoulder, and then stepping on his toes. “Just stay away!” she cried, as she ran off down the corridor in her black boots, the high heels clicking on the cold floor. She had to get away from this place she told herself, as she frantically ran around the maze of corridors, trying to find the locker room with her belongings in it. Finally, she found the right one, and ran through the open door, her hair flying in the wind behind her, unable to keep up. She ran around the couch and picked up her bag, before reaching deep into a locker and scooping all her belongings off the shelf, allowing them to fall into her grey rucksack. With a final humph, she tossed the straps over her shoulder, and hanging onto them she exited the room with as much haste as she entered, slamming the door behind her. Then she stopped. Slowly turning around, her eyes focused on a shiny metallic plate drilled onto the door with four screws placed in each corner. The bold text read ‘A-Star Stable’, followed by the names of seven current members. Rage building up in her eyes and cheeks turning glowing bright red, she spun her head around and scanned the hallway, looking for something. Finally, she found it and jogged over to a wooden table with a number of stationery objects on it. Reaching out with her fingers that had pink nail varnish on the tips, she took a marker pen and ran back towards the locker room door. With satisfaction, she plucked the lid from the pen and tossed it over her shoulder. It hit the floor ten metres behind her, bounced twice and rolled under a table, to be discovered by some annoyed arena dustman in the early hours of the morning. Siobhan elevated onto the tips of her toes, and stretched up, her 5’4” body not high enough to reach the name plate even with high heeled boots. Holding the pen in the far reaches of her fingers, she found the third and fourth names on the plate, before scribbling them out with satisfaction. Admiring her handiwork, Siobhan looked up and saw only five names left under the A-Star Stable banner – Airborn and Siobhan Tyler, the cousins who grew up together in Cork, Ireland (even though Airborn was born in London) had left the family faction. Pivoting, Siobhan marched off again towards the rear exit. With new found exuberance, she threw the pen behind her, and it hit the door of the locker room before falling down and landing in front of the entrance to the room, for The Elements to find later on. Stroking her hair to check it still looked okay, Siobhan walked across the road and sat down in a bus shelter. It was raining heavily in New Orleans, perhaps reflecting what had been Siobhan’s mood a few minutes ago. But then again, it was strange what a release can do to you. Smiling to herself, Siobhan stood up with her back pack hanging loosely behind her. The rain flew across the headlights of the oncoming bus, which pulled over and came to a halt with a puff of smoke from the exhaust. The double doors slid open, and Siobhan walked up the stairs onto the four wheeled vehicle. There was no one else on board, and the mood was eerie, not helped by a moody bus driver who grimaced and took a puff from his cigarette. “To the local hotel please,” asked Siobhan, opening her purse and taking out a few coins, placing them in the black tray. The driver, in his dust ridden suit of work trousers, blue shirt and grey waistcoat, reached under the plastic window to take the money, and exchanged it for a ticket. With a nod of gratitude, Siobhan took the paper and sat down on the nearest seat. At least the journey to the hotel was only a few minutes. Then she could sleep in the same room as Airborn, feel secure once more and wake up the following morning with sun streaming through the curtains, and feel a darn sight more optimistic about life. “Hey,” she told herself as she looked out the window at the trees flustering in the night time wind, “it’s a new start.”
