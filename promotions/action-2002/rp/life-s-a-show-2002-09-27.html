Life's a Show

27 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 27th, 2002 Noah took one last hit from his cigarette before throwing it down into the puddle below him. His boots had become water soaked and squished with each step, but at least it was better than hearing that damn tapping noise. The days air had gotten colder as a rainstorm had flooded parts of the town. Dark clouds hung over the sky ready to relinquish the water they had soaked up on the town below them, and unaware pedestrians walked like cattle from their cars to their nine to five jobs. Noah just stood there, eying the crowded streets and the umbrellas in their hands. Briefcases and laptops and watches and palm pilots. This was the day and age of computerized socialization. You want to call a meeting with the board? Let's network it. You want to organize your schedule? Buy a palm pilot. You want to talk to your parents or friends? AOL. Hawkins hated every minute of it. He was the kind of guy that'd like to see you face to face when he had something to say. He enjoyed the expressions and the mannerisms that people would make when both good news and bad news was thrusted apon them. 'You can get a real good feel,' Noah always said 'by how a person reacts to what you say.' For the most part, he was right. So Hawkins leaned against a corporate building, one way windowed for the view without distractions from the outside. No kid with his slurpee tongue today, just Noah. He thought about how all these people are doing things they hate, while he, in reality, was the only one doing what he loved. None of these people wandering the streets cared about their job, or else they might show the least bit of enthusiasm. Smile in the morning on their way to their cubicles. Hawkins loved his job. Noah never felt more alive when he was in the ring. Then again, he felt dead outside of it, so that's not a very astute observation. Noah did what he wanted with his life, and he enjoyed that. Even if it was the only thing he enjoyed. He wondered how he could be so lucky, when other thoughts began to creep into his mind. Thoughts about friendship. Thoughts about relationships. Thoughts about socializing and everything else that the world is supposed to teach him but hasn't. I guess being lucky in one area regulates you to the rest of your life being destroyed. You have a good job, you hate your wife. You love your wife, you're unemployed. You have tons of friends, but you hate your job. The perfect life is so unattainable, even if it really is imperfect. So, Noah's one claim to fame, his reason to live is that he enjoys his job. His JOB, is his reason to live. Beyond the death and the suicide and the restrictions his religion has placed him in, his JOB is a real, concrete reason to be alive. Not someone who cares about him. Not his desire to go on enjoying life. Not the work he's doing in the world that he feels will immortalize him or save mankind. His job, as an entertainer. And it entertains himself. You may get your fifteen minutes of fame, but after that, when you're thrown and tossed to the curb like just another circus rat. What then?
