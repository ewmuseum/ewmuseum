Ship Wrecked: Lord of the Fly Boyz: Part Three

2 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/2/02 Back and forth, back and forth. Their eyes followed. A group of twenty or so children sat near the flower bed below, transfixed on the foot ball sized shell that their leader was moving from side to side in a swinging fashion. A faint snigger escaped Nate’s mouth. These children were his faithful and loyal people. Each did as they were told without any grievance. “Dude, I was thinking, maybe we should head back to the boat, these dwarfs are giving me the creeps.” A rather uneasy Steve-O whispered to his brother as was laid back in the large stone formation resembling an emperor’s thrown. “You can’t be serious dude,” said Nate while he quickly swung the oversized shell to the right, and quickly to the left. Testing his tiny followers, trying to stump their young minds. “Excellent.” Steve-O looked intently all around him. They were positioned in a large clearing on the out skirts of some tropical island neither of them had ever heard of. Part of the reason might have had to do with the fact that they skipped all but one geography class during high school and even during that particular one, they were stoned out of their teenage minds. But even with their ‘wealth’ of knowledge, things still did seem out of the ordinary. The tulopes spoke German fluently, the sky was constantly morphing to different colors (at the present moment it was a dull shape of apricot that Steve-O deemed pretty), and all the children were in loin clothes with the Gap brand name printed on them. Strange, indeed. Steve-O continued to speak softly into his tag partner’s ear, pleading with him to leave. To be one hundred percent truthful, he never really wanted to leave. Steve-O was just insanely jealous that he wasn’t picked to be their ‘God.’ He was ‘God’ material, too. “Can I hold the Conch, dude? Just for a sec.” Nodding absorbedly, taking a moment to ponder this request, he replied, “I don’t know…” “COME ON! GIVE IT TO ME!” He clamped down on the Conch, getting a fair grip, and quickly pulled back- resulting in a seesaw tug of war. One would just get the sacred Conch in his direction when the other would gain a second wind. This carried on; both persistent on being the person who possessed the conch until just one faulty move ended it all. Grease from the potato chips Nate had been munching on during the boat ride over lubricated his fingertips. The Conch slipped from his grasp and into his brother’s awaiting arms. Unexpectedly, Steve-O dropped it and the lost boys’ fragile procession tumbled to the ground with a plain shatter. “Uh-Oh.”
