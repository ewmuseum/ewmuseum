A Nacreous Shade of Gray: Two of negative twelve

26 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Needless to say, the more complex the move is, the more beautiful it is. Add another revolution on a four fifty splash. Makes it that much more amazing. Simply put, that’s my thought process walking into any match. How can I wow you? How much blood must I spill to get your attention? How many strings of gravity deifying offense must I put together to get you people to stand and cheer like wild Indians. You see, after I built myself up, I began going into much deeper training. Running around Los Angeles helped. But not as much as the stamina workouts I still put myself through to this day. It’s a simple workout, really. I do pull ups until my arms go numb. I do push ups until I collapse on my face. I do crunches until it becomes impossible to lift my back off the ground. I then ride ten miles on an exercise bike. Actually, I used to do that workout, until I started doing higher repetitions on simple lifts. Hey, whatever maintains my physique, you know. At the age of fifteen, I was helping my father train the other wrestlers. He put me in this position because he knew I was already further along then anyone had ever anticipated. I catch on to things rather quickly, and after just three years, I was already above everyone else. Wrestling was great. I wrestled around downtown LA for money, which essentially financed my life for there on out. High school was a blur. Didn’t matter. When I dyed my hair cloud white at the age of sixteen, everyone started looking at the Japanese child a little different. I got a lot of hot babes to test my stamina on. Physically and mentally, I was superior to my classmates. Didn’t matter anyway. I was popular, but in a methodical way. I was always walking around by myself, or with some girl. People envied me. In the end, though, at the age I was at, it didn’t matter. My mother had just passed away from kidney failure. I cried that night. Wow, boring shit. Wake the hell up. Father never was the same again. He didn’t have anymore projects, and as wrestling started rising, my stock in the underground did as well. I was only sixteen when I signed a contract to wrestle dark matches with some company I can’t discuss due to contract restrictions. I was making more then my father way at the age of sixteen. Needless to say, I moved out on my own after that. Not the right move.
