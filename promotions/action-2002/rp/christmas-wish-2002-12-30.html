Christmas Wish

30 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/30/02 As the snow gently fell in the chilly evening air, David Brody sat at a small card table in the dimly lit basement of the Brody homestead and shuffled a deck of cards. “Waddayasay Alex?” David said with a smile. “A few hands of blackjack for the last of that tiny paycheck you cherish so deeply?” “Sure, I guess.” Alex shrugged his massive shoulders, not seeming to care one way or the other. He stared at the dull green tabletop, scratching at it with his thumbnail. David felt the awkward silence that usually came when his brother was in one of his moods. They’d been close since they were both children, but ever since the accident that put him in his wheelchair, David felt like he didn’t really know the man sitting across from him. “Decent Christmas this year. Did you get everything you wanted?” asked David, hoping to lure his brother into an actual conversation for once. “No,” was all Alex would say, still focused on scratching the ugly green off the table top. “Did I thank you for the new cushion?” David rocked back and forth, side to side, in his wheelchair. “This thing’s comfortable. It’s like sitting on a cloud.” “Glad you like it.” Alex didn’t sound like he was glad about anything. “Are you planning on watching Players and Pawns? Vic and I might have another present for you. One that the whole country can enjoy as well.” David set the deck down on the table and wheeled himself away from the table. Alex glimpsed with a pained expression at his brother’s atrophied legs and went back to scraping the table top off. The wheelchair darted between furniture and stopped in front of a refrigerator. “You want a beer Alex?” The elder Brody shook his head and worked on widening the small hole he’d dug in the old wood surface. David returned to the table and set the chilled beer can on the table. “I can’t believe you’re getting along with Vic. What with the history you two have and all.” “I hate him,” Alex said flatly. “I can’t stand to breathe the same air as him. But he’s the lesser of two evils. I’ll use him to get what I need.” “Do I even want, or need, to ask what you need?” Alex became silent again. He didn’t need to say any more. David knew what Alex wanted, and he knew how far Alex would go to get it. Maybe it was better that David didn’t hear anymore. He’d hate to have to testify against his brother in court. “Boys,” shouted a woman’s voice from the top of the stairs. “I’m making some turkey sandwiches if either of you want one.” ‘Sounds good Ma!” David shouted. “You coming Alex?” Silently, David hoped his brother would surprise him and come upstairs. All he wanted for Christmas was to have his brother home without the house feeling like a funeral parlor. Unfortuately, David’s wishes weren’t to be. “I’ll be up in awhile.” David became irritated. “Busy digging a hole in the table? Jesus Alex, sometimes I think you’re confused about which one of us is in the wheelchair. If I can let it go, why can’t you?” Alex looked up from the table and stared at his brother. “Oh, you let it go? You’re forgiven Morg for putting you in a wheelchair?” “It took some time, but I’m adapting to my wheels.” Alex stood up and walked over to his brother. He grabbed the side of the wheelchair and tipped it over, sending his brother tumbling out of his cushioned seat. Alex kicked the wheelchair towards the stairs. “C’mon Dave. Wanna go for a run? Maybe you’d rather walk up the stairs instead of taking that stupid elevator thing?” David pushed himself off the floor. “Bring me my chair Alex.” “Go get it. Still feel so forgiving towards Morg?” David crawled towards his chair. “Morg didn’t just dump me out of my chair.” Alex walked across the room and pushed the wheelchair towards his brother. “But Morg’s the guy who put you in there. He put you in a wheelchair. Look at when he's done to you. To us. He’s goinna have to pay.” With the wheelchair beside him, David pulled himself back up into his seat. Alex handed his brother the cushion he’d bought him for Christmas. “Sorry about that. Losing my cool is becoming a regular habit for me.” “Well let’s go upstairs and you can lose it over a turkey sandwich.” Alex took hold of the wheelchair’s handles and pushed his brother towards the stairs. “And Alex?” “Yeah?” “Hurt that big son of a bitch.” Alex smiled a grin that would make the Grim Reaper proud. “Merry Christmas little brother. Merry Christmas.”
