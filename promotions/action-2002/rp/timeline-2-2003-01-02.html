Date: 01-02-03 Monday, December 1st, 1986. A young Jordan Copeland sat down on a snow enbankment on a street corner known as Aylesworth watching the other children having a snowball fight. He despertaely wanted, and desperately needed, to play with the other children, but they wouldn't allow it. They were all white children, and he was black. They didn't want him to play in their game, for some strange reason, they assumed that he would ruin their fun. Jordan had lived in the neighborhood for three years, every time he would go outside, he would have to play by himself or with one of his caring foster parents. It wasn't the same as playing with your peers, people your age who were interested in the same things. Jordan continued to watch them, looking dejected and distant. He was about to cry, so he got up and slowly began to walk towards his foster home. He didn't want any of the other children to see him crying, it would just give them another reason to make fun of him. As he walked through the snow with his snow boots making a crunching nose, a man stepped in front of him. It was a man whom he didn't know and hadn't ever seen before. He appeared to be an African American man in his 40's, and he looked homeless. He had on a very worn brown Cleveland Browns jacket, some brown jeans, and some brown work boots. He had on gloves that were torn at the tip of the fingers, leaving his fingers exposed to the cold. 'Don't worry about them, son,' the old man said in a scragly voice. Jordan tried to ignore the man and he tried to the side of the man and go past him, his foster parents always told him not to talk to strangers, but everywhere he moved, the man moved. 'You don't need them to be happy,' he continued. 'One day, sometime in the not too far future, Negroes like you and myself will rule this world. We'll have our own big houses, and there will be four to five white, houseboys for each black man. I lost my job due to downsizing, and now I'm here, living on the streets. Those honkeys got to know, what goes around, comes around.' Jordan ran past the man, not understanding exactly what the man had told him. The man looked at the boy and nodded his head, before he continued on.
