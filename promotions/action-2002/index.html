An angle federation that opened in March 2002 and wrote every card as television, running Pressure Point weekly for ninety-one of its hundred and thirty cards, giving its first annual awards in March 2003, carrying five championships, and ending on a card of ten scenes and no matches in April 2004. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Action Wrestling opened on 13 March 2002 with the first Pressure Point and called itself, on its own frequently-asked-questions page, “Angle based, one hundred percent”. Its cards were television written down: runs of titled scenes, more than half of them headed by a Starring line, about three to every match, with the matches themselves written up in prose and nearly all of them closed on an Outcome line — results booked by the writers rather than won on a roleplay — and the show, in its own telling, aired on the WB.

Pressure Point was the weekly and carried ninety-one of its hundred and thirty cards; Whatever joined it in November 2002 and ran twenty-two more alongside. Around them it staged fourteen one-off events — Resentment, Juggernaut, Til Death Due Us Part, Down 4 the Count, Retaliation, Last Laugh, Players and Pawns twice, Blood, Sweat, and Tears, Make It Or Break It, Instinct, Out of Control among them — and three specials. Its cards were billed to fifty-five real arenas, the 1st Mariner Arena in Baltimore thirty-eight times, and once to the basement of a tavern.

That basement was the setting of The Action! Awards of 13 March 2003, its first annual ceremony, written entirely as seventeen scenes with no match on the card: fifteen awards, each with a presenter and a list of nominees. Joey Malone took Wrestler of the Year, LLB took Best Champion and Greatest Shock, an Iron Man match between Coral Avalon and Jeff Garvin took Match of the Year, and a Ring of Gold, the company’s own hall of fame, named three wrestlers for enrolment once they retired. A trophy of another kind, the King of Submission, had been settled in February at Blood, Sweat, and Tears, where Jeff Garvin lost the final when his own corner threw in the towel.

Five championships ran through it. The Action Championship began with Chris Fear on 20 March 2002 and passed through nine reigns among eight champions: LLB held it twice, the second reign ending when the belt was taken from him off the card, and Jeff Garvin won the vacant belt at Instinct and lost it in January 2004 when the owner handed it to Phil Atken with no match to decide it. The Bantam Championship was settled by tournament in July 2002; the Carnage Championship and the Dyad, a tag championship with two holders to a reign, ran the length of the company. The fifth, the Action! “Television” Championship, is always written in those scare quotes, carries its own champion marker on the cards, and passed through five reigns among four wrestlers between January and May 2003.

Sixteen of its cards survive without their results. Its own record marks each one, having looked for them in two places, and puts a single scene in its place saying the results have not been located and asking anyone holding a copy to come forward — among them Instinct of 31 August 2003, the night Jeff Garvin won the vacant Action Championship. Three more cards its own numbering proves ran have no record at all, all in the last six weeks of 2003. The weeklies run to 28 January 2004 and stop; the last card is Pressure Point 92 of 7 April 2004, ten scenes and no matches, published the same day as a Main News post of its own titled “The Return.”, and its last word of any kind is a news post three months later, on 9 July 2004, called “Beep… Beep… Beep…”. What survives beside the cards is the cast: 240 biography cards, 830 roleplays and 51 news posts.
