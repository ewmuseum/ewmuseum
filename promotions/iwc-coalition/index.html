Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Internet Wrestling Coalition dates itself by its own words: a 10 November 1997 recruitment post describes it as “a very small fed, with only maybe two and a half months under its belt,” placing its opening at roughly late August of that year. Its surviving record runs to a single month, November into early December 1997, and holds two weekly broadcasts, Monday Night and Saturday Night, alongside a four-hour pay-per-view.

Its Cruiserweight Championship changed hands by choice rather than pin or count. The Raging Dragon, undefeated and champion, walked to the ring against the White Phoenix and relinquished the belt on the microphone rather than defend it in what he called a personal war: “this war is by no means professional... It will become but a token to us, and that will demean its presence,” he told the crowd, dropping the title to the floor before the bell.

Its main roster carried a running feud between two camps, the Usual Suspects and the Extreme Team, that spilled into a masked-identity angle: a wrestler billed as Death Without Permission was unmasked mid-match as Lector, and a second disguised man beneath a matching mask turned out to be Steve Tadd, both stomping the promotion's tag champions before a group including Torpedo, Nate Shaft, Jesse Williams and the broadcast's own announcer hoisted the brawlers into the rafters to end the show.

Its surviving record closes at its own biggest card, the four-hour Ringmaster Tournament of 1 December 1997. The main event put the IWC World Championship on the line in a triple threat: champion Gabriel Whitecross, “The Era of Defiance,” defended against Rocket Launcher and “Suicide Machine” John Payne, and retained the title by forcing Payne's submission with a finisher called the Broken Talisman.

Nothing dated after that pay-per-view survives, and no closing notice of any kind is on record — the last word the promotion left is its own champion's hand raised.
