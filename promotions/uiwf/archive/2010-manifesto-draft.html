The Manifesto — draft

c. 18 Jan 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Planning document A working plain-text draft of the finished Manifesto page, saved within seconds of it on the promoter's second laptop — evidently the same editing session, not a separate earlier pass. Thinner than the finished version in one place: it stops after “entertain us and only us,” without the finished page's closing “beacon in the sky” paragraph. One line still carries a typo (“exagerating”) the finished page corrects to “exaggerating.” Served as its own artifact per the canonical-copy rule, not merged into the finished page.
In September 2006, the Universal Wrestling Federation shut its doors on nine years of history. And when those doors finally shut, half of the UWF raised their heads, said their farewells, and moved on with their lives. They went on to other promotions (Seymour Almasy, for example, went on to 58 and a half promotions in the four years since), to other glories, and to other walks of life. The UiWF has nothing to do with any of those people. The UiWF is ... the rest of them. The ones that had nothing else to go to. The ones that spent the last three and a half years in Chicago eating from dumpsters and holding "Will Wrestle For Whiskey" signs over their heads. The ones no other wrestling promotion on the face of the earth would even consider giving custodial positions to. The UiWF is what Corky Spectre, bastard child of UWF Hall of Famer Lew Spectre, does with his hush-money. This isn't the big time. This isn't even the small time. You might even be exagerating in calling this a flea-circus. The UiWF doesn't have a television deal. It has webcasting done by local-area amateur porn webmasters. The UiWF doesn't have a PPV deal. It has DVDs the quality of which a Chinese vendor would spit upon. The UiWF doesn't have an arena. It operates from a Chicago-area Jewish community center, on Wednesdays, Thursdays, or Fridays, depending on that week's activity schedule. (We affectionally call it the Kosher Slaughterhouse~!) The UiWF doesn't have fans. It has Judith Mannheim, 78, who showed up for Bingo on the wrong day and didn't have anything else to do. The UiWF doesn't have solid gold championship belts. Corky found the UWF's Insert-Name-Here belt on eBay a few months back and slapped an "i" sticker on it. The UiWF doesn't have nation-wide advertising. It has the local Vietnamese drycleaner and a used-car lot (Big John's Working for You!). The UiWF doesn't have a plan. It just ... is. The UiWF is what happens when, four years later, you still haven't moved on. The UiWF isn't punctual. Or well run. Or professional. Or mature. It is here to entertain us and only us.
