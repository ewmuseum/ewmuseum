Genesis — the first card

c. 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
What this is The founding script of the UiWF — the fed's origin story, built around Corky Spectre (kin of one of the Legends) and the returning Rabbi Mort Schwartzman, “once known as the Rabbi of Pain,” and the debut of a roster carried over from the UWF's orbit. Recovered as first card.wps from the promoter's own drive and unsealed under the 2026-08-16 curation ruling. It names no championships — it is a debut card, not a title ledger — which is why the UiWF wing remains an honest blank.

Once known as the Rabbi of Pain, he has long since given up his referee stripes for the somber suit of a local Jewish community leader. But the Rabbi hasn't forgotten his past as an official for the Universal Wrestling Federation. In fact, he's headed right now to the home of a former colleague of his, young Corky Spectre. It's been some time since the Rabbi has seen the boy due to his responsibilities at the synagogue. But that doesn't mean that the scrawny and pimply lad isn't still near and dear to his heart. Coming to a quaint townhouse, the Rabbi climbs the steps and rings the doorbell. As he waits, he checks the sack he carries with him, making sure the soup and rolls inside are all right. "I wonder when the last time Corky had a home-cooked meal was. Oy, poor bubby." No one has come to the door, so the Rabbi rings the bell again. He waits longer, but still nothing. He pokes his head in and can just make out a faint clicking sound. "Corky?" There's no reply but the Rabbi swears he heard what sounded like heavy breathing. Concerned, the Rabbi enters the townhouse. The living area is empty except for mismatched furniture, multiple video systems, and a big TV. A look in the kitchen reveals that Corky has been subsisting off of pizza and Chinese takeout. The Rabbi sighs sadly and puts his bag of home-cooked goodness on the counter. He walks back out to the hall and notices the clicking sound gets louder by the stairs. Cautiously, the Rabbi climbs up until he reaches a door that's ajar.
