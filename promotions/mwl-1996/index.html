Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Mayhem Wrestling League opened its first card, Saturday Night Slamfest, on 30 March 1996, days after renaming itself from EWF once its own operator learned another federation already used that name. That first night settled both of its early titles at once out of a Royal Rumble: Suicide won a mini-tournament among the Rumble's last three survivors to become its first World Champion, and Cancer beat Jeff “Rocket” Wrobleski to become its first Television Champion.

Its next card, Wednesday Night in the Cage, on 3 April 1996, turned both titles over. John Henry dethroned Suicide for the World title inside a steel cage, and Wrobleski took the Television title back from Cancer in a no-countout, no-disqualification ladder match decided when each man's own strapped-together second interfered against the other. The same broadcast introduced recurring menaces to its record — Raekwon attacking wrestlers without explanation, a stable under Mark R. Rose costing the Spartan a match through outside interference, and Grizzly turning on King Curtis mid-contest with a string of piledrivers the broadcast called the start of a feud.

Its own May Mayhem card, staged 12 May 1996 before a sold-out Kingdome in Seattle, ran a sixteen-man single-elimination tournament for the right to challenge Wrobleski's Television title, run across all five parts of the broadcast to a final won by Christian North over Mister Man.

Four days later, from Madison Square Garden in New York, its Wednesday Night in the Cage returned with two more turns: “The Enforcer” Jerald Pezzino beat “Master of Submission” Alex Vega in an “I Quit” match to take the North American title a second time, teased on-air as a future member of a stable called the New Horsemen, and the card's main event — a Survivor Series elimination match pitting the league's own champions' team of the Hellcats, World Champion John Henry, Television Champion Wrobleski, and Suicide against a rival faction, the Imperial Alliance — ended in the Alliance's favor, RAF Bomber left standing as the lone survivor and Lord Baltimore earning a Television title shot out of it.

That same broadcast previewed its next card, an upcoming Saturday Night Slamfest built around its own Tag Team and World title defenses. No such card, or any later document, survives; the league's own record ends on that preview, and no closure notice was ever posted.
