A federation that ran Saturday nights out of Connecticut in 2003, whose own site survives as a front page of buttons, a dirt-sheet column with no column under it, and a roster page with no names on it — its own record of wrestling comes only from the three men it sent to an alliance supercard. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Twilight Slams called itself TS and put the reason in its own title bar: TS: because you like to wrestle. It ran a website at ts.e-wrestling.org through 2003, kept a second address on free GeoCities space, and ran a weekly it called Saturday Night.

The second Saturday Night went out from the Hartford Civic Center in January 2003; the first had run the week before. That night opened on an interview, put Alison Saint James against the Outsider and Terrance Williams against Marcus Arelius, closed on Crazy Killer and Yeahboy, and stopped mid-card so President Koch could introduce the promotion's brand new Vice President, who promised to debut a new wrestler the following week. The card after it was billed live on public access television from the Mohegan Sun Hotel and Casino in Uncasville, Connecticut, and previewed match by match. Nothing held says how it went, or whether it went at all.

It was a member of Primetime Central, and on 20 April 2003 it sent three wrestlers to the alliance's New Era supercard — “Pride of Japan” Samba Tatenaka into the Global Title match, Laguna and NecroWraith into the Extreme Cluster. That is the one night of its own that anything held can date to a day, and the only night any of its wrestlers are staged in surviving text: Samba Tatenaka cleared eleven men out of the ring in one blow on his own entrance before a top-rope legdrop eliminated him; NecroWraith fought through most of the Extreme Cluster's own field, including a turn against his own delegation-mate Laguna, and reached its final four before being put out with a dragon sleeper; Laguna is last seen recovering a dropped steel slab as three other men crash into him, and the record loses track of him from there.

On 26 July 2003 the alliance's newsdesk reported that Koch had gone. He had stepped down as chairman some time earlier, it said, and had spent the days since disagreeing with the two owners the promotion passed to; the newsdesk printed his leaving in his own words, and they are not fond ones. “I've led it to die now,” he said of the fed he had built.

What the archive kept of the site is three pages, and they are two different sites. The front page of 3 August 2003 is black, set in Trebuchet, and made entirely of buttons: headline news, latest chapters, live events, superstars, development, a latest-event wire, a dirt report. Not one of the seven pages those buttons point at was ever captured. The two pages from 31 October are grey, set in Verdana, and sit at filenames the August navigation never names — an editorials page carrying the Dirt Report's own column head and a byline with no column under it, and a talent page carrying Singles Roster, Tag Roster and Staff with nobody under any of them.

How it ended is not in the surviving record. The last capture of its own site is 31 October 2003, both of its addresses are gone, and the second of them left no page behind it at all.
