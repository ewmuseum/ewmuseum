Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Championship World Wide Wrestling opened on rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy on 6 September 1995 with three singles championships, a tag team title, and a women's division title that never drew a roster. Its own announcement promised weekly matches on “Friday Fire,” a monthly card running up to sixteen fights with every title defended, a pay-per-view every two to three months, and title tournaments once the roster grew large enough to support them.

Its surviving flagship broadcast, “Battle n' Brawl” out of Detroit on 9 November 1995, opened with commentators Ron Connor and Blockbuster scrapping the night's scheduled title matches for an eight-man Battle Royal to crown a new North American Championship contender instead. The build ran through a growing stable around Vivacious Vixen, Jumping Joey Smith, Dead Zone and Decimator declaring war on “the Elite Champs”; a Shadow Master promo accusing a “drugged up official” of costing the Shadow Connection their belts and needling Doug Simpkins, Loki and Sid Starr in the same breath; Mr. Terayaki speaking for the Great Oni and Ninja Blade; and Tully Roebuck, cutting a promo from a Texas ranch, naming the Battle Royal his own road back to a shot at Simpkins.

The Battle Royal itself ran fall by fall to a final three of the Great Oni, Loki and Cryptical, Cryptical clotheslining both of them out in the same sequence to win it and earn the North American Championship shot. The Shadow Master, Fizban and the Shadow Connection stormed the ring the moment the bell rang.

On 1 January 1996 the CWWW's own president announced he had shut the promotion down “because I had too much other stuff going on,” already recruiting for a new, all-fictional fed to replace it. A same-operator post the following August floated a CWWW comeback under an unstated new name; the surviving record never says what became of it.
