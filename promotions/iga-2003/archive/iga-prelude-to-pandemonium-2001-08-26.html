Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
DeLuica: Welcome to Prelude to Pandemonium!! [The camera pans over the open air venue, mats have been placed down over the pavement the ring is set up in the middle of! The grass is filled with seats as stadium lights and the IGA tron have been errected! Tonight the TV title and World title will be decided! DeLuica: And finally Sean Smith and Todd Bishop will meet in the ring! Let's start this show off right and go back to last Tuesday's Combat Zone, this is the footage you missed! [Moments after the opening of the pay-per-view extravaganza, the house lights began to flicker. With the illumination cast over them seeming to be about to give way, the crowd begins to murmur in confusion. The camera pans toward the entry way slowly, pulling in tighter to show the IGATron and the staging area. A sound began to drown out the crowd noise, static crackling over the speakers throughout the arena, accompanied by the popping as heard on old, worn out records. It had a rhythm to it, something the random hissing of static was not normally possessed of. The sound grew steadily louder until it was suddenly replaced. The thundering static suddenly became the thick bassline that was the opening of Tool's song titled "Forty-six & 2." Another strange occurrence happened then, the compact disc the song was being played from began to skip heavily, dashing through the song. That discordant sound only lasted a few seconds though, because Maynard James Keenan's singing voice picked up again.] Over the PA: See my shadow changing, stretching up and over me. Soften this old armor, hoping I can clear the way by stepping through my shadow and coming out the other side. Step into the shadow. Forty-six and two, just ahead of me. [With the sound of a needle being jerked off of a record, the music came to an end. This was not the prelude to the entrance of some mysterious wrestler as the crowd had begun to believe. Instead, the IGATron came to life. A tight shot of the screen showed a white symbol flashing to life. The symbol was that for infinity, a figure eight turned onto it's side. It flashed brightly over the huge screen, as well as every other monitor in the building for a few seconds before it cut away. The lighting in the arena ceased flickering, returning to normal. The entire incident took no more than a minute and a half, but it left hours worth of questions in the minds of all who bore witness.] Streamline: WHAT WAS THAT?!
