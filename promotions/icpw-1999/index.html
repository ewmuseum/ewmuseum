Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

International Championship Professional Wrestling built its own roster in 1999 in part from the wreckage of a defunct rival, North American Championship Wrestling, absorbing several of its wrestlers after that promotion closed that summer.

Its weekly broadcast, Sunday Night Slam (22 October 1999), ran qualifying matches for an upcoming World Title tournament under president Drew Fortesque, with Kenny Dragon advancing past Paranoia. The same broadcast was interrupted mid-taping by a wrestler calling himself Blaze, who crashed an unrelated independent show its own camera crew happened to be filming, grabbed a microphone, and mocked the promotion's own cameramen directly to the crowd before turning to business with his own upcoming title challenger.

Its year-end awards column (20 December 1999) named “The Annihilator” Alan Soriano its dominant figure of the year: World Champion, Most Popular Wrestler, and Wrestler of the Year, with his best-two-out-of-three-falls World Title win over “The Deal” Chris Kitching at Bad and Nationwide II voted Match of the Year. Its own president, Justin Russell, doubled as an in-ring competitor whose finisher, a spinning powerslam he called the Suicide Solution, was voted the year's best move.

The same column reported Kitching, forced into retirement after that loss, resurfacing at Gulf Coast Wrestling Alignment, and previewed a New Year's Eve card, Millennium Massacre, with a newly signed ACW champion, Mike Stryker, set to challenge Soriano.

No report of Millennium Massacre survives; how it ended isn't in the surviving record.
