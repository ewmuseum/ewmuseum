Marcus: WHOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOO!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!! (Marcus leans against the turnbuckle, drapes the belt over his shoulder) Marcus: I guess I should explain. Last week, I was inside of a Thunderdome Cage with a man named Hazard. Through the use of Rubber
Gloves, and boots, we climbed along the top of the cage, trying to make
the other man screw up. A slip of the hand, and I saw sparks, like a muffler dragging on the interstate. A paralysing surge rushed through my
body, and I flew back. As I fell, all I remember is the smell of burnt flesh, and the sight of a great white light. Then, the backwards momentum stopped, and I saw nothing.
(Marcus Breaths) Marcus: Next thing I remembered was waking up in a hospital, being told that I was being treated for electricution, and being told that I was no longer GFWA champion. I did not even know what happened until I watched the tape, and saw Hazard fall atop of me. You know, that night, Hazard had a look in his eyes. He was hungry, he wanted the win. More then I at
any rate. I suppose my paper-thin commitment to the belt was partially the cause, but this is not a new feeling for me.
(Marcus stands back up, and paces towards the center of the ring, dragging the belt) Marcus: Fact is, this loss, combined with the past month in the MLWO has given me a lot of doubt about my own carrer. I came here early to avoid problems of jet lag. When you're in a foreign land, don't know anyone, and the only understanding of the language is a little bit of
Katakana, you feel very isolated. I spent most of my time in the hotel. Working out, watching TV, basically doing a whole lot of nothing. I have tried to come to some conclusion about my carrer, and the only thing that I could come to is that I had no chance in the match, and if I lost, that's the end of my days as a top draw...
(pause) Marcus: (Softer voice) i suppose that, reaching your peak at a young age is a curse disguised as a dream. I'm 22, not mature enough to handle the constant swinging pendulum between fantastic success, and dismal failure that comes with Wrestling. With the way I went about getting this success, the only thing that I could figure is that maybe it was better
for me to give it up while I could still be remembered as one of the great ones. But, I solved my problem from an unlikely source...
Marcus: Marlon and Gwynne joined me in Japan, only to find out that I would be retiring. "OWNS" me. Yet, despite this, he wanted nothing further to do with me, then to say that I am nothing. At that moment, the fire was lit, and I felt a rush that I have not felt since December 31, 1996. All of a sudden, I realised my mistake, I was thinking of my carrer in the past.
I was thinking of the legacy I had already established. This was the problem with Shane Dalton, or Scott Hibbs. Legendary names in the sport.. Couldn't do a GOD DAMN thing here. Suddenly, I thought of my carrer in the sense that I still had something to prove, not that I had

so, we wrecked the controls, and I cut my way into the match. And when the smoke cleared, I became champion.
