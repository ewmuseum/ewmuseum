A May 1997 Chicago-based merger of two predecessor feds, running two weekly cards and a point-ranking system under a two-co-president front office. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Global Action Packed Wrestling launched its own Players Guide V1.0 on 17 May 1997 out of Chicago, Illinois, created by two co-presidents. Its own guide credits one of them with an earlier fed, the World Fantasy Wrestling League, which “folded to lack of help,” and the other with a second folded promotion, ECWA; both are billed as veteran roleplayers across several other feds beforehand.

Its own house rules barred “Out of the World” gimmicks outright — “no bionic plumbers or clowns” — and any wrestler built as a knockoff of a real one, “no heartbreaker, stone colds, or nature boys.” Applicants could enter one wrestler or one tag team apiece, against a roster capped at 48 singles competitors and 12 tag teams, each required to submit two roleplays a week. It sanctioned a World Heavyweight, a Trans-Continental and a Skyweight Championship among singles wrestlers and one World Tag Team Championship, with match outcomes ultimately decided by its co-presidents against a running storyline rather than by roleplay quality alone.

It announced two weekly cards, Sunday Night Fright and Wednesday Night Trashing, and a first special event for 27 June 1997 called Judgment Day.

By 12 June, one of its own co-presidents was pleading publicly for match writers, admitting the promotion had launched with only three: “we should have gotten writers before we even started the freaking fed.” No further document survives, and whether Judgment Day was ever held is not in the surviving record.
