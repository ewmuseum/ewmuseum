Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Proving Grounds was a farm league with a touring schedule. Inside six months of 2005 its weekly show ran out of the Budweiser Events Center in Loveland, the Dunkin Donuts Center, Roger Williams University, U Mass, Buffalo, London, Belize City, Osaka, Milwaukee, Moline, Erie and Cleveland, and finished the year in Madison Square Garden.

It had eight championships and almost no record of who held them. A Title History was posted to its own board on 4 December 2005 and was never captured, which is why most of its champions arrive here undated: Wil the Thrill retaining the International Championship in a spring main event, Chris Watts named a former World Champion in somebody else's roster notes, Lacey introduced as the Women's Champion to a crowd chanting that she sucked, Randy Walker walking out of the company still carrying its Hardcore Title and offering it to anyone with a use for it.

What does survive in full is two nights. On 9 October 2005 it ran the entire first round of an International Championship tournament on one card and wrote four of its eight matches out longhand. On 17 December it staged End of an Era III at Madison Square Garden: So Cal Val took the Diva Championship off Trish Stratus in under four minutes, Michael Duncan won a number one contender's match, and Kris Adams won a World Championship the commentary said had returned to the promotion, finishing with a frog splash off the top rope under the Guerrero banner still hanging over the ramp.

Between those two matches the founder walked back through the curtain. Teddy Graham told the building that the current roster couldn't hold the jockstraps of the men who came before them and asked why an International Championship was being paraded around with the Pride Title sitting on the sidelines. The chairman who had replaced him offered to burn the International belt in front of him, and booked them both into a Hell on Earth match for January.

It outlived the fed it fed. The board it ran on still carried TPG Warzone, TPG Presents and a Farmageddon in May 2007, eighteen months after everything else on that board had stopped. When it started isn't recoverable; its own Vault held 765 archived topics from brands — TPG League, Havoc, Showdown!, Mid-South, Mid-West, Joint Productions — that no document in the surviving record describes.

Storylines

Two of The Proving Grounds' own nights survive in full and one week of its roleplay board survives around them, alongside the booking sheet its roleplay forum and results forum were showing on 15 December 2005. The arcs below are recapped from those, and stop where they stop.

A tournament for a belt the founder wanted burned Written by AI, 2026

TPG Uncensored opened on 9 October 2005 with Scott Steiner telling Loveland he was walking out of Golden Opportunity as International Champion, and then ran the whole first round of the tournament for it on one card: Junior beat E-Rock with a Pedigree and a springboard swanton, the Inquisitor beat Leviathon with a chokeslam and two Hopping Tombstones, and Eternal caught Matt McCoy coming off the top rope. The night closed with Clarke Andrews walking out to announce that the title match at Golden Opportunity would be a ladder match; the second hour of that show is listed on the fed's own results forum as “Gladiator/Eternal, Diva Title, Ladder”, and was never captured. By December the belt was on JC Sinn, and the commentary at Madison Square Garden said the last two International Champions had both been crowned by tournaments and both had gone through David D on the way — which is the sum of what the record knows about how either of them won it.

The founder and the chairman Written by AI, 2026

Teddy Graham founded the promotion and left it. He came back through the curtain at End of an Era III on 17 December 2005 to say that his invitation must have got lost in the mail, that the show's own “reintroduction to old school” billing was a lie, and that the men on it could not hold the jockstrap of the men before them. What he actually wanted was the Pride Title back and the International Championship gone. Mikel Sweeten, the chairman brought in to resurrect the company, came out with the World Championship on his shoulder and told him that he had left when things got rough; Graham told him that this was the PWE and not the UCW, and that the promotion did not belong in the hands of a UCW loser. They made a Hell on Earth match out of it for 21 January 2006 — three of Graham's old timers against three of Sweeten's current roster, the International Championship on the line, the Pride Title to come back if Graham's side won and the International belt to be burned in front of him if they lost. The show was promoted on the page itself as HellBound 2. Nothing in the record says whether it happened. The Pride Title came back anyway: the last line the surviving capture of the board ever writes about the promotion has Montel Vontavious Porter defending it in May 2007.

Two women's champions, and one belt left over Written by AI, 2026

On 9 October 2005 the ring announcer introduced Lacey as “the Women's Champion” and, four paragraphs later, Trish Stratus as “the other Women's Champion”; the commentary called Lacey “one of two women's champions” and the segment after the match called the pair of belts, collectively, both TPG Diva Titles. Trish Stratus and So Cal Val beat Lacey and Eliza Marie, and Stratus took the microphone to challenge Lacey for both titles at once. Lacey accepted and the two of them fought on the floor. Whether that match was ever made is not recoverable — the Women's Championship is never mentioned again in any surviving document, and ten weeks later Stratus lost the Diva Championship to So Cal Val in three minutes and fifty-seven seconds.

If you don't win tonight, you're fired Written by AI, 2026

Backstage on 9 October 2005 Clarke Andrews wished Matt McCoy luck in the main event, pulled him back in by the hand and told him that if he did not win it he was fired. The crowd watching on the monitor booed. McCoy lost: Eternal caught him coming off the top turnbuckle and chokeslammed him. The fed never said whether the ultimatum was carried out, and McCoy is billed on nothing afterwards.

The title that did not exist Written by AI, 2026

David D had impressed the new chairman enough to earn a shot at a United States Championship, and then the Competition Committee decided the promotion did not need one, so he was put in a number one contender's match for the International Championship instead. His manager, Tom Daniels — who speaks every one of David D's four roleplays for him, David D himself never saying a word in any of them — called it the Intercontinental Championship, and kept calling it that. Michael Duncan spent four roleplays of his own correcting him: “One of those titles exists, one doesn't.” Daniels answered that they both meant the same thing and that a champion could call a title whatever he liked. Duncan won the match at Madison Square Garden in nine minutes and fifty-nine seconds, after David D answered a slow count with a low blow.
