Where do I go to help out? 10. Joining - Application 1. What is the purpose of this fed? The ACIW is an 'independent' promotion, although I hope you've gathered that by reading the name of the fed itself. The ACIW is a smaller promotion, unlike the larger promotions out there, like EMWC or UWF. The basic premonition behind the ACIW is to keep it a smaller promotion, somewhere where the handlers don't have to worry about making it into the 'big time', and can concentrate more on having fun.
The ACIW is going to be a normal, run-of-the-mill promotion, with standard rules, unless otherwise specified. Yes, there will be a great deal of matches that will be 'extreme', and most matches will be pushed to the referee's discretion. The standard match will be housed under regular wrestling rules, where wins can come by pinfall, submission, or countout. The referee is also at liberty to stop a match with his decision, which will normally be a disqualification. Of course, the degree of rule breaking that goes on before a DQ is entirely up to the referee, some might be a bit more lenient.
The ACIW will be run out of its headquarters in Chicago, Illinois. The promotion will be run much like the real-life ECW, where many of the broadcasts will be strictly from Chicago, but it will travel all over the USA.
What is our objective here in ACIW? Firstoff, it's for the handlers to have a good time. Interact with each other, come up with angles, you know, things like that. It takes a LOT off our backs if we have everyone coming up with their own feuds, instead of spending our time trying to figure out who would feud well with whom. We'd also like to have everyone come into ACIW with an open mind, and come to the realization that you will NOT win all the time. Please don't expect to. If you handle a guy that was 123-0 in the XCGWKJAF, it doesn't mean he'll go
123-0 in the ACIW. That's how it is. We don't enjoy having someone e-mail us expecting their wrestler to block and counter EVERY MOVE in the book. It's not possible.
Bottom line - If you expect to have an invincible wrestler, don't join the ACIW. 2. Who runs ACIW? (Fantasy Purposes) After CIW fell into near coma, CIW CEO Jack Firestone sought help to breathe life into his floundering organization. He found none other than a former employee of his in MRW CEO Setzer Van Strife to help him out. Actually, they'd be helping each other out. As Firestone had the money and the staff neccessary to build a successful organization, but lacked the eager talent. Van Strife on the other hand had all of the talent in the world, but lacked the cash and staff. So they got together and formed a little organization called ACIW combining CIW and
MRW into one complete organization. Firestone and Van Strife both have financial control over ACIW, whether they will turn over everyday operations to another has yet to be determined.

4. What are the titles in ACIW? 4.1 ACIW National Heavyweight Championship The ACIW National Heavyweight Title is the top prize in the ACIW. The man that holds this title is the leader of the ACIW, showing the skills and the amount of work to truly be called the best in the ACIW. This title can be held by any member of the ACIW, regardless of the weight class. It is normally reserved for those members that are ranked in the top ten contenders, though special arrangements might be made if a champion answers a challenge to defend his title. The ACIW National
Heavyweight Title will be defended every month, as well as at all PPVs or Supercards. 4.2 ACIW Regional Heavyweight Championship The ACIW Regional Heavyweight Title is the secondary prize in the ACIW, and will normally be reserved for those members that aren't yet ready for the 'upper tier'. The title can be held by any member of the ACIW, regardless of the weight class. It is normally reserved for those members that are ranked in the top fifteen contenders, though once again, special arrangements might be made if a champion answers a challenge to defend his title. The ACIW Regional Heavyweight Title will be defended every other card, as well as at all PPVs or Supercards.
4.3 ACIW Junior Heavyweight Championship The ACIW Junior Heavyweight Title is the 'weight limit' title, and is considered the tertiary title in the ACIW, though it could be made a point that the holder of this title is the top champion in their weight class. The title can be held by those members of the ACIW that are 235 pounds and under. It is normally reserved for the top five junior heavyweight contenders, though as stated earlier, special arrangements can be made if a champion answers a challenge to defend his title. The
ACIW Junior Heavyweight Title will be defended at every card, as well as at all PPVs and Supercards.
4.3 ACIW National Tag Team Championships The ACIW National Tag Team Titles will be decided a little more differently than most are used to. The idea behind the Tag Team titles came from the NLWP, who used the idea of having singles wrestlers forming teams, instead of actual tag teams joining. ACIW members are responsible to find themselves a partner, or face the consequence of having a random tag team partner. The titles can be held by any pair of wrestlers, regardless of weight class. The ACIW Tag Team Titles will be defended every month, as well as at all PPVs or Supercards.
5. What are the shows? 5.1 ACIW Thrills, Chills, and Bloodspills This is the ACIW's flagship show, coming to you bi-weekly. TC&B will showcase the wrestlers of the ACIW, and will consist of anywhere from four to six matches, a few in-rings/backstage interviews, and anything else that could be considered, with a title match normally headlining the entire show. In keeping with the 'indy' feel to the ACIW, the show will be broadcast from a smaller venue, hall, or club.
This show will be broadcast on days TBA. 5.2 ACIW Flashwire This will be ACIW's roleplay show. It will be broadcast on the weeks that TC&B is not, and will consist of the roleplays for the week, match previews, and dark matches. This will be a FULLY WRITTEN show, except for the dark matches of course. It is an open forum for the wrestlers of
ACIW to talk about past matches, upcoming bouts, or anything else. This show will be broadcast on days TBA. 5.3 Supercards and Pay Per Views. The ACIW will run PPVs four times a year, with a 'supercard' most likely being run during the months that a PPV is not taking place. The PPVs will be a massive card to end existing feuds, start new ones, and have every champion in the ACIW defend their titles. The names of these PPVs will be determined at a later date, as well as the supercards.
6. Who are the staff members? 6.1 Announcers 6.1.1 Evan Brown Brown is a 30-ish male, who is keen to the sport of wrestling. He knows what he's talking about when he speaks, and will be the one to call it right down the middle, hardly ever showing leaning one way or the other.
You can't get much more professional than Evan Brown. Always quick to give an opinion of a wrestler, either good or bad, Brown studies the background on many wrestlers before even agreeing to calling one of their matches.
6.1.2 Jason Keller Keller is a young, punk color commentator. He's quick with the insults and has an obsession with sex. He is known to poke fun at Mr. Brown as well as scout out the female audience. He has had experience as a color commentator in numerous organizations such as MWF, SOW, ACWA, NJWC, and many others.
6.2 Interviewers These are the people you use when you wish to have someone interview your wrestler in a flash.
6.2.1 Joey Masters Masters lives up to his last name. He is calm, cool, and collected, the perfect person to run an interview. His questions are always straight to the point, he never dances around what he really wants to ask. Most wrestlers enjoy his style, while others feel like choking him. But no matter what, Masters always nails the interview.

7. How do I succeed in ACIW? 7.1 Roleplaying Roleplaying is the backbone of the ACIW. It shows you're actually giving an effort to be active, as well as gaining heat and showing what your character is all about. It is the BIGGEST factor in deciding what your wrestler's push will be like, and who wins or loses their match. Please keep the following in mind as your write your roleplay for ACIW.
1. SWEARING IN ROLEPLAYS. Bottom line here - if you hear it on television, go right ahead and use it... words like 'ass', 'damn', 'bitch', and things like that are absolutely fine. Anything else isn't cool to use, and you know what I mean. If you absolutely MUST use those words in roleplays or in-rings, please use [CENSORED] or [BLEEP]. No, not even doing stuff like f**k or anything like that is cool. We know what you're trying to say.
2. ATTACKS IN ROLEPLAYS. Refrain from attacking/beating/killing/maiming other wrestlers, announcers, commentators, refs, executives, or anything else you don't handle yourself. If you would like to do so, PLEASE contact the handler and get permission. Otherwise, I get bombarded with e-mails asking why I let so-and-so attack them when they didn't know.
3. KAYFABE IN ROLEPLAYS. We are the ACIW, not the WWF, WCW, or ECW. Don't mention those real-life promotions, nor any of their wrestlers. Please refrain from naming other promotions in e-wrestling as well. It's not that I don't like giving them the plug, but the focus on your roleplays should be the ACIW, not any other fed you might be in. Also, for purposes of this hobby, try not to say 'e-wrestling' or
'e-wrestler'. We try to keep this as real as possible in ACIW, so it should actually be considered as a real promotion. Not to sound nerdy, but it looks weird if someone says 'e-wrestling'.

This will count towards your win/loss in your match for the week, or give you an additional edge for another week, if your character isn't involved in a match. You may also send in an in-ring or pre-match interview, but they won't count as much towards a win or loss, just normally used to further or start an angle. Preference is given to joint in-rings.
7.2 Strats A wrestler's strategy, or 'strat' for short, helps the match writer out IMMENSELY. We can see what moves you'd like to use, how you'd like to defend an opponent's offensive attack, and even interfere in other wrestler's matches. They hold some merit on booking, as one of the final deciding factors if its close. They also let you have complete control over your character, instead of letting a match writer run him ragged. A simple strat looks like this:
ENTRANCE: How you want your wrestler to enter. OFFENSE/DEFENSE: Moves your wrestler will use on offense or defense. Also, any bumps or spots you'd like to see. WIN/LOSS: What your wrestler will do if he wins or loses. ANYTHING ELSE: Other things you'd like to include... attacks in other matches and the like.
7.3 Angles Angles are what make the ACIW work, and make it a good time for everyone. They're what make you want to send in roleplays and strats, and it gets people to get to know one another. Basically, we would ask that you make your own angles, because we really don't want to be responsible and/or creative for every single member of ACIW. We'd rather have all of you create your own angles with others. Find out other handlers' IM names, ICQ numbers, or IRC nicks, and ask them if they'd be interested in working something with you. Just make sure you let us know what your plans are beforehand, we don't want anyone killing anyone else. =)
8. Booking Policy The ACIW is going to be a booked fed, with the ACIW Executive Committee being the _final_ judge of who wins and who loses. We will make the decision based on quality of roleplays, the quality of strats, whether or not it would benefit an angle, as well as the amount of heat/push your character has. Also, to a lesser extent, a factor might be your willingness to write a match when we're in a pinch. Someone that shows they're willing to write a match shows that they're also willing to back
ACIW, and would be a better candidate in those close matches. There _will_ be reasons behind any win or loss, and if you're curious as to why you won or lost, please _ask_ us why, and don't _bitch_. We don't have a very high tolerance for someone whining and crying that they lost, so just don't do it, or you might find yourself with a boot in the ass out of ACIW. You'll get reasons if you ask. You'll get told where to go if you decide to bitch about it. And that's just how it is. Deal.

Whether it's with match writing, coming up with angles, or anything like that, it would be appreciated, and make the ACIW run a lot more smoothly. Otherwise, any suggestions, comments, or anything else that you would think would make
ACIW better is always appreciated. Just drop us a line. 10. Joining - Application Alright, if you've sat through this entire thing, and read it all... and you STILL would like to join, please fill out the application below.
