CWO SPECIAL REPORT: The Trial of Bane (fwd)

21 January 1997
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
CWO SPECIAL REPORT 1/21/97 Producer: [name withheld] ( [detail withheld] ) Executive Producer: Brent G. Alles ( [detail withheld] ) (Fade in to Johnny Starr in the CWO Control Center.) JOHNNY STARR: Hello, everybody, I'm Johnny Starr. As we promised you, we now have the resolution of the supposed rampage of Bane. We now present to you a dramatic re-enactment of: the Trial of Bane. (On Court TV, there is a case that a multitude of people are tuning into. The reason this case is popular because it is the trial of an ex-wrestler named Bane. He is charged with counts ranging from attempted murder and assaulting an officer to trespassing on private property. The counts list of crimes is much too long to name, but if the man on trial is found guilty, he could face up to three life sentences. Bane has already pleaded not guilty to every count against him. This is the first day of the trial, and the defendant is being led into the courtroom. He is shackled and there is a multitude of officers around him. This is the first time that the public has seen the face of this man who usually wears a mask. He posses blond hair, and is strikingly handsome despite a couple of scars on his neck and face. He gets to the Defendants table and meets his attorney Travis Darden. The two look at each other, and Travis whispers something into Bane's ear. Bane sits down. The judge comes in and the trial starts. The judge asks Bane a question.) JUDGE: For the record, I want you to tell the court your real name. I will not tolerate calling you "Bane." (Travis whispers something into Bane's ear, and Bane just nods. Darden then stands up and speaks to the judge.) TRAVIS (In a smooth professional voice): Your honor. My client changed his name to Bane sometime ago. It is his legal name, as you will see if you look it up. Therefore, the court must recognize my clients legal right by upholding the legal name that he chose for himself. JUDGE (his voice gruff as if he just smoked a pack of cigarettes): You have to be joking. (sigh) Well, very well, the court will recognize the defendants legal name: Bane. The Prosecution may begin with its opening statement. (With that the prosecution starts its job. It paints the picture of a menacing villain who should not be allowed on the streets. They go over all the horrible crimes, and beg that justice be carried out. Next, the defense team gets up and explains to the jury that their client is innocent and they shall prove that in the up coming trial. A few days go by with the prosecution calling all of its witnesses, and experts. They build their case, and show all the evidence that they possess to the jury. Likewise, the defense counters the prosecution, and tries its best to overcome the overwhelming amount of evidence. They play their court games. Then the prosecution takes out a piece of evidence and explains to the jury what it is. It is the mask that was found at the crime scene where the officer was brutally assaulted. The officer is still in a coma hence he is unable to testify.) PROSECUTION: What I have here is the mask that a brave officer tore from the defendant during a struggle when he pulled him over. I will now ask the defendant to please try the mask on to see if it fits. (The attorney hands the mask to Bane who just stares him down. Travis Darden stands up and speaks.) TRAVIS: This is a crucial piece of evidence. You must understand its meaning. It is to see if it was actually my client, not someone else, dressed as my client, was the one who committed these horrible crimes. Remember, if the mask does not fit, you must acquit. (Bane stands up and tries the mask on. It pulls down easily over his face, and appears to fit loosely. The prosecution tries to explain it by saying it must have been stretched during the fight. The prosecution goes on with its case until it finally rests. It is now time for the defense to do their job.) TRAVIS: Ladies and Gentlemen of the jury. I no that what the prosecution told you sounds pretty convincing, and that my client my look rather callus. But, I am going to now prove to you that he did not commit these crimes. I would like to call my first witness. Her name is Lust. (A gorgeous woman walks into the courtroom, and goes up to the witness box. She swears in and takes a seat. Travis asks her some questions on her history and relationship with Bane. Then he gets to the point.) TRAVIS: Do you think that Mr. Bane is capable of committing all of the crimes that he is charged with? LUST (her voice is rich and sexy): No I do not believe that he is capable of those acts. TRAVIS: Why do you say this? LUST: Because we were at my house all of the time having sex while the crimes were being committed. I know where he was on all of those days, inside me. JUDGE: That will be enough of that young lady. Any more talk like that and I'll charge with contempt of court.. TRAVIS: Is this true Mr. Bane? BANE (he stares right at Lust and then smiles): Yes, I f***ed her. JUDGE: That's it. I find you in contempt of court. Any more comments like that and I'll double the fine. TRAVIS: Thank you, Lust, that is all. (The prosecution goes through its routine trying to ruin the reputation of Lust and her credibility. When they are done the defense calls its next witness.) TRAVIS: I would like to call our next witness: Samuel Goodwin also known as Scourge. (Scourge is lead into the courtroom. He takes the stand, and Travis starts to question him. He questions his history, psychological problems, and alibis for the time of the crimes. He pounds Scourge orally with attacks on his character, and where he was on the nights of the crimes. Then he asks the final question.) TRAVIS: Mr. Goodwin. Is it not true that in your jealousy of my client and of your violent nature that you are the one who committed all of these crimes? (Scourge is sweating and looks as if he is going to cry. He looks around for help, but there is no one there to give him any. Instead his eyes meet with the imposing Bane's eyes. Bane stares right through him with a look that would send shivers down a dead man. Scourge drops his head and meekly replies:) SCOURGE (in a whisper): Yes, I did all of the crimes. I admit to them. (He then breaks down and cries. Everyone in the courtroom is stunned. They cannot believe what just transpired.) TRAVIS: Your honor, in light of this recent testimony and evidence, I would like for this case to be thrown out. JUDGE: Due to this turn of events, I cannot let this trial proceed. Mr. Bane is free to go, the only charge being a one hundred and fifty dollar court fine. Guards, take Mr. Goodwin away. (The case is over and Bane sits at the table with a calm look on his face. No emotion can be seen in him. Scourge is lead away crying with a police escort. Bane gets up and leaves the building. Reporters rush and ask him a million questions. Bane only has one comment.)
