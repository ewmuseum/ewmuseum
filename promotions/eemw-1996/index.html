A small e-mail wrestling league run out of Georgia State University in the spring of 1996, opening with a four-man roster and a single recorded match before spending its remaining months hunting for writers and wrestlers under an operator known online as BLAZE. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Extreme E-Mail Wrestling introduced itself on 29 April 1996 with two posts on the same day: a founding announcement promising two weekly shows, Monday Night Wars and Saturday Night Power Hour, plus a full year of named pay-per-views running from “Cruisen for a bruisen” that May through “Hell fest” the following April, and a first show carrying its actual roster.

That roster was the whole promotion. BLAZE, its self-appointed World Champion, wrote plainly that he held the belt “because it is my e-mail league” and had only four people signed, promising to drop the title once more joined. The other three were “contender” Doom Boy, Mad Dog Max Smith, and Gary Quick.

Its only recorded match, Doom Boy against Gary Quick, ended with Doom Boy piling two tables on his opponent for the pin — and introduced, mid-broadcast, the league's own house rule against disqualifications, coined on the spot for the stipulation it had just decided not to enforce.

No further show survives past that single match. The last dated document held is a 30 June 1996 post still asking for writers, wrestlers, and tag teams.
