Tsunami, Part Two

29 November 1998
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
[Tsunami continues...] TK: Welcome back everyone. Many SJPW fans have wondered why we have not seen Shinji Hamada much of late. He was in the six man match at the inagural Tsunami, and last week defeated Masashi "Lightning" Lanther in the Juuryou tournament. AO: Other than that, he's been absent from SJPW arenas. PN Magazine reporter Joutei Boshoku recently conducted an interview with Hamada, and with their kind permission, we have a videotape of that interview. Hamada-san asked us to show this, so he could explain his absence in his own words... [CAMERA OPENS on a living room, furnished with only a table and a few mats on the floor surrounding it. A traditional style Japanese home. Sitting on the mats are SJPW Superstar Shinji Hamada, and PN Magazing reporter Joutei Boshoku. Hamada is dressed in a traditional Japanese robe, while Boshoku is wearing a stylish suit. The two men are talking, but the first thing we hear is a VOICE OVER: "It used to be that Shinji Hamada appeared on just about every card that Super Japan Pro Wrestling held. While Super Tengu could claim to be the SJPW "Franchise", everyone realized that Tengu would never have achieved the level of fan interest without a proper feuding partner, and that was Hamada. So while Hamada may not have been the franchise of SJPW, there was no question that he was one of SJPW's top draws. His personality may have changed at the drop of a hat, but he was always a top contender for any title, and indeed he held the SJPW All-Asian Title and is the last person to hold the SJPW Cruiserweight title. Since the reformation of SJPW, Hamada has wrestled twice. Once as a tag team with Mitsunori Takizawa, and once as a member of a 6-man team, again with Takizawa and with Akira Hashimoto. Both times he was on the losing team, His next match, in fact, was his first victory, when he faced arch-nemesis Mashashi "Lightning" Lanther. I sat down with Shinji Hamada at his Osaka, Japan home to talk to him about his role in SJPW and the rumors about internal divisions in Chiimu Tora".] Joutei Boshoku: Hamada-san, first of all, thank you for taking the time to do this interview. You've been known to value your privacy very much. Shinji Hamada: Hai, I do value my privacy. When I was growing up, my father did the same thing. It's not always a good thing to make yourself too much a public figure. Wrestlers often have small cult followings that can invade into private lives, and my father always told me to keep my private life just that... private. It's good to have a personal life, away from spotlights and autograph hounds. JB: You sound almost resentful of the fans. SH: No, not at all. I just like to keep my personal life as someplace where I can get away from the spotlight. I love the fans, and I appreciate all the support they've given me, so I do try and get out and meet the fans as much as possible. When I'm not doing that, though, I like to have my free time be my own. JB: Fair enough, and it seems like you have more free time lately than ever before. You've had plenty of time off from competing in SJPW since your last tag match against Black Sun Rising. That, coupled with rumours about you and Takizawa breaking up your tag team, have led to a lot of speculation about your position in the promotion. SH: I've been given a light schedule in SJPW at my own request. I'm going to be competing in the Twin Cities Invitational Cruiserweight tournament, and so I'll have to be spending time flying from Japan to the United States. I want to give the tournament as much attention as I can, and I'm training as hard I ever have so I can represent SJPW to the best of my ability. I'm training as hard as I did when I was preparing for my cage match against Tengu... maybe harder. JB: Well, that explains your light SJPW schedule... but what about the rumours? SH: Persistent, aren't you? It's true, yes. Takizawa and I have decided that our styles just don't gel as well as they should, and more importantly, our goals are different. I've been SJPW All-Asian champion, I've been SJPW Cruiserweight champion... now I want to be SJPW Heavyweight champion. That's my goal, but unlike my first time around, I'm not in a hurry to win it. I believe that was my main mistake in my rookie year. I wanted the Cruiserweight title so much, and was so focussed on my feud with Tengu, that I missed several opportunities. I don't want that to happen this time. As for Takizawa, well... he wants to just beat people up (laughs). I think he's interested in doing as much possible in as short a time as he can, since ... well... he's not getting any younger. He hates when I say that, but it's the truth. He's probably the oldest wrestler in SJPW right now, and when his retirement finally comes around, he wants to be able to have some trophies to show for it. He definitely wants the tag team titles, and he has found a partner with goals similar to his. JB: Has he? Who? SH: I can't tell you. Everyone will find out eventually. Suffice to say, the man he's found will be a great assett to Chiimu Tora, and I think he's the perfect partner for Takizawa. JB: Since you're mentioning Chiimu Tora, now seems like a good time to ask about it. Some people are saying you're too young to be the head of Team Tiger. Others say the pressures of being the "leader" of the stable are getting to you. Is there any truth to any of it? SH: I have a lot to prove, no doubt about it. I don't know if I'm too young to be leading Chiimu Tora, and in truth I don't feel like I *have* been leading the team. Right now we've all been doing our own things, as each of us seems to have our own feuds to settle. Our joint foe, at least for now, seems to be the Daiymo, and if they want a war, believe me... they'll get one. I do intend to become more personally involved in that battle, and even though you don't see me on TV getting in the middle of things, all the members of Chiimu Tora know that I'll be there if they need me. I think that all members of Chiimu Tora are very confident in their own abilities to handle these kind of problems, however, so don't expect a lot of additional support at ringside on our side. JB: You mentioned earlier that you wanted to hold the SJPW Heavyweight title. Right now you're still listed as being around the 235 mark. Isn't that going to be a problem against bigger men? SH: I plan on adding a few pounds before I seriously begin pursuing that goal. I had wanted to start training and adding weight when SJPW reopened it's doors, but with the TCI tournament that isn't possible. Once the tournament is done, you'll see a heavier Shinji Hamada. I'm trying to add about 15 or 20 pounds total, and with Takizawa as a training partner it won't have a chance to become fat.. it will be all muscle. JB: That additional weight should be an assett to you, I'm sure. You also seem to have really started relying on submission holds instead of just trying to overpower your opponents. SH: My father was a shoot-fighter when it boils down to it, and when he helped train me, he told me that power is a good thing, but to take away your opponents power is a better thing. I lost track of that when I started out, mostly because I was trying to seperate Shinji Hamada from the "legend" of Gori Hamada. Also, it was a bit of ego. I knew I was the strongest cruiserweight, and I knew I could match power with even the strongest of heavyweights.... so I did that. Towards the end of SJPW's first run, though, I realized I was wrestling a very one-dimensional style and that I had neglected my father's teachings. So.... I started working on my submission holds again and my "strong style" as it's called. I think it's let me become a much more well-rounded... and dangerous... opponent. JB: Speaking of opponents... I'd like to toss out a few names, and get your first impressions of each. Let's start with the man whom you're often associated with... Super Tengu. SH: Great athlete, but his ego gets the better of him. The toughest opponent I've ever had. JB: Ok... what about Jensei Tenru? SH: Hmmm.. well, since I'm sure you'll end up asking about Jinsei Tenru too, I'll deal with both them at once. Both have a lot of talent, but like Tengu they let ego get in their way. In fact, it's getting harder and harder to tell the difference between the three of them. Jensei.. or maybe it's Jinsei... either way... one of them goes around showing off his toys and other endorsements, and Tengu did all that over a year ago. The other Tenru is making movies or somesuch nonsense and again, Tengu did that about a year ago. At least Tengu seems to have settled down a bit and is concentrating more on actually wrestling. JB: Ace Kilcannon? SH: Ace and I make a good tag team, but we never seem to last very long. He's got talent, but he's too intent on proving how dangerous he is. He's good, but I'm better. JB: One last question... What can we expect from Shinji Hamada in the near future? SH: You can expect Shinji Hamada to represent SJPW to the best of his ability in the TCI tournament. Mostly you can expect that I'll be doing everything I can to ensure a prosperous future for not just SJPW, but for the sport of puroresu as a whole. You can expect me to live up the standards of my mentor, Tiger Kikuchi, and to the standards of my father. That's about all anyone should need me to do. [camera fades for a moment, then reopens on the broadcast position] AO: Hamada-sama was very stragithforward with his opinions. TK: Is that a band thing? AO: Not at all. He gets that from me you know. TK: Oh? I remember when your voice was loud in calling him Shinji "Dive" Hamada... AO: No more so than Takao Nakami, a man you embraced into Chiimu Tora after his near death from drugs and alcohol. If you could forgive him, why persist in tormenting me? TK: Because I repay you for chiding me concerning my association with Chiimu Tora. As the gaijin say, "You can dish it out, but you can't take it" [Kikuchi smiles at his partner] AO: Your an evil man Kikuchi-sama! TK: And you are an angel? AO: Never an angel. Just pragmatic. I guess now we get to see "Jigoku" Tiger Extreme face El Gato Diablo TK: That is correct. AO: Althought Hakiro Matsuoko guides "Jigoku", my yen is on Diablo. TK: Why is that? AO: Because he is a wrestler! Why else? TK: Doug Thomas is a wrestler too. AO: No, he is an SHVF stooge! TK: If you say so.
