Simmed vs. Booked

1 December 1997
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
ERoy486 wrote: > Howdy [name withheld]. Long time not talk. Not my fault; I saw you on irc, and you didn't even say hello before you left :) > I think my point was not that good gm's do it on purpose, it just that it would > be EXTREMELY difficult to pull it off with out someone screaming "NO FAIR! HE > ALWAYS WINS!" and forcing the gm to have to justify why someone won a > particular match. I'm not saying that happens in the SJPW, because I don't > know, I just think it would be tough to at least to maintain the outward > appearance of objectiveness without a lot of complaints. Same goes for a booking committe. Someone will always complain about something. However, I can assure you that no one has ever screamed "He Always Wins!" in SJPW. All of the top handlers, with the exception of Dan Kauffman [who has had only two matches so far, 1 win, 1 draw], lose matches. Jet Jaguar, the rookie who draws almost as much heat as Super Tengu, LOST the Middleweight title finals against Ishikawa. Everyone had him pegged to win. Even his opponent started to doubt he could beat him. However, in the end, Ishikawa defeated him. Not because he was the better roleplayer either. Ishikawa worked hard to get to the finals, and being the underdog, he won by having a better strat [ie detailed, and very realistic]. Even Jet Jaguar's handler thought it was a smart move [they all thought I had Jaguar picked to win by the semi-finals. They were wrong]. While there have been complaints by a handler or two that they should have won a match over someone else, like I said, it happens in every fed I've been in, booked or simmed. > To me, at least, the whole problem who *I* don't like booking is that it put > too much power in the hands of the fedhead. I know it's his league, but to me > what simming does is take some of that power out of the league's hands and puts > it in somewhere else. You can encourage and reward roleplaying in other ways, > like Blitzmatch's Push Levels or Bodyslam/Ringmaster's Fan Support. Both > encourage, promote and reward roleplay without making that one of the main > determinations on match results. And we went over this before too :) I'll give you an example of how I book things. One of the handlers has a guy named Mr. Big. Kind of like the Giant. He did well at first, but was not a major player. However, he never complained, and when I sent out info on doing strats, he started to work on making strats for each match. After seeing that his flashes drew no heat [he continually demanded title shots, when SJPW does a challenge system; ie challenger challenges from top 10, and champ can pick one to defend against, or he can choose a non-top 10 opponent], he started to work on developing the character more. His last flash impressed other top 10 contendors, who told me that he deserved the next title shot. Even the champion felt he deserved the title shot. I had planned on it anyway after seeing how he had improved. He started as a mediocre player, but he was consistent. I had kept him in feuds mostly with people on par with him, and slowly but surely, he got better. He's earned everything he has no [ie #2 ranking, and a title shot at the next ppv]. They way he's going, he'll be the next world champion. He's been in the fed since april, and the closest he came to a title was his tag team made the finals of the tag tournament by pure luck [a few draws turned his semi-final match into the finals] Another example is Super Tengu, the franchise of SJPW. He's the man, and everyone knows it. He lost the cruiserweight title awhile back, and was nursing an injury. Part of his background is that he is a second generation wrestler. He finally made a comeback match, and faced a wrestler I use for angles [a Liger clone]. Tengu lost, not becuase he was my guy, but because Tengu, who is a heel, had become too much of a face, and he needed a rude awakening to get the fans against him [and to start a face turn by his arch enemy, who went heel as Tengu went face] This is an angle that started back in May, and will end next year. An Epic storyline, so to speak. Tengu's heel actions later in the card [getting the champ who put him out dq'd, cause he wants to beat him for the belt] got me quite a few emails applauding me for finding the perfect way to turn Tengu back heel. His handler was ecstatic, because we had been unable to turn him heel properly for awhile now. That is the strength of booking: improvisation. It's not perfect, but if you use enough twists, people respond. If a guy everyone expects to win loses to someone who has been consistent, but not one of the top handlers, it's not punishing the top guy, it's rewarding the lesser guy for trying! That is the way I book things. Not everyone likes it, and not everyone will. It's not perfect. Neither is a sim, or a dice system. They all have flaws to different people. All I'm saying is that my one-man booking system works well enough for my fed that I get bombarded by applications every month. Obviously I'm doing something right to some people :) > Now, before anyone jumps down my throat again, I don't want to argue with > people about booking and sims, we're discussing the differences. If a league > wants to run a booked league, great. I'm sure that the good GM's can pull it > off and make it work fine, just like the sim GM's can do it, so y'all don't go > off on me this time, I'm being nice. :-) I'm not gonna jump down your throat [name withheld]. We all ready agreed to disagree on that subject, so I Have no intention of starting that up again :) > With those players that complained, how do you handle it? When someone > complains about a match result in the AWI, the handler knows that it's run by > Bodyslam, and sometimes the dice don't always roll the way people want them to. Well, it depends on the complaint. If it is becuase they lost to a certain opponent when they thought they should have won, I simply explain to them why they lost. Sometimes it's because of outside interference, sometimes the other person simply did a better job. Most complaints I've seen in booked feds [mind you, a few people did leave SJPW, but didn't complain, though I know they left because of certain lossess they sustained] is that a handler thinks they were a better roleplayer than the other person. That's all good and well, but my wrestler form does ask handlers if they are willing to lose matches for the sake of an angle. That doesn't mean their angles. It means ANY angle. Everyone agrees to it, or they don't get into the fed. No one doesn't agree to it, so they have to accept that it will happen. EVERYONE loses. It happens in real pro-wrestling, and it happens in e-wrestling, whatever form you use. When someone joins a booked fed, they agree [whether openly or not] that the booking the fedhead does for that league determines the winner. To disagree with that isn't wrong, but it shows the handler is forgetting that fact. Some feds use angles [IIWF] for booking, others [MLWO] is totally based on roleplay/strats to win. I do it a mixture of both, as not all the handlers have angles going. I made strats optional now, but I do use them when I see how they can make a match better. Since most of the original roster is still here [and only 2 left for reasons pertaining to their losses], I think I've done a fair job with my booking style. Not everyone agrees with it, but they don't make the decisions. Sure, I listen to opionions. Sometimes I ask neutral people for advice if I'm stuck [which has only happened twice since I took over SJPW], but other than those 2 exceptions, I did it my way :) [name withheld] SJPW
