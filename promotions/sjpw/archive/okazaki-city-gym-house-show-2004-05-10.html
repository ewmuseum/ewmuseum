SJPW House Show — Okazaki City Gym (5/10/04)

May 25, 2004, 12:00:00 AM
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Monday, May 10th, Okazaki City Gym (Cap. 700), Aichi, Japan: "GAIJIN Extreme" Jun Suzuki vs Kenichi "Kurasshaa" Kubota: The opening bout saw two of the contenders from the Gunryo title free for all square off. Word had come down that Suzuki wanted revenge on Kubota for causing him to be eliminated from the match, and the hatred was definitely there. Suzuki started off by nailing Kubota with a series of running lariats that kept him off balance, before catching him with a Rolling Elbow. Kubota was dazed in the corner, and Suzuki went for another lariat, only to catch a boot to the face. As he staggered backwards, Kubota kicked him in the gut, executed a Manhattan Drop, then took Suzuki down with a lariat of his own. Kubota went for an Exploder, but Suzuki countered with some elbows to the side of Kubota's head. Kubota lost his grip, and Suzuki grabbed him, and hit a belly to belly suplex, followed by a corkscrew legdrop. Kubota tried to get to his feet, only to be planted with a Rocker Dropper. Suzuki then signaled it was over, and tried to go for the Aloha Extreme, but Kubota dropped to his knees, and low blowed Suzuki. With Suzuki on his knees in pain, Kubota raked his eyes, then nailed him with a knee to the head. Kubota went for a cover, but only got a two count. Kubota dragged Suzuki to his feet, and whipped him to the corner. We charged in for a lariat, but Suzuki caught him with a boot coming in, then planted him with a spinebuster. He then hit a split legged moonsault out of the corner, went to the ropes, and hit a senton. Instead of covering, he dragged Kubota up, hit the Aloha Extreme, and pinned him easily for the three count. Winner: "GAIJIN Extreme" Jun Suzuki via pinfall Otaku says: Suzuki got a measure of revenge tonight! Ono says: That he did. This young lion is someone to watch out for! Masashi "Lightning" Lanther & Jiraiya vs The Crush Crew: The Crush Crew, ever enemies of Chiimu Tora, took on their most experienced unit in Team Raiden. Kobayashi started off with Lanther, and used his size and strength advantage to batter Lanther down with a series of elbows and forearms. Kobayashi then hit a big kneelift off the ropes, followed by a scoop slam to get a one count on Lanther. Kobayashi then started to land a series of stiff chops on Lanther's chest, forcing him back to the corner. Kobayashi started to taunt Lanther then, before Lanther caught him with a rolling Koppo kick that staggered the bigger Kobayashi. Kobayashi staggered backwards, and Lanther caught him with a dropkick to the face, landed in a crouched postion, and nailed him with a second Koppo kick. Kobayashi was stumbling around in the ring, and Lanther went to the top rope, and hit a missile dropkick that sent Kobayashi to the floor. Lanther went to the top again, and as Kobayashi got to his feet, nailed him with a flying bodypress. Lanther then went for a whip into the railing, but Kobayashi reveresed it, sending Lanther into the steel. Kobayashi then lifted Lanther up, and dropped him across the railing. He dragged Lanther into the ring before the ten count, and tagged in Nakatoni. Nakatoni hit a swinging neckbreaker, then went for a sleeperhold. Lanther almost succumbed to it, but with the fans chanting his name, was able to back to the ropes. As Nakatoni broke the hold, Lanther caught him with stiff back elbow, then a back spin kick. Lanther rolled to his corner, and tagged in Jiraiya, who springoarded to the top rope, and came off with a high spinning wheel kick that almost decapitated Nakatoni. He went for the cover, but Kobayashi broke it up. Jiraiya went after Kobayashi, landing some stiff elbows to the side of his head, then ramming him head first into his corner turnbuckle, causing him to fall to the floor. Nakatoni use this time to attempt a roll up from behind, but Jiraiya kicked out at one. Jiraiya got to his feet first, and as Nakatoni got up, Jiraiya sent him back down with a hiptoss. He dragged Nakatoni to his feet, and hit a bulldog, then a standing moonsault. He covered, but only got a one count. Jiraiya tagged in Lanther, and they hammered Nakatoni, with a double suplex followed by a double elbowdrop, then hitting double legdrops off the ropes. Lanther covered, but only got a two count. Lanther then whipped Nakatoni to the corner, following in with a nasty running dropkick that staggered him. Lanther sat Nakatoni on the top rope, and hit a superplex, rolling over into a pin. Kobayashi tried to make the save, but Jiraiya caught him coming in the ring with a springboard dropkick, allowing Lanther to get the three count. Winners: Masashi "Lightning" Lanther & Jiraiya Otaku says: Team Raiden strikes again! Ono says: Indeed. The Crush Crew get better each week, but Lanther & Jiraiya have been a team too long for them to easily defeat. Gregor Davinsky & Wolf Jager vs Se-hong Kwan & Pramoj Ananda: After injuring Kwan a over a month ago, the Korean fan fav made his return, teaming with his protege Ananda against Jager and his ally Davinsky. Jager refused to start off with Kwan, forcing him to square off with the larger Davinsky. Kwan caught Davinsky with a series of kicks to this thighs and legs, keeping him off balance. Kwan then went for a thrust kick, but Davinsky caught the leg, and took Kwan down with a stiff lariat. Davinsky then started to stomp on Kwan's arm. Kwan rolled around in pain, and Davinsky dragged him up, before hitting an arm-DDT. He then whipped Kwan into the corner, and went for a lariat, but Kwan caught him with a Shotay palm strike, which he followed with a jumping front kick. Kwan tagged in Ananda, and the crowd went wild as Ananda punished Davinsky with a series of Thai elbows, kicks and knees, which sent Davinsky to the mat. Ananda went for a cover, but Jager broke it up. Ananda then switched tactics, and hit a stiff double underhook into a backcreaker over the knee. He slapped on a single leg crab, but again Jager broke up the move. Ananda then allowed Davinsky to tag in Jager, and motioned for him to enter the ring. Jager did so without hesitation, and Ananda pounced on him, pummeling him with a series of elbows to the head, followed by a stiff knee to the gut. Ananda went to whip Jager to the ropes, but Jager reversed it, and caught Ananda with a beautiful armdrag, which he held onto, turning it into an armbar. He dragged Ananda to his feet, moved his body, and brought the arm down hard over his shoulder, before going back into a standing armbar. Ananda tried to power out of it, but was unable to. Wincing in pain, he stomped on Jager's bare foot with his boot three times, causing Jager to break the hold, and back off, wincing when his foot touched the mat. Ananda tagged in Kwan while Jager was checking his foot, and the crowd went wild when Jager realized who was in the ring with him. Kwan started to hammer Jager with various chops and punches, trying to get revenge for Jager almost breaking his arm. Jager tried to tag in Davinsky, but Kwan did a legsweep that only made Jager hit his head on the corner turnbuckle. Kwan then hit a german suplex, bridging for the pin, but Jager was able to kick out at two. Kwan dragged Jager to his feet, and was going to suplex him again, but Jager blocked it, then thumbed Kwan in the eye. With Kwan blinded, Jager scooped him up, then hit a shoulderbreaker. He dragged Kwan to his feet, hammerlocked his arm, and slammed him on the arm. He went for a Jujigatame, but Ananda broke it up before Kwan could submit. Jager hit a second shoulderbreaker, then went for the Fujiwara armbar, only to have Ananda again break up the hold. Jager then went after Ananda, slamming into him, and causing him to fall off the apron, and into the railing. He went back to Kwan, and slapped on the Crossface Chicken Wing. Kwan tried to fight his way out of it, but couldn't, and was forced to submit, as Ananda wasn't able to recover in time to break up the hold again. Winners: Gregor Davinsky & Wolf Jager by submission. Otaku says: That Jager is a master at taking someone's arm apart. Ono: He's definitely one of the more gifted submission specialists in the promotion. Someone to watch out for in the future! Main Event Non-Title Bout: El Santa Azteca vs Super Bakemono: The current NCWF cruiserweight champion had the ring announcer tell the crowd that he would not defend the title tonight, as he considered Azteca to be unworthy of a title shot. Azteca came out to loud applause from the crowd, and before anyone knew it, threw his cape over Bakemono, before tackling him, and pummeling him with his fists. The referee tried to break it up, but Azteca simply took his cape, and started to strangle Bakemono with it. The ref threatened to DQ Azteca, who pointed out the match hadn't started yet. He then threw Bakemono over the top rope, hanging him by his cape. The ref then called for the bell, and Azteca let Bakemono go, and he slumped to the floor, clutching his throat. Azteca went to the top rope, and As Bakemono got to his feet, caught him with a spectacular elbowsmash off the top. Bakemono went down hard, while Azteca rolled to his feet, and started slapping hands with the crowdn. He then went back to Bakemono, dragged him to his feet, and whipped him hard into the railing. He rammed Bakemono's head on the apron, and rolled him into the ring. Azteca rolled in after him, and went for the cover, but only got a two count. Azteca then tried to whip Bakemono to the ropes, but Bakemono reversed it, catching Azteca with a dropkick as he came off the ropes. Azteca staggered back, and Bakemono hit a rolling koppo kick, then whipped Azteca across the ring, charging after him. As Azteca came off the ropes, Bakemono caught him with a lariat, sending him hard to the mat. Bakemono then ran to the ropes, springboarded off, and landed a high corkscrew legdrop across Azteca's chest. He went for a cover, but only scored a one count. Bakemono then dragged Azteca to his feet, and hit a series of 3 snap suplexes, followed by an Impact DDT. Bakemono again went for the cover, but only scored a two and a half count. Enraged, Bakemono went to the top rope, and drew this thumb across his throat. He went for the Bakemono Bomb, but Azteca rolled out of the way, and Bakemono hit nothing by mat! Azteca got to his feet, and as Bakemono got up, Azteca went to the ropes, and came off with a flying forearm smash that staggered the champion. Azteca then hit an Aztec Suplex, rolled Bakemono over, and slapped on the Azteca Speciale. Bakemono was caught in the middle of the ring, and tried to fight it, but was unable to make it to the ropes. Without being able to get to the ropes, Bakemono submitted, giving Azteca his first big win in SJPW! Winner: El Santa Azteca vis submission Otaku says: This is an incredible upset! Ono: That it is. Azteca has been winless to date, but now he defeats one of the top men in the promotion. Yamashita has got to grant him a shot at the NCWF title now!
