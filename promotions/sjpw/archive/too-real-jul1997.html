Too Real? (booking-ethics disclosure)

25-29 July 1997

----- Too Real? (Jul 25, 1997, 12:00:00 AM) ----- [name withheld] wrote: > > Very interesting, my compadre. I agree. Too many handlers have > Hoganitis, that is, refusal to job. EVER! The refusal to lose > premeates e-wrestling, and the result is constant desire to make a GIANT > wrestler. We had an applicant who was 8'8"!!! C'mon. Just to add my .02: A few weeks ago, I updated the SJPW wrestler form. Two things I did to screen for the kind of people I want in the fed were for a sample roleplay, and a few questions. One question asked if a handler was willing to have his wrestler job for the sake of an angle etc. If they won't, they don't get in. If any of you read Battlecade '97, the first SJPW ppv, the match between Super Tengu & Shinji "Live" Hamada for the Cruiserweight title was a job for Shinji. The two handlers worked this out weeks before, and it is handlers like this [thank god for James and Scott, and all my other handlers!] that make SJPW as good as some people here on the ng claim. Angles exist in real wrestling, and jobbing for the angle is a necessity. I don't have problems jobbing for an angle in feds I'm in, and I don't think others should either. Of course people will, but they're the ones who need to learn a little what e-wrestling, and real wrestling, is really all about ----- Too Real? (Jul 29, 1997, 12:00:00 AM) ----- [name withheld] wrote: > Here's my take on this. I Help run SWF, with Shawn (quick plug - > http://www.geocities.com/TimesSquare/Alley/7415/index.html ) > We both pretty much agree, if someone's got a good angle going, and a > certain person needs to lost to help further that angle, they'll lose. > The wrestler that I have in my league is almost there just to help > with angles. I do feel blessed that we have many good roleplayers in > the SWF that are willing to volunatrily sacrifice themselves for > someone else's angle (I've noticed that many of the handlers talk to > other handlers about their angle to see if they are willing to help. > most say yes) We even had one guy voluntarily get himself eliminated > from title contention to help further 2 other angles. I think that is > a true rarity. It happens in a few feds. IIWF for example. As Matt Findlay pointed out, Casey James was set to lose the title, so someone else could win it. In SJPW, handlers are constantly doing this' I have 4 title changes planned in just the Cruiserweight division alone over a 4-6 month period, and all 4 handlers know who they will beat, and who they will lose the belt to. They planned it that way. Too bad more efeds don't follow this example, but then again, not everyone likes to lose, even for the sake of an angle.
