[name withheld] wrote: > > Thanks for the offer, [name withheld], but in looking around here, I see that I > wouldn't make it past the first match in e-wrestling. Besides, Sayo is > a new wrestler I'm trying to taylor to a hardcore style. (I don't like > hardcore or extreme wrestling, but the head of Hardcore World asked me > to help him start a ladies division, so I'm trying to accomodate him. > Besides, he loves my writing.) Anyway, I'll just remain a casual > observer and fan for now. You never know until you try. I'm new to e-wrestling myself [I started in Feb of this year] and by April, I took over SJPW. It's alot of fun, and the majority of the handlers seem to like it. SJPW does have a small amount of hardcore matches, mostly in the Men's division [with the Battlecade title], but the women of SJPW have thus far proven to me more violent in their quest for the gold. Our ppv saw the two semi-final matches end up with quit a bit of brutal acts happening before and during the matches. Not how I want things to go, but the whole ppv was no dq [except fot the NACW tag team finals], so it was allowed. Chris Devlin is a great guy, and Hardcore Japan looks highly competitive. I still think you should give e-wrestling a shot. You may find out that you're better at it than you think. > I like the way this conversation is going, so let me throw out another > question; with all of these feds floating around here, I'm wondering, > are they your vision of what you think pro wrestling should be, or are > you just creating something you don't see enough of on TV? The > popularity of hardcore/extreme wrestling is evident these days, but > there also seems to be back-to-basics movement of sorts; a remembering > of the best parts about pro wrestling. Even the territorial stuff is > making a comeback. It must be fun for all you guys to play the booker; > "if I were in charge, here's what I would do..." > Anyone care to comment here? I'd say it's a bit of both. I'm a major fan of mexican/japanese wrestling; I get mexican wrestling every week, and I have some tapes of japanese wrestling. When I had the chance to take over SJPW, I took it, and never looked back. It's corporate [which is a part of japan's modern ways], yet still regional [it is in Japan, not worldwide], so it has a regional feel, but with the high-tech glitz of a major fed. I am trying to push wrestling over the extreme stuff, but to some extent I allow it. I have nothing against extreme wrestling; I'm an ECW fan [and FMW/W*ing!] However, I don't want to run an extreme fed. I want to see matches like Dean Malenko vs Ultimo Dragon, Eddie Guererro, or Rey Mysterio Jr. The Michinoku Pro 6 man match at ECW's Barely Legal PPV is the kind of style I am shooting for. Fast paced action, with little rulebreaking; just two sides trying to beat the other by their skill. Sure, SJPW has faces and heels, but the japanese fans love wrestlers, good or bad. WHen a wrestler executes a move they like, they applaud the wrestler, even if he is a heel. The Japan AC, which is a group considered pretty major heels in other efeds, are some of the most populare wrestlers in SJPW. In fact, they've decided to embrace this support of the fans, yet keep their heel edge, which is fine with the fans. They were heels to start, and the fans cheered them, so why would they stop now? So in the case of SJPW, I'm definitely booking it the way I'd like to see wrestling in the states do more of, both live and on tv. The handlers have a major say in their angles [like any other roleplaying game, the players should have some say in things, regardless of the plans the storyteller has], and most of them follow along the vision I started. It might not work for everyone [I've had people leave becasue they wanted to do more rulebreaking stuff, and I wouldn't allow it], and that's fine with me. I have plenty of people who can work with the restrictions I made, and SJPW is only stronger for it. I think I made my point, so I'll get off my soapbox now [name withheld] SJPW  > > [name withheld], handler of PBM wrestlers Jennifer Stargayzer (RWI), Bessie > Pythian (RWI), and Sayo Arakawa (Hardcore Japan) >
