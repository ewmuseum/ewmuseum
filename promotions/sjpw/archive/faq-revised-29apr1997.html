SJPW FAQ, revised (29 April 1997 edition)

29 April 1997

Sorry to post wrestler requests once again. I'm the owner of Super Japan Pro Wrestling. It is an efed based in Japan, and is run like a real japanese fed. I am still looking for Cruiserweights, some heavyweights, and tag teams. The Women's division may be open soon if the original handlers get back to me soon. I am posting a revised Faq with the rules of the fed etc. As of now, unless you all ready contacted me about entering, SJPW is *CLOSED* to all American wrestlers. I have too many now. I am looking for roleplayers who want and like the challenge of playing a japanese wrestler (or Korean/Chinese etc) DO NOT email me about entering a US wrestler! SJPW is trying to simulate the feel of japanese wrestling, so no more Gaijin (non-japanese, non-asian) wrestlers will be accepted. SJPW is a member of the GIWA. The GIWA homepage is at: http://www.bconnex.net/~[detail withheld]/giwa.html is you want to find out what the GIWA is all about. It has since been modified by [name withheld], and [name withheld]. INTRO & STAFF: Welcome to Super Japan Pro-Wrestling (SJPW)! The SJPW started operations in March of 1997. The CEO of the SJPW was [name withheld]. He has since been replaced by Shinsaku Yamashita. Contact is [detail withheld] . My real name is [name withheld]. I run virtually the entire SJPW, which is definitly not an easy task. I book and write up the matches and put together the cards. SJPW is part of the newly formed Global Internet Wrestling Alliance (GIWA), so I get some input from the other GIWA fedheads. We also recognize the GIWA world title as the true world title. SJPW security is headed by Shinya Misawa. It is his job to enforce the laws of SJPW. As SJPW is trying to simulate japanese style wrestling as best as possible, Shinya keeps those laws intact with his enforcement of it. The rules of conduct are his job to enforce. They will be listed later in the faq. WRESTLERS: SJPW is primarily a federation for Japanese wrestlers. the ratio of Japanese to Gaijin (non-japanese wrestlers) is 3-1. Gaijin wrestlers are any wrestlers not from japan (american, mexican, etc) Please contact the SJPW fedhead if you wish to enter a non-japanese wrestler, to secure the few slots available INTERVIEWS: The more you participate the better off your wrestler will do. By writing up flashes (comments with no interviewer present), interviews, and strategies (for your matches) you have a good chance and being successful in the SJPW. This is especially helpful if your opponent did not send in anything. Form and length of interviews are mostly up to you! You can either rant and rave, or have an interviewer there. The SJPW interviewers are: Miss Sharon Akiyama, an attractive woman in her mid 20s, that loves the good looking wrestlers and is biased towards them (no matter if they are heel or face). Akira Ono, a former wrestling champion and legend in the orient. Was a heel wrestler and continues to favor heel wrestlers in his broadcasting and interview days. RULES OF CONDUCT: The following rules of conduct are strictly enforced by SJPW, to help simulate the feel of japanese wrestling more. Violation of these rules can lead to wrestler suspensions. Repeated violations can lead to expulsion from SJPW. The Rules Of Conduct are as follows: 1) No attacking SJPW officials (including announcers) 2) No attacking wrestlers (This one has SOME leeway, but very little) at ringside or on the way to the ring 3) Only 1 licensed Bodyguard/Manager/Valet allowed at ringside. This includes tag team partners! 4) No attacking SJPW fans 5) All wrestlers must act in a professional manner. RULES REVISIONS & UPDATE: The following rules were featured in the first SJPW newsletter. This is a more detailed explanation of the rules of SJPW, and why these rules exist. Since SJPW is trying to simulate traditional japanese wrestling (like New Japan), this fed is run slightly different than other feds. This may turn some of you off, but I want SJPW to stress the skills of the wrestlers, not how violent they can get. Since the Faq may not have been descriptive enough, I'm going to detail the rules more thoroughly. These rules reflect alot of what [name withheld] wanted to do with SJPW. If you feel these rules are restricting, and wish to leave, I wish you the best. Hopefully you won't feel that way, but if you do, I understand if you leave. SJPW is very strict about the use of objects and attacking people. Wrestlers who come to ringside will be restrained by SJPW security. This is to avoid sneak attacking wrestlers as they fight. I want wrestlers to win their matches by their skills (ie strats/flashes etc), which is how the japanese run things. No screw-jobs (except maybe draws!). Attacking someone backstage will only get you in trouble if the attack causes severe damage to your opponents (like keeping them from wrestling). Putting the boots to them a few times is ok, but putting them in the hospital is not. Doing that will get you fined the first time, suspended the second, and banned the third time. Using objects is also frowned upon. If you're caught, you get dq'd. Out of the ring, you can use chairs, put someone through a table, and whipping someone into the railing. Using objects (Brass knuckles, canes, etc) is a no-no. Security will forcibly remove such objects from your possession if they catch you using them. The fans in Japan don't advocate this, so don't expect them to hand you anyhting either! You want to win, WRESTLE! That's how it is in SJPW. STRATS: As I said before, Strategies give you an extra edge in your matches (as long as you send them in fast enough for me to use them!) A sample strategy might be something like this: Kensake Sasuke will not try to match power with Great Muta. He will use his speed to out maneuver the bigger man. He will hit as many high flying moves as possible. Dropkicks, Flying Elbows, Spin Wheel Kicks, etc. He will also go for pinfalls whenever the big man is off his feet (hopefully stunning Great Muta with a Dropkick off the Top or something similar). If he gets in trouble, he will roll out of the ring, hoping for a breather. If Great Muta comes out after him, he will get back into the ring asap. TITLES & RANKINGS: The SJPW has 5 titles.. - The SJPW Heavyweight Title - The SJPW Super J Crown Title - The SJPW Cruiserweight Title (lightweight, 235 lbs. & under) - The SJPW Battlecade Title (This title is for specialty/extreme style matches, and is usually only defended at pay per views) - The SJPW Tag-Team Titles Titles must be defended every 4 cards (equivilent of 30 days). They must also be defended against a top contender or it doesn't count as a defense. You can, of course, still give unranked wrestlers a shot at the title(s). All titles except the Super J Crown Title must be defended against a wrestler (or wrestlers in Tag-Team) in the top 5. The Super J Crown Title must be defended against a wrestler in the top 10. The Super J Crown Champ is the #1 contender to the Heavyweight title, and can force a Heavyweight Title match at any time. If they lose, however, they forfeit another shot for 12 cards (3 months). If the Super J Crown Champion (or Cruiserweight Champion for that matter) defeats the Heavyweight Champion, they must give up their original title to the Board of Direcors (who will determine a match or matches for a new champion). If a wrestler is 1/2 of the SJPW Tag-Team Champions, they may ALSO hold a singles title at the same time. They will be busy though. :-) The SJPW Champions themselves have most of the say in who they defend their title against. There will undoubtably be challenges from the other handler's wrestlers, and you may accept or reject them. If there are no challenges, then throw one out yourself. I'm sure most wrestlers wouldn't mind getting a title shot. :-) Rankings are computed after each wrestling card. The ranking system takes into account the ranking of your opponent and bases the positive points (for a win) and negative points (for a loss) accordingly. In most cases, the rankings are based per division. The Cruiserweights are a special case in the rankings, however. Whenever a cruiserweight (235 lbs or less) takes on another cruiserweight, the outcome is based in the lightweight rankings. If a cruiserweight takes on a heavyweight, then the outcome is based in the heavyweight rankings. Therefore, the lightweights could be a contender in both divisions. Basically, what they do in each division is totally independent of the other. HOW MATCHES WORK: The SJPW is a BOOKED league, meaning the I decide who wins the matches, based on the current angle(s) and participation of both parties in question (flashes, strats, etc..) CARDS: We have 1 main show a week from the Tokyo Dome in Tokyo Japan called SJPW: Ring Wars. In addition to that card, flashes are sent out twice a week on a show called SJPW: Trash Talk. Occationaly, I will send out house show reports with only results being shown. This special card is called SJPW: After Dark. SJPW Trash Talk (TT) is an interview show hosted by SJPW American broadcaster Eddie Rose. All the flashes received will be shown on this card. depending on how many flashes I get, this show will come out twice a week. SJPW Ring Wars (RW) is the actual wrestling card. There are between 5 and 8 matches on each card. The cards range in the divisions of the matches, but I like to try and have at least 2 Cruiserweight matches, and 1 tag team match. It is normally not a problem getting numerous heavyweight matches for the card. Ring Wars is commentated on by Eddie Rose and Akira Ono. Usually, there are interviews on this show as well (in between matches, pre match comments, etc). Every once in a while there might be a bonus match in between Ring Wars. Basically if I have some extra time, or the challenges for a particular person gets too much to spread out over weeks, then I'll do one of these. There may also be press conferences, upcoming card promotions, and other such messages sent out. PAY-PER-VIEWS: I try not to encourage specialty type matches on Ring Wars. Maybe one every once in a while, but this is generally for regular 20-30 minute matches. Specialty matches take place on the Pay Per Views. Pay Per Views normally take longer to go out as well.. They are more in depth and can take weeks to do. Here's the plan for this coming year's Pay Per Views... SJPW Battlecade (BC) - May (The Battlecade title will be decided here) SJPW Super J Crown Tournament (SJCT)- July (Note: The Super J Crown tournament will most likely include competitors from other GIWA affiliated federations) SJPW '97 Carnival of Champions (CC) - October (Note: Carnival Of Champions will also include wrestlers from other GIWA affiliated federations) SJPW Wrestle Fest (WF) - January I do have to state here that I reserve the right to change dates, change format, and add or remove pay-per-views for any reason. (what power, eh?) CONTACTS: You may contact the other wrestlers in the SJPW to get to know them, set up teams, feuds, etc. Make sure that once you set something up, that both (or all) of the parties confirm it with me so I know. There is a list of the current SJPW Handlers that goes out every once in a while through the mailing list and can be found on this website. FINALE: I hope you all enjoy/have enjoyed the SJPW as much as I have bringing it to you.
