Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Federation of E-Wrestling Superstars opened in February 1996, according to a contemporaneous community directory's own dated entry, listed there as a booked promotion already “Closed to new wrestlers” by the time that directory was updated in April 1997.

Its one surviving broadcast, FeWS Thunder, opens on 16 October 1997 with an overhead shot of the Greensboro Coliseum and a voice-over recalling real wrestling history there — Lex Luger and Sting hoisting the 1988 Crockett Cup, Ric Flair chopping Dusty Rhodes, Magnum TA suplexing Tully Blanchard — before cutting to its own new World Champion, Akira Hashimoto, holding the FeWS World Title belt over his head after what the broadcast calls “the greatest match in FeWS history,” the main event of a prior card, Heat Storm.

Its broadcast team that night was play-by-play man John Cruise, color commentator “Hollywood” JP Palmer, and “Dirty” Doug Simpkins, whose own t-shirt read “DIRTY DOUG KICKED MY ASS” over a picture of Jason Midnight. The announced main event pitted “Sinful” Steve Mercer against Hashimoto for the World Title, and Simpkins used the broadcast to argue that his own pick, Chris Fantasy, had been “robbed” at Heat Storm — a grievance he promised would surface on a program called Weaver's World.

The show opens on a Television Title defense, and the capture of it ends mid-sentence during the challenger's introduction. How that title match, the Mercer–Hashimoto main event, or the promotion itself ended is not in the surviving record.
