Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Missouri Wrestling Alliance introduced itself to new talent on 18 August 1998 as “The Devil's Playground,” a regional arm of the National Wrestling Council that had, in its own words, “been going strong for nearly 9 Months” despite a recent staffing setback. Its own co-president, self-billed as a former two-time Golden Gloves champion under the name Johnny Casanova, signed the recruitment post “maximum respect” and pitched an already-running roster: “VIP” Eddie Zam and Victor “Big Hurt” Manson, billed together as the Ruthless Villains and, per an outside magazine, undisputed tag champions of the world, alongside veterans Jon Atlas and Mr. Sinister.

It ran its own Missouri State, Ozark Mountain, Television and Cruiserweight titles, with a Council-wide title shot at the NWC Heavyweight or Tag Team Championships awarded to a wrestler or team of its own president's choosing.

It continued under its full name into 1999: a 31 January broadcast, Genocide, opened from the South Broadway Athletic Club in St. Louis with signs in the crowd reading “I've got PRIDE in the MWA!”, and billed its main event as Council pay-per-view material — Victor Manson defending the Missouri State title against “Honolulu” Billy Page for a Council World title shot at an event called Snowbrawl. The surviving broadcast runs only as far as an undercard debut match, won by Justin Trouble over Paul Cueffos with a maneuver its own commentary called the Trouble Shooter; the main event's own result isn't in the surviving record.

No closure notice survives. What became of the Missouri Wrestling Alliance after that January 1999 broadcast isn't known from what remains.
