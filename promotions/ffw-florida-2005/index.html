Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Florida's Finest Wrestling's own founding document, “GLORY DAYS! The Chronicles of Florida's Finest Wrestling,” posted 14 June 2005, presents itself as the chronicle of an already-closed 1980s Florida territory: a revisionist-history promotion whose own role-plays were to begin with a January 1985 event and run action that, in its own words, “technically happened twenty years ago.”

Its own narrated history opens in 1978, when “Diamond” Jack Tatum founded Florida All-Star Wrestling out of high school gyms and state fairs around Jacksonville. Renamed Florida's Finest Wrestling as it expanded into Tallahassee and Orlando, it crowned its own first State Heavyweight Champion, “GQ” Johnny Handsome, in June 1982, and its own first State Tag Team Champions, the Baker Boys. Absorbing a rival promotion, Sunshine State Wrestling, in 1983, it merged the two title lineages: “Dirty” Dick LeBaron beat Handsome to become its own first Heavyweight Champion, and the Baker Boys unified the tag titles over Mr. Puerto Rico II and Masked Menace. A second absorption, of South Florida Wrestling, left it, in its own telling, the only wrestling body of significance left in the state by the end of 1984.

Its own account of its downfall follows: Big League Wrestling, by then the country's top promotion, began raiding its top draws in 1985, taking Heavyweight Champion “Dirty” Dick LeBaron and Tag Team Champions Bryan Random and Billy Vance — the “Tampa Bay Express,” repackaged in Big League Wrestling as “Heavy Metal.” By its own account, financial difficulty and Big League Wrestling's rising fortunes forced it to fold and merge into that larger promotion in 1998.

No further document under this name survives, and whether the promotion's own planned 1985-set role-plays ever actually ran isn't in the surviving record.
