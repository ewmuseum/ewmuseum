A Pittsburgh-area independent whose F.A.Q., staff roster and two Addiction cards survive from a five-week June 2001 run. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Tri-State Wrestling dated its own creation to 5 June 2001, its F.A.Q. describing a promotion built around New York, New Jersey, and Pennsylvania and headquartered in Trenton, New Jersey. It ran two shows: Dissention, an interview program compiled from submitted flash packets and sent out early in the week, and Addiction, its main wrestling card on Wednesday nights, with occasional pay-per-views planned every other month or so. Two championships were named for the roster to chase, a TSW Heavyweight Title and a set of Tag Team Titles.

A recruiting post of 12 June found only three wrestlers signed — “Redback” Baz Quinn, Rain, and Derek Decker — ahead of a debut card already set for 20 June, and a follow-up the next day, titled “Everything Scrapped, basically,” weighed delaying that debut until the roster reached eight or ten wrestlers so the promotion could start “the right way.” Its own F.A.Q., reposted around the same time, had already promised an eight-man tournament to crown the first TSW Heavyweight Champion on that very debut show.

The debut went ahead regardless, airing 20 June 2001 from Three Rivers Stadium in Pittsburgh with no tournament in sight: Rain lost to a newcomer named Brian Payne who wasn't even signed to the roster, Baz Quinn defeated Trent Justice, and the main event nearly lost its match entirely when Derek Decker arrived at the building only minutes before his scheduled bout against Shawn Cage, going on to win with a move billed as the Power Drill.

TSW's first Dissention, on 25 June, opened with its own president welcoming viewers and running down eight names on the roster, including a lawman-gimmicked wrestler called Sheriff, shown in a vignette confronting a shoplifting teenager inside a K-Mart while an actual theft went unnoticed behind him.

A lineup posted for the next Addiction, dated 27 June 2001, set Derek Decker against Baz Quinn in the main event, paired the newcomers Sheriff and Will “The Thrill” Jaxson, and built a handicap match around Rain facing two anonymous callers who had phoned the TSW office to issue a challenge.

That lineup is the last document Tri-State Wrestling's own record carries. Whether the 27 June card was ever staged, whether the eight-man heavyweight tournament promised for its debut ever ran on a later show, and how or when the promotion ended aren't in the surviving record.
