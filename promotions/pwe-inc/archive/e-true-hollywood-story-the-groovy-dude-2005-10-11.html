E! True Hollywood Story: The Groovy Dude

11 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
E! True Hollywood Story: The Groovy Dude The Groovy Dude — Posted: Oct 11 2005, 01:42 AM I don't really need this graded, I just wanted to introduce Groovy to y'all. So... here goes the The E! True Hollywood Story: The Groovy Dude Some old home video footage is shown of a young Groovy Dude playing on a slip-n-slide in the front yard of a suburban community. Groovy’s Mom is sitting on a chair on the deck, overlooking her son playing, and it can be assumed that Groovy’s father is behind the camera. Narrator: The Groovy Dude’s story starts like most children under the warm rays of the golden Californian sun. He was born and raised near the beaches of Santa Cruz, California, where he spent most of his free time as a teenager. Groovy’s Mom is sitting in her living room, camera set up to record her interview. Groovy’s Mom: My son was a typical kid. He would wake up, eat some Fruit Loops, go play outside, come back in for lunch, dinner, and have some of my homemade pudding. He was such a sweet kid, the best anyone could ever hope for. Narrator: Groovy’s childhood was normal, but something happened to him at the age of twelve, something that grabs the attention of most boys that age, but for The Groovy Dude, it was a life changing experience. A video clip of WrestleMania is shown, Hulk Hogan battling The Ultimate Warrior in a title vs. title match. The narrator speaks over these images. Narrator: The Groovy Dude sat tranfixed to the television set, watching these two behemoths battling it out in the World Wrestling Federation ring, for the most coveted prize in Pro Wrestling, the World Wrestling Federation Championship. Groovy’s Mom: From that point on, there was no pulling my son away from the world of wrestling. I think by the time he was thirteen, I knew that this was his destiny. To become one of those men that he loved to watch every week on television, and every month in Newark. Narrator: The Groovy Dude was obsessed with the world of wrestling, not only did he watch the World Wrestling Federation’s shows every week on television, but he would ask his mother to take him to Newark, California to watch the GEC in live action. The Groovy Dude is sitting in an empty arena, as a ring is being built in the center, the construction is seen going on behind him, but the main focus is on The Groovy Dude, as he answers the unheard question of the interviewer. The Groovy Dude: Aww, dude... this Groovy Dude remembers the very first time he watched wrestling. Hulk Hogan against the Ultimate Warrior at WrestleMania... From then on, this Groovy Dude knew that through that sport, he would be the happiest, dude, so he really couldn’t get enough of it, you know? This Groovy Dude had to see it as often as he could, this Groovy Dude had to read as much about it as he could, and he definitely had to be a part of it someday. Narrator: At the age of sixteen, for his birthday, The Groovy Dude’s mother gave him the best gift anyone could offer him... tuition for the training school for the GEC. Promoter for the GEC, Rick Rogers, is sitting in his office. Behind him are pictures of greats that came through his wrestling school, including one of The Groovy Dude from his GEC days. Rick Rogers: Right away I knew there was something special in this kid. Had he been a little older, I would’ve sent him out on his first match four months into training. But, I have this little rule about my wrestling business, and that is I only put out people who are eighteen or older. So, this kid was definitely ready for his first match. With two years of training behind him, he was sure to be a crowd pleaser... Old video of The Groovy Dude during his days with the GEC is shown. Narrator: The Groovy Dude was able to please the crowd, and it wasn’t long before the scouts had locked their sights on the young Groovy Dude and he was offered a contract with the WHWA. Groovy’s Mom: Oh, I can remember the day that my son came running up the stairs to the porch and bursting into the living room, waving this little paper around in his hands. He yelled “Moogie! Moogie! They want an audition!!” It wasn’t long before I figured out that this could be his break into the world of big time wrestling, that his dream could be realized before you knew it. The Groovy Dude: Dude, this Groovy Dude can’t describe the feeling of when you’re offered your first major contract, you know? This Groovy Dude showed his mother, grabbed a bowl of pudding and signed that contract, then called everyone that had ever told this Groovy Dude that wrestling was for losers. WHWA owner Kent Peterson is sitting in his own living room, in a large Lay-Z-Boy. Kent Peterson: The Groovy Dude was someone that we saw great potential in, and we desperately wanted to be the ones to bring him to the big league. Unfortunately, we were suffering as a company at the time, and well... things had to be reevaluated before we could totally unleash his abilities in the ring. Footage from The Groovy Dude’s WHWA days are shown. It’s mainly tag team footage between him dressed in jeans and cowboy boots, and another man wearing long black tights with flames. Kent Peterson: We teamed him up right away with one of our mid-card stars, Flame. We figured teaming the two up would be a good start for the rookie, since Flame had been with us for about a year and a half, reaching moderate success, and we thought if we put The Dude, or The Outlaw as we called him, under Flame’s wing, he would naturally come out of his indie shell and become part of the big time. Magazine pictures and newspaper photos of Flame and The Groovy Dude together, wrestling, posing, winning matches. The Groovy Dude’s old partner, Matthew Travis (Flame) is sitting in his den. Matthew Travis: Groovy was a good guy, I had a lot of fun with him. After things got sour in the WHWA, we were pleased to have found work together for the next few years, he’s definitely a great man to work with, and a delight to have backstage. Narrator: The Groovy Dude, known as Outlaw, and Flame, wrestled together in the WHWA for the next few months. But disaster struck the company when their stock plummeted when their star, Ken Andrews, was sidelined with a major injury. The WHWA was forced to make cuts from their budget, which happened to include The Groovy Dude and his partner, Matthew Travis. Groovy’s Mom: When my son was laid off, it was a terrible time for him. He felt that he wasn’t good enough for the big leagues. He thought that he didn’t do enough to impress the people who were important in the Wrestling world and he was sure that his career was over before it really got a chance to begin. Matthew Travis: It was hard, I was used to having that gig. We were featured a little less than often, but we figured that when the stock went sour, our jobs would be secure. It simply wasn’t the case. Groovy was devastated, but this was his dream, wrestling was his destiny, so he wasn’t about to just let it die. Narrator: Contrary to Groovy’s own beliefs, he had impressed someone out in the world of Wrestling. Just two weeks after his contract was terminated with the WHWA, the GEC was interested in the team of Matthew Travis and The Groovy Dude. Groovy was contacted about jumping to the GEC to compete in the newly born Tag Team division. The GEC logo is shown, as well as tournament brackets, which show a team called “The Idiots” with a small caption reading M.T. Headed & Triple D. GEC owner Luke Swanson is in an office, it’s not clear on whether it is a wrestling office or something else. Luke Swanson: I remember when we got The Groovy Dude, he had such untapped talent, we were thrilled when we heard that the WHWA had released him, as well as M.T. They had great chemistry together and we were very pleased when we were able to sign the both of them. Narrator: The Groovy Dude and Matthew both signed with the GEC in a matter of days. The GEC and the two had a long discussion about the direction of the team, as well as the direction of the Tag Team division within the GEC. Matthew Travis: We talked for a long time about what was going to happen. I, personally, had grown tired of his persona we had created in the WHWA, Flame just wasn’t something I was looking for in a long-time career move. I wanted to break out of his “I’m tough” mentality and have some fun with Groovy. Luke Swanson: We eventually came to the agreement that they would form a new team called “The Idiots.” M.T. would take on the role of M.T. Headed, and his partner would be none other than Groovy, known then as Triple D, or Duh Duh Dumbass. They were a joy to watch, as well as listen to behind the microphone. Though they never won our tag team titles, they were among the tops in the league... Narrator: At the height of their success, disaster struck. Footage of a tag team match between The Idiots and The Titans is shown, Triple D (Groovy) is thrown over the top rope and on to the floor, but he lands on one leg and immediately topples over. Groovy’s Mom: When my son hurt his knee... oh... it was so scary for me. To watch him fall like that... oh my... Groovy’s Mom wipes a tear from her eye. Matthew Travis: I had no idea what had happened to him. The match was supposed to end with The Titans doing their signature double DDT and Groovy was supposed to save the pin, but when he didn’t show up, I was able to kick out in time. I glanced over at Groovy on the ground, writing in pain, holding his knee. We had to do something fast, The Titans noticed Groovy’s problem, and I quickly came up with the new end. I superkicked Titan One and clotheslined Titan Two over the ropes, climbed to the top, hit him with the Dropzone, basically a flying headbutt, and ended the match before rushing quickly to Groovy, trying to find out what’s wrong with him and if there was anything I could do to help. Luke Swanson: It was a terrible day for Groovy’s career. We had no idea what was going on, and we’re sure he didn’t either. Groovy was out for over a year with that injury. I think the person who suffered the worst, though, was M.T. Matthew Travis: Something my father always told me, was to never sign a contract for more than a year, especially if it was with something new... This time, however, it turned out to be a problem for me. When Groovy got hurt, creative couldn’t figure out what to do with me, I was still on the active roster, but really wasn’t being used too much. When our contracts came to expire, the GEC simply didn’t know what to do with us, so we didn’t get resigned. Groovy’s Mom: It really was a shame. He had been injured, and then his contract expired. Again, he was devistated, and wasn’t sure if his career would continue. But, he held in there, and when he was well enough, we did get a good call from his friend, Matthew. Narrator: Luck, again, was on Groovy’s side. Not long after the contracts expired, M.T. Headed got a call from the AWA, offering the team yet another run. The Groovy Dude: That injury was probably this Groovy Dude’s darkest hour. Seriously, this Groovy Dude didn’t know if he would ever recover. Luckily enough, M.T. had found work within the AWA, and they were kind enough to help this Groovy Dude out with medical expenses while he worked through the worst of his injury. Narrator: The deal was to have Matthew Travis work in the AWA as a singles star, and when The Groovy Dude healed from his injury, he would rejoin his friend in the ring to reform their team. Matthew Travis: It was a good move for all of us. The AWA treated us well, they payed for Groovy’s rehab, as well as giving him a salary to pay for whatever expenses he had. I think that generosity led to the AWA’s downfall. Scenes of M.T. Headed and the newly named Groovy Dude’s debut match is shown. They ultimately lose the match, setting forth one of the biggest losing streaks in recorded history. Narrator: When The Groovy Dude was well enough to rejoin the ring, the AWA immediately put the team together. M.T. Headed was faced with a handicap match situation, and was saved by his long-time friend, Groovy. The Groovy Dude: An interesting story comes out of that situation, dude. Somehow, M.T. was able to walk out of the GEC with rights to the name “M.T. Headed” but this Groovy Dude wasn’t. There was a meeting after this Groovy Dude was cleared to wrestle, and thus this Groovy Dude was born. Narrator: This new team of M.T. Headed and The Groovy Dude was met with fan support, but not because of their tremendous victories. It was probably their defeats that made the fans love them more. Matthew Travis: We were certainly the underdogs. That’s why the people loved us. They hungered for the day that we would finally be victorious, and they were with us, through thick and thin, never giving up hope that we would get a victory. The Groovy Dude: Dude, this Groovy Dude and M.T. were stopped on the street by fans who wanted to tell us that we had their support. It was a good feeling to know that people were watching, cheering for us, and admiring us for our dedication. Narrator: The team’s final record came to 1-107. Unfortunately, the AWA was met with financial difficulties, and sadly the owners had to pull the plug on the slowly dying company. Matthew Travis: It definitely was a sad day for all of us. Groovy and I were never met with such care and generosity in all our years working in the business. We were most certainly company jumpers, but the end of our runs with a certain Federation was never our choice. We were cut twice, and finally the AWA went bankrupt. However, they didn’t let us go without giving our fans something to scream for. Scenes from Groovy and M.T.’s match on the AWA’s final broadcast is shown, their match is shown, and finally, their victory and celebration. Narrator: During the final broadcast of the AWA, The Groovy Dude and M.T. Headed went into a match against the Tag Team Champions for their titles. After a long and hard-fought match, the team came out victorious. The Groovy Dude: Dude, it really was a fitting end for the run that M.T. and this Groovy Dude had, the fans had something to remember us by, something to hold on to, something that we would never have the chance to top, since after that, the spark left M.T., and when it was time for this Groovy Dude to move on, M.T. didn’t really want to go with him. Narrator: With the growing stress of traveling, Matthew Travis decided that it would be a better idea if he stayed at home with his pregnant wife, Jennifer Travis, back home in Nebraska. Matthew Travis: I saw this as my opportunity to get out. I was very tired, and it was hard for me to be with my wife for only a day or two out of the week, especially when she was carring our child. After my son, Drake, was born, there really was no choice. I stayed out of the business and haven’t gone back. Sometimes I miss it, the rush of the fans, the thrill of the fight, but I really believe that I’ve made the right decision. Narrator: Before the AWA finally came to their end, The Groovy Dude had been in talks with the RWA, to become their newest singles wrestler. Groovy’s Mom: This time, there wasn’t much of a transition period between jobs for my son. He won the Tag Team titles in the AWA and after two or three weeks, he was in the ring for the first time with the RWA, and that is where, I feel, he really found where he wanted to be in life. RWA owner Boyce of Reason is sitting behind a desk in an office, its not clear on whether it is an office for wrestling, or something else. Boyce of Reason: I remember the first time I saw The Groovy Dude back in the WHWA as Outlaw. Like many others, I saw great potential, but I didn’t have the money to lure him away from the contracts that was being offered. When I heard that the AWA was closing, and Groovy would be fair game, I immediately contacted him, worked out a deal that Groovy liked, and it was a matter of signing on the line. We debuted him as soon as possible, and our fans were delighted. He was a hit, and the RWA has the distinct honor of giving The Groovy Dude his first singles championship of his career. Narrator: Before the RWA gave him a title shot, they wanted to do something to grab the fans attention by doing something that was out of Groovy’s nearly-always used persona. Boyce of Reason: We wanted to see Groovy as a heel. It had been something I’d be dying to see, and since I was in charge, I got to see it happen. We had Groovy take a nasty chair shot, we said that he “knocked a screw back into place.” A week later, Groovy ran out during a particularly brutal match, we shut off the lights, sounded some particularly annoying alarms, and the lights came back on to show Groovy holding a dented chair, with the two other wrestling lying unconscious in the ring. Groovy wore a shirt and tie, and announced that he would henceforth be called “The Censor Guy.” It was a really interesting angle. A clip from the episode where Groovy premieres as The Censor Guy is shown. The Censor Guy: Ladies and gentlemen, what you have seen here, is a statement to all in the back, all in the truck, and all watching at home. I will no longer sit idly by and watch this sport go down the toilet as we succumb to this brutal display of gore and sexual displays, I will personally put an end to the outrageous behavior shown by the RWA’s owners, employees, and every fan that sits in those seats. The age of The Censor Guy, has begun! The Groovy Dude: Ha! Dude, the Censor Guy was kind of a kooky guy, you know? This Groovy Dude didn’t really know what he was thinking at the time, but it seemed right, you know? Luckily for this Groovy Dude, someone totally freakin’ awesome was around to pull this Groovy Dude from the darkness and back into the light... unfortunately, it wouldn’t be the last time that this Groovy Dude has fallen from grace. Narrator: Even if the owner of the RWA enjoyed the new character, the fans didn’t take to this new Groovy Dude, and eventually it was decided that he had to return to his original ways. Boyce of Reason: Something that is consistent with The Groovy Dude, is his absolute love for The Ultimate Warrior. The Warrior might not be so popular right now, but back when he was on top, The Groovy Dude worshiped him, and probably still does to this day. To bring The Groovy Dude back, all we had to do was knock that screw back out of place, and remind The Groovy Dude of where he really wanted to be in life. The Groovy Dude: They had it set up so this Groovy Dude got to meet his idol, The Ultimate Warrior. It was the best thing ever, dude... EVER! The clip is shown. The Ultimate Warrior walks up to The Censor Guy, who is standing in the hall drinking a beverage out of a foam up. The Warrior approaches The Censor Guy, looks at him right into the eyes, takes him by the throat, shoving him into the wall. The Warrior simply mutters “snap out of it” and The Censor Guy goes limp. Later on that night, The Censor Guy’s followers are standing in the ring, demanding to see their leader, find out what’s going on. The lights go out just like before, but this time “I Wanna Rock ‘N Roll All Night” by KISS hits and The Groovy Dude, wearing his Hawaiian shirt, tank top, and orange shorts runs out, tackling his former followers. He hits them all with Groovetastic Slaps and the fans go nuts. Groovy grabs some pudding and downs it on his way back up the ramp. Boyce of Reason: I love how Groovy is so agreeable. He really is eager to try anything, do anything... and that’s exactly got him his first singles championship... It was The Groovy Dude, versus former follower and US Champion, Tommy Agostino, first blood match. Scenes of the first blood match are shown. It’s done all at the ring, never going beyond the barricade. Agostino tries time after time to bust Groovy open, but it just doesn’t work out for him, and all Groovy seems to be doing is wearing down Agostino, he never really seemed to be going for the kill. Groovy’s opponent, Tommy Agostino is sitting in the same office as Boyce, just without Boyce. Tommy Agostino: When the Boyce told me that I would be dropping the title to Groovy, I knew it was the right thing to do, and I am certainly not one to bitch about what the boss says... But the idea of a first-blood match? Not really Groovy’s style up ‘til then. But we made it work, definitely. We played it off as if Groovy was too timid to bust me open, but when he did, it set him up for future glory, most certainly. Boyce of Reason: Groovy wasn’t sure how he wanted to end it all, so we sat down with him and Tommy and broke it down. Finally, it was agreed upon that Groovy would walk out of the arena with the US Championship due to... a paper cut. The Groovy Dude whips Agostino into the barricade, he grabs a fans Coke, and their sign, dumps the coke on Tommy’s head, and takes the sign, raking the edge across the arm of Tommy, slicing him open with a paper cut. The referee calls for the bell, and awards Groovy with the US Championship. The Groovy Dude: It was one of the greatest experiences of this Groovy Dude’s career. Winning his first singles championship was a distinct honor. Groovy’s Mom: Groovy’s career totally took off from there. Offers from other companies started to pour in, and there was one that he just couldn’t refuse. Narrator: Brian Benoit of the UWA had contacted The Groovy Dude many times, each time upping the price he was offering. Groovy felt strong ties to those in the RWA, but a series of events would force Groovy to accept the UWA’s offer. Groovy’s Mom: I had some health complications, and my MediCare didn’t cover all of the hospital bills, Groovy didn’t make enough with the RWA to help me out... as much as Groovy loved the RWA, he simply couldn’t stay with them, and his signing bonus with the UWA was enough to pay for 75% of my cost. All I did was hope that the fans would forgive him. UWA President, Brian Benoit is sitting in some comfortable room. Brian Benoit: Groovy was a guy that I’ve wanted to have with us for a long, long time. I kept sending him offers, and they were always being rejected. It frustrated me, but in the end I finally got his attention, too bad it had to come at such a high price, emotionally, I mean. Not only did he leave the RWA and his friends and fans there, but his mother was hospitalized, and he certainly hasn’t been quiet about how he feels about his mother. All and all, I think it worked out just fine. Former UWA Executive, Donovan Troi is sitting in his filing cabinet filled office, desk covered in papers going into detail about an upcoming Tag Team Tournament. Donovan Troi: How to describe The Groovy Dude in a word... Pudding. And I don't mean that because he loves the stuff, I mean it by the way he is. He is a rather relaxed guy, and very unique. He has a certain flavor to him that you just can't mimic, and he's nearly always a laugh waiting to occur. It's been nearly six years that I have worked on and off with The Groovy Dude, and he's one heck of a Superstar. He always give 110% in all he does, and nearly always comes away with a big smile. He truly cares about his mother, his friends and his fans. Narrator: For most of The Groovy Dude’s term with the UWA, he worked in the Hardcore Division, and at the end of his run there he had the UWA Championship strapped around his waist. Groovy’s Mom: My son had a lot of television time with the UWA, but they did very little with him creatively. He had the hardcore championship for a long run there, but when the AWA invaded, he wasn’t even included with their main players. Brian Benoit: I really don’t know what had happened with The Groovy Dude and our creative team. He sorta fell through the cracks. It’s a shame for me, since I tried so hard to get him on the roster, and we really couldn’t figure out what to do with him. When we ran the angle that the old AWA was reborn, and was invading, he was sorta forgotten about. It’s especially terrible because Groovy worked in both the UWA and AWA, and I feel as though he would have added something special to the mix. Narrator: Tiring of only defending his Hardcore Championship, Groovy began to look for opportunities elsewhere. Eventually, the UWA was struck with similar financial hardships that the AWA had faced, and it was inevitable that they were to close it’s doors. Brian Benoit: When the end came, I felt so terrible that we had done Groovy so wrong, that I insisted that we give him a proper farewell. We stuck him in the last match the UWA ever had. It was a Flaming Hell in the Cell Match for the UWA World Championship between Silver Wolfe, X-Dragon, and The Groovy Dude himself. We knew that this was our last show, and we also felt that we had done Groovy wrong, so we had him win that match, and take home the UWA Championship, for no particular reason. Matthew Travis: I kept with Groovy’s career even after I retired, and there was one part, only one part about his run with the UWA, and that can be summed up in three words: Rubber Chicken Championship. Groovy had a huge part in the development of that Championship, it was the unofficial second Hardcore Championship during the UWA/AWA wars, defended 24/7. It was really an excuse for John Miller, a referee, and “Magic” Mark Kurr, a backstage interviewer, to go at it on television, of course them being great friends off camera... but a few other people were able to hold that championship, and I think that they all had a good time with it. Former co-worker within the EFWO, Casey Strata. Casey Strata: The Rubber Chicken Championship was something that was coveted, you know? It was like..the last potato chip in the bag. By itself, it was worth nothing. But it had a legacy, a real one. Narrator: This legacy of the Rubber Chicken Championship would follow The Groovy Dude for the rest of his career. The next step in his career would take him to the EFWO. President of the EFWO, Brian Oxenreider sits in a living room. Brian Oxenreider: I really didn’t know much about The Groovy Dude before my advisors had told me he was available. Once I heard it, I knew exactly what I wanted done. My character on television, X-Dude, had just gone missing, leaving the spot of the Presidency open. I figured we would have the board of directors assign him as the President, and boy did he run with it. Fellow superstar, Casey Strata. Casey Strata: Groovy was one of those guys that always brought a smile to your face. He...It was like when you go to the Grocery Store, and they have the Handicapped bagging your goods. And you know they're trying their best to fit in and lead a normal life, despite having afflictions. It's kinda like that The angle was to have Groovy be corrupted by this “Compliance Committee” which was forcing the EFWO to comply with new, strict, FCC regulations. They eventually won Groovy over, by taking his pudding away from him, I believe, and cutting contact off from his mother. It was really interesting to watch the transformation from this fun-loving guy, to this suit-wearing rule enforcer. He did a great job with that, and it was the right thing for the company to do, though it wasn’t something I wanted to see, I felt it was out of a sense of desperation. Narrator: But, like with all things, it had to come to an end. Groovy, as Mr. Dude, would be on shows weekly, but his run as President wouldn’t last forever, and he was itching to getting back to doing what he loved, wrestling every night, instead of just talking. Donovan Troi: When it was time for the Mr. Dude character to end his run, we put a little vignette of Groovy’s career together to the song “Wake Me Up Inside” by Evanessence. It really was a piece of art, and on television it did the trick to bring Groovy back to his more popular ways. Narrator: The EFWO staff had seen Groovy’s work in other promotions in the Hardcore division, and decided to move him there, too. The Groovy Dude: TV Donovan wanted to give this Groovy Dude a gift for coming back to his senses, and that was the Rubber Chicken Championship. Dude, this Groovy Dude accepted it with open arms and started defending it right away. Eventually, it led to a unification match between this Groovy Dude and Red Star, winner walks away with either the EFWO Hardcore Championship, or the EFWO Rubber Chicken Championship. This Groovy Dude won that, and the Rubber Chicken legacy was brought to the EFWO. Narrator: After a run as the Rubber Chicken Champion, and a short stint as the Television Champion, Groovy was found left in the dust again, creatively. Matthew Travis: From what I gathered from Groovy’s calls, it seemed as though he couldn’t get anywhere higher than he was, simply because there are people there who had been in the EFWO longer, and wouldn’t budge to let a new guy into the top ranks, which is really too bad, since they could have used that momentum that he had when he won the UWA Championship, and carried it to the EFWO. Groovy’s Mom: And again, Groovy was found without work. After his run as Mr. Dude, the EFWO couldn’t find any more use for him, and he fell through the cracks once again. It really is a big shame, since he has so much charisma, so much personality, and so much talent. It’s too bad that he can’t get above the mid-card ranks. The Groovy Dude: This Groovy Dude doesn’t care what anyone says. He is here to do what he loves, and that is to wrestle, and put on a good show for the fans, dude. Narrator: Groovy’s fans, friends, family, and companions all hope that he will find work again, and soon... According to sources, Groovy has received a few offers, and only time will tell which one he will accept.
