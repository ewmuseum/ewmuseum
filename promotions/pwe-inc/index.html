Pro-Wrestling Entertainment ran two brands out of one Invision board, posted its shows a segment at a time, and finished 2005 in Madison Square Garden with a Deadly Game fought across four promotions that all lived on the same forum. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Pro-Wrestling Entertainment lived on a free Invision board and split itself in two: Revenge and Underground, each with a forum where its roster wrote roleplays and a second forum where the shows those roleplays fed into were posted. The shows went up a segment and an hour at a time, so a single broadcast is three or four threads that were never joined back together, and what survives of most of them is one piece.

Its roster ran its own characters alongside wrestlers lifted whole from television. The one document that lists them all — a running board post its own keeper last updated on 7 November 2005 — puts Nicholas Pryde, The Regulator and Clarke “The X” Andrews on the same page as Shawn Michaels, Sting, Triple H, Brock Lesnar and Eddy Guerrero, each with a win-loss line, a debut date and a single match the fed thought was the biggest one he had had. The Regulator's says he held the Television Championship for 308 days, the longest reign of any kind the promotion ever counted.

It was not the only promotion on its own board. ICW, The Proving Grounds and the Foundation of Brutality each had forums of their own beside PWE's, and a fourth, UCW, turned up with a championship of its own. When the year ended they all fought on the same card: Deadly Game 2005, from Madison Square Garden on 27 November, where Shawn Michaels made Marty Jannetty submit in a retirement match, a Silver Crown and a WWE Intercontinental Championship were unified in a three-way asylum match, Team Underground lost the Deadly Game match itself to Team UCW, and Nicholas Pryde made Ryan Styles tap out for the Heavyweight Championship.

The record reaches back further than the board does. Its own roster page credits its longest-serving wrestler with a debut on 6 June 2003 and three championships under the name PW(S)E, at a Deadly Game held that December — a whole earlier era of the promotion for which not one document survives. Every member of the board itself joined in 2004 or later; the first two arrived on 9 and 10 January 2004.

How it ended isn't in the surviving record. Ten days after Deadly Game its own booker posted a thread called Show Status to say he was still working on getting the last matches in; the newest writing anywhere in the corpus is a match thread of 17 December 2005. The board itself was still standing in May 2007, its forums frozen where they stopped.

Affiliations

The Proving Grounds (TPG)

Academy — feeds into PWE (2003) (2004–2005)
Talent moved both ways in the fed’s own storylines: a December 2005 roleplay has a wrestler boast of beating “one of the best superstars on the TPG roster, who just got promoted to Underground”, and PWE’s own Saga III of May 2005 opened its first hour with a TPG Showcase. TPG’s own record carries eight championships and two years of its own touring shows.

Foundation of Brutality (FoB)

Academy — feeds into PWE (2003) (2003–2005)
Two of the fed’s own championship vacancies are recorded as call-ups: “Due to Lennex’s promotion to Revenge.” and “Brian Stars moved up to Revenge and vacated the title.” Chris Harvard held its tag titles from the belt’s birth to its death and wrestled for PWE at the same time.

Ultimate Championship Wrestling (UCW, Ultimate, 2005–2006)

Acquired by PWE (2003) (2005) — The purchase is staged in-fiction. What is out of fiction is that UCW's roster, its shows and three of its championships moved onto PWE's own board from October 2005 and were defended there, and that PWE's own announcer calls the promotion “the defunct UCW”.

ICW (2005)

Crossover booking with PWE (2003) (2005)
A hosting arrangement rather than a partnership: ICW had already closed, and the board gave it one night and then an archive shelf. No shared championship, no shared storyline and no return.
