New fed, join ... or don't.

29 September 2004

******************************************************************************* [NECW] NORTH EAST CHAMPIONSHIP WRESTLING [THE FAQ] ******************************************************************************* TABLE OF CONTENTS: I. Introduction II. Who does what? III. Titles IV. Pay Per Views and Shows V. Characters of the fed VI. Closing Comments VII. Application ******************************************************************************* I. Introduction Ron Copeland is a rich man, a very rich man. He sees that there is much money to be made in wrestling, but he doesn't know the ins or the outs of the business. Andy Grey is not a rich man, not rich at all. However, Andy does have a pretty good grasp on what wrestlers and their fans want. So, an the men came to an agreement. Ron would give Andy a sizable amount of money, and Andy would would go out and create new stars in the wrestling business. This will start small, but if Mr. Copeland and Andy Grey have their way this will end up being huge. In reality, NEWC is run by me, [name withheld] with [name withheld] asking as a co- booker. All we want for this fed to be in fun. If everyone who joins reads the cards, stays active and keeps giving creative input, this is going to be a blast. So, read some more of this and join if you like, I promise it will be fun - just like the old days. ******************************************************************************* II. Who does what? As players, you have a few tasks to do for every card to work. 1. Roleplay 2. Strat (simple ones) 3. Read Basically, if you roleplay well, do some simple strats, and read everything we put out, you'll be good to go and you'll do well in the fed. You'll do even better if you want to help me write. Nah, that was just a tiny joke, if you want to help write great, but if you don't you will never be punished for it. Mike and I have some other things to do. 1. Read your stuff. 2. Pick who wins matches. 3. Write the matches (that's just me). Reading the stuff is easy, however, when it comes to picking who wins we will use a pretty simple formula of how much stuff is worth. Roleplays: 75% Strats: 10% Heat: 10% Interest: 5% ----- 100% Obviously, roleplays are the most important. Then strats and heat (your present angle) take a smaller part of the formula. Then finally, it's the interest you have in other people's angles. If you participate on the forums and talk to each other you'll get this small percent. After we pretend to scientifically tally everything up, we write the matches and the deserving wrestler gets the win. If you win, lose or draw, we are expecting you be adult about it. No one likes a braggart or a whiner. It's just a game. ******************************************************************************* III. Titles NECW has two titles, one for singles and one for tag teams. East Coast Heavyweight Championship: This belt is for the best of the best in NECW, and will be defended pretty often. East Coast Tag Team Championship: The same, but for a team instead of just one wrestler. It still signifies that this team is the best there is. ******************************************************************************* IV. Pay Per Views and Shows NEWC is a small company, so we probably won't be running any PPVs in the near future. When we do, I don't think we'll ever have more than 4 a year. We have only one show, and for now it's every other Friday. It's called East Coast Eternal. There will usually be one or two full matches, then the rest will be in a detailed summary format. ******************************************************************************* V. Characters of the fed Ron Copeland, 49: The owner of NECW, but he is rarely on the show. Andy Grey, 28: The figurehead of NECW, he's the in-character booker and calls the shots behind the scenes. Paddy Martin, 55: The first contract in NECW: Paddy Martin of course. Paddy is probably the most famous East Coast indy commentators ever, and he brings his blind faith in the good guy here to NECW. Leeland Bancroft Jr., 26: Going by LBJ, this guy thinks he's worth more than he really is ... which isn't much. He's egotistical, tacky and a great color man. Eddie Timlin, 22: The interviewer, he's kind of flavorless - but that makes the dull wrestlers look better. ******************************************************************************* VI. Closing Comments Like I said before, NECW is all about fun. We won't be keeping track of wins or loses or any of that bullshit. So, join. ******************************************************************************* VII. Application -- HANDLER INFO: Your Name: Your Age: (Not that it matters, it's just curiosity.)
