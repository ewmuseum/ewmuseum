A January 1996 Usenet fed built around its own operator's recurring "Weizal" ring persona, whose two title belts changed hands repeatedly across its first two house-show newsletters before the surviving record breaks off mid-storyline. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Weizal Wrestling Championship Federation opened on 9 January 1996 with a recruitment post to rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy: “WWCF is here! ... we already have eight competitors,” naming its founding roster — including the operator's own in-ring persona, The Weizal, sitting champion Masked Mayhem, and Shawn Michaels entered as a roster wrestler — and signing off, in the operator's own voice, “Dude, Weiz!” A house-show results report dated the same day corroborates the roster in action.

Its constitution, posted 13 January 1996, set out six titles it meant to award — a World, Intercontinental, Television and King of the Ring championship, plus World and United States tag titles — each to be decided by tournament once enough wrestlers had enrolled, matches run on the Wrestling League Simulator, and a full year of monthly pay-per-views mapped out from that January's Midnight Raid through the following December's Boxing Day Bash.

Only the World and Intercontinental titles are attested changing hands, and both did so repeatedly across the fed's first two house shows and its two surviving newsletters: the World title passed from Masked Mayhem to The Hitman, to The Soul Swiper, back to Masked Mayhem, and finally to The Executioner inside nine days; the Intercontinental strap went from Robbie “Boston” Cooper to Tecker — won with interference from Jamie Cutlass and manager Bobby Heenan — and on to “The Fantasy” Brett Young by a manager's distraction and a foreign object.

The surviving record breaks off on 21 January 1996, previewing that Saturday's Midnight Raid pay-per-view — an eight-man King of the Ring tournament and a mystery-opponent World title defense against The Executioner already lined up. Neither the pay-per-view's results nor how the promotion ended are in the surviving record.
