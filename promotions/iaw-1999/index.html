A weekly broadcast federation whose own president wrestled and spoke on-screen as its authority figure, running a flagship show and a first pay-per-view before a roster decline in its final weeks. Twenty-eight wrestlers are recorded across one served show. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

International Allstar Wrestling dated its own start to August 1999, and by its own later account did well at first, if unevenly: “Everyone roleplayed, but, no-one roleplayed good.”

Its flagship broadcast, Thursday Showdown! (28 October 1999, Georgia Dome), opened with Tommy Hardcore beating Barbed Wire Brian in a hardcore brawl that spilled out of the arena and into a bathroom, ending when Hardcore tore a toilet from the floor and broke it over Brian's head. Co-president Angus upgraded the night's scheduled cage match into a handicap match on the spot, pairing Soul Assassin with Daddy Mack against The British Werewolf; the match spiralled through repeated cage-top escape attempts, a double superplex off the top of the cage, and a late ambush by the ringside “IWO army” before Daddy Mack escaped through the cage door to win. Backstage, its co-presidents teased a mystery debuting star for their coming pay-per-view.

That pay-per-view, First Revolution (31 October 1999), survives only as a results announcement pointing fans to an outside results page, calling it “the best one IAW has every had” and noting that “Champions got crowned and champions got hurt” — no card text or title history for the show itself survives.

By 1 December 1999 its own president admitted the promotion had cut its roster and was struggling to find active roleplayers, seeking ten new members ahead of a 9 December Thursday Showdown! card and a 19 December pay-per-view, Doomsday.

No report of either card survives; how it ended isn't in the surviving record.
