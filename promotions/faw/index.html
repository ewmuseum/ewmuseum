A roleplay-and-results website federation that ran from January 2001 through December 2003 across two homes, faw.projectefed.com and faw-wrestling.com, crowning twelve title lineages and thirty-four stables and tag teams across 133 shows and 274 roleplays. It retired its own Television Championship unfilled in memory of wrestler Joe “Soulja” Smallze and later named a memorial cup for him. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Furiously Addictive Wrestling opened in January 2001 and wrote itself for three years. It ran on roleplays: the application form asked for twenty lines minimum and said plainly that without a sample there would be no reply, the backstage area existed so members could post promos and add their own in-card segments, and the fed judged its own best people by how much they wrote — its Hall of Fame measures two of its inductees in kilobytes.

The television schedule grew as fast as the roster. Panic and FAW is WAR ran through the winter of 2001, Distortion and Hot Stuff through the spring of 2002, Breakdown that August, Uncut in 2003; the pay-per-views came monthly — Taking Hardcore To The Extreme, Deceit, Evil Intentions, When Worlds Collide, Vendetta, Extreme Beating, May Day Massacre, Collision Course, Diabolical Day, SummerSlaughter, Entrapment, Breaking Point. The cards were staged worldwide and billed as fact: 150,000 in Rungnado, Pyongyang; 120,000 in the Salt Lake Stadium, New Dehli; the Maracana, the Millennium Stadium, Madison Square Garden, the Tacoma Dome.

Every July the fed ran a tournament on its own Diabolical Day pay-per-view and crowned a Diabolical Day King, who took a World Championship shot with the title. It carried eight championships at its widest — World Heavyweight, International, Tag Team, Television, European, Hardcore, Extreme and Woman's, with a Continental and a Light-Heavyweight belt behind them — and cut the list to three in 2003, saying so on its own champions page. Two of its own men, Jim ‘JLC’ Phoenix and Skipster, held everything it had.

It retired one of those belts rather than defend it. Joe “Soulja” Smallze wrestled here and died in April 2002, and where a champion's name should have stood on the fed's own Television Championship line it wrote “Retired in honour of Joe Smallze R.I.P” and left it there. It also gave a cup in his name: on 10 August 2003 the ring announcer introduced Tony Vigilante as “the Joe Smallze 2003 memorial cup holder”. That one sentence is everything the surviving record says about the cup.

FAW announced its own hiatus on 13 October 2003 in a press release that asked every member to go to the community forums and confirm they were staying, and promised handler logins and more columns when it came back. It came back inside two months on a rebuilt site, with a live rankings table and a card booked for 15 December. That card is the last dated thing it wrote. The community forum stayed reachable into the summer of 2004 with 382 posts on it, and by that September the domain answered to an advertising page.

It did not start as a fed of its own characters. Through the spring of 2001 FAW booked a roster taken largely off television: its own March and May 2001 rosters run Bradshaw, Crash Holly, Farooq, Chyna, Lita and Trish Stratus beside Bain, HaHaKlown, Jon The Bomb and Spike, with Mick Foley billed as Commissioner and Michael Cole on commentary. Lex Luger and British Bulldog traded its Universal Championship between them; HHH and William Regal held its European Title. Then the arrangement ended, quickly and on camera — the Universal Title was scrapped on 5 May 2001 “due to it getting in the way of progression,” Chris Jericho was fired on air six days later for not roleplaying, and every FAW roster after that summer is the fed's own creations, which is the fed the rest of this wing holds.

The fed's own billed roster, 2001 FAW billed these wrestlers on its own March and May 2001 rosters, and no surviving FAW document stages any of them in a match, an angle or a promo: Bradshaw, Buh-Buh Ray Dudley, Chyna, Crash Holly, Debra, D-Von Dudley, Edge & Christian, Farooq, Lita, Raven, Road Dogg, Shawn Michaels and The Rock. The billing is the fed's own record and is kept as one; no character is minted for a name the fed never put to work, and nothing here is a claim about the wrestlers themselves.

Storylines

The two arcs below are recovered from a full read of every 2002 and 2003 show FAW's own site carries a page for, sixty-plus documents in all.

The World Heavyweight Championship's own long war, 2002–2003 Written by AI, 2026

The Omega Fro pins champion Spawn at Extreme Beating II (31 March 2002) to take the title, only for it to slip away undocumented within a month: by Breaking Point, a debuting tag team billed New School runs in during a four-way with Fro, Spawn and Ben Hart to clear the way for Tony Vigilante to submit Spawn — not the champion, permitted under that match's own explicit no-disqualification rule — and walk out champion himself. Vigilante's reign lasts barely a month: Spawn makes him tap to a reversed Justification in a four-way Hell in a Cell at Distortion 6 that also involves Skipster and The British Bomber. The belt keeps moving all summer — Bomber wins a tournament final at Diabolical Day 2002, JLC takes it from him at SummerSlaughter, and Spawn is billed the new champion entering Entrapment with no match anywhere in the surviving record showing how.

By the time When Worlds Collide 2003 opens the new year, the champion is The 9th Wonder of Foam, and he holds on to the title for the whole of FAW's own 2003 tail this wing's own records cover. He survives Fro in a Lions Den main event at WWC itself; talks down a title challenge from a returning JLC on Panic the following month while Rzarector, that night's own Rumble winner, looks on; and retains again at Extreme Beating Three in a Triple Threat, No Disqualification match against Rzarector and Xander — competing that night "representing" Lars on the strength of an old wager. The title outlives this wing's own show-by-show coverage from there: FAW's own later champions pages (not full recaps) show Rzarector taking it in June 2003, Vigilante regaining it before year's end, and Violator dethroning him on 9 November — the last champion on file.

FAW's own ownership handoff: Hunter to Foster to Tien, 2003 Written by AI, 2026

FAW opens 2003 with a new figure introduced simply as its own president and owner, Lisa Hunter: at When Worlds Collide she strips an unspecified honor from HaHaKlown and crowns Sean Mason the FAW Universal Heavyweight Champion with no match at all, only for Deadman to interrupt the celebration before the locker room clears him out. A month later on Panic, Mason's own return promo credits "Prez Lisa Hunter" personally for luring him back from retirement with the one thing he'd never gotten from FAW before: a Hall of Fame slot. By Extreme Beating Three the same authority figure is billed "Lisa Foster" instead — the show's own text never explains the change — and it's Foster who closes that broadcast announcing she is selling FAW outright, installing her own husband as an "unsackable commissioner" the following week.

The buyer surfaces on Diabolical Day 2003: Lisa Tien arrives billed FAW's brand-new owner and general manager, previously the co-owner of a fed called Innovation of Wrestling, and spends the show rehiring a fired Floyd Rotterdamn and fielding a locked-door visit from X-Rodd whose subject the broadcast never reveals. She is still running FAW as the surviving record closes: the fed's own Panic preview for 15 December has Mafia Incorporated slip a match past her in the small print of a signed contract, and frames her own reaction to a returning Tony Vigilante — shot by an unidentified attacker the previous month — as still genuinely uncertain.

Primetime Central (PTC)

Member of FAW (2001) (Member of PTC Since: February 2002 (PTC's own profile page for FAW))
Channel 11, “EEL Network,” on PTC’s April 2002 channel map. Opened Jan 2001; spun off a training promotion, FAWA. Sent a delegation to PTC’s own “New Era” interfed supercard, 20 Apr 2003, whose Superstars page groups the whole card by member fed. PTC’s own president had rescinded an earlier offer to admit FAW on 8 October 2001, over an EWC front-page incident during the PTC's own president affair (“we are rescinding our offer to allow FAW into PTC”). FAW’s eventual February 2002 membership postdates this rescission — the sequence runs offered, rescinded, then joined.
