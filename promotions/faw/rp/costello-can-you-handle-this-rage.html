Costello can you handle this rage

12/07/02
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Costello can you handle this rage Spawn Furiously Addictive Wrestling Roleplay #93 Date: 12/07/02 Location: panic rp 2 'Are we almost done? This thing is heavy.' 'If you spent as much time working as you did complaining, we would be there by now.' [Fade up.] (Welcome to one of the many beautiful backstage areas in the Crown Coliseum in Fayetteville, North Carolina. The surroundings: wall, ceiling, floor. That's it. Not really all that beautiful, once you think about it.) (There is something of note back here, though. Otherwise, we wouldn't be here. Some of us don't believe in wasting the viewers' time. The something of note happens to be a rather sizeable business desk, which is being moved by two men, one of whom is also rather sizeable. A third man is supervising the entire affair. He holds a clipboard in one hand and a coffee cup that reads 'Property of Sigurd Von Glatz' in the other.) (Time for introductions. The larger man is Jim Sullivan. He's a grip, and he's good at his job. This is the last time his name will be mentioned, probably ever. The man on the other end of the desk is Devin Mills, FAW Backstage Reporter--you know, the one whose legs don't go all the way up. Well...maybe they do, but we haven't really been--never mind.) (The third man is 'The Hell Master' Spawn, as if you couldn't guess. The reason he is supervising the moving of a desk is that William Barnum--yes, that William Barnum--has given him an idea.) DEVIN: Umm...Spawn. Is there any reason why you're not helping with this?' Spawn: I shouldn't even dignify that with an answer. DEVIN: Well, I just thought that since it's your desk, you should-- Spawn: You think? Don't. I'm sure Costello would love nothing better than for me to throw my back out in the days before my match with him. DEVIN: You'd rather have us throw our backs out? Spawn: You catch on fast. DEVIN: What about The Great 1 you have to beat him too? (Spawn responds with the rather nasty look. The kind of look you'd give someone if they asked you who was going to win that big Raiders-Bengals game.) Spawn: Please, Devin. That drooling lack wit couldn't beat me if I cut my own head off before the match. DEVIN: If he isn‘t that good how did he defeated you the last match? Spawn: Is this an interview, Devin? No. No, it is not. This is not an interview; this is you moving a desk. Now what say you get going with the 'moving a desk' part. (Devin Mills, intrepid backstage reporter, scowls at Spawn, then gets back to work. Spawn slips in front of their path, then walks briskly down the hall. Let's follow him, shall we?) Spawn: It's so hard finding good help these days. Cliché, I know, but it's true. (Before too long, Spawn arrives at a door, which is opened slightly. He steps inside, to reveal what looks to be a half-finished office. There's a big comfy swivel chair in the middle, a TV/VCR off to the side, and a plant in the corner. Pretty bare bones, but it's all that's needed. All except one thing, but Mills and that other guy are bringing that in.) Spawn: It's amazing. Buried in the awesome stupidity that is Costello’s train of thought, I actually managed to find something I could use. Costello you need an agent, I‘ve heard quite a lot of people talking along those lines. Always one to serve, I'm once again expanding my business. I've been meaning to open a new branch, ever since I closed down my insurance office. And now, here for your sports agent needs, I hereby open my floating wrestling agent branch office. Yes, now you can have, negotiating your contracts, the same man who has made himself one of the biggest names in wrestling today. (Then he flashes that 'winning smile' of his. The kind that agents, car salesmen, lawyers, and the rest of the scum of the universe have surgically implanted when they leave college.) Spawn: But you aren't here to listen to me shill my talents as an agent, did you? No, you're here so I can tell me what losers my opponents are. I never disappoint on that front, and I am not about to. (He takes a sip of his coffee. Mmm. That's good coffee.) Spawn: You know...my first match in relation to the wrestling world was in the SMIT tournament back in the days of HCW, against Lightning. And I thought to myself at the time, 'this is it. They can't possibly get any dumber than this.' Even I'm surprised with how many times that statement has been proven false since then. And wouldn't you know it, I once again find myself in competition with someone who is mentally inferior, even in comparison to Zippy the Amazing Technicolor Retard. Of course that‘s Costello, but I'll also am not going to just defeat you but put you out of wrestling for good Costello. Spawn: Okay, Costello. As a general rule, I pride myself on knowing as much about my opponents as I possibly can. This case is an exception, however. I quickly realized that not only did I not care about Costello, but I could get away with not caring about Costello. I've seen many instances of someone getting utterly destroyed because they were unfamiliar with an opponent. But in most of those situations, they lost because they didn't recognize said opponent as an actual competitor. Such a claim cannot be made by Costello. He is not a competitor; he is a buffoon. Working on the assumption that he can form an entire sentence by himself, Costello is probably thinking right now, 'But how can you be so sure, Spawn? How do you know that you will not fall victim to my wrestling skill.' The answer, besides the obvious one--that is, 'you don't have any.' No the real reason is. See, the thing is... (He casts a long glance to either side of the room, making sure there's nobody else here. Satisfied, he leans closer to the camera. He's acting all sneaky and stuff.) Spawn: I really am in the mafia. No, really. (To accentuate, he grins widely and nods. He's probably lying.) (Probably.) Spawn: Oh, that was fun. Okay, Costello. When I am done with you, you will go back to the sandbox and play with your trucks. (Smirk. If facial expressions were text format devices, this one would be a paragraph break.) Spawn: Costello, I appreciate the sentiment--imitation is the sincerest form of blah blah blah--but I really wish you wouldn't do that. It's creepy in the Single White Female kind of way. Besides, it's really not very good to try and label yourself the way you have. Especially when there's no possible way you could live up to that label. If I am The Great 1 without the physical prowess or features, then you are Spawn without a clue. Dwell on that for a moment. At any rate, that desk should be here any moment now, so I suppose I should get going. But given the nature of my opponents this week--Let me just say one more thing. Costello you idiot you need to start reading between the lines. I never said you said you had beaten the has beens of the FAW. I said it. You seem to think because you‘ve beaten a jobber here and a jobber there that you deserve a spot in some hall of fame. Please Costello, and if I‘m not mistaken with your big lousy mouth was it not you who came out and told me how hard-core you were?--I leave you with this bit of philosophy. Life is like a circus. And you're all a bunch of fucking clowns. SFX: RING RING. (That's a phone. It's a phone in Spawn's suit pocket. Yes, of course he's wearing a suit.) SFX: RING RING. (Spawn plucks the phone out of said pocket and pushes the little button.) Spawn: Spawn here. (The voice on the other end might be oddly familiar to some of you, but not many. We won't spend time describing it.) VOICE: I'm very disappointed in you. (Just know that Spawn's face turns very pale when he hears it. Immediately, he slams the door, which has already been fitted with the nameplate 'Spawn.') [Fade to black.]
