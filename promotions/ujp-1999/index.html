A puroresu-styled promotion that debuted its own television broadcast from Korakuen Hall with an All-Asian title tournament. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Ultra Japan Pro staged its debut broadcast, “Fighting Spirits,” on 14 August 1999, live from Korakuen Hall and presented in the style of Japanese puroresu television, right down to its own announcer, “The Great” Baba Matsouko, opening the show in Japanese-accented English. On air, UJP's own president, Akira Musashi, announced that the hired co-announcer had quit and gone home to America, then introduced a surprise replacement: a masked wrestler called the Iron Tiger, who used the moment to unmask on camera and reveal himself as Hakiro Sakazaki.

The card itself opened an All-Asian Title Grand Prix tournament. Its results, carried in a separate results-only recap of the same date, show Kenji Mizukawa defeating Demon Shintai, “Vainglorious” Vincent Sterling submitting Dan “The Snake” Jacobs (who came out of the match with a dislocated shoulder), “Baby Bull” Marcus Irons defeating “The Future” J.B. Destiny in what turned out to be Destiny's last match for the promotion, and Hybrid beating “The Phoenix” Akira Shinashi in the main event. UJP's own broadcast credits the night with a 1.8 rating and a sellout crowd of 2,150.

The last surviving document previews a third broadcast, from the Osaka Dome. Whether that show, or the rest of the All-Asian tournament, was ever staged isn't in the surviving record.
