Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

United Japan Pro Wrestling introduced itself on 23 May 2002 as a new fantasy league “based in a fantasy Japan, emulating the style of All Japan, New Japan, NOAH,” planned as a King’s Road promotion of Heavyweight and Junior Heavyweight divisions that would work “without so much of the sports entertainment aspect of the business.” For its first months it signed only Japanese characters, closed to what its own copy called “gaijin.”

Its first signings, announced the next day, drew on the puroresu circuit’s own recent history: former Neo Japan star and one-time Japan Puroresu Association owner Genji Yamato, who had reactivated and then held a set of unified titles he called the Double Titles before that promotion’s own closure; Tetsuo Matsumoto, wrestling also under the gimmick name Super Tengu; and junior heavyweight Tasogare no Prince, billed across Super Japan Pro Wrestling, Neo Japan, Japan Puroresu Association and the American UWF.

Two more waves of signings followed. On 27 May it added trainer Iron Chef Keiji Kobe as liaison to its front office alongside juniors Jin’ei Tamago and Dragon Mitsu and four heavyweights, including Ryouga Kenzan and Akira Tanabe; by 1 June its roster stood at sixteen, with new juniors Yoshito Katsumura, Kazuya Asakino, Winger Shizumi and Mitsuya Tenzaki and heavyweight Jinshi Togawa, and it was calling for “native Japanese heavyweights” to round out a debut card it had already named, Infinity 2002 Gong.

No record of that debut card, or of anything after it, survives.
