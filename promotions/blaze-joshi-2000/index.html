Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

BLAZE traces its own founding to a meeting between the wrestler Akira Kahn and New Rising Japan chief executive Takashi Koshinaka on 5 October 1999, called, in the federation's own account, to address “the evaporating state of women's wrestling in Japan” and to preserve joshi puroresu “for the benefit of future generations.” It billed itself as “heavily booked” rather than freely roleplayed, deciding matches on a formula of promos, ongoing angles, and written strategy, under the motto “Progression Beyond All Standards.”

Its first show ran at Korakuen Hall on 10 December 1999, opening its own 3WU championship tournament, and “Beauty Star” Angel Saito became its first Triple Crown Women's Universal Champion nine days later at Madison Square Garden, defeating “Tornado” Tara Smith in the tournament final. The title passed through Heidi Leick, Li'l Cheetah, Tara Smith again, and Dalbello Rage before Madison Stone won it on 11 August 2002, the last title change on the record.

On 8 January 2001 BLAZE merged with Izumimax Productions, a licensing and distribution company carrying rights to programming for the Fuji Television Network. A year later its own newsdesk framed Akira Kahn's in-ring retirement, after eight years, as a story in its own right — a final match against Angel Saito at “Massacre In Shibuya” and a farewell statement crediting her career with everything “from becoming world champion in both Japan and the United States” to training students of her own at a dojo the promotion called Hellfire.

That same card had been delayed nearly five months, and the promotion's own operator apologized for the gap at length, citing the aftermath of September 2001 and events in his own life; the promotion's original website did not survive the interruption, and it relaunched from a Yahoo Group thereafter.

In May 2002 it began sharing wrestlers with the Japan Independent Women's Wrestling Association, each promotion's own roster eligible for the other's cards while the two stayed separate federations.

Its own last surviving document, from 15 August 2002, previews a further Silent Winds card and looks ahead to matches still to come. No closure notice survives beyond it.
