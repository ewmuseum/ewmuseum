A Usenet e-fed that opened a September 1997 launch weekend under a full staff masthead, stalled, then relaunched in October with an announcing team and a title tournament whose result the surviving record never reaches. Four wrestlers are named across four served pages. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Ultimate Internet Wrestling introduced itself in an application and rules post on 12 August 1997, billing itself as “the most competitive, most interactive and most fun league to participate in on the internet EVER.” Its roster ran on invention rather than real names — one original wrestler per handler, up to thirty singles and ten tag teams — with matches decided on the TNM6.2 simulator, under a full staff masthead of a president, a vice president, a talent manager, a webmaster and two credited card writers. It scheduled an intro show for 1 September and a three-night Championship Weekend battle royal, 5 to 7 September, to decide its World and North American titles.

An emergency announcement on 25 August reported a flood of applicants, a coming chat room and home page, and named its five-person announcing team. The same post carried an internal naming dispute: four wrestlers — Big Daddy Cool, Ticking Time Bomb, Smash and Warrior — were ordered to change gimmick names that echoed other federations' own stars or be released.

Its Championship Weekend did not go on as planned. A special report on 17 September called the following month a “re-building and re-set to go” stretch, moved the battle royal and a first round of tag-title matches to a 1 October “Championship Coronation” at Madison Square Garden, and disclosed that five wrestlers had quit its original roster without notice.

It relaunched on 24 September with a full October card calendar — Thursday THUNDER and Saturday Night Showdown shows building toward a Madison Square Garden bill on 18 October carrying the World Tag Team and North American title finals. No result of that schedule, or of the promised 1 October World Title battle royal, is in its surviving record; the relaunch announcement is the last surviving document of the promotion.
