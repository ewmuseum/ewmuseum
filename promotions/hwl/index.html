Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The HWL opened in January 1996, TNM-simulated with roleplay layered on top, billing itself “the only league run by an emperor, not a commissioner.” Its own fed-head published an essay on the job the following February, signed simply “The Emperor”: “I think everyone needs to be treated as an adult... It's not my job, nor is it my responsibility, to babysit, to censor or to make all the decisions.”

Its title picture grew from five championships at founding to four singles, two tag, and one six-man tag title by the summer of 1997, run on two weekly cards and regular pay-per-views; its own recruitment copy called it “the best-kept secret in cyberwrestling” and “family-friendly, well-written.”

A card from Madison Square Garden that August shows its own comic register: a debuting duo calling themselves 2 Tough to Boogie won their first match outright, Large Lori sat on two smaller opponents in a row to injure their ribs and win by default, and the World Tag Team title match ended in a disqualification after a manager's cane found its way into the ring — the champions kept their belts regardless.

That same card's own scheduled six-man tag dissolved into a mass brawl up the aisle before it could start, and its main event closed the night on the World Title itself: two-time champion T Rex Ron had his own finisher, the Heart Punch, reversed back on him, and Jack the Ripper walked out the new champion.

By the following New Year its own pay-per-view and awards show crowned “The Ultimate Cool Dude” Log both Wrestler of the Year and, for the fourth time, World Champion, beating Johnny Hollywood; Sparklin' Stacey took Female Wrestler of the Year and Most Popular Wrestler, Coffee Cake captured the World Tag Team titles from House of Style, and the Texas Rangers and Carolina Crusher each retained their own titles.

A successor federation later reported the promotion closed in November 1997. No closure statement from its own hand survives, and how its final months played out is not in the surviving record.
