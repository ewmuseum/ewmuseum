Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

USPW: 65 and older get in for free is one author's comic diary of United States Pro Wrestling, a CornellVerse company whose roster is mostly old men and whose headquarters used to be a retirement castle, kept in a Total Extreme Wrestling game from January 2007. Its narrator, Jay, buys the head booker's job at an auction for one dollar, is told by the owner, Sam Strong, that he signed a binding contract, and spends his first months interviewing absurd replacements so that he can be fired.

Each show is a card of segments and matches with a letter grade for every one, a Final Rating, a quoted "Alzheimer's Post Show Recap" and the narrator's asides, with first-person scenes at the office between them and readers' replies quoted in the thread. The author says in a note that USPW is "a company based on entertainment, humor and being family friendly", and that he will not turn it into a pure wrestling promotion.

The first ten shows, January to March 2007, clear out the oldest of the roster for a New Talent Initiative, with Jacob Stranton and Umaga squashing what is left. Freddie Datsun takes the World Championship from Bruce the Giant at Stars, Stripes and Slams and is stretchered out on the next show; Citizen X takes the National Championship from Jim Force with a golf club; the tag belts pass from The Towers of Power to The Dudez!!!1! and then to the Canadian Crusties. Peter Valentine hates Darryl Devine for no reason and a muffin, the network cancels the show and a midnight slot is found on another channel.

The diary's first ten shows are served here whole, as five pages (three months and two special shows), with the roster, titles and reigns they establish. The saved copy, 921 pages in all, breaks off in the middle of a sentence in an epilogue chapter; the rest is collected and not yet served.
