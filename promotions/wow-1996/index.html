A Usenet federation reorganized in May 1996 out of a folded predecessor's own front office, whose weekly Superstars broadcasts and two WrestleFest pay-per-views carried it into a three-fed ALLIANCE with AWI and FWA by that August. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

WOW's own debut broadcast, 31 May 1996, opens with an in-fiction history of the promotion it rose from: the United American Wrestling Federation, presided over by its own president under an owner, Ross Perot, until Perot “lost interest,” its television slot went with him, and its talent started drifting away. Its president talked Perot into signing over full control, fired the whole roster, and rehired the wrestlers he wanted — “and the WOW was born.” Its first show was a seventeen-man tournament narrowing the field to six finalists for the World and International Championships.

The promotion's own World Champion, managed by his brother Louie, won the title at its first WrestleFest that June, and by 23 August 1996 it had banded together with two peer feds, the AWI and the FWA, into a standing three-way ALLIANCE for “talent and profit sharing.” The stipulation that decided WOW's own representative to the Alliance's first champion was blunt: whoever lost that night's Television Title match would carry the promotion's own flag into the triangle match for it. Sonic Soldier won the belt with outside interference, leaving James Havok — hurt in the loss — as that representative.

WOW hosted the Alliance's own first tag team title at its own Devil's Night Dance supercard that October, its first Halloween special; its World Champion was still on top there, still managed by Louie, still fending off questions about where he'd been since Wrestlefest.

Its last surviving broadcast, a 28 December 1996 pre-show, previewed a second WrestleFest built around an Intercontinental Tag Team Tournament and a rematch pitting James Havok and Jerry Straite against Ogre and Nicholas Vorpal, billed as the promotion's own biggest match of its first year. No recap of that show, and no closure notice of its own, survives beyond this preview.
