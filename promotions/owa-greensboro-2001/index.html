Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Two full broadcasts survive of the Online Wrestling Alliance, staged out of a fictionalized Greensboro Coliseum in North Carolina and cast almost entirely from the real-world World Wrestling Federation's own contemporary roster: Deztruxion on 24 January 2001 and Summerheat two days later. Both open explaining a company already two months into a chaotic ownership fight — a founding owner, Andrew Summerz, had stepped down to wrestle under the ring name SummerZ; a second owner had sold the company on without naming the buyer; and Vince McMahon had arrived declaring himself one of at least two silent partners still unidentified.

Deztruxion put five of its own titles on the line. Rhino defended the Hardcore Championship against newcomer Cobra until a masked debutant, Cheeba, ran in under a 24/7 rule and stole the pin and the belt. The APA, Farooq and Bradshaw, took the Tag Team Championship from Edge and Christian. D-Generation X, now fielding Da Brick Ruffhouse John alongside Mr. Ass and HBK, lost the number-one contenders' spot to Andrew Summerz's own stable, the bWo. In the main event, a triple threat for the vacant World and United Championships, Emerald Fusion stole the United title off a distracted Stone Cold Steve Austin, and Vince McMahon's interference then cost Austin the World title to HHH. Austin broke his own vow not to touch McMahon, stunned him twice, and was fired on the air.

Summerheat opened with Shane McMahon, newly announced as co-owner, rehiring Austin with a contract clause guaranteeing him a title shot that night. Andrew Summerz beat Emerald Fusion for the United Championship; Jeff Jarrett won the vacant European Championship in a four-way with an assist from Kevin Nash, Scott Steiner and Hall; Cobra and a hooded partner beat DX in a dumpster match; and the Acolytes' tag title defense against the bWo ended in chaos when DX ran in. The main event turned again on an outside arrival: a rival promotion's own president, Mick Foley, stepped out to declare himself the OWA's third silent owner and turned HHH's title defense into a four-way, which Tha Kid won cold to leave the broadcast holding both the World and United Championships.

No result survives past that broadcast. A March 2001 recruitment post calls it “one of the oldest feds on the web,” claiming nearly three years online already; further posts that month announce a reopening and a listing as e-fed of the month on the FWLNet directory. Its last surviving document, in October 2001, still describes it as newly “back for a second time” and still recruiting a roster. Whether it ever aired the title picture it set up that January, or how the ownership fight it staged was ever settled, isn't in the surviving record.
