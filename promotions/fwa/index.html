A promotion known only from its own merger notice, absorbed into the WWA in the summer of 2000 across a five-title unification event whose own record survives on the WWA's own side of the merger. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Fresh Wrestling Alliance survives, in full, as one page: a merger notice, dated 15 May 2000, announcing that the FWA had folded into the World Wrestling Alliance and pointing readers to the WWA's own site. No roster, title history, or show survives in this holding's own capture — everything known of the FWA as a going concern is what the sister WWA wing's own served record preserves of it from the other side of the merger.

That record is a five-title history. Three weeks before the merger's own headline event, on a WWA card dated 25 June 2000, Buck Floyd won the FWA North American Championship from Kris Kasanova — the only FWA title change of any kind independently dated in the surviving record — and on the same card the FWA's own World Tag Team Champions, Allied Chaos (Kyle Niker and Connor Pearce), challenged the WWA's own champions, Random Acts of Violence, for a scripted unification; the WWA pair refused the loss the promotion's own authority figure, "The Don," had ordered, and won outright, leaving the FWA Tag Team Championship's own lineage ended in a defeat rather than a transfer. At Independence Day Rumble, 2 July 2000, Jim Black, arriving from the FWA, defeated WWA World Heavyweight Champion Nik Segier to unify the FWA World Championship into the WWA's own belt; on the same card Buck Floyd defended his new North American title by defeating WWA United States Champion Shawn Cage to unify that lineage into the WWA United States Championship, and Lyle Wile defeated the defending FWA Television Champion, Chad Goodman, to unify the FWA Television Championship into the WWA's own. Three and a half weeks later, on 28 July 2000, Shaman defeated the defending FWA Hardcore Champion, The Dehibilitator, unifying the FWA Hardcore Championship into the WWA Extreme Championship. No earlier champions, challengers, or shows survive for any of the five belts beyond what is recorded here.

One further wrestler is attested in the FWA's own ranks without ever appearing in its own capture: Eddie Van Dorn, later a multiple-time WWA champion in his own right, is credited on his own later WWA bio page as protege to Buck Floyd during the FWA run, briefly splitting from him in an on-screen feud that page describes as a trick to bring down a faction from the inside.

Nothing else of the FWA's own run survives. Its own home page names two contributors only by handle — "The Don" as its president, "Web Man" for its own site — and nothing about who built or ran it beyond that.

Storylines

The arc below is recovered from the WWA's own served record of the merger — the only place any of it survives.

The Don's Unification Written by AI, 2026

Every FWA title changes hands into the WWA across five weeks, and the FWA's own president, "The Don," gets his way three times out of five. On 25 June 2000 he books Buck Floyd to beat Kris Kasanova for the North American title, then orders the WWA's own reigning World Tag Team Champions, Maim and Torture, to drop the belts to Allied Chaos and unify the lineages — the pair refuse on-page, beat the Don's own chosen champions clean, and walk out holding both sets of belts anyway. A week later, at Independence Day Rumble, the script holds: Jim Black, Buck Floyd, and Lyle Wile all go over as booked, unifying the World, North American, and Television titles into the WWA's own without incident. The last title stands apart. On 28 July 2000, defending FWA Hardcore Champion The Dehibilitator and WWA Extreme Champion Shaman fight to a mutual knockout on a simultaneous double chair shot — and rather than call it a draw, the referee physically drapes Shaman's arm over Dehibilitator and counts the pin himself, moments after the Don and Jim Black arrive at ringside. The served commentary calls the referee "obviously for the WWA" and does not resolve whether the Don's own arrival caused the finish or merely coincided with it; Dehibilitator confronts both men afterward, and the Don is described as visibly angered by the outcome himself. The FWA's own unification ends, five for five, on a title its own president is never shown collecting the credit for.
