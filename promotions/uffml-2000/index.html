A March 2000 minor-league branch of the Underground Fighting Federation, built to season newcomers before they move up to its parent roster. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Underground Fighting Federation's Minor-League describes its own start precisely: “The UFFML was fromed on the 10th of March and was officially opened on the 12th of March” in 2000. Its own handbook frames the whole enterprise as a feeder league for its parent, the Underground Fighting Federation (UFF) — a place “where newcomers to the e-fed scene can prepare themselves for the 'harsh-reality' of big-time e-feds,” with results run through a Zeus Pro Simulator rather than decided purely by roleplay quality, winners not pre-determined even in an angle, and roleplays as short as two lines accepted since “the results are simmed.” Wrestlers who performed well enough were promised promotion to the senior UFF roster.

Its title picture ran to seven belts: the Ultimate Heavyweight Title (win it twice and “u'll be promoted to the UFF”), the tag-team Gang-Fight Titles fought under Four-Corner Elimination rules, a United States Title fought under Ladder Match rules, a Television Title under Dumpster Match rules, a Mad-Man hardcore title under Hell-House Match rules, a Lightweight Title for wrestlers under 230 pounds, and a 3-Men-Gang trio title. Two weekly shows carried the league — Wednesday Night Storm, billed as “like the NITRO of the UFFML,” and Sunday Night Hell.

At its own opening the roster stood at three jobbers and “5 bona-fide fighters — Jack, Boy-Z, Jo, Zo & Bo,” with its president pitching the whole venture in one line: “COME ON, IT'S JUST FOR FUN!!”

No document later than its own opening week survives. Whether the roster grew beyond its founding five, whether any of its seven titles were ever contested, and how or when the league closed are not known.
