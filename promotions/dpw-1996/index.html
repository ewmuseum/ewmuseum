A single-operator 1996 e-mail e-fed that inherited the DPW name from a CVWF/EWA2 merger, ran a five-title structure and a full weekly card slate through a 'Motor City Madness' PPV before its own community later receipted a 1997 closure, merger into PCW, an abandoned revival, and a further short-lived Minneapolis-era return. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Dawg Pound of Wrestling was announced on 23 March 1996 as a merger of the wreckage of two other leagues: the CVWF, whose own operator could no longer keep up with simulating cards, and EWA2, whose head had dropped out with medical problems. Rather than keep either dying name, the organizer secured permission from the DPW name's original owner — who had since rebranded his own league ICW — to revive it, announcing “the New Dawg Pound of Wrestling Promotions... the accumulation of the original DPW, CVWF, and EWA2” around four titles: a Heavyweight, a regional Central Valley Championship, a Skyweight title, and a Tag Team title.

It grew fast and shut its own doors to new members within two months, its operator reporting that of everyone who had stepped into the ring, “only 4 handlers left DPW.” By May 1996 the roster ran to dozens of singles wrestlers and more than a dozen tag teams — among them Boston Battalion, Hardcore Heaven, Made Out of Steel, the Crypt Killas, and Evolution X — running across three regular shows plus a news segment, Damage Control. A first Hall of Fame induction, published in that same roster sheet only a month after the promotion opened, credited Boston Battalion with carrying two championship runs and a rivalry with Made Out of Steel forward from the operator's earlier leagues.

Its title lineages moved often through 1996. The Heavyweight Championship changed hands five times between March and July — won first by Aegis in a 30-man Royal Rumble, then by King Curtis twice, Ebola Zaire once between, and “Sadistic” Steve Stone, before Steven “The Edge” Spector took it in an eight-man tournament final that July. The Central Valley title was vacated outright after its champion, Drac “The Raven” Dravin, was hospitalized and unable to defend it; his own promotion awarded the belt to his scheduled challenger by forfeit, and that challenger refused it, calling the win unearned. A Northeastern States Heritage Tag Team title, inherited from a defunct predecessor league, was revived under Dawg Pound of Wrestling that June.

Its first pay-per-view, “Motor City Madness” on 21 July 1996, settled several of those lineages at once: Hollywood won a vacant Network Championship in a sixteen-man Rage in the Cage, Hardcore Heaven took the World Tag Team title from Made Out of Steel, and Spector dethroned Steve Stone for the World Heavyweight Championship in the tournament's own final.

The promotion carried into 1997: “Devastator” Mick Morton beat Hollywood for the World Championship at a New Year's Bash card, drawing an immediate challenge from a wrestler billed as Deranged. By the operator's own later account it closed its doors that April, “after exactly 1 year of operation,” merging into another promotion, PCW, once no one could be found to take over running it. A revival pitch that May looking for new writers went nowhere, and the promotion returned once more that November from a new home in Minneapolis, Minnesota, after losing its previous arena in Yakima.

No document past that November 1997 return survives.
