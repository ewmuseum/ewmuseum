Fusion, 5 April 2002

5 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Mercury Fusion - 4.5.02. - Live From the Arco Arena in Sacramento, California A dim light flickers in a dark room, swinging back and forth as a hand fiddles with the bulb. After giving it a few screws, the light suddenly shines forth with great luminescence as "It Ain’t Like That" by Alice In Chains plays in the background. The hand comes into view again, tinkering with the bulb before it explodes in his hand, morphing the shot into a video package for Mercury Fusion! Pariah celebrates with his world title (There I was, laid out on the table) Geoffrey Slate tosses Magnum Wolf off a cage at Silent Nightmare (Screamin’ Sweat and bare feet to the floor) Harbinger stretches out Pariah (In my life, I’d not soften) The Trents raise the tag titles high above their heads (Things that cut, and burn so often) Magnum Wolf pummels on Rush (But I sit, think of somethin’) Robert Young tosses Gol around (Scared to face, the dyin’ nothin’) Gol superkicks Pariah at Silent Nightmare (See the cycle I’ve waited for) And Oblivion poses in the ring (It ain’t like that anymore) Blaze McCoy punishes Memosarks with a ladder (Where I go is when I feel I’m able) Glamour takes it to Mikel Trent (How I fight is why I’m feelin’ sore) Memosarks frolics about ringside with a wicked grin on his face (In my mind, not forgotten) Pariah spits out a tooth at Nightmare (Feel as though a tooth were rotten) Dave Beaumont sits on Edward Olmstead (Behind the smile a tongue that’s slippin) Picasso works over Josh Trent (Buzzards cry, when flesh is rippin’) Harbinger dreadlocks Blaze McCoy into submission (See the cycle I’ve waited for) Magnum Wolf tosses Slate into the cage (It ain’t like that anymore) Jin Akujin is piledriven from the top rope by Picasso (Here I sit writing on the paper) Pariah Nausea’s Apex (Trying to make the words you can’t ignore) Blaze McCoy delivers the Hook, Line, and Sinker! ( In my eyes, what I’m lacking) Dirge and Brimstone create havoc in a promo (Score at face, a ten for slacking) Gol trash talks in a promo (Sign the deal, set in motion) Slate hugs the North American Title (Smaller fish, so huge the ocean) Harbinger makes Slate feel his wrath (See the cycle I’ve waited for) The Trents deliver some brotherly love (It ain’t like that anymore) Magnum Wolf and Slate trade blows in a cage (See the cycle I’ve waited for) And Pariah drops Gol with a Nausea as the scene crumbles (It ain’t like that anymore) As red and silver pyrotechnics burst throughout the arena, "It Ain’t Like That Anymore" by Alice in Chains plays. As soon as the residue from the pyro clears, the entrance stage becomes more visible. Two large video screens in the shape of an octagon hang on both sides of the entrance tunnel, with swirling lights on all sides of the screens. In the middle, directly above the tunnel hangs a circular video screen that changes from the Fusion logo to the Mercury logo every other 10 seconds. And below that is the entrance tunnel, a long, round tunnel enclosed by Plexiglas that stretches out to where the fan barricades begin. And for a change, there is no ramp to this stage! The Mercury camera crew makes their scan of the fans, prompting the sings to jump out and high in the air. They range this week from "Nachos here are expensive" to "Dirge 3:06" and finally "I paid 6 bucks to throw this soda at Blaze!" Puddle of Mudd’s "Blurry" strikes up over the PA, heralding the arrival of KC Steele and "The Iron Man" John Hughes! The two men bicker down the ramp before taking a seat at ringside, and placing their headsets on. KC Steele: Welcome to another great edition of Mercury Fusion! Tonight the World Heavyweight Title is on the line as Pariah takes on Jin Akujin, AND the main event spot against Pariah for Suicide is on the line as The Trents and Magnum Wolf take on Picasso, Slate, and Harbinger in a 6 man elimination match! "Iron" John Hughes: Plus Gol defends his North American title against Dirge! Suddenly, Metallica’s "Struggle Within" hits the arena speakers, and out comes David Beaumont, stern look on his face as he enters the ring. Dave: I realize that it's a bit trite for me to be coming out here, yelling at the Oblivion guys, but that's exactly what needs to happen. Last week, I asked for the identity of the person responsible for the group's creation, and they refused to name names. So I figure, why not make wild accusations and let the media work it out? After all, it's worked in Washington for the last couple centuries. I have made a list of people whom I consider to be logical suspects. He pulls said list from his right pants pocket. Dave: First off, Inferno. He's the only guy in Mercury who hasn't either been attacked by them or joined them. Might sound farfetched, but hey, the Trents weren't exactly prime suspects either. Next, from Evo, Rook Black, Duel, Edward Olmstead and Gravity. Their hatred for the right way of doing things is well founded, and I wouldn't put something like this past any of them. Finally, former LCW champion Blayd, who was all about gaping voids and amorality. Out of nowhere, "I Stand Alone" by Godsmack plays, and Geoffrey Slate walks to the ring with a mic clenched firmly in hand. Slate: You list all those guys because you think they fit the profile? I say, it could be ANYONE, even guys they roughed up a little. Tonight is the perfect night for someone nobody suspects to crawl out of the woodwork. Someone like, oh, Harbinger. Dave: Now wait just a damn second! Slate: We both know that he's capable of a lot, and it would all be designed to screw with Pariah. So, I'll tell you what I'm going to do with the situation. Rather than just sit back and throw names around until the Leader gets revealed, I'm going to stay on the sidelines of the match until Harbinger and Picasso are gone. It might not be neighborly, but at least I won't wind up screwed like I did last week! "Courage" by Alien Ant Farm blasts the crowd, and Magnum Wolf decides to make his presence felt as well. He also comes equipped with a microphone. Magnum: As much as I love listening to two paranoid losers bitch at each other, I'm afraid that I have to make a request of the good Mr. Beaumont. See, everyone's looking at Oblivion this week expecting run-ins. Well, I'm willing to vow that we won't run in during either of the title matches, and the main event, if you'll give me one thing in return. Make Pariah's match no disqualification. Dave: ...sure, I guess, but why do you care? Magnum: The way I figure it, everyone wins. Pariah gets to do what he wants, fans watch a more exciting match, Slate here gets to avoid straining his neck looking over his shoulder- Slate: No you DON'T, you manipulative son of a bitch! I'm not relaxing until I've got one of Mercury's singles titles, and none of your damned schemes are going to keep me from it! Magnum: Hey, fine by me. I'll just beat you the old fashioned way in the main event. Wolf takes a step towards Slate, who clenches his fists. Wolf chuckles and leaves the ring, followed after some distance by Slate. Beaumont stays in the ring a few seconds. Dave: Anyone else have a hard time following that? Well, enjoy the show everybody, and remember, it's never too early to order Suicide! "Struggle Within" hits, and Dave walks out. Steele: Well, without further procrastination, we better get to our first match, Inferno versus Memosarks! Match 1, Singles Match-Inferno vs Memosarks The arena begins to glow with a dim orange when suddenly flames shoot up on both sides of the entrance stage, and Disturbed’s "Stupify" blares over the pa. With red and orange lights swirling, Inferno stomps out of the entrance tunnel, his wet hair concealed under a doorag, and wearing a leather coat. Once in the ring, Inferno raises his arms high above his head as flames up from two of the ringposts, and he is ready for action. Steele: Well, Inferno hasn’t seen much sucess with Blaze... Hughes: He hasn’t seen any success. Steele: Well, you are right, as he has won zero matches in the tag division. But Inferno had a strong singles career in LTD, and it just might turn into one here in Mercury. Confetti starts to fall from the rafters over the entranceway and Mr. Bungle's "Golem II The Bionic Vapor Boy" fills the arena, signaling the arrival of Memosarks, who appears in the entrance tunnel, a big grin on his face. He slowly walks to the ring, smiling at all the cameras as he goes, and stopping at one to give it a big smile and a wave. Hughes: What a goofy looking son of a... Steele: Watch it! The match begins, Inferno first to strike, nailing Memosarks with hard rights before whipping Memo into the ropes, and going for a clothesline. But Memo ducks, and when Inferno turns around, Memosarks hits him with a picture perfect dropkick! Memosarks goes for a pin right out of the gates, getting 1, kick out by Inferno! Steele: Memosarks looking to put away the massive Inferno early. Inferno is up before Memosarks though, and simply decimates Memo with a thunderous lariat! Inferno picks up Memosarks, and Irish whips him. On the rebound, Inferno picks up Memosarks into a gorilla press slam position, bench presses the former North American champion a few times, and drops Memo down to the mat, face first! Hughes: I never knew Inferno harbored such strength! Steele: I have the feeling his true colors will come out in singles matches. Memosarks reeling, Inferno picks him up, and tosses Memo back into the corner. Inferno backs up, and charges in with a nasty knee to the midsection of Memosarks before backing up, and charging in with a clothesline! Inferno then just goes to town, hauling off with rights, lefts, and even a few elbows! Inferno backs away, and Memosarks staggers out before Inferno goes for a waistlock from behind. But Memo breaks the grasp, and is able to pull out an ace crusher from nowhere! The two men lay on the mat, winded, gasping for air as Smirnow begins a ten count. Hughes: Well that came out of nowhere. Steele: Both men are physically taxing each other fast and early in this match....hey, wait, who is that in the entrance way? With Inferno complaining to Smirnow about his knee, Blaze McCoy sprints from the back with a fire extinguisher in hand! With Memosarks now standing up, behind Smirnow, Blaze hops on the apron, pulls the pin, and extinguishes Memosarks! Blaze hops off the apron, and hides under the ring as Inferno hops up, and then chokeslams Memosarks for 1, 2, 3! Winner via Pinfall: Inferno Hughes: I’ll be damned. Steele: Well, Inferno got a win. I’LL be damned. As Smirnow raises Inferno’s arm, Blaze jumps up from under the ring, and embraces Inferno as the two men walk to the back, exchanging fist bumps and high fives. Steele: You have to give it to those two. No matter how mentally challenged, or physically challenged, they manage to find a way to pull the wool over our eyes. Backstage We cut backstage, where we see Blaze talking to Inferno, who sits on an anvil case. Blaze: That was beautiful! The boy*z* have to stick together man, and we proved tonight we are winners, we are a success! Inferno: We still have your match, B. Blaze: Oh come on! If I can’t handle that fat fool Flabbo Young, I’m just a total loser! Plus, you know, you’ve got my back, right? Inferno: Of course! Blaze: All right! High five! The two men exchange high fives, and we go back to KC and John at the announce desk, where John his rubbing his eyes, and KC is shaking his head. Steele: I’ll never understand how two.. Hughes: DON’T even try, Steele. Don’t even try. Is it illegal to fire mentally handicapped people? Steele: Well, after his stint out here, I understand Blaze is up next! Match 2, Singles Match-Blaze McCoy vs Robert Young The arena goes dark and the letters OutKast are shown one by one on the screen. Blaze McCoy is shown close up in slow motion from the soles of his boots to the spiked blonde hair on his head. Golden pyro explodes on both sides of the stage, and "Outkast" by POD begins to blast throughout the arena. Blaze McCoy stands at the end of the entrance tunnel, ready for the matchup. Blaze ignores the crowd's boos as they head to the ring. Blaze hops over the top rope and heads for the nearest turnbuckle, steps up to the top rope and stares at the crowd as he slowly takes off his sunglasses and gives the "thumbs up" with a smile, and then the "thumbs down" with a frown and another blast of flames are shot out of the other three ring posts. Blaze hops down off the turnbuckle, ready for action. Steele: Blaze, who’s struggled to make a name for himself in the tag division with his partner Inferno, is looking for a singles win here tonight. Hughes: Remember when he spelled his name with an ‘I’? And he had all his fat friends to help him? Those were the days… The arena lights dim to a soft glow, and golden sparks rain down from the rooftop as "Aerials" by System Of A Down strikes up, and Robert Young comes pounding out of the entrance tunnel. As blue and green lights pulsate throughout the arena, Young hops in the ring, and raises his arms high above his head. Steele: You talk about a guy experiencing a career revival. What improvement Young has shown! Hughes: Remember when he had the Sex Machine with him? And let’s not forget Carlos Rodriguez! Steele: I apologize, but it appears that John is on what we’ll call a ‘nostalgia trip’. The two men circle each other, but quickly stop when they realize that there’s no ref in the ring. As they look around bewildered, Right Said Fred’s "Too Sexy" blares over the PA system, and the crowd goes bezerk! Hughes: And to complete the early 2001 feel, it’s YISH~~~~! Steele: Good Lord. Yish Kbish runs as fast as he can (which ain’t all that fast) down to the ring, wearing a ref shirt that appears to be about 7 sizes too small for him. He climbs the ring apron, rolls himself into the ring and signals for the bell to be rung. Both competitors chuckle in spite of themselves, but as soon as the bell rings, they charge each other and lock up in the center of the ring! Steele: It’s on! Hughes: All this night needs is Kid Krazy, and it might be the greatest show ever. Steele: I’m not even going to comment on that. Blaze gains the early advantage, managing an arm-wringer on Young. However, Robert shows surprising agility as he flips out of the hold, untwisting his arm and allowing him to twist Blaze’s arm! He yanks up hard on Blaze’s arm and pulls down, slamming McCoy face-first to the mat. Blaze recovers quickly, and ducks a Young clothesline. As Young turns around, he charges again, but this time goes right into a spinebuster from McCoy! Blaze covers, and Yish verrrrrrrrry slowly slides over two count. As such, Young kicks out at one-eighth. Hughes: Heh. He’s so slow. Blaze is not happy about the count, and lets Yish know of his displeasure before stomping away at Young as the Aussie struggles to get up. Young scrambles to the ropes and hauls himself up using them, but gets sat right back down with a DDT from McCoy! McCoy covers again, and again Yish is slow getting down to make the cover. This time, Young kicks out at one-sixteenth. Blaze glares at Yish, who throws his hands up as if to say ‘Forget it’, and remains on the ground. Steele: Blaze on the warpath here, but remember, Young’s resilient. Case in point being last week’s show, when he took World Champion Pariah to the wire. Again, Young struggles to get up, but when he does, he blocks a big right hand from McCoy, and fires away with several rights of his own! He grabs Blaze’s hand and whips him towards the ropes. Blaze ducks the Young clothesline, and turns around, ready to land with one of his own. He hadn’t counted on Young being ready to grab him in a bearhug and launch him overhead with a belly-to-belly suplex! Hughes: What a beast Young is. After last week, it’s a wonder he’s even walking. Steele: Huge impact move from Young there, and he’s going to look to capitalize on his downed opponent. Indeed Young does, dropping an elbow on McCoy’s sternum. He drops another one as well before picking Blaze up, applying a headlock and nailing a vertical suplex! He floats over for the cover, as Yish, who has remained on the ground since the last count, is ready this time. He counts a one, and two, but not a three, as Blaze powers out. Steele: Yish a little better prepared to count a fall that time. Hughes: I don’t want to detract from the action, but that man is so fat that it’s sad. Young helps McCoy up by grabbing his hair and pulling up as hard as he can. He whips Blaze to the ropes, and this time connects with a vicious lariat! However, instead of going for a cover, he springs off the ropes, looking to drop a leg. Young takes off, but when he lands, there’s nobody home. Blaze rolls out of the way. Young gets up, looking for McCoy, who is behind him. McCoy grabs Young, whirls him around, applies a headlock, and hits a fisherman’s suplex! Yish counts, 1… 2… no! Young throws up his shoulder. Steele: Resiliency, stubbornness, tenacious. All of those are words you could use to describe Robert Young. Hughes: Just don’t call him a hoss, KC. Blaze picks up Young in a headlock, and just as quickly as Young finds his feet, he’s back down again, courtesy the Blaze of Glory headlock drop! Again, Blaze covers. Again, Yish counts. This time, however… Young kicks out at 2 and a half. Hughes: That’s gotta be frustrating. Steele: The only way to beat Young is to wear him down. Blaze hasn’t done that yet. Blaze again picks Robert up, and whips him into the ropes. On the way back, Blaze grabs Robert in a chokehold, and lifts him up into the air! But Robert kicks at Blaze’s midsection until Blaze finally is forced to drop him. When he does, Young picks him up and tosses him with a uranage suplex! Hughes: Wow! Steele: That move is almost impossible to complete when the opponent has no momentum! What a display of strength by Young! Hughes: And he… uh-oh. We got a run in. The camera pans to reveal Inferno, chair in hand, sprinting toward the ring, where both Young and McCoy lay. Both men are attempting to get up, and Inferno goes over to Blaze’s side and hands him the chair. Then, he hops up on the apron, which causes Yish to roll his way over to him and block him from entering the ring! Steele: Blaze has a chair, and an easy target! Young, groggy, helps himself up from the ropes. He turns around to see Blaze charging hard, chair in hand. Blaze takes a home-run swing… Hughes: I can’t look! And whiffs! Young ducks! Blaze tries to regroup, but Young kicks him in the chest, causing Blaze to lose the chair right into the hands of Robert Young! Steele: The tide has turned! With one swing, it could be all over! Young brings the chair down across McCoy’s back hard, causing the crowd to erupt and causing Blaze to keel over. Young drops the chair, and applies a standing headscissors. He hooks both arms, and sits out, connecting with his new finisher, the Pure Annhilation sit out Tiger Driver '91! Hughes: Since when did he start channeling Dean W… Steele: Shhh! Young’s gonna do it! Young flops on top for the cover, and Yish worms his way over to count. 1… 2… Inferno tries to pull Yish out but can’t because he’s too fat… 3! Winner via pinfall: Robert Young Steele: And miscommunication leads to the downfall. *sigh* As Young walks to the back, head held high, Inferno rolls in the ring, and trys comforting Blaze. But Blaze pushes Inferno aside, and walks to the back, alone. Hughes: Do I sense a..tiff? Steele: One can only pray so. Hughes: Ready for the North American Title match? Steele: I was born ready, much like Dirge, whom I think is going to step up and take it tonight. Hughes: We’ll just see, we’ll just see. Match 3, Singles Match for the North American Title-Gol (c) vs Dirge Steele: Gol won the North American title last week from Geoffrey Slate after a bit of help from the Oblivion, lets hope his reign doesn't last too long and Dirge takes the strap from him tonight. Hughes: Isn't Dirge still that Warrior guy? Steele: You really don't keep up with LCW TV, do you John? Hughes: We're on television? Hey mom! It's me, Johnny! 'Prick' by Something For Kate, plays, as the arena lights dim and smoke covers the entranceway. On the Mercutron, the letters G-O-L each flash onto the screen, in bright green, interspersed with rapid clips of Gol doing various things, such as parading with the National Title and walking to the ring. As the song hits the heavier riff, Gol walks out under the entranceway and into the aisle, looking up at the Mercutron, which now shows clips of him in action, delivering the Sydney Superkick to various LCW superstars past and present. Gol turns around as golden pyro fires down the aisle and to the ring, and he proceeds down to the ring, the North American title secured around his waist. He slides under the ropes and posing on all four corners with the belt to huge jeers and boos from the crowd. Steele: Not the most popular North American champion ever, is he? "Angels Holocaust" kicks in over the PA as clips of Dirge running all around the world (actually Dirge running against a blue screen) are shown on the MercuTrons. As multicolored lights pulsate in the arena, Dirge storms out from the entranceway, accompanied by two beefy men wearing black jackets with the words 'EEI Security' printed on the back, making it pretty obvious what they're here to do. Dirge gets into the ring after giving his security final orders, and this contest begins. Steele: And the challenger, who, like many wrestlers in LCW, has vowed to remove the Oblivion from LCW. This is his chance tonight to take out one of their most prominent members. He has a good chance now he's neutralized their outside interference, bringing his own security to take care of any possible Oblivion interference. Gol doesn't waste any time, locking up with Dirge, who kicks him in the gut for his troubles and clubs him with an elbow to the back of the head. Gol picks himself up only to be met with a stomp, and another, and another, until Dirge has forced him into the corner, where he stomps him a little more. Despite this display of technical prowess from Dirge, Gol gets to his feet again, smiling, and offers a test of strength. Dirge obliges with both the test and the attempted kick to the gut, but Gol knew it would be coming, and grabs his leg, swinging Dirge over in a legwhip that sends him crashing into the corner. Gol then takes his turn at stomping a mudhole in Dirge, before the referee breaks them up and Dirge gets to his feet. Steele: Gol looks to use his strength in this match, while Dirge should follow his skill and speed. Hughes: Or just be a cheating bastard. They enter a slugfest, which Gol gets the upper hand in, so Dirge rakes the eyes and puts Gol on the mat with a bodyslam. He drops an elbow into Gol's sternum, then another, and then locks on a reverse chinlock, a move which barely lasts five seconds as Gol gets to his feet and twists out of it, pushing Dirge into the ropes and flooring him with a flying clothesline as he returns. Dirge pops back to his feet, but Gol fires another clothesline his way and floors him again. This time Gol drops his knee into the forehead of Dirge, leaving the EEI member reeling. Gol helps him up then attempts to whip him into the corner, but Dirge reverses, and Gol runs into the turnbuckle chest first, and as he staggers backwards Dirge comes up from behind and hits the ten head slams into the turnbuckle of DOOM with the crowd chanting the whole way. Gol staggers out to the middle of the ring, then falls flat on his face, to the roar of the crowd. Dirge covers, but Gol kicks out after two. Steele: We wont be seeing a Savage/Steamboat contest here, this one looks more like it belongs in the car park. Dirge throws Gol into the corner, punching him along the way, then sets him up on the top rope. Dirge climbs up himself, hooking Gol's head and falling backwards, attempting a top rope DDT, but Gol hooks his legs and Dirge falls flat onto his back. To make matters worse, Gol gets to his feet and flies off the top rope himself, and the 270 pound Aussie lands viciously on Dirge's chest with an elbowdrop. Gol covers, but Dirge squeaks the shoulder out at two and a half. Hughes: Resilient little booger, isn’t he? Steele: He is determined more than almost anybody to win a big title. Dirge gets to his feet slowly, holding his chest. Gol lines him up for a huge roundhouse, knocking Dirge into the ropes, then another, which knocks him straight over the top rope and to the outside. Dirge lands on his feet, and his security step away, letting Dirge get his breath. Gol steps out onto the apron, but before he can do anything, Dirge comes to life and grabs him by the legs, then falls backwards, draping Gol straight over the barrier, head first. Gol falls backwards, out cold, and Dirge picks him up, throwing the big man into the ring after some effort. He covers, one, two, two and a half, and that's all he gets, as Gol weakly gets a shoulder up. Steele: Dirge taking the offensive, really putting it Gol here. Dirge takes Gol over with a hiptoss, then helps him up again and drops Gol over his knee in a backbreaker, holding the North American champion there and pressing down on his head and stomach, pressuring the back. Gol struggles, but Dirge has him in an uncompromising position, and he flails his arms, looking for the ropes, but only getting air. He soon gets an idea with these said arms, and forms a fist, punching away at Dirge's head, hitting a few weak blows at first, but eventually firing out with enough force to release the hold. Dirge gets to his feet and bounces off the ropes, dropping an elbow into Gol as he tries to get to his feet. He covers, but an elbow ain't going to put Gol away, and he kicks out quickly. Steele: As I said, this isn't going to get five stars on the Internet folks, this is an out and out brawl. Hughes: Internet? Are you allowed to mention that here? Steele: Too late now. Dirge kicks Gol in the gut as he gets to his feet, and hits a DDT. He covers, but only gets two once more. Dirge kicks away at Gol, until he's rolled out of the ring, where Dirge follows, again the security making room for them. Dirge slams Gol's head into the barrier, then the announce tables, then the ring steps, and finally the ring post, leaving Gol dizzy and stumbling around, before falling flat onto the announce table. Dirge considers it, but decides against anything high risk, simply pushing Gol off the table then throwing him back in the ring. He perches on the top rope, waiting for Gol to get to his feet, and after about twenty seconds, realizes he might not be getting to his feet, and leaps off for a splash, in which time Gol reveals he was suckering Dirge all along, rolling out of the way, then pouncing on a prone Dirge with a crossface chickenwing. Steele: What? A hold? Somebody stop the match! Gol rolls Dirge onto his back, hooking the legs and stopping any escape. Dirge struggles, and wriggles, and struggles some more, puts his hand down to tap, but resists the urge, and instead rolls his weight over and pushes up, freeing himself from the hold. Gol grabs Dirge from behind as he stands up, and drives his head into the mat with a bulldog. He smells blood, and drives Dirge into the corner, picking him up and placing him on the top rope, attempting a superplex of some sort. He gets in position, but Dirge counters with a revolutionary knee to the groin, which doubles Gol over, and Dirge pushes him off, leaping in the same motion, landing on Gol a second after he hits the mat himself. Dirge covers, but only gets a two. Both men get to their feet slowly, Dirge first, and he takes the advantage by punching Gol and hitting a vertical suplex, then slides over to cover, one, two, not three though, as Gol kicks out. Hughes: As much as Dirge wants a belt, Gol looks to retain and make another legacy for himself. Dirge helps him up, and whips Gol into the corner, Gol comes flying out so fast that Dirge whips him into the opposite corner, where he stays, and Dirge charges in for a splash. Unfortunately, all he gets is whiplash as Gol fires out with a weak Sydney Superkick out of nowhere, but crumbles to the mat himself. After a few seconds he gets up, seeing Dirge still out next to him, he signals to the crowd and yells at them, drawing a huge negative response, then he picks up Dirge and drops him again with a vicious powerbomb. Gol covers, one, two, thre....no, somehow Dirge kicks out, to the delight of the crowd! Steele: He may not be the Warrior anymore, but I think Dirge is hulking up John! Indeed, Dirge seems to get a sudden surge of energy, throwing Gol into the corner with a huge right, then whipping him into the ropes and hitting a big boot. Dirge decides against a legdrop, however, and instead picks up the North American champion and tries for a powerbomb of his own. Gol, however, sees it coming and pushes his weight forward, overbalancing Dirge and he falls backwards, Gol's weight landing in a sitting position on his chest. Gol kips up and waits for Dirge to get to his feet. Steele: Each man is touching his breaking point here, and...wait a second, who the hell is that? From nowhere Geoffrey Slate has hit the arena, and stands just outside of the ring, getting Gol’s attention. But this gives Dirge time to get up and let out a loud scream, getting Gol’s attention again. Gol, not sure what to do, goes for the Sydney Superkick. But Dirge ducks, and when Gol turns back around, Dirge snags him into a fisherman's suplex, hits it, bridges, and picks up 1, 2....Gol kicks out...after the three! Winner and NEW LCW Mercury North American Champion: Dirge Steele: Holy sh....Dirge has won the North American championship! Darkness reigns supreme! As Gol rolls out and collects himself, Dirge grabs the title and stands high on the second turnbuckle, fans eating it up big time. Hughes: If that just isn’t...wow.
