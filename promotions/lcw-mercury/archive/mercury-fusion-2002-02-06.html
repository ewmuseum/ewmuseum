LCW Mercury Card Archive

Fusion, 6 February 2002

6 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Mercury Fusion-2.6.02-Live From The Molson Centre in Montreal, Canada! A dim light flickers in a dark room, swinging back and forth as a hand fiddles with the bulb. After giving it a few screws, the light suddenly shines forth with great luminescence as "It Ain’t Like That" by Alice In Chains plays in the background. The hand comes into view again, tinkering with the bulb before it explodes in his hand, morphing the shot into a video package for Mercury Fusion! Harbinger celebrates with his world title (There I was, laid out on the table) Geoffrey Slate tosses Magnum Wolf off a cage at Silent Nightmare (Screamin’ Sweat and bare feet to the floor) Pariah lets out a scream with a crimson mask on (In my life, I’d not soften) The Trents raise the tag titles high above their heads (Things that cut, and burn so often) Magnum Wolf pummels on Rush (But I sit, think of somethin’) TR Saxton tosses Gravity around (Scared to face, the dyin’ nothin’) Gol superkicks Pariah at Silent Nightmare (See the cycle I’ve waited for) And EEI poses as a group in the ring (It ain’t like that anymore) Blaze McCoy punishes Memosarks with a ladder (Where I go is when I feel I’m able) Glamour takes it to Mikel Trent (How I fight is why I’m feelin’ sore) Memosarks frolics about ringside with a wicked grin on his face (In my mind, not forgotten) Pariah spits out a tooth at Nightmare (Feel as though a tooth were rotten) Dave Beaumont sits on Edward Olmstead (Behind the smile a tongue that’s slippin) Picasso works over Josh Trent (Buzzards cry, when flesh is rippin’) Harbinger dreadlocks Blaze McCoy into submission (See the cycle I’ve waited for) Magnum Wolf tosses Slate into the cage (It ain’t like that anymore) TR Saxton walks around, tangled in barbed wire (Here I sit writing on the paper) Pariah Nausea’s Rook Black (Trying to make the words you can’t ignore) Blaze McCoy delivers the Hook, Line, and Sinker! ( In my eyes, what I’m lacking) Dirge and Brimstone create havoc in a promo (Score at face, a ten for slacking) Gol trash talks in a promo (Sign the deal, set in motion) Slate hugs the North American Title (Smaller fish, so huge the ocean) Pariah kicks and curses at Security Guards (See the cycle I’ve waited for) The Trents deliver some brotherly love (It ain’t like that anymore) Magnum Wolf and Slate trade blows in a cage (See the cycle I’ve waited for) And Harbinger makes Gravity play taps before the scene crumbles (It ain’t like that anymore) As red and silver pyrotechnics burst throughout the arena, "It Ain’t Like That Anymore" by Alice in Chains plays. As soon as the residue from the pyro clears, the entrance stage becomes more visible. Two large video screens in the shape of an octagon hang on both sides of the entrance tunnel, with swirling lights on all sides of the screens. In the middle, directly above the tunnel hangs a circular video screen that changes from the Fusion logo to the Mercury logo every other 10 seconds. And below that is the entrance tunnel, a long, round tunnel enclosed by Plexiglas that stretches out to where the fan barricades begin. And for a change, there is no ramp to this stage! The Mercury camera crew makes their scan of the fans, prompting the sings to jump out and high in the air. They range this week from "Pariah got screwed!" to "Peace out Tim!" and finally "Why don’t they have Crystal Pepsi here?". Puddle of Mudd’s "Blurry" strikes up over the PA, heralding the arrival of KC Steele and "The Iron Man" John Hughes! The two men bicker down the ramp before taking a seat at ringside, and placing their headsets on. KC Steele: Fans, welcome to Mercury Fusion! We promise to deliver one hell of a show tonight as we have a killer Main Event! Tonight, Magnum Wolf, looking for revenge after last week, takes on Gol in a No DQ Grudge Match with special referee Pariah! *A picture of Magnum Wolf pops up on the screen with a red and silver background before turning into one of Gol with the same background, and then it becomes one split screen shot, with a smaller picture of Pariah in the middle of the two men and the word "NO DQ" supered on the bottom of the screen* "Iron" John Hughes: And tonight pure hardcore chaos meets total class and style when Harbinger takes on TR Saxton the World Title Match! *A picture of TR Saxton splashes onto the screen with a red and silver background before becoming one of Harbinger with the same backdrop before becoming one split shot with the words "World Title Match" supered on the bottom of the screen* Steele: Also tonight, The Trents take on Picasso and Glamour for the match to settle it all, and Slate defends his North American Title against 2 other men! Before either Steele or Hughes can get another word off, Metallica’s "Struggle Within" kicks in, and Dave Beaumont, along with Gol and Harbinger, makes his way down to ringside. Dave glows as he steps in the ring, and gets on the mic. Dave Beaumont: Last week was a good week. We laid out Pariah, again, and Magnum Wolf. But Magnum got a little upset, complained to Bruce DeAula, and got himself a no DQ match with Gol. But do you see Gol sweating? No. Do you see me sweating? No. I know Gol can take Magnum Wolf, and I know Gol will have no trouble disposing of him. What I am worried about though is Pariah. You see, Bruce took it upon himself to name Summers the guest referee, and we all know how crooked he is. Fans boo in outrage at Dave’s slanderous words. Dave: But that is ok. That is ok. Because as owner of this federation, I have the power to take Keith out of that main event, and into the North American Title Match, making it a 4 way. Have fun Keith. More boos rock the arena. Dave: But I’m not done yet, folks. I have more to do out here. You see, Harbinger and TR Saxton have a world title match, and Harby was complaining to me before the show that he wanted to stretch the hell out of somebody. So Harb, tonight, it’s you and TR in a Squeaky Clean I Quit match! Steele: What? Who in the hell does Beaumont think he is? On the fly booking crap... Hughes: I’m loving this. Dave: Well, that is about all, I can’t really make my day much better. Gol? Herb? Anything? As Gol is about to take the mic, Guns-n-Roses "Out Ta Get Me" blares up, and out comes Talent VP Bruce DeAula! He stands at the end of the entrance tunnel, mic in hand. Bruce DeAula: Dave, what kind of crap is this? Just when I thought it was safe to let you back on TV, you go and do this. You know what, I have no problem with Herb and TR in a Squeaky Clean I Quit Match, but I do have a problem with you yanking Pariah out of the main event. I guess what I’m saying is.. you either put Pariah back in the main event or I don’t sign my name on that contract for the world title match. And if my name isn’t on there, it’s not sanctioned, and it can’t happen. Bruce begins to takes steps to the ring until he reaches it, and gets right in Dave’s face. Bruce: You have only one choice Dave, so tell these people who will be reffing the Main Event. NOW! Dave: pariahwillbetheref.. Bruce: What was that? Dave: I said Pariah will be the ref! Before Dave can get another word off, "Nausea" by Machine Head starts up and Pariah marches down to the ring. He hops in and poses on the middle buckle with his arms folded over each other before hopping off and grabbing the microphone from Bruce. Pariah: The way I see it, I’m glad to be reffing this fine match. And I’m glad I get to be in the same ring as Gol. But...what am I going to next week? And the week after that. It’s clear I want to get at Gol, but there is a time and a place for that. And after Nightmare, Dave won’t let me within 5 feet of a title match with Harbinger... Pariah points at the world title belt of Harbinger while staring him dead in the eye, as Herb gives Keith a little smirk. Pariah: So what to do now, Dave? What tricky little thing are you going to throw at me next? A number one contenders match that is stacked against me? An I quit match with Frank Gotchs’ great grandson? What now Dave? What now! Dave promptly gets in Keith’s face, and gives him his answer. Dave: I have an idea. How about me and ol’ Bruce over here pick some number one contenders for two of Mercury's biggest belts? The World, and the North American! The fans roar in approval as Bruce snags his mic back from Pariah. Bruce: OK. For the World Title, I choose Pariah over here and... Before Bruce can get his second choice off, Dave interrupts. Dave: And Magnum Wolf! A heavy sign of approval is seen throughout the arena as Keith grins and Herb smiles along with Gol. Dave: And for the North American Title I pick Gol and Yis.. Bruce gets his turn to interrupts now, this time cutting Dave off in mid name. Bruce: And Memosarks! Gol keeps smiling, but not before Alien Ant Farm’s "Courage" blast in the arena everyone turns to the entrance way, looking for Magnum Wolf. Gol leans over the top rope, shoving Pariah aside. But everyone is looking the wrong way as Magnum sprints from the crowd, leaps over the guardrail and announce desk, and then slides in the ring where he tackles Gol and begins to pound away on him! Pariah spikes his mic and exits the ring as Bruce, Dave, and Harbinger attempt to separate the two men. As Dave and Harbinger restrain a near frothing Gol, Bruce practically hauls Magnum backstage as he points at Gol and lashes out at the Sydney native. Steele: Man, what a way to kick off the show! Next week Pariah and Magnum for a shot at the World Title and Gol against Memosarks for a chance to take on the North American champ! Hughes: Why did Bruce have to ruin the main event, AGAIN! Dirty DeAula. Steele: We better get to the show! Match 1, Singles Match-Blaze McCoy vs Rush Steele: Blaze is a mess of paranoia recently, accusing LCW Mercury management of a conspiracy against him. This stems from his recent inability to win the 'big one' as he calls it, as he has had trouble putting away title matches and getting a taste of Mercury gold. Hughes: This guy is just a crybaby. He chokes under pressure, simple as that. Mind you, he wont have much trouble here, Rush isn't looking the best recently, in fact, he seems rather quiet. Steele: That's true, maybe if McCoy gets some solid wins under his belt, he'll gain more confidence and drop this whole conspiracy theory stuff. Hughes: At least, that's what LCW management is hoping. Steele: Stop it Hughes, there is no conspiracy against Blaze McCoy. Hughes: Sure, that's what they tell you to say. We'll see, if anyone can uncover this, Blaze can. The arena goes dark and the letters OutKast are shown one by one on the screen. Blaze McCoy is shown close up in slow motion from the soles of his boots to the spiked blonde hair on his head. Golden pyro explodes at the center of the ramp and "Outkast" by POD begins to blast throughout the arena. Blaze McCoy steps out from behind the curtain. Blaze ignores the crowd's boos as they head to the ring. Blaze leaps over the ropes and heads for the nearest turnbuckle, steps up to the top rope and stares at the crowd as he slowly takes off his sunglasses and gives the "thumbs up" with a smile, and then the "thumbs down" with a frown and another blast of flames are shot out of the other three ring posts. Blaze hops down off the turnbuckle and tosses his shades into the crowd, ready for battle. "Wait and Bleed" by Slipknot fires up, and the arena begins to pulsate red and white lights, marking the entrance of Rush. He runs down the ramp and slides straight into the ring, only to be met with a barrage of stomps from an enraged McCoy. Rush eventually shields himself from the blows and the referee gets McCoy to back off, allowing Rush to get to his feet, but only for a second as Blaze charges in with a clothesline, knocking Rush to the outside. Rush stumbles around, holding himself up on the guardrail, not seeing McCoy leap from the apron in a double axhandle, knocking Rush to the floor again. McCoy stomps Rush a bit more, then picks him up and smashes him into the ring steps. The ref begins his count, but McCoy doesn't listen as he whips Rush into the ringpole, then charges in with a kneelift to the back, sending Rush onto the announcers table, which he meets up close as Blaze throws his head onto the wooden barrier, and then throws him into the ring. He covers, but Rush kicks out quickly before the two count. Steele: McCoy is vicious here, Rush is meant to be a loose friend of McCoy here, but Blaze is just taking him to the cleaners, this one might not last long. Blaze whips Rush into the ropes, but Rush reverses, and hits Blaze with a knee into the gut, then drops an elbow into the back of his head, putting him down to the canvas. Rush drops an elbow into McCoy's back, then another, and another. McCoy rolls to the ropes, and Rush attempts to stomp him out of the ring, but McCoy grabs a hold of his foot and trips him, then holds onto his leg, stands up and stomps away at the extended limb. McCoy then picks up Rush and punches him into the corner, and drives his shoulder into Rush's gut. With Rush doubled over, Blaze hits a quick DDT, then covers for a two count. Rush stumbles to his feet, and McCoy slides behind him, grabbing him in a full nelson, hoisting him up and dropping him down in a full nelson slam. Rush wriggles to the ropes to help himself up, but just as he gets to his feet, McCoy grabs on a headlock, then runs to the center of the ring and slams Rush with a bulldog. Rush rolls onto his back, leaving him open for a hard kneedrop to the face, then a quick hook of the leg gets another two count. Rush punches weakly at McCoy, who absorbs the blows and throws Rush towards the ropes. As Rush comes back, McCoy tilts his shoulder down, picks up Rush and nails him with a quick Samoan Drop, another quick hook of the leg, and another two count. Rush is in dreamland as he stands, and McCoy steps around him, throwing quick jabs at his head, before lining up a big roundhouse which decks Rush, leaving him lying in the center of the ring. McCoy quickly darts up to the top rope, perched waiting for Rush to stand, which he eventually does, only to be met with a huge flying clothesline which nearly decapitates Rush! McCoy stands and stares around at the crowd for a second, then picks Rush up by the face, lifts him for a vertical suplex, bounces him off the ropes and drops him hard in a jackhammer suplex, the Outkast. Blaze drops down in a lateral press, and gets the elementary three count, for an impressive victory. Winner after the Outkast: Blaze McCoy Steele: Well, that one was quick and easy for Blaze McCoy, he really looked possessed out there, not to mention ruthless. Hughes: I liked it, Blaze needed to do a job and he did it, he destroyed Rush, who really paid for lacking intensity and dedication. As Blaze raises his arms above his head in victory, the arena suddenly goes dark as one single spotlight focuses on the staging area before "Praise" by Sevendust hits the speakers and four men dressed in leather jackets wearing black ski masks make their way down to ringside. As Blaze begins to back away from the men, a fairly larger man in the same attire as the other four hops over the barricade and nails Blaze in the back of the head with a pipe! With Blaze in a heap on the mat, the five gather around Blaze and at the same time, reveal themselves to be LTD President Dr. Jacob Paxton, and The Inferno Kid, Bob Yobbo, Jr., Headbuster, and Scott Royal! Paxton gets a hold of the mic, and gets in Blaze’s face. Dr. Jacob Paxton: Blaze McCoy, you pathetic hack, there was no conspiracy. It was your own short comings. You are taking up space here in Mercury, and I have four boys here who want to kick your ass and take your spot! So next week, me and Dave Beaumont agreed that there will be a special Battle Royal. My four boys, Royal, Headbuster, Yobbo, and Inferno against you. You and one man start it off, and every minute one other person comes out. And whoever is left standing after you get dumped out is in Mercury! And you...you just get to go back to being mediocre! Suddenly, Dave Beaumont’s face comes on the video screens. Dave Beaumont: You are forgetting something, Jacob. If Blaze happens to outlast those four, he gets a world title shot in the near future. Thank you, that is all. Steele: Well that was odd... Hughes: So now those LTD kids get a chance at the big-time? This should be fun. Blaze is going to go ape on them. Steele: Or so you think. 3 of the 4 in that match have had LCW experience, and probably want more. And you have to think Headbuster wants in as well. Hughes: Hrmm, perhaps... Steele: Well up next is the war to settle the score. The Trents have had some great battles with Picasso and Glamour, and this one promises to deliver. Match 2, Tag Team Match-The Trents (c) vs Picasso & Glamour for the Mercury Tag Team Titles Glamour and Picasso enter to "Angels Holocaust". A gritty, black-and-white shot of them is shown on the MercuTron, accompanied by the man's name. The feed changes to full-color highlights from various federations, including those in Mercury. Finally, the letters "E", "E" and "I" shoot out of the background, and the video shatters and crumbles into nothingness. Steele: Can they do it? Will tonight be the night EEI finally succeeds? Hughes: What kind of question is that? Of course, silly! The Trent Brothers' video package begins with a close-up of Mikel Trent's face, his eyes closed, as the beginning of Drowning Pool's "Bodies" fills the arena. Mikel's eyes then open, and his face fills with anger and rage before the camera pulls back to show his elder brother Josh with a smile on his face. The feed then switches to a split screen as pyro erupts from the ramp and the brothers appear from behind the curtain. On the left of the MercuTron, Josh hits several high-flying moves and attacks enemies with weapons, while on the right, Mikel spears and pummels other opponents. As the brothers reach ringside, their video ends with a clip of Mikel powerbombing Josh through an opponent and a table. Josh and Picasso immediately step into the ring without any opposition from their partners. They stare each other down, then lock up. Neither man can gain an advantage, so they break, and then just as quickly lock up again. This time, Josh Trent gains the upper hand, and locks Picasso’s head. Josh hits a snap suplex on his antagonist, and holds on, dragging a pained Picasso up with him. Again, Josh lands a snap suplex, and again, he drags Picasso up with him. Josh looks for a third suplex, but Picasso won’t have it. He fires off elbows at Josh’s midsection until Josh breaks the headlock. As soon as he does, Picasso drops him with a dropkick. Steele: EEI getting right down to work here tonight. As the aggressor, Picasso takes a more violent approach. As Josh gets up, he is met with blistering knife-edge chops from Vincent. As Josh staggers backwards, Picasso picks him up as if to slam him. And slam him he does. Head first, with a hard Michinoku driver. Picasso covers, but Josh kicks out at two. Picasso keeps the attack coming, stomping away. When satisfied with Josh’s prone state, he hops up to the turnbuckle. Picasso leaps off with a frogsplash, but Josh rolls out of the way and Picasso hits hard, chest first, on the mat. Hughes: That had to hurt. Glamour strides right up to Mikel Trent and throws an eye poke. Mikel uses the Greco-Roman (Curley) block, then uses the bottom of his fist to bash Glamour in the head. Glamour retaliates with a heel (of the boot) stomp to Mikel's left foot, followed by a front side headlock into a brainbuster! Mikel clutches at his recently busted brains, but is still able to kick out at one on the ensuing cover. Glamour rolls Mikel over and cinches in a camel clutch, followed by a choke, only to have Mikel immediately power out by standing up! Johnny is surprised by the show of strength, and decides to use the opportunity to flip into the Runway inverted sunset flip! Mikel blocks this, however, and grabs Glamour's legs in order to place him in a wheelbarrow. Glamour tucks himself into a victory roll, but Mikel blocks that by kneeling on his arms to garner a one, a two, Glamour kicks out! Steele: Surprisingly good counter wrestling from Mikel Trent. Hughes: Except that had Glamour's moves gone through, this match would be over. Mikel don't got the goods to beat someone as beautiful as Johnny Glamour. Not tonight. Mikel follows up on his advantage with an Irish whip, but Glamour reverses. At least, in theory; in practice, Mikel doesn't budge, and he pulls Glamour into an absolutely vicious short-arm lariat! Mikel does an elbowdrop to keep the model in check, then lets Josh back in. Did I mention that Josh was already standing on the top rope? Okay, well, that would help to explain why he is immediately able to do a short, turning jump, then a much larger one for a breathtaking moonsault onto Glamour! He hooks a leg, one, Picasso is in for the break. Josh, ever the quick-thinker, uses precise timing to deliver a Joker's Wild axe kick as Picasso ducks between the ropes to go to the apron! Josh kips up, turns around, and runs over to give the same to Glamour... who ducks and floats over into a swinging DDT! Steele: Nice move, but he's got nobody to tag in. Hughes: ... Steele: Yes? Hughes: I got nothin'. Steele: Will wonders never cease? Glamour notices the distinct lack of Vincent Picasso on the apron, and goes to his own devices; specifically, he puts Josh in a standing headscissors, then crushes him with a piledriver! Josh bounces from the impact, and Glamour rushes at Mikel to give him a flying eye gouge! Blinded, Mikel is unable to help when Glamour puts Josh through a bridging fisherman's suplex and gets 1, and 2, and not 3! On the EEI side, Picasso has finally come to his senses. At least, enough to wave off the prospect of a tag. Glamour huffs, and absently points referee Yish Kibish's attention towards the crowd. That done, Glamour has more than enough time to give a Vinatieri-sized boot between Josh's legs! Josh crumples, Glamour covers, and after Johnny yells for Yish to slide down and count, Mikel is able to come in and break that up. Hughes: That Johnny Glamour. What a rascal. Steele: 'Rascal'? Hughes: Yeah. Steele: What is this, 1930? Hughes: Well, what should I have said? 'Knave'? Glamour finally tags in Picasso. Vincent is rather dazed by the Joker's Wild, however, and he takes a while to get over to Josh. Specifically, it takes until Josh makes it to Mikel for the big tag! Mikel knocks Picasso down with a sidewalk slam, then he gives Glamour (who decided not to exit the ring) a tilt-a-whirl backbreaker! Glamour rolls out, and Mikel places Picasso in an inverted front facelock to set up the Inner Rage DDT! Mikel lifts, and Picasso manages to use the momentum of that action to flip all the way out behind. Mikel realizes that it didn't work and spins around, only to have Picasso lift and plant him with a michinoku driver! Picasso bounds into a superkick that knocks Josh off the apron, then climbs the turnbuckle and turns around to face his opponent. With Mikel halfway across the ring, Picasso is forced to put everything he has into the jump, but when he lands the Starry Night shooting star press, it pays off with a one, Josh slides under the bottom rope, a two, Josh reaches out for Picasso's left ankle, and... a three! Winners by Pinfall, and NEW Mercury Tag Team Champions: Picasso & Glamour Hughes: They did iiiiiiiiit! Steele: By a fraction of an inch, EEI has finally succeeded in getting the tag team titles off of the Trents. I can only suspect that the war between these four men will not end with tonight's match. Hughes: That's for sure. But I can tell you one thing that isn’t for sure. Steele: What's that, John? Hughes: That whacko Dominator! I saw your interview with him. Steele: Well, it was interesting to say the least, and I must admit I have no idea where he is coming from. How about you fans take a look as here is an interview I did with Dominator earlier today. We go to a room with white walls, grey carpet, and two black leather chairs. In between the two leather chairs is a TV and above the tv is the Mercury logo fastened to the wall. As the TV plays highlights of The Dominator's early Mercury career, KC Steele and "The Dominator" Victor Williams enter the set and sit in the two chairs. Steele: Good afternoon, Mr. Williams. Dominator: Hello KC. Let’s get this out of the way, okay? Steele: Fine. You've been with Mercury for several weeks now. How do you feel you've done in that time? Dominator: Well, not too good. Out of the matches I've had, I've come out victorious once. I wouldn't call that particularly good. But there's nothing I can do, but keep trying. Steele: Are you happy with the direction your career is taken, and why do you think it has gone the way it has? Dominator: Up until joining Mercury, yeah I was happy, but since getting here I can't say I feel the same. Mainly because what opportunities that I thought I was going to get where not given to me as option. I don't think I was even given any options. I was just thrown into matches just to keep things from getting boring. Just as something new. Steele: Why do you suppose that you haven't been given these opportunities? Dominator: Why? Well, because if you're not in with the right people, then you're screwed. You can't just be a loner, like I was attempting to be at first. But then, I realized that and paired up with Magnum Wolf. That doesn't even seem to be working at the moment. But I assume that soon enough Beaumont will realize what Merc is missing out on. Then Mags and I will be brought up and seen as more than what we're seen as now. Steele: So, you blame Beaumont for your current situation. Do you have anything to say to him in regards to how he should consider using you in the future? Dominator: Well, not fully blame him... but I do think he had a part in it all. I'd just say that he needs to see more than just what he wants to. Steele: Is there anything you'd like to say to the wrestling public? Dominator: Public, public.. hmm.. Just one word. That word? Transgression. Need I say more? Steele: Anything you'd like to add, in general? Dominator: Not that I can think of. I don't have much to say, but the moment I do... you'll be the first to know. Steele: Thanks for your time. We cut back to the arena, where we go right into our next match. Match 3, Triple Threat Match For The North American Title-"Devestating" Geoffrey Slate (c) vs Memosarks vs Dirge North American Title Match – Dirge v Memosarks v Geoffrey Slate Confetti starts to fall from the rafters over the entranceway and big top music fills the arena, signaling the arrival of Memosarks, who appears at the end of the entrance tunnel, a big grin on his face. He slowly walks to the ring, smiling at all the cameras as he goes, and stopping at one to give it a big smile and a wave. Steele: Memosarks, a former North American Champion, will be fighting for the right to have another North American title shot next week with Gol. But he may win here and end up defending it against Gol next week instead! Hughes: Yeah right. Don’t make me laugh, Steele. Dirge enters to "Angels Holocaust" after the words "I WILL KILL HIM!" echo throughout the arena. Gritty, black-and-white shots are shown on the MercuTron, accompanied by the man's name. The feed changes to full-color highlights from various federations, including those in Mercury. Finally, the letters "E" and "C" in cryptic lettering shoot out of the background, and the video shatters and crumbles into nothingness. Hughes: Right there is my pick. Dirge is due, and I can just feel it. From the stage a large blast of white pyro shoots just above the heads of the fans. Disturbed's "Violence Fetish" heralds the entrance of Geoffrey Slate; on a pitch-black MercuTron appears the word "Devastation", which quickly fades into a standard entrance video featuring a splicing of stills and clips which is made special primarily by the ring of fire encircling it. Slate walks coolly and calmly to the ring, barely recognizing the fans on the way. The three stare each other down. Dirge makes the first move, charging at the champ. As Dirge slides under Slate’s legs, Memo charges and rocks the champ with a clothesline! The two stomp Slate down briefly before Dirge turns his attention to Memosarks. He whips the clown to the ropes. On the rebound, Memo is spun around and dropped back first across Dirge’s knee! Dirge pauses to taunt, but gets blindsided by Geoffrey Slate. Slate hammers away at Dirge’s chest with hard rights. Without warning, Slate puts Dirge in a headlock and flips him over with a stiff snap suplex! As Dirge lands, Memosarks gets to his feet, runs to the ropes, and springs off, nailing a perfect springboard Asai moonsault on Dirge! Memo covers, but Dirge kicks out at one. Steele: Fast pace to begin things here, each man wanting to wear the other down. And remember, it isn’t who gets the first pin, it’s the man who gets the last pin that wins. Slate continues the assault on Memosarks, stomping away at his prone carcass. He hauls up Memo and lands a hard reverse neckbreaker. He stomps away some more, then turns his attention to a recovering Dirge. Dirge eats a face full of Slate’s boot, and he topples backward. Slate, however, rescues him from falling, applying a standing headlock and nailing Dirge with a sit-out powerbomb! Slate covers, but Dirge manages to get a shoulder up at two. The champ is slightly peeved, but instead of going after Dirge, he looks after Memo. The two slug it out in mid-ring, Memo gaining the upper hand. Memosarks kicks Slate right in the stomach, bending Slate over. Memo takes advantage and nails the champ with an axe kick! He covers, but Slate kicks out under his own power at two. Hughes: Memosarks is actually impressing me here. Steele: Don’t let the makeup fool you. That man is one hell of a wrestler. With Dirge still trying to regain his bearings, Memo continues the assault on the champ. He picks Slate up and leaps onto Slate’s shoulders. Memo stays on the offensive, looking for a clothesline. However, Slate ducks, and as Memo turns around, nails him with a hard superkick! Slate covers, 1… 2… no, thanks to a diving Dirge!
