Fusion, 11 March 2002

Mercury Fusion - 3.11.02 - Live From The Tacoma Dome, In Tacoma, Washington A dim light flickers in a dark room, swinging back and forth as a hand fiddles with the bulb. After giving it a few screws, the light suddenly shines forth with great luminescence as "It Ain’t Like That" by Alice In Chains plays in the background. The hand comes into view again, tinkering with the bulb before it explodes in his hand, morphing the shot into a video package for Mercury Fusion! Harbinger celebrates with his world title (There I was, laid out on the table) Geoffrey Slate tosses Magnum Wolf off a cage at Silent Nightmare (Screamin’ Sweat and bare feet to the floor) Pariah lets out a scream with a crimson mask on (In my life, I’d not soften) The Trents raise the tag titles high above their heads (Things that cut, and burn so often) Magnum Wolf pummels on Rush (But I sit, think of somethin’) TR Saxton tosses Gravity around (Scared to face, the dyin’ nothin’) Gol superkicks Pariah at Silent Nightmare (See the cycle I’ve waited for) And EEI poses as a group in the ring (It ain’t like that anymore) Blaze McCoy punishes Memosarks with a ladder (Where I go is when I feel I’m able) Glamour takes it to Mikel Trent (How I fight is why I’m feelin’ sore) Memosarks frolics about ringside with a wicked grin on his face (In my mind, not forgotten) Pariah spits out a tooth at Nightmare (Feel as though a tooth were rotten) Dave Beaumont sits on Edward Olmstead (Behind the smile a tongue that’s slippin) Picasso works over Josh Trent (Buzzards cry, when flesh is rippin’) Harbinger dreadlocks Blaze McCoy into submission (See the cycle I’ve waited for) Magnum Wolf tosses Slate into the cage (It ain’t like that anymore) TR Saxton walks around, tangled in barbed wire (Here I sit writing on the paper) Pariah Nausea’s Apex (Trying to make the words you can’t ignore) Blaze McCoy delivers the Hook, Line, and Sinker! ( In my eyes, what I’m lacking) Dirge and Brimstone create havoc in a promo (Score at face, a ten for slacking) Gol trash talks in a promo (Sign the deal, set in motion) Slate hugs the North American Title (Smaller fish, so huge the ocean) Pariah kicks and curses at Security Guards (See the cycle I’ve waited for) The Trents deliver some brotherly love (It ain’t like that anymore) Magnum Wolf and Slate trade blows in a cage (See the cycle I’ve waited for) And Harbinger makes Gravity play taps before the scene crumbles (It ain’t like that anymore) As red and silver pyrotechnics burst throughout the arena, "It Ain’t Like That Anymore" by Alice in Chains plays. As soon as the residue from the pyro clears, the entrance stage becomes more visible. Two large video screens in the shape of an octagon hang on both sides of the entrance tunnel, with swirling lights on all sides of the screens. In the middle, directly above the tunnel hangs a circular video screen that changes from the Fusion logo to the Mercury logo every other 10 seconds. And below that is the entrance tunnel, a long, round tunnel enclosed by Plexiglas that stretches out to where the fan barricades begin. And for a change, there is no ramp to this stage! The Mercury camera crew makes their scan of the fans, prompting the sings to jump out and high in the air. They range this week from "Wolf Was Jewed!" to "I hope Dave is ok...NOT!" and finally, "Will write for food" scribbled on a cardboard sign, held up by a man with a thick beard and even thicker New Yawk accent. Puddle of Mudd’s "Blurry" strikes up over the PA, heralding the arrival of KC Steele and "The Iron Man" John Hughes! The two men bicker down the ramp before taking a seat at ringside, and placing their headsets on. KC Steele: Welcome one and all to Mercury Fusion! Tonight, will Pariah finally drink from the golden cup of victory, or will Harbinger see that none of the such happens? "Iron" John Hughes: Whatever the case, not only is it the biggest main event Fusion has ever seen, it also has stipulations to be announced! Suddenly Guns -n- Roses "Out Ta Get Me" blares up over the pa, and out walks Talent Relations guy Bruce DeAula! Bruce hops in the ring, and gets on the stick as the pop dies down. Bruce DeAulua: Hello Tacoma! I must say it is great being here, but right now, I want Dave Beaumont to get his ass down to this ring, ALONE! Metallica’s "Struggle Within" hits the speakers, and Dave comes out, sporting a buttefly bandage over his right eye. He hops in the ring, carrying his own microphone. Dave Beaumont: Now listen, DeAula. These two are salivating to get at each other, and I say, once and for all, we end this! This is the last time Pariah will EVER get a shot at Harbinger, the champion. The fans do a roaring boo, some happy, some mad, as Bruce nods. DeAula: But with that kind of stipulation on the line, we need something to make this fair. So that is why Dave I propose that EVERYONE is barred from ringside, and if a person interferes, that person will be fired, and not only will the person they interfere for lose the match, if it happens to be Harbinger, he will lose the belt! Dave nods, fans pop. Beaumont: And with that kind of stipulation, Bruce, comes need for a special kind of match. One that says, "This is the end of the road, and we will settle the score tonight." So how about a shootfight. Knockout, submissions, or ref stoppage. No time limit, no DQ, and of course, a special guest referee. The fans begin to boo, as they know where this is going. Beaumont: And luckily enough, I just happen to have my referee shirt in the back, so why not me? DeAula: I can think of a million reasons not, but what the hell. I trust your judgement, Dave. But I’m warning you. If you even think about screwing Pariah over, their happens to be a lot of the boys in the back that want a FAIR title match, and if you don’t give them one, I’d suggest putting up some help wanted ads. I mean, you already lost TR to Philly, how many more do you want to wind up in that glorified beer hole, spitting on you? Beaumont: It will be fair Bruce, believe you me. You’ll just have to wait and see about the outcome, though. "Struggle Within" blares again, and Beaumont leaves shortly before DeAula does. Steele: HUGE rammifications for tonights Main Event, and one has to think they tend to lean towards Harbinger! Hughes: It’s a shootfight. Pariah fans, begin your sobbing....now. Steele: Well, actually, right now we have our first match of the evening! Match 1, Singles Match-Headbuster vs The Gol Steele: Headbuster has impressed since his debut a few weeks back, but was the unfortunate victim of a Blaze McCoy conspiracy with Inferno at Mass, and now, he steps into the ring with one of the most experienced wrestlers in Mercury, Gol. Hughes: This is the type of match I love to see, Gol apologised earlier in the week for what he will be doing to Headbuster tonight, so I can't wait to see this. Steele: Don't count out the newcomer, but he does face an uphill battle against a very angry Gol, who is rabid after losing to Pariah in their last man standing match at Critical Mass. The lights turn blue, and the fans in the crowd who follow LTD and those who have taken a liking to the graduate begin to pop as "Virtual Insanity" by Jamiroquai hits the speaker system. The Mercutron lights up with clips of Headbuster performing his trademark moves in LTD. Blue pyro bursts out from the ramp, and Headbuster steps out onto the stage carrying a large English flag. He runs down the ramp, high-fiving some fans at ringside, and waving the flag high above his head, then rests his flag by the announcer's table before sliding into the ring and climbing the four corners to a nice response. ' Prick' by Something For Kate, plays, as the arena lights dim and smoke covers the entranceway. On the Mercutron, the letters G-O-L each flash onto the screen, in bright green, interspersed with rapid clips of Gol doing various things, walking to the ring, cutting promos, smashing his fathers house with a sledgehammer, hitting his mother in the face, relieving himself on his brothers grave, shooting clay targets at the range, breaking a glass bottle over a tombstone, standing with the National Title high above his head, or around his waist. As the song hits the heavier riff, Gol walks out under the entranceway and into the aisle, looking up at the Mercutron, satisfied with his new entrance package, which now shows clips of him in action, delivering the Sydney Superkick to Lex Laww, Gravity, TR Saxton, Harbinger, La Mosca (in mid air) and various others. Gol turns around as golden pyro fires down the aisle and to the ring, and he proceeds down to the squared circle, sliding under the ropes and posing on all four corners to huge jeers and boos from the crowd, and a 'Gol Sucks' chant breaks out as he stares out Headbuster from across the ring. Both men step forward to lock up, and Gol easily overpowers Headbuster and drives him to the corner where he gives him a few kneelifts, then some Flair chops, which would get a 'whoo' if you could hear it over the anti-Gol chants. Gol whips Headbuster across the ring and into the corner with so much velocity the ring shakes, and Headbuster actually flips all the way over the turnbuckle and to the floor. Gol stands in the ring with his hands on his hips and stares out into the crowd, then leans over the ropes and signals for Headbuster to 'bring it', which he does by grabbing Gol's leg and tripping him, but as he tries to pull the Aussie out of the ring, he receives a kick to the jaw which knocks him back to the barrier, and Gol just slides himself back into the ring. Headbuster rubs his jaw and slides under the bottom rope, and quickly gets up and hits a spin kick to a charging Gol who is attempting to knock him back outside with a clothesline. Gol drops to the ground, but is up quickly, glaring at Headbuster, who just takes a step forward and hits a hard right to Gol's temple. Gol reels back a little, but only to come forward with a huge roundhouse right of his own, which knocks Headbuster into the corner. Gol pulls him out by the head and whips him into the ropes, Headbuster ducks the return clothesline, but can't duck Gol's boot in time on the next run and hits the mat hard. Gol drops his knee into Headbuster's back, resulting in a yelp from Headbuster, which draws a smile from Gol, but the ref seperates them as Headbuster lunges towards the ropes to get to his feet. Steele: Gol showing a lot of aggression out there, not letting Headbuster get a whole lot of offense in. Hughes: I haven't been impressed so far yet, no blood, nothing broken, this is weak. Headbuster charges at Gol, who ducks an attempted clothesline, but then gets hit with a running dropkick on the return. Gol staggers back into the ropes, then is toppled to the outside with a running cross body press, but he holds on to Headbuster as he goes over and Headbuster gets the worst of the move as Gol twists mid air and lands right on top of Headbuster on the outside. Gol gets up and shakes out his leg, and picks up Headbuster in a front facelock, then attempts a suplex, which is blocked, so he punches Headbuster in the gut, then picks him up, holds him for five seconds in mid air, then drops him with a vertical suplex onto the ramp, but cleverly lands so his back hits the mats around the ring, while Headbuster's entire body hits the steel. Headbuster shakes from the pain as Gol picks him up and throws him back into the ring, then follows himself. Headbuster tries a weak spinkick to Gol's head, which rocks the big man back to the corner, where Headbuster hits a few standing dropkicks, then hip tosses Gol across the ring, and climbs to the top rope. Gol stands up and turns around, glaring at Headbuster, who launches himself in a twisting cross body press, but Gol just hits the mat and Headbuster flies over him, landing on his hands and knees, and snaps to his feet in time to launch a spin kick at Gol, who just catches his foot, trips the other one out from under him, and slingshots Headbuster into the cornerpost, which he is draped over as Gol picks him up from behind, and hits a release belly to back suplex. Steele: Headbuster tried to outpace Gol for a while there, but the veteran wasn't taking any of it, and now the tables have turned back his way after a short offence by Buster. Hughes: I'm still not impressed, Steele, Gol apologized for a few suplexes and a slingshot? I don't think so, he will hurt this rookie! I can't wait! Gol starts stomping his foot, in anticipation of the Sydney Superkick, and charges in as Headbuster reaches his feet and ducks, knowing what is coming. Unfortunately, he doesn't see Gol just fake the kick and run straight into him, hitting a running DDT on the ducked over Headbuster. Gol is pretty proud of himself for that little trick and taps the side of his head and nods to the crowd. Headbuster groggily picks himself up from the canvas, and Gol just slips behind him and locks on a crossface chickenwing, catching Headbuster by surprise, and he is locked into the hold before he knew what hit him, with Gol wrapping his legs around Buster's waist, and dropping to the mat. Headbuster wriggles from side to side, attempting to roll the hold over, but Gol has too much leverage. Headbuster's face shows the pain he is in as he desperately tries to escape the hold, using his feet to push ever so slowly towards the ropes, until he has pushed himself and Gol nearly out of the ring, where the ref forces the break. Steele: That was a bad situation for Headbuster there, the hold locks in both of his arms and his legs, leaving him very little leverage to escape, and he has further to go as well, not being able to just reach out for the ropes. That will take its toll on him. Gol picks up Headbuster once again and starts taunting him with slaps and jabs to the head, trash talking him all the way into the corner, where Headbuster catches the Lord of Lethal with a kick to the stomach, then another to the face, punting Gol back across the ring. Headbuster climbs upstairs, waiting for Gol to get to his feet, before launching off with a moonsault. Gol, however, sees it coming, and launches into a vicious Sydney Superkick, hitting Headbuster in mid air and landing the kick right on his jaw. Headbuster stops midair and just drops to the mat head first with the impact, crumpled over in a heap, as Gol stands over him with an evil grin, one arm in the air. Steele: Gol nearly killed Hector Carreras with that same move last year, and many say Hector was never the same after that. This could have done some serious damage to Headbuster. Hughes: That's what I came here to see! That was beautiful. Steele: Yeah, real beautiful, he could have broken this kid's neck, you do realize? Hughes: That's what's so beautiful about it! Gol rests back in the corner as he waits for Headbuster to regain consciousness, but after a minute gets sick of waiting and just walks over to him, picks him up and rests him on the corner. Gol then steps outside and climbs to the top rope. Steele: No, not more! This is uncalled for, really, he's beaten, theres' no need for this! Hughes: DO IT! DO IT! Gol picks up a limp Headbuster and pulls him to the top rope, then places his head between his legs and signals to the crowd once more, looking around with a smile. Gol then hoists Headbuster up over his shoulder, and launches him across the ring with the Fall From Grace top rope powerbomb! Headbuster lands dead centr in the middle of the ring, and Gol steps down from the top rope, walks over to Headbuster and places his foot on his chest for the one, two, three. Winner via Fall From Grace: Gol Steele: Well, I hope Gol is happy, he's proven here he can destroy a rookie, I'd like to see him try that against someone like Magnum Wolf or Pariah. Hughes: He sent a message to Mercury, Steele, he's not happy, and if he comes out here and does this every week, I'll start enjoying this show a whole lot more! Mercury staff bring out a stretcher and place Headbuster on it carefully, checking for any neck damage. Headbuster moves his arm, and the fans cheer, glad to see he's alright. Gol stands by and watches all of this, and walks out alongside the stretcher, as he walks past the mic picks him up saying 'Sorry kid, I did what I had to. You ain't the first, and you won't be the last.' Steele: What an ***hole! Hughes: STEELE! Steele: Moving right along, now we're about to see... Suddenly, "Golem II the Bionic Vapor Boy" starts to play over the arena and a shower of confetti starts to lightly fall over the crowd and the ring. Hughes: What the hell? This goes on for about ten seconds, with cameras and eyes focussing on the entrance, but no Memosarks. Suddenly, the camera pans up into the rafters, where Memosarks is ever-so-slowly descending, attached to a cable from the roof. Hughes: Oh, great! A flying turkey! Steele: Well, the crowd seems to love this turkey John. Amongst a shower of confetti, Memosarks strikes a majestic pose as he comes lower and lower, finally touching down in the centre of the ring. He places on the ground two cans that were in his hands and pics up a microphone. He regards the crowd with his trademark grin for a few seconds before speaking. Memosarks: He regards the crowd yet again, but can't control himself. He bursts out into laughter Memosarks: Now can you IMAGINE how much greater someone like The Gol or Harbinger would be with just a little confetti or perhaps some high flying antics. Well. Mercury men, women and all those illegitimate children of John Hughes. Hughes: Hey! Steele: Shhh. Memosarks: Since I have entered this federation, my only aim has been the entertainment of you, the fans. I have bled, I have been battered, and I have put my body through hell. For the fans. And do you know something? Do you really wanna know something?? <beat> Memosarks: I will be entertaining the fans for a long time to come! Memosarks laughs again while bathing in the cheers of the crowd Memosarks: Now then! I was going to make myself a big ole' announcement at the PPV in regards to the fine young Dirge. Now once upon a time, this Dirge asked my good self to be his partner in his sad day of loss. So I was to partner him, in a union worthy of the late great Demolition. Or some other obscure early 90s wrestling reference. But what happens? I see him tagging with longtime EEI buddy Picasso. The horror! The horror I say! He brings a hand to his forehead in a classic melodrama pose. Memosarks: So what shall we do about it? Well I shall tell you! Dirge, I challenge thee to a match of wrestlingness! That's right. FINALLY we will give this fine crowd the privilege of a wrestling match WORTHY of the admittance fee. FINALLY the crowd shall be ENTERTAINED! And won't that be fun, yes? Because I am the most entertaining wrestler the LCW has ever seen. I am the most colorful character the wrestling world has ever known. He picks up the two cans in one hand Memosarks: Do you really want to know how far I will go? And with that he drops the mic and takes a can in each hand. He raises them ceremoniously over his head, and proceeds to tip them; a stream of red paint from one can and a stream of blue paint from the other starts to pour all over Memosarks. Hughes: I'm speechless. He's gone mad! Steele: I'm sure there's a perfectly good reason for this! Hughes: Which is? Steele: Er... The entire contents of both cans are emptied all over him, all the while his cackling laugh echoes over the arena. Slowly he puts the empty cans down and picks up the mic again. Memosarks: Rather a nice shade, don't you think? And they say we must suffer for our art? Well then, I am the first! Now then, what else is going on in the world of the wrestler? Well what a surprise. Another good ole' Pariah-Herbinger feud. Can you believe it? I wish I had a kleenex for all of you good people who pay for ticket after ticket, yet see the same thing; not a bad thing in itself, but come on! It's Harbinger and Pariah! Please! Memosarks' voice starts to deviate from his normal pleasant tone into something that sounds just a touch more snarlish. Memosarks: While they go about their battles all the way to the country of ad nauseum, WE will be giving you something you can watch. WE will give you your money's worth; and while everyone is just watching the 'clown' goofing off. His voice drops down to a hiss Memosarks: WE will give the people what they want to see! The turnbuckles suddenly explode, spraying confetti at high velocity at every angle. Memosarks starts to twirl slowly with his arms in the air, confetti sticking to his paint-covered body. "Golem II The Bionic Vapor Boy" starts playing as Memosarks slowly makes his way out of the arena. Hughes: Pssst! Who's 'we'? Steele: I have absolutely no idea. Hughes: Aren't you the one who likes Memosarks? Steele: I thought I was too. Hughes: And what's up with that challenge? Steele: I'm not sure, but I doubt we'll actually see the match. Word from the back is that we won't have enough airtime for another match given the nature of the main event. Hughes: We better move along to our second match then! Match 2, Singles Match-Jin Akujin vs Picasso "Master of the Shadows" begins playing as out comes Jin Akujin. Jin rubs his wrists as he makes his way down to the ring wearing his leather jacket with the easily recognizable "flame circle" stitched to the back. The "Kamikaze Tiger" leaps over the ropes then makes his way to the turnbuckle, where he removes his jacket, and hangs it on that very turnbuckle. Jin smiles as he leans against the turnbuckle, waiting for the match to start. Hughes: That Jin guy ain't bad and all, but I dunno. Can he go so far as to win a match? Steele: From what I've heard, he's got more moves than anyone aside from Harbinger. It remains to be seen how well he can utilize what he has. "Down in the Ground where the Dead Men Go" by the Pouges plays, and Vincent Picasso strides down to the ring to a chorus of boos as red and blue lights swirl about the entrance stage and clips of his more famous moments play on the two MercuTrons. Steele: One half of the tag team champions, Picasso retained the titles in a triple threat match by pinning his opponent tonight. Hughes: It happened at Critical Mass, it'll happen today. Jin Akujin eagerly locks up with Vincent Picasso at the toll of the ring bell. Picasso backs Akujin up to the ropes, where referee Jason Sanders calls for a break. Picasso lets go, but doesn't break clean; he sneaks in a right hand, which causes Akujin to fire back with an elbow smash. Picasso sends two more right hands in return, then whips Akujin to the ropes for a superkick. Akujin holds on to avoid the kick, then comes out with a rolling elbow smash. Picasso ducks and counters it with a backslide, only to have Akujin roll through and fire off a point-blank roundhouse kick. Picasso blocks using his forearms, so Akujin tries a back roundhouse kick, but Picasso has enough time to roll back out of the way and kip up. Akujin charges in with a palm strike, but Picasso counters with an armdrag takeover into a Fujuwara armbar. Akujin gets his legs under himself to facilitate standing up and doing a hiptoss to get free. Picasso recovers quickly and sweeps Akujin's legs out with his right arm, then he dives atop Jin's chest for a cover, but before he even gets to 1, Akujin bridges out! Picasso kips up, and both men stare each other down to a tremendous ovation. Steele: That's the kind of wrestling you'll only get on Fusion! Hughes: I got lost somewhere around Picasso punching the Japanese guy. KC shakes his head sadly. Jin wants another tie-up, but Vincent stops that thought with a boot to the midsection and a snap suplex. Picasso then whips Akujin to a corner, and follows up with an incredibly stiff running dropkick to the chest! Picasso links that with an atomic drop segued into a northern lights suplex, one, two, Akujin kicks out. Picasso holds onto the front waistlock and tries for another, but Akujin plants his feet and fires off a snap swinging neckbreaker. Akujin rises with all due haste and maintains his advantage with a gutwrench suplex. He rolls into another, but when that fails he switches up with a double-underhook suplex. A second such fails again, so Akujin 'settles' for a tiger driver! Feet-on-shoulders cover, one, two, Picasso gets a shoulder up! Hughes: All that, and Picasso is up with room to spare. I'm tellin' ya KC, this Akujin kid won't be able to put it away. Steele: John, my guess is that Jin is starting with his easier moves, then he'll try the more complex stuff if he can get Vincent worn down. Akujin backs Picasso into a corner with a series of blindingly fast palm thrusts to the head. That done, Akujin runs halfway across the ring, circles back, and pulls off a cartwheel into a handspring leg lariat on Picasso! The tag champ flops down on the canvas from the impact, allowing Akujin to manage a split-legged moonsault to the back! One roll onto the back later, and Jin hooks a leg for one, and two, and one-third, and no more. Akujin lets the disappointment roll off him, and he gets Picasso to his feet for a German suplex. Well, an attempt at one anyway; Picasso does a standing switch for one of his own. Akujin blocks using his legs, then throws some back elbows en route to breaking the waistlock and hitting an achingly beautiful overhead kick to the head! Steele: What agility! Hughes: Pfft, I could do that blindfolded. Steele: Your mother drank a lot during pregnancy, right? Picasso stumbles back from the impact, all the way through the ropes to the outside. After allowing him some time to recover and walk around, Akujin jumps onto the top rope without using his hands, then nails a flashbulb-popping somersault plancha onto the dazed Artiste! Akujin lands on his feet, and receives a warm round of applause for his efforts before he sends Picasso inside. Picasso turns around to face his foe and gets to his knees before Akujin hammers him in the side of the head with an elbow smash. Not impressed, Picasso spits at the newcomer to Mercury, causing Jin to deliver a series of elbow smashes followed by a spinning back elbow that visibly weakens (but does not shut down) Vincent. Akujin finally grabs two handfuls of Picasso's hair, draws his left leg back, and does a mercilessly stiff knee directly to the face! Hughes: NO! HIS FACE! HIS DELICATELY FEATURED FACE! Steele: 'Delicate' notwithstanding, that had to be one of the most powerful blows I've ever seen in a match, not to mention following a modified version of what Jin calls the Aoi Blaster. The crowd counts along with Sanders as Akujin grabs the pin, one, two, thr-kick out! Hughes & Steele: Whaaaaaaat?! Jin clenches his fists and visibly has to keep his emotions in check over the closeness of the fall. Not done yet, however, Akujin drags the seemingly unconscious Picasso up and places him in position for a belly-to-back suplex. Knowing what's coming next, Picasso wisely flips out of the Ryuujin driver and grabs a waistlock. Akujin blocks, so Picasso hammers him with forearms to the shoulders before doing a release German suplex that sends Akujin haphazardly into a corner! Hughes: Eeeeeeew. Steele: Akujin just went shoulder-first into the middle turnbuckle! I think the momentum of this match just took a 180. After shaking off the effects of the knee to the face, Picasso pulls Akujin out of the corner in a headlock for a basic DDT, then rolls him onto his back. With a few agile bounds, Picasso is able to mount the turnbuckle for an asthetically pleasing frogsplash; from this he gets ich, and ni, but not san, as Akujin kicks out. Showing perseverence, Picasso scoops Akujin up and plants him with a michinoku driver. Rather than a finisher, Picasso uses it to make sure that Akujin stays down. Picasso gracefully heads up top, then comes off with a textbook senton backsplash to the ribs! Both legs hooked, one, two, two and a half, no! Akujin finds some way to again kick out. Steele: Jin is showing a lot of spirit. Hughes: If by "spirit" you mean "he's doomed", then you're right. Picasso wearily pulls Akujin into a standing headscissors, only to have Jin fall to one knee by instinct. Picasso throws some forearms to put him in his place, but Akujin asserts himself by shoving his way out of the headscissors and throwing several palm strikes. Akujin slaps on a headlock and lifts into a vertical base, out of which Picasso flips behind Akujin in order to secure a waistlock. Akujin blocks the intended suplex and spins around for the Ryuujin Driver, but that too is stopped with a series of Picasso elbows to the head. One solid boot to the gut later, and Picasso has re-secured the headscissors before pulling off a cradle piledriver! Picasso smells victory, and with an athletic leap he perches himself up top one last time so that he can deliver a flawless Starry Night shooting star press! Cover, one, two, and at long last, three! Winner by Pinfall: Vincent Picasso Steele: A fine showing from Jin, and a solid win by Picasso. Hughes: Just like I said. Steele: Fans, I understand Dirge has something special to say.... The scene opens up on the lavishly decorated interior of an oversized helicopter. Fred the Interviewer is conducting and interview with LCW superstar and current Tag-Team Champion Dirge, who at the moment is lounging on a couch too nice for most millionaires to afford. The interviewer is forced to sit on the ottoman in discomfort. Dirge is wearing a James Bond looking suit, and is sipping absinthe from a large glass. Around his waist is his Championship belt, which now has Dirge's smiling face on the gold plate instead of the traditional logo. EEI is seems hastily forged over LCW on the plate as well. Fred: Thanks for tuning in folks, tonight we have 1/2 of the reigning tag team Champions, Dirge. We have a sattelite link to the internet so that those of you who are watching the show on webtv or are chatting online with us for the simulcast can send us your questions in real time. Now, Mr. Dirge, we have a few questions already prepared for you, starting with... How does it feel to be the newest reigning Tag Team Champion? Dirge: (takes a sip of his neon green absinthe) Well, it feels like a pacifier,frankly. Fred: A... what?
