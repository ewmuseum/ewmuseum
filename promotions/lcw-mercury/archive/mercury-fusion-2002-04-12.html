Mercury Fusion - 4.14.02 - Live From The Cow Palace in San Francisco, California A dim light flickers in a dark room, swinging back and forth as a hand fiddles with the bulb. After giving it a few screws, the light suddenly shines forth with great luminescence as "It Ain’t Like That" by Alice In Chains plays in the background. The hand comes into view again, tinkering with the bulb before it explodes in his hand, morphing the shot into a video package for Mercury Fusion! Pariah celebrates with his world title (There I was, laid out on the table) Geoffrey Slate tosses Magnum Wolf off a cage at Silent Nightmare (Screamin’ Sweat and bare feet to the floor) Harbinger stretches out Pariah (In my life, I’d not soften) The Trents raise the tag titles high above their heads (Things that cut, and burn so often) Magnum Wolf pummels on Rush (But I sit, think of somethin’) Robert Young tosses Gol around (Scared to face, the dyin’ nothin’) Gol superkicks Pariah at Silent Nightmare (See the cycle I’ve waited for) And Oblivion poses in the ring (It ain’t like that anymore) Blaze McCoy punishes Memosarks with a ladder (Where I go is when I feel I’m able) Glamour takes it to Mikel Trent (How I fight is why I’m feelin’ sore) Memosarks frolics about ringside with a wicked grin on his face (In my mind, not forgotten) Pariah spits out a tooth at Nightmare (Feel as though a tooth were rotten) Dave Beaumont sits on Edward Olmstead (Behind the smile a tongue that’s slippin) Picasso works over Josh Trent (Buzzards cry, when flesh is rippin’) Harbinger dreadlocks Blaze McCoy into submission (See the cycle I’ve waited for) Magnum Wolf tosses Slate into the cage (It ain’t like that anymore) Jin Akujin is piledriven from the top rope by Picasso (Here I sit writing on the paper) Pariah Nausea’s Apex (Trying to make the words you can’t ignore) Blaze McCoy delivers the Hook, Line, and Sinker! ( In my eyes, what I’m lacking) Dirge and Brimstone create havoc in a promo (Score at face, a ten for slacking) Gol trash talks in a promo (Sign the deal, set in motion) Slate hugs the North American Title (Smaller fish, so huge the ocean) Harbinger makes Slate feel his wrath (See the cycle I’ve waited for) The Trents deliver some brotherly love (It ain’t like that anymore) Magnum Wolf and Slate trade blows in a cage (See the cycle I’ve waited for) And Pariah drops Gol with a Nausea as the scene crumbles (It ain’t like that anymore) As red and silver pyrotechnics burst throughout the arena, "It Ain’t Like That Anymore" by Alice in Chains plays. As soon as the residue from the pyro clears, the entrance stage becomes more visible. Two large video screens in the shape of an octagon hang on both sides of the entrance tunnel, with swirling lights on all sides of the screens. In the middle, directly above the tunnel hangs a circular video screen that changes from the Fusion logo to the Mercury logo every other 10 seconds. And below that is the entrance tunnel, a long, round tunnel enclosed by Plexiglas that stretches out to where the fan barricades begin. And for a change, there is no ramp to this stage! The Mercury camera crew makes their scan of the fans, prompting the sings to jump out and high in the air. They range this week from "Sickness: Infect me!" to "I paid to see Midgets bleeeeed!" and finally "Where’s Metalhead?" Puddle of Mudd’s "Blurry" strikes up over the PA, heralding the arrival of KC Steele and "The Iron Man" John Hughes! The two men bicker down the ramp before taking a seat at ringside, and placing their headsets on. KC Steele: Welcome one and all- "Iron" John Hughes: Shut your trap, Steele! Welcome boys and girls to Mercury Fusion, blah blah blah, I drink on air, blah blah blah, Pariah is God, blah blah blah, ahhh screw it! Tonight Pariah takes on some mystery opponents, and the tag straps AND a chance at the Suicide main event are on the line! Steele: John, that wasn’t- Cutting of Steel again is Metallica’s "Struggle Within", bringing out Dave Beaumont! Dave hits the ring, and steals a mic to kick off the show. Dave: I've been in this business my entire life. I've always known that I was preordained to have a big role in the sport of professional wrestling. I consider it a noble, honorable profession, as do all of you. But something like what happened last week... it makes me wonder. Sickness! You got some 'splaining to do! Static X's "Cold" hits, and Sickness gets booed right out of the building the instant she appears in the entrance tunnel. She pauses to look over the mob of people chanting “slut” over and over again, and smiles broadly. (Today the slut is wearing black jean overalls, and a white baby-t that has a smiley face with fangs.) She continues on her way to the ring. Dave: I- "Playground for a Wedgeless Firm" gets the loudest pop it has in the calendar year. Harbinger power walks to the ring, eyes fixed on Sickness. He goes to a corner and drapes himself over the top turnbuckle, seemingly not there to talk but rather to listen. Meanwhile, Sickness seems even more amused than she did before. Sickness: Seeing you two together makes be realize something. The only reason Dave here has been so touchy lately hasn't been because he has a legitimate hatred for the Oblivion. No, it's because he's afraid of anyone usurping his authority. I did that through my relationship with Herb, and I did it again when Magnum, Gol, Mikel and Josh all agreed to join the Oblivion. That's why you're resisting. Not out of moral outrage... but jealousy. Beaumont points at her. Dave: You conniving bi- Harbinger: Shut up. Beaumont stops, shocked that Harby is telling HIM to shut up. Harbinger: Last week's Fusion was one of the worst days of my life. But I don't blame Sickness, she just made an error in judgement. I don't blame the guys in Oblivion. No, I blame YOU, Beaumont! Your crappy judgement is what caused dissention, and that dissention caused you to create a situation that DIRECTLY led to my greatest personal humiliation! You know, I think it's time that these people realize just how shallow your allegience to me is. See, I've always been loyal, but I'm not the one that was supposed to lead Mercury as its first champion. No, that honor was reserved for that spotty, leave-on-a-moment's-notice bastard named Hector Carreras! And when he left the country and stopped returning your phone calls, THAT is when you finally settled for ol' Harby as the main man. Well, screw you, and screw your little war with the Oblivion. I'm not fighting them, because I won't fight her. I'll do my own thing, and let you make due with the hole you dug yourself, Davey! Harbinger dumps his mic, says something to Sickness, and leaves. She blinks at Herb as he leaves. Beaumont finally regains his composure. Dave: You bitch. You've got five men wrapped around your fingers, and you're using them and Mercury as your own toys. Well, I'm on to you, and next week, I'm going to have a fitting punishment worked out for all the damage you've caused in Mercury. Sickness: Dave, I'd ask you to apologize for calling me a bitch, but I don't think one will be forthcoming at this particular juncture. She steps over to Beaumont and leans in, mere inches from his face. Sickness: As for punishing me, Dave, I believe I can take anything that you could have to offer. Remember, the Abyss awaits. Static X's Cold plays and she walks on out with Beaumont left fuming in her wake. Steele: What a strange and twisted web we weave, John. Hughes: Cripes Sick has a tight a--*ahem* What was that? Steele: You perv. Without further hesitation, AND further erections, lets get to our first match, ok John? Match 1, Triple Threat Match-Magnum Wolf vs Jin Akujin vs Memosarks Confetti starts to fall from the rafters over the entranceway and Mr. Bungle's "Golem II The Bionic Vapor Boy" fills the arena, signaling the arrival of Memosarks, who appears in the entrance tunnel, a large metal bucket with a lid on his right arm and a big grin on his face. He slowly walks to the ring, smiling at all the cameras as he goes, and stopping at one to give it a big smile and a wave. Steele: Memosarks hasn’t been making much headway in Mercury lately, he might be in a bit of a slump. Hughes: We all knew it was coming sooner or later. Steele: What the hell is in that bucket? "Master of the Shadows" begins playing as out comes Jin Akujin. Jin rubs his wrists as he makes his way down to the ring wearing his leather jacket with the easily recognizable "flame circle" stitched to the back. The "Kamikaze Tiger" leaps over the ropes then makes his way to the turnbuckle, where he removes his jacket, and hangs it on that very turnbuckle. Jin smiles as he leans against the turnbuckle, waiting for the match to start. Hughes: How is he still alive after last week? Steele: Befuddles me. Hughes: Bef*ckle? What? Steele: JOHN! The lights in the arena die. Darkness and silence fall... when suddenly, the entranceway is filled with flashes of blue and red. "Courage" by Alien Ant Farm blasts the crowd, but then the lights die again and all you can hear is the guitar. As the chorus hits, the lights come back on in blue and red, and standing at the end of the entrance tunnel is Magnum Wolf. He hops in the ring as "Courage" fades and the lights return to normal. Steele: After what happened last time Akujin and Magnum were near the same ring, I’d say Jin has plenty to be angry about. Memosarks is quick to be attacking, as he hits Magnum with an ax-handle from behind! But Magnum simply slowly turns around, and clobbers Memosarks with a nasty right, and then tosses Memosarks backwards into the corner. Magnum sizes up Memo and attacks with elbows as Jin simply watches on, allowing the onslaught. As soon as Magnum gets tired of beating on Memosarks, he backs out of the corner and turns around, only to get kicked in the leg by Akujin! Akujin fires off rapid fire kicks, taking Magnum down to his knees! Hughes: It’s the same thing he did last time! Steele: Great strategy, why not let the kid stick to it? As Akujin rears back and looks for a kick, Memosarks clotheslines Akujin out of nowhere! But Akujin blocks an attempted punch from Memosarks, and delivers a multitude of palm strikes to Memosarks, dropping the clown back into a corner. Akujin the backs up, and takes off in a dead sprint before leaping in the air and delivering a thunderous dropkick square to the face of Memosarks! Akujin gets up, celebrating, but that soon stops as Magnum Wolf turns the youngster around, and sambo suplexes Jin out of his boots! Magnum pins and gets one, two, Memosarks saves! Steele: Close call for Jin! Hughes: Memosarks will save him. Unfortunately. Magnum gets up, snarling at Memosarks, who is now backing away in a corner. But Magnum shows no mercy, and wraps his meaty hooks around Memo’s throat, choking the life out of him! Magnum then tosses Memosarks back into the corner again, and stomps away at the frame of Memosarks! Jin looks to break it up though, spinning Magnum around. But Magnum doesn’t stop his throwing of fists, and levels Akujin! Magnum points at the rookie and yells some foul language, but he notices Memosarks stirring. Hughes: Hey, wait a minute, Memo took the lid off that bucket... Indeed Memosarks has, and is now standing as Magnum turns around. Memosarks then rears back, and tosses confetti from the bucket into the face of Magnum Wolf, blinding The Oblivion member! At the same time, Akujin rolls Magnum up with a school boy and gets one, two, three! Winner via pinfall: Jin Akujin Steele: Jin Akujin just beat Magnum Wolf! Akujin quickly rolls out of the ring and exits through the crowd as Magnum gets up, blood on his mind. Memosarks, making a "confetti angel" is unaware that Gol has now came from the back, and gets in the ring. Gol whispers something in Magnum’s ear, who rolls to the outside, and pulls out a table from under the ring! Steele: Memosarks, run. Run now, and run fast. Gol stands over the joyous Memosarks, who suddenly stops making "confetti" angels, and slowly gets up. But Gol hauls off and cold-cocks Memosarks! Gol drags Memo over to the outside, where Magnum has a table well set up. Hughes: I can’t watch...I can’t watch... Steele: You know you can. Hughes: Ahh hell, who am I kidding! With a table set up in front of the announce table, Magnum picks up Memosarks for a powerbomb, and drops him on the table! But...the table doesn’t break! Magnum looks on in disbelief as Memosarks rolls on the table, holding his abdomen and rolling around in pain. Magnum then picks up Memosarks off the table, and hoists him up for the powerbomb again. Memo gets dropped, and the table doesn’t break for a second time! Hughes: He’s spitting up blood! Steele: This is horrible! As Magnum hoists up a severely hurting Memosarks for a powerbomb, Gol stops him in his tracks, and whispers something in Wolf’s ear again. Wolf’s face lights up, and he rolls Memosarks into the ring, as Gol ascends to the top turnbuckle. Wolf picks up Memosarks as he would for a belly to back suplex, and places him atop the shoulder of Gol. Gol points at the table, and stands up on the middle buckle with Memosarks firm in his grasp. Steele: Oh my God, he’s going for the Fall From Grace to a table on the outside! Then, in a swift motion, Gol tosses Memosarks off his shoulder with a release powerbomb from the second turnbuckle to the outside, and Memo hits the table, which cracks on the third try! Paramedics hit the scene quickly, attending to the now blue in the face Memosarks as Gol and Wolf walk back to the lockerroom, sick looks on their faces. Hughes: That..was...incredible! Steele: Incredible? INCREDIBLE? They might have just ended Memosarks’ career! You are horrible, John Hughes. Backstage We cut backstage, where we notice Pariah stuffing a cell phone into his pocket, and then walking into his lockerroom. He throws his belt onto the wooden table in front of the leather couch, and notices a fruit basket with the note. He picks up the note, and reads it aloud. Pariah: "Cordially Yours, The Oblivion". What in the hell... Pariah stares at the fruit basket, and scratches his head before we cut to another backstage scene, one of paramedics rushing Memosarks into an ambulance. Paramedic: Quick, we need to relieve pressure! Other Paramedic: What’s it look like? Paramedic: Collapsed lung, internal bleeding, other severe forms of internal trauma. Other Paramedic: No time to waste, we need to move! Back At The Arena Steele: What was with Pariah’s gift? And poor Memosarks... Hughes: I’m not sure, but things don’t look good. He was on the oxygen thingies and everything... Steele: We send Memosarks our highest regards. But right now, with the Suicide main event on the line, we head to one of two matches to determine who will be fighting in the main event alongside Magnum Wolf and trying to take Pariah’s title away! Match 2, Singles Match for a spot in the Suicide Main Event-Gol vs Robert Young Steele: Two former friends and allies, and countrymen, do battle here for a shot at the Mercury title at Suicide. The stakes don't get any higher than this, John. Hughes: There's nothing like a bit of bad blood to get the violence going, bring on the blood! The lights dim and a grey haze appears at the entrance as the Opening to "Blood on the Ground" by Incubus plays over the PA. As it does pictures of a bloodied Robert Young standing over Big Pee On in a bar room brawl flash. That fades as the Mercutron simply states "Robert Young". Young walks out with his hands raised to a mixed response. He proceeds to the ring and slides in. He tours each side of the ring and leans over the ropes taunting the crowd. Steele: Young, with little to lose, tons to gain. 'Prick' by Something For Kate, plays, as the arena lights dim and smoke covers the entranceway. On the Mercutron, the letters G-O-L each flash onto the screen, in bright green, interspersed with rapid clips of Gol doing various things, such as parading with the National Title and walking to the ring. As the song hits the heavier riff, Gol walks out under the entranceway and into the aisle as golden pyro fires down the aisle and to the ring, and he proceeds down to the ring, staring straight at Young. He slides under the ropes and begins to stretch on the corner, as the bell rings and this match gets underway. Hughes: He is looking great tonight! Steele: I must admit Gol is one helluva physical specimen. Gol and Young waste no time locking up, and after an initial struggle, Gol pushes Young into the corner, pushing him back into the turnbuckle for the refs count, then breaking, staring straight at Young the whole time. They return to mid ring, and lock up again, with Gol this time taking advantage quickly with a headlock, but not for long, as Young tries to dump him backwards in a back suplex. Gol wriggles in mid air and shifts his weight, getting his feet on the mat again, but Young tries for the suplex again, this time Gol counters it by throwing himself forward and holding onto the headlock, driving Young's head into the mat with a bulldog. Steele: This is going to be rough, and hard. No fancy flips here. Both men are up relatively quickly, Gol surprised that Young recovered so easily, but not surprised enough to get hit by his charging clothesline, and on the rebound Gol throws a back elbow into Young's jaw, knocking him into the ropes, then a mafia kick sends Young over the top to the outside, where he lands on his knees. As Young helps himself up using the barrier, Gol runs the ropes and comes flying across the ring, over the top rope and straight onto Young with a plancha. Steele: My God, did we just see a 270 pound, 6'7" Australian just fly over the top rope to the outside? I'm getting the feeling Gol might just do anything to win this match. Although the same can be said about Young, and this is just for a title shot! Imagine what these men would do to win that gold. Hughes: What was that you said about no fancy stuff? Gol gets up and throws Young back into the ring, bouncing his head off the mat along the way. He gets a double underhook, but we don't get to see where he's going with it, as Young pushes off with his feet and drives Gol straight back out of the ring, between the ropes this time, with what can best be described as a driving tackle. Young lands on the outside with all of his weight driving straight into Gol's gut, which winds the big man, and he rolls off to the side to catch his breath, stalked by Young across the ring. Young eventually catches up with his fellow Aussie, spinning him around by the shoulder straight into a big roundhouse right. A slugfest ensues with Young getting the best of it with some powerful right hands, eventually flooring his former stablemate, with Gol's head bouncing off the steel steps. Young picks up Gol and throws him back in the ring, doubles him over with a kneelift, then hits an implant DDT, driving Gol's head and neck to the canvas. He rolls the limp big man over, but only gets two, as Gol kicks out. Hughes: Gol reached deep down for that one. Steele: As he is going to have to tonight. You can’t take Robert Young lightly. Young is in control, and whips Gol across the ring into the corner with so much force Gol bounces out to mid ring before falling straight into Young's grip for a gutwrench suplex. Young covers once more, one, two, but not three, as Gol kicks out. Steele: Robert Young is in control of this match now, and I wonder if Gol thought he'd put up this much fight so early. Young punches Gol into the corner, then sets him up on the top rope. After a few uppercuts to Gol's jaw, Young goes up himself, hooking one of Gol's legs and attempting a fisherman suplex from the top rope! Gol blocks desperately, but is soon overpower, but lucky for him as Young picks him up, he loses his grip on his leg and ends up falling in a superplex, rather than landing with all the impact on the back of his neck. He's still in a fair amount of pain but, and Young capitalizes, sneaking behind Gol and locking on a dragon sleeper. Steele: Young, with a very rounded out arsenal, showing Gol he can go anywhere and hold his own. Hughes: LTD sure did a lot for this guy. Gol flails for a moment, then has the presence to twist in the hold, to release the pressure, then drive Young backwards with his legs until he reaches the ropes. Young releases the hold, then, before Gol gets a chance to stand up, hooks on a full nelson, then picks Gol up and drives him into the mat with a full nelson suplex. Gol lands awkwardly on his hand, and he rolls across the ring in pain, clutching at his wrist. Hughes: Broken wrist! Broken wrist! I haven't seen a bone break on LCW television in so long, this is beautiful. The ref checks Gol, who is still holding his wrist in pain, but after a few checks, the ref seems satisfied that he can continue, as he shakes away the pain, and John Hughes' hopes that he did indeed break his wrist. Young immediately takes aim at the injury, trying to put on an armbar, but Gol will have none of it and takes him over with an armdrag, then holds on and drops his leg over Young's extended arm. Gol then twists his arm and bends it behind Young's back, but Young just spins out of the hold, and Gol decides that technical wrestling isn't for him in this clash, grabbing Young as he spins and hitting an overhead belly to belly suplex. Young gets up quickly, so Gol slides down and puts him flat on his face with a drop toehold, something Young was definitely not expecting, and he gets up enraged at being dropped embarrassingly on his face by the man once considered his sidekick. Gol smiles his cocky little smile at Young, who tries to slap the taste right out of Gol's mouth, but Gol jerks his head backwards and away, then charges over Young with a clothesline. Gol taunts Young, daring him to come at him, which Young does, allowing Gol to just pick him up over his shoulder, and fall backwards, sandwiching Young's head between Gol's back and the mat. Young clutches at his neck in pain, and Gol covers, one, two, but only that as Young kicks out. Steele: Ouch, Young is getting frustrated and angered by Gol's taunting, but both men had to expect that with so much history between the two. If anything, I would have thought Young would be using that to his advantage, but Gol seems to be the cool and calculating one here. Young gets up favoring his neck, which Gol notices pretty quickly, and decides a neckbreaker would be the best form of action, very cleverly putting together that a neckbreaker hurts the neck, which Young has injured. Anyway, Young sees what is coming, but his counter attempt of backing into the corner fails and Gol pulls him out with a swinging neckbreaker, then covers for a two count. Hughes: I thought Gol had it there! Steele: So did I! Young gets up very slowly, leaving Gol plenty of time to figure out his next move, but he never gets a chance to execute it, as Young suckers him in and backdrops him over the top rope to the floor. Gol lands hard on the mat, again on his arm, and this time the injury looks pretty bad as he rolls to the corner with his arm limp by his side. He cradles the limb and retreats, but Young senses weakness and charges at him, driving him over the barrier with a clothesline. The ref breaks them up and helps Gol out of the crowd, and wants to check his arm, but Gol will have none of it, pushing him aside with his good arm and sliding back into the ring, to be met with several stomps from Young, right into his arm. Gol rolls onto his stomach to protect himself, but this doesn't work too well as Young drops elbows right into his spine, and he ends up not being able to protect all of his body at once, leaving himself open to vicious stomps and a general pounding all over from the man former known as Yobbo. Steele: Young is just getting vicious now! The ref eventually pulls Young away and checks Gol, asking if he wants to quit. Gol shakes his head and gingerly uses the ropes to help himself to his feet. Young is ruthless and charges in straight away, picking up Gol over his shoulders and dropping him with a DVD. He raises one arm in the air and kicks Gol over on his back, and locks on the Hangover submission! Gol screams in pain but has obviously prepared for the move, twisting his body, but he can't power out using his arms as only one of them is working. Instead, he throws his body into a desperate fit, swinging himself around wildly, enough to eventually loosen Young's grip on him and to escape the hold, much to his relief, as he breathes heavily on the opposite side of the ring to Young. Young stalks in, only to be low blooded by a desperate Gol, who then makes the most of not having any power in one arm and hits a rocker dropper on Young, driving his face into the mat. Gol falls flat on his back himself, exhausted and hurt, with the ref starting a ten count. Gol is up at seven, Young at eight, and both men start slugging it out mid ring, which is an even contest until Young uses his free arm to block Gol's punches, something Gol can't do, with only one good arm, and slugs him into the corner. Gol is pulled out by a Young snap suplex, and Young signals to the fans that the end is near. Steele: Bad news for Gol here, normally he can think quickly in these situations and counter or escape what might be coming, but here he has no strength in one arm, so there isn't a whole lot he can do except run. Young grabs Gol by his head and turns him over, then picks him up over his shoulder for the Yobbo Drop, but Gol slides down Young's back and snaps into the Sydney Superkick, hitting Young square in the back of the head. Young falls forward and an exhausted Gol on top of him, but since Young is face down, it doesn't count as a pinfall, and the ref starts his ten count. Gol stirs first at around six, and rolls Young over very slowly, with one arm. He falls over Young in the cover, one, two, thr...no, Young pulls the shoulder up. Hughes: In the nick of time Young gets a shoulder up! Steele: Both men are going balls to the wall, and delivering one hell of an epic battle! Gol shakes his head and stands up, letting Young get to his feet, which he does very slowly. Gol steps into him, and Young ducks out of instinct, expecting another superkick, but Gol instead punt kicks Young in the chest, which back Young into the corner, and Gol hits another Sydney Superkick on Young, this one sandwiching his head between the turnbuckle and Gol's boot. Young slumps to the canvas, this time landing on his back, and Gol just flops down on him, one, two, thr...ee. Winner via Pinfall and now in the Suicide Main Event: The Gol Steele: And it came down to who made the first mistake. Hughes: So far, we have Magnum Wolf vs Gol vs Pariah in the Suicide main event? Add Harbinger to the mix, and I think we’re getting a new champion! Steele: Harbinger has to win first, John, and I think Geoffrey Slate agrees with you there. Backstage We go backstage, where we see Geoffrey Slate stretching out, and Jason Schatz, the newest LCW Mercury interviewer, standing next to him. Jason Schatz: Geoffrey Slate, how do you feel about your match with Harbinger tonight? Slate stands up from his toe touchers stretching routine, and stares a dead glare at Schatz. He doesn’t speak a word, and then walks off, leaving Schatz with dead air. Schatz: Um...back to the arena! Back At The Arena Steele: Poor kid, first day on the job and Slate gives him the cold shoulder! Match 3, Singles Match for a spot in the Suicide Main Event-The Harbinger vs "Devastating" Geoffrey Slate From the stage a large blast of white pyro shoots just above the heads of the fans. Godsmack's "I Stand Alone" heralds the entrance of Geoffrey Slate; on a pitch-black MercuTron appears the word "Devastation", which quickly fades into a standard entrance video featuring a splicing of stills and clips which is made special primarily by the ring of fire encircling it. Slate walks coolly and calmly towards the ring, barely recognizing the fans on the way. Steele: Geoffrey Slate is going to be really, really fired up. He blames Harbinger as much as anyone for what happened at the end of last week's main event, and now he's got a chance to get back what was taken away! Hughes: Why are we doing more contendership matches, anyway? Steele: Because of how Wolf got into the Suicide main event, Beaumont has decided to add two more men to the match in order to lessen his chance of winning. The eerie first seconds "Playground for a Wedgeless Firm" lead to the word 'Harbinger' slowly fading in on the MercuTron. It flashes at the sound of the thunderclap, and a picture of Harbinger's face takes its place while he walks out to the top of the entrance ramp, suprisingly to some cheers. At the first electronic pulse, the picture of his face is slowly replaced by clips of Harbinger wrestling, including him doing the Purge, Dreadlock, and many variations of the Punk-Out. Harbinger walks down the ramp to the drumbeat, rolls into the ring, and stretches out before the start of the match. Steele: Mixed reaction from the fans after what was a very big pop at the start of the show. I think they were expecting him to confront Sickness rather than Beaumont. Hughes: I understand Harbinger's motivation. I'd probably carry a torch if I got me some of that disease. Steele: Yet another mental image I simply don't need. Harbinger locks up with Geoffrey Slate, with Slate pulling Harbinger's left arm into an armwringer. Slate uses his leverage to snap Harbinger onto his back, allowing for Slate to apply a cross-armbraker. Harbinger blocks by clenching his hands together, then he stands up while Slate dangles from his arm. Harbinger breaks the hold and slaps a legbar on Slate's left leg, but mere seconds later Slate rolls onto his chest and stands up with Harbinger's left leg in a half Boston crab! Slate sits back and starts to tweak Harbinger's ankle, which causes Harbinger to crawl to the ropes for a break. Steele: Round one goes to Geoffrey Slate. Harbinger seems to be lingering in the ropes, so Slate throws some kicks to the ribs and follows it up with an Irish whip. Harbinger ducks the ensuing flying clothesline and grabs Slate's left arm before spinning into a la majestral cradle, getting a one count before Slate kicks out. After putting some distance between each other they tie up again, this time seeing Slate push Harbinger to the ropes. Bo Smirnow calls for a break, so Slate lets go of Harby... then rips into him with enough rapid-fire knife-edged chops to make Harbinger's pale chest turn a bright red! Slate then snapmares Harbinger and nails him with a sickening superkick to the back of the head, making him prone enough for a running senton splash!
