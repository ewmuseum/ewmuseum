LCW Mercury - Continuing the Classic LCW Tradition LCW Information Sheet Greetings, and welcome to LCW, also known by its full name, Lethal Championship Wrestling. If you're reading this, you're in the sub-fed of LCW known as LCW neo, which is angle-based and its roots and development will be discussed in-depth later on in this page. LCW is one of the hundreds of thousands of efeds on the Internet today, which means that it is an online wrestling federation based on roleplaying (LCW Mercury) and character development (LCW neo). Just like a real federation, we have a booking committee and executive officials, most notably Mercury's owner, David Beaumont, and neo's owner, Edward Olmstead. Of course, these are really just characters, but they play the roles that their real-life counterparts would in an actual federation. In neo, matches are, for the most part, determined for angle advancement purposes, based on the quality of roleplays from the various members of the federation. For more information on this, check out our FAQ page and/or our Regulations page . LCW was first started in November of 2000, after splitting from the moderately successful EHW (later GHW) federation. EHW itself was an offshoot of the then-defunct MMWF (Mark Madden Wrestling Federation), the highly popular official efed of MarkMadden.com. For a while, we had a "sister" fed that enjoyed the markmadden.com endorsement, but it has now closed several times, and we feel that LCW better embodies the emphasis on traditional wrestling that was a tenet of the original MMWF, and one of our bookers was responsible for booking the MMWF at the peak of its popularity. Although relatively new on the scene, LCW is already enjoying a fair amount of attention and popularity, receiving between 1,000 and 2,000 hits on an average day, with more page views on the days after our wrestling-based shows are held. We also enjoy slightly higher page views on the weekends, as many of our members have school of one type or another (either high school or college) and/or work, and they have more free time on the weekends. Also, we've attained Top 10 status on two Top 1000 lists, as well as receiving a third place and a fifth place ranking on EWN's Countdown, a Top 10 list for feds in that "circle". (Note: At the time of this writing, Countdown had only occurred twice.) In order to provide a greater level of satisfaction to our members, it was decided in late August of 2001 that LCW would branch off into two sub-federations, LCW neo and LCW Mercury. Also, we were approached by a former member about setting up a minor league system to develop promising applicants into LCW stars, and so LML was formed. Because you have come to this page from the LCW Mercury site, the rest of this paragraph will be devoted to LCW Mercury's basis and vision. It is our desire as the booking committee of LCW Mercury to provide our roster with quality shows based not only around wrestling matches but also with a great deal of attention to fueds and the advancement of talent who consistently put out quality content. LCW Mercury is a roleplay-based federation, meaning we base the outcomes of our matches primarily on the better roleplayer; however, we will alter the storylines and/or outcomes in very extreme situations in order to advance a storyline. LCW's primary goal is to recreate the feel of real professional wrestling as much as possible, whether it is our hand-written full results, our attention to detail in matches, or our promotional efforts and some added features that will be debuting in the near future. LCW is designed to give both our members and other wrestling fans an enjoyable experience, and our psychology-fueled matches are a throwback to the days of more traditional professional wrestling. LCW believes in letting our wrestlers do the talking outside the ring, and letting their wrestling speak volumes for them inside the ring. LCW also maintains a humorous aspect, albeit a secondary one, and some of our members focus on amusement rather than winning titles. Not even they are humorous at all times, though, as our members take their characters to the utmost extremes of realism, and we strive to stay away from things that are unrealistic in both our matches and our roleplays. Generally, the best place to find humor in LCW is our Out-Of-Character Message Board, where our more lively members can nearly always be found ribbing one another. For new visitors to the LCW website, we recommended that you view our characters' roster pages before you start reading the cards. These pages have been designed in conjunction with the members of our roster, and they accurately reflect the member's take on their respective characters. In this way, you will begin to understand more about the characters and what brought them to LCW. After getting to know the personalities involved with LCW, visitors can read challenges and interviews on the LCW Mercury Roleplay Board, which will include at least a month's worth of older roleplays. The Roleplay Board is the best place to learn about the history behind a feud, how a character evolved into the person you see today, or just to be entertained by some damn fine roleplays. Our show archives are your source for reading every card LCW Mercury has hosted in chronological order. If you're interested in shows from the LCW Classic age (November 2000 to September 2001), head on over to The Vault , which has every show from LCW Classic. Currently, LCW features three to four shows per week, two wrestling-based shows - LCW neo Impulse and LCW Mercury Fusion - a recap show, LCW Weekly, and a late night talk show, Late Night Lethal. Impulse and Fusion are televised shows, and are covered in such a fashion, with full-length hand-written results (we don't use TNM or any kind of simulator) complete with commentary, backstage interviews, and various other non-match materials. LCW also holds a Pay-Per-View event every month, and we provide a Calendar, so that you can see how many shows are going to happen and where the events are going to take place. Pay Per View events are similar to our regular televised events; however, they are generally much longer and the matches are of higher quality, as we tend to do more long-term planning on PPV matches and also put more attention into them. Please take some time to familiarize yourself with the LCW website, as we've put a lot of hours into making it great, and the more people that see it, the more our work is appreciated (in theory). If you have any questions, comments, or concerns, don't hesitate to drop us a line, but please check our detailed LCW FAQ page before sending an email, as the answer to your question may be found there. Thank you for your interest, LCW Wrestling Staff
