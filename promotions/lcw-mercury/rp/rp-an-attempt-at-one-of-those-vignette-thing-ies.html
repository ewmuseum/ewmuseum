LCW Mercury - Continuing the Classic LCW Tradition An attempt at one of those vignette thing-ies An attempt at one of those vignette thing-ies Posted Wednesday, April 3, 2002 by Slate Slate is sitting at gate B-3 of the Tulsa International Airport, on his way to Fusion. He’s dressed simply; a black pair of jeans, a white tee shirt and a black LCW logo cap. On the seat directly right of him rests his carry-on, just a black gym-bag. Very few people are at the gate with him, not that many people fly on a Wednesday afternoon. There is one other noticeable person sitting at this gate, however, a beautiful blonde young woman. She noticed Geoffrey as a professional wrestler while he stood behind her in the check-in line, and since she saw him, she has done everything in her power to get his attention. But Slate has either forgotten or abandoned the art of flirting a long time ago. After over an hour, she’s finally sat down away from him, and has since fallen asleep, dosed off into her own world, leaving Slate to his. He sits in his chair, writing on an old, ragged legal pad that rests in his lap. Ever since his childhood, Slate has had problems accurately expressing his feelings in words, so he writes. He doesn’t share the writings with anyone; simply writing his thoughts, trying to give them order when they appear chaotic and contradicting, soothes him. And for the short time he writes, he is at peace. Today he writes: Why do I fight Oblivion? I shouldn’t care what they do. Until I decided to fight them, they had done nothing to physically harm me. It even appeared that they had made it a point to leave me be. Gol even stated that, originally, Oblivion had no desire to fight with me. When I decided to pick up the fight, the only wrong they had done me was to keep me from being able to wrestle Harbinger. But was that really so bad? Herbert Ingles is the best technical wrestler in Mercury. Odds are what they did allowed me to hold on to my belt one more night longer than I should have. So, why do I do it? It’s because they work together. It’s because they help each other get ahead. That’s why I fight them. What they’re doing is wrong. It’s a slap in the face to every man who’s ever broken his back, figuratively or literally, to get ahead in this business on his own. But more than that, it’s because they have something I’ll never have. Wrestling is a world filled with politics, backstabbing, and personal conflict. I’ve made no friends, because I know sooner or later, I’ll have to face that person in the ring. But they’ve somehow managed to get around all of that. In Oblivion, the men have found camaraderie. They know they can trust the other members, and that as long as they’re together, they’ll work for the betterment of each other. They have faith that each person is working for the group, and not just personal gain. I fight the men of Oblivion because what they stand for is wrong. But I also fight, because I’m jealous of them. They have found their niche in this company. They belong to something, whereas I show up to each show alone, I sit in my dressing room until it’s time for me to wrestle, and then I go home. Outside of the ring I don’t interact with anyone employed by Mercury. Fighting them is a way to end that, if only for a short while. Being against Oblivion won’t make me any friends. It won’t cover up the emptiness inside me. But at least, as long as the effort against them lasts, it’ll make me part of something. It puts me in a group effort. I know that I have to fight Oblivion on my own. It’s the only way I really know how to fight. But, in the overall picture, I’ll still be part of a group. I’ll be part of the joint effort against them. I’ll be a part of something that resembles friendship. I’ve been alone almost my entire life. Any time a cure to the loneliness comes up, somehow, someway it gets messed up. I fight Oblivion, because they’re not alone, and because they’re seemingly not going to lose that. They share common interests; they are a flock of like-minded people resolved to stick together to accomplish their goal. That’s why I hate them. That’s why I loathe them. That’s why I’ve committed myself to trying to destroy them. Because they have the something I know I never will, and I will fight with all my might to take that away from them. I’ve always said that God has a sense of irony. The one thing I’ve always wanted from this world is someone else to share my life with, and yet I will always be alone. Everyone lives with a curse, and it seems this is mine. With that, Slate lays his pen down on the paper. End.
