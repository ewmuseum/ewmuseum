Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

LCW Mercury took the roleplay half of Lethal Championship Wrestling when that federation split itself in September 2001, and said what it was in one sentence: “we base the outcomes of our matches primarily on the better roleplayer.” Its masthead promised to continue the classic tradition. Its owner on screen was David Beaumont, who in the split's own storyline had fired the champion and then built a roster out of whoever stayed.

It crowned its first world champion in a fourteen-man single-elimination tournament, giving byes to two men who surrendered the singles titles they already held in order to enter, and ran the brackets out across three weeks of its weekly show, Fusion, through October 2001. Pay-per-views followed roughly monthly — Mercury Rising in November, Silent Nightmare at the end of December, Critical Mass in March, Suicide in May.

The shows are long and hand-written, staged from named American arenas — the Firestar Center in Cincinnati, the Tacoma Dome, the Delta Center in Salt Lake City — with two-man commentary, backstage segments and full entrance detail. Suicide opens on a shot of bullets lying in a man's palm and runs a montage of the whole roster under Creed's “Bullets,” the song's lyrics printed in brackets through it.

In March 2002 its newsdesk told the roster something the results had already implied: “While we are a roleplay oriented federation, we have to use storylines as well to keep things fresh… But remember, in the long run, if you are the better roleplayer, it all will pay off.”

How it ended isn't in the surviving record. Its newsdesk put a Fusion show up and a card for Climax out on 3 June 2002; Climax was never captured, and the last dated post on its own board comes eleven days after that.
