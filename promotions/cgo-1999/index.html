Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Chicago Grappler's Organization opened with a single televised event: a three-part pay-per-view broadcast, Rude Remembered, on 6 May 1999, staged from a venue it billed as the Twindome in Bloomington, Minnesota. Announcer Joey Roberts introduced it on air as a “fledgling federation” founded by Chris Osmon, who co-owned it and sat at the announce desk, and Danny Sundown, with Ryan Starr installed backstage in an unstated role.

The broadcast doubled as a tribute to the real-world wrestler Rick Rude (billed under his given name, Rick Rood, 1958–1999), with proceeds pledged to his family. A recurring segment cut away between matches for wrestlers to share, on camera, a favorite memory of Rude, and the broadcast opened by seating a group of real-world guests ringside without any of them competing on the card: Minnesota's sitting governor, Jesse Ventura, alongside Sean Waltman, Curt Hennig, and Ric Flair.

Eight matches ran across the three parts. Al Morbis and “Mean” Scott Green opened with a Chicago street fight decided outside the ring, interrupted by a returning Mark Dedmon, who went on to squash the Jerzy Foundation in a handicap match and was billed by Roberts as “the Phenom” of the promotion. The Frat Boyz debuted with a win over a pair of substitute jobbers after their scheduled opponents, Everlasting Pain, failed to appear; Tommy Gunn, billed as a returning former outside talent, won his own debut with a decisive victory over Jack “Black” Shaw; and a triple-threat between Big Dawg, Steel Titania, and “The Extreme Playboy” Brendan Bowes ended with Dawg ambushing Titania mid-move to steal the pin.

The main event unified two of the HWA's own titles — Ryan Starr's Panhandle Heritage Championship against Richey Razorblade's World Heavyweight Championship — in a Hell in a Cell with Mr. Fabulous installed as special guest referee. Fabulous turned on Starr mid-match, teaming with Razorblade to work him over, before Starr recovered enough for Danny Sundown to run in as an emergency second referee and count a pin nobody else was positioned to make. As the broadcast's own closing beat, Starr climbed the Cell to celebrate and fell roughly two stories through the announce table during a brawl between Razorblade and Fabulous below; the broadcast ends with the announce team asking the crowd to keep him in their prayers, its account of the fall left unresolved on the page.

No document survives naming a founding date, a following broadcast, or how the promotion answered the fall at the center of its own biggest night; this pay-per-view is all that survives of the Chicago Grappler's Organization's own record.
