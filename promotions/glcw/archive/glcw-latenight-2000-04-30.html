Latenight (4/30/00)

30 April 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
[Fade in on a press conference setting in a hotel meeting room. Print journalists -- some scribbling on steno pads and others just chatting quietly -- occupy four rows of chairs. A battalion of cameramen stand ready with their equipment behind the last row. A podium with a brass "Dallas
Downtown Hilton" plate attached stands alone in the front of the room, which falls silent as Henry B. Coltrane walks through a door. The white-haired lawyer has abandoned his customary white suit, opting for a black version -- complete with an ornate string tie and black ten-gallon hat. He resembles a portly funeral director as he casts somber nods at a few selected journalists on his way to the podium. Coltrane removes some folded papers from his coat pocket and steps to the microphone.]

[A smattering of chuckles circulates among the media.] HBC: But there is no denyin' that J.W. Hardin is known in the sport of professional wrestling. There's a folder in my office thick with contract offers from around the world. From Tokyo to Toledo, wrestlin' fans want to see Jay Dub... always have. [he glances down at the papers and begins to read] J.W. Hardin burst onto the wrestling scene at the IIWF's Coronation
Clash and...
[Coltrane pauses as he looks toward the back of the room. Several heads also turn as Hardin steps through the door and stalks toward the podium. To no one's surprise, the big Texan is dressed in his usual black attire -- from his boots and stetson to a black "Syndicate" t-shirt. Reaching the podium, he unceremoniously bumps Coltrane aside.]

My contract with the GLCW says I can terminate it at any time.
[Hardin reaches into his pocket and produces two folded papers.] JWH: This is the time. [He tears the sheets in half and lets them fall to the floor.] JWH: Last week, that Reaver runt spoke some o' the truth when he said that people been tryin' to imitate the Outlaw fer years. He oughta know...
'cause he's become one o' 'em. What the boy failed to realize is that I ain't never had to tell people over and over what I was going to do to 'em.
It ain't never been 'bout talkin', just the fight. Reaver promised to take me to his world? Hell, I took him to _my_ world just to see if he could survive. I took everything he had... then crushed the lil' sumbitch. So I hurt him even more by lettin' him walk away.
[Hardin stares at the faces of the reporters.] JWH: The truth is that there ain't gonna be no successor... and mebbe that's how it was meant to be all along. Brody Thunder's out in Arizona fightin'
cancer. Otto Verhoeven's walkin' 'round with a crutch. Mota's suckin' down some o' that Canadian piss beer. Nyx Dunne's sittin' home tryin' to remember what day it is. And then... Tiger Claw and Casey James walked away.
[He pauses and shakes his head.] JWH: Gawdammit... it's time. When the last page o' the calendar turned, I wasn't supposed to be the last one standin'. But the lil' [BLEEP]s comin'
into the business today want everything handed to 'em on a silver plate and
I wanted to hand 'em their damn heads. Call it old school, call it tradition, call it whatever ya damn well please, but ya better call it a dyin' breed in the same sentence. We all put in our time in lil'
horsesh[BLEEP] feds and, when it came time to move up, I made Spreadbury rich and he did the same fer me. But when we was diggin' a grave fer the
Double Eye, we was standing in the grave, too.
