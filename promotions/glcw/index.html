Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Great Lakes Championship Wrestling was already running by October 1997, when its own president was also promoting a wider alliance, the World Internet Wrestling Council, on the side. Its surviving record picks up that December with a full holiday supercard, “A Very Twisted Christmas,” staged before a claimed 25,000-plus fans at the GLCW Arena in Charlevoix, Michigan, and called by announcers Jeff Stewart, “Iron” Mike Calhoun, and Jimmy D.

The card leaned all the way into its own theme: an Audience Participation match, a Christmas Present match fought over gift-wrapped weapons, a Christmas Tree Farm match staged live at a tree lot, a Rookie Rumble, and a Sleigh Ride of Hell that hid the Lightweight title somewhere aboard Santa's own sleigh as it was hauled through the woods. Its own World Title bout, “Santa's Revenge,” put champion Chang Khan against Sips and Maverick with wrestlers sneaking into the match disguised as Christmas characters, unmasked one at a time.

The match turned on outside interference: a disguised, 500-plus-pound rival star billed as Valtharius the Mad crashed the ring dressed as a giant elf, and a mysterious ally called Cerebus — unmasked earlier that night as the figure who'd played “the Grinch who stole Christmas” — fought him off long enough for Chang Khan to retain with a Double Oriental Death Drop.

Between segments, its own two co-presidents played it as a running double act — one doing all the talking, the other reduced to murmurs while staring at the interviewer — to announce a crossover triangle match sending two of GLCW's own wrestlers into a rival promotion's ring for a shot at that promotion's own title, with a contingency World Title tournament bracket drawn up in case champion Chang Khan went with them.

Its next surviving document, over a year later on 13 January 1999, is a press conference: a regional rival had just closed, and GLCW announced it was absorbing that promotion's arena, rebuilding it in Ann Arbor, Michigan, taking on some of its roster, and naming its former co-owner, a wrestler known as Zodiac, as GLCW's new commissioner under the name Michael Yinessa going forward. It also announced its first show outside Michigan, All-Stars of Wrestling, at Caesar's Palace in Atlantic City.

A three-federation supercard the following April shows GLCW's own wrestlers still active, crowning a new Cruiserweight Champion and contesting a newly created interfederation title, though that card's own text is held under a partner promotion's record rather than this one's. No further document of its own survives past the January 1999 press conference, and how the promotion ended is not in the surviving record.
