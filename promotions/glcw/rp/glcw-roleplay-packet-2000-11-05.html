Roleplay Packet, 5 November 2000

Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.

The area is dusty, dirty, and more than likely smells bad. Numerous men, we'll guess they're Mexican, work hard in a field. They are dirty. They sweat. You know they smell bad. They speak
Spanish. You don't understand Spanish. You wait for some well dressed man to appear, holding a small child, asking you for money. However, after a few seconds, you realize this isn't going to happen. You wait. And wait.
Christ, this sucks. I WAS WATCHING GLCW, DAMMIT, NOT THE MIGRANT WORKER NETWORK! Suddenly, one of the workers stops working. He picks up his head. It's like he heard you yell. But, he didn't, naturally. That'd be pretty impossible, seeing that you're nowhere near him. Anyway, the worker drops his shovel and walks out of the field. His supervisor yells at him in
Spanish, but he ignores him. The man is in a daze. He just walks away, as the supervisor throws down his straw hat.
We cut to a home. Shabbily furnished, it houses a woman and her three children. The woman attempts to do a pile of laundry while the children run about, the youngest one crying his full head off. The worker we saw earlier enters the home. This is his home. The woman, confused, is happy to see her husband. She runs over to hug him, but he shows no emotion. He is still in a daze, and he's not snapping out of it anytime soon. The woman yells at her husband, getting no responce. The children, now scared that their mother is yelling at their father, begin to cry. The woman yells more, then begins to cry herself. The man ignores it all and throws some items in a bag. He then leaves his crying family, but to where?
Cut to a lone highway. It is dark, and there are no lights on the road. How do we know it's there? Trust me. A car drives by the camera, allowing us to see the worker on the side of the road, walking. His thumb is extended, looking for a ride, apparently. Several more cars go by.
Cut to a car interior. Three wrestling fans are in it, the car strewn with luchador masks and various other items from their trip to Mexico. They attempt to talk to the worker, who sits in the car, but he is still in a daze. He doesn't speak. He freaks out the wrestling fans.
Cut to another car, than another, than another. The same shot, just different interiors and different drivers. Same deal; the worker sits in the back, never speaking, never even so much as blinking.
Cut to a car driving off, and our friend, the worker, standing in front of a building. An office building to be exact. He enters the building, with his bag in hand.
Cut to Rob James' office. A lovely place, it's where Rob does his commish "work." Rob sits behind the desk, doing Rob James things. He doesn't seem that interested in anything, until the door opens. Who's there? Haven't you been paying attention? It's that dazed worker. He looks even worse than before, with more dirt, stench, and the such on him. Rob looks at this man, unsure how to react, when he takes the bag he was carrying and reaches into it. Rob looks on, not exactly concerned about a possible assassination attempt, but more distraught at the smell now in his office. The worker pulls out...
..a business card. And as he gives it to Rob, he utters his only words.] Worker: He...is coming. [And with that, the man collapses, and he sure looks dead to me. I'm getting out of here before the cops come. Fade.]
* * * * * (As the scene fades from black, there is a young man standing in front of a concrete wall. His dark hair is cut short, and his eyes are covered with a pair of dark shades. He is dressed in a black collar shirt and tan khakis and he stands with his back against the wall, arms folded. He raises his head when the camera pans in closer, and pushes away from the wall in order to speak. His voice is calm and even... )
MD: The first steps of every child are a moment of pride for the entire family... it marks the first moment a child begins to make their own way in the world. When they fall, they get back up... brush off the dirt, and wipe away the tears in some cases... they get right back up, and they move foreward. Everyone dotes on them... mommy cries and daddy gets ready to go play ball with Junior. Grandparents throw out quarters and siblings stick their feet out for the eventual trip. You see, the first steps are always the hardest... and I am about to take my first few steps in the GLCW. A child on the brink of manhood, you could say... and in the traditional manner of all rookies, I do plan on making some kind of an impact. So how can I do it?
Could I come out with a sledgehammer and ... BASH someones' head in? Frankly... not my style. One hit, it's over... too soon, too quick, and absolutely no challenge to it. There's no finesse... so where does a rookie turn then? Oh, I suppose I could call out a wrestler by name... make a pronouncement, declare my superiority and dare them to prove me wrong.
*chuckle* Yeah, right... I haven't proven a damn thing, so what gives me the right to make that kind of a challenge? Nothing, so that method of earning some kind of fan reaction or heat is right out. So the stars and stripes of being a star slowly fade from the immediate picture... instead, I plan to do this whole thing the hard way.
You see, when I step into the ring... I have three goals. The first one is to step away with a solid win notched into my belt. There will be no doubt as to who is the better man that night... because I don't need to take a short cut. I don't need a flashy display of violence... just put a victim in the ring, and watch him scream. Because my second goal is to establish the Murder Death clutch as /the/ most feared submission hold in the GLCW...
no! In all of professional wrestling. Each man that ices down his knee... each man that soaks his neck... they're going to remember the Murder Death Clutch for the rest of their shortlived lives. Because my third goal in this organization is simple... I plan to make a road to infamy. Getting there isn't nearly have as important as /how/ I do it....
I'm done. [Fade.] * * * * * [Well, here we are at yet another bland scene. This is a living room of sorts. Nothing special, just your common living room scattered with furniture, decorations, and the like. And, sitting in a reclining chair in the middle of the room is Brett Rogers.
At the present, Brett's taking a look at a sheet of paper. What's on it? Can't tell you, camera won't zoom in on it. But no matter, since Brett sets the paper on a small desk to his right, then turns his attention toward our conveniently-located camera. How are they always there like this?]
BR: So, Keith Stebbins, huh? Guess my little spiel last week actually got someone's attention. I figured they'd start me off with someone like The
Tennessee Fiddler. Or maybe David A. $mith. But Stebbins... welcome back to the ring, Brett, huh?
[Brett pauses, stretching out his bum wheel in the process.] BR: Wilczak and the suits have some confidence in me, or they're just seeing if I can back up my big words. Either way, I really have no choice but to go out there and prove that I ain't just full of hot air and meaningless promises.
And Keith, that's exactly what I intend to do. [Rising to his feet, Brett begins to slowly walk through the room, leading us past a wall enshrining the history of his family in the sport. No stopping to fill us in, though. Which is probably for the best.]
BR: I probably couldn't have drawn a better opponent. Stebbins is about as "old school" as it gets these days. No nonsense, no flair for the dramatic, just a brass tacks approach inside the ring. All business... just like I've been taught all my life.
And that's the thing that's gonna make our match fun, Keith. Won't be any attempts to leap into the spotlight. Won't be any preoccupation with getting in good with the crowds. It's just going to be two men with one thing in mind... winning the match, period. And I don't want it any other way.
Yeah, I ain't been in the ring much over the last three years. In fact, my match at Ultimate Gamble was it. But that don't worry me, and I sure as hell don't plan on using that as a crutch or an excuse. I'm a wrestler, a competitor, just like Keith Stebbins... and if I climb into that ring, I throw any possible excuse out.
[A pause, a muscle twitch of the face. Real exciting stuff, I know.] BR: What do I expect from our match, Keith? Simply put, I expect you to take me to the limit. I expect you'll re-introduce me to what it's like being in the middle of the ropes. I expect that if I let up one little bit, you'll kick my butt from pillar to post. I expect you'll be no less than a competitor.
So Keith? Don't expect anything less from me. Expect Brett Rogers to get in there and give one hundred and ten percent. Expect Brett Rogers to do everything he can to kick your tail all around the ring. Expect Brett
Rogers to try to make a name for himself at your expense. Why? Because that's what this game's all about, isn't it? And you know what?
I wouldn't have it any other way. [Fade.] * * * * * [Fade in to... a Target! That's right, Target, the place with annoying commercials and all sorts of suburban yet oh-so-ghetto goodness. We're looking at a cash register now, with a pretty little thing named Vanessa.

Pfft!
MoonGirl... zonked out pfft! EggBaby... giggle giggle pfft! Agent Chambersssss... ssss pfft! The Robfatah... fat ass pfft! Jamie Randall... punk bitch pfft! Jamie Randall, let's talk about Ultimate Gamble 2 for a second. Now, granted, I put myself through a table 'cuz I really didn't see a point in the match anymore. I mean, you'd stuck yourself in it figuring you were the shit or something, actually... why you were in it, I don't know.

Randall. Well, I haven't beat the piss out of someone in a long damn time. In fact, it's been so damn long, I don't even wanna beat the piss out of him. Why should I? Beating the piss out of someone is so overrated, so 90's... there needs to be something new and enjoyable for me to do in the 2000's.
And that's why I'm not going to beat the piss out of Jamie Randall... oh no... I'm gonna beat the Good God Fuckery outta him! [~!] G: What exactly is "Good God Fuckery", you ask? Well, it's simple. No more fuckin' for Jamie in fear that once his kids pop out of whoever's snatch, there'll instantly be a foot in their ass thanks to me!

* * * * * [The soft glow of a single candle flickers as the camera zooms in closely. The wick burns away, like time passing by, slow. A small fume of smoke trails off the end of the candle, leading to somewhere, leading to nowhere.
Behind the candle, a presence appears, moving in towards the candle, at a methodical pace. The candle allows for a hint of the presence to be seen, and it's unlike anything many of you have ever seen. His face, black, black as night, black as hell, seems chiseled out of stone, hard, solid. His head, topped with nothing but the skin the Lord blessed him with is covered with a soft glow from the candle. His massive chest, rippled, chiseled, absorbs the light of the candle, softly. His eyes gleem at the camera, as he slowly comes over the candle light]
[A soft, angelic, yet deeply troubled voice comes over the candle] "Let the words of my mouth and the meditation of my heart Be acceptable in Your sight, O Lord, my strength and my redeemer." [The voice, is that of a man troubled by his past, troubled to fight the demons that haunt him, yet willing to put it all aside, to spread the message]
Shamir Wallace: Those that do not know of my existence, you will soon be blessed. For I come from a life of poverty, a life of pain, a life of never ending hardships, and I still stand, tall, and strong, in front of you all.
For I come from the streets of a place much worse then hell, much harsher then any wrath the devil himself could have handed down. And through it all...
I still stand tall. For, I come to this place, the mecca for many of this sports greats, and I come to spread the message. I come to enlighten the lost, and troubled souls that deem necessary their actions against the will of their maker, all to gain false success. True success comes from within, and I have found that.
True success comes from a man much stronger then any of your titles, or your "glorious fights". _He_ has fought the good fight more then any one, and he did it for us...
For me. And now, I must do it for him. [Wallace slowly closes his eyes, and contemplates the darkness that lies within him] Shamir Wallace: For I have seen the darkest pits of hell, and I found them to be enlightening. I have seen the dark lord himself, and now, I shall seek redemption for such a sight, and I shall do so through all of you. For those that _need_ to see the light, well, you shall, you shall soon. For without the light, you are nothing but mere corpses, walking around with no chance for hope, nor redemption.
Many of you brag about the fights, about the "wars", yet you have no comprehension of what a war truly is. You know nothing about staring into the eyes of a demon, and slowly wilting away to his powers. You know nothing of seeing utter dispair, knowing there is nothing you can do to solve it.
You know nothing of the feeling of cold steel bars, sheltering your hate, sheltering your chance at redemption.
And so I come to show you all. [Wallace peers at the camera, and then above] Shamir Wallace: He will show me the strength to carry on the message, to carry on his word. I have given my soul to him, and for that, I must seek redemption through spreading _his_ word. Some may resist, and they will continue to find themselves lost, lost without a trace of hope. Others will resist through violence, for those, I speak to them, and I speak the reality. If you do so choose to lash out at the words of God, then I shall bring his fury, and I shall do so with strength that you cannot comprehend.
I shall lash back, at those that lash foward, and I shall show you that I still remember my demons, and their violent ways.
[Wallace peers over to his "CTN" tattoo, a harsh reminder of his past. "Compton Thug Niggaz", a time of violence and death that he wished had never happened] Shamir Wallace: For I have walked your path, and I have seen the darkness that you all find through some alterior manner. And now, now I shall show you the light...
By any means necessary. [With that, Wallace, slowly withdraws from the candle, and backs away from the light, out of sight from the candle. The candle continues to burn, burn without hesititation, for it serves as a reminder. No matter what the conditions, it must follow it's purpose, much like Wallace]
