Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Connecticut Wrestling Promotions announced itself on 11 February 1999 in a taped promo staged in an empty arena, its own on-screen president, “Superman” Shawn Rosenthal, calling a worldwide open tournament whose winner would become its New England Champion and advance to a joint pay-per-view with OWWA, unifying the belt against OWWA's own World Heavyweight Champion.

A second announcement, ten days later, opened two more cross-federation tournaments — an Extreme singles bracket fought under falls-count-anywhere rules and a Tag Team bracket — each capped at three entries per promotion, with $100,000 and a contract to join as champion on offer to each winner.

Its first televised broadcast, Shockwave, aired 5 March 1999 from the New Haven Coliseum, hosted by commentators Jeremy Bane, son of its own on-screen commissioner, and “Mean” Mike Henderson. The show ran entirely on introductory vignettes rather than completed matches: Jim “The Clan” Cambridge, cradling a newborn on camera, cast his rivals Iron Spike, Ghost, and Razor as a cancer on the promotion he compared to his own son's recovery; Raid, a self-described six-year veteran, aired an old grudge with Razor while vowing to become wrestling's hardcore standard-bearer; and Ghost delivered a graveyard monologue naming Razor as the first wrestler he had been sent to collect for an unnamed master.

The broadcast closed on the debut of a tag team billed as the Oriental Alliance, whose manager, Mr. Naguta, had one member kick through a locker door and the other headbutt through a wooden bench for a backstage reporter before promising a run of tag team death matches.

No completed match and no further document survive after that 5 March 1999 broadcast.
