Spring Classic — Roleplay

TripleV Flasssh (L-Cup flash)

15 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Bonjour RSPWF. After a good 6 years at this game, I figure it was about time I post something on this group. What better place to start than L-Cup, of which my character Vile Vince Viper was set to participate.
Given the limited notice of the first L-Cup flash, I figured I would phone it in (1-800 collect as it were), putting little to no effort in as the turn out of my much busier competition, would most likely make the first flash a throw away anyway. However, in light of recent
L-Cup events, it has made me look at this "interview" in a new light. With Mr. [name withheld]'s wonderful acceptance of Viper's scrupulous moral system, the last words spoken by Viper are given far more subtexts then they could ever originally intended (those understandable not wanting to read the whole thing can easily spot the line).
I have felt it for years, but its nice to see Vile Vince is finally an inspirational figure. :)
-Geoff *********** [Our scene opens with a black and white crane shot of the city of Toronto, home of Spring Classics. The crane is located between two run down buildings, so a quarter of the lower screen has a fire escape in the frame. Despite this amateur looking establishing shot, all the essentials are there, the CN Tower, the Sky dome, a number of clean looking sky rises. The average Spring Classics viewer will have very little little doubt why Toronto is the _capital_ of Canada. Though they might wonder why a large event of this magnitude would be held in such a cold, desolate wasteland. Before the viewers can draw parallels between the area's disposition and The Scrayper's personality, perhaps changing the channel to avoid hearing his nasal voice, our camera crew attempts to remove the historical landmarks.]
[Panning down the first rung of the fire escape, the landscape is replaced by a filthy brick wall, which is accentuated by some marvellously tacky attire on a clothesline. These outfits are so hideous they would make someone as flamboyant as Cesar Risley blush.
Leaving the safety of the fire escape, the camera uses a phone line as a visual consistent, panning slowly to the left. Passing over an alleyway, the camera arrives at the next building, stopping at an open window. A window which most of the viewers, even a plebeian like Jeff
Keenan, should figure out is the location of our actual interview.] [A snake wipe takes us into a small, dingy, office. A drastic change in lighting and picture quality reveal this place to be shot in a different area, and indeed with a different crew, than the previous action. At a much higher quality, the crane shot of Toronto was obviously stock footage while this office, at least in part, is real.
The average viewer might infer that the crane shot would have cost too much money, or that the thought of stepping foot in Toronto is almost as unappealing to our Protagonist as locking up with the grotesquely overrated Rosco Riggins.]
[The office itself has poor lighting, which is probably a good thing, as it makes it more difficult spot its countless other imperfections through the darkness. Although, much like a Derek Irvin match, there are so many glaring flaws, it’s hard to miss all of them. The walls are painted a puke looking shade of green, and feature more cracks than Lord Lionheart’s work rate. Out of these holes stare large red eyes, undoubtedly belonging to our hero’s little people entourage. Everything in the room tilts slightly to the right, as the floorboards are as warped as our humble protagonist’s mind. A television sits in the far corner of the room, on a stack of ancient wrestling magazines, displaying highlights of the subject's career. In the centre of the room is a mahogany desk, behind which a figure sits with his back to the camera in a black, leather, recliner.]
[Lying back in his chair, an elderly, physical wreck known in some circles as the king of snakes, puffs away at a Cuban cigar. Vile
Vince Viper wears a black, snakeskin robe, over a red, satin shirt. Some yellow serpentine contact lenses obscure the pinks of his sinister albino eyes. With his right hand, Vile occasionally knocks the ashes of his cigar into a coffee mug on his desk reading "#1 Dad"
while playing around with a nutcracker, twirling it around his fingers, with his left. Looking at this abomination one thing jumps into the viewers minds, if Brian Taylor ever makes it past Rosco
Riggins, which he will not, is Taylor ever screwed. The freak show known commonly as TripleV makes no attempt to acknowledge the cameramans existence, perfectly content in his own little world.]
[At this point the viewer is probably wondering exactly who this self-important character is. Not realizing the magnitude of this self-proclaimed wrestling god who appears before them. He is not in
NEO, GWC/MPW would not touch him with a three-foot pole, a pole taller than there average wrestler, even ICW would not return his phone calls. WHO DOES THIS OLD, SHORT, FAT, PIGMENTLESS MAN THINK HE IS!?!
Why exactly is he a lock for winning the L-Cup? In short, who the hell is he? Perfectly good questions. So why isn't Vile answering them? What is his game? Is he so sure of victory over Tommy
Lionheart that he can waste this precious airtime? Does he really think that little of his competition? Why does the viewer get the feeling he would burst into laughter if you called wrestlers of
Riggins calibre his competition? Vile is plotting something. Something truly villainous. Something so intricate that it takes his whole brain. Which isn't saying much. Or is it? Cutting a promo at this point must seem like a lost cause, as Vile just sits there. He could be saying a thousand things about his past with Tommy Lionheart.
Their encounters in the NWWA. Instead there is silence. Perhaps feeling that such material is so beneath him he can safely leave
Lionheart that bone. Bad mouth the no talent punk? Vile appears to be saving his breath.]
[Vile drops the Cuban in his #1 Dad mug, perhaps signalling a change of pace. Perhaps not. Vile opens a drawer on his desk, pulling the contents across his desk. Vile then proceeds to pick up one of the walnuts, placing it in his nutcracker. Does the walnut represent
Derek Irvin? Does Vile really think he can emasculate a character with as many BSCW accomplishments as Irvin has? Viewers must quickly throw this theory out the window, as Vile seems to be having some problems cracking the nut, and Irvin is not one of the competitors that Vile will have problems cracking. Oh no, if anything this nut belongs to Cesar Risley who Vile will meet in the finals. It would be a lot easier to establish these borders if VVV would just say something, alias nothing. It is just fortunate he did not have a hankering for poultry or some form of produce.]
[Reaching back into the drawer, Vile pulls out a large mallet, which he starts slamming down on the walnut. For the most part he misses it, hitting the desk. Though the loud smashing sounds can get a bit annoying, they are a welcomed change from the silence, and vastly superior to his hate filled lisp. Finally getting lucky, Vile hits the walnut dead on, sending pieces of it scattering to the floor. The force of the blow also sends a small trophy plummeting to his warped floorboards. Shooting down faster than the hopes of The Scrayper when he found out the rest of the talent competing for the L-Cup. In the time it has taken for Vile to crack the walnut the best of tape in the corner has shown him win 6 world titles. Why would a man, or snake, as decorated as him be competing to re-carve a name for himself?
Clearly a sore subject, as Vile is not saying.] [Standing up Vile reaches around the corner of the desk. Picking up his trophy, Vile blows some dust off of it. The more acute Spring
Classics viewers, grasping at straws to entertain themselves, are probably wondering if this is not some pot shot at Jeff Keenan. After all, Keenan started his career as the Golden Boy, only to reinvent himself as Quicksilver. Anyone with a basic knowledge of metallurgy or the stock market recognizes that silver is a step down from gold.
Perhaps, as this trophy Vile holds suggests, after Keenan's humiliating loss to the Original 3-V, he will be forced to change his nickname once more to the "Bronze Bitch." A little too far fetched?
The Spring Classics viewers don't seem to think so. Why haven't they turned off the channel? This is unbearable! Why, we have already lost so much time, whatever Vile is building towards must be
BRILLIANT.] [Placing the award back down on the desk, Vile sits back down. He looks up at the camera, the first sign of recognition since this
"flash" started. He begins to open his mouth, finally, THIS HAD BETTER BE WORTH IT! Only to stop, looking back down at his shattered walnut particles. Bastard. As it becomes more and more apparent that this static shot, along with lack of dialog, is to give his opposition no material with which they can work, the viewers become more and more anxious. He is going to say something is he not? He'd better. What the hell is he waiting for? Damn is he cocky. Does he think we will just wait here? Is he really that good? No one is THAT good.
Silence.] [I am giving this crap fifteen more seconds before I change the channel. I cannot believe I put so much time indulging the whims of this sick fuck. But I can't. If I leave now, I will never hear the line. He has to speak eventually. ...In order to justify all this dead air he has no choice but to give us one of the greatest, most quotable lines ever. The kind of line that EVERYONE will go around spouting off! Or at least an interview, which is as equally long winded. What does this prove? By this point the momentum of Spring
Classics has come to a complete halt, which is something that was hoped would not happen until Riggins and Taylor walked into the ring.
FUCK! If they weren't going to give us any voice-overs or action at least they could throw on some music. Damn.]
["Horney" by the Sex Krazed Superstars inexplicably plays in the background. The perfect theme to the perfect interview.]
[That is it. Nothing. The final straw! Silence. I AM turning this crap off. He can't treat professional wrestling fans like, like, wrestling fans! I figure he would want the L-Cup. Why else would he appear at Spring Classics? Bastard will not even tell us why he wants to cup, or why he was going to win it.]
Vile Vince Viper: ...And thatsss the bottom line, caussse Vile Vince sssaid ssso!!! [That was it? The payoff? The one line universally shunned by the e-wrestling community? Vile sits there will an irritatingly smug sense of satisfaction plastered across his wrinkled, pale face; having used this large forum to demonstrate the textbook version of a lousy interview. He could have used this large venue to make a point, to get recognized, or at least seen by the RSPWF big wigs he has spent his life seeking the approval of. No. Not Vile, he was too good for
that. *He is too good to even be original.* If the thousands of viewers who are right now either choking on their own rage, or pretzels, could sue Spring Classics, they probably would. Sadly, twelve minutes have already been lost, wasted on this sad little man.
Contemplating acts of cruelty previously unknown to man, at least until Vile carries them out on his opposition on Night One, would only waste precious more seconds. BASTARD. With any luck Vile is the last person to throw in his two cents, as a large portion of television sets which had been receiving this signal, are simultaneously turned off. You have not turned off your set yet? Well, Vile plans on sitting at his desk congratulating himself for the next hour. He has not given the cameraman any sign to cut the feed, so unless Spring
Classics decides to cut it off, VVV's interview isn't going anywh...* -SIGNAL LOST-
