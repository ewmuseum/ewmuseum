"Higher" by Creed begins to play as Robert Jackson walks to the ring where a huge podium and a red carpet have been set up. The spotlight hits Robert, wearing a tux in the middle of the ring.)
Robert Jackson: TOKYO, JAPAN...WHAT DID YOU THINK OF THE DWO???? (The fans stand up in cheer chanting "DWO, DWO!") Robert Jackson: Unfortunately, the Deadly Wrestling Organization has seen it's last match, and what a last match it was. The Deadly Wrestling
Organization opened up a few years ago. Since that time, it's been a federation full of creativity, great wrestlers, great talent, and HISTORIC cards. In about 30 minutes the DWO will close it's doors. But before that happens, we want to honor a select few who made the Deadly Wrestling
Organization what it was in the short time it was around. WELCOME TO THE DEADLY WRESTLING ORGANIZATION HALL OF FAME INDUCTIONS! (The fans cheer.) Robert Jackson: It's only right that we kick off the Hall of Fame by inducting the DWO's First World Champion. Please welcome to the arena...and give a HUGE CONGRATULATIONS TO......
A P O L L O ! ! ! ! ! ! ! ! ! ! ! ! ! (Apollo is wearing all black, with a bright blue sportcoat. He is very serious as he turns to the podium)
Apollo: Well, I certainly have mixed feelings about this moment. This certainly is an honor to be inducted into the federation where I won my first e-wrestling championship, and yet I am sad to see it go. Outside of my home federation where I pretty much have the run of the place, the DWO
(and formerly the APW) were my first chances to take on top competition, and I was fortunate enough to be able to win both the World Heavyweight
Championship and the Tag Team Championship. A bit later, in an attempt to save the DWO, I went into the front office. It was a place I didn't belong, but it was my attempt to do the best I could for the federation I loved. Finally, I had to step back and wonder if this fed would survive. But it came back.
I enjoyed a decent run on the now less-frequent cards, including a continuation of my legendary battles with Sick Rick. Though he tended to enjoy the upper hand, I am satisfied that we both gave 110%, and I learned and got better each time.
Then my least-proud moment, when I had to turn heel, again to try to save the federation. I'm surprised that my fans didn't realize what I was doing, but I think everyone understands now. When Joan Osborne was going to close down this federation, I was going to do anything inside the law to stop her. Now
I realize I should have taken the law into my own hands -- but instead I joined a band of thugs destroying everyone in our path trying to get after her.
But the DWO won. The DWO is back one last time for a celebration of what we have been over the last few years. Whether I like them or I don't, Sick
Rick, J'Ster, and the rest of the competitors in this great fed are among the true greats, once you can look past all the fake rankings, surveys, and polls that are floating out there. I will carry on... maybe not now, maybe I will step away for a while, but I will resurface somewhere. But no matter where I end up, I will always be a better competitor and a better person for what I did here.
And whatever you think of them personally, every one of you should be on your feet applauding Mike Bilodeau and Shawn Roy for these several years of a great fed. Goodnight, everyone. (The fans in Tokyo are all standing cheering for Apollo, who walks to the side of the ring with a tear in his eye.)
Robert Jackson: Apollo said it right, the superstars of the Deadly Wrestling Organization have always been the best, regardless of the fake rankings and surveys on the internet. Apollo, the Deadly Wrestling
Organization would like to thank you for your contribution to this great organization. We wish you all the luck in your future endeavors.
(The fans cheer loudly.) Robert Jackson: The next inductees could not make it to Japan tonight. They are the MERCHANTS OF CHAOS! They were one of the first to hold the Deadly Wrestling Organization Tag Team Championships. A lot of people were skeptical about their worthiness of holding such a prestigious title. A lot of people mocked the Deadly Wrestling Organization for having such silly chamions. But, SHAWN ROY and MIKE BILODEAU saw something in them.
They were mocked, but soon the Merchants of Chaos proved every one wrong, and the Merchants took the entire tag team world by storm. Everyone wanted them on their cards. The MERCHANTS were THE MEN in tag team wrestling.
They have since retired from wrestling, and the Deadly Wrestling Organization would like to thank them for their dedicated service to the DWO. Thank you Merchants, good luck, and God bless! (The fans in attendance cheer loudly.) Robert Jackson: Our next inductee has always been at the forefront of controversy as was seen tonight during the Final Fight Pay Per View.
Please welcome, and give a big congratulations to..... D A R K V A N I T Y! ! ! ! ! ! ! ! (Dark Vanity walks to the podium wearing a black Armani suit. He takes his mask off, and begins to talk to the fans in Tokyo.)
Dark Vanity: This is possibly the hardest thing I will ever have to do. Tonight, I announce my retirement from the sport of wrestling. I'm going to take some much needed time to spend with my newly wed wife, Ali. We just found out yesterday that Ali is pregnant, and I intend to be there with my wife and kid or kids every step of the way. I want to surely thank the Deadly Wrestling Organization for letting me into it's hall of fame.
But more importantly, I'd like to take this time to thank everyone in the wrestling community who ever helped me in any way. Thank you for standing by me. Good luck, and Godbless!
(Dark Vanity stands next to Apollo at the side of the ring. The fans cheer loudly.) Robert Jackson: More congratulations on the new marriage, Dark Vanity. The Deadly Wrestling Organization certainly thanks you for your time and effort, and hard work toward making the DWO a huge success.
(The crowd cheers, they start chanting "DWO, DWO.") Robert Jackson: Unfortunately, our next inductee could not make the trip to Japan, he is enjoying his retirement from wrestling with his family.
That man is KYLE KAMIKAZE! He caused a legal stir over the Deadly Wrestling Organization Championship last year, and has always been a true legend, and a great performer. He and Rick Styles headlined the New
Alliance over in the UEW. Kyle has been a true professional to deal with, and a very creative wrestler. The Deadly Wrestling Organization would like to take this time to honor Kyle Kamikaze!
(The crowd cheers loudly. The "DWO" Chants continue.) Robert Jackson: We couldn't have a Hall of Fame without this next inductee. He is the man who is known as the SICKEST CHAMPION EVER...please welcome and congratulate....
S I C K R I C K ! ! ! ! ! ! ! ! ! ! ! ! (Sick Rick walks up to the podium, dressed in the best he has. Not a tux, but a decent suit and tie. He looks somewhat ill-prepared amidst all the flash bulbs that envelope him, and looks to Mother Madeline, who smiles away his nervousness.) Sick Rick: I have a lot of things to say, as usual, I guess, but I'll try to be brief. First, of all, I'm going to try to thank everyone (except for two notable exceptions who I'll get back to later) at the outset. I'd like to thank
Mike Bilodeau for taking a chance on me, hiring me despite (or perhaps because of) my record. It was the first 'real' job I've ever had, and maybe it made me think that work wasn't so bad. It was definitely harder work than being a street thug, and infinitely more worth it. I want to thank J'ster for all he taught me when we trained together--and Rick Styles, who helped my career out in ways I (& he) sometimes didn't realize. He probably doesn't know how many hours of tape I watched of him, and how many of his moves I copied when I first started out. & I've heard from reporters and those in the know that there were times when he spoke up on my behalf to promoters, times when he would refuse a high-ranking midcard match knowing that I was next in line to have it. I want to thank Coach Roderick for helping me refine my technique, and everyone ever affiliated with the DWO for being a part of this. it's been a long, strange trip, definitely worth it. I came into this business with a lot of anger pent-up and sometimes (often) expressed at the wrong people. Now I know the difference between a champion's intensity and a challenger's frustration. And so, to all of the announcers, ring announcers, referees, fans, managers, and anyone else who I took out or verbally maligned...I don't regret it (it was what I had to do at the time) but know that it was nothing personal, and I've matured since then. Like I said, it's been a long strange trip. When I started in the DWO, it wasn't even the DWO. I was a complete unknown, brashly challenging anyone and everyone I had ever heard of before; I'll still take on anybody, and even now I'm still hungry. but it was different--then I was doing it out of insecurity. Now it's out of something else. A general contentment, I can't explain it. I know I'm the best, and I'm willing to show it, but I don't have to prove it.
I just know. All good things must come to an end, and so the DWO, and probably my career, are coming to an end tonight. I'll probably cry after my match...right now
I'm too focused on it to think about the nostalgia and sadness of this event. I'll never forget it, though. How could I? My body reminds me of every day. I remember J'ster, our dueling switchblades. At the time we were making wrestling history and not even knowing it. Probably the matches that defined the DWO as being Deadly. Or me vs. Apollo, the classic "evil"-vs.-"good" confrontations. Maybe you want to think about me & Gangsta going at it like the two street thugs we were? Or me & Kyle Kamikaze, two champions challenging death every time we took our fight to the top rope. I remember waking up not remembering my match with Dave Stewart, because he dropped me on my head on the concrete so hard...the only thing I remembered was that I won. or my match with the
Prophet, where he picked me up for one last move, and I looked into his eyes and saw my no quit...he hesitated for a second, and I wrapped up the win. I remember almost every punch, almost every ring announcer, the way almost every match felt. & so, I want to say thank you to everyone. And now, for the two I most need to especially thank. First of all, Mother Madeline, you know how I feel about you. You have been my calm when I needed it, my fire when I needed it, my anchor when I needed it, my mom when I needed it. You came into this business as green as I was, but you almost seemed to pick it up quicker. You have an incredibly innate talent for reading people, and you saw the potential power and goodness in me back when I was getting into knife fights on mean streets. I love you, and I'm looking forward to the day when our jobs no longer distract us from each other.
And to all my fans, who saw the good in me despite the fact I hated them, who cheered me simply because they saw how hard I was trying and appreciated how much I was on my own man...I must extend my deepest gratitude. I'm not usually vulnerable like this, but I gotta say (at this point tears are almost in his eyes) that I didn't have many friends or family growing up, I never knew love...it was all hardness and coldness and kill-or-be-killed. But I discovered that wanting to live is a stronger force than not wanting to die, and that fighting out of positivity is much harder, ultimately more effective, riskier, but DEADLIER than fighting out of negativity and weakness. I wish I could shake each and every one of youse's hands. You've been just too good to me .And so tonight, when we're going out, I'm fighting for myself. And for you. I want to give the DWO fans one last way to remember me, to remember me as the conquering champion I came in wnating to and worked so hard to be. It's only your last match that counts, and you are all going to remember my last one. I'm going out on top. The DWO is going to end with a bang, not a whimper. And I'm going to end a champion, not a quitter. (Rick walks down from the podium, forceful determination glistening in his eyes, and takes his seat, shaking hands with the other Hall-of-Famers on hand, and sharing a knowing style with Rick Styles.) Robert Jackson: Sick Rick, you've always been the FATHER of Hardcore in the DWO, and you probably molded the DWO into what it was with your intensity and devotion. You lost your DWO TITLE tonight, but you will always be remembered here in the DWO as "THE MAN." Congratulations Sick Rick! (The fans cheer very loudly. They continue their "DWO" Chants.) Robert Jackson: Two more inductees that will complete this Hall of Fame induction ceremony are of course, RAZI and JAWAAD MAHMOOD! They couldn't make it here tonight, but the former NICW Presidents will always hold a special place in DWO History. They were tag team champions on several occasions, probably setting the standards for all great tag teams in wrestling. They were also great friends of everyone at the DWO Offices.
They are enjoying their retirement in Canada. THANK YOU MAHMOODS! (The crowd cheers loudly. The "DWO" Chant gets louder and louder.) Robert Jackson: Our next inductee left a little early tonight. J'ster. I have been instructed to tear this card, but I just can't. J'ster will always hold a place in the DWO. He's been a champion. He's been a fighter. He's been a spokesman. J'ster and Sick Rick defined Hardcore when wrestling was entering the "Extreme Revolution." J'ster now holds the
UEW Presidency, and we can only say good luck to him! (The crowd cheers very loudly.) RJ: Our next inductee first made sparks back in October of 1997, when he joined a tiny farm fed called the Mainstream Wrestling Federation. Within months, he was recognized, not only as one of the bigger draws of the MWF, but also as one of e-wrestling's fastest rising stars. However, his raw talent was only truly recognized by the e-wrestling community when he finished third in an epic battle royal during the
DWO's Deadly Draw. Since that time, he has defeated virtually all who has dared come in his path, earning his spot as one of e-wrestling's finest, as well as a true DWO legend. Ladies and gentlemen, here is.
.N I C K " T H E N I G H T H A W K " L E S H E N N ! ! ! !.. (Nick stands and walks up to the podium. Wearing black jeans and a Toronto Maple Leafs home jersey, Nick looks out of place surrounded by the sea of tuxedos on stage. He grins and shakes Bilodeau's hand, accepting a large plaque. The two briefly pose for photographs before Nick takes the plaque and moves to the mic.)
Nick: Huh.In the two years plus I've been in e-wrestling, I never thought I'd be inducted to a hall of fame. I must admit, it feels kind of weird.
Where to start? I guess this is mostly for [name withheld], who was the first to give me the chance to roam free. I remember my first day in the MWF. I must've flooded him with about a thousand e-mails, asking about what moves I could do, what a strat entailed, and what RP stood for.
(Light laughter from the crowd.) Nick: I learned pretty quickly, thanks to [name withheld]. Whenever I needed advice, I turned to him. He gave me advice about how to go about competing in more than one fed at a time, about the dos and don'ts of flashing. Without [name withheld], I'd probably be some nobody simming in the TWCWF. Thanks.
(The crowd applauds.) Nick: This is also for one of my closest friends in the e-wrestling community, Clint Fletcher, a.k.a.
Fletch. Fletch and I first crossed paths back in the WSW, a farm fed for the NeCW. I won that belt on the first night, and it folded soon after. So on I went to the big fed, and that one folded, too. Somehow, through all that, Fletch and I became pretty damn good friends. We talked about family, about the differences between Canada and New Zealand, and just about life.
Fletch was also there to help me through my problems in wrestling. He had a way to make things clear.
He also gave me my first taste at being a "suit." I remember when he approached me with the idea of a
Japanese-based fed, and asked me if I wanted to be a part of it. I jumped at the chance, but as a booker and writer instead of a wrestler. Soon after, I was the vice president. Together, we made Gunyro Pro the best damned fed on the net, bar none. I still look at
G-Pro's pay per view, Cold Blood, as the best of all time. I absolutely had a blast there. So thanks, Fletch. Thanks for giving me advice, thanks for teaching me how to write my first-ever match, thanks for unleashing the hardcore fanatic in me, and most of all, thanks for being a friend.
(More light applause.) Nick: I'd also like to thank Mike Bilodeau, our very own man of the hour. When I first heard of the DWO, I was still in the MWF. [name withheld] suggested we all join, and I figured it would be a good opportunity to learn more about those UEW boys so I signed up. As a result,
I had the greatest match of my e-wrestling career. That thirty-man battle royal was huge for me. I was in the ring with guys I had only heard of before on the
RSPWF 200, guys like "Lone Wolf" Brody Thunder, Rick Styles, and Sick Rick. I stepped into a ring full of legends, and I outlasted all but two of them. It was a huge accomplishment for me and my career. It told me I was doing some good stuff after all. So thanks, Mike.
Thanks for giving a rookie a chance. (More applause.) Nick: I should also thank a man who helped shape The Nighthawk into what he is today. That man, of course, is Alex Martinez. From the very beginning, he and I have been at each other's throats. I'm not sure what it is about us that works so well, but he and I are just magic when we feud.
It was feuding with Alex where I really learned how to RP. It was feuding with Alex where I learned how to strat. Hell, it was feuding with Alex where I learned what e-wrestling was really all about. And although he definitely has a winning record over me, I've learned that sometimes you can get just as much from losing as you can from winning. I don't mind that I've lost so much to Alex, because I always had fun, and we always put on a great show. Alex, thanks for no-selling so many of my damn moves.
(The crowd laughs.) Nick: No, seriously. Thank you for being such a great friend on and off the road. You were never too selfish to lay down for an angle, always willing to give my ideas a try. I hope you had as much fun as I did.
(The crowd applauds.) Nick: And last but not least, I want to thank my father, Jacob Leshenn. Dad, you were the reason why I became a wrestler. All I ever wanted to do was to be half as good as you. I knew that'd be mighty big boots to fill, but you were always there to push me, make sure I never gave up. When I wanted to train as an aerial wrestler, you were the only one who didn't laugh. You helped me train, you helped me perfect my craft.
Looking back, you were the single biggest influence on my entire life. You were the best role model a kid could have. Even when I reached the peak of my fame in the UEW, you were there to keep my ego in check. When things seemed rough, you were there with some words of advice, and with just one phone call, you made everything right again. Dad, this plaque is for you. I love you.
(The crowd stands and applauds for several minutes. Jacob Leshenn stands in the crowd and takes a small bow before grinning at his son and sitting back down.
Nick raises the plaque above his head one last time before making his way back to his seat.)
Robert Jackson: Nick Leshenn, I know for sure that the Deadly Wrestling Organization will always been apologetic and ashamed that it never gave such a talented individual such as yourself the DWO WORLD TITLE! Tonight you proved to us even more that you are a great performer, and one of the best wrestlers ever to grace the DWO RINGS!
(The crowd cheers very loudly. They are still standing. The "DWO" Chant gets very loud.) Robert Jackson: Our next inductee has just left the arena, he has to get back to the United States quickly to spend time with his family. That man just won the main event, that man was PROFESSOR PAYNE! Payne hasn't been in the Deadly Wrestling Organization for too long, but in that short time, he became the Champion and a Legend! He became a dubious figure lately, but the DWO knows what a true legend he is. GOOD LUCK AND GOD BLESS
PROFESSOR PAYNE! (The fans cheer very loudly.) Robert Jackson: Our next inductee has disappeared, but will always be a star here in the DWO!
VOICE: Hey Wait a minute! (Mini Robski and Tommy Lang walk to the ring. They slide under the bottom rope and push Robert Jackson away from the podium.)
MINI ROBSKI: I am here, and I AM SO HONORED TO BE INDUCTED INTO THE DWO HALL OF FAME! I know the Deadly Wrestling Organization is closing, and I know I'll never have an opportunity to get my revenge on some stars so I
MAKE A PROMISE TO EVERYONE! I am coming to a federation near you with my manager, Tommy Lang. I AM TAKING OVER THE WORLD! Thank you.
(Mini Robski and Tommy Lang walk out of the ring, they run through the crowd and leave the arena.)
Robert Jackson: This HALL OF FAME would not be complete without the TOP DOG! Rick Styles had to catch an early flight, but he sent these words. "I am honored and speechless that I have finally earned something I can be proud of." Rick Styles, the DWO Salutes you, and all your hard work and dedication. Rick has always been the "Icon" here in the DWO. If you didn't respect Rick Styles, you probably weren't that good at what you did for a living. Rick Styles COMMANDS respect. We all try to be him. We all want to be him. Rick Styles is the man that we can truly call....the HULK
HOGAN of the DWO! Thank you Rick, and God Bless! (The fans cheer extremely loud. The "DWO" Chant has not ended.) Robert Jackson: Our next inductee proved tonight that he is one of the greatest wrestlers to grace ANY wrestling ring. He had humble beginnings here in the DWO, but tonight showed us as he FIREBOMBED NICK LESHENN through the Deadly Cell, that he is truly a MAIN EVENTER here in the DWO!
HE IS..... A L E X M A R T I N E Z ! ! ! ! ! ! ["Dammit" by Blink 182 blares over the loudspeakers, and the fans erupt into a large pop, as the seven foot Alex Martinez makes his way to ringside. He is dressed rather simply tonight, wearing a pair of black jeans, and the red and gold "Hot Stuff" t-shirt recently designed by the
UEW. Tucked under his arm is a large wooden plaque. He walks slowly down the aisle, soaking in the cheers of the fans, before he climbs the steps to the ring, and moves towards its center. Motioning to the ring announcer,
Martinez is tossed a microphone, and begins his speech.] AM: So, I roll in here this afternoon, and thee is good old Mikey Bilodeau, handing me some plaque, with my name on it, and saying "here, go make a speech tonight."
[Martinez chuckles, and holds the plaque up, looking at it for a moment] AM: It says "Alex Martinez: for service to the DWO, and for constantly entertaining some of the most demanding fans in the world." I guess that's true. What do you all think?
[The crowd pops loudly] AM: But hell, I ain't never been one to give speeches. I was never one to accept awards, ya know? I'm just a guy, who beats the hell out of people.
And I guess, if they want to give me some kind of reward for being good at it, then hell, I'll accept it.
So, Mikey, the rest of DWO, its an honor, or something. I mean, where else but in a fucked up place like DWO would Alex Martinez actually get into the hall of fame? I've been here for a long damn time, and no matter what, it was always fun. I mean, when I first came here, I was just some mid card scrub curtain jerker. I fought Brass Knuckles, and actually won. I don't think I've won in the DWO since..
[Martinez chuckles.] AM: But it was in DWO that my rivalry with Alex Extreme and Magnus Colby was born. And it was here that I truly began to strut my stuff, and become the dominant force I am now. So, thanks for the opportunity.
Now, strike up the music! [Martinez's music starts up again, as the fans roar their approval. Martinez moves back behind the curtain, the pop dying out only many long minutes after Martinez has left]
Robert Jackson: Our last inductees are the men who made this all possible. THEY ARE S H A W N R O Y and M I K E B I L O D E A U (Mike Bilodeau stands up and walks to the podium.) Mike Bilodeau: Tonight, I'm afraid I couldn't induct everyone who ever participated in the DWO. Lord knows I wanted to, but we wouldn't have the time. Shawn Roy couldn't be here tonight, he's off at a Metallica concert somewhere in England. NOTHING could distract Shawn from a Metallica concert. I don't have too much to say, I feel bad. I certainly want to thank everyone who ever made the DWO what it was. I don't want to make this a long Academy Awards speech. I just have one last thing to say. The
DWO is now closed, and I don't know where I'll end up, but I'm glad that I ended up here in the DWO with the people that were here, and I'll always remember everyone that ever said, "Mike Bilodeau, you're doing a great job." Thank you!
(Mike Bilodeau sits down. The fans stand up and cheer. Robert Jackson takes the podium.) Robert Jackson: THE HALL OF FAME INDUCTEES!!!!!!!!!!! (The inductees stand up and take a bow. The cheers are deafening.) Robert Jackson: Good night, and God Bless. THE DWO IS NOW CLOSED!
