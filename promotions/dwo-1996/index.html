A Westchester-area house fed running weekly Wrestle Mayhem cards out of the Utica Memorial Auditorium through the fall of 1996, its five-title picture (World, Eastern, National, Tag Team, Women's) rewritten across a single Holiday Bash supercard. 61 wrestlers and 18 shows survive, plus its own Hall of Fame and Final Fight Tournament. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Deadly Wrestling Organization's surviving record opens already mid-run, on 30 November 1996, with a World Heavyweight Championship the promotion itself refused to recognize. J'ster won a fast three-count over Sir Giles Cavendish, MP in a cage match aided by interference from four separate wrestlers, and Co-President Mike Bilodeau ruled the finish too controversial to credit either man, vacating the title outright.

Its Holiday Bash supercard, 24 December 1996, rewrote most of the title picture in one night. Tommy Van Dam took the Eastern Championship from Super Destroyer after a returning John Jennings, meaning to help the champion, knocked out the referee by accident and cost him the match instead; Apollo retained the National Championship over Sick Rick in a steel cage, the only title match on the card not to change hands. The vacant World title went to Jamie Underwood, but he held it only long enough for Justin Slayne and Rick Styles to close the broadcast carrying the belt they had stolen from him — no sanctioned change was recognized.

A rematch on 29 December settled it, at least for the moment: J'ster, recognized as champion once more, retained the World Heavyweight belt by submission over Rick Styles. No further document from 1996 survives.

The record resumes in March 1999 with a one-time revival and a second Deadly Draw pay-per-view that May, run under the same ownership. Its Final Fight card that November, broadcast from Tokyo, closed with a Hall of Fame ceremony inducting its own past stars and, last, its two founders, Shawn Roy and Mike Bilodeau. Roy could not attend — a Metallica concert in England, Bilodeau explained from the podium alone — and it was Bilodeau who delivered the promotion's own closing line on air: “The DWO is now closed.”

It returned again in September 2003, Bilodeau narrating the promotion as “originally opened in the 90s” by himself and Roy and recounting its own past champions to recruit a new roster. Its fullest surviving broadcast from that era, WrestleKade X on 31 October 2004, closed on the same unresolved thread as 1996: J'ster, the former champion, beat Brody Thunder in the main event, and the broadcast's last image was Justin Slayne and Rick Styles back on the stage, still holding the World Title belt they had stolen from Jamie Underwood eight years earlier and never been recognized as rightfully holding. A further card, Saturday Stampede, was announced two weeks later; no result was ever posted.

In February 2005 the promotion renamed itself the Defiant Wrestling Organization for one further, open-challenge supercard, and within days was already announcing that show under yet another name, Dawn of Defiance, billed from the SkyDome in Toronto. No document survives after 2 March 2005, and neither that card nor the stolen title's own resolution is in the surviving record.
