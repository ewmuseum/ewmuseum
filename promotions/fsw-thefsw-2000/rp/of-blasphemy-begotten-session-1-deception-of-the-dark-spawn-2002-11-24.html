Of Blasphemy Begotten: Session 1 – Deception of the Dark Spawn (GTT repost; RP #2 of tourney)

24 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Life is not a fairy tale. More often than not, a paladin in golden armor brandishing a lance simply doesn’t ride out of the far north to deliver us from evil. It’s the truth. And, like life often times, the truth is cold and bleak. The human race is frail, a mere infant in the vast portrait of endless cycles that reprocess and rekindle this universe over countless aeons. Mankind is hopelessly vulnerable against the onslaught of the more prevalent and infinitely stronger beings inhabiting the cold recesses of space, the dark and brooding dimensions bordering our own, and even the fathomless trenches of the oceans and the sprawling reaches of departed alien civilization beneath the crust of our own planet. Sometimes we like to take refuge in the idea that the assault of otherworldly life-forms would be a trivial matter of defense, a petty nuisance to be warded off with the surfeit of nuclear weaponry and astounding artillery wielded by the most elite military powers. But from a sane and rational perspective, that supposition is wholly absurd. Nevertheless, humanity stands alone. Eternally on the brink of annihilation, whether by our foolish hand or that of immortal foes poised to reclaim the territory over which they once held dominion. The predominant ideology is that man likes to control his own destiny, determine his own fate. But that is not always his decision. Regrettably, it is an issue preordained. Already inscribed in the stars and traceable back countless millennia before the most primitive form of man set foot on the lush terrain of Earth, their destiny was affirmed. Long before the theory of man even twinkled in the eye of God, it was avowed that many of the Old Ones would rule the Earth as a perpetual reprimand for their wicked deeds, exiled far from their past territories of regime and without beings to deify them as in the past. For incalculable millions of years they dwelled without limit on the Earth, eventually worshipped by the multitude of alien species of which came to settle on the verdant lands of this fertile world for the mining and harvest of resources. Then He said “Let there be Man.” And with that a new chapter was written in the genealogy of this world, and a new clash of eternal rivals came to be, with the Earth-dwelling Old Ones constrained beyond the capacity to assail mankind beyond hideous dreams and dreary visions. The only records of this sinister history are recorded in dark and forbidden lore chastised by the Church and the educated. Blasphemy to some, mindless depravity to others. The Book of Eibon, the Cthaat Aquadingen, the G’harne Fragments, and the notorious and equally insidious Necronomicon itself. Ranking among the foray of tomes hinting at blackened planetary lineage, all rebuked in one way or another by the scholarly and the pious. And for those who have actually seized these volumes and studied their cryptic passages, madness and lunacy are habitually the result. Such is the effect of the cold and bleak truth. Yes, humanity claims to seek both comfort and the truth. But they cannot coexist; only the latter can be attained in the presence of the former, two parallels which cannot converge. Fortunately, there are scores of men who had glimpsed into the madness that is Earth’s macabre past (and likewise realize the truth about its present). Rule breakers, rogue scholars, overtly inquisitive adventurers, even investigative government officers have all stumbled way or another onto the truth. Bruce Shanahan knows the truth. A crusader of sorts, though forever at war with his own conviction and faith, the one known the world over under the alias of ‘Violence Jack’ was made aware of these realities against his will through confidences in allies, spiritual counsel, and the unavoidable psychic energies emanated from these slumbering Old Ones to enlist (or subtly enslave, that is) worshippers, or madden those who refuse the elder beings’ treacherous bargains. Shanahan’s brotherhood of scholars and warriors united to fight off the always-ominous threat of the Great Old Ones and their resurrection-minded cults of deranged worshippers has deteriorated into a civil war between rival factions: the followers of the controversial High Council of Elders, and the radicals seeking to overthrow the shadowy quintet of veiled overseers. Shanahan’s ally in recent months, Captain Stryker, is on trial before the Council for his failure to withdraw his unit in time from the now-infamous ‘Tropical Massacre’ only several dozen miles off of the Jamaican coast. Left with an enigmatic message in his dreams, he fears for his friend’s life and now races off to rend him from the clutches of whatever vile force threatens him, and hopefully to also fend off whatever infernal fiend rested in that fetal shell etched in his memory, its coming prophecized by the winged minion of the dark depicted in his nightmares. For Shanahan, things would only get worse. Bruce Blade is but a memory, a menial statistic in the annals of tournament records who failed to even reveal himself to the Boston-based hoodlum before bowing out of the competition Shanahan found himself engaged in. Demented, an agent of the GCW promotion, now stands in Shanahan’s path for supremacy. Bruce had developed the killer instinct necessary to survive on the streets, and combined with his new insight into the harsh reality of humanity’s being, makes him infinitely more lethal than at any point in his life on the mean streets or in the wrestling ring. Humanity still has no knight in shining armor. But only by the reunification of the Blackhelm Coalition can the Old Ones be repressed long enough for mankind to truly control its own ultimate fate. * * * Shanahan’s cab screeched to a grinding halt across the empty avenue from Mooney Hall in Rutland, Vermont, home of New Miskatonic University and the trial location for the accused Captain Marcus Stryker. Shanahan absently tossed a $20 bill into the driver’s side window and darted across the crosswalk, giving nary an expression of gratitude to the driver. The yellow-tinted car sped off, leaving the weary Boston traveler to trot up the steps of the towering structure and hurl open the main doors to the compact lobby. The screech of the closing doors in his wake echoed through the lobby and adjoining corridors, running a sharp chill down the spine of the semi-distressed ruffian as he scours for an aide at the desk. Oddly there is no receptionist or even security personnel watching over the building, a position whose presence is most notably conspicuous by its absence considering the normally securely guarded secrets and experiments in constant development in the lower subterranean levels of the Mooney Hall state-of-the-art science facility. “What the f*ck is goin’ on here?…”he cursed sharply aloud, managing to keep his voice hushed. He had no idea where the actual conference itself would be held, or for that matter where Stryker was held. It was theoretically a minor trial and there were no true criminal charges, thus Shanahan couldn’t imagine the captain being imprisoned in a customary jail cell anywhere on Miskatonic grounds. He had his doubts about the true intentions of the new regime of Council Elders, especially in light of his newest relevations, but undoubtedly they were far too shrewd to do something as striking as imprison Stryker like a common criminal. It would simply draw far too much heat on their activities, which were rich in strife as it was already. Frankly, the guy could be anywhere….. “Hey! Who the hell are you?!” The gruff snarl resonated from behind him, his back having been rotated to face the west hallway. The azure-shaded carpet had muffled the footsteps of the brawny male security guard, who now stood facing Shanahan with narrowed beady eyes and a hand pressed to the radio at his side. A .38-caliber service revolver also dangled ominously from his belt. Bruce, despite his unfavorable view of anyone dressed in that all-too-familiar style of police uniform, curbed his sense of loathing long enough to probe the guard for what he searched for. “I know a guy who’s supposed to be around here somewhere, has a meeting with these church elders tomorrow. Happen to be able to point out where he is?” The other scoffed, setting hands on hips before giving a shake of the head. His brow wrinkled minutely, a forlorn sigh escaping his throat. “Naw, absolutely no idea. At least it ain’t in this building. But unless you have specific business and the necessary paperwork to prove to me you have authorization to be here, this facility is off-limits during the night hours. I have to ask you to leave.” Shanahan felt that familiar pang of rage that consumed him when the cops mistreated him. Only now he realized this guy didn’t harbor the same prejudice as those corrupt pigs that patrolled the slums back in Boston and targeted he and his kind for their reputation and social status; this guy was only doing his job, what was outlined in the job description. Maybe a kid just out of college still looking for a steady job. Bruce’s shoulders heaved in a cross disappointment and frustration, and he spun on his heel to depart the shadowy hall. Behind, the officer shambled across the light carpeting and took his place behind the desk. As the doors creaked behind the stocky visitor, a nefarious grin danced across the puffy lips of the night watchman. He detached the radio from his belt and grinned, baring what now could be distinguished as razor-toothed incisors throughout his jaws as a yellow-tinted glaze enveloped the whites of his dilated eyes. The first sounds uttered forth from the inhuman imposter were a hideous array of profane monosyllables, alien in tongue and maddening in tone. After a brief pause the speech slowly altered from degenerate alien discourse back into English. “Yes, he is here.” A brief pause permeates the tension, shadows beginning to spin and distort awkwardly in the lobby, twisting and weaving as some outlandish ritualistic dance of untold horror. “Of course it’s him. He carries the mark foretold in the prophecy. And he searches for the one called Stryker.” Another momentary pause, accompanied by the further warping of the sinister living darkness and the lurkers within. “Yes, m’lord,” iterates the noxious pretender. “The time draws near. And all across this wretched Earth will feel the ire of a thousand liberated Gods…..” * * * The night air was chill with the sensation of bad weather looming, and a brisk breeze had begun to sweep like a fickle tide across the barren campus. Shanahan gazed aimlessly across the manicured lawn and the exquisite central garden to the towering silhouettes of the scantly used New Miskatonic halls on the north end of the university’s property, their basements holding strange curiosities contrived of alien structure and queer materials of the most furtive origins. He had seen these otherworldly marvels with his own astonished eyes, and was devoid of plausible doubt that his ally was being held in one of these buildings as he awaited his tribunal before the enigmatic Council of Elders, professed officials of the Catholic Church who governed the Coalition and approved all major operations, militant and scholarly alike. Shanahan’s heart, though heavy with his numerous obligations to friends and profession, dwelt on nothing at the moment other than locating Marcus, whom a gut feeling surmised was in mortal danger the longer he remained in the presence of those faceless elders. His hands were already doused in the blood of a hundred sacrificed innocents; he wouldn’t let one of Stryker’s devotion and loyalty join those already lost. Shanahan ventured forth through the gloomy New Miskatonic campus in the direction of the murky brick monoliths, unaware of the bizarre shadows dancing beneath the dim lights, amending and modifying as they sprawled cast on the ground in a growing composition of bulbous limbs and shirking masses……
