Of Blasphemy Begotten: Session 4 - Into the Catacombs (GTT repost; RP #4 of tourney)

24 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
NOTE: I'll have #3 up whenever I get onto my other computer tomorrow) ----------------------------- Slowly it closed on him, shifting its greater mass ever so slightly as if taking a perverted sense of sinister glee in the impending kill. Bruce eyed the narrow corridor formed between the wall and the fetid bulk of the looming thing before him. Those eyes, bubbling vicariously and constantly upon the flesh continued to leer. And still came those horrid shrieks that threatened to shatter his eardrums and drive him mad in their awful whining pitch. “Tekeli’li! Tekeli’li!” Was it worth the attempt? Success would get him out of the hall and evade the dark-spawned monstrosity and the winding passage further past would, as he recalled, take him into the subterranean levels and basements where Captain Stryker was inevitably being held. Failure? Unfortunately he feared failure would result in his being caught and crushed between the flowing plasm of the nightmare mound, almost undoubtedly before being sucked in and having his body compressed, ruptured and sucked to pieces. He knew the downsides of his attempt, but could brainstorm no better option as the tentacles of amorphous slime slammed into the floor around him and those horrible eyes gurgled in constant shift on the beast’s skin. Without a moment more of hesitation, Shanahan burst into a run and headed for the small opening. The monstrous creature, sensing his scheme, sent a wave of its pungent ooze to block the fleeing human and fill the crevice. He grit his teeth, and with a cry, hurdled the noxious slime as it spilled onto the floor beneath his feet and crashed against the wall. Without breaking stride, he continued his frantic run toward the hallway sprawling before him and the staircase that veered off to the left. Already he could sense as the abomination sent more of its amorphous bulk after him, dragging the greater part of its massiveness after him. Bruce could hear the collapsing of furniture as they tumbled over in the immediate lobby, the shattering of glass as its ‘head’ scraped against the light panels placed across the ceiling. He was just over halfway down the hall before he could hear doors swing open under the weight of the flowing slime as it pressed against the walls. With a final surge of energy he sent a strong shoulder into the door and slid between the wedge it created. Bracing himself, Bruce slid down the flight of steps beyond before coming to a rolling halt on the level below. Still more of the unending slime rolled down the stairs, those maddening eyes popping up like black bubbles to scan all in sight. Bruce slid behind a doorway and behind a podium used in this what appeared as a small classroom. The awful suction sound of the black slime as it oozed across bare floor revolted him, as did the ever-present putrid odor that accompanied the deathly fiend wherever it flowed. He held his breath for what seemed like hours before he could hear the black slime retract and slowly ascend up the stairs. After hearing the door to the floor level shut, Bruce pulled himself up and peeked out the doorway. He was in the first level corridor, and before him stretched classrooms and laboratories used by the majority of the student body attending New Miskatonic. It simply wouldn’t make sense for Stryker to be here; he had to be on a lower level of the building. Shanahan walked back out of the hallway and into the stairwell. From there he quietly walked down the stairs, unable to suppress a faint click that echoed through the chamber. He began his search on sublevel 2, and finding nothing, continued on sublevel 3. Further down he had to use the pass-card kept (mercifully) in his wallet. He had to keep the keycard at all times in case of emergency and in the event he needed access to the lower facilities but was unaccompanied by appropriate personnel. Thereon, the levels below held vast laboratories and resources at the disposal of Blackhelm personnel. These assets were the true ‘claim to fame’ of New Miskatonic, those resources salvaged from the wreckage of the original Arkham-based Miskatonic University and those discoveries made in the past 15 years since the opening of the new campus. The institution’s breakthroughs in the world of standard academia were nothing compared to the arcane manuscripts and forbidden lore studied out of the public eye in these lower levels, and the experiments conducted on ghastly and reprehensible things kept under lock and key in the deepest levels of the grounds. Bruce crept down snaking halls and through the virtual labyrinth of labs and libraries forming the lower stories. He was nearly out of options, having combed what he had thought of being every solitary inch of space and having the terrifying thought that the abyssal guardian stationed above ground was a decoy. Then he remembered. The Hell Pit. The Hell Pit: the nickname given by the resident scholars and researchers to the corridor of fortified cells where some of the most notorious experiment ‘subjects’ were kept. Bruce hated going in there, refused to do so until the overhead lights were angled to shield the sight of the monstrosities within with the reflected glare. The glass walls were in place only because the things within seemed subdued by the visual presence of the others. Alas, there was no alternative; Stryker had to be confined in one of those glass prisons amongst the captured representatives of the darkling races of the Cthulhu Cycle. Apprehensively, Bruce walked through Laboratory F of Sublevel 6, nudging by the cluttered benches and fluid-stained slabs toward the metal sliding door. With a deep exhale, he slid his keycard through the slot of the automated reader and watched as the multi-colored lights danced and blinked on the main panel before issuing forth a monotone buzz. The message ACCESS GRANTED – BRUCE SHANAHAN glided across the monitor strip. The door opened and the lights automatically clicked on, causing a rush of activity from the inhabitants within. Bruce calmly walked down the narrow hall between the glass-paned walls, avoiding meeting eyes with the alien denizens he so dreaded. The repulsive and semihuman Ghasts, the absurd myth-dwellers of the far Arctic called the Gnoph-Keh, the serpentine Cthonians with their always-groping face tentacles, and the marine terrors from the deep known only as the Deep Ones; all of these and more were represented in the catacombs of the ‘Hell Pit’, lurking about in their cells and held at bay by such mechanisms as perimeter-arranged ‘whistle-boxes’ which emanated squeals that warded off the hearing-sensitive Gnoph-Keh and the Elder Sign adorning the walls of the irreverent Star-Spawn which acted in place of the ineffective crucifix. At the far end of the room was Shanahan’s prize: huddled in the corner of his cell sat Captain Marcus Stryker, his crumpled uniform in a heap beside him. Bruce paused several cells away, out of the corner his eye spying the inquisitive look on the face of that monstrous Gnoph-Keh, dweller of the outermost Arctic wastes and rarely observed by man. Bruce took a moment to savor his own curiosity by gazing over its snow-white fur, the black-crowned tusk protruding from its nose, and the jagged claws and teeth is used in melee and hunting. In its eyes was an primal impulse intermingled with a degree of intelligence alien to the animal kingdom, as if it were studying Bruce yet staying on its guard at the same time. Shanahan, careful not to peer into those unearthly eyes, continued to walk down the passage until he reached the door. He fumbled a moment with the keys before snapping the correct one into place in a minute panel, giving it a twist and watching as the door slid into the ceiling. Only then did Marcus raise his eyes, which Bruce was alarmed to find appeared panic-stricken, bordering on those tell-tale eyes of a madman. Shanahan stepped into the cell, kneeling beside Stryker. He had no physical marks on him, but just by his eyes and pallid features it was certain he had endured terrible during his ‘visit’. “Bruce?” the captain finally uttered in what was difficult to distinguish between a question and a statement. “Wh…what are you doing here? How did you know?….” “Call it a lucky guess,” sighed the shaven-head Boston citizen. “And it’s a pretty sure thing you were in the area when I had the security guard transform into some a massive, eye-covered jelly-thing and chase me through the lobby.” Stryker nodded absently, almost as if only partly registering the comment. “A shoggoth,” he said matter-of-factly, emotion totally devoid from his cold tone. “Intelligent, shape-changing slaves of the ancient race called the Elder Things, now servitors of the terrestrial Old Ones and the most infamous of the Outer Gods. They grew too intelligent and too powerful to be controlled any longer by their masters, rose up in rebellion and essentially massacred the entire Elder Thing race dwelling on our planet.” “Glad to see you didn’t fall asleep in class the day they taught Necronomicon 101 back at Blackhelm HQ,” quipped Bruce, eyeing the trio of Deep Ones floating in their water-filled cell and staring right back. “Some are so intelligent they can take on the form of other beings, even form the proper organs for producing sound and other normal life processes. Thankfully they find it difficult to match the emotion and wit of man…..I take it that’s how you knew it wasn’t really a man?” Shanahan shrugged, trying to pull the prone Stryker to his feet. He didn’t want to be too forceful (for once), as it was hard to tell how the unstable commander would react. “Well the fact he had pure black eyes was a dead-giveaway that he was a bit odd. About confirmed that when that black jelly began to flow onto the floor and his head turned into a whipping tentacle.” “I see. Just you?” came the blank inquiry from the sitting captain. “Just me. I’m alone and couldn’t find a phone to get help. Old Jelly-Mold upstairs sort of ruined that idea before I could put it into action.” “Ah…good….” Stryker said in an almost childish lack of mind. “No match tonight then?” “Trinidad can wait,” murmured Bruce, still trying to coax Stryker into pulling himself up. “I’m bailin’ us outta here, then I’ll worry about his sorry ass. This is concern number one. Now if ya don’t mind, we need to find a way out of here! Are ya alright? Did those bastards torture ya or anything?” Stryker’s head waved slowly from side to side. “No…but they were going to brainwash me. Actually they called it ‘recondition’, but basically they were going to make it so I kept this trial secret and became a loyal servant of their plans, so I couldn’t tell them of the horrors they so arrogantly told me of and exposed me to in these dark rooms!” At that point the impassive captain shot to life, pulling what could be called an emotional U-turn and going into a frenzied diatribe. Bruce recoiled a bit, taken aback by the rapid 180 turn in terms of emotion. It was as if the mention of the council’s plans had triggered something within his psyche. “The schemes, the prophecies, and even the decrees of their masters from beyond the stars! Bruce….those men are evil, they’re mad! I can see it in their eyes, its like staring into a bottomless chasm, into a black and unending nothingness! All the suspicions, all the theories of our allies …. dammit, they’re right!”” “Calm down,” soothed Bruce, glancing nervously around the glass-walled room. “Ya can explain it to the others when we get back. Hawkins is already on the verge of assembling a force to overthrow the Council, you can add the final fuel to the fire and remove them from power…” “You fool!” exclaimed Stryker, nearly unable to restrain himself. “Don’t you get it?! We can’t overthrow them! It’s not possible!” Shanahan could only stare in sad pity at his wide-eyed comrade, who was obviously on the edge of insanity by the trauma of whatever had happened to him in these past days in the bowels of Blake Hall. “If that damned Hawkins tries then he’ll be sealing his own fate, along with everyone who joins his foolish crusade! Bruce….they told me of the prophecies of those blasted tomes of the occult. It has been foretold, the civil war within the Coalition! And now we have to stop it! At all costs we must prevent that reckless Hawkins from sacrificing lives!” Bruce didn’t like the way this conversation was going, and felt a sense of dread rise in his throat before even issuing the next question. “How do you know he’ll be sacrificing lives?” The insane look in Stryker’s mad eyes reaffirmed the fear in Bruce’s heart. “Because it’s already foretold in the prophecy, you poor fool! There’s a horrible battle, Bruce, an immense and terrible skirmish between the rogue forces of the Blackhelm Coalition and the otherworldly terrors commanded by those corrupt Elders of the Council! And the Coalition, those who have single-handedly held back the assault of those wretched enemies of mankind for so long…..their forces are completely annihilated!” Apathy: the loneliest of masters. Where rage dictates emotion and the adrenaline of conflict fuels your actions, where love is but a forlorn and distantly remote myth and temperance appears as a convenient cover for cowardice. Empathy substituted by ire, pity by scorn. This is but a peek into the unstable, chaos-ridden mind of one Bruce Shanahan. It stems from twenty years of existence on the cold streets of the Boston, Massachusetts slums where salvation is a bleak parody hinted at only in the dreams of idealists, and sin was not vice but virtue. Is it truly a wonder that the venerable ‘indy’ wrestling veteran Shanahan is volatile, impulsive, and often paranoid? Being a casualty of the social hierarchy, witness to the cold-blooded murder of his closest friend and now an ill-fated prophet to the End Times have all culminated over time and pressed the Boston native into a corner. Yet no amount of depraved street violence could prepare Bruce for the horrors awakened in his later days, the countless revelations of most obscene substance and the treks through fathomless vistas in the valleys of shadow-veiled dominions of evil. The outrageous perversions incarnated in sleepless nights, In a business surrounded by a veritable sea of street toughs who claim to have laid eyes on the most vile of mankind’s wanton exploits, Bruce presses alone through still-uncharted territory. Those scant few who have seen similar horrors are either dead or now sit rambling madly in the walls of asylums, gibbering revolting chants to appease their celestial tormentors. Those of the former who have experienced similar blasphemies have usually chosen to end their own pain, viewing death as a merciful escape to the torment of a thousand nights of dream-filled decadence. As has been predicted, life gets no easier for the bitter resident of Boston Massachusetts. After a bitter and violent struggle with AJ Trinidad for the right to represent his individual bracket in the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament back home, Shanahan now enters the battlefield to duel Drummond Base, surprise upstart of the minor EPW promotion, for the honor of moving into the ‘Sweet Sixteen” of the competition. To emerge from this fight, he must overcome his opponent, the insidious symbolisms played almost nightly in his terror-plagued slumber, and the demons nipping at his heels relentlessly on an almost hourly basis. And with the latest in a line of outrageous bombshells, the horrors of life only expand tenfold. His story goes on….. * * * Bruce and Captain Stryker plunged for what seemed like hours into the immeasurable depths of the black abyss called N’Kai, the profane netherworld where dwelled the slothful Tsathoggua and his abundance of servitor pit-spawn. Still arose the crackling flames of untold profundity, the screeches and howls of the deformed inhabitants slinking in the underworld darkness. Everything seemed to move in slow motion as they fell, nearly brushing by those skyward surges of living fire and anticipating falling into the jaws of whatever obscene things lurked in the shadowy masses below. The terror-stricken duo inexplicably caught a glimmer of light far beneath in their descending path, growing ever wider as they traveled at dazzling velocity to impact. After only seconds, they passed into that great radiance and were swallowed by a sea of all-encompassing luminance. Still they fell, but the terror in their hearts was curiously altered to a numbing sense of calm and emotional tranquility. The light vanished in a blink, and the travelers pitched into what awaited below: a vast bay bordering the lighted horizon of New York City. Stryker flailed in the water, surging upward to break the surface in a shower of brine and spray. Bruce did likewise a moment later several feet away, treading water until he recollected his senses and dragged himself through the serene waters towards a buoy hovering in the waves only yards to the north. Stryker joined his side a moment later, one arm wrapped heavily around the rocking object. His head slumped forward in a blend of exhaustion and disbelief. Bruce sputtered a minute, choking on the salty water in his gullet. “What the….hell just happened?!” he exclaimed with a gasp, tugging at the drenched shirt clinging to his brawny form. Stryker raised his head, eyeing Shanahan intently. Water trickled into his eye and the captain wiped the stinging stream from his brow. “I don’t know,” he breathed hoarsely. “Apparently we’ve been spared, if they wanted us dead they would have let us fall into that damned gulf of N’Kai. But they opened a rift in dimensional space and tore us from would have been our deaths. After all….” His eyes narrowed as a multitude of clashing thoughts broiled in his mind. “….I guess they wouldn’t want to kill their representative to the world of man.” Bruce couldn’t believe the detest accompanying the statement of Stryker. He was obviously furious at the revelations spread from the tainted lips of Ramses Meier, though whether it was of his bloodline or his foretold destiny he could not be sure. Either way, regardless of the rage fuming inside himself at such a heretic accusation, Shanahan realized of how his compatriot must be thinking at the time. Shanahan glanced left to gaze on the harbor of New York City, where boats still rumbled through the frothing bay even at this time of night. “I don’t intend on betraying my own species,” he sighed despondently. “I don’t give a damn what the stars say, or what some f*ckin’ ancient book declared a thousand years ago. I don’t know what that thing in my dreams was, or what the prophecy uttered by that abyssal monster guarding it meant. But I know damn well I will have no involvement in it unless I’m the one stoppin’ it.” Stryker swiveled his head back to see a tugboat gliding through the harbor in their general direction. He knew the power of the Old Ones and their capacity to alter the wills of their dream-plagued victims through the most simple suggestion. Bruce wouldn’t willingly betray mankind…..but the power of the enemy was strong. Yet he couldn’t hold Bruce responsible for something he knew that, as a man, he would never voluntarily do. But that didn’t mean they wouldn’t keep him under surveillance. His eyes were pleading at he cast a woeful look at his soaked comrade. “Please Bruce…we have to forget this rebellion. We have no option, we MUST tell Major Hawkins and the others that the Council is simply misunderstood, victims of bad circumstance. Otherwise we will seal our own doom.” They were forced into a corner, and either way would invite the certain loss of lives. To continue under the guide of the Council, and walk into trap after trap and ambush after ambush orchestrated by the faceless leaders. Or to fight back, and be crushed in one fell blow and allow the star-seeded deities to run rampant and unopposed across the lands of Earth. But there was little choice, and both realized that. The tugboat cruised through the harbor, stopping alongside the floating buoy and dropping a flimsy ladder on both of the forlorn swimmers clinging miserably aboard. As they did so, Bruce silently prayed for an abrupt current to violently sweep him out into the vast ocean and remove the terrible burden he knew lurked on the horizon for him. But there was no such salvation; the Old Ones wouldn’t permit such a painless solace.
