State Provided

3 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 3rd, 2002 Damn. That's the one word that comes to mind when I think about the occurences of my life. First my father, then my friend, and now this crapola. Let me take you back a few years so you know what the hell I'm talking about. Here I was. Almost exactly five years ago. Walking down 23rd Street. I'm walking, it's about 9:30 at night. As I pass an alley I hear a loud hell of a shrill. It wasn't just a shrill, it was a blood curdling scream if I ever heard one. I stop in my tracks, and focus in to hear the loud scream. I backtrack, and start walking slowly down the alley. I see a man, falling to the ground, and I see another man taking off down the alley. I start pacing faster and faster, and then turn it into a gallop. I slide my feet along the asphalt as I screech to a stop, and kneel over the injured man. But it's too late. He's bright purple, without air, and gone. I look up ahead of me as I see the man far off in the distance turning a corner. The next thing I know, I can hear distant sirens. They become less and less faint, and suddenly, cop cars slide into the alleyway from both directions. The cop cars stop, as the policemen get out and hide behind their doors, pointing their guns at me. Jack Brooze: What seems to be the problem offi- Policeman: FREEZE! PUT YOUR HANDS IN THE AIR OR WE'LL SHOOT! I do what they tell me, as they close in on me. Then, for no apparent reason, 6 cops wrestle me to the ground and handcuff me. I didn't show any signs of aggression or rebellion. Yet they still feel the need to pummel me like they did. They practically carry me to the cop car, as five assist in the haul, and one reads me my rights. What a joke. Rights. Is this rights? Attacking an innocent man and carrying him around like nobody's business? I spent the night jail. The next morning, a man came to see me. Man: Mr. [name withheld]? Hi, I will be your state provided lawyer for your case. I don't say anything, then he goes on. Man: I'd like to have a private meeting. Excuse me, guards. May I have a word with him. The guards nod, and they let in the lawyer. So he starts asking me questions. Man: Mr. [name withheld], have you ever committed a crime? Jack Brooze: Yes. Man: Please detail it for me. Jack Brooze: 5 Speeding Tickets, 6 Parking Violations, and oh yeah, I was charged with 3rd degree murder. Man: What? You were? Jack Brooze: Yea. About a year ago. I won the case. Man: Did you commit 3rd degree murder? Jack Brooze: Hell no! Man: Okay...damn, this is going to be difficult. Do you know the name of the man who you've been charged with killing? Jack Brooze: No. Man: Have you ever seen him before? Jack Brooze: Sometimes. I think I recognize him from the corner deli. He's there alot. Man: I see....Mr. [name withheld], tell me in your words what happened. Jack Brooze: Okay, but stop with the Mr. crap. Just call me Jack. Man: Okay, Jack. Jack Brooze: Okay. I was walking down the street. I heard a scream, so I walked down the alley. I saw a man falling to the floor, and another man running. I ran to the man and kneeled over to him. He was bright purple. I checked his pulse. Nothing. I looked up and I saw the guy who was running, and he was turning the corner towards the next street. Man: I see. Jack Brooze: Then the cops came. And that's pretty much what happened. Man: Did you touch the man with your hands? Jack Brooze: Just on his neck. When I checked his pulse. Man: Damn...If you hand't had done that it would have been much easier to prove your innocence. Because you could easily be charged with killing the man by strangling him in that same area. But don't worry. Jack Brooze: Don't worry? I'm sitting in a jail cell, charged with murder! What's there to worry about? Man: Alright, alright. So I'm being a little positive. What does that hurt? Jack Brooze: It hurts plenty. You're on the other side of the bars. The sound of a key entering the doorhole is heard, crackling and creaking as the door opens to the Brooklyn apartment of Jack Brooze. He throws his keys on the floor, and plops on his leather couch. Man: Well, I’m sorry for you that you have to be here, but you are here, and yes, you’re on the other side of the bars. Now the only way you can get out of there, is if you cooperate. So what do you say? Are you willing to cooperate? Jack Brooze: Of course! Man: Alright. Well, it’s getting late, and I believe my visitation period is up. I’ll be back tomorrow morning. Your hearing is tomorrow. Get some good rest. I nodded, as the security guards escorted the lawyer out of my cell, and out of the long hallway. I let out a deep sigh as i rested my weary head on the fist of my hand, thinking about what I was to do. I decided to lay down on my cockroach infested mat. I was awoken the next morning by the rattling of keys. I rolled over slowly, stretching out my arms and letting out a deep yawn. I placed my feet on the floor and looked towards the cell door. The guard was opening up my cell. Guard: A visitor. From the corner of my eye I saw my lawyer, walking forward and into my cell. Lawyer: Hello Jack, good morning. Wiping my eyes to remove the overnight residue that built up in the corner of my eyes, and looked at him before letting out a greeting. Jack Brooze: Hi. The lawyer pulls out from behind him a suit in a plastic bag. Lawyer: I brought you a suit. Today’s your hearing. I suggest we do some more talking before we go down to the courthouse. I nodded, as I got up and took a look at the suit, and nodded in satisfaction. I tossed it on my mat, and sat down as did my lawyer. Lawyer: Jack, I just want you to know something. The victim’s cousin, who goes by the name of Hot Butter. He’s after your life. You better watch your back if you get out of this, cause he could kill you. I’m serious. I nodded once again. Lawyer: So let’s see. You’ve been charged with murder in the first degree and manslaughter. We’ve already concluded that neither of these you have did. Now tell me. Can you remember anything regarding the man you saw running? Jack Brooze: No, just seeing him running. Lawyer: Can you remember his size? Jack Brooze: I’d say maybe six two, six three maybe. He was built, probably about 250 pounds. Lawyer: Anything else? Jack Brooze: Just one thing. The word “rot” in capital letters was written over his forehead in spray paint it seemed. Lawyer: rot? What the? Jack Brooze: That’s what I was thinkin’. Lawyer: I don’t see how the pieces fit. But don’t worry. Do you have an alibi? Jack Brooze: What would an alibi do me? I was at the scene of the crime, remember? Lawyer: Yes that’s right. But maybe someone saw the other man running. You are almost definitely sure that he killed the man right? Jack Brooze: He had to. It couldn’t have been anybody else. Lawyer: Okay. I’ll talk to some people. See what I can come up with. Things don’t look great, but they don’t look awful. We’re still in this.
