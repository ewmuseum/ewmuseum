Moby Fish: The Legend of Ol' Pete and Stumpy Jenkins

20 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10-20-02 “Call me Timo.” The loud roar of a dingy old outboard motor cuts the mountain air, and it signals the coming of two of the greatest fishermen…well, no, not really, but it still heralds two great wrestlers as they partake upon a quest for…The Black Bass! Gaunt soldiers of the fishing world clad in only the armor that consists of a fishing vest or a flannel shirt. Their weaponry are that of shiny lures and slimy worms. Their swords are seven-foot long rod’s of destruction, not taken lightly in the world of fish. No, these weren’t just men…they were Fishermen. Noble and true, the code unspoken between all fishermen was clear. Never divulge your secret spot, lest it be taken by another. Never tell exactly how big the one that got away actually was, lest it be found for an untruth. Never tread upon another fisherman’s rod, for it would bring forth a reign of bad fortune like thou hath never seen. Above all else, to the fool who knows not how to fish, do not tread in the waters of Ol’ Pete, for the monster Black Bass would consume him. Timo Bolamba, also known to most as The Dastardly Bastard sat upon the middle seat of the small boat named ‘The Spud Runner” while his friend and hetero-wrestling mate Mike Marvelous stood upon the front, much like a monolith of Canadian heritage in this field. The guide was a staunch old man, weathered and gamey. He stunk of rotten cigars, and his clothing had moth holes in them. The whiskers that covered his haggard face were long and grey, and seldom trimmed. He piloted this boat through the uneasy waters of Lake Castaic, California, through depths unknown, through logs, reeds, weeds, and over the occasional duck. These proud gladiators were here for one mission. They needed the experience given by this fight to win another…For what is it to overcome a man when one is in their element? No, this training was different, it was to clear their minds, and present a challenge against an unknown foe. “Mike, I have a good feeling about this, like we are one with nature….what do you think……..Mike?” While Mike busies himself becoming “one with nature” over the side of the boat, Timo languishes in the vast aromas of the outdoors. The scent of a pine tree, the mellow smell of the lake itself, the stench of a bull moose on the shore… “You know, I kinda like this outdoors stuff. Reminds me a lot of climbing the trees to get pineapples back home.” Mike turns and laughs a bit, almost slyly at his Samoan compatriot. “You mean you actually did that?” “Yeah, everyone does when they are a kid. You haven’t lived until you have had a fresh pineapple right off the tree.” “Wow, and I thought that was something Tyler teased you about…” Timo shrugs a bit and looks at the countryside as the outboard motor roars and leaves behind a torrent of oil in the water. Trees pass along, so do weed beds and the occasional stump in the water, leaving a trail as the wake of the small boat crashes upon them. “So, when are we actually going to do some fishing?” “I don’t really know, I figured we would be there by now.” Mike turns and looks at the old man. He has a name badge that reads ‘Stumpy” on it. The Canadian ponders for a moment then speaks to him. “So umm…Stumpy, when we actually going to fish?” Stumpy seems to shift his weight in the seat a little, he harumphs, and spits into the lake. The boat comes to a crawl in a small cove, and he shuts the motor off. Timo and Mike look at each other as not a word has been exchanged yet. The old man shoves the anchor into the water and leans back in his chair. “Or if you don’t want to tell us we could do that too….” “Hush you!” The two words silence Mike and draw interest from Timo. They look at the old man as he spits over the side of the boat and puts a dip of snoose in his mouth. “Have you boys ever heard the legend of Ol’ Pete?” “Is this going to take long? We should really be getting back to the…” WHACK! Timo recoils in pain as he is swatted by Stumpy. “Wow he really is quite quick for his misleading age…” The old man reaches for his tackle box and produces a huge crankbait that is torn in half. Viscous bite marks have rended the once noble bait useless, its lip dented, its hooks rusted and bent. Timo and Mike look on in reverence. “Salty dogs and us old coots alike have all seen this fish, he swims about lazily in the moonlit nights, and sometimes a feller can catch him sunning himself, almost daring us to try to catch him. What I have in my hand is the only lure ever to catch his lip…oh I figger about twenty eight years ago…” “But, that’s impossible, no fish lives that long!” A hand silenced Timo in the lake, and as a calm wind drew slight ripples across the water, Stumpy looked into the clear, deep loch that he called home and nodded. He tipped his hat, silently feigning a bit of courage, for today was his day, and these were his warriors. “That may be true for some fish boy, but this is no ordinary catch. This is the meanest, most vile, most dangerous fish in the known world...this thing will kill you five times before you hit the bottom, and drag you across the depths of this god forsaken lake if you hook it! Look at me youngster, these two hands are the only ones that know his hatred…and for that, I guess I owe it to him to be the one that catches the god forsaken demon.” Timo and Mike both look at one another and realize suddenly they are in way over their head. This crazy old hillbilly was something of a modern day Ahab, and this fish he spoke of, if the legend held true, might just be more than either of them knew how to handle. Timo spoke quietly, almost half afraid now at what they had gotten themselves into. “Mike….your middle name…it isn’t Queequig by chance is it?” “No, it’s Melvin, you know that.” Timo looks at him funny. “Just kidding Timo…no, but I catch your drift. Hey old man, can we just fish or is there a law against it?” The old man produces a jar of Uncle Josh Pork Twister Tails and opens it, holding it to face the two men. Their faces recoil with the stench of death warmed over, and though it gags them to do so, one by one they grab a salty pork rind, age undeterminable, and hold it between their fingers as if it were an atom bomb warhead. “Good good, he likes those. Soon as the sun drops below the seventh degree, he will swim through this channel, creating a bit of a wake behind him. You boys are in for a real treat, I guarantee it.” Timo watches Mike put the nasty pork bait onto the spinner he is using, and he emulates his tag partner. The slimy chunk of scented fat that is probably older than Mike and Timo goes on without a fight, and it dangles, a moldy omen of things to come. Suddenly the wind shifts a bit and a chill breeze blows across the lake. Stumpy turns to his left and points as a line is beaded through the water. A razor like spine first penetrates the surface, followed by a torn web of a dorsal fin, the spikes that protrude from the wide back look like spears… “Here she comes!” Timo looks at Stumpy and shouts as the wind picks up. ‘That’s IT? Here she comes? That’s the best you could think of?” Mike swats Timo on the arm. “Shut up Timo, the guy isn’t exactly Melville, you know.” Stumpy grabs his pole and lifts it up in the air, but just as he does, the fish changes course, drawing a bead for the boat. He sinks below the water like a submarine leaving nary a trace behind him and three perplexed fishermen. Stumpy grabs the sides of the boat, Mike and Timo do the same. ‘He’s going to ram the boat!” No sooner do the words escape Stumpy’s mouth than the giant fish slams into the boat, spinning it headlong to the right. The two wrestlers grab their fishing poles and clutch them tightly. The boat rocks back and forth again as a giant resounding thump can be heard again. “Damn you Pete, show yourself!” Stumpy stands up and swings his pole wildly. The fish lines up another attack run and dives, deep beyond the sight of Mike and Timo in the clear water. Stumpy looks back and forth in the water, trying to figure out the direction the fish had took, but the watery demon surprises them all, hitting the bottom of the boat and sending Stumpy overboard. “Stumpy! Hold on old timer! We will help!” “Wait Timo!” Timo turns suddenly. “Huh?” Mike shakes his head silently and smiles in a cunning fashion. “He knows what he is doing besides, it’s not the dastardly way to…” His words are cut off by a scream as Stumpy disappears below the surface of the water. Timo leans back quickly, surprised and topples the balance of the boat, sending it into a pitch and dumping everything on board, including himself and Mike. The cold water of Lake Castaic is no place to be mortified, but there was never a time in Timo’s life he was less scared. He felt a warm spot in the water behind him and turned to see Mike Marvelous white as a ghost. “Christ Mike, you pissed in the pool! Now that fricking fish is going to be mighty peeved!” “I panicked!” While they tread water Timo looks around trying to form a plan. Mike grabs the side of the boat and makes to flip it over while Timo thinks. Suddenly there is a spray of water as a gigantic Black Bass jumps in the air, trying to toss a half broken crankbait from its mouth. Stumpy emerges next from the water being pulled behind, holding his old Mitchell rod and reel combo. He pops out of the water and clenches his fist emphatically. “FROM HELLS HEART I CAST AT THEE!” He rears back and fully sets the hook, the fish flipping up in the air, he loses his grip on the pole from the mighty torque of the fish pulling upon it, and it flies into the water. He laughs like a madman and dives after it, swimming feverishly to catch up. Behind him the boat has taken on so much water it wont even float, and as is sinks slowly to the depths, the laugh still haunts in Timo’s mind. He and Mike tread water slowly, and soon they begin to get tired. “Mike, I don’t know how long I can keep this up. This gear is really weighing me down!” ‘Come on Timo, we have to keep it up. Stumpy….would have wanted it that way!” Soon their legs and arms grow weary and the sun beats down upon them. Timo looks up at Mike and even though he pants heavily, he manages to make a humorous quip. “You know, I never thought I would say this…but Water Water Everywhere, and Not a Drop to Drink…” “Dude, just drink the lake water!” ‘No way! You pissed in it!” “There is a dilution quotient Timo!” Timo hears a bubbling from behind him, and up floats an old beaten tackle box that looks as if it had sat at the bottom of the lake for almost thirty years. The reaches for it, as does Mike, and as they do, they see the name ‘Stumpy” awled on the box, probably from an old jackknife. “Timo, I think we should head for the lodge…we should tell someone about Stumpy.” “Yeah…I think you are right Mike.” The long swim home was a quiet one. Both the men knew somewhere out there they had lost a man who had tried to teach them the methods of his madness, and they also knew Ol’ Pete could still be lurking. After what seemed like an eternity, they finally made it to the shore. As they crawled into the bank, Timo kissed the ground several times, and Mike turned to the lake. He grabbed Timo and pointed as the box floated back out into the lake with a wave, and sank, disappearing from view forever. They find the energy to get up, and they make it into the lodge, the man at the counter, Steve, looks the dripping wet duo over and smiles. ‘What can I do for you boys? You look terrible.” Timo gasps and slaps his hand on the counter. He and Mike catch their breath and sit down, Steve looks concerned. “You have to go out and look for him…” “Who?” “Stumpy…he went overboard, and we couldn’t find him.” Steve walks to them and draws a chair. He sits down silently and puts a hand on Mike and Timo’s shoulders. He sighs a bit and nods to himself. “Boys…Stumpy died about 28 years ago. He drowned out in Lacuna Channel when he fell overboard fighting with Ol’ Pete. You couldn’t have seen old Stumpy Jenkins…” They both look at one another silently, as if to say “But then, who was that?”. And somewhere in the night, if the moon shines just right, and the wind picks up, a passing angler might hear Stumpy Jenkins as he laughs, and they might see a gigantic Black Bass jumping in the pale moonlight…But that…is a different story.
