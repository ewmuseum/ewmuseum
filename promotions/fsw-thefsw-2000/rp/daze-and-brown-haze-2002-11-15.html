Daze and Brown Haze

15 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11-15-02 A famous wrestling personality once said that the line defining good and evil had faded, and it was growing more and more difficult to differentiate the two. It became good to be bad, and it became bad to be good. But the line seems to be growing ever clearer. The line between good and evil seems to be more difinitive than ever. Kimbusa does not define himself as good. But Steven Caldera, simply put, is evil. It is approxamately eight thirty in the morning. The sun has risen over Houston, Texas, and is greeted with the usual thick haze that tints its golden rays brown. The city is ablaze with activity in the busy urban streets below, as the population rushes to the start of their nine to five jobs. Kimbusa lays in a stupor on his silk sheets, having remained in the same bewildered position for hours. His eyes are glazed and swollen from a long, sleepless night. His face is dry and unshaven, his clothes now three days old. The room is dark and unkempt, now thoroughly soiled from three weeks of neglect. And the padlock on Kimbusa's front door is now more secure than ever. It's maddening being trapped in the same room for weeks on end. The real world seems like a mere dream; it was as if the events of the previous night's Glory were imagined in his confused and disoriented mind, and he had never truly left. Anything lying beyond that door is a myth, and Kimbusa fears he may never know anything more. It had been months since he'd last seen Epiphany. Her face had become more and more beautiful in his dreams, to the point that her energy was the only thing sustaining him in his luxurious prison. He finally saw her face again last night... And it was a face of fear. Of agony. A face of pain. How much more could Caldera take? Caldera had taken his freedom, he had taken his energy, he had taken his life... But was he not content? Must he now destroy Kimbusa's love as well? What could this man possibly be achieving by destroying Kimbusa's very existence? Was he getting some sort of sick satisfaction from it? Or was it simply a vulgar display of power? Evil has returned to the world of professional wrestling, indeed. And it has manifested in the form of Steven Caldera. Kimbusa will never forget the grin on Caldera's face as he watched Epiphany's torture. Violence Jack has already proven that he is more evil, more twisted, more sadistic than Kimbusa had ever been. Perhaps this is what Caldera was searching for all along. Caldera had always demanded that Kimbusa be as evil and hateful as he could be. But at heart, Kimbusa has always sought a release from those emotions. Violence Jack has embraced them. It has been three weeks since a single unpolluted ray of sunshine has struck Kimbusa's face. His eyes are black and worn, his hair matted and dry. Steven Caldera had locked Kimbusa away forever, releasing him just long enough to destroy whatever hope might be left. Epiphany's last words to Kimbusa were a solemn warning... That Steven Caldera was not a man to be trusted. Epiphany had only met Caldera once, and Caldera had been a perfect gentleman. But she had seen right through him, just as she had seen right through Kimbusa. Epiphany knew this was coming long before it happened. And perhaps she knew that Kimbusa was not strong enough to defeat Angelo Deville. Angelo... Angelo... That name has haunted Kimbusa for months. Angelo Deville was a cruel man, but not one Kimbusa had particularly despised before the tournament. Now, the name Deville is associated with Kimbusa's incarceration... And it stings every time he hears it. Deville... O'Neil... Caldera... Violence Jack... Mike McClure... So many forces working against him. Why do these people delight in his torture? What had he done to deserve such torment? There is no doubt in Kimbusa's mind that, at one point, he was a killer of the worst nature. At one point, Kimbusa was more evil and more sadistic than any of these men have ever been. But his one redeeming factor is that he had fought hard to find love in a loveless world... He had found the bright center of a dark and evil world. But the dark and the evil are now in posession of the one bright light in Kimbusa's life... and without it, Kimbusa can no longer exist.
