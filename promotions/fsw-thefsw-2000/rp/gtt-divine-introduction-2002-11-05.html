GTT · Divine Introduction

5 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: whoo~! Light, pure and benevolent in it's intent, dances through the leaves of the Sycamore tree and the window beyond into the dark recesses of a little girl's room... And mind. As if engaged in a ritualistic trance, the ghouls' silhouettes sweep malevolently along the walls and their dressers, giving form to the anguished howling of the wind. The room and house seem quiet, but something is definitely amiss... The light itself seems horribly wrong. It was just a dream, Rebecca MacGregor tries to comfort her mind with a soothing inner voice. The demons have set up shop inside her brain, however, and no small amount of comforting and prayer will exorcise them. No, it was a nightmare... It is a nightmare, the voice amends, now on the brink of panic. She is fairly sure she heard a noise moments before, and while a part of her fights to rejoice with the sound for waking her, there is an underlying evil in the air raising the hair on her arms. They stand upright like an army from God set to defend her, but that brings little comfort to her six-year-old mind. Heart trying to pound a hole through her chest, she forces herself to a sitting position and casts a suspicious eye around the room. It normally makes up for it's small size with an overwhelming feeling of comfort and fuzziness, but in the bouncing light of the moon, her surroundings only serve to kindle the waste left behind by dreams. The pile of stuffed animals in the corner looks like a carnivorous blob, the closet undeniably hides vampires and imps, and the line of Barbies standing on top of the dresser looks like a wall of fame ready to consume them all. Whipping the covers off of her, Rebecca barely manages not to squeal as she scrambles off the bed to the warm and somehow ticklish carpet. Her pink Lilo and Stitch pyjamas are crumpled from their wrestling match with the sheets and her curly blonde hair bobs on her head as she warily approaches the door. A thin face peeks out from beneath the gravity-defying mop, eery light accentuating the terror in her eyes and the tremble in her lips. One thin arm reaches cautiously up to the doorknob at eye-level, and the pink little hand at the end grabs and twists it noiselessly. Whack! The sound makes her jump and jerk back, swinging the door open. Straining, she manages to pick up the sound of voices - or at least one voice - coming from down the hall, in her parents room. She didn't like the sound of whatever got hit getting it, but she liked the monsters in her room less. Creeping out in to the hallway, she is resolved to sleep with mommy and daddy. Just a nightmare, Becky, she often used the nickname her parents gave her when keeping her on the right track. It isn't real. No one is here. The men from her nightmare were hideous, dressed in red suits with horns jutting from their skull. They had just finished with her parents and were moving onto her. They seemed to swallow up light as they moved, yet fire burned brightly in their eyes. Like a gateway into Hell they stared at her, then mercifully she awoke. But is it over? Did she dream it. Her footfalls make no noise on the carpeted floor; the MacGregor's had the house built only two years after she was born and everything was still fresh and straight. An unknown acrid smell assaults her nostrils as she gets closer, poisoning her with the toxic fumes of a cigar. But daddy doesn't smoke, her mind protests as she reaches the door and stares at it's wood-panelling in wonder. The door being closed doesn't surprise her, but at this distance she can tell that there are obviously people talking beyond, and they are definitely trying to be quiet. She raises her hands to the doorknob and pauses just as they enclose it. Would her parents be angry with her for being out of bed? She didn't understand what or why it was, but there was something about being up this late that seemed wrong. She couldn't imagine her parents being pleased at seeing her up and about. Rebecca almost turns away and slinks off to bed, but realises she'd rather face whatever is coming with her parents than alone. A loud creak tears through the whispering silence as she presses the door slowly open, her head leading the way in to ensure her safety. 'Mommy?' She calls in a scared and tiny voice, desperately needing to be hugged and held. Heels clack across the hardwood floor (father couldn't imagine carpeting in his bedroom), footsteps with deadly intent. Rebecca releases the door and begins backing away, mouth moving soundlessly, but a figure swoops through right on top of her and grabs ahold of her shoulder. The hand is gentle enough, but cold as ice. She screams. 'Shhhhh,' a comforting whisper from behind her. 'Easy now, sweety. Everything's fine.' Rebecca turns slowly around and regards the kneeling man before her with eyes large enough to rival the moon. Dressed in a spotless white suit that almost shines in the darkness and wearing heavy black Italian shoes, the man is much larger than her father. The blue eyes that peer down into her seem haven't even a trace of kindness, contradicting the pleasant smile beaming out from three inches lower. The visible cheekbones spur a stereotypical sense of lordliness, as does the neatly slicked back blonde hair and the burning cigar in his hand. 'W- Where's my m- mommy?' She asks in a meek voice, distrusting the smile more and more the larger it grows. 'I want my mommy.' She states, trying to put some force behind her and failing miserably. 'You're Rebecca, right?' The large man asks in a cheery voice, then nods as she does to show he knew. 'Well don't worry your little heart, Rebecca. Your mommy is just over there,' he throws a hand back towards his shoulder to indicate the room, then turns his head back and snaps, 'say hello, mom.' There is a slight slapping sound, followed immediately by a quick inhalation of breath, then a shaky voice. 'H- Hi Becky. Go to bed, precious. Everything is fine.' The mother tries to reassure her daughter since she herself is beyond doubt. No need to worry the child. The large man turns his head back to Rebecca and flashes a wink that says, I told you so. 'Can I sleep with you?' She calls over his shoulder, the plea dripping with urgency. No answer calls from the darkness, however. 'Your parents are sort of tied up at the moment, so why don't you go back to bed?' The scary man suggests, then seems to think better of it. 'Here, Uncle Angelo will escort you, how does that sound?' She can't answer as he scoops up one of her little hands in his massive one and stands up. He reaches back to close the door as he half-shouts over his shoulder to someone else, 'take care of them while I'm gone.' The door slams shut. 'Having a bad dream?' Angelo asks the child in a sympathetic voice as they begin taking baby steps down the hallway. She nods, her eyes glued to the ground, and he nods along as well, sagely. 'Well, don't you worry your pretty little head about it. It's all make-believe... Like Santa Claus.' Her eyes go wide at this last revelation and her tender minds cries to fight, but her fear prevents her from arguing. 'Are you an angel?' She queries, looking up at him hopefully. He chuckles and pats her head. 'Some would say so. I've been called the Crimson Angel on more than one occasion. Having been taught about colours in kindergarten, Rebecca knows crimson basically meant red, and her dreams rocket back into her mind with terrifying ambitions. 'Please don't hurt mommy and daddy,' she pleads with a snuffle, then finally bursts into tears that have been trying to claw their way out since she stirred. Angelo feigns shock as he stops and tilts her head up to him. 'I'm not hurting anyone, Becky, your parents and I are just having a little talk,' Angelo speaks as if scandalised at the accusation. 'A party of sorts,' he elaborates, the smile materialising on his face once more. 'Nuh-uh,' Rebecca argues through sobs, shaking her head defiantly. 'Mommy only has friends in the bedroom during the day... And she says daddy can't find out about it,' Becky states innocently. Angelo stares at her for a moment, stifling the laughter scratching at his throat. He doesn't argue with her or even speak to her at all, just scoops her up in his arms and carries her the rest of the way down the hall. They enter her room with her sobbing quietly and him smiling to himself. As he sets her down on the bed she looks up into his face with desperately beseeching eyes. 'Please don't kill them,' she squeaks. Angelo again looks taken aback, then tucks her in and gives her a little peck on the cheek. 'I won't. Promise.' He tells her, happy with himself for not having to lie to a child. She gives a weak, disbelieving smile, then obediently closes her eyes. Angelo regards her for a moment, then turns and leaves the room, shutting the door behind him. The smile washes off his face as he strides mechanically back down the hall and re-enters the master bedroom. Tony is standing in the middle of the room with a bloody knife in his hand, two corpses staining the floor with blood at his feet. The MacGregors are both stripped to their undergarments, having been sleeping peacefully in their bed when the break-in occurred. When Angelo last saw them half a minute ago they were alive and praying, but now their lives are bleeding out of them, stealing away silently from the gashes across their necks. 'We should've kept the bitch alive,' Angelo says with a smirk as he steps forward and kicks the door closed with his heel. 'Apparently she's a frisky one.' Tony just shrugs and wipes his knife across her underwear, trying to get the blood off. Angelo shrugs himself as he makes his way over to the desk along the western wall and begins snooping through the drawers. Tony is a middle-aged man with streaks of grey staining his slicked-back and otherwise black hair. The belly protruding from his unbuttoned suit jacket marks many years of living too easily, as do the half dozen gold rings lining his fingers. His face is pretty well flat, possessing all the features - and charisma - of a concrete slab. A jagged scar runs from his nose to his left ear, cutting across his face like war paint. 'Say anything interesting before they died?' Angelo asks without turning his head, rifling through the papers in the dark. 'Or were they as uncooperative as before?' 'They were pretty fuckin' uncooperative,' Tony responds with a thick Italian accent, speaking much louder than Angelo. 'The guy here kept goin' on ?bout how the ?disciples' would get us, but that's about it.' Tony holds up his hands helplessly, then gives the uncooperative dead a hardy kick. Angelo finishes up with the desk and moves over to a metal filing cabinet in the corner. He has to bust the lock off of it before it will open, but that proves to be little hassle. 'So, who are these disciple guys anyhow, Angelo? And what do we care what they're up to or where their base is?' Tony asks as he stumbles over to join Angelo at the filing cabinet. His knife is clean now and resting in it's shoulder scabbard. 'Group of religious fanatics. They run around killing people they suspect are under Satan's power... In the name of the Lord, of course,' Angelo sneers with an unconscious rolling of his eyes. 'Any type of quackery you can think of and these guys have a stinky finger wriggling around in it. It's pitiful, really.' He slams the filing cabinet closed and curses under his breath. Turning around to face Tony, Angelo heaves a sigh and ruefully shakes his head. 'What's wrong?' Tony asks, narrowing his eyes to get a better look around. 'Nothing in there, either,' Angelo whispers thoughtfully. 'Though I didn't really expect to find anything. Everything I need was in that man's head.' He darts an accusatory glare at the bled Disciple sprawled across the floor. 'And what exactly do you need?' Tony demands with impatience. Angelo had dragged him along on this mission without explaining any of the reasoning for it. Everything he knew he had garnered from the interrogation of MacGregor, and that didn't give him much. He never knew Deville to be religious, but he wouldn't put anything past the man. Maybe extorting the church had become fashionable. 'Information,' Deville answers simply, yanking the sheets from the bed and spreading them across the floor. 'Anything on their organisation, but more specifically on one of their agents, a Matt Blitz. I want to pay him a little visit before our match this weekend, so I have to find out where he is,' he continues, puffing mightily on his cigar. 'Your match?' Tony asks, incredulous. 'You mean for your wrestling crap?' Tony is aghast, mouth hanging open like a cavern as Angelo nods. 'We just killed two people because of a goddamned wrestling match?' 'Not just any match,' Angelo corrects, sounding upset at his pal's uncomprehending. 'This is for a tournament... A big tournament. Shit... You know how many officials I had to bribe and strong-arm to finally get in at such a late date?' Angelo demands rhetorically, raising his eyebrows to emphasise the significance. 'I can't fuckin' believe it,' Tony shakes his head and pauses. 'A wrestling tournament.' The words sound foul coming from his mouth, as if they're the most disgraceful he's ever encountered. He begins speaking again, but Deville cuts him off. 'Besides... We're killing three people, not two,' Angelo informs his partner, whose immediate response is outrage. 'We can't kill the little girl! She can't be older than five!' Tony almost shouts, a stubborn look on his face that Angelo has seen - and conquered - a hundred times before. 'She saw my face, Tony,' Angelo states almost regretfully, placing a hand on Tony's shoulder. 'So what! You can't do it! It's sick!' Tony exclaims, stomping his foot like a three year old protesting his early bedtime. 'I never said I was gonna kill the little girl, Tony,' Angelo corrects him patiently, and Tony's relief is instant and overwhelming. 'You're gonna.' The words jolt through Tony like a lightning bolt and he takes a retreating step before he realises what he's doing. 'But Angelo...' He begins whining, but Angelo cuts him off with a bark. 'Tony!' He snaps, and the subordinate obediently shirks his shoulders and cringes. Deville holds out his zippo lighter and Tony grasps it with a shaking hand. 'Nothing cruel or sadistic, Tony. Just set this room on fire. It'll spread.' Angelo's voice is cool once more as he drags on the cigar. Tony doesn't protest any more. At least not with his mouth. Sulking, he flicks the cap open and strikes the lighter, then winces as the flame catches and holds. He casts one last glance at Angelo but sees no mercy in his face, so he drops the lighter into the pile of sheets by the bed. They stand in uncomfortable silence as the sheets slowly take to flame, then - assured the fire will prosper - they turn and leave the room. 'Wrestling isn't all colouring books and action figures,' Angelo enlightens Tony as they move down the hallway. 'There's a serious side to the business. A darker side.' He pauses to look at Tony, but the man won't meet his eyes. On account of the glazed look there, few would blame him. 'My side, Tony. My side,' Angelo laughs as they exit the house, Tony following behind him with more than a few concerns for the man's sanity. Flames dance up around the walls of the house and soon the entire building is ablaze. The light shines out like a beacon through the night; like a threat. It illuminates the corpses before consuming them entirely, making them coalesce in sync. None of the MacGregors, faithful followers and enforcers of the Word, live through the night. Tonight the light has been their destroyer... Sometimes there is safety in the dark.
