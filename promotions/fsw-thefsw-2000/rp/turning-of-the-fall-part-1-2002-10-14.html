Turning of the Fall (part 1)

14 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The axe, implement of work and war. Savage in it's design and use; origins based in neccessity from a time long ago, when a super intelligent monkey crudely lashed a rock to a stick in lieu of an antelope thighbone. Over the years, the efficiency of tool has changed, but not the purpose. Smash. Split. Rend. Even now it's purpose is singular as the gleaming steel edge of an axe glints roughly under an overcast sky as it arcs toward it's target with lethal intent... **this is not a goth promo** and splits a length of knotty pine in half. The axe lies buried in the oak stump where Jason McAllister has chopped wood for his fireplace for two years now. As he fetches the chunks of pine, he mentally notes that this will probably be the last year for using this stump. It's become worn, and has started to give way in the center. Soon it's center will start to rot. Better to hallow out the center and preserve it, make a nice birdbath for the garden. Kelly'd like that.... And for a moment the color fades from his face as the lonely wail of the prairie wind echoes around him. But only for a moment, as the silence it interrupted by the jarring sounds of punk rock blasting in the distance. In annoyed discontent, Jason checks his watch. 12:15 PM Is thirteen years enough to be truly considered a 'generation gap'? Trotting across the yard in a pair of baggy pants and a hooded sweatshirt is one Kyle 'Velocity' Lamen. Kyle: Jesus, it's cooooold out here. It'd be a freeze advisory in Tallahassee. Jason: We're not in Florida anymore Toto. Suck it up a bit. Exasperated, Jason swore under his breath. He'd originally called up Lamen on the advice of Jason Core, a former PCW Extreme champion. Core said Lamen was the real deal. Somewhere in the back of McAllister's mind, a mental 'post it' note was affixed. It said to kick Core in the dick next time he saw him. McAllister had invited Velocity out to the farm for the week to go over some technical wrestling aspects and work on teamwork and non-verbal communication skills. So far it'd been a week of watching KVL roll out of bed somewhere around the crack of noon, and daydream instead of focusing on how to properly apply an armbar and roll the shoulder joint around in the socket. Simple things really, but the kid couldn't seem to focus. Kyle: Man, I couldn't find any cereal. What am I supposed to eat for breakfast? Jason: There's eggs and bacon in the fridge, but breakfast was served between the hours of 6 and 7. A groan arose from the seemingly teenage KVL. Jason: Lil' hard work'll put some hair one your chest. Kyle: But I don't want hair on my chest. Jason grunted in response, prompting Kyle to quit stalling. Kyle saw Jason's outburst last Wednesday after Nelson copped out of the match. The temper was short and the patience thin these days. McAllister was a sleeping rabid dog that he'd rather just let lie. Kyle: Ok, so what are we in for today. Single-leg's? Japanese standing leg locks? Jason: Something a little less technical than that. With a sweeping gesture, Jason motioned to the axe, and the half cord of unsplit wood that remained. Kyle: I ain't never done this before. Jason: It's real easy. Let gravity do most ot the work and just guide it down. Consider this a lesson in focus. Kyle: Yeah, right. Jason watched as Kyle took hold of the axe and swung it ungainly, only piercing halfway through the stock. Kyle struggled to get the blade unlodged, before lining up the wood on the stump again. Jason started off toward the door of the machine shed, looking for a new project to consume his time. Always something that needed to be done, had to be. Stopping for more than a moment meant thinking about her. It was easier to focus on a task, on a match, on anything but that. (to be continued)
