Turning of the Fall (part 2): Echoes

30 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Oct 30th 'Hell yes I think it's bulls#!t!' Jason McAllister spat out these words into his cellphone as he walked across the floor of the machine shed, the heels of his cowboy boot clcking angrily on the shining and recently re-sealed concrete. He gripped the phone heavily, unintentionally squeezing it till his knuckles turned white. Somehow the phone withstood the shock. It wasn't one of those new paper thin flip jobbys, but a throwback 'brick' style from the mid 90's. It wasn't flashy, but it did the job and that was enough. The phone was annoying enough without having to be 'cute'. The cellphone was one of those damned conviences that you couldn't seem to live without, especially now that damn near everyone has forgotten the uses of short-wave or Citizens Band. Being out on the road half the week, you needed to be able to be contacted. Back in the day a slight change in the carrier wave would have caught his attention, now it was the digital rendition of the Iowa Fight Song. 'I know John, it's not like he can back it up like Doyle did.' The back of Jason's neck started to flash red, along with the rest of his face. Flushing with color as the veins along his temples throbbed. If there was one thing that really, REALLY got under the skin of the Highway, it was someone setting foot in the ring that truly had no business being there. That was his 'workplace', his area of expertise; and it was not a stage for any loudmouthed jackass who felt like being a gloryhound. Worse yet, Orion seemed to think that Jason was his own personal 'clean-up' crew. Instead of having security do it's job, he sat on his hands at 'Bad Moon' and let things get out of them. It wasn't that he really minded smacking Caldera with a chair, but the fact that he was the only one out there said something about the system. 'Yeah, I saw that crap. If they wanna play gang warfare, let 'em. He can keep stacking them up, and I'll keep knocking them down.' Announcers and their delusions of grandeur aside, it had been a good night. It was good to see ol' Migs again, ExF together again, even if but for one brief night. 'Right, WE'LL keep knocking them down.' It'd got the adrenaline pumping the way it used to, the old swagger. Somewhere in the back of the mind, he could here the Astone Brothers shrieking and screaming as tables and chairs, broken glass and shattered bodies were the order of the night. 'Hey, they're the ones that're asking for this. It's not gonna be our fault when they figure out what they did wrong.' Pushy power brokers and their henchmen. Control by force of numbers. It seemed like things were coming full circle, right past the end and back to the beginning. Back to the beginning of that long road they'd travelled before. Jason reached out with a hand and pulled on the seeming mountain of canvas that stood before him, the yards of fabric falling to the floor, revealing the awesome behemoth that lay beneath. The chrome of the bulldog emblem gleamed with the vibrance of struggle, offsetting the deep, deep red that begged to run. The big Mack diesel wasn't meant for the garage, it was meant for the road. It was meant to run down the asphalt plowing through anything in it's path, and leave the tatters that remained floating in it's wake. 'I'll see ya' there... An' I'm bringin' an old friend..' Jason pressed the end button and jammed the phone in his back pocket as he fished the keys to the mammoth vehicle out of his pocket. Barely a click registered as the door came unlocked and Jason climbed up into the cab. There was a bag in the passenger seat left dormant but not forgotten, resplendant in it's blue and gold logo from a place long gone but not entirely dead. There was the slightest hint of perfume from a small purse in the sleeper section. The light scent of the jasmine made his mind swim and his guts churn. His thoughts turned to the cellphone that was now mashed into the seat under him and number imprinted for these last two years as speed dial #1. His vision momentarily flashed as red as paint on the rig, as he mindlessly jammed his key into the ignition and turned it. The sleeping diesel giant roared to life with a throaty growl and plumes of black smoke from the dual stacks. Clutch.. Gear.. Accelerate.. From the past, something wicked this way comes...
