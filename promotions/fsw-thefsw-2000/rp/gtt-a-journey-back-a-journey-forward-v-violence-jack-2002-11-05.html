-GTT- A Journey Back, a Journey Forward (v Violence Jack)

5 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It had been an emotionally turbulent week. The inner conflict within Kimbusa had faded to simple confusion, which in reality was more frightening than anything else he had experienced. Who was in control now? Had his brutally hateful heat won control, or was his merciful human side starting to regain lost ground? Was he really prepared to use any means necessary to capture victory, or would he be forced to adapt to a different world and become fully immersed in it? He needed answers. He needed guidance. Insightful as Caldera could be at times, he was not the man to seek. Kimbusa needed to speak to a life-long friend. He needed someone who knew him, who knew his inner terrain, who could accurately judge his capabilities and his intentions. He needed Yoshi. And so Kimbusa set out to find his estranged master, who he had spoken to only once since leaving prison. Courtesy of Steven Caldera’s private jet, he had been flown to a little town in Pennsylvania, the place where Yoshi had last been. At the very least, someone in town would know where to find him, had he moved on. A car had received him at the air strip, and would help him track down his mentor. And it is here, in the confines of a dark tinted Mercedes, that Kimbusa sits in brooding anxiety, seeking out his former master. He had first met Yoshi in Japan several years ago. He had recently been banished from practicing sumo, forever condemned to live without his art form. The Yakuza had taken him under their wing, which led to the hotel incident and the young woman who would plague his nightmares for the rest of his existence. After days, weeks, perhaps even months of uncontrollable rage that caused even his own allies to fear him, he fled the Yakuza. And so, having left the world of crime and the world of sumo, Kimbusa found himself with nowhere to go, and with an enormous amount of hatred and resentment both outward and inward, with nowhere to direct it. And then he met Yoshi Kimawa. It was a particularly nasty evening in a little countryside town on the island of Hokkaido. A coastal storm had rolled in and was unleashing its fury directly upon Kimbusa’s skull, soaking the shelter-less monster to the bone. The raincoat that he had found amid the grime in an alleyway did little to comfort him, as the cold rain seeped in, grinding the filth deeper into Kimbusa’s flesh, and chilling his very soul. Perhaps the chill was the only thing from causing him to explode. He had made his way to Hokkaido on the goodwill of a local family, who had picked him up on the mainland and transported him to their island at no charge. It seemed that the family was, in fact, merely afraid of Kimbusa’s response should they turn him down. But no matter, it had moved him away from Tokyo, away from the crime and corruption, and that was all he cared about. The town was antiquated and worn. The roofs looked as if they might cave in from the intense rainfall, and in fact the entire village looked as if it were abandoned. No townspeople came to greet him, no clamber was caused at the arrival of this beaten down monster. Kimbusa has looked like a demon upon his entrance to the town, and inside he felt like one. It was no wonder that not a single door opened to him; he was drenched from head to toe, from flesh to soul, and was little more than a hulking mass of darkness, dripping with rain and sin. After drifting aimlessly for an hour or two, Kimbusa finally found shelter beneath an canopy extending from a long-closed store. The store had hand-crafted pipes and hand-woven quilts on display in the windows, along with cobwebs that seemed to signal the store’s distant extinction. Kimbusa assumed that no irate owner would come to rouse him in the morning, and he might be able to rest a while beneath this limited cover. Setting himself down amid filthy puddles of blackness, Kimbusa removed his raincoat and used it as a blanket, which offered him a very slight bit of protection as he closed his eyes and fell asleep on the hard dirt ground. Hours later, he awoke. It was still dark outside, and though the rain had stopped, the black storm clouds overhead signaled its imminent return. It was early morning, and Kimbusa still seemed tired. The sleep had done him little good, managing only to stiffen his neck and back. With a drowsy glance, he turned to the store, which now had several candles lit inside. The shop owner had indeed come to open, and had stepped over the massive form at his doorstep to do so. For some reason, he had not disturbed Kimbusa. Perhaps out of respect; perhaps out of fear. It seemed as good a place as any to seek shelter. Kimbusa gathered himself and timidly pressed through the doorway and into the shop. The store smelled wondrous inside, a magnificent melding of tobacco, herbs, and incense. Almost immediately, Kimbusa’s fears and worries seemed to fade, and for a moment he felt perfectly comfortable. “Hello?” a weathered old voice called out from somewhere in the deeper regions of the store, speaking in perfectly articulated Japanese. “I’ll be with you in just a moment.” Kimbusa nodded, although he was unsure of what to say. Looking around, he found the store to be just as old and worn as it had looked from the exterior. Old mats and rugs hung from the walls, and a fire crackled in the fireplace. The room itself was lit only by a few candles and the small fire, causing the walls to dance with the reflections of the various knickknacks and collections scattered about the room. At the back was a lone black desk, which protected a curtain that hung from the ceiling. It was from behind this curtain that an old man appeared to Kimbusa, and began to study him carefully. “Can I offer you something?” he continued in his perfect Japanese. “Pipe? Rug? Cigar? Or are you merely browsing?” “I seek nothing,” Kimbusa stated flatly, not quite sure how to react to the old man. “But you do,” the man quipped back. Kimbusa was surprised by the man’s insight, especially for one so fragile looking. Standing at about five feet and six inches, and weighing no more than one hundred fifteen pounds, the withered old man seemed little more than a speck to the monster Kimbusa. The old man’s hair was thin and gray, his long beard frizzled and ungroomed. He wore a black silk cloak and smoked a long pipe, puffing some amazingly aromatic tobacco. His eyes were a dark black that almost gave Kimbusa reason to think the old man blind. But the man’s eyes were looking the big man up and down, studying him thoughtfully. “Please, sit.” The old man gestured for Kimbusa to take a seat, and Kimbusa dumbly obliged, still confounded and weary. The man disappeared behind the curtain again for a few moments, giving Kimbusa time to wring his hair dry from the continued rain. The rain had begun falling once again, though not as hard as the night before. Despite the rain’s mercy, water had begun to leak in through a few cracks in the weathered ceiling, falling with a light drip into buckets carefully placed below. The man emerged quickly into the store once again, this time carrying with him a short wooden walking stick, and a glass of warm water. Without a word the old man presented the water to Kimbusa, who took it with great pleasure and sucked it down in one quick swallow. The old man finally made his way to a spot about five feet from Kimbusa’s seat, and took a spot on the ground as well. “Now, my son, let us have a look.” Kimbusa, still having said very little, sat uncomfortably for a few moments while the old man studied him. He considered every aspect of Kimbusa’s form, every inch of Kimbusa’s face. After a few awkward moments, the old man finally came to his conclusion, which he stated to Kimbusa with uncanny succinctness. “You are running from something. But it has followed you.” For a moment, Kimbusa simply blinked. The man’s words had been thin in speech, but his eyes seemed to glow with power. His black eyes seemed to light up with some heavenly inspiration, some sort of divine insight. The man’s entire body seemed to be projecting a hypnotic power, and it immediately took Kimbusa under its spell. Unable to answer, Kimbusa simply sat on the floor, staring in fascination at this odd old man who seemed to have been able to sum up Kimbusa’s existence in a few words. “What are you running from, my son?” the old man inquired in a soft, rumbling voice. The soothing sound of the man’s voice, the hypnotic power emanating from his eyes, and the overwhelmingly comforting aroma wafting through the room caused Kimbusa’s eyes to sink closed, to the point where he seemed to be aware only of the words being spoken to him by this perfect stranger. His head, the entire room, all began to spin lightly, as he left his own body and seemed to walk with this ancient prophet in a place far more divine than the decrepit store he was in. “What answers do you seek that you do not already possess?” the man pressed on. His voice seemed to begin to break, to crackle, to crumble beneath the weight of his words. But they remained so perfect, so divine, that they penetrated Kimbusa’s very soul, soothing it, calming the fires. “You do not see that the demons within were created at your own will, and your own will can defeat them. You do not see that your anger is self-contained, and can be eased from within. You hold the answers within your mind, my son, and this is where the battle begins.” Kimbusa’s mind was flying. The aromatic old store had conquered him, and the reality he had entered with had faded away into a world so perfect that it could only be described as heaven. There were powers working aside this elderly man that Kimbusa had never felt, had never considered. His world had always been dark, had smelled foul, had been cold and unforgiving. But this place, wherever he had been taken… it was bright, it was alive, it had a majestic beauty and a calming aura that brought Kimbusa to a state of total ecstasy, to the point that even the old man’s words had faded, and he was left alone in the wonderful void of heaven. But in an instant, the room was plunged into darkness by the terrifying sound of an explosion of incredible proportions. The explosion rumbled off into the distance, ripping through the air as it went. Thunder; the storm was waging war again. The room was darker than before. One of the candles had blown out from the cold draft flooding in from a crack in the wall. The returning rain had blocked out any semblance of natural light, leaving the monstrous sumo and the fragile old man alone in a small corner of the store, the only two bodies illuminated in the rotting store. “What is your name, son?” the man inquired, much to Kimbusa’s surprise. He was still recovering from his abrupt return to reality, and all at once his fears, trepidations, and hatred flooded back within, sending the big man reeling back into a state of total exhaustion. Realizing that he had not been breathing, Kimbusa sucked in a deep gasp of air, relishing the cold oxygen. He finally recovered enough to meet the old man’s eyes, which were black and barren again, waiting patiently for their answer. “My name… is Kimbusa.” The old man smiled politely and raised his pipe to his mouth. After inhaling a deep breath of smoke, his eyes seemed to light up again. “Kimbusa,” he repeated quietly, sending smoke pouring out of his mouth, as if the name itself had combusted and was extinguished in the old man’s words… Kimbusa needs to see him. He opens his eyes in the car again, having relived this memory against his will. His sense of urgency increased to the point that he felt as though he might leap from the car and pursue his mentor on foot to wherever he might have gone. After a few moments, Kimbusa returns to the vehicle in full, and realizes that the car has stopped moving. He wearily peers out the tinted window to an old building across the street. Yoshi’s dojo. He was there. Anxiously the big man steps out of the car, causing the car to rise at least two inches at the relief of his weight. Taking a moment to recover from his journey to the past, Kimbusa takes a deep breath of the warm, dry Pennsylvania air. It had to be close to eighty five, but the cool breeze brought in by the setting sun was enough to make the incoming night rather pleasant. But the agreeable weather did little to ease Kimbusa’s anxiety; he had spoken to Yoshi only one since the night he brutally assaulted him in the middle of an Old School Wrestling ring. And it had not been a pleasant conversation. Nevertheless, Kimbusa had to go inside, he had to speak with Yoshi. Any insight would be helpful, complimentary or not. Kimbusa crosses the small cracked pavement street to the sidewalk across from the car. The street itself seems strangely reminiscent of that old town back in Japan where he had first met his master. Perhaps it was this resemblance that had evoked the memory of their first conversation. This section of town is older and definitely Asian dominated, as Asian cuisine, Asian stores, even signs inscribed in Japanese occupy this small row of shops and apartments. The sidewalk seems gray and worn, the buildings themselves growing old and tired. Kimbusa himself seems to feel old and tired, as if the town was immersing itself within Kimbusa’s body. His knees actually feel weak from exhaustion as he reaches for the knob and turns it with an awful squeak. Immediately he knew that he had found his master. The exact aroma of tobacco and incense assaulted his body and drove the weakness from his legs. His mind immediately felt comforted, eased at the scent of Yoshi’s heavenly concoctions. He eagerly presses inward into the dojo, now with a renewed sense of confidence as he seeks his master. The dojo itself is not unlike Yoshi’s original shop. The walls are papered with plain white parchment, tinted yellow from months of smoking. The floor creaks loudly under the weight of Kimbusa’s solid steps, as he bounds forward, searching corridor after corridor for his estranged mentor. After only a moment of searching, a young Japanese girl emerges from a side room to investigate the movement, and is shocked to find this monster at her doorstep. Kimbusa ignores her fright and immediately inquires about Yoshi. Speaking in the perfectly articulated Japanese his master always insisted upon, Kimbusa asks, “Where is Master Kimawa?” The young girl seems confused, still a bit frightened at the monster’s presence. Kimbusa grows impatient, as it becomes apparent that this young girl speaks no Japanese, despite her heritage. “Where is Yoshi?” Kimbusa asks again, this time in less than perfect English. The girl’s face suddenly drops. With a deep sigh, she turns away from Kimbusa. “Sir, Master Yoshi passed away many weeks ago.” No. She had misunderstood. “Master Kimawa, Yoshi Kimawa. Where is he?” But the girl’s face remains dim. “He is not here, sir. Master Kimawa is dead.” Kimbusa prepares to register another protest, but reality suddenly struck him hard. He felt as if he had been shot, shot right in the head. For a moment he wanted to lash out with anger, then to shout out in pain, before finally sinking to his knees in utter defeat. “How?” is all he is able to choke out, before his throat swells shut with overwhelming sadness. “We aren’t sure, sir. We awoke one morning, and he was just… gone. The doctors tried to revive him, but…” the girl trails off. A tear seems to have formed in her eye, but Kimbusa’s own tears are far more pained. With a distraught and agonizing look he lifts his head to face the girl, and the two exchanged painful stares. After a moment, Kimbusa lowers his head to mask his emotion, and speaks again. “My name is Kimbusa. Did he leave anything for me?” The young girl’s face seems to brighten slightly at the mention of the name. Quickly she turns and moves back into the room from where she came, before emerging just as swiftly with a small paper in her hands. Bowing her head slightly she presents it to Kimbusa, and shuffles her way out of the room. Kimbusa swallows down the rage and opens the note, reading the words handwritten in perfect Japanese. My dearest Kimbusa, As you have no doubt sought me out, and as you have no doubt learned of my passing, I leave you these finals words. I have never stopped watching over you, my son. And I never would have allowed you to stray from the right path. Embrace Steven Caldera as your mentor, for you can and shall learn from him. But never forget your homeland, never forget your nature, never forget your past, and never forget me. You have grown strong, my son. Do not let my death go unanswered. Conquer your adversaries, and do it for my honor. Your loving father, Yoshi
