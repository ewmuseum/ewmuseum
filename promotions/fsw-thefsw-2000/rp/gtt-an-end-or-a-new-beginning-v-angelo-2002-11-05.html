-GTT- An End or a New Beginning (v Angelo)

5 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
When a human being loses all compassion, all sympathy, all capacity for remorse, at what point do they cease to be human? At what point do they cross the line between human and animal? At what level does one move beyond simple existentialism and into an ultimately inhumane and evil existence? That point, where a human being sacrifices all his inherent traits and evolves beyond basic treachery, can be found in the eyes of one Steven Caldera. And it is this inhumanity that is forcing Kimbusa back down into the depths where his demons lie, the same demons he had so recently conquered. The innermost workings of the mind are revealed only by the eyes, and only to those who are well tuned enough to perceive them. Kimbusa’s eyes exuded pain and suffering, hate and anger. But beneath the pain, beneath the fury, a slight bit of light reveals that the anger is simply a disease plaguing an essentially good man. But in the eyes of Steven Caldera lies something more frightening, something more terrible. Within his eyes lie the secrets to the innermost workings of his mind; he seeks power and power alone, and will cause whatever harm necessary to achieve it. Kimbusa learned to see these traits, to see the deeper regions of the mind, from his now deceased mentor Yoshi. Yoshi was a man so finely attuned to the higher powers of the world that he could see nearly all that dwelled in one’s mind. It was nearly impossible to hide anything from Yoshi, and Kimbusa had benefited greatly from learning this gift. And now, staring directly into Caldera’s deep blue eyes, Kimbusa finds himself confronted with a man so devoid of feeling, so empty of compassion, that he is truly disgusted with his very presence. Kimbusa had committed horrific crimes, he had inflicted terrible pain and suffering upon helpless targets. But his crimes were a result of his tumultuous inner battle, and had left him with an uncontrollable feeling of remorse and self-hatred. Kimbusa was not devoid of feeling, he simply had lost control of his actions. But Steven Caldera was in complete control, and was entirely conscious of his crimes. Caldera had never killed, but he had taken lives; a lawyer by trade, Caldera had destroyed lives for the profit of his clients, and subsequently profit of his own. It was with these tainted funds that Caldera had built his professional wrestling legacy, and it was this blood money that had afforded Kimbusa the opportunities he had been so blessed to have. It had been a long battle. Opponent after opponent had fallen beneath his might, and with each victory came a slight bit of release, a slight bit of recovery. Kimbusa had found himself free enough to feel the most intense and awesome emotion of them all; Kimbusa had found love. But Caldera must have possessed the ability to read Kimbusa as well as Kimbusa read Caldera, as Caldera had quickly and efficiently sought to return Kimbusa to his terrible, infuriated state. Kimbusa had fought the relapse as best he could, but he was far too proud to allow this simple American businessman to walk over him. He had seen Caldera’s intentions immediately, but this return to the ways of old was inevitable. Caldera had accomplished exactly what he wanted to accomplish. He had brought out the intensity in Kimbusa once again, and to Caldera’s credit, he had once again manufactured Kimbusa for victory. In his mind, Kimbusa was entirely unsure of what the next match with Deville would bring. Would he release his anger and his fury once again and ride his frustration to victory? Or would his resentment and rage towards Caldera cause him to let down his guard and put an end to Caldera’s dream? Kimbusa had accomplished everything he wanted by reaching the semi-finals. There was nothing more in this tournament for him. But as long as Caldera was his primary support, he would be forced to play ball. And that meant beating Deville. And so it was with great reluctance that Kimbusa submitted to another unpleasant jet flight to Caldera’s wrestling headquarters in another anonymous American metropolis. He had found Caldera sitting behind an overly extravagant desk in front of a tremendous expanse of windows which opened to the city skyline beyond. Kimbusa had not bothered to inquire about the location of his destination nor the significance of the building. He had no doubt that Caldera would get him to his matches on time. Caldera, for his part, had been content to bear down on Kimbusa with his cold eyes for quite some time. It was in this time that Kimbusa was finally able to grasp the extent of Caldera’s duplicity. Caldera was a respected and competent businessman; which in itself made him a truly despicable inhuman being. For his part, Kimbusa had remained calm. He had fought away the desire to confront Caldera violently, to break down and allow his frustration to release upon his benefactor. But Caldera’s intensely heinous stare, backed by the terrible truths revealed in his eyes, was slowly deteriorating Kimbusa’s ability to appear at peace. Kimbusa finally realized that continuing this staredown would be a mistake. He quickly turns his eyes away and begins idly studying Caldera’s office; admittedly, it was quite a sight. Rare Southeast Asian and tropical flowers occupy each corner of the office, of which there are many. They emit a pleasant odor that brings a rather elegant aura to the otherwise cold room. The walls are covered with an off white paint, nicely textured to appear at least seventy years old. Paintings of various landscapes and animals hang from the walls in perfect intervals of twelve inches; some appear to be well-crafted replicas, and other seem to be authentic. The floor is a deep white marble that appears to be without imperfection. The office itself must be at least six hundred square feet, far too expansive to be reasonable accommodations for one man. But the office seemed to match Caldera’s extravagant and self-absorbed personality; in fact, it was Caldera’s replica title belts hanging above the desk that were obviously a major focal point of the room, second only to Caldera himself. Kimbusa’s eyes finally return to Caldera’s, which have not moved or changed in any way. He remains fixed on Kimbusa, bearing down on him with a powerful glower that few men could withstand. Kimbusa, frustrated by Caldera’s silence, finally speaks up. “What am I doing here?” Kimbusa vehemently demands. Caldera is slow to reply. At first, his eyes remain locked in cold combat with Kimbusa’s, before finally showing the slightest bit of light. An answer is forthcoming. “I think you already know. This is nothing personal, Kimbusa. It’s just business. And I think we both know that I’m the only one here capable of handling such business.” Caldera says this in a very matter of fact manner that is almost insulting in its simplicity. Kimbusa is not satisfied with this response. “Then, while you are denying me my peace, perhaps you could at least provide me with some useful information,” he responds bitterly. Kimbusa sits back in his rather uncomfortably small wooden chair, and Caldera likewise reclines in his leather seat. Caldera stops for a few moments, returning once again to his silently aggravating glare. Kimbusa refuses to accept this and quickly prepares to comment once more, but Caldera stops him with a quick flip of the wrist. With a light thud a white file folder plops down on the table in front of Kimbusa, which Kimbusa had not noticed was in Caldera’s lap. With a slight bit of hesitation, Kimbusa sits forward and opens it. “What is this?” he inquires suspiciously, flipping through the pages with one apprehensive eye on Caldera. Caldera smiles with a slight air of arrogance and provides Kimbusa with a rather confusing answer. “These are the recent movements of your next opponent, Angelo Deville, as catalogued over the past five days. I thought you might be interested to see what he’s been up to.” Kimbusa’s full attention falls upon Caldera for a moment, who sits with a cocky, informed smile on his face. Kimbusa returns to the folder and finds some rather interesting information. “Japan? Why has Deville been in Japan?” “Little do you realize, Kimbusa, this Angelo Deville is a rather dangerous man. He’s a killer, much like yourself,” Caldera states simply. With those last few words, Kimbusa begins to wince; for the first time it becomes apparent that this meeting will not continue without incident. But Caldera presses on despite Kimbusa’s obvious discomfort. “He went to Japan to find you, Kimbusa. He went to Japan to kill you.” This simple statement is enough to send the big man’s mind flying with rage. The entire office melts away into a sea of hellaciously infuriated blackness. His temples begin throbbing with explosive anger, which he feels that he might be forced to release at any moment. Only the well-dressed Caldera remains visible in this tumultuous sea of hatred; and Kimbusa is forced to remind himself that it is not wise to assault his only ally. Caldera obviously senses the anger building within Kimbusa, and smiles at his handiwork. But he refuses to cease the assault. “Deville is a fool, Kimbusa. If he comes to the matches this weekend, he will be taken into custody by the feds, and he’ll be thrown into your cold iron prison cell. He doesn’t want that to happen. The only way he can continue now is if you do not show up. And the only way you aren’t showing up is if you’re six feet under.” The last sentence uttered by Caldera seems to pass Kimbusa by in a rippling echo of nonsense. Kimbusa’s reality has suddenly boiled down to this overwhelming sense of anger; not just anger, but a terrible wave of remorse at the same time. “He has killed?” Kimbusa manages to utter under his breath, his face turned to the floor. “Yes, he has,” Caldera replies plainly. Kimbusa turns his eyes forward towards his benefactor, who becomes noticeably unsettled. For a moment, Kimbusa catches a slight hint of regret in Caldera’s eyes, as if he is reconsidering his plans to provoke the big man and incite a relapse of anger. But Kimbusa is beyond caring for this American man’s feelings and insecurities. A moment from his past has suddenly returned to him, and his terrible world of deceit and murder has flooded once again into his mind. “Thirty three,” Kimbusa mutters, his voice trembling beneath the weight of his emotions. Caldera tilts his head quizzically, his blank stare now giving away to genuine interest. “I have taken the lives of thirty three human beings. And I can remember the name and the face of every one of them. I can still see their terrified expressions as I take their lives from them, as I damn their souls to places no mortal has business interfering with. And I remember the names and faces of their families, and I remember the smell of their tears as I spared their lives.” Kimbusa’s eyes suddenly begin to well up with the surprising appearance of tears. They do not fall from his eyes, but cause the growing infuriated redness to shimmer in the light from Caldera’s windows. “What does Deville know of life? What does Deville know of death? Does he not care for the people he kills? Does he not realized the consequences of what he does? Who is he to decide the fate of another human? He is worse than you, Steven Caldera, he is more despicable and more inhuman. You think he is dangerous? No, this man is not dangerous. This man is afraid. He meddles with forces he cannot comprehend. Steven Caldera, it is because of you that these images are still haunting me. And it is because of you that Angelo Deville will soon know a fate worse than death. Do you not realize what you have done, Mr. Caldera? You have sealed the fate of this man. You have ended his life. And that makes you no better than the two of us.” Kimbusa’s head finally drops again, failing under the weight of his thoughts. Caldera remains rather cold despite Kimbusa’s breakdown, and seems to become only more uncaring with each passing word. “What the hell is wrong with you?” is all Caldera can muster. Kimbusa slowly raises his head to face a steaming Caldera. How could this man say such a thing? “Don’t you see? Deville is waiting for this. He’s waiting for this weakness. You cannot change your nature, Kimbusa. You are, by nature, a killer. You are a criminal. That is the way it was, and that’s the way it always will be.” Caldera seems to spit these words at Kimbusa with a lace of venom that penetrates Kimbusa’s flesh and attacks his very soul. And this serves only to inflame Kimbusa’s anger. “Criminal? I am a criminal?! What do you know of crime, Steven Caldera? What does Angelo Deville know of crime? You Americans are all the same. You have so much that you’ve forgotten what this world is about. You take possession for yourself and think yourself a criminal. Deville takes lives and thinks himself a killer. But what do you know of true crime? I am not as ignorant as you think, Caldera. Deville murdered men in the destruction of a building. It was the act of a coward. But tell me, Steven Caldera, have you, has Deville ever looked into the eyes of another human being, have either of you ever seen or smelt the terror and the fear of a man confronted with his own death? And have you ever looked into those same eyes moments after having silenced them, only to see yourself in their reflection? What do you know of crime? He has taken lives, Caldera, but he has never taken a soul. The true crime is robbing these men of their spirit… and robbing yourself at the same time.” Caldera takes a few moments to soak this statement in. Kimbusa is visibly shaking at the very power of his thoughts, the full extent of which he can never hope to express in words. His mind is still flying with emotion, threatening to rip his body and anything around it apart; yet one final shred of control remains, and Caldera prepares to tear it away. “Look at you,” he begins. “Deville thinks you’re nothing but a wannabe gangster. A dribbling fool who can do little more than wallow in self-pity. An intellectually impotent madman capable of nothing more than mindless self-destruction. And who can refute this? Who can blame him for coming to such a conclusion. Look at you! How can you be so weak?” Caldera has finally succeeded in removing that last shred of restraint. With his full weight, Kimbusa stands from his chair, bringing the right end of Caldera’s desk with him. Kimbusa sends the five hundred pound oak desk toppling onto its side, sending papers and contracts flying in a mess of debris. Caldera winces at the sound of wood cracking marble, and watches in silent satisfaction as Kimbusa reaches towards the toppled table and splits a protruding leg free of its parent. Wooden club in hand, Kimbusa turns and launches the leg to the corner of the room several feet away, and manages to perfectly crack a clay pot apart, sending Caldera’s tropical foliage sprawling to the floor below in a mess of leaves and mud. Kimbusa turns back to Caldera and reaches down into his chair, pulling him to his feet by his lapel. “Weakness?! What do you know of weakness? What do you know of my weakness? If I am a criminal, Steven Caldera, if I am a killer, then who are you to call me weak? You know nothing of me, you know nothing of my capabilities! And to call me weak, to call me incapable, simply because I feel remorse for caving in the skull of an eighteen year old girl, will not go unpunished.” Caldera shoots back with equal intensity, despite the big man’s grip on his collar. “Then prove it! Punish Angelo Deville for this insult! Show him what it is like to be a killer! Show him what being a criminal is all about! Destroy him!” Kimbusa quickly lifts Caldera two feet from the ground, and launches him mercilessly to the marble floor below. Caldera groans as he collides with the hard floor, his back buckling beneath the weight of the blow. Kimbusa steps forward to continue the assault, but quickly backs away, second guessing the immanent beating. Kimbusa clenches his fists and closes his eyes, forcing the anger from his body. And for the first time, the faces of these demons become visible; there are thirty three demons assaulting his mind, one for each of his victims. One by one, Kimbusa drives them back with the power of his will alone. His body begins convulsing with a commanding tremor of resolve, as he fervently presses the demons further and further from his mind. For a moment, he finds himself in a new place. He opens his eyes and finds them stung with a glorious brightness. He shuts them again quickly to block out the light, but cannot help but open them again to savor more of the radiance. In an instant, he recognizes this place; he is standing on the beach on his last day in Japan, his master Yoshi Kimawa shuffling in the sand behind him. Frothy waves rush from the Pacific Ocean and waft over his feet, drowning the heated anger amid a coolness that pacifies Kimbusa’s entire body. The sand sizzles and crackles as the waves rush back to sea, filling the beach with the soothing sounds of oceanside. Gulls call from the distance, signaling the dawning of a beautiful new day, and the sun beams down upon Kimbusa’s face, warming his cold, weary eyes. A wistful smile fades across Kimbusa’s face, and he finds himself incapacitated by this fantastic dreamscape. A light breeze rushes in from the sea, sending Kimbusa’s loose black hair flailing wildly and freely. He takes in a deep breath of seaside air, allowing the warm scent of sea salt to comfort his weary head. For a moment he finds himself perfectly at peace again, exactly as far from that terrible office as he possibly could be. And then a voice enters the trance. “Kimbusa? Kimbusa?” The faint call is coming from directly in front of him. It is his master, Yoshi. The ancient man stands before Kimbusa with an unbelievable aura surrounding him. It seems for a moment as if the uncharacteristic brightness is radiating not from the full sun but from Yoshi himself, and the entire scene is revolving around his spiritual powers. Kimbusa’s trance seems to be feeding from Yoshi’s powerful presence, and he finds himself warmed by the presence of his adopted father. “Kimbusa, why do you let this man control you?” Yoshi inquires in a voice too powerful to be emanating from such an ancient old man. Kimbusa finds himself unable to respond, as he stands in a daze, not fully capable of understanding what stands before him. “Find the strength, my son. Find the strength within. Find the strength to conquer your adversaries yourself. You do not need this anger to guide you any longer. Kimbusa… you have something far greater to empower you. You have love, Kimbusa. You have found peace in the love of my daughter. Seek her out, Kimbusa. She loves you, Kimbusa, just as I do.” Kimbusa finds himself near tears once more, as the much missed voice of his mentor instills a new life into his frame. He finds himself strengthened by his master’s brief words, strong enough to see the reality of his trance. The beach, the air, the waves, everything slowly fades. The last vision Kimbusa can make out is that of his fading master, who leaves him with one final, solemn statement. “I love you, my son.” In an instant, Kimbusa finds himself in the cold office once more. Caldera has managed to prop himself against the fallen wooden table, gingerly tending to his wounded body and his bruised ego. With a glower Caldera notices Kimbusa’s closed eyes reopen, and he immediately returns to his relentless verbal assault. “You son of a bitch. I have had it. When this thing is over, I’m going to see to it that you’re working for me the rest of your life. You’re mine now, Kimbusa, I OWN you. Do you hear me?!” But if Kimbusa had heard him, he was easily hiding it. Kimbusa does not look at Caldera; he does not react in any way. With an abnormally light heart, Kimbusa turns towards Caldera’s office door. For the first time, he would be boarding Caldera’s jet with a definite destination in mind. Caldera watches astounded as Kimbusa turns his massive back to him, and begins shuffling away. “Hey! Don’t you turn your back on me! Get back here, you son of a bitch! I’m not through with you!” But Kimbusa disregards the verbal tirade, and reaches for the door. Without one moment of hesitation, Kimbusa pulls the door open and exits the room, slamming the door behind him with an authoritative slam, driving home his message to Caldera. Caldera slumps down onto the cool floor in defeat. For a moment, he had achieved his goal. But Kimbusa truly had accomplished his goal. Kimbusa had gained control of his mind; he had gained control of his heart. And now that Kimbusa’s goals had been reached… Caldera could sense his slipping from his grasp.
