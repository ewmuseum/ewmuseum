GTT · Fills A Lot Of Coffins

5 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: whoo~! One underlying purpose of prison is to provide time with oneself... Things obviously aren't gestating properly upstairs, so the criminal is allotted an opportunity to reflect on and consider where life went awry. Truly one of life's most rejuvenating experiences, total seclusion of the spirit and mind rehabilitates more effectively than a thousand soothingly friendly voices. Angelo Deville slumps against the cold cement wall of his cell, trying to ignore the mysterious yellow tinge that seems to have infested everything in the glorified cubicle. The mattress beneath him is completely soiled with it and he can almost imagine it oozing from the wall to his back, pulsing and straining with insatiable hunger. Time drags like a buried mule, yet Angelo clings to it as he would his very salvation. Already seven thirty and still dark as midnight, he marvels numbly, bloodshot red eyes burning into the black. Where he is, dark as midnight is not pitch black. There is a dim yellow light on the ceiling directly above the bunkbed bathing the room in an eerie buttery glow. It is never turned off, for security reasons. Despite this, guards still patrol with flashlights and shine them on the beds every fifteen minutes like clockwork. Which is how Deville knows what time it is... He's been up all night counting it away. What kind of an institution doesn't have any windows? How do people live like this? his mind rambles on, the words floating across the barren blankness of his conscience like a presidential speech across a teleprompter. A man cannot survive in this world if he doesn't look out into it... No wonder we're surrounded by losers. How can you win if you can't see beyond yourself? The entire night has been a storm of such pseudo-revelations, hastily bludgeoning through his being and bestowing unbridled bitterness. The rebirth associated with introspection eludes Deville, just as it has throughout the rest of his short tenure behind bars. Instead of the promised peace, he's plagued by unyielding anxiety that only grows with each passing of the guard. So much for satisfaction atop the mountain... Perhaps being alone isn't all it's cracked up to be. 'You up yet?' A raspy, sleep-laden voice calls from the bottom bunk. Apparently he isn't alone. Angelo shudders and decides pretending to be asleep is his best option. His bail hearing is in two hours, breakfast in ten... If he can get through the next ten minutes without getting bogged down in another conversation with this fool, perhaps he'll be forever spared the privilege of his rambling. Perhaps he'll get out of here without having to kill anyone. Time alone would have been good, Deville sneers to himself, willing the man below him to have a sudden heart attack. We shouldn't have to deal with this... We shouldn't have to deal with anyone. It won't be long, though... This so-called legend will be behind us in no time. Deville doesn't even hear the bed beneath him shuffle as he addresses his mind as if speaking to a group. It isn't until the head pops up and those two beady black eyes lock onto his that he realises he's actually going to have to entertain this fruitcake. 'I knew you were awake,' the elder gentleman says with a smile, his face wrinkling up like an accordion. 'I could hear you thinking.' The wink following the statement elicits another shudder from the mobster, and it is with closed eyes and a ruefully shaking head that he addresses the man. 'Go back to bed, Kyogo... I don't want any of your shit right now.' The words come out as a snarl, and Kyogo's face contorts with pain and confusion. Deville raises his finger as if to say more, then thinks better of it and simply continues shaking his head. In just one day he'd heard enough from this man to last him a lifetime. 'Something is troubling you...' The voice is soft and patient, sympathetic in a disturbing way. 'I'll bet I can help... I've been everywhere and done everything... Twice.' Kyogo slowly backs up to the wall and leans against it, keeping those eyes trained on Deville all the while. His orange jumpsuit is identical to Angelo's, only much smaller, and long black hair frames his face like an upside down mop. 'Fuck off,' Deville growls, turning his attention to the cell door and fixing it there. 'Your wisdom on life has no place with me... Or anyone else who's sane.' 'Why do you hate, Angelo?' Kyogo persists, his eyebrows arching down in concern. 'I don't hate... I pity. I pity people like you... People who've lost their balls. People who used to do whatever it takes to get the job done, but have since been brainwashed and forced to swallow moral and societal dogma of what should and should not be done.' Angelo pauses to reflect a minute, then turns an icy glare on his cellmate. 'You don't want my pity, Kyogo... I'm much more compassionate than I let on... I'll gladly put you out of your misery.' Kyogo chuckles at the threat. 'So you pity me because I do not kill anymore, as you do? I've been down that road, friend, and it's a dead end. Life is all about karma... Balance. Yin and yang.' The lights suddenly flicker on in the hallway, followed immediately by the main light in their room. There is suddenly commotion outside as the guards begin opening the cells so the daily routine may begin anew. 'Balance my ass,' Deville sneers as he leaps down from his bunk, pausing momentarily where he lands to loom over Kyogo. 'Balance isn't for the individual... It's for the whole. And since I want more, others must have less. You must have less. If you don't like it, fight for it.' Angelo turns and moves over to the doorway, waiting impatiently for the guards to save him from this conversation. 'You can take nothing from me,' Kyogo almost sings, still completely unruffled. 'Because I have nothing. Nothing of importance can be physically possessed, my friend, nor can it be taken away.' 'That's the mating call of the pussy, Kyogo, and you know it. You have nothing because others already took it all. Now you feign this moral and ethical highground because you don't have what it takes to be your own man. You're too busy trying to accommodate everyone else you've forgotten who you are.' 'And you?' Kyogo sidles silently up beside Angelo, causing the bigger man to start in annoyance. 'I know who I am... And I don't hide it for anyone or anything... I don't modify my thoughts because yours disagree. That's what it is to be a champion,' Deville spares him a glance as the guard mercifully opens the gate and the two file through. 'You might have known that once, but I doubt it.' 'A champion of whom? The people? How can people appreciate one who cares nothing for their existence?' Angelo speeds up but Kyogo stays right beside him. 'How can people look up to someone who would kill them without remorse?' 'Because deep down,' Angelo begins in a condescending tone, narrowing his eyes at the congregation around him. 'Deep down everyone wishes they could do it.' 'Untrue. The people are better than that... They'll choose peace, understanding, and love over selfish vigilantism every day of the week and twice on Sunday. You need to open your eyes and look around, son.' The men arrange themselves in a single file line to be counted and escorted down to the mess hall, most keeping quietly to themselves and glaring around at everyone else from behind walled eyes. Deville turns around so that he's facing Kyogo, his face the very picture of scepticism. 'You're nuts,' he scoffs. 'People want bloodshed. They envy ambition and the ability to make things happen. They're all sheep... They crave power to rally behind. Real physical power.' Kyogo is already shaking his head, and Angelo's hands ball up into fists at his sides. He's tired of this cycle. 'They seek the power of spirit and moral conviction above all. You're deluding yourself... You keep up this attitude and you will grow old alone, friendless. Society dislikes aggression.' Kyogo pauses for an instant, seeming to revel in the vein pulsing in Deville's forehead. 'Open up your eyes and look around you for once. You'll see how the r-' The word mutates into a sickening crack as Deville's left fist shatters the old man's jaw and crumples him to the floor. A resounding cheer strikes up from the onlookers as Deville mercilessly kicks the already mangled face with feverish intensity. A puddle of blood begins forming around Kyogo's head, and drops of it splatter across the wall. All of the inmates are hollering with unmitigated glee, craving the violence like a heroin addict does the juice. One of the guards tackles Deville from behind, muscling him up against the wall while other guards come over to help with restraints. Angelo offers little resistance, having accomplished what he set out to do. 'Power!' He shouts at the unconscious Kyogo over the lusting of the crowd. 'It's all about power!' Some people are better left alone. ~><>...<><~ 'The court calls the state versus Angelo Deville,' rasps a voice that sounds ironically like a recording. The stenographer sits back at her desk as the backdoor opens and Deville is hustled through by the bailiff, still wearing his orange jump suit and dragging chains from his wrists and ankles. A murmur breaks out among the crowd and a few people in the back begin making hasty sketches of the alleged serial murderer. Deville's gaze takes in everyone in attendance, defying them with an arrogance befitting a free man. The courtroom is standard-issue, with ominous double doors leading to rows and row of benches, followed by the tables set out for the prosecutors and defence. A knee high wooden wall separates the involved parties from the spectators, and runs along the right side of the wall to create a section for a jury. Up against the far wall is a grand stand full of little cubicles for various legal personnel, including the judge, who sits atop the highest pedestal in all his grey-haired glory. Just behind him on the wall is a full-sized American flag, spread majestically to remind the masses to whom their duty lies. There is a prosecutor in place, standing regally beside his table while he glares smug challenges at his new opponent. Deville smirks at him as the bailiff escorts him past and to his own table, then turns to face the judge. A small black nameplate rests before his honour, identifying him as Justice Markham. Markham scours down at the empty table beside Deville. 'Where is counsel for the defense?' The judge demands in a booming voice that belies the frail frame hidden beneath the gown. The prosecutor puffs himself up to answer, but Deville beats him to the punch. 'My lawyer will be here shortly, I'm sure,' he glares over at the clean-cut prosecutor in his black and blue suit. 'He wouldn't miss this for the world.' Markham quirks an eyebrow at this, but leaves the question unasked, opting for another instead. 'He better show up soon... We're off to recess in five. Who is representing you?' The judge's stare is cold and penetrating, deeply intense. Angelo imagines that many stalwart men have cracked under the spell of those eyes, and guesses that judge must be used to getting answers. 'You'll have to wait and see,' Angelo teases, prompting a scandalised murmur from all those in attendance. 'But in the meantime, I'm more than happy to represent myself. It's just a bail hearing, afterall... Though I must warn you my wallet is in my other suit.' This causes an upstart that is immediately quashed by a rapping of the gavel. 'Order!' the judge cries. 'Order!' The room goes deathly silent. 'I advise you begin taking things a bit more seriously, Mr. Deville. You are up on very serious charges here, and I must tell you I am not pleased with your decision to proceed without counsel. It is your decision though, so...' He turns to the prosecutor. 'Any objections to this man receiving bail?' 'Yes, Your Honour...' He almost shouts, inclining his head slightly to the judge. 'We have been monitoring Mr. Deville for quite some time now, and I tell you with personal assurance that he is a very dangerous and unstable individual. He is charged with 23 counts of first degree murder, extortion, racketeering, money laundering, conspiracy to commit fraud, and the list just goes on and on. 'The state feels it would be a great disservice to our community and the legal system in general if this man is allowed to leave on his own recognisance. He mindlessly and remorselessly trades the lives of others for a short continuation of his own success and freedom. The most recent of which we have evidence about is the murder of over thirty special agents sent to arrest him at his home.' The prosecutor intersperses his speech with occasional pauses to cast a disgusted look at Deville, who shows the slightest hint of a smile and nothing more. 'His merciless destruction of anyone set in his path makes him a threat to all who must share his world. He will unabashedly go down any muddy road to obtain what he desires. How many must needlessly suffer and lose to facilitate this one man's victory?' He concludes his sermon with this heart-wrenching question, and the reporters in the crowd scribble frantically into their notepads. The prosecutor nods to the judge, then turns to audience so they may admire him also. 'And Mr. Deville?' The judge prompts, looking down at the report on the case before him 'what say you in your defence?' 'Well, firstly, I don't know what evidence is being falsified here, but there is nothing that paints me in a murderous light,' Deville begins with a pious glare at the prosecutor. The look is returned. 'I am a professional wrestler and simple businessman, nothing more. I pay my taxes, attend political functions, donate to charity... And in return I must fa-' Deville cuts off as the double doors rip open and bang against the wall on either side. A wave of gasps run through the gathered people as each turn their head to discover the source of the intrusion. Every set of eyes is twice the size it was mere moments before, but the newcomer doesn't even seem to notice as he stalks purposefully towards the front of the court. Those in the aisle seats shirk back in fear as he passes by, as if a passing touch from his trenchcoat would send them to the reaper. He walks over to stand beside Deville and places his briefcase on the table, then turns and gives an apologetic nod to the judge for the sake of formality. 'Forgive me, your honour,' a voice like crumbling bones booms effortlessly from the giant as he pats his client on the back. Garbed in a straight black suit jacket, the lawyer stands a full head above Deville, and head and shoulders above everyone else. Long black hair cascades down to his shoulders, skirting an odd skull mask that gives him the appearance of a giant apocalyptic corpse. Glowing red eyes pierce out from beneath that mask, swirling and coalescing like molten rock. 'I am Hessian,' he introduces himself plainly, and another ripple of whispers runs through the crowd. 'Splendid,' the judge says sarcastically, impatiently clasping his hands before him. 'Allow me to catch you up on what has-' 'That won't be necessary,' Hessian interrupts, waving one gloved hand dismissively. 'I've heard all of this parrot's rhetoric before,' he jerks his head towards the prosecutor. 'I know exactly what he said today, and I assure you that it is all exaggerated half-truths blown out of proportion to further his own bawbag career.' Another round of gasps. 'Objection!' The prosecutor flusters, his face going beet-red as he unconsciously takes a threatening step towards the monster. 'Sustained!' the judge agrees with the prosecutor and flashes a warning glance at Hessian, whose face is unreadable beneath the mask. Deville stands nonchalantly at his side with an arrogant smirk on his face. 'Angelo Deville is an international icon of respect,' Hessian begins anew, changing his area of focus. 'Aside from all of his businesses and donations spurring the local economy, he also provides entertainment for the civilised world. Where would the man find time to do all of the things he is accused of when he's spent the last two months training for and competing in the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament?' 'His crimes extend to long before this tournament ever started, your honour, and I feel it imperative to point out that the tournament only hosts weekly matches. That leaves plenty of time for a dedicated man to get into mischief... And Deville is a very dedicated man.' The prosecutor nods slowly through this last, implanting a sense of evil into the word dedicated. 'It doesn't take long to steal someone's life,' the prosecutor continues after a pause, snapping his fingers to stress his point. 'It only takes a second. If we let Mr. Deville out on bail we're providing him with hundreds of thousands of seconds with which to wreak havoc... I'll be the first to admit that he won't kill hundreds of thousands of people in that time, but he'll try. Keep him safely locked away until the trial, I implore you. You know of my reputation, you know I will deliver on what I say. Keep him until the trial, then I will take from him the seconds he has stolen from everyone else.' 'Your honour,' Hessian's voice is patient and amused, but the tone with which he addresses the court is deadly serious. 'I have stood in this man's place before, facing the exact same opponent as he now finds himself against. I know his mind. More importantly, however, I know the mind of the man persecuting him. 'Deville deserves to move on in spite of this man's ego,' As he continues, his voice raises a few decibels and he becomes increasingly more impassioned. 'This poor excuse for a lawful entity has nothing on my client, and condemning the latter based on the former's strawhouse reputation is folly.' The Honourable Judge Markham raps the gavel again, calling for calm. 'Alright, before this degenerates any further I'm going to wrap it up... Mr. Hessian, can you give me one good reason to release your client?' He wearily rests his chin on his hand as he awaits a response. 'I can provide many reasons, Your Honour, but foremost among them is-' Hessian sets up for the kicker, but finds himself interrupted. 'I'll pay whatever exorbitant bail you impose...' Deville suddenly pipes up, flashing a wink at the judge. 'Up front.' The mask hides his emotions from the public, but Hessian is ready to kill. This release was in the bag, he's certain of it, and now... 'Very well,' Markham says hastily, casting a furtive glance at his watch. 'Bail is set at five million dollars.' The gavel slams the wood again and the judge is up like a shot, leaving a gaping prosecutor shaking impotently beside his bench. 'All rise!' The bailiff shouts, though the judge has already disappeared into his chambers. The spectators all get to their feet and begin babbling amongst themselves, shocked and appalled at what they have witnessed. The bailiff hobbles over to Deville and removes the handcuffs, then tells him where to pick up his clothes and belongings. Without a word to him, Hessian turns his back on Deville and begins exiting the courtroom, prompting the other to follow. The crowd parts as quickly and easily as drunken Aimz's legs, staring at the two men with unmistakable horror. 'Nice job, Hessian,' Deville says as they make their way through the doors into the lobby beyond. 'Thank you.' Sunlight glares in at them from the huge bay windows, bathing them in autumn's gentle warmth. 'You almost screwed everything up in there,' Hessian scolds in a humourless voice, still walking briskly for the front doors. 'Never interrupt me again,' the words are unquestionably minacious, and Deville is taken aback. 'Hey, look man... It got the job done. I had that fool pegged ? no worries.' 'You do not know as much as you need to,' Hessian snarls, 'otherwise I wouldn't be here, would I?' Deville reluctantly shakes his head and Hessian seems to be satisfied. 'I managed to arrange the meeting you requested... Albeit illegally. It happens tomorrow. Be at my office no later than noon.' 'Will do,' Deville nods happily, ecstatic that his plans are coming together. 'What about the list of names I gave you?' 'Minion is tracking those people down right now,' Hessian responds with an anticipatory grin. 'If he is half so competent as he pretends, a few of them should already be taken care of.' 'Good... Good. There can be no witnesses,' Deville reminds his monstrous lawyer, who nods slowly in response. The casual air with which this subject is addressed is a testament to each man's conscience. 'What I leave of them will not be identifiable... And it definitely won't name names.' Hessian turns as they reach the doors leading outside and points an admonishing finger at Deville's chest. 'You follow my lead tomorrow... I know this man, you don't. I know how all of this works, so you let me do the talking, understand?' 'Whatever you say,' Deville beams, refusing to commit. Those red eyes intensify for a moment, then Hessian disappears out of the court without another word, leaving Deville staring after him through the glass. Strange fucking guy, Deville thinks with a pleasant shudder. Sometimes it pays to have friends. ~><>...<><~ The Golden Turnbuckle Tournament pulses and glows alone in the darkness, sensing the time for a Champion drawing perilously near. It doesn't think, perse, as humans do, but it has knowledge and it considers. Immortality has granted it the luxury of something beyond thought, a sort of entity within itself that begins to form as the chosen steps forward. This entity fuses with the rest of the body, staining, tainting, and ultimately changing it as a whole. It knows this. It knows it and respects all of the growth it represents, both positive and negative, and so it refuses to leave anything up to chance. The entity is a reflection of the winner. In their combination they make one another complete, the Tournament and the Champion, and one chooses the other as surely as the earth chooses to revolve. There is no luck involved, there is no roulette wheel... The GTT decides how it wants to evolve and selects the one whom will take it there. Defying its omniscience, the deity considers its options from deep within the dimension of the intangible, weighing one option against the other in a ceaseless struggle for peace of mind. The entity welling within is presently amorphous, but already the Tournament feels it taking shape, gaining personality. It doesn't experience time the same way we do, yet it knows it is running preciously short. The time is nigh. A new partner must be chosen... An end put to the loneliness. How will it evolve? Which Champion will step forth? Who will the Tournament choose?
