Damn It Feels Good To Be The Bad Guy

29 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09-29-02 Make. Me. Bad. Three words that would echo through the FSW for the next untold and uncertain era. The so-called hero Timo Bolamba had done it, he had turned his back on the fans, on the FSW, and on his own morality. Timo had a look in his eyes, one long lost, but now very alive. Now, walking alone on a dark night, the wind catches his hair and it blows in plumes behind him, making him smile. Turning around a corner, he bares his teeth, the once shiny smile that launched a thousand brilliant matches, now tainted and misshapen in some way toward a small group of teenagers, and they scattered. -Look at them run, the idiots who revered me as some sort of moral hero. They are such hypocrites. They wouldn’t even stand here and shout insults…no instead they run away. Shows how much my words really mean to the fans doesn’t it?- He continued on briskly, his feet tapping lightly as he strode upon a pair of Wingtip shoes. His long dark overcoat covered layers of clothing so uncharacteristic to the Samoan, yet somehow welcome now. The night was cold for September, even as late as it was, and the well groomed man pulled the coat closer to his body, shivering slightly. The bleachers swarmed as Timo stood backstage, ready to turn his back on the world, he had no qualms about it really. Wasn’t the first time, and really what had he to lose? He had money, he had respect, he had a beautiful wife and a child, and a student. His life was complete except for one thing really. -You still desire that stupid lump of metal no doubt?- “Of course.” The truth of the matter is that no amount of money, no cars, no woman, nothing could bring what he wanted now. Yeah he did it for the gold, and that’s what everyone should believe. Timo Bolamba, Mr. Wholesome as a glass of milk, turning his back, irony bites deep eh? -Well one thing is for sure, this time around, people will take you seriously. Not like that joke of a turn in the PCW. This time things will be different. This time, people will learn that there is no fucking around.- He paces on and suddenly stops as a teenager, maybe fourteen years of age peers over a fence. Timo stops and smiles at the kid, and the kid flicks him off. “Timo, you suck!” “Yeah?” “You heard me!” “Come down here and say that you little jackass!” Timo made to untie his overcoat and the kid ran off, disappearing behind the fence forever. Timo harumphed and continued walking, smiling to himself. -Stupid arrogant little kid. Just like the rest of the boneheads in FSW. Sure they talk the talk, but when it all gets down to it, they run away.- “So then Mr. Savior, what should we do on this fine night?” He looked up the street and one sign in particular caught his attention. He made for it and smiled as he approached the door, greeted by a large black man in dark shades. Timo rolled his shoulders a bit and flexed is fingers to limber them up. “Sign says you want some pit fighters. That right?” “That’s right…but if I were you, I’d turn around. There aint no room for fakers in that ring.” “Fakers? Please tell me you didn’t just say that.” The large man takes his glasses off and smiles, laughing at the smaller man, but the Slick Back Mack Timo reaches into his wallet, pulling out a wad of hundreds. He manages to strip one off, and tucks it neatly into the mans pocket, then invites himself in the door, not saying a word. “What da hell was that all about? Damn fool gonna get his ass kicked.” The small tightly packed arena was abuzz with battle cries and blood sport. Timo smells what he can smell and a malignant grin topples his features. The ring announcer stands next to a gruesome man, covered in scars and blood. A fallen warrior is being drug from the ring. The announcer grabs the microphone and riles the crowd up. “Who amongst you has the guts to take on GORE!?” The Samoan walks to the front row and as the crowd dies down he begins to clap a bit. The mammoth of a man looks down at him as he makes sure to get the large mans attention. Steam erupts from the mans nose as he breathes heavily, and Timo simply wipes a bit of the sweat droplets of his face mockingly. “You got a problem punk?” “No…not really, I was just wondering, GORE…that’s an interesting name. Do you preach about saving trees and bore people to death with your long winded monotone speeches?” The monster grabs the wall of the cage and squints down at the Samoan. He snorts and begins to laugh. The announcer begins hyping the match up trying to goad Timo into the ring. Gore parades around getting the crowd into the match, and making Timo laugh around even more. ‘What your problem runt? You want to get in here and face me or what?” “Well to be perfectly honest I wasn’t planning on it. I was hoping to see a man walk through that door, not unlike me, and stand here, mocking you like the dumb ox you are, then proceed to kick your ass all over the ring, but since that man is apparently not coming, I guess I will have to do it.” ‘Haw Haw Haw, that’s rich. Get in here smart ass! “Better than being a dumbass isn’t it?” With that remark, Gore flexes and backs up, waiting for Timo to get into the ring. The Samoan removes his Knights Chamber overcoat and hangs it from the ringpost. He rolls the sleeves up on the white Armani button up shirt he is wearing, and cuffs the Khaki Dockers he wears. The whole crowd laughs at his antics, and his attire, but he doesn’t let it bother him. Timo steps through the ropes and the ring crew locks the door behind him. He puts his dukes up and the big man charges forward, knocking Timo halfway across the ring with a haymaker. The oddsmakers start to pay off as the large man walks around with his arms in the air. The Samoan wipes a bit of blood off his lip, then looks at his white shirt, now stained red and smiles, shaking his head. Gore’s arms fall as he turns around and he charges again, this time receiving a boot to the face for his troubles. The fans clamor as Timo begins a series of unmerciful kicks to the side of the mans head, even when he is on the ground, and when the big man starts to cover up, Timo grabs him by the ears and pulls him to his feet slowly. “Remind me again, what was it you called me? Was it a punk?” He throws a chop to the mans neck, making him grab it and gasp. Timo pulls his fist back and cranks it into the mans nose, breaking it, and spraying blood all over his own clothes. He looks down at the mass of man as it rolls around in pain, and the bell rings, then to his shirt. “You…soiled my clothing. Do you have any idea how much this cost me?” He rips the Armani shirt by the buttons and reaches down, shoving it in the mans mouth. He smiles and holds his hands up to a stunned crowd. He traces a line across his stomach where he has been tattooed in the traditional Pe’a. He lowers his head and looks at the fallen man as he rolls about on the ground. “I feel no pain, I fear no man, I do not fear you, lumbering oxen. Prepare for defeat.” As if it were not enough, he reaches down and grabs the mans leg, calling for support, the stunned crowd now worked into a fervor as he twists it, hearing the crack of cartilage before he is pulled off. The Samoan stands above the men, covered in the blood of the other man, wearing nothing but his soaked Dockers, and a pair of Wingtips. Timo tosses the ring hands a few hundred dollar bills and grabs his overcoat. He slips it on, then almost steps out of the ring. He turns and spits on his foe, in true heel fashion then looks to the men approaching the fallen “Gore” “Clean that piece of garbage up, he is starting to smell.” Timo wades through the crowd and up to the door. The black man standing there before looks at him with fear in his eyes, and Timo approaches him, face to face. “Boo.” The man jumps and flinches back and Timo laughs, walking lightly down the street and into the night. He rounds the corner and is out of sight. The night has once again seen the emergence of the heel in him and there is really only one thing left to say… “Damn it feels good to be the bad guy.”
