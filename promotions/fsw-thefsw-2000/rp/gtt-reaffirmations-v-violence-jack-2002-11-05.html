-GTT- Reaffirmations (v Violence Jack)

5 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
“Jesus, Kimbusa. The son of a bitch almost beat you.” Steven Caldera states this to his five hundred pound counterpart from across the room in a voice just loud enough to be heard. It had been a long night for Caldera. His phone was still ringing off the hook with offers from the media for exclusives on Kimbusa. He was receiving offers from everywhere trying to recruit the big man. Kimbusa, all the while, had remained oblivious and uncaring. His only interests remained in the continued abuse of his opponents. Beyond this, he would allow Caldera to manage the business aspect of his matters. But with Kimbusa’s continued success, management of the big man had turned into a full time job, and Caldera is now completely spent. Lying lengthwise on Kimbusa’s silk sofa, Caldera loosens his tie. He is still clad in his usual business attire, but as has become the trend, it is wrinkled and days old. His face is unshaven and dirty, testament to his constant and tireless work on behalf of Kimbusa. The apartment seems cold despite the humid heat outside. Kimbusa always had preferred it that way, and as a result had run up the air conditioning bill to unheard of levels. But Caldera was satisfied with this. He was willing to spare no expense to keep the big man rested and comfortable, so that he might be easily molded into the model American sports-entertainer. Kimbusa wears his silk robe while standing at the window, looking down at the city of Houston, Texas below. He has been slightly annoyed not only by Caldera’s comment, but by his continued presence. He had grown closer to Caldera in the past weeks leading to his most recent confrontation, but his appearance at Kimbusa’s apartment this morning was undesired. In fact, the two had not yet made eye contact; Caldera had let himself in, and taken his position on the sofa without a hello. “He was easily defeated,” Kimbusa counters back at Caldera, without turning his head away from the window. He, too, speaks in a low, tense voice. The two seem to exchange words in a manner so programmed that it seemed they had already had the conversation many times over in their minds, and they were simply getting it out in the open. “It was too close for me, Kimbusa. This little kid should have been easily destroyed, but you almost let him get the best of you,” Caldera presses on, still speaking idly from his comfortable position on the sofa. Kimbusa does not respond. He stands in stillness and in silence by the window, glaring outward at the bustling metropolis beyond. He has yet to adapt to the stench of the city’s terrible pollution, and the constant haze over the skyline is revolting. He had actually grown fond of the modern amenities of the apartment itself, but everything beyond remained absolutely repulsive. He had, indeed, defeated the Ninth Wonder of Foam. Perhaps it was more closely contested than he had anticipated, but he had been victorious nonetheless. He stands in perfect suspended animation, wondering why Caldera had come to pester him. After a pause, Caldera finally sits up in the sofa, and turns his head to focus on the broad back of Kimbusa. He finds Kimbusa’s eyes in the dim reflection of the window, and manages to speak to them. “Do you understand my worries here? This kid should have been the perfect outlet for this rage you keep talking about. But I didn’t see it. Hell, if I didn’t know any better, I’d think you’re a damned professional wrestler.” This seems to get Kimbusa’s attention, and the big man angles his head slightly, to ensure that Caldera might hear him. “I felt no desire to destroy the young man’s life. There will be others.” Caldera sighs, not content with this response. “Look, there’s no middle ground here, Kimbusa. One of two things can carry you through this tournament. Either power from anger, or power from wrestling. If you’re conquering your demons, then good for you. But we have a deal here. You’re going to win this tournament for both of us. But if you stop caring about it, if you lose focus, you will be defeated. We’re too far in to play this game. We’re so close to the end, we’re among such great talent, that if you flinch or show even the slightest bit of weakness, you won’t survive.” Kimbusa suddenly spins around, glaring directly at Caldera. Speaking in a voice that seems to contain more than a little bit of animosity, Kimbusa begins growling. “What is this ‘we’? Why do you say ‘we’? ‘We’ have not done anything. Our agreement was that I shall win this tournament, correct? If I am to win this tournament, I shall do it on my own terms. If I want to end someone’s career, if I want to break someone’s neck, I shall do so when I please. But if I wish to spare an opponent, if I show them respect, does that make me any less capable of holding my end of this deal? You tend to your business, Steven Caldera. I shall tend to mine.” Having put Caldera in his place, Kimbusa spins around rapidly, returning to his hulking statuette form by the window. Caldera, speechless for a moment, gives Kimbusa a good look. The big man stands in front of the window, where the rising sun is blaring in. Amid this fiery redness Kimbusa appears to be nothing more than a dark figure, a blackened silhouette of his own massive form. He remains completely motionless, leading Caldera to the same conclusion that so many have made before; Kimbusa does not need breath. Caldera stands from his seat, quietly adjusting his tie. His face gains an air of confidence and even conceit, as he begins to grin. “You think you know what you’re up against?” Caldera sneers. Taken aback by Caldera’s tone of contempt, Kimbusa’s head turns again, just catching a glimpse of Caldera out of the corner of his eye. Caldera does not turn to address Kimbusa directly, but simply begins to meander through his little corner of the living area. “Do you even know where in the world you are? How can you be so thankless? How can you be so ignorant?” Caldera inquires sourly. Kimbusa, growing a bit surprised and even a bit agitated by Caldera’s interrogation, turns fully to face Caldera. Caldera, too, finally stops, places his arms on his hips, and slowly begins strolling towards Kimbusa. “You’re in a world that you can’t possibly begin to comprehend. I’ve given you the luxury of safety and financial insurance. I’ve given you time, I’ve given you freedom. Freedom! Freedom from that iron prison cell that you would have rotted in for fifteen years had I not intervened. Have you already forgotten that? Have you? I haven’t. Look at where you are. You’re sitting in a beautiful apartment that you haven’t paid a dime for. You’re a member of a very elite few who have made it to levels of prestige that more than a hundred sought after. And I, Steven Caldera, put you there. You’ve been damn impressive, Kimbusa, I’ll give you that much. But the least you can do is give some respect to the man who gave you the chance.” Kimbusa seems genuinely effected by this. His face, having risen in intensity and fury with each passing word, suddenly fades to a look of understanding. Caldera’s face remains contemptuous and scornful, his deep blue eyes incisive and penetrating. Kimbusa slowly begins to step down towards Caldera, who greets him with a heated glare to the face. Kimbusa’s look of contentment and pacifism remains even as he reaches down to pick up the massive leather recliner, and in a display of uncanny power, launches it into the flat screen television hanging from the wall, sending both down to the ground in a jumbled mess. Caldera’s visage does not change; his attention never wavers from Kimbusa’s eyes. Kimbusa turns to look at Caldera, who at six foot four is slightly taller, and matches his glare in intensity. “Do you have any idea who I am? Do you have any idea who you’re dealing with? Do you have any idea who is standing before you? No, you don’t. I know this because I, myself, have only now discovered who I am. And for your sake, I will tell you who I am. I am a monster. I am a freak of nature. I am a living, breathing dragon. I am a man who is fully prepared to do whatever it takes to win this tournament. I am a man who is fully capable of destroying anyone at any time I choose. And that includes you, Steven Caldera. I am fighting demons inside my heart, inside my soul, that YOU can only begin to comprehend. I am waging wars on levels you have never contemplated. But I am not beyond mercy. So do not judge me, Steven Caldera, do not analyze me. Because as much as you think you know, you don’t know the half of it. So, for the remainder of our business arrangement, I shall show mercy to those I choose, and I shall bring unspeakable destruction upon those I desire. And you, Steven Caldera, will not judge me or berate me because of it. Is this clear?” Caldera’s face, having remained stone cold the entire time, continues to be intense. “You are a monster?” he inquires. “Yes.” Caldera’s intensity suddenly fades, and a thin grin comes over his face. “Good.” “Good.”
