GTT · Necessary Complications

5 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: whoo~! 'Deville definitely doesn't need this,' a soft and weezy voice states, and the other man soberly nods his agreement. The speaker is a middle-aged man with slicked back graying black hair, brown eyes set a little too close together on his head, and a very sharp Patrician nose. His double-chin is neatly clean shaven, and the rest of his appearance falls in line behind, from his fancy silk suit to his pair of gold pinky rings to his immaculate fingernails. He presses his body back against the leather chair, revelling in it's comforting softness and coolness, and locks his eyes on those of his guest. 'It's much worse than it seems, Don Salvatore,' the man says hurriedly in his guttural voice, bowing his head slightly as he slips over the title. He hates having to deliver bad news to Salvatore - especially the day after receiving his monthly bribe. 'The police haven't officially recognised him as a suspect yet.' His eyebrows raise to emphasise the significance of this, but the gesture is unnecessary: one doesn't become a major mafia boss without reading between the lines. The second man is much younger than Salvatore, somewhere in his mid-twenties. His already-thinning brown hair is sharply styled as a crew-cut, and his body is lean and sharp to match. Dressed in a golf-shirt and slacks, he doesn't exactly blend in with the extravagant surroundings. The desk he stands before is the size of a small truck and made of solid oak with gold trim, beautiful painted portraits line the walls above the bookcases, and a crystal chandelier shimmers from the centre of the ceiling. Huge bay windows look out onto the opening and closing holes of a backyard golf course, speckled with pine and maple. The man is very uncomfortable where he stands, and though he takes great pains to hide the fact, it couldn't be clearer if he broke down into tears. Salvatore purses his lips as he turns the chair on an angle, twisting his body so they're no longer facing eachother. Even though they are relatively loyal to him, he despises every one of the stoolies he pays for his information and virtual immunity. Most of the time he'd rather do away with the whole lot of them, but he knows they're a necessary evil in his business. As were people like Angelo Deville. Or are they? Salvatore delves inward, ignoring the man before him. Is Deville still necessary? He might actually be more trouble than he's worth. His stunt with the Angels, killing poor John, this... The list is growing so long that it overshadows his usefulness. Perhaps... The thought trails off as he swivels is chair back around and arches an eyebrow. 'I understand, Officer Orwell, but what can I do?' Salvatore demands irritably, then continues before the other man can respond. 'He's already allegedly killed this cop, and the whole force knows about it.' He lays his palms flat on the table and heaves a mighty shrug of his shoulders, to reinforce the helplessness of his situation. 'Well,' the honourable officer of the law clears his throat and shifts his feet, 'there is nothing you can do.' He winces and averts his eyes, expecting the man to call down lightning for such blasphemy, but Salvatore simply continues staring. Orwell sheepishly looks back at Salvatore, then manages in a whisper, 'I just though you should know.' 'Yes, very nice of you,' Salvatore patronises him with an inward rolling of the eyes. More likely you thought I'd give you some kind of bonus, he thinks, characteristically cynical. What he says next, however, is totally uncharacteristic... He never shares his mind. In this instance, however, he feels it doesn't matter. A slight smile touches his lips as his mouth opens, 'Even if I could rectify this situation... I think Deville's finally exhausted his favours.' ... Tessa Windsor is dead, Deville chuckles to himself as he takes another gulp of Cognac. He is standing in the den of his riverside mansion in pitch darkness, resting an arm on the mantel as he leans against it. It's only fair she be reunited with her brother, is it not? A sadistic laugh echoes through the empty room, seemingly growing more sinister as it fades. Deville has been standing and pacing around in his den for the past three hours, waiting for some sort of news. He figures he should have heard something by now, and it disturbs him somewhat that he hasn't. It's not that he has anxiety about coming out with the shitty end of the stick, he just likes knowing. Uncertainty is bad. It's a disease. Which is why Deville takes steps to eliminate it, regardless of the moral, legal, or financial implications. Peace of mind is a glorious thing, worth any price. So he stands here and broods, impatiently awaiting a move that he may counter. To take his mind off of the dead cop situation, his thoughts retreat to the tournament. The date draws nigh and still she hasn't retreated. Perhaps she didn't hear about those who came before her... Perhaps she doesn't care. Angelo downs the rest of the glass as he dismisses that last thought. No, she cares. She's scared. Should we let her make it to the ring? Deville queries, referring to himself as a collective. Or should she suffer the fate of Blitz? Interesting choice... The determining factor is the risk value. If we take her out beforehand we definitely move forward. On the other hand, if we let her show up and wrestle her, who will win? An intrusive rapping on the door rips him from his thoughts, and before he can react the door opens up a foot, letting a harsh beam of light molest the perfect dark. A head and face Deville cannot make out - but still recognises - peers around the side of the door and and scans the room until it lands on his silhouette. 'They know,' a mellifluous voice sings to the room, then the head and light retreat as the door is closed. Deville nods his head slowly, spinning the glass in his hand as a smile creeps over his face. I know they know, the smile grows and gives birth to mirthless laughter. I know, too.
