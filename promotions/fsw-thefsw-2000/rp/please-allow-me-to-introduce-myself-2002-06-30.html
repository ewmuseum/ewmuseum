Please Allow Me To Introduce Myself

30 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/30/02 The one-room hotel suite is small and claustrophobic, but at least it offers a kind of constrictive and distant safety. In an attempt to inspire coziness the owners have covered all of the walls with wood-panelling, creating a cabin-like atmosphere that would be relaxing under normal circumstances. Despite countless assurances of his security within the beachfront Newburg Inn, Paul Risling finds himself pacing between the disinfectant-reaking bed and the waist-high dresser with palpable angst. His eyes dart towards the mirror resting on the dresser every time he passes, and seeing the concern stamped on his drawn face only serves to increase his anxiety. Although his identification marks him as a thirty-four year old American from Langley, Idaho, Paul Eldridge Risling was born only a few measly weeks ago. He looks rather old for a newborn, with tinges of gray at the temples of his thick brown hair and a boisterous Budweiser belly wearing the elasticity of his blue sweatsuit. Delicate hands that have never seen an honest day's work have been rubbed red with nervous dry-washing, and dark patches of sweat have worn through the armpits of the sweatshirt in a blatant snub of the air-conditioning. What have we gotten ourselves into? Paul's mind inquires as he pauses and stares at his reflection in one of the room's two rain-streaked windows. We had it all... Now what do we have? Shit, we don't even have our own n? A flash of lightning abruptly chases the thoughts away, as though they need retreat from the light into the dark recesses of his mind. The stricken horror in his widening, blood-shot eyes indicates that the interruption wasn't due to an immature fear of storms, however. 'It can't be,' a strangled voice whispers through trembling lips in a vain attempt to mollify himself. He knows it's counterproductive, but his mind won't concede to reality just yet. It can't be over already. The thunderclap shakes him from his hypnotised fear, thrusting him instead into a surreal panic. Fuck! No! A sob wracks his body as he stumbles back from the window, shaking his head defiantly as tears make their slow journey down his round red face. Another bolt of lightning illuminates the world outside, but Paul has moved too far back to see the apparition again. He doesn't need to see it again; once was enough - once was too much. A gust of wind rattles the windows, carrying an eery verbal message. A message he had just seen carved out in the sand. A message he hoped he would never see. The indisputable knowledge that he never will again is everything but comforting. Hello Frank The simple greeting shouts through Paul's head over and over again, choking off his sweat-glands as it chills him to the core. A voice in the back of his mind tells him he knew this day would come, but the coward at the main controls refuses even now to take what he knows he deserves. Frank Stalinski entered into the Witness Protection Program roughly three weeks ago after he was arrested. Rather than do the criminally honourable thing and try to beat out the charges levelled against him, he decided to drop the dime on other members of the organisation. The FBI arranged a new identity and hid him out in the boondocks of Idaho. He had traded his identity and his friends for his freedom, or rather, for his freedom from the law. Even at the time he knew it was a mistake, but self-preservation was number one on his list, so he traded the figurative hanging for the literal version. If he wasn't so damn scared he'd probably realise the irony of the situation. Gotta get outta here! He decides as he turns and wobbles over to the door. One trembling hand reaches up to disengage the pathetic chain-lock latch reserved for hotel-rooms, but just as his fingers close around the knob a booming knock echoes through the room like a gunshot. Emitting some hybrid of a scream and a whisper, Paul stumbles blindly backwards. 'Room service,' a gruff voice shouts through the door immediately following the knock. Even if Paul had actually given the hotel staff occasion to come visit him at his room, he knew that voice didn't belong here. He knew it from before. That was the voice of The Black Hand, Anthony Carpelli, and wherever he was... Panicked, Paul looks frantically around the room, then realises there is only one way out. Answering the door is definitely not an option, and simply keeping it locked and trusting to it's security will amount to the same fatal ending. Giving his fate into God's hands, he crosses his arms up in front of his face and charges the window through which he had seen the impending danger only moments before. With his mind blessedly clear for the first time since he had become Paul Risling, the former mobster clamps his eyes shut, crashes through the third-story window, and plummets to the earth below. The rain once again acts as his saviour, having dampened the ground enough to spare him the broken bones that would normally accompany such a fall. No matter how much softer the ground had become, however, it still collaborated with the air to suck all the breath from his lungs. He lay gasping and wheezing on the ground in a fetal position, trying desperately to get up to his knees so that he may escape, but failing. Get up! his panicked mind scolds as the rain begins seeping through his clothing. Do you really want to die? A trickling of air manages to invade his esophagus and find his lungs, and though there is still a dull ache, as if metal shackles were fastened around them, they begin pumping consistently and efficiently. A small puddle of mud and water oozes around his hand as he uses it to push himself up off the ground, and he finds himself facing the lake. The beach that begins twenty feet in front of him is completely devoid of people and the message that he had seen before isn't visible from this angle. He doesn't miss it. The grass courtyard seems to be vacant as well, though there is a picnic table just a few feet away that would not have made for such a pleasant landing. The only light comes from the windows of those rooms where people are still awake, and in it Paul begins slipping his way along the grass, frantic with the desire to get around to the other side of the building and the parking lot. If Carpelli is upstairs, Paul begins, then reconsiders. If Carpelli was upstairs, the other one must be out here somewhere, his petrified mind manages to reason. He has absolutely no desire to run into the other one. If he had his choice in things, he'd take The Black Hand over the Crimson Angel every single time. So where is he? His mind probes itself as the body rounds the corner and begins it's shuffling run through the grass. The high parking lot lights can now be seen, as can the parking lot itself leading to the dirt road. A sense of salvation begins worming itself into Paul's mind, but something holds it back. Something isn't right. As if to symbolise the light bulb flaring to life above his head, lightning strikes somewhere off to his right, providing an instant of frighteningly brilliant illumination. With a strangled gasp, Paul skids to a stop just shy of rounding the corner of the building. Half of the parking lot is visible from his vantage point. Two more steps would have brought him to the parking lot... Two more steps would have brought him to death. The realisation that the other would definitely be waiting in the parking lot makes Paul's blood run cold. He almost ran directly into death's outstretched arms. Breathing heavily as much from fear as from pushing his out of shape carcass into a run, Paul sidles up to the corner and peaks his head around just enough so that one eye can survey the lot. Most of it is empty, but there are a few scattered cars, including one with it's engine running and a rather large individual leaning against. An individual he recognises. An individual who turns and looks straight into Paul's wide, petrified eye. He smiled! Paul thinks as he scurries back from the corner and begins sprinting in the other direction. He fuckin' smiled! Somehow that simple expression scared Paul worse than if the man had pulled out a shotgun and pointed it at his temple. He is running for everything he's worth now, sobbing and wheezing and crying as he slips and stumbles over the slick grass. The sounds of the inevitable pursuit are hidden by the rain and the wind, as well as the sounds his own body betrays him with. The sinking feeling that he's about to be shot in the back increases with every step, reaching a fever point as he gets within spitting distance of the corner. His mind is screaming sweet terrified nothings at him, mercifully preventing the possibility of rational thought. Certain he won't make it, Paul lets out a primal scream and dives for the brief sanctuary the other side of the building will offer him. Pain flares through his body and he sees only red for a moment, then everything fades to black. After another moment he realises that he isn't shot and opens his eyes, gingerly bringing one arm up to rub the shoulder he landed on. Still alive, he marvels. Let's keep it that way! He climbs to his hands and knees, now thoroughly soaked, and looks up to see a relatively dry pair of legs covered by black dress pants. At the same instant the unmistakable cold opening of a gun presses against the crown of his head, inviting him to rest a minute... Or an eternity. 'Get up,' a booming voice like that of God reaches down and plucks any last hope of survival from the fallen Paul. 'Get up, you filthy piece of shit!' God snarls, then prevents the other from complying by bringing one black Italian shoe straight up into his face. Paul rears up, dazed, then collapses sideways to the grass. God turns out to be a lanky Italian man with a crooked nose, brown eyes sets a little too close together, and a smirk that has probably been etched onto his face for the better part of thirty years. He is dressed completely in black, from the fedora to his suit to his shoes. And the gun, of course... Mustn't forget the gun. Mumbling curses Paul can't quite make out over the ringing in his ears, the man steps forward and brings the foot swiftly up into his ribs. It stings fiercely, but Paul manages to bear it and wrap his arms around the leg just below the knee. Knowing it's the only chance left, he twists his whole body as hard and fast as he can, dragging the leg - and the rest of the man - with him. A shocked cry of protest escapes from the man's throat as something in his knee snaps and he is flung to the ground, unconsciously releasing his hold on the gun as it happens. Agonising pain tears through his mind and he begins shouting curses at Paul, Paul's mother, and everyone else he can think to name. A prickling sensation that he might actually get out of this intact begins wheedling it's way through Paul as he releases the man he recognises as Carpelli and goes for the fallen weapon. ''Just get the gun! Fuck the FBI! Fuck their program! Get the gun and run!' These simple ideas drive him forward and his hand closes around the handle of the gun ? his salvation. Carpelli is rolling around in the wet grass, holding his knee and unleashing hordes and hordes of expletives in gasps and groans. The gun is slippery in his grasp but he holds onto it like life itself: with panicked, desperate intensity. 'First we must kill the other one,' he states with a crazed, maniacal smile. 'Time to send The Crimson Angel home!' He laughs as he pushes himself wearily to his feet, the pain in his jaw almost forgotten in his mad exuberance. The bullet rips through the flesh of his lower back a nanosecond before the sound registers in his brain. Dread explodes in his mind as he realises he's been shot, and he falls to the ground in a limp, helpless heap. At first he is pleasantly surprised by the lack of pain accompanying the wound, figuring that his adrenaline must be giving him the strength to emerge victorious, but then he discovers that not only can he not feel pain, he cannot feel anything below his neck whatsoever. The bullet ruptured his spine and paralysed him. The overwhelming sense of vulnerability and terror that consumes him as he lies facedown on the ground, unable to move, is undescribable in it's truculence... Like reaching down to scratch your balls and finding only an amorphous yet obviously tumourous lump there. 'Speak of the Deville... and he appears,' mocks a disturbingly calm voice from behind him, amplifying his horror. The man wielding the smoking gun takes slow, measured steps towards the immobile Paul, his dancing blue eyes peering down at his victim from a smoothly angular face, piercing into him like no bullet could. Angelo Deville is draped in a pin-striped black trench-coat, from under which jut a pair of black pants and shoes almost identical to his compatriot's. Medium-length blonde hair that is almost brown from the rain branches out wildly in all directions, like flames in a fire, and the seemingly custom-made pistol dangles loosely from his left hand, as if trying to appear harmless and innocent despite it's recent deed. His teeth are all visible in the dim light in what could be either a smile or a grimace. 'For Christ's sake, Anthony,' Angelo begins irritably as he places one foot on Paul's back, directly over the bullet-hole, and pushes down with a twist, then turns his gaze onto the moaning Carpelli. 'Would you stop that goddamned wailing? You sound like a donkey in the throes of an orgasm.' Carpelli promptly quits his belly-aching and raises himself to a sitting position, still rubbing his knee. 'That bastard busted my knee!' Anthony declares incredulously, looking at the paralysed man beneath Angelo. 'I'll kill `im!' He continues, stating the obvious. 'Bite your tongue, Anthony! The way you talk, poor Frankie here might get the impression we're heartless thugs.' Angelo says with feigned outrage, casting a facetiously scornful gaze on Anthony. 'Now get over here and apologise... Frankie is still one of us, after all - right Frankie?' A muffled sob from Frank - Paul - is his only reply. He knows his time has come, and though he'd like to fight it there's only so much thrashing your head around will do. Anthony struggles to get up, first placing a little weight on the injured leg, then trusting more and more until he manages a jolted limp. His face is beet-red and covered with a mixture of rain and sweat when he reaches Angelo's side. He bends down to retrieve his gun from Frank's unfeeling hand, then resists the urge to end the scoundrels life that very instant. 'Now put your gun away. We're not going to need anything like that,' Deville says with a smile Frank should be thankful he cannot see. 'Frank just got a little confused, that's all. He forgot that you can't just leave without telling anybody. But that's all right... We'll remind him, right Anthony?' 'Uh... Yeah, I guess,' Anthony replies hesitantly, pretty certain Angelo still intends to kill the stoolie. Some doubt has crept into his mind, though. Why not just plug the leak and get the hell out of there? Angelo smiles at Anthony and winks, sensing his confusion and feeling deeply amused by it. 'Yeah, we'll get him cleaned up real nice. Real proper.' The smile is still frozen on his face. The look of bewilderment is still plastered on Anthony's. 'And we'll start by fixing up his outfit. I mean, shit... He's not even wearing a tie, Anthony.' Angelo gestures down towards Frank's neck with the hand holding his gun and reaches into his trench-coat with the other. Anthony thinks he understands now, and his pleasure at the implication is immense. 'You must have an extra one, Anthony. Give this son-of-a-bitch a necktie,' Angelo spits through gritted teeth as his eyebrows draw down, all amusement and jocularity now gone from his demeanour. He pulls his right hand out from the trench-coat and flourishes a rusty hunting knife, then hands it to Anthony. With a twist of his heel he steps down from Frank's midsection and begins strolling back to the parking lot, his feet sloshing on the wet ground. In a malicious voice he calls back over his shoulder to a grinning Anthony. 'A Columbian necktie.' As Angelo disappears around the corner, Anthony bends down with a sadistic smile on his face and grabs Frank by the hair, roughly pulling his head up. Frank lets out a strangled cry and tries to struggle and fight the murderer off, but his body still refuses to co-operate. 'Say goodbye, rat,' Anthony hisses into Frank's ear, bringing the knife down to begin the awful business.
