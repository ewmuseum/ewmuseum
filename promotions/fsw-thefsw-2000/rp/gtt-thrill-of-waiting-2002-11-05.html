GTT · Thrill of Waiting

5 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: whoo~! The police cruiser lurches as it's front wheel breasts the pothole, jerking and shaking as it slows to a stop along the curb. Aside from the cruiser itself, the streets are barren; the dozens of small businesses that line the road have mostly shut down for the evening, clouding everything in darkness except for the occasional light from a window or street lamp. The only sound is the rumbling of the engine, and when it shuts off the area revels in its momentary silence. The driver and passenger door whip open simultaneously, producing a grimy looking pair of undercover police officers in cheap tweed jackets. Their faces are lined with many years of worrying and scowling, and there is not much to behold there now as they emerge from the car besides the unmistakable mark of pessimistic anticipation. They look as if they were expecting to find the holy grail overflowing with cognac, but instead came upon a tin goblet of piss. This charming look shifts to the object of their appearance, a run down bar dubbed Gulliver's Inn. 'Place looks condemned,' McLean, the driver, complains as the pair slam their doors and stroll up onto the sidewalk. 'Are you sure this is it?' The skepticism is prevalent in his voice as he narrows his eyes at his partner, who dismisses the question with a swipe of his hand. 'There's no questioning my sources. If he's not here now, he was not too long ago,' Boutilier assures him, walking slowly up to the dark front window and attempting to peer in. 'What kind of a bar closes before 7pm?' 'Your sources?' McLean guffaws, eyes bulging from his head. 'Your sources are crackwhores! They'd tell you communism is a feasible plan if you flashed enough snow.' 'Rubbish,' Boutiler snorts, on the border of indignant. 'My crackwhores - as you call them - are some of the most reliable people I know.' McLean just rolls his eyes. 'Well look,' his voice brims with impatience as his hands plant themselves on his hips. 'I don't see Angelo Deville anywhere... And I doubt he's ever deigned to cast a glance at a place like this.' McLean motions to the hole beside him with both hands, sneering. 'He was here, I'm certain of it,' Boutilier insists, looking around for any sign that the mobster was indeed here. He places a thoughtful finger to his chin and surveys the area, then his eyes suddenly widen and he shouts, 'wait!' 'What?' McLean does his best to sound disinterested, but intrigue owns his voice. He marches over beside Boutilier and tries to follow his gaze. 'Look there,' Boutilier exclaims, smiling broadly and pointing to the ground at their feet. 'Where?' 'There!' He bends down, still pointing, so that his finger is only inches away from a sample-size vodka bottle. 'The bottle?' McLean demands in an incredulous voice, looking at his partner as if to gauge his sanity. 'Not just one, fool,' Boutiler scorns, spreading his arms wide to encompass the whole street. 'There are dozens of them, starting here and leading down the road... See?' McLean follows his gesture and notes all of the tiny bottles littered along the road. 'So what...? This is a fucking bar, by the way.' The irritation has crept back into his voice and seems to be increasing by the second. 'This confirms my suspicions that Deville was here,' Boutiler boasts and McLean can only stare. 'He is the root of all of these vodka bottles.' 'Deville's an alcoholic?' McLean asks, thoroughly confused by all of this 'policing'. 'Maybe, how should I know?' Boutilier responds with a question of his own, momentarily as confused as his partner. He quickly shakes the thought away and gets back on track. 'Deville's been having some problems with the red army, according to my sources.' 'Alright,' McLean puts in after several drawn out seconds of silence. 'What the fuck?' 'Don't you see? Communism... Vodka...!' McLean keeps staring. 'For crying out loud, man! Deville's obviously been abducted by communists - likely Russians - and taken that way!' Boutilier shouts as he points triumphantly down the road. 'Right,' McLean says, eyeing his partner like he would a rabid wolf. 'Damn communists have to drink the stuff like we breathe air... How else could they support those ideals?' Boutilier demands and waits for an answer, but McLean has none. Whether because the question is too stupid to justify a response or because it makes sense is up to you. 'All we have to do it follow the trail of empty bottles and we'll have Deville!' '...What?' McLean asks dumbly, uncomprehending. 'Get in the car and follow the trail of vodka!' Boutlilier shouts chafingly, rushing over to the passenger side door and climbing in. McLean shrugs and gets in the driver side, thinking the plan is ludicrous. ~><>...<><~ Deville floats serenely among the clouds, despising all of the inferior gods as they stare up at him with jealous eyes. They wish they could be like him, but it can never be... For if they ever approach his level he'll kill them. Deville reflects happily on this as he enters his sky palace, enjoying the sight of his scintillating harem, the sound of countless violins being played properly as fiddles, and the overpowering smell of shit... He floats majestically ov- waitasecond... shit? Deville awakes rudely from his slumber, his head being savagely raped by the mother of all headaches. Groggily, he tries to raise his left hand to clear the sleep from his eyes and finds that he can't. Writing it off to numbness, he tries with his right and fights it useless as well. Agitated more than panicked, he snaps his eyes open and regards his surroundings with Byron's - dare I say - virginity. Deville surely doesn't recognise the shack he's in, as it seems more fit to holding pigs or horses than it does for entertaining people. The walls are constructed of haphazardly thrown together two-by-sixes, and only one layer of them judging by the moonlight peeking through the cracks. There is no floor to speak of, only the cold dirt of Mother Earth. A small fire has been constructed near the centre of the room, allowing Angelo to look around, but other than that the only thing in the room are the two large Russians who he last remembered speaking with outside of that seedy bar. Them, and a fresh pile of human shit in the corner. 'Damnit guys,' Angelo says as he struggles with the ropes binding his hands. He is seated in what appears to be the only chair in the room, fasted to it by two separate rotted ropes - one around his upper arms and the other around his calves. Ignoring the fact that he could probably snap right through their diseased fibres, he continues, 'if you insist on procreating, do it outside.' 'Hey,' one of the men says to the other, apparently missing the insult. 'The American is waking.' 'Waking? I'm already awake, dummy.' Deville smirks as the two men make their way over to him, eyes wide and shining like a teenager who has just discovered porno magazines. The smirk fades away under that look, and he seriously begins to question his arrogance. 'We would not like to be calling us dummy if we were in your dummy shoes, dummy,' the second man pipes in incoherently, his voice like crumbling leaves. 'That would make us dumb, right Nikolai?' 'Right Boris,' Nikolai says, nodding in full agreement of his friend's babble, as if it had sounded from his own head. Deville eyes the zombies warily as they stop just in front of him and flash twin grins. 'So, uh...' Angelo prompts, shifting his eyes back and forth uncomfortably. 'Are we partying here or what? Why don't you guys untie me and we'll start rockin' this palace!' The feigned enthusiasm fools Boris, and he lunges forward to undo the ropes, eyes dancing with the anticipation of a party. Nikolai places a restraining hand on his shoulder just in time, and Boris' face scrunches up in confusion. 'He is trying to trick us,' Nikolai informs his associate, casting a disparaging look at Deville. 'This is how democratic dogs treat eachother.' Both men snort their disapproval. 'Aw, come on guys,' Deville moans, keeping up the act. 'If we're not partying than why are we hanging out in such a happening place? And why are you shitting in the corner, Boris? Don't try and tell me you're not drunk, big guy.' Angelo throws the last bit in affectionately, winking at Boris like an old friend. 'Well yeah,' Boris says stupidly as he again moves to untie the ropes, but Nikolai cuts him off. 'You will wait here for Comrade Mortality! He will deal with you good and proper.' Nikolai says this with an authoritative nod and turns around. 'We all wait for Comrade Mortality!' 'I've noticed,' Deville quips, rolling his eyes. 'So he's actually planning to show up?' 'Of course he is!' Nikolai roars, turning on Deville once more. 'Comrade Mortality always shows up! Why wouldn't he?' Nikolai asks the last rhetorically, as if it's the stupidest question in the world. He doesn't like the smile that crosses Deville's face. 'Because he's scared,' he states simply, grinning defiantly at Nikolai. Boris and Nikolai break into simultaneous laughter, slapping eachother on the back and wiping tears from their eyes as Angelo's amusement gradually fades and dies altogether. They fall all over one another, absolutely howling with glee at the suggestion. Eventually they begin to calm themselves, and Nikolai straightens up, choking down each new burst as it comes. 'Mr. Deville, please.' Nikolai beckons sympathetically. 'Comrade Mortality fears nothing... We are the great red army... You will bow before the sickle and hammer, or it will crush you!' 'If this fruitcake fears nothing,' Deville begins, moving his eyes from one to the other slowly, 'then why the hell isn't he here yet?' Both Russian faces go dark and angry as they try to think up a response. If this were some cheap-ass cartoon smoke would likely jut from their ears, but since it's only some cheap-ass roleplay you'll have to live with the sounds of gears grinding in their heads. Deville waits patiently for an answer, arching an eyebrow at his hosts as they glare inward. 'Comrade Mortality is very busy,' Nikolai fumbles shakily, scratching his head and squishing his face together. 'Right,' Deville sneers. 'He's out living the life of capitalism, boys... Making as much for himself as he can while shafting the rest of you. I'd wager that his ambition rivals even my own... And I want the world.' Boris and Nikolai look like mirror images of eachother as their jaws drop to the floor and their eyes threaten to leap from their sockets. Boris can only work his mouth uselessly, but Nikolai pounces forward and slugs Deville hard across the mouth. 'Blasphemous dog!' Nikolai screams, spitting all over Deville as he does so. 'Comrade Mortality will kill you when he hears such nonsense! You will not be so smug and insolent with him in your presence, filthy American!' 'Well, let's hope that he shows up soon,' Deville says levelly, merely agitated by the spittle on his face yet absolutely enraged at his inability to wipe it off. 'We've got ourselves a match this Saturday... And whether he shows or not, I plan to be there.' 'You stand no chance, American,' Boris interjects, laughing. 'Comrade Mortality has the full support of his countrymen... We will do what we can to make you lose.' 'Tell us, dog...' Nikolai begins, somehow managing to appear thoughtful. 'How do you plan to fight against such a comrade as Comrade Mortality?' 'I don't plan on fighting him,' Deville responds with a cocky smile. 'I plan on murdering the hypocritic sonofabitch.' Both henchmen almost shit their pants at such blatant disrespect for their leader. 'And if you assholes try to interfere... Well, then I'll kill you, too.' The atmosphere inside the cabin quickly changes as Boris and Nikolai lumber forward so they loom over Deville. He is lost in their shadow momentarily and not a sound can be heard, then there is a thick thwack followed by a muffled grunt of pain. Nikolai and Boris work Deville over like a five cent steak as the night wears on and still there is no sign of Comrade Mortality.
