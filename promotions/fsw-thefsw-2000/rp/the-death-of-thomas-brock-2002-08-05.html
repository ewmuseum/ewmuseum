The Death of Thomas Brock

5 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/5/02 Jason O’Neil stands in a cold, clandestine room, covered wall to wall with stainless steel shelving. Tears roll from his eyes, as he looks to the floors. Blood, still caked to his head, has run down his face to his neck and chest. His eyes, red and heavy, look tired. So tired. Slowly he looks up. “How…”, he asks, not sure of the words that should follow. “It happened in his hotel room. Probably 3 or 4 hours before they found him.” A voice answers. O’Neil looks across the room at an older man, with short gray hair. The large overhead light shines off the bald spot on top of his head. He wears a white lab coat, stained with blood. “He asked for a wake up call, and when he didn’t respond, the concierge went to his room, and found him there.” “I see.” O’Neil looked down again, tears again rolling from his eyes. “Did you…notify his family?” “No, they’re on vacation in the South of France. He was here alone.” The man smiled a weak smile. “Would you like to see the body?” “Yes.” The man leads Jason down a dark corridor. Jason looks at all the lockers filled with dead bodies, and shakes. He’s been through this too many times. He hoped this would be the last time. But then again, he said that every time he ended up in the morgue. The man stops at one of the stainless steel doors. The tag on the outside reads “G-13”. Slowly, he opens the door, and pulls out the cold shelf. His hands go to the zipper. He unzips it, to reveal the face of Thomas Brock. He looks almost at peace, his eyes half open. His lips turned a slight shade of blue. Jason covers his mouth after a gasp, and closes his eyes tight. “That’s him. Thomas Brock.” Jason takes Brock’s hand in his own, and holds it close to his heart. He looks into Brock’s eyes, and speaks softly. His right hand moves to Brock’s face and closes his eyes, for the last time. “Any word on when the autopsy will be complete?” O’Neil asks, letting go of Brocks hand, and wiping the tears from his eyes. The man hand’s O’Neil a handkerchief, and he wipes his hand and eyes. “Not long. Probably get to him in a few hours.” “Good. Foul play suspected?” “No. His heart just gave out. He was an older gentleman, and stress, especially in his line of work…well…lawyers don’t usually last too long.” “I know. That’s why I got out of the business” O’Neil answered. “Oh? You’re a lawyer? Didn’t I see you on TV tonight wrestling?” “Yeah. I’m that guy too.” “A wrestling lawyer. I’ve seen it all. Why’d you give it up?” “Family. It became more important to see them. I worked for my wife’s father doing some light accounting. Nothing big, just helped balance the books in a small family shop. But, she’s gone now.” ”The shop?” “No. My wife. She passed on 3 years ago next Monday.” ”How old?” “28. She was just a baby. So young, so full of life. And I took that life from her and our 4 year old daughter.” “Sad. Very sad. You should get that cut looked at upstairs. Want me to take you?” “No. I know these places pretty well. Thanks. If you find something strange, give me a call. You’ve got my number.” “I will. Thanks Jason.” “No…Thank you.” Jason opens the door and walks down the corridor to the exit of the hospital. For a man of 25 years, he’s been through this all too much. Next time however, it could be his last. Because when Angelo gets word of his agenda…Life will never be quite the same.
