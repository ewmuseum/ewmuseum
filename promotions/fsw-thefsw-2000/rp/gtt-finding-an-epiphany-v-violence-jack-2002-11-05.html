-GTT- Finding (an) Epiphany (v Violence Jack)

5 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
An excerpt from the last will and testament of Yoshi Kimawa, as delivered unedited to Kimbusa by Mr. Kimawa’s attorneys. …I regret that I have so few material possessions to leave to what little family I have left. My estate has been entrusted to my daughter, and she shall inherit all material belongings I have left behind. But what I leave to my pupil Kimbusa is that which I hold most dear. Kimbusa, I have made it quite clear that this passage should be delivered to you personally, as it is of great importance to me. As I write to you, a cancer of an aggressive nature is slowly and methodically eating away my most vital organs. My human body will soon be incapable of supporting my life, and my soul shall move elsewhere. To you, Kimbusa, I entrust this soul. To you, Kimbusa, I entrust my welfare, and I leave it to you to decide where I shall spend eternity. Be aware of yourself, my son. Never forget who you are, where you have come from, and where you are going. Never forget your origins, the most basic of which being your true parents, and your upbringing in our homeland. Never allow our fond memories to fade, as it is in these memories that you shall preserve my spirit. And never forget those terrifying memories of your torturous past, as it is in these memories that you draw strength, and it is where you shall find the power to overcome them. Be aware of your feelings, do not deny yourself expression nor emotion, hatred nor caring, fury nor mercy. Do not allow these memories to become enemies. Befriend the horror, Kimbusa. For in the horror you will find the will to overcome and destroy it. Do not confuse your lack of restraint with immorality. Do not confuse your hatred with depravity. Allow your thoughts to remain clear, do not permit them to become clouded with doubt. I am certain that, as I watch over you from wherever I might be, I shall see a man who grows more and more powerful with each setting of the sun, and a man who one day shall be known simply as immortal. Preserve my memory, Kimbusa, preserve our past. Never forget that which I have taught you. But allow space for new knowledge, Kimbusa. Open your thoughts to this new world. For it is here, in this American wrestling ring, that your gravest battles will be waged and won. Allow your American mentor to instruct you and mold you. But beyond this, never forget who you are in the simplest form; you are a force of nature the likes of which this country has ever seen, nor are they likely to forget. As you no doubt have come to learn of my passing when returning to seek my forgiveness, I leave you with these final words… I love you, my son. Yoshi Kimawa ------------------------------------ It is a particularly murky morning. The sun has risen only to find a steamy soup of cold fog blocking its light. There was no spectacular sunrise, there were no birds singing, there was no picturesque scenery. The hilltop remained concealed beneath a shroud of thick fog and impenetrable haze, denying the body of Yoshi Kimawa a last view of the blue sky. A storm had blown in overnight, causing Steven Caldera’s personal jet to be inconveniently delayed. As a result, Caldera and Kimbusa find themselves alone in a long black limousine, whose luxuries are marred by the darkness and the solemnity of the morning. Racing onward towards the burial site, the two sit in near silence, with only the light whirr of the tires against pavement to keep them company. The plane had been diverted by air traffic control and landed, rather reluctantly, on an old dirt airstrip in a minute village forty miles from the cemetery. A car had met them and began speeding them towards the funeral of Yoshi Kimawa, but the extended time alone with his thoughts had been accepted rather reluctantly by Kimbusa, who sits in a state of total discomfort as they head towards his mentor’s burial. Caldera had done Kimbusa the courtesy of arranging a very respectable service. He had flown a small group of Kimawa’s biological family to this remote spot in Japan, and even had a black suit tailored for Kimbusa. Caldera himself had a new black suit tailored just for this occasion. The spot for the burial was chosen by Kimbusa and Yoshi’s daughter, who had never met prior to the funeral arrangements. It was a nice little spot atop a hill in a remote cemetery many miles from the metropolitan areas of Japan. The hill stands tall overlooking a small pond, where ducks routinely arrive each morning to be fed by the nearby village’s children, a spot that Caldera had gone out of his way to obtain for Yoshi’s family. Caldera had never met Kimawa personally, and yet had shown him a great deal of respect in his passing. But Kimbusa, while appreciative, wished to God that Caldera had not come along for the ride. This long trip had given him a long time to think, and he could never help but get the feeling that Caldera was not only aware of his thoughts, but ridiculing them at the same time. Caldera, for his part, had been respectably silent for the duration of the ride. He had allowed Kimbusa to remain within the confines on his own mind, without once ushering him back to reality. But even with his head constantly turned towards the window and his face staring idly out at the Japanese countryside, Kimbusa felt that Caldera’s mind was working every bit as hard as Kimbusa’s own, analyzing every moment for future use. The drive took half an hour, an agonizing ride that Kimbusa was elated to end. One the duo arrived at the cemetery, it was a brief walk up the hill to where a few quiet observers were waiting for the sponsor of the event and its five hundred pound keynote speaker. Kimbusa had reluctantly agreed to deliver a brief eulogy on behalf of his late mentor. He had felt a great deal of apprehension on the drive there, but his apprehension grew to outright terror as the faces of the gathering came into view. In reality, there was only one face he was looking for; the face to match the soft voice he had heard over the telephone in America; the face of Yoshi’s daughter. He discreetly begins scouring the group for a face to match the voice. The crowd was, for the most part, quite unfamiliar. Everyone’s face seemed dark and brooding, perfectly reflecting the gloomy morning they are present for. They are all native Japanese dressed in ceremonial black garb, and the group seems to meld together in a massive void of sadness and grief. He quickly finds it impossible to see the individuals beyond the dominating weariness encompassing them, and turns his attention towards the grave. The two finally take their positions in the small crowd, with Caldera respectfully fading into the background, and Kimbusa taking stance at the head of the gravesite, only inches from the coffin containing the body of his master. There were no introductions. A priest was on hand, but said nothing. The entire crowd had watched in silent awe as Kimbusa lumbered up the hill, knowing that their speaker had arrived. Without a word of welcome, the onlookers had stopped their quiet conversations and remembrances to allow Kimbusa to speak. But Kimbusa drew a complete blank. He hadn’t prepared for his speech in the least. He had hoped that the speech might flow directly from his mind, and to a lesser extent his heart… But no words came to Kimbusa to accurately describe his deceased mentor. Oblivious to the dumbfounded crowd around him, an emotional Kimbusa looks beyond to the landscape. The fog has set in fully and has banished almost all brightness from the morning. Kimbusa takes comfort in the sound of the pond rippling somewhere below the hilltop, even though the fog restricts his sight to a few short yards. In reality, the entire scene is that only of the ten gatherers, a six foot hole, and a simple wooden coffin. All other reality, the rest of the world, has faded away, leaving Kimbusa only with a million thoughts to sort and express to the saddened hilltop. Even the breeze seems unpleasant, and a small whip of cold air moves beneath the protection of Kimbusa’s jacket, sending a shiver deep up his spine. The shiver seems to rile him from his stupor, and without any real thought or introduction, Kimbusa begins to speak to the Japanese crowd in his native language. “Yoshi Kimawa… was the embodiment of something originating far beyond this earth. His spirit and his mind were clear and crystalline, untainted and pure. His mortal body was frail and weak, powered by a divinity that always eluded the closest reaches of my intellect. Yoshi Kimawa was my mentor and my master, and the only father I ever had.” “He was not intellectual. His mind contained something beyond intelligence. He possessed another sense, the ability to see beyond, the ability to see that which exists only in the spiritual world. He had the ability to see inside the mind, to read and analyze and predict based only on the look in one’s eyes. He existed in this world in body only. In soul, in mind, he lived in a world far beyond my own, a world where there are no limitations, a realm where possibility is infinite and limitless. He looked upon us all with a caring and a love that can only be given by the divine. And thought his body is gone, his soul remains, his mind remains. His presence is here right now, looking upon all of us with the same love he did in his mortal life.” For a moment, Kimbusa finds himself overwhelmed with emotion. Tears begin welling up in his eyes, and Kimbusa quickly wipes them away. But his tears are not of sadness. His voice begins to tremble as he desperately seeks the words to describe his emotions. “In his final words to me, Yoshi entrusted his soul to me. This is a tremendous weight to carry, and I find myself desperate to find the strength to do him justice.” Kimbusa reaches out to the coffin, and places his hand on the rough surface. The fog seems to have encompassed everything near the grave, and the entire physical world seems to disappear. Kimbusa finds himself speaking directly to his dead father, who lies still within the coffin. “Give me the strength, father. Help me glorify you.” With a deep sigh, Kimbusa collects himself. He had been brief, as was expected. But he felt the sudden urge to leave. He felt that he could not bear to watch Yoshi lowered into the grave, and without another word, Kimbusa turns and begins trotting down the hillside. His ears tune in carefully to listen for Caldera to follow, unwilling to turn his eyes back upon his master’s grave. After a moment, another set of footsteps accompanies Kimbusa’s, and Kimbusa quickens his stride, prepared to return to America immediately. The walk is a long and solemn one, as Kimbusa treads over the resting places of countless deceased men and women, carefully navigating through the foggy cemetery with Caldera in tow. After what seems like an eternity, Kimbusa finally comes within viewing distance of the limo, a distance that cannot be more than ten yards. With a sigh, Kimbusa turns back to address Caldera with a bit of reluctance. But instead of the large frame of Caldera, Kimbusa finds himself in the presence of a breath taking young woman. Her face seems tired, but nonetheless radiates an energy that quickly warms Kimbusa. She is clad in a plain black dress that does not in the least detract from her perfect figure. Her long, jet black hair flails loosely in the breeze, and her black eyes penetrate Kimbusa with a sort of uncanny familiarity, quickly giving away the young woman’s identity. “I am Yoshi’s daughter,” she states plainly in her native language of Japanese. Kimbusa actually returns her statement with a thin smile, which is quickly returned. The two share a long glance, locked in a timid stare, as if each were searching for the true nature of the other. “I apologize for my… insufficiency in my words,” Kimbusa states simply. The young lady chuckles slightly in a way that comforts Kimbusa, and even seems to melt away a bit of the fog. “It was beautiful,” she assures him, further raising Kimbusa’s comfort level to the point of near bliss. In the distance, the suited silhouette of Steven Caldera slowly trots into view. Kimbusa suddenly realizes that he very deeply wishes a few moments without Caldera’s intrusion, and is relieved to see Caldera stop, and then quickly alter his course. Caldera fades out of view and into the fog again, once again doing Kimbusa a considerate courtesy. Kimbusa returns his attention to the smiling young woman, whose eyes never left Kimbusa’s. He finds himself smiling again; the day suddenly seems brighter, despite the fact that the steamy fog is still thickening, preparing to swallow the entire cemetery whole. “Father spoke of you often, Kimbusa,” she says in a wonderfully radiant voice. As the words replay in his head, Kimbusa realizes that there is an air of pain and heartbreak in her voice, but they seem no less mystical. “I saw so little of him while the two of you were in America… yet I felt joy to know that he was there fighting for a good cause.” Kimbusa actually feels a bit embarrassed by this. Yoshi had spoken only little of his daughter, and what he had said was vague and discreet. But he feels so completely uninhibited in this woman’s presence that he feels the need to be completely honest. “I only learned of your existence a few short weeks ago,” Kimbusa confesses somberly. But the young woman merely smiles. “As it should be. I asked father to keep me a secret. All this time I have been following your progress, Kimbusa, but it took something like this to bring out of the shadows… and into the fog, so to speak.” Kimbusa’s eyes suddenly drop to the ground, embarrassed once more. After another moment of relishing the young woman’s voice, Kimbusa realizes that he may be deceiving her. After all, she obviously knew very little of Kimbusa’s true nature, or she would not be standing so close unguarded. He has no desire to deprive this young woman of the truth… but rather than openly admitting his troubles, he takes the long route. “I apologize… I must be going momentarily… I am returning to America to compete in… some wrestling tournament.” The girl smiles again, apparently better informed than Kimbusa had imagined. “Your opponent, Violence Jack… he is a threat,” she chuckles. Her laughter spawns from the obvious look of astonishment on Kimbusa’s face. For a moment, Kimbusa becomes truly swept away by this glorious woman, who seems pulsating with energy amid the dreary bleakness of the aging cemetery. “He thinks you to be weak, you know,” she continues, again to the surprise of Kimbusa. “But he knows nothing. He doesn’t know what I know. He doesn’t know the true nature of your strength.” Her words suddenly disturb Kimbusa, as he realizes the extent to which Yoshi must have informed his daughter. He had allowed his demons exposure to the outside world, but for some reason he felt the responsibility of keeping them nearby was required. He had no desire for his past to make its way into the thoughts and minds of people across the planet. But this girl knew. For a moment, he seems unsure how to react. His mouth gaping open slightly, Kimbusa stares at the still-beaming young woman, whose eyes suddenly seem reminiscent of the knowing energy possessed by her father. But the murk suddenly comes rushing back into view, and Kimbusa realizes that the dense fog covering the cemetery is still overpowering the brightness of the morning, and he is still in the presence of his deceased mentor. Completely taken off guard and unsure of his next move, Kimbusa is actually relieved when Steven Caldera makes his presence known. Caldera moves into the picture from between the two, and greets the young woman with a smile. “My apologies for interrupting,” Caldera says pleasantly, extending a hand to the girl. “Steven Caldera,” he states simply, introducing himself. The young woman greets Caldera’s hand with her own ginger touch, and Kimbusa realizes that he still has yet to catch her name. As they shake, both the girl and Caldera turn to face Kimbusa, though Caldera does so more discreetly. The girl, however, still shaking Caldera’s hand, introduces herself directly to Kimbusa. “Call me Epiphany,” is all she says. Caldera grins and retracts his hand, now turning fully to face his massive weapon. Kimbusa is beaming again at the sound of the girl’s name, and his eyes have become entranced with glee. Caldera seems almost angered by this, but remains polite. “I do hate to do this, but Kimbusa and I must be returning to our flight. He’s got to be wrestling in less than twelve hours. It was a pleasure, Ms. Kimawa.” Epiphany smiles pleasantly at Caldera, who bows slightly before heading toward the limo. Kimbusa waits to hear the sound of Caldera’s door slamming shut before saying his own goodbye. “A pleasure, indeed,” is all he can muster beneath the power of the young girl’s eyes. “Indeed,” she returns, and slowly turns away. With a lightness in her step she begins heading back towards her father’s gravesite, parting the fog like Moses parting the sea; with the power of divinity. Kimbusa turns towards the limo and begins quietly heading back. After only a few steps, the ecstasy induced by Epiphany fades. Suddenly he finds himself back in the dense fog, with wisps of moist, cool air penetrating his uncomfortable wool suit. The entire world seems to collapse upon him with the weight of the situation. The young girl had held off the terrible frights that lived in the cemetery. But without her defenses, Kimbusa found himself back in the realm of unbelievable sorrow, alone in the most melancholy of places. Reality had taken hold of his mind again; he had just buried the only father, the only family he ever had. And now the closest thing to companionship was waiting in this ludicrous limousine, wearing an expensive new suit, and trembling with anger. Kimbusa throws open the limo door and seats himself inside. He comes eye to eye with Caldera, who sits directly across from him, legs stretched out far. His eyes seem to exude frustration and anger, but he speaks with a controlled voice once again. “You liked her… didn’t you?” he says. Kimbusa’s eyes remain focused on Caldera’s, locked in a brutal battle to conceal his true feelings, which in reality he himself is still unsure of. “I could see it in your eyes, Kimbusa. It was a look I’ve never seen from you before. No anger. No hatred. No fury. It was almost… dare I say… love?” Kimbusa’s eyes momentarily give him away; Caldera had seen the truth. But Kimbusa’s defenses quickly go up again, hiding away any semblance of emotion. Caldera suddenly sits forward in his seat, leaning in close so that Kimbusa might hear his low, firm voice. “It’s a look that I don’t want to see again until this tournament is over.” Kimbusa is taken aback. Caldera had just denied him the right to happiness, he had taken away a possible path to emotional freedom. But Kimbusa was not about to give up without a fight. “She could be what I need, Mr. Caldera. She could be the path to reconciliation.” Caldera quickly responds with the same tense words. “We have a deal, Kimbusa. If you win this tournament, I will grant you whatever you want, including this girl. But until then, please… hold on to your rage. Hold on to your fury. Violence Jack thinks they will be your undoing. But we know better; they will be his.” Kimbusa considers this for a moment. The limo slowly pulls away, and he glances out the tinted window to the foggy cemetery beyond. The young girl had disappeared beneath the fog… her radiance had disappeared beneath his fury. Caldera had angered him to the point that Kimbusa seemed ready to explode, thereby accomplishing his goal. Kimbusa’s eyes turn back to Caldera, tinted with the familiar, angry red that Caldera has treasured so much. Kimbusa’s voice begins to tremble beneath the weight of his convictions; to win the right to seek out this Epiphany, and finally find enlightenment. “They will be his,” he snarls, having once again allowed the dormant hatred inside to take control…….
