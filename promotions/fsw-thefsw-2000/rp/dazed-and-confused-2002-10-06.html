Dazed and Confused

6 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Somewhere between 10-08 and 10-11 It starts in his left thigh, and trickles its way upward. Slowly but surely it begins working its way up his spine, until it reaches his shoulders and neck. With a final surge of energy it thrusts its way into his head, until his entire body is throbbing with soreness. His eyes bloodshot and glazed, Kimbusa slowly awakens to find himself slumped clumsily on the cold concrete floor of a rotting prison cell. Who… what… how had he arrived there? Who had placed him there? How had they taken him unaware? Where is he? The questions plague Kimbusa’s mind like so many locusts feeding upon the spring crops of a fertile thought. With a tender groan, Kimbusa tries to hoist himself off the cold ground. Finding it rather difficult to stand erect, Kimbusa simply throws himself down upon the thin prison mattress, and takes in a much needed breath of damp, stale air. There is no light in this place. The prison appears to be on lockdown for the evening; the sun has long been set, but in the autumn months it may still be early night. Either way, it seems obvious that there is no way out. The past few hours are a blur. Ever since Glory, the hours had melted together. He remembers wrestling in his match… he remembers defeating Michael Stevens… and he remembers the feeling of steel against flesh, as that fool Jupiter assaulted him after his match. This much mattered very little; he had exacted his revenge. But upon returning to the back, he had promptly collapsed in a remote area of the arena, and it had been hours before someone found him and attended to his injuries. Jupiter had swung the chair rather hard… Reaching up with one stiff arm, Kimbusa touches the dried blood covering the split in his forehead. He hadn’t considered the ramifications of such intense blood loss, and had suffered as a result. Now he finds himself delirious and confused, most likely the victim of not only tremendous blood loss, but a concussion as well. What is this place? With a great deal of effort, Kimbusa manages to turn to his side and examine the room, which proves to be rather claustrophobic. The walls are bare and made of a gray tinted concrete. The floor and ceiling are of the same material. The only break in the monotony is the steel sealing his compartment, the yellow-stained toilet in the corner, and his lonely flat cot that provides him with minimal comfort. Outside of the cell lies only a deep black void. No matter… he doesn’t appear to be venturing outward for some time anyway. Reaching downward, Kimbusa finds himself still clad in his blood-soaked ring attire of black silk pants, and his after-match addition of a white tank top. They hadn’t stripped him of his belongings; perhaps they would be coming for him in the morning. With a groan, Kimbusa manages to raise himself to a sitting position, and he begins gingerly rubbing his head. His temples are throbbing, producing a rather audible ringing in his ears. His head is patched together only by a thin layer of dried blood, and a single finger-sized bandage. His hair is dry and matted with blood, his face growing rough and unclean. They must have brought him here promptly after his collapse, and it must be at least three in the morning. Eyes drooping, Kimbusa realizes the extent of his exhaustion. The previous evening’s contest had not been a particularly draining one. But somehow, Jupiter had managed to deliver a chair shot devastating enough to induce such a bewildered state upon the big man. Kimbusa remembers rather well the destruction that he inflicted in the match, but can’t help but feel somewhat ashamed at his public display of weakness. But his anger for Jupiter only exists skin-deep. In reality, Kimbusa has finally regained command of his senses long enough to realize who had put him in this cell. Steven Caldera had made good his word to enslave the Asian Assassin. Caldera would never have deserted his broadcasting responsibilities. Hence the blood loss; Caldera had been informed of Kimbusa’s desperate situation backstage, but had obviously waited for the conclusion of the show to tend to him. Only after two hours of lying prone on the floor, bleeding profusely, had he received medical care… and transport to this godforsaken prison. On his first trip to American prison, Kimbusa had been wallowing in self-pity, consumed by an insurmountable hatred that had fatally turned inward. Upon his release, he began a journey that lead him to find pleasure in American culture, the release of his pain, and the discovery of the most valuable emotion of them all… love. But in the end, the man that manufactured his successful emancipation had been responsible for returning him to the place from whence he came. There is no anger this time. Kimbusa feels no fury. What good will hatred do? Kimbusa’s hatred had burned hot enough to melt his very soul, but not hot enough to melt the steel that incarcerates him. In the end, he had become trapped in that lonely cell with only his demons to keep him company; it was enough to drive the big man mad. He had no need to relive these hellish few weeks. Still, something needs to be done, of this Kimbusa is certain. First and foremost, Kimbusa knows that he needs to speak with Epiphany… to hear her voice would make the nights sleeping upon his stiff, worn prison bed just as pleasant as his plush silk sheets in Houston. But for the sake of his sanity, Kimbusa realized immediately that he would have to take firm hold of reality and work with it; and the reality of the situation appears to be that Caldera had put him in a place where Epiphany would never find him. It is at this point that Kimbusa finally realizes that he has no idea where in the world he is. He didn’t even know where his matches were last night, much less where he had been taken. It depends how long he had been unconscious, in reality… but he had no idea of knowing how long he had been out. And it is this that begins to aggravate the big man. For a moment, he begins to curse his mortal body’s limitations. His soul begs him to stand and remove the steel bars that restrain him, but his body is so weak that it is barely capable of sight. With this in mind, Kimbusa decides to conserve his energy, close his eyes, and go back to sleep.
