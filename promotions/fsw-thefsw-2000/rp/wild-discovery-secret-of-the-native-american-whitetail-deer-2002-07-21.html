Wild Discovery: Secret of The Native American Whitetail Deer

21 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07-21-02 Narrator: The Native American wilderness covers great expanses across the United States, and supports a great ecosystem unbridled and raw. Many creatures inhabit this territory, and we find ourselves chasing the mystery of the great North American Whitetail Buck. The Wild Discovery theme plays while several shots of wildlife from all over the earth flash. A Grizzly Bear swats a Salmon out of a stream, followed but a Lion running down a Thompson’s Gazelle. The credits flash by and the final scene is that of a large Gray Wolf chasing towards the camera and baring its fangs, which are long and ivory like white. The scene opens within a large wooded area, somewhere in the southwest of The United States. Several trees have been downed, maybe the product of a whirlwind, or a heavy storm. Large drops of rain fall, but not in quantity, maybe just a low hanging cloud was heavy in the air. Narrator: Wildlife teems in this environment, everything from the large Pileated Woodpecker to the small Reticulated Ground Squirrel. Insects gather near small pools of water, and crickets chirp heralding the coming night. We will hopefully find one of the great specimens that inhabit this region. Joining me as always is Dr. Mumbo Jumbo, noted scientist and handy indecipherable native. Dr. Jumbo: Mumbo Jumbo Mumbo Jumbo Mumbo Jumbo. Translator: Today…the wilderness has a good feeling…birds are singing…the leaves ripple…the air smells…clean. Maybe…we will see a deer before too long. Narrator: Indeed they do. After a short travel down into a ravine, they spot a few does and a fawn playing in a grassy knoll. Not quite what they were after, but where the does play, the buck can never be far behind. In the distance, the camera picks up the deer. The young one is barely out of the womb, and has spots covering its small body. One doe looks to be much larger than the other, suggesting perhaps it is still a yearling. The camera crew and Dr. Jumbo take a closer look, walking toward the small band of deer, but something frightens them in the distance. Dr. Jumbo: Mumbo Jumbo Mumbo Jumbo Mumbo Jumbo. Translator: Something has spooked the does…could it be a buck? No…look…down the ravine! Narrator: What spooked the deer was an enormous wolf, walking alone in the wild. The crew will quickly take cover and hopefully avoid a confrontation, although these majestic canines generally like to keep from human contact. They hope maybe on this day they will get more than they came for, and perhaps see nature unfold. The large wolf drags its nose close to the ground then stops, licking its chops and staring toward where the deer had gone. He suddenly lifts his nose in the air, and looks to where the crew are at baring his teeth as he does. Dr. Jumbo: Mumbo Jumbo Mumbo Jumbo Mumbo Jumbo. Translator: The wolf…does not like us here….and we should be respectful and back away. We….as humans…try to umm…infringe upon his territory…now he has nowhere left…and he feels threatened. Narrator: Dr. Jumbo and his crew will leave this area for now, but he seems intrigued to watch this large male, for he has a strange quality not normally found in a wolf. He hunts alone, making him either an outcast or dominant. The Lone Wolf will likely hunt The Native American Deer before the day is out, and it seems Dr. Jumbo wants to see it play out. Blackness unfolds in the night air, and the scene has obviously changed to some unknown time at night. The sounds are different, as is the air. It is now dark, calm, almost spooky knowing what creatures lurk. A small man stands upon a rock holding an infra-red scope, and wearing a smile. Narrator: It seems Dr. Jumbo has spotted something and is motioning for a camera. Perhaps it’s the great Stag he has searched for? The calm and silent night does little to hide the wary deer as its hooves crunch the underbrush. A camera catches the whole unfurling of its glory, stepping out from behind a thicket of Popple trees, and into the open. Dr. Jumbo: Mumbo Jumbo Mumbo Jumbo Mumbo Jumbo. Translator: Wow…I have never seen a specimen like this one. Its great…antlers stretch high…I believe it has over ten tines…and a great gray back…signifying age. I want to…get a closer look…let us try to move in. The crew begins to move forward silently when the deer’s head snaps backward and Dr. Jumbo holds his hand up to signal them to wait. They do, and watch as the drama unfolds before their eyes. Narrator: The Stag has spotted or smelled something he really dislikes, and he is making it clear to us. The largest male, or dominant male will warn the forest by stamping his hoof on the ground and snorting. One can only wonder what he has seen, because he faces away from Dr. Jumbo and his crew. Dr. Jumbo: Mumbo Jumbo Mumbo Jumbo Mumbo Jumbo. Translator: Look! Out of the forest, the wolf emerges slowly, head down, fur bristled. His teeth are bared and his tongue lolled in frenzy. He can smell the old stag, and he can taste its fear and worry. The large buck looks around nervously, but has nowhere to go. Narrator: This is what Dr. Jumbo has been looking for! The Native American Stag has no choice but to fend off The Lone Wolf! We will watch as the drama unfolds, and only hope that these majestic beasts are not spooked away, as Dr. Jumbo has made a life long vow to not disturb the natural balance. The eyes on the wolf look possessed with hunger, and a shrill cold wind whips through the air. The old buck lowers his head in defense as the wolf closes in, preparing to fight to the death. The skilled hunter circles slowly, the buck never letting the wolf out of his sight. Dr. Jumbo: Mumbo Jumbo Mumbo Jumbo Mumbo Jumbo. Translator: The Wolf has huge canine teeth…made to rip its opponent’s throat out…and disable them. He hopes to grab the Stag in a moment…of weakness…they have now entered the mortal…coil. The deer makes an unprecedented move and strikes for the wolf, catching it off guard and throwing it backward on the ground, but the wolf is quickly back up, growling extremely loud and fierce. Saliva drips from the great maw of the huge canine as he lunges in a moment of indecision by the deer, and grabs for the jugular vein. Blood is drawn, and it quickly rains down upon the leaves that are being trampled underfoot. The does lurk in the shadows, and can do nothing but watch as the Native American Deer is slowly brought down by The Lone Wolf. Narrator: The deer bleats now, as if it pleads for its life, but it is too late, a bloody end has come to the revered animal at the hands of a more powerful adversary. We can do naught but watch, and wait for the end to come. Soon the end does come, and the deer gives its final kick, then it breathes no more. The wolf is victorious, raising its head, the muzzle covered in the blood of his enemy, and he howls in triumph. Somewhere, several thousand miles away, Timo Bolamba looks out at the stars and notes the full moon. The Wild Samoan hears the call of the wild and lets out a howl that is heard only by the night creatures. He looks down to the piece of beef jerky in his hand and he snaps it up. Narrator: The night air has gone cool, and the forest is once again calm. The circle of life has played out, and this night has been good to the hunters. Nobody knows what the future holds for this impressive beast, but tonight he is victorious. The Wild Discovery theme plays and the shots pan across the screen again, complimented by production credits and jungle style music. -elsewhere- The night air is calm, and Dr. Jumbo’s crew set up to film again, hoping to find another specimen. The camera man sets his camera on the tripod and readies to film. He takes the lens cover off and turns it on, only to stare straight into the face of a snarling Lone Wolf. A scream is heard in the night as the shot fades… To black.
