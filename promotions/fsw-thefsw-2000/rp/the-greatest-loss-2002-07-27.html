The Greatest Loss

27 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07-27-02 Timo sat alone on his deck, overlooking the city of Detroit, deep in thought. He rested his head upon his fist, and sighed. He was bored and it showed. It seemed he had finally taken care of Johnny Raindance, and proved he was the greater warrior, but something still bothered him. This was FSW, a new federation to him, and he wasn’t used to the people. Sure, there was Mike and Big Davey, but he never really liked those guys to begin with. To top it off, he had to fight Marvelous this week, and he just hadn’t the will to do so lately. He could feel a chill in the air, and it troubled him. The phone rang and he reached over to pick it up, carrying the cordless receiver with him onto the deck. It was, after all, early. He held up the handset and half-heartedly answered. “Hello?” “Timo, is that you?” He recognized the voice immediately, and it almost brought a smile to his face. Almost. “Father! I haven’t spoke to you in ages How are you-“ There was a certain stop in Timo’s voice as he could tell something was wrong, and a cold chill ran through his body. A slow shake built inside him and he soon felt it hard to hold the phone in his hand, for the grip was not stable. “Timo, you need to come to Philadelphia. Domino is sick.” Edward “Domino” Dimitri, long time friend of the family, and Timo’s mentor was old, and Timo knew his day was coming, but he could never be ready for it. He stood up and bravely spoke to his father, Rufan. “Okay, I’ll be there as soon as I can. Tell him I am coming Father.” “He knows you are coming, that’s why he has held on for so long Timo. Please, make haste.” Timo hung the phone up with a push of the lightly lit button, and turned on his heel, his knees barely functioning now. The man who had stood up to men like Ivan Stanislav and Meanstreak was shaking, and as he made his way into the bedroom where he usually slept, he silently sat, lightly waking his wife. “Gwenneth honey, we have to go now. Domino is sick, and they don’t think he is going to last long.” She simply looked up with compassion in her eyes, and knew his were glassy, and waiting to burst like an over full dam. Still he seemed calm, unusually. She let out a soft sigh as she sat up in the bed, and he got up, as silently as he sat, and left the room. She could hear him rummaging around in his garage while she prepared to leave. She packed up for the two children and began to get clothing for herself when she heard him snap all at once. It was enough to bring a tear to her eye. She never understood the tribal words that escaped from his mouth, but she knew that they were not the type that young Samoans were accustomed to using. She knew that the clatter of metal and shattering of glass was likely venting of frustration, but it still worried her. All of a sudden, a quiet calm enveloped the room, and he walked in, blood trickling from his knuckles. “I cut myself.” She made quick to bandage the wound and they packed up the family car, a Jeep Grand Cherokee, then they headed to the small airport Timo always used. The whole way, aside from the oblivious murmurs and burbles of the children, there was nary a noise made. And it somehow seemed better that way. The ride on the plane was short, and sadly the first of the children. Timo had always wished it would be under different circumstances, but this was a family emergency in a way. He looked over to Gwenneth as she entertained the twins, and then down to his bandaged fist. -Stupid, should have thought about that first- Timo was calm, cool, and collect, and remained that way throughout the flight. He stayed silent for quite a long time, and nobody seemed to mind. It was clear millions of emotions must have coursed through his body and mind, but he made no efforts to show them. They signed up for a rental car, and traveled briskly through the traffic and usual urban scenario that was Philadelphia. The small Ford Focus they drove hummed a bit as Timo drove it past what the normal rental driver would. He shifted through the gears, the shifter sounding mechanical, but that was most of the sound in the car, outside of the occasional semi he passed. “You should probably get some food when we get there. I’m pretty hungry, and I am sure you are too.” The first words he had spoke in hours came out finally, and she smiled a bit. Timo was always hungry, and it was uncharacteristic of him to be so solemn, but then again, she knew that “Domino” was a little more than the average mentor to him. He had taught Timo life. He had taught him how to fight the demons, and to make good on his promises to himself. He had simply taught Timo what nobody else seemed to be able to. They arrived at the hospital, and he stopped in front of the entrance. The Samoan exited and Gwenneth got out to park the vehicle. He disappeared into the large glass doors, and found his father waiting for him at the door, where he had likely been for several hours now. Timo was greeted with a hug, the kind that only a father and son can experience, and he looked up with eyes clouded and painful. “How is he Father?” “He isn’t doing well Timo, they say its cancer. He had been fighting back the pain for a long time now and ignoring doctors advice.” Rufan sighed. “You should go Timo, I will lead the way.” They walked down a series of corridors, and traversed an elevator, coming to the fourth floor. The elevator dinged and the doors opened briskly. Timo followed Rufan out the door and down to the left. They stopped seven doors down, and the name above the door read “Dimitri.” Timo entered the room with a heavy heart, he had never saw his friend like this. He didn’t look, right, covered with tubes and hoses, not at all like the back alley pug that had so much fire just a few months before. The Samoan sat alone alongside the friend he had learned from, and watched as Rufan closed the door. “That you kid? My eyes aint so good right now.” “Yeah Dom, it’s me.” “You heard eh? Seems I finally got an opponent I can’t lick.” Timo bit his lip, and wiped an eye that had begun to shed waterworks. “Don’t say that Dom, you can beat anything.” “Naw kid, it’s okay. God knows best what to do with this old pug, and this is just my time. Everyone has their day kid, everyone has to meet the big guy.” Timo reached his hand out, and the normally cold Domino took it. He grabbed onto the shaking hand of The Samoan and he smiled. “You got strong kid, I remember when you was a little shrimp. You know why you got strong?” “No.” “Because you got heart, you aint never said you cant win. People like that get strong.” Domino coughs a few times, and there is an uneasy silence. Finally, the old man breaks it. “Timo, you are grown now. You got kids, a wife, you don’t need an old fart like me trying to give you lessons. You got it all right here.' He reaches over to Timo and places an old beaten and gnarled hand over Timo’s heart. “You got spunk, charisma, and you got more talent than I ever saw in my life kid.” “Dom, I am scared man. You are my hero, you can’t leave.” Tim had begun to let the tears flow from his eyes now, and the concerned face of his friend looked over to him and smiled. It was a soothing smile, and for a moment it brought the tears to a stop. “I aint leaving kid. You got enough of me in you to last forever. Look at you, all scarred up, you got battle wounds. Wounds from life. Never forget them, how you got them. You know, I aint long for this world kid. I got a lot to do when I get upstairs, lotsa skirts to chase.” He lets out a couple short bursts of laughter that are marred with fits of coughing. Timo feels his grip loosening over the period of the conversation, and he knows too well what it means. “Timo, I only got one lesson left for you.” “What is it Dom?” “This one is the last lesson, call it the final teaching of your old pal.” Timo waited with an open mind to hear his friend’s advice. “Timo, patience is the key to happiness in this sport. You may not win today, or tomorrow, but someday, if you are patient, you will be at the top, and you will look down, seeing the faces that want to learn from you. You got to embrace them Timo, like I embraced you.” Domino coughed a bit more, and his grip suddenly became tight. “There aint one day kid, one day I regret. Don’t ever regret it. You have to give in to your problems, and I know you got a lot of demons buggin ya. Learn to cope with them and use them to your advantage.” Timo nodded. He let go of Domino’s hand as the old man turned, using what strength he had left to get a box off the table he had next to him. “Timo, I got something for you.” The Samoan took the box in his hands and looked with inquisitive eyes at the old man as he reached for the top. “You left these at the gym when you were running with those New Breed punks. I figured maybe you would want them back before I kicked off.” Timo opened the box and inside his old holy wrestling pants were neatly mended and cleaned. He pulled them up, and took in the sight of his old days, smiling a little as he traced a line down the side of the sewn on Cobra’s that were stitched to each leg. He looked back down to his friend and Domino held his hand out again, Timo took it without hesitation. “Kid, you can be the best, you got it in yah. Go out there and show the world that there is still fight in you! You train yourself up proper! For the love of god, fight them worthless bums hard!” Domino gripped Timo’s hand with all the strength he had left, as he did, tears began to streak down Timo’s face. Domino was forcing himself to talk amidst coughing attacks. The nurse busted into the room just as his heart monitor began to beep erratically. “You are the best thing to ever come into my life kid. Go out there…and show them all…what Domino taught yah.” His hand went limp and the heart monitor flat lined. Timo fell from the chair and held his mentors hand to his forehead, sobbing as he did. From the doorway, his father, mother, and wife all watched as the man who had taught him everything slipped from the world. Silently, Rufan walked to his son and placed a reassuring hand on his shoulder. Timo stood up and watched as the doctors shook their heads. He felt a fire burning in his heart as they covered Domino with a bed sheet. His knuckle had burst open, and blood shown through on the bandage that covered it as he gripped the wrestling pants that he had received from Domino. “It’s time to go Timo.” Timo stood tall and still like a mountain for a moment, then he nodded to his father. He stood for just a moment as he still held the hand of his mentor. Words seemed to flow from him as he prepared to leave. “Goodbye my friend…I WILL show them all.”
