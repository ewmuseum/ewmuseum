'While you were away' (flashback)

2 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Sept 2nd, 2002 He'd built the bay window so she could look out over the cascading garden that she'd designed. The garden and the bay window were meant to be her refuge, her place in this new home; but home wasn't here anymore. The entire house shook and quivered in the silence that came from the room at the end of the hall. From the nursery they had built together. The crib unused, the basinet empty these last few weeks. There were still clothes in the closet, tags on, unworn. She glanced at her watch and hoped her cab would arrive before the rains came. A late season storm that was coming in fast from the west. For a moment, she worried about him being out there on top of the hill, but only for a moment. Nothing could touch him, nothing could hurt him now. He'd rebuilt the wall around himself as only he could. It had taken her the better part of two years to coax down those walls, to gain access to the 'cave of wonders' filled with a prideful man's kindness, love, anxiety, and fears. Two years to see him honest and open, as he truly was. But those walls had come up and left her alone beyond them in less than a heartbeat. And now the walls had been locked down so tight that even if the almighty came calling, all he would get is swung at and ran off in a hail of confused anger and bitterness. Imagine that for a moment, god out amongst the massed crowd at the foot of the battlements of pride and stubbornness, equally unable to make any headway against the defenses. Were that the gates of heaven so staunchly guarded, Lucifer might never have tried. Wiping the streaked mascara from her pale cheeks, she sighed and brushed her brunette locks away from her face as her cab rolled quietly up the gravel driveway. She knew there would be no tearful goodbye kiss or heartfelt embrace. That would have been far too much strain on the fragile tether that bound them. She'd known that fact from the moment she told him she was leaving. His curt nod of understanding betrayed not a shred of emotion. Both understood that what the other needed weren't the same, but equally paramount to their own senses of self. She needed the quiet company of close friends and that stability that comes with 'home'. He needed his hands dirty, sure that the answer would come in the work. And as she rode away from all that they had built, she prayed that he would find his reckoning before his walls would crumble under the weight they were forced to bear and collapse in, consuming the fragile beauty she knew hid inside. As he hammered home the last of the cross members, he felt the stir of the cold wind rattling through the limbs of the heavy oak he toiled under. She had gone. Gone to rest, to heal, and to wait for him. As he gathered up his tools, the wind began to shriek, driven by all the force Mother Nature could muster. The rain poured down in sheets, each drop an icy finger that ripped at the flesh as lightning flashed in the swirling clouds above, reflecting off polished marble that lay at the center of the fenced in area at the base of the oak. Thunder echoed through the ravines and timberland as lightning sizzled the air and pounded the earth. The wind howled even greater still, whipping the rain into torrents that tore whatever they touched. Only when the roar of the storm reached it's zenith, only when he was absolutely sure that no one could hear him, did he let it out. All sound masked by the driving wind and booming thunder. All tears hidden by the driving rain in his face. And although no man could hear his cries, in the barn the horses pranced nervously in their stalls. The bull stood defensively at the head of his herd, pawing nervously at the ground in anxious fear. Cats raised their hackles and dogs cowered, covering their faces with their paws. It has been said that all creatures on this earth are kindred spirits with a basic sense for all that exists. Dogs smelling fear, wolves preying on the weak. Somewhere it needs to noted that all beasts great and small can sense and fear the wrath and rage of a grieving father, from which no one is safe. No one...
