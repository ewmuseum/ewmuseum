-GTT- Revelations (v Foamy)

5 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
'I do not like it here.' Kimbusa registers his all-too familiar complaint with an extra sense of hesitation, as he stands in the doorway of his brand new apartment. The day had started at four in the morning, when the violent beating of an incoming helicopter woke him from his sleep. The night had been a difficult one, and Kimbusa was quite eager for a change in scenery. Caldera's helicopter then flew Kimbusa to a small airport, where the Global Championship Wrestling jetliner was waiting. The plane flew Kimbusa across the nation to the city of Houston, Texas. Once there, Caldera's limosuine collected him at curbside, and he was driven to a high rise apartment complex, where Caldera himself was waiting in the lobby. Together the duo took the elevator to the thirty second floor, where Caldera had arranged new quarters for Kimbusa. Kimbusa immediately detested it. 'Perhaps the city was a mistake,' Kimbusa offers. He surveys the apartment with a sense of apprehention, as the modern era was in full force in this place. 'Come in, take a look around. You might like it,' Caldera says to him happily. In reality, Caldera seems unreasonably excited about the new room, as if it were something very special. But to Kimbusa, it appeared to be a rather generic modern-world apartment, not totally unlike Caldera's high-rise room in New York. Caldera, clad in his usual business attire of black suit and red tie, walks across the hardwood floors of the main living area to a series of windows against the wall. Caldera throws back a layer of thick grey curtains, and reveals the city skyline beyond. Caldera regards the city with great esteem, and seems overjoyed to be there. Kimbusa, however, was most unhappy. The skyline, in reality, was only half-visible behind the thick layer of smog that constantly hung over the city. And what might have been a skyline to make for promising sunsets was marred by two monstrous buildings in the distance. 'What is that?' Kimbusa asks curiously, turning his nose up at the buildings outside his window. 'That's the Astrodome, and just behind it is Reliant Stadium,' Caldera explains proudly, almost seeming as if he'd built the things himself. Kimbusa turns in a slow circle, surveying the room. The floors are made of a dark colored hardwood, the walls coated in a deep grey shade of paint. But above the dark undertones that might have otherwise made Kimbusa comfortable, the modern world has taken over. A thirty-six inch flat panel LCD television hangs from the wall, complete with surround sound. The recliner in the corner offers automated massage features. The sofa next to it is upholstered entirely in Italian silk, and appears to be too fragile to sit on. A fireplace in the remaining corner contains gas logs for easy lighting in the winter time. A beautfully hand-crafted wooden coffee table rests in the center of the room, just in front of the sofa. And the entire room feels to be exactly ten degrees too warm. 'I do not like it here,' Kimbusa reiterates. Caldera seems frustrated. He had pulled many strings and spent a great deal of money to set Kimbusa up in this apartment, and he was receiving no gratitude whatsoever. Caldera sighs heavily and moves towards the sofa, but thinks twice about seating himself on the silk and instead takes a seat in the recliner. Kimbusa remains standing, glaring down at Caldera in a constant silent protest. Outside, even thirty floors above the street, car alarms and police sirens can be heard. The entire city, including the room itself, carried an odd odor to it, which Kimbusa eventually attributed to the pollution. The apartment was far too big and open to qualify as reasonable. In reality, Caldera had set Kimbusa up to live in extravagance, which Kimbusa had sworn to avoid years ago. Seeing that Kimbusa was preparing to register another protest, Caldera leans forward in his chair and locks eyes with the big man. 'Don't you see what I'm trying to do here?' he asks. Kimbusa remains silent, willing to hear Caldera out. 'I'm trying to incorporate you into this country. You don't have to like it, you don't have to stay here forever. But you need to be among it, you need to see it, to smell it, to taste it, to live it for a few weeks. You can't win this tournament without this experience, Kimbusa. There is so much more to this industry than brute force. You have to understand the business, Kimbusa. And you can't understand the business without understanding the country that it operates in.' Kimbusa, still dissatisfied with his current situation, nods in agreement with Caldera, while still plotting his next complaint. Caldera presses on. 'Look, I know that you don't like me all that much. You don't have to. That was never part of our deal. But we can still help each other, Kimbusa. We can still help us to each other's goals. But what I want from you right now is to sit down...' Caldera beckons Kimbusa to have a seat on the sofa, having finally given in to the fact that someone must inevitably sit on the silk. '...And talk to me for a moment.' Kimbusa tilts his head to the side, confused about this statement. In reality, Kimbusa hadn't truly talked to anyone since the departure of his lifelong mentor Yoshi Kimawa. He had avoided conversation for months, and had gone to great lengths to preserve his privacy. But now, it seemed, he would be forced to have a conversation with Steven Caldera. Kimbusa timidly heads to the sofa, and takes a seat. His face tells the story of his surprise as he finds himself very comfortable on the silk sofa, and for a moment he seems content to sit on this comfortable imported sofa and talk with an American businessman. 'So, Kimbusa... tell me... what exactly did you do back home in Japan?' Caldera begins. Kimbusa's contentment quickly disappears as Caldera begins with this touchy subject. Caldera had asked the very question that had been plaguing him for years. It was the same question that the American media bombarbed him with upon his arrival to the country, and it was the same question that Kimbusa had thought considered for years, trying desperately to come up with an answer. 'I was Sumo,' he says quickly, trying to move away from the subject. But Kimbusa isn't much of a conversationalist, and Caldera, who sits in his expensive Italian suit while slouched down in his recliner, presses on. 'But after that. Forgive me for prying, I know that the media has bothered you about this for a long, long time. But I'm just curious to know more about you... more about who and what you are.' Kimbusa takes a few moments to let this soak in, weighing his options for answers. In reality, telling Caldera the truth here would have been like lowering the defenses to his subconscious, and letting the rage in his heart conquer him. He knew somewhere deep down that these things had gone on, and he had tried very hard to supress them. Now, even having been asked the question, he found it difficult to keep his feelings contained. Caldera had allowed the memories to awaken from their dormant state, and begin to run rampant through Kimbusa's mind. Kimbusa immediately takes a deep breath in an attempt to regain control, as Caldera looks on, clueless about the inner battle being fought before his eyes. 'Why is this important? Why must everyone...' Kimbusa trails off, his head lowering steadily to avoid eye contact with Caldera. 'I'll tell you why,' Caldera begins. He suddenly grows quite serious, as he leans forward in his recliner. The entire room seems to disappear to Kimbusa, as everything goes dark and only the sound of Caldera's voice can be heard. 'Because there are people in this industry that are more than entertainers. And there are people in this business that are willing to do anything and everything to win. Because there are people that can and will find out about this, Kimbusa, and they can and will use it against you. If you show the slightest bit of weakness, they will exploit it. The Ninth Wonder of Foam isn't clever enough to do this, so there's still time to cover for your weakness. But-' Kimbusa suddenly lunges out of his seat, and with both hands and a heavy toss flips the wooden coffee table over, sending it crashing into the wall. He turns to Caldera looking as if he might attack, his eyes blazing with a frightening red glow. Caldera seems surprised but remains unintimidated, even as Kimbusa begins to shout. 'Weakness?! There is no weakness! The only weakness is that of the man who shows mercy! But there is not mercy left in me!' Kimbusa then quickly turns to the coffee table, which is lying propped against the wall, and delivers a stiff side kick, splitting the table directly in two. Caldera winces at the loss of his expensive furnishing, but nonetheless remains collected. 'You won't show mercy? You won't show restraint? Get ahold you of yourself, damnit! You can't win this tournament if you're back in prison!' Caldera shouts back. Kimbusa seems surprised that this much smaller man has remained firm in spite of his fury, and it seems to calm him. The fires widthdraw from Kimbusa's eyes, and retreat back to the depths from where they came. After a moment, Kimbusa's head drops again, and he retreats away from the physical world again, letting only Caldera's now soothing voice accompany him. 'Don't you see what I'm trying to do here? I just want to make sure that we find a happy medium here. We can't have you let this anger defeat you from the inside, and we can't let the anger defeat you from the outside. You can't hold it all back, or someone will easily take advantage of it and send us home. And you can't let it take control of you, or you'll go too far and find yourself back in that cold prison cell.' Caldera speaks softly now, trying to ease Kimbusa's anger with each word. After a pause, Kimbusa sighs and looks up at Caldera. Caldera seems to have grown in the past few moments... Or perhaps Kimbusa simply feels smaller. With a thin groan, Kimbusa returns to his seat on the silk sofa, which again feels incredibly comfortable. Caldera presses on. 'All I had to do was ask you about your past, and you exploded. You ruined a ten thousand dollar table. That could have been someone's neck, for God's sake. You need to take control, Kimbusa. You need to let me in so I can help.' Kimbusa considers this for a long time. The expensive room seems to fade away; all the modern amenities disappear, the comfort of the silk on his skin is washed away, as he sinks back entirely into the realm of his thoughts. Caldera notices this, as Kimbusa's eyes seem to go blank, and his entire body relaxes. Kimbusa's eyes suddenly flash red again, causing Caldera to brace for another tirade... But it never comes. Instead, the firey Kimbusa, still seemingly apart from the room, speaks softly to Caldera, who is forced to lean inward to hear him. 'There were three of us that night in Tokyo... We were returning from a job, when we saw her...' Caldera, realizing that a rather intimate confession was about to be made, smiles thinly. This is what he had wanted to hear.... 'My associates insisted that we find a place of solitude, where this young girl might... entertain them. At the time, all I knew was how beautiful she was...' The rain fell in a way that seemed aggressive and hateful on that night. It was as if someone in the heavens were seeking to punish those below with a constant downpour of tiny needle pricks. Thunder groaned and rumbled in the distance, a constant reminder that the torrential downpour was merely the beginning. The darkness that night was thick like soup, the air palpable and cool. It carried with it a foul stench and even fouler taste, as if the putrid corpse of some rotting animal had melded with the thick emissions of a diesel engine. Kimbusa had looked quite ominous and threatening in his black trench coat and soaking wet fedora. As if something out of film noir, Kimbusa and two of his fellow Yakuza were walking through the streets, allowing the horrible rainfall to cleanse their bodies of the crime they had just committed. In truth, it was blood they were seeking to cleanse; the blood of just one of their many victims. At this time in his life, Kimbusa was struggling to survive. Banned forever from sumo and disowned by his family, Kimbusa had been adopted by a bigger, stronger, more brutal family. He had found himself a new home. This was the night Kimbusa had dreamt about for months... years. It was the fateful night that had shaped his every emotion, his every action, his every word since. Everything he had come to know was affected by that night. Every moment he was breathing was a constant reflection back. At this point, walking through the cold backroads and alleys of metropolitan Tokyo, the trio felt nothing about the sin they had committed. In truth, it did not feel like sin. The man had been a drug dealer and a murderer himself. He deserved what he got. Kimbusa had never felt remorse for these crimes. The men he had met in this business were crooked and evil. They were of the most demented variety that anyone, regardless of their regard for human life, would have quickly condemned them to the fate they met at Kimbusa’s hands. Kimbusa acted under orders, anyway. He was a businessman, nothing more. He had justified and rationalized his actions to a point beyond caring. Nobody ever knew what they were doing, and if they did, they dared not confront them about it. There was a silent understanding in the town, after all. Kimbusa’s masters were the ones who ran the city, and the elected city officials simply went about their business, hoping that they might never come face to face with the five hundred pound Yakuza. Kimbusa had become a legend in the city. In truth, no one had ever seen him. At least, no one had ever seen him and been around to tell of it the following morning. But tales of a monstrous man of immense proportions and unstoppable fury had grown beyond the underground, and parents often warned their children away from any dark place that the monster might be waiting. But Kimbusa never had and never intended to harm anyone innocent. He was strictly a businessman, nothing more. He was not a murderer, he was not a killer. He did favors for his superiors at their request, and he did them to the best of his ability with a minimum of questions. All he knew was that the men he had been ordered to act upon were the scum of the earth; otherwise, they would not have been in business with his superiors. The two men he was with on this night were both dwarfed by the five hundred pound mammoth. They were merely there for the diplomatic stage of their missions, which was usually rather brief. Kimbusa would then step in to finish the deal, and the three would move on to other business. On this night, they had completed a rather routine visit to the home of a former business associate of their employer’s. They had accomplished their goals, and unlike the more brutal and merciless men in their profession, spared his family and their home. Kimbusa’s heart had been turned black by some inner force that he had yet to identify. But his heart was still a heart after all. A street light above was flickering wildly, convulsing a few last times before its death. He was not sure why the light stuck out in his memory, but Kimbusa remembered it well. Perhaps it was the young lady standing beneath it, flashing in and out between reality and imagination. Every few moments, the light would remain solid just long enough to catch a full glimpse of the young lady. She was positively radiant, seemingly miles away from the hellacious storm that was still raging. It did not strike him at first why a young lady of such beauty and class would be standing on a street corner during the pouring down rain. After a moment, one of his colleagues realized the situation, and beckoned the young lady to their side of the street. Kimbusa could not take his eyes off the young girl. Slender and beautiful, she seemed too well kept to have been waiting in the rain for so long. In truth, her hair was matted and her clothing damp, but her deep, dark eyes seemed to shine with an inner energy that warmed Kimbusa inside, and sheltered him from the downfall. His associates did the talking, negotiating with the young lady for her services. Neither of his partners was of great physical prowess, and neither presented any sort of outward significance. Both wore the same black noir attire as Kimbusa, but neither struck Kimbusa as the kind to attract such a fine young woman. Deep down, Kimbusa knew the truth behind this woman’s exhistence; but some part of him enjoyed the fantasy of being in the presence of the most beautiful young girl in the world. Kimbusa had ignored the negotiations, and continued to examine the young girl as the foursome began walking further down the street. Her figure was perfect; slim waist, wide chest, slender legs that seemed to tease Kimbusa’s fancies with each tender step. He had been truly enchanted by the girl, a feeling he had never thought possible. Even as they entered a run-down hourly hotel on a darker corner of the street, he felt as if the young girl had descended to heaven, and might take him back with her. The girl disappeared behind the creaking wooden door to the hotel with the first of his associates, and remained there for quite some time. But Kimbusa’s mind was flying with emotion; all the anger and brutality he had felt for the past year had been swept away in an instant, all defeated by this one fragile young lady. Even as the first associate emerged and the second entered, he remained oblivious to her true nature, convinced that she was truly divine in origin. He had been in the presence of a holy being, and wished nothing more than to feel that warmth again. An hour had passed since they first entered the hotel, and the second associate emerged. From within, the young lady beckons Kimbusa inward with a voice so tender and sweet that it made Kimbusa’s black heart ache. 'Are you next?' she cooed, as Kimbusa timidly entered the room. She was lying beneath a single white sheet, smiling widely at the five hundred pound man as he entered. Kimbusa’s face must have been absolutely blank, as the girl seemed confused by his silence. But inside, Kimbusa was flying. 'Well?' she asked impatiently in her native tongue of Japanese. With a sigh the young lady reaches to the pocket of her clumsily thrown aside slacks, and emerges with a single slender cigarette and a lighter. With a flick of the lighter she lights the cigarette, and inhales deeply. She closes her eyes and relaxes further back into her bed, sighing again with a softness that seemed to sooth Kimbusa’s fears. Kimbusa slowly removed his trenchcoat, the reveal his solid black silk pants and shirt. He tossed his jacket aside, and slowly inched towards the rickety old bed, where the young lady lie in wait. Her legs began to slide back and forth beneath the sheets, as she again cooed from a place far beyond this rat-infested hole they had found their way into. Her eyes were shut tight, as she was no doubt preparing to return both angel and devil to heaven where they might be saved from such a terrible world. And then it happened. It happened in what must have been an instant, but felt like an eternity. It started in his toes, and worked its way upward. It felt as if his legs had been set of fire, and the heat was too much to endure. The pain from the flames worked its way upward, until the heat reached Kimbusa’s eyes, causing them to burn red with fury. Sensing the momentary hesitation, the young lady opened her eyes... ...And saw the devil above her. Kimbusa’s eyes flared red with passion and hatred, a kind of unexplainable fury that seemed to take immediate control of his body. His grey heart had been turned red by this young lady, but it had suddenly flashed black; a single tear fell from the fiery eyes of Kimbusa and landed on the young lady’s neck, and the girl began to scream. She had no sooner began to scream than she had been cut off by a violent fist to the face; her screaming had been silenced by the impassioned fists of the five hundred pound demon. Outside, his partners turned to each other in worry, confused as to what might have caused the young girl to scream out in fright. Her voice had seemed so horrified, so absolutely terrified that nothing less than Satan himself could have created such a cry. After a moment, violent shaking noises, pounding, thumping, grinding, growling... and then nothing. Moments passed. Minutes. Minutes forever lost in Kimbusa’s memory, lost in the void of places too dreadful for his mind to return to. But they were minutes recounted to him by his partners, who remained outside, confused and worried for their comrade. After a few moments of absolute panic, the wooden door creaked open slowly, allowing no light to come through. Kimbusa emerged from the darkened room with his trench coat and fedora once again, looking as if nothing had happened. With a painful look in his eye, Kimbusa simply turned from his associates and began moving towards the exit. His associates dared not enter the room, but already knew what had happened. They could tell by the blood Kimbusa’s hands had left on the knob. Kimbusa returns to the expensive high rise apartment, and the first thing he sees is Steven Caldera, sitting is absolute shock across from him. Caldera had been listening intently the entire time. Kimbusa's eyes seem red now not with hatred but with remorse, as the inhuman qualities that had ruled him for so long seem to fade into something softer. After a few moments of silence and heaving breathing, Kimbusa turns upward to Caldera. 'Am I a monster?' Kimbusa asks, already knowing the answer. But Caldera has a different answer for him. With a slight smile on his face, but sounding entirely content, Caldera whispers back. 'No... You are a wrestler.'
