An Introduction Of Sorts, Part One

29 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 29th August, 2002 I am Cassius Deamont. I guess you’d call me a good guy, even a hero. I’m what the wrestling industry calls a baby face, the fans idol. I don’t want to be some hero, I don’t want to fight for other people’s beliefs, I do not want to be some sort of champion for the cause, and if I’m honest I’m probably not the best man for the job, but it would seem that, whether I want it or not, that is the position that has been assigned to me… * * * * * Midday, Thursday 29th August 2002. Cassius’ apartment, located above the Danse Macabre nightclub in Los Angeles. Having just woke up, showered and got dressed, clad in a black silk shirt and a pair of black jeans, Cassius Deamont stands in front of his bedroom window, basking in the suns light as he gazes out at the world. The rays of light are flattering as they reach his body, emphasising his muscular body and making his silver hair shine, emphasising its colour. “You been awake long?” a seductive female voice asks from behind Cassius. The RUSH Champion turns to find an immensely attractive brunette stood in his bedroom’s doorway. The tight dark blue sweater she wears does a good job of showing off her well-attributed chest, while the black leather skirt she has on shows off her shapely legs very well. “No,” Cassius begins his response, “Only for about 20 minutes or so… You and Erica weren’t here when I woke up.” “I know,” Kathryn replies, “You were in an almost comatose state when we woke up, so we figured we’d go shopping and come back to make you some breakfast.” “Ah… Yeah, I expect I would be pretty out of it. Last night was pretty exhausting, and I still haven’t really recovered from what Boda did to me at Crossroads,” Cassius says, brushing aside a few stray strands of hair that were beginning to cover his face. “Well, at least now you can stop beating yourself up about letting Boda catch you off guard,” she begins her response with a smile, “I mean, you got revenge for that with your big arrival in FSW.” * * * * * Ah yes… My “big arrival” in FSW… Hardly one of my best surprises for my foes, but it achieved its purpose. I was sat at ringside, that night, watching the show as it went on. I don’t doubt there were some interesting matches going on, but I wasn’t really watching, or at least not paying attention. I was just waiting. Waiting for my time to come. Waiting for my chance to gain a measure of revenge against that jackass Boda. My opportunity came at the end of the show. The big brute was in the ring, with the fallen bodies of Timo Bolamba and Mike Marvelous laying at his feet. He’d taken out all physical threats to him. He was dominant. On this night it would be he who would walk out of Glory with a smile on his face. Or so he thought. Chip O’Meara was soon out in the ring, patting his lackey on the back for dealing with the losers in the ring. It was soon to business for him though, as he began making his announcement about FSW’s Dead Or Alive Tournament. I watched as he named the first few teams, knowing full well what would happen next. It wasn’t long before CEO Paul Orion was out from the back, evidently to O’Meara’s chagrin. The two engaged in a war of words, going back and forth until the commissioner brought his ace into play. He threatened Orion with Boda, and Orion just kept talking. Chip made the signal and Boda started making his way up the ramp, cracking his knuckles like the moronic thug he is as he did so. Orion wasn’t scared – or if he was he didn’t show it – and he had no reason to be. Boda thought he was the big shot. Boda thought he was gonna beat on an old man and somehow make himself look good. Boda thought he had power. Boda and O’Meara thought they were in control. But then, Boda thought I was dead – or at least in a coma – after that brain haemorrhage he gave me. Boda was wrong on all counts. He was just starting to walk up the ramp when I got up, picked up the Steel Chair I’d sat on, and started making my way through the crowd to the ramp. He should have noticed the commotion in the crowd. Heck, I’m pretty distinctive, he should have noticed me. But he kept his eyes locked onto Orion, didn’t pay attention to anything else. I’m not sure if he saw me at the last moment as I jumped the barricade or not. Either way, it doesn’t matter. He didn’t have time to react, couldn’t defend himself. He was headed for Orion, I cut him off. A classic ambush, and he walked right into it like the big idiot he is. Maybe we could have come up with something more elaborate, but Boda doesn’t really warrant it… One chair shot took him down. Some more kept him down. Probably less than a minute of time passed, but that was enough. Enough for me to just begin to get my revenge. With Boda taken out, I guess my turn was over, so I sat back – or, rather, just stood their aimlessly – as Orion gave a speech to O’Meara I didn’t pay much attention – their managerial feud doesn’t really interest me, if I’m honest – but I think his point was basically that he was in charge of FSW, and that with me now in action, Boda wasn’t going to be an issue. Boda had thought he’d be the one smiling as the show went off air. Boda thought he’d be the one who could leave and call it a good nights work. As usual, Boda was wrong. * * * * * “Yeah… I guess I did get a measure of revenge there. But it won’t stop at that, Kathryn,” Cassius responds, a smile on his face. “You seem strangely happy about that Cassius,” Kathryn remarks, seeming confused by Cassius’ carefree manner. “Chris Sloboda does not scare me Kathryn, and right now I don’t care what it takes, I just want to hurt him. I want to hurt him a lot,” Cassius explains, speaking with passion, “That bastard put me in hospital. Maybe not for long, but I was in there all the same. Worst of all, now that waste of space Chris Matthews thinks he beat me. Matthews is claiming that he defeated me for the RUSH Title. He doesn’t seem to understand that he did nothing for that belt. He did nothing to earn the shot, he did nothing to earn the win, it was all just dumb luck on his part that Boda came out and took me out for him.” Kathryn laughs. “So the thing that pisses you off most of all is that you lost…” “To Chris Matthews!” Cassius says, seeming completely confused as to why Kathryn is laughing, “I mean, a loss I can take… But a loss to that jobber? That’s another matter altogether!” “You shouldn’t let that bother you Cassius. I hope you’re not forgetting what you told me… How it was you who walked out of Crossroads as the true winner. Even if you weren’t walking at the time,” Kathryn responds, trying to console Cassius. * * * * * OK… Here’s the deal. When you leave a Pay Per View in an ambulance, having just lost a world title match, you’re a loser… Right? Wrong. You want to know who the big losers at RUSH’s Crossroads Pay Per View were? The new champ, Chris Matthews, the guy who put me in hospital, Chris Sloboda, and the biggest loser of all… RUSH, the company that released me as soon as there were rumours that I was seriously injured. Matthews, the RUSH “Champion”. Exactly how long will he keep hold of that belt? I’m betting he loses it at his first defence (which, if I’m correct, will be later today). Why? Because the guy is a joke. Because he has no talent whatsoever. Because, put bluntly, Chris Matthews is the biggest freakin’ loser I’ve ever met. And given that I’ve met Tim Walden… To think that I was in a “team” with that fuckwit… To think that I helped him out. But then, I guess that given my track record – Matthews, Steve Brent, Tim Walden, D-Kay Calyx, Rich Cannon, Vulcan Flare… Boda – it’s fair to say that I suck at choosing allies. I really should stop letting people leech of my success… Chris Matthews. A boy amongst men. A joke. He has no credibility, because it’s glaringly obvious he won the belt on a fluke. He has no talent, because his parents gave him bad genes. And, pretty soon, he’ll have no belt, because he doesn’t have what it takes to hold onto it. He thinks he’s beat me. He doesn’t realise that after our “feud”, which basically involved me kicking his ass every single week while he attempted to get his meek offence in, he’s nothing but a laughing stock. In RUSH, and everywhere else. Because he needed Boda to beat me. Heck, the fact that I burst Matthews open first and Boda knocked out the ref before he could see it is testament to that… Besides, I got Kathryn back, and while I hate to sound like a sentimental old fool, she means more to me than the RUSH World Title ever did. Ok, next up is RUSH. As in, the whole company. Rush Mason is their owner. Chris Matthews is their Champion. They have lost me. They have lost Boda. They have lost others too, but sadly not people I can take credit for getting rid of. They have lost all credibility, and they have lost all their fans. Who alerted Mason to the fact that he could take control of RUSH? Me. What led to Boda’s leaving RUSH? His loss of the ownership. What led to Matthews becoming Champ? Boda taking out his anger at losing the ownership on me. What led to me leaving? Me having the chance to fake an injury to get my release. Sure, the whole Boda beating me up thing wasn’t part of my original plan – which would still involve me somehow giving Matthews the belt and leaving – but, while it was far more painful, this method made things much easier for me. I guess you could say this is a prime example of me using my philosophy… I can do anything I set my mind to, by myself. If other people can be used to achieve my goals though, I’ll have no objections to doing just that… Using them. The biggest loser of them all though? Chris Sloboda. But his story is long, and I grow weary of this, so perhaps that is a story best saved for another time…
