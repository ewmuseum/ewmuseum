GTT · Pain

5 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: whoo~! My dearest Crimson Angel, the letter opens affectionately, flirting with a name it couldn't possibly grasp the significance of. The note was found tucked underneath one of Deville's windshield wipers, by the man himself, as he approached his car to leave his figurative in-ring murdering of the mundane communist Digital Mortality. Assuming it was a parking ticket of some small consequence, he didn't even pay it half a mind until he arrived at his decoy home. By now he has already read through it twice, but the pure absurdity of it forces him to do so again. Scribed on regular white paper, the pen strokes lie thick and heavy upon the page, as if written by someone for whom rage is a false friend. It is with regret that I inform The impending tumult of a storm; Through actions wry and wrought of shame You've 'voked the fear that is my name. Proof do I have of your tourney misdeeds Money I seek or this further proceeds Unto the ears of errant electives With will enough to wilt your objectives. Angelo's eyes blur as he scans over the remaining four verses, his mind caught between outrage and purest amusement. The act of someone extorting him is comparable to placing a police officer under citizen's arrest, and that in and of itself concerns him none at all. It is the principle behind it that irks him; the idea that someone seeks to strongarm and defeat him through underhanded means. The rest of the poem outlines the presentation of proof to GTT officials, followed by instructions on where to drop the payment, and finally ends with: Succumb to my will and your chances spared duly; Err just a hair and to the tourney prove my duty. What the hell is this guy on? Deville's mind demands incredulously. Who does he think he's fucking with? He leaves both questions unanswered as he considers how to deal with it. There is certainly a case to be made showing that he hasn't been rigging the tournament, but at the same time... An industrious individual could easily connect the dots. As he lets the letter float down to the desk he knows one thing for sure: No one's knocking me out of this tournament. He wishes he had the time to go to the drop-off place and wait around for his blackmailer, but between the cops, FSW, and the tourney itself, it simply isn't feasible. A shame, really... Waiting for him to slip up and pouncing would be easiest and most satisfying... But I've got a schedule to keep... I suppose there's only one thing to do. Deciding it would be best to place an envelope where the lyricism directed, he sighs and gives a rueful shake of the head. A tiny smile crosses his angular face as he marvels at his own uncharacteristic actions. His accuser would find the envelope in the designated place at the designated time, but its contents would be unexpected... Instead of meeting his demand for money he'd respond in kind. He chuckles as he grabs a pen and begins writing on a piece of scrap paper, growing more pleased and amused with the situation with each passing second. My fondest Frankie, the heartless thug, Who seeks to be my ruin; Think for a moment on what you've done, And an elegy for your tomb. In pushing me you've condemned yourself To a week of relearning pain; The best way you can do your duty Is in front of a speeding train. ~><>...<><~ Don Salvatore reclines in his leather laz-y-boy and mercifully allows his tired eyes to close for the first time in over twenty-four hours. His mind is only half-focussed on the rambling report being given him by his second, Marco Giuliani, the other half dwelling on the subject of the report itself: Angelo Deville. To say he's at his wits end with the man would be alike to calling a deluge a mid-summer sprinkle. 'Not only is the FBI covering up the murders of the two policemen and the inexplicable pair of Russians, but they're still not issuing a warrant for Deville's arrest.' Marco's voice intrudes momentarily, and Salvatore feels the first tell-tale signs of a headache creeping into his brain. 'They still think they can handle it, and I quote, ?in our fashion'.' The man snorts to show what he thinks of that, then silence consumes the room as he awaits a response. Salvatore snaps his eyes open and regards the man coldly. 'What would you have me do?' He demands, arms outstretched in an exaggerated gesture of helplessness. 'I'm not intervening on his behalf, and I'm certainly not going to go against him.' He pauses to consider this for a moment, then adds 'yet' in a resigned tone. 'I really don't think we could be too hasty when it comes to this man,' Marco warns and is about to continue when Salvatore cuts him off with an agitated shake of his head. 'Enough for now... I need to think about it.' Marco opens his mouth to protest, but thinks better of it and turns his attention instead to the grand bay windows and picturesque landscape beyond. Instead of thinking on the subject, Salvatore again closes his eyes and lets the worries seep out of him as sleep begins to overtake him. Perhaps things would be easier if... Just one little bullet... No more worries, his groggy mind pacifies in a soothing voice. A forceful and abrupt one follows it up almost immediately, and no more of is money or muscle. This last staves off sleep for a few moments, as he weighs the pros and cons of doing away with his best earner. 'Y'know,' Marco interrupts his mind with a thoughtful voice. Salvatore sits bolt upright, ready to tear the man a new one for defying him. 'I could do this on my own... You wouldn't have to take any responsibility.' He lets the suggestion trail off meaningfully, hopefully. 'Deville would string you up faster than some bottom-feeding thug,' Salvatore snaps. 'And with half the remorse. Now let's have some quiet so that I can-' The sentence is interrupted by a loud smashing open of the double doors, causing both men to start and turn wide eyes in that direction. Angelo Deville storms into the room and right up to the front of Salvatore's desk, ignoring the enraged look on the Don's face. He casts a dismissive glance at Marco, then arches an eyebrow at his boss. 'Excuse the interruption, Don Salvatore,' he growls in a falsely mild tone and inclines his head slightly. 'But we need to talk.' Salvatore only stares at him for what seems like an eternity to every man in the room, then the anger washes out of his face and he nods his head slowly. 'Yes,' he sighs, 'yes we do.' 'I presume you've heard about The Law's attempts to put me out of commission...?' Deville asks, crossing his arms over his chest. 'I may have heard something to that effect,' the Don allows, meeting Deville's cold glare with one of his own. 'Well,' Deville begins slowly, darting his eyes left and right before continuing. 'When were you planning to intercede?' Marco fidgets and moves away from the window, inching his way closer to the two men. Neither takes any notice. 'I don't plan on getting involved this time, Angelo,' Salvatore states with feigned regret. 'This is something you're going to have to handle on your own.' 'That's not good at all,' Deville snarls, shaking his head. 'I've already got too many things on my plate without having to deal with a bunch of bloodthirsty pigs. Besides... Taking care of things like this is your part of our bargain. I'm supposed to be operating with impunity.' 'Only when working for us!' Marco says in an almost-shout, stopping just behind Salvatore. 'We can't be responsible when you go gallivanting around killing police officers! You must be outta-' 'I don't recall asking you, Marco, nor entering into business with you,' Deville interrupts him in a tone fit to wilt flowers. 'So why don't you shut your fuckin' mouth, before you and I do have something to discuss.' The threat hangs heavy on the air and all three men are silent for the better part of a minute, Marco looking sheepish, Deville glaring death at him, and Salvatore regarding his desk. 'You'll have to forgive Marco, Angelo,' the Don says, burning the man with a scorching look. 'He hasn't learned when to keep his mouth shut, as you so eloquently pointed out. However, that isn't to say his words themselves are foolish; he raises a good point. You receive my protection while working for me, not any time otherwise.' 'With all due respect, Don Salvatore,' Angelo spits, mockery heavy on the title now, 'this isn't a regular job. I don't punch out at the end of the day and get on with my real life... This is my life. I'm living with what I do twenty-four hours per day. There's no escaping it.' He pauses for a moment and glares at Marco, daring him to open his mouth again. 'If you're not going to use your puppets on my behalf, what the fuck do I pay you for? This isn't the old country... I'm not doing it as tribute.' Salvatore's eyes widen in shock and Marco actually gasps. 'You're out of line, Deville,' Salvatore warns, pointing an admonishing finger at him. The anger has crept back into his face and voice, and he now has the bearing of a wild animal set to defend its den. 'You've been flouting my authority since day one, and now that you've gotten ahead of yourself you come looking to me for help. I tell you now, for the final time, that it's not coming. If you straighten this out, good... If not, well, to be honest it really doesn't ruffle my feathers too much. Either way, you're doing it without my backing.' 'I see,' Deville whispers through gritted teeth, then raises his voice as he continues. 'Well then, Salvatore,' he pauses to emphasise the lack of the title, 'perhaps I would be best to handle this on my own... Perhaps I should begin handling everything on my own.' Marco looks ready to choke to death at this last, but Salvatore, not one to be bullied, simply angles his chair so he's no longer looking at Deville. 'Do what you must, Angelo,' is all he says, and his voice is thick with the resignation of a weary parent. Deville only nods, choking back a million things he wants to say. His mind is a tempest of thoughts and curses, none of them materialising long enough to have resonance. He only knows that his mouth is best kept shut for the time being. Bide your time, he lectures himself. Let the fool think the battle is over and wait for the opportunity. 'Don Salvatore,' he says in a farewell that could be mistaken for an apology, inclining his head before retreating from the room. Don Salvatore grins with satisfaction, but not completely. He knows all too well the mind of Deville to dismiss him so easily. 'We must kill him,' Marco growls, glaring at the empty doorway as if Deville still stood there flouting him. 'Now. Before it is too late.' 'No,' the Don says softly, reclining in his chair once more. Marco opens his mouth to argue but Salvatore just rolls over him. 'He will die, but not by our hand. Your spies neglected to tell you, but mine have told me that the FBI is upping the ante.' He pauses for a moment and an involuntary smile engrosses his face. 'Deville will be dead before week's end.' ~><>...<><~ 'A gangsta?' Agent Halloway shouts, the exasperation in his voice peaking on the second word. 'We're sending a two-bit thug after Angelo Deville?' His grey eyes are practically bulging out of his chubby face, and his mop of brown hair bounces in reverberation of his verbal protest. He is dressed in a classless three-piece suit, his hands jammed into the pant-pockets as if he is wearing a pair of jeans. 'Calm yourself,' his supervisor, Agent Maxwell soothes with a chuckle. 'It's not like he spends his time roughing up druggies and painting walls. This man is an assassin. One of the most dangerous in the world... He's yet to fail.' He looks almost identical to Halloway, except his eyes are a deep green instead of grey and he's about twenty pounds heavier. His reassuring words fail to make an impact on the younger man. 'I don't care if he's Suge goddamn Knight, we can't send an untrained thug after a cultured animal like Deville... He'll eat him alive. I've been studying this guy ever since he got involved with Salvatore's crew, and trust me when I tell you we need more... Alot more.' The older man is already shaking his head, so Halloway throws up his hands in frustration and kicks the small trash can beside the filing cabinet. 'You underestimate our black friend, Dave,' Maxwell grins. 'You trust me when I tell you he's more than capable of handling any petty mobster. Deville doesn't stand a chance.' 'What's his name?' Dave Halloway asks doubtfully, making no attempt to hide his dismay. 'No one knows...' The other agent responds with a wolfish grin. 'They call him Pain.'
