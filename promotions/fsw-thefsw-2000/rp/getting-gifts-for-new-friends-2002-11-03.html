Getting Gifts for New Friends

3 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/3/02 The Bill Conklestein Mercedes Dealership sits apart from the other car lots on automobile row, with its meticulously manicured lawn and carefully landscaped grounds as a layout for the high-priced luxury cars. The majestic vehicles, long a symbol of wealth and status, shine brightly under the Texas sunshine. The metallic finishes glisten under the rays of light, sparkling with every motion of the camera. The vehicles are parked on the lot no less then ten feet from each other, giving them plenty of space for each to display its wares. The models range from the conservative SLK-Class to the classy-yet-sporty SL-Class Roadster convertibles to the SUV M and G-Classes. Colors range from the classic Metallic Silver to the exotic Magma Red. The dealership building is of a progressive and futuristic architecture. The steel and glass structure fits its surroundings perfectly, nestled right in the center of the plot. Inside the showroom are the crown jewels of the inventory, many of the cars equipped with extra’s to accentuate their looks and make them more appealing to potential buyers. Salesmen sit at their desks, some of them working the phones and yet others working customers. It’s fairly busy at this time of day, right around the time people are getting off of work. From the street and onto the lot pulls a long, black stretch limo. It’s freshly polished finish reflects the sunlight as the shiny, spoke wheels slowly navigate the drive up to the building. The limo comes to a halt and the back door pops open. Onto the pavement steps a pair of Bruno Magli shoes which hold the feet of the black Armani suit wearing, Six Star Wrestler, Tyler Nelson. He looks around contently and then shuts the door behind him. The Greediest Player in the Game strides confidently up to the showroom and enters the building. Several of the salespeople look up from their customers or phone conversations and immediately scamper to their feet. It’s a dead sprint to be the first one to greet Tyler. One salesman, a rather pudgy one with thick glasses, falls behind but strengthens his chances by diving and tripping two of the other salesmen. Those two fall forward and take out three other salesmen with them. The two men in the lead look back and then smirk to each other. Suddenly the one on the right swings his leg out and trips the other man, sending him tumbling to the floor. The lone standing salesman continues his sprint to the finish line seemingly unabated. As the dollar signs ring up in his eyes he fails to notice the desk coming up in front of him. He turns his head a split second too late and crashes into the desk. He flies through the air and does a face plant into the floor. Tyler simply shakes his head in dismay as he looks out upon the mass of men writhing on the floor in varying degrees of pain. The customers that were being helped by the salesmen angrily storm out of the showroom, flashing dirty looks at Tyler on their way out. Tyler just smirks and shrugs his shoulders. Suddenly a voice comes over the intercom. “Mr. Conklestein to the showroom floor.” The fallen salesmen moan as they struggle to regain their feet. As they do a short, skinny man with balding hair and tiny wire-rimmed glasses comes walking down from a set of stairs at the far side of the showroom. He stops momentarily to survey the carnage and sighs, then continues on as he sees the source of the commotion. He zigs and zags around the bodies and finally reaches his destination. He extends his hand promptly. “Hello there, Mr. Nelson, what can I do for you today?” asks Mr. Conklestein in a nervously hesitant voice. Beads of sweat are already forming on his forehead as he forces a smile to his face. “We just got a new shipment in yesterday.” Nelson just looks down on the man, who is dressed in a plain white button-up dress shirt, tie, and black slacks. “You think I don’t know that already?” asks Tyler rhetorically. “Why do you think I’m here today?” The manager looks defeated and lowers his head, glancing at the ground. Tyler chuckles to himself and slaps Conklestein on his shoulder. “C’mon, Artie,” says Tyler, “you think I don’t know when my cars come in? Your name my be on the dealership signs, but don’t forget who’s place this really is.” “I know, I know,” says Artie apologetically, “it’s just I…..” “You’re too honest, Artie,” Tyler interrupts and finishes Artie’s sentence. “That’s why I have you working down here. I need someone honest to look after the books down here and make sure that things get done.” Artie forces a smile across his face. “Thanks for the vote of confidence, Mr. Nelson. I do my best.” “Of course you do,” agrees Tyler, “because if you don’t you know what will happen.” Artie gulps hard and rubs a hand across his jaw, wincing as if he is remembering something painful. “Speaking of Mr. Grant,” Tyler says, “he really liked the black G500 out there. I’ll need one of those in addition to four SL55 AMG Roadsters.” Artie’s mouth drops open, nearly reaching the floor. “F-F-Five vehicles?” stammers Artie. Tyler leans forward, a look of dead seriousness on his face. “Is there some sort of problem with that?” “N-No,” Artie says as he regains his composure. “It’s just that, one car is really difficult to hide on the books. Now you want five?” “I have a few new friends that I would like to get gifts for,” explains Tyler. He then hands Artie a piece of paper, folded up neatly. “I expect delivery to these addresses within the next 48 hours. The choice of colors is yours, as long as you choose black.” Artie nods slowly and tucks the sheet of paper in his pocket. Tyler nods toward the other salesmen, who have all returned to their desks and nurse various minor injuries. “What’s the deal with those guys?” “They all think that you are in here buying these cars every two weeks,” Artie says with a sigh. Tyler wraps his arm around Artie’s shoulders as he heads toward the door. “Cheer up, Artie. There’ll be a nice bonus in this for you. Just make sure that things continue to run well down here and I won’t have to do anything bad to you and your family, got that?” A silent nod serves as Artie’s response. Tyler pats him on the back as he pulls the glass door open. “Atta boy. See you in a few weeks.” Tyler smiles insincerely as he heads back to the limousine waiting outside. He piles into the back and the limo pulls away from the dealership. Artie watches the limo leave, a small tear beginning to well up in his eye. He takes a handkerchief out of his pocket and wipes his eyes before heading back inside the building.
