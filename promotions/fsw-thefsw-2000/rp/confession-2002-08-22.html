Confession

22 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 22, 2002 'I can't believe I actually lost her...' I had been sitting in the Church tiers that afternoon, awaiting myself to build the emotional courage to enter the booth and preach to the priest who so graciously dedicated his time to hearing the problems in which cursed the citizens lives. I had been waiting for hours at this point. Partly because there were a few others who had went before me. And then there were those whom I had let go before me because I had not yet built up my emotional power enough to enter. Staring at Jesus, pinned to the cross, I had come to realize how much of a sacrifice this one man had made for his beliefs. How he had went to extreme lengths in order to satisfy his loved ones and defend their honor. I wished to be Jesus. I craved the feeling to die for the ones I love... ...not have the one I love die for me. 'I just wish to let you know...' a man had approached me, wishing to relay some sort of advice. 'The booth will probably be closing soon if nobody else enters. I've seen you here all day, waiting. Perhaps you better get in there soon.' I nodded my head in compliment and gratitude. 'Thanks,' I mumbled underneath my breathe. The man gave me back a little smile and nodded his head in return. He turned and limped his way out of the church doors. He seemed to have suffered from some major injury to his leg in prior years that has never come to heal. Yet even through his misery, he can still be half decent and look out for a complete and utter stranger. Why is that? Those who suffer the most...comfort the best!?! And then those who are comforted the best make others suffer the most. The human race tends to confuse me at times. I don't quite understand how they work. Anywho, I peered down upon my wrist, checking the time on my old scraungy watch and noticed that the time was nearing. I had to pull everything from within me to get my body to stand from the tier. Slowly, I paced my way to the confession booth, still having doubts as to whether or not I should enter and confess my life to the father. I grasped the handle of the door tightly, yet didn't pull it immediately. For a second, the doubt in my mind grow large and promising. Never the less, I twisted the handle and slowly pulled the door open. A huge relief for myself. I stepped into the booth, as the door closed behind me. The room darkened at the shutting of the door and I was left alone...with a small bench. Naturally, I sat. It wasn't long before the father spoke to me. 'Good afternoon,' he greeted me. I was silent for a couple moments, contemplating whether or not I should just get up and walk out. Finally, I replied, 'Hello father. I've come to confess my sins.' Duh. Why else would I be here? I didn't really think about what I was saying before I said it. I definitely felt like a complete and utter imbosil. 'Speak to me and I am sure that your sins can be forgiven by God.' He made it all sound so easy. But would God actually forgive me for what I've done. I was here now, so there was no turning back. I spoke forth my undying secrets. 'Father, I've killed someone.' There was a moment of uncomfortable silence. 'Not how you're thinking, though.' I had to clarify the situation. I'm not that scorn of a person. 'I feel as though I am responsible for the death of my now deceased girlfriend, Rebecca.' 'Why?' Asked the father, taking the situation a whole lot lighter than I had expected. 'I couldn't save her, father. I failed her and in turn, her life was brought to an unhappy closing.' The father tried to reason with me, to remove the guilt. I could tell he was just trying to ease my pain. 'Suicide is not your fault. Rebecca was not living to her fullest extent here on earth, thus God thought perhaps things would be easier for her to join him in heaven. God chose the path for Rebecca. Just know now that she is in a better place for her sake.' I found this utterly ridiculous. 'A better place,' I questioned sharply. 'You're telling me that no longer being able to see her family or friends...or be with her soulmate is a better place? You're on crack.' He replied calmly, to my dismay, 'She is constantly watching over her family and friends. She protects them. And she's always with you, in your heart and in spirit.' 'NO!' I snapped at the priest. 'HOW CAN SHE WATCH OVER HER FAMILY WHEN THEY'RE ALL DEAD!' I stopped for a second as the tears began rolling down my face. 'WHERE WAS GOD WHEN HER FAMILY WAS BRUTALLY MURDERED? WHY WASN'T HER FAMILY WATCHING OVER HER?' I continued on a rampage, yelling at the top of my lungs. I stood from my bench. 'WHY DIDN'T THEY HELP MAKE HER LIFE RIGHT?' I leaned myself against the wall and started to break down emotionally. The tears streamed down my face as my arms flung wide open along my sides. 'Why...did he...do this...TO HER?!?' I couldn't last in there any longer. I had to make a getaway. But not without getting blessed for one last sin. 'Father...' 'Yes,' he replied, 'continue.' A smile came upon my face, from underneath the tears. A sadistic smile, at that. 'I have one other confession I need forgiven,' I said softly. 'What may that be,' he asked. A slight chuckle rolled out from my body. 'Forgive me now for I will sin. As I will soon pull all the hate that lies within. Let it be known and let it be sworn. Davidson and Magnum Wolf will SOON BE TORN!' I shoved open the door and stepped out, with a new feeling of life. I spent little time thereafter in the church as I had a new mission to complete. And that mission... TO END TYLER DAVIDSON FOR GOOD!
