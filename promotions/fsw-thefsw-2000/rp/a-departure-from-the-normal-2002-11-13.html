A departure from the Normal

13 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/13/02 Ahh, the open road. In our lifestyle, it's something we look forward to, and regret at the same time. Jesse Ramey and I became good friends when we both started in FSW. Both of us had quite a fondness for playing spades, as well as vintage cars. Hours would go by as Ramey would tell me about how much he wanted a '57 chevy with a 350 under the hood, and some really cherry paint. Many times, we shared a rental car to and from the arenas, and even if the next venue was within driving distance, we would split time. Soon after my return, Violence Jack would accompany us as well. He's a great guy, but man, he talks. Alot. And not just about one thing. He talks about everything, and anything. Road signs, cars, women, world news, his hatred of pop music despite knowing everything about it, you name it and this guy knew about it. He's a walking encyclopedia of worthless knowledge. Still we had fun together, and just a few days ago, had a trip I will never forget. Leaving Atlanta, the site of the last Glory, Jesse stopped at the airport to get our Lincoln Navigator. The guy's been driving since he could walk, and I swear, theres been times he's been asleep at the wheel, and driven better than most of the other guys around. Especially Kimbusa. I swear FSW will never let him rent another car again, after getting into a one car accident, without even putting the car in gear. So, Jesse pulls up in the Navigator. I load my bags into the back, and grab VJ's, and do the same. VJ climbs into the back, and lays down on the seat. A few fans are staggered around, and Jesse and I sign some autographs, talk a little, and take a few pictures together, with the fans. About 20 minutes later, we leave, headed for Miami. I took a look around Atlanta, and decided that if Statuz Quo wanted to represent Atlanta, he could have it. It's a trash heap. 'Hey, Jason?' 'Yeah, Jesse?' 'Yall wants to stop and gets somethin' to eat on the way down? I got a hankerin for a new 24 hour burger joint i done heard about just inside Orlando.' 'Sure. A burger sounds good right now. Maybe some bacon and peanut butter...' 'Bacon and Peanut Butter? Are you sick, Jason?' VJ said from the back. 'Dude, you eat that, and you're not going to be able to breathe, much less wrestle.' 'We'll hit the gym when I get down there. No biggie. Good carbs.' 'Whatever, man. They better have some veggie burgers. That's all i gotta say. I mean do you know what kind of stuff goes into that processed meat?' 'Jacko, i growed up smack dab in the middle of West Virginny. I doubt anything yall wanna tell me is worse than skunk, or squirrel.' '...fine.' Jesse got on the highway, and I turned on the radio. The DJ, at this particular station had a little thing he liked to do at 12 midnight, and played 'Tiny Dancer' by Elton John, the song made famous from the bus scene in 'Almost Famous'. Embarassed I actually knew them, I mouthed the words, while looking out the window, watching the lights go by and Atlanta grow smaller and smaller. 'Ballerina, you must have seen her dancing in the sand / And now she's in me, always with me, tiny dancer in my hand' I sang the words, silently in my head, when I heard Jesse, just barely singing. I looked over at him, and he looked at me. He looked away quickly, embarassed as well. I looked out the window again. Suddenly, from the backseat we heard it. it started quietly, then grew in intensity. 'Hold me closer, Tiny Dancer...Count the headlights on the highway.' I looked back to see VJ singing his heart out. I started to chuckle, as did Jesse. Soon, we were all singing... Then, the song went off the air, and just like that, we stopped, sighed, and kept on thinking about whatever it was we were thinking about before that. 'Man, I hate that fucking song' came from the backseat. Jesse and I looked at VJ and laughed. 'What? What's so funny? What?' Good Ol' Violence Jack
