Life, Death, and Whatever Comes After

5 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/05/02 Beep…..Beep…..Beep…..Beep…... The eerie, rhythmic sound of a heart monitor brings us in from darkness, to what is obviously a hospital room. There is a myriad of technical equipment, monitors, instruments, and other medical devices scattered throughout the room creating an uneasy atmosphere. The beeping continues as we gaze upon the bed that is positioned on the far wall inside the room, a body connected to what seems like hundreds of tubes and IV lines lying motionless on it. The identity of the lifeless form cannot be determined due to the thick layers of gauze surrounding the head and the respirator tube that is taped tightly to the mouth, pumping the life giving oxygen into the body’s lungs. Beep…..Beep…..Beep…..Beep….. The monotonous beeping continues, seemingly in concert with the pumping of the respirator. The drip from the IV bags joins the morbid concert, creating a deathly music that is familiar to hospitals. As we continue to focus on the bed, wondering what the identity of the person in such a condition could be, we remember what happened on the last edition of Glory. We remember the sick and heinous attack perpetrated on the person of one Tyler Nelson by the Monster They Call Meanstreak. We visualize the former CEO of Primetime Championship Wrestling being driven head first through the roof of a Cadillac Escalade. We remember the coldhearted and seemingly nonchalant way Meanstreak acted as he calmly walked away, leaving Tyler battered and near death. Then we gaze again upon the bed where the form still lies motionless. It all comes together. Beep…..Beep…..Beep…..Beeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeep…….. The morbid concert is interrupted as the heart monitor breaks rhythm with the other devices, a constant beep emitting from it like siren to the dead. A flat line scrolls across the monitors screen, signifying that the person in the bed is losing the fight for life. In a flash the door to the room bursts open and a whirlwind of activity takes over the stillness of the room. Doctors and nurses spring into action, checking vital signs of the patient. The shouts of the medical personnel all jumble together, echoing in the room and melting together all at once. They frantically try to save the life that lies in front of them fleeting from existence. Beeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeep….. The solid beep continues, in unison with the flat line that continues it’s dance across the heart monitor’s screen. CPR is administered on the patient as a defibrillator is brought into the room. They charge the paddles and shock the chest of the patient. Nothing. They charge and shock again. Still nothing. The doctors continue compressions to the patient’s chest as a nurse administers a shot of adrenaline. The feverish pace at which the doctors work is all for not, as the heart does not react to the treatment. Disappointed, the doctors call off the revival attempts and pronounce the death of the patient. They hang their heads as they leave the room, some even letting a tear escape their eye. A nurse walks over to the heart monitor, which continues to signal the unbeating heart. Beeeeeeeeeeeeee She turns it off and silence fills the room. She walks out, leaving the room empty save for the dead body lying on the bed. Some of the tubes and IV’s are still attached to it, like some sort of medical experiment gone awry. Suddenly the quality of the picture changes somewhat. “Well that movie sucked,” blurts out a cocky and disgusted voice from somewhere. “How tha fuck can they show some shit like dat in a muthafuckin hospital?” asks another. “That’s depressin’ an shit.” The drama we had just witnessed starts to see credits scroll up from the bottom of the screen. We pan back and see that we are focused on a television set that is suspended from the ceiling of a different hospital room. The camera now pans around to the right, letting us see our current location. This hospital room is considerably more pleasant than the one depicted on the television. Earth tones serve as the color scheme and backdrop for dozens of bouquets of flowers setting on either side of two non-hospital-standard beds. On one bed lies the aforementioned Tyler Nelson. His head is lightly bandaged and a few bruises and cuts are apparent on his forehead and face. The money green colored silk pajamas he wears, embroidered with a gold “TN” on the right breast pocket, almost glisten in the rooms lighting. He stares up at the television with a befuddled look on his face. “How long until they let me out of this hell-hole? If I have to keep watching crap like this they might end up having to admit me to the psych ward,” quips Tyler. “And there is no way in hell that a Six Star Athlete like myself should be reduced to the level of that asshole Ethan. He’s the one who belongs back in the loony bin, attacking me like that. Now I know that he’s lost his damn mind.” On the other bed rests one of Tyler’s bodyguards, Orpheus Grant. ‘Tha OG’ is decked out in a white wifebeater and black Nike basketball shorts. He also has several injuries dressed and treated, nothing that looks to be life threatening though. “If I see that muthafucka again anytime soon, I’ma get buck wild on his freakish ass ya heard?” mutters Orpheus with conviction in his voice. “Easy my friend,” says Tyler soothingly. “This needs to be done in a calculated manner so as to make sure that once it is indeed done, Ethan Giroux will never have to be dealt with again. I want efficiency and guaranteed victory, and I will accept nothing else. I am on a mission in Five Star Wrestling, and getting Meanstreak out of the way is one of the prime directives. Once he is out of the way, FSW will be ripe for the pickings.” A devious and sneaky grin slips across Tyler’s lips as he raises his arms up, clasping his hands behind his head. He leans back and closes his eyes, visions of a defeated Meanstreak dancing in his head.
