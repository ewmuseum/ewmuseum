Deceptive Hearing

11 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: day after yesterday The wheels of the Cadillac Deville DTS squeal with delight as the car whips into the parking lot at breakneck speed. While the car's navy blue exterior is devoid of rust, it has picked up a lot of dust and made victims of many bugs since being picked up from Big Sal's Car Rental almost an hour ago. The large, suit-adorned arm hanging out the window bounces rhythmically to the sounds of the Stones, which are playing loud enough to corrupt the air for a good block in all directions. Squealing again, the rapidly wearing tires yank the Caddy into an empty parking space and the car jerks to a stop. A moment later the music and engine both die abruptly, and the driver's side door swings soundlessly open. One black Italian loafer emerges from the darkness of the car, followed soon after by the rest of an ecstatic looking Angelo Deville. Nonchalantly puffing on a cigar, Deville closes the door behind him and surveys the parking lot, squinting slightly under the harsh light of the noon sun. He finally spots the small ticket dispenser and blows out a thick cloud of triumphant smoke before striding over to it and reading the directions. 'Five bucks for half an hour?' He demands of the machine incredulously. It ignores his protest, of course, as it has heard the same thing from almost every other person who is forced to feed it's insatiable appetite. Deville glares at the machine a moment longer, as if hoping to intimidate the words written there into changing. Finally, with a sigh, he pulls a wad of bills out of his pocket and manages to find a twenty. Small price to pay to put an end to this bullshit, Deville thinks to himself, giving the machine the silent treatment as he submits to it's demand. He takes the little ticket it produces and puts it on the dashboard of the rental car, then checks his watch. Twenty minutes early, he thinks with satisfaction. Good. With all these idiotic drivers in Boston we coulda been held up in traffic for hours. Content, Deville leans back against the hood of the car and takes another set of hauls from his Cuban. He shakes his head with disgust as he regards the building that brought him here on such a nice day... The building that would someday seek to end his livelihood. The State Court of Massachussettes, Boston Division, towers majestically over the neighbourhood, arrogant with it's power to dictate and control. Angelo regards the grey brick with mounting revulsion, seeing it's dusty colour as a symbol for the aging and dying it condemns people to undergo within it's other institutions. His jaw clenches involuntarily. Hey, relax! The brain pleads with the body. We're here for a civil suit, damnit, and we've got the thing all sewn up. The body's resolve finally crumbles and a smile breaks across his angular face. He loves how he always manages to get out of these little predicaments. They are like little tests, challenging him to see if he has what it takes to live the way he wants. Yeah, that's right. It's all taken care of. He begins puffing on the cigar again. That little bastard James is in a coma; Thomas Brock committed 'suicide'; and just this morning she took care of the boy's mother. Angelo drifts off for a moment, thinking about her, and a stupid gleam enters his eyes. Knock it off! What are you doing? The mind demands, attributing this weakness to the body alone, even while knowing otherwise. Falling for her? Stick to the task at hand, damnit, or you'll overlook something! He gives his head a shake and the smile washes away, replaced by a look of deadly seriousness. Point is... There's no one left to justify this claim. The courts will be forced to rule in my favour. With that pleasant thought dancing in his mind, Angelo straightens up and begins swaggering towards the building with a bounce in his step. A group of young teenagers with pants they must've stolen from a circus and countless face piercings regard him with a strange awe as he passes. A few of them hold skateboards in their hands, and Angelo notes with approval that one even has a baseball bat tucked into a pant leg. 'Hey dude,' one of the kids acknowledges him with a newly acquired deep voice, 'killer fuckin' ride!' He flashes a smile and the rest of the kids all nod, some of them with a mischievous look in their eyes. Angelo doesn't like that, so he stops and regards the kids solemnly. 'It's not the type of car you drive that matters, kids, it's the size of the arm hanging out of the window.' Deville informs them, paraphrasing a great man. Then he goes on with a little improv as he produces a gun from his breast pocket and points it at them. 'And once I'm gone, you remember this arm when thoughts of vandalism enter your fucking heads.' Half a dozen sets of eyes all bulge simultaneously, and some of the kids' mouths drop open to reveal yellow teeth and pierced tongues. None of them can find words to respond, so Deville shows them his own pearly whites in what could be conceived as a smile, but never would be. Much to Deville's satisfaction, they shrink back a little and one of them even whimpers. Figuring that should hold them, he turns on his heel and continues on his way to the court, replacing the pistol and puffing merrily on the cigar. People mill about in every direction as Deville approaches the front doors; lawyers, criminals, and petty shoplifters. A tattered looking bum sits beside the doors and holds a cup out to Angelo in a plea for help. Obliging the man with a smirk, Deville flicks his finished cigar into the cup and struts through the doors, ignoring the feeble curses that follow him inside. Everything is going right today, ol' boy, he tells himself merrily as he enters the building. Angelo immediately finds himself inside a large hall, at least twenty feet wide, with distraught looking people lining benches on one side and an information counter on the other. Paying no mind to either, Deville goes down the hall and finds a sign indicating mediation is down the hall. He follows the helpful little white arrows until he finds a door with the number three on it, then opens it up and steps inside. This preliminary hearing has been arranged to try and save time for the courts, where the two involved parties meet before a mediator and outline their case. The mediator will tell them how he thinks a judge will see the case, and recommends a settlement. If either party disagrees with the settlement, and the judge does indeed agree with the mediator, it means hell for the uncooperative party. But that doesn't matter, Deville thinks smugly, because when no one shows up on behalf of James, the courts will have no choice but to throw this shit out. He closes the door behind him and straightens his suit, then turns around. 'Son of a bitch!' The growl escapes his lips before he can stop himself. The room is small and bare, except for a small wooden desk and a collection of chairs. Behind the desk is a large chair with an older, wise-looking gentleman in a suit sitting in it. On this side of the desk are uncomfortable wooden chairs, and in one of them sits a bald man in a black suit. A bald man with a dividing line down the middle of his head, where on one side of the line the skin is peach-coloured and the other is silver. Jason O'Neil starts from his position and turns an appraising eye on Angelo Deville. 'What the fuck are you doing here, freak?' Angelo asks, having lost his arrogant cool of a moment ago. His face is contorted into a mask of rage, and his empty hands have clenched into fists. 'Excuse me, sir!' The man behind the desk exclaims in a soft but serious voice, standing from his chair. 'Mr. Deville, I presume? Yes, I thought so. I recommend you enforce a bit of civility on that tongue of yours. Mr. O'Neil is counsel for the plaintiff.' 'What?' Angelo asks from a far-away place. No! Fuck this! This can't be happening! A cycle of such thoughts spins through his head and he has to forcibly restrain himself from reaching into his breast pocket. Who the fuck does O'Neil think he's fucking with? On cue, O'Neil stands up and extends his right hand to Deville with a smile. 'Mr. Deville, it's a pleasure,' he states in a bold voice and flashes Angelo a wink. Deville can only stare. Finally, he seems to get a little bit of control over his rage and takes a step further into the room, now ignoring O'Neil as he moves up beside him. 'Your honour,' he begins in a strained voice, 'I must insist that we postpone this hearing a few days.' Deville almost wins the fight to keep the desperation out of his voice. The mediator simply snorts. 'For what possible reason, Mr. Deville?' The mediator queries in an already strained voice. Because no one was supposed to be here you old bastard, Deville thinks to himself, but his words are much more diplomatic. 'I've had absolutely no time to prepare for this Mr. O'Neil. No one informed me he was representing the plaintiff. I'm not ready for this; I didn't even bring my briefcase.' Deville holds out his empty hands to emphasise their emptiness. 'That's preposterous, Mr. Deville. Regardless of whom counsel for the plaintiff is your defence should stay the same. This is mediation, not the trial. We're just going to go over the events leading to the suit and see if we can come up with a resolution here today.' The mediator explains all of this in a calm patient way, as if speaking with a child. Then his bushy eyebrows lower and unify, and he states in a harsher tone, 'Now sit down. Both of you.' O'Neil returns to his seat immediately, smiling at the judge and clasping his hands as he does so. Deville pauses for a moment to cast one disgusted, hateful look at O'Neil, then sits down in the chair right beside him. The tension is already thick and unpleasant, but the mediator is unfazed; these things usually are. 'Alright gentlemen,' the mediator begins, his eyes darting between Deville and O'Neil as he speaks, like a large animal deciding which victim he'll eat first. 'Without referring to my notes, I believe the meat and potatoes of this case lies in the claim that Mr. Deville continually terrified and assaulted a young man at his place of work, resulting in him being afraid to show up for work and suffering horror when he did.' Angelo opens his mouth to object, but the mediator raises a restraining hand. 'He is suing for pain and suffering and loss of wages for a total of $48 000 in American money. In your response, Mr. Deville, you didn't really justify or defend your actions, you simply took issue with the amount of the claim. ...Sound about right?' 'That sounds about right, sir,' O'Neil chimes in, nodding his head slowly. 'Unfortunately, the torment hasn't stopped there. Since the claim was filed, Mr. Deville has continued to har-' 'What?' Deville interrupts, scandalised. 'I've done absolutely nothing since he filed this bogus claim. What evidence do you have that I've done anything to harm the lad since then? Or hell, who says I did anything to the boy whatsoever?' Deville challenges, wondering what exactly is in the briefcase beside O'Neil. 'Pardon me, Mr. Deville,' O'Neil says in a patronising tone, 'allow me to retract that.' O'Neil pauses for a nod from Deville, then continues a moment later when he doesn't get it. 'Immediately following the claim against Mr. Deville, an unbreathing James was rushed to the hospital and miraculously brought back to life. He resides there even now, in a coma, kept alive by machines and his mother's support. The doctors have confirmed that the teenager didn't die of natural causes... He was poisoned.' 'That was nothing to do with me,' Deville states firmly. 'An hour or so before James was found sprawled out in the parking lot,' O'Neil begins slowly, casting a disgusted glance as Deville, 'a video camera caught Mr. Deville supposedly making peace with him. By offering him a small tray of homemade cupcakes.' From the tone of his voice it is obvious O'Neil believes the cupcakes were poisoned, and the look of shock on the mediator's face doesn't exactly say he disagrees. His composure is momentarily shattered, with his mouth gaping open soundlessly and his eyes bulged, he looks like the old cliche of a fish out of water. 'What,' the man asks, shaking his head in disbelief, 'do you have to say to that, Mr. Deville? Have you been charged with anything following this discovery?' The latter question is directed at both of them. Deville pipes up first. 'Do I look like the type of guy to bake up a batch of cupcakes?' Angelo asks with a smirk, dodging the real question. 'Hold on, sir,' O'Neil says. 'There's more.' The original shock is still on the mediator's face when more hits it, and he looks truly disturbed. Angelo simply looks pissed off, but like he's trying to hide it. 'A week later, the boy's mother was heinously assaulted by Mr. Deville, and another week after that the boy's original lawyer was found dead. They suspect suicide, but...' O'Neil leaves the implication unfinished, but it still rings loudly through the small room. 'All of this is spec-' Angelo begins to object but O'Neil steamrolls over him. 'And the boy's mother is now officially missing. She was supposed to meet me here this morning at around nine, but mysteriously,' O'Neil pauses to glare at Deville as he puts emphasis on the last word, letting both men know that he sees nothing mysterious about it. 'She didn't make it. I spoke with her last night at her hotel just down the road. She wouldn't have missed this if presented with a choice.' The room goes silent for a moment following that, the third accusation of murder in as many minutes. O'Neil leans back in his chair, finished for now. The mediator is staring at Deville, fixated, eyebrows drawn down in concern. Deville unclenches his teeth and barks an obviously forced laugh. 'This is all unfounded. Mr. O'Neil has absolutely no proof that any of that was my doing. This is a disgrace that a successful businessman such as myself can be forced into com-' 'I have videotapes of all of the incident not directly involving murder,' O'Neil states simply, picking up his briefcase and setting it on his lap. Shaking himself out of his daze, the mediator waves his hands in front of him to show they won't be necessary. 'Like I said earlier, this isn't a trial,' he explains again and O'Neil puts the briefcase down. The mediator turns a wary eye on Deville, who is trying not to look like a raging murderer but failing miserably. There is no doubt in anyone's mind that if it were only he and O'Neil here in this room, only one would walk out of it. 'What are you going to say in your defense, Mr. Deville?' 'Fifty grand?' Deville demands after a moment's deliberation. 'That little son of a bitch wouldn't make fifty grand if he lived to be that old! If he think he's getting one red cent out of me - if you think you're getting one red cent out of me,' Deville amends, turning his enraged eyes on O'Neil, 'you're out of your fucking mind.' The room goes silent once again. After a moment, the mediator clears his throat and begins to speak slowly. 'This issue is obviously not going to be resolved here. Even if I thought there was a small chance, I'm not sure I'd want to spend any more time looking for it.' The man pauses to shake his head ruefully. 'Mr. Deville, I doubt your pride will let you do it, but I strongly urge you to settle this matter out of court. If a judge in a court of law heard half of what I'm hearing today, you can bet your fortune that you'd be under serious investigation before the hour ended. I'm not going to pass judgement on you, it's not my place to do so, but things look bad. You'll settle if you know what's good for you. Now please... Get the hell out of my office.' He stands up to show that the hearing is over, and O'Neil and Deville follow his example, one quietly and the other, well... 'And you'll watch your fucking mouth if you know what's good for you, pops,' Angelo growls at the man across the desk, his temper definitely getting the better of him. The mediator gasps and takes a step backwards, all of his years as a hardass lawman amounting to jack shit right now. Deville takes a menacing step towards him, then suddenly turns and lunges into O'Neil, seizing the collar of the man's suit in a maniacal death-grip and throwing him against the wall. 'Hey!' The mediator begins to protest, then thinks better of calling the obviously crazy man's attention back to him. 'You want to get involved in my personal life, O'Neil?' Deville hisses, so close his nose is touching O'Neil's. 'Then I'm gonna fuck with yours. You're gonna wish you were dead and buried by the time I'm finished, freak, I promise you that. You'd reconsider your position in all of this if you were a smart man... Otherwise you may find yourself reunited with the rest of your freakish family.' O'Neil's face goes beat-red at this last comment regarding his dead family, but before he can respond or react Deville launches him over the desk, where he collides with the mediator, knocking them both to the ground. 'I'll see you on Wednesday,' Deville says in that quietly crazy voice, then turns and practically rips the door off the hinges as he leaves.
