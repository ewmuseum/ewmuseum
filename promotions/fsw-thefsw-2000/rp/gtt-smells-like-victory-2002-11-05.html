GTT · Smells Like Victory

5 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: whoo~! 'Oh great,' Deville sighs as a pudgy, bald-headed man enters the room and sits across from him, straightening his tacky blue suit with a patronising smile. 'One last ditch attempt to talk me out of taking what's mine?' The question is more like a resigned, pitying statement, and we all know how he harbours pity. Black hatred emanates from his blue eyes, unbound disdain from his rigid body. 'Good morning, Mr. Deville,' the pudgy man beams in a rough, rumbling voice, extending a hand for Angelo to ignore. 'I'm Akino Sujara, bank manager.' He lets his hand dangle there for a minute, then audibly clears his throat and motions for Deville to shake it. 'I'll shake your hand once you stop trying to fight me for what's mine,' Deville snarls and crosses his arms over his chest, leaning back in the padded wooden chair. His mouth twists with displeasure; he's not used to such shoddy accommodations. Akino finally withdraws his hand and looks uncomfortably down at the papers scattered about his desk. He is silent for a moment, permitting the grandfather clock to tick-tock its say on the situation. The room they're in is surprisingly cramped, barely fitting the desk, clock, and three filing cabinets against the east wall. There is a window directly behind the manager's chair, but irregular curtains like vertical blinds hang in defiance of the sun's light. Deville is startled to note that there is no plant-life... With all the hot-air here this place could be a jungle. 'Well, it's an awfully large amount of money you're asking for, Mr. Deville, and it takes time to accommodate such requests. Besides, you've yet to tell us what the money is to be used for... Surely we're entitled to know that,' Akino holds his arms out to his sides, signifying the ludicrousy of any disagreement. 'We're the bank.' 'It's my fuckin' money,' Deville explodes, keeping his voice low but saturated with intensity and incredulity. The temptation to stand up and choke some sense into the man is overwhelming, yet Deville remains seated, glaring the scenario instead of acting it. 'Well, these things take time,' the bank managers fumbles, running through every piece of his well-practised spiel. 'You've known I was coming for almost a week... And you knew what I was coming for!' Deville exclaims, slamming a hand down onto the desk in frustration as he speaks. 'And you know what'll happen if you keep screwing me around.' The last is said in a much calmer voice, but the gentle threat extols more than any amount of yelling ever could. 'Let's not go down that road,' Akino says bravely, meeting Deville's glare. 'Why don't you just leave the money with me... I mean, with us. We handle it much better, I guarantee you.' The smile is arrogant and self-assured, almost condescending. Muttering curses, Deville rises to his feet and begins making his way around the desk, his arms seeming to be the only loose section of his body. Akino jumps up like his shadow, his face blanching with surprise. His head darts to the left and then the right, looking for help of some kind... Any kind. Seeing nothing, he settles for his own body, and readies it against the impending assault. Deville seems stunned by this resolve, but it is in no way unpleasant. He can smell the man's fear. It's repugnant and ashamed, but Deville finds it and exploits it. 'Listen up, Akino,' Deville growls when he reaches the man, seizing him by the collar of his suit and muscling him back against the wall. Akino hits the wall with force enough to smash right through, but it miraculously stays in tact. 'Time is running short. I want what's mine and I want it right now.' 'W-well, Mr. Deville,' Akino stutters, trying desperately to think of a reason to deny the man but finding none. His eyes shift uneasily for a moment, uncertain, then the equalizer that is greed gives them new light. 'You can't have it... You're too violent. Unhand me at once and walk away... Come back when you're calm and reasonable.' 'Walk away?' Deville cries, rage blooming throughout his being like mosquitoes in a swamp. The slap is loud and sharp, hanging in the air like a gunshot in a tunnel. 'Do I look like some cowardly pushover? Do I look like a fucking pacifist? I'll walk away just as soon as I receive my prize, not a moment before.' Akino snaps his head back to Deville, the smack still ringing loudly in his ear and pulsing in his cheek. His eyes are wet with restrained rage, and Deville sees in them the dawning realisation of defeat... One he is certain the man will twist and skew until memory treats it as a victory. Akino opens his mouth to say something, but the sound is stolen by another vicious slap, this one much harder than the last. Akino would have fallen to the ground if it weren't for Deville's stabilising hand. 'Don't you say another word unless it has to do with excusing yourself to get my money,' Deville snarls. 'Any more resistance from you better be violent.' Akino stares blankly for a minute, his right cheek glowing hotly. 'I won't sink to your level,' he snuffles, 'Some of us have ethics that make us strong! I'll turn the other cheek.' As if on command, Deville whips his other arm back and backhands the man across his other cheek, this time allowing the force to carry him stumbling into the wall and finally to the ground. 'Then you can't win,' Angelo sneers. 'You can't defeat me with flowery words and ideals, fool, because they have no business in my world. I respond only to power.' 'Power lies within!' Akino sobs from the ground, climbing slowly upright on shaky legs. 'It is of the mind!' He flinches as Deville balks another slap, then looks abashed by it. 'Power does lie within,' Angelo agrees with a sinister smile, moving forward so that only inches separate the two of them. 'So that it may be wielded by the body and mind. To use only one or the other in its application is to condemn yourself to defeat. Case in point: You only have power of the mind, so I win.' 'You win?' the manager whispers in confusion, 'But you haven't gotten what you wanted...' '...Yet,' Deville finishes the statement for him. 'The mind cannot defend against the body forever.' 'What?' Akino demands, shirking back from the look in Deville's eyes and the manner of his bearing. 'I'm going to beat you until you submit,' Deville replies calmly, the savage grin on his face contradicting the tone of the words. Akino's eyes go wide and he begins to back away, but Deville lunges forward and knocks him to the ground with one foul swipe. 'How do your ethics like that?' Deville asks rhetorically, laughing as he moves in to finish the job. ~><>...<><~ The gravel beneath the man's feet groans and grates against itself as he sets up the metal tripod with an air of familiarity. The high wind atop the building ruffles long black hair and occasionally makes it stand on end, like waves in the ocean. Everything about him is grim and deliberate, from his black eyes and clean shaven angular face to his immaculate black suit and high-powered sniper rifle gripped in his right hand. Quirking one side of his mouth up in what passes for a smile, he clamps the gun atop the tripod and fiddles with it, kneeling behind and staring through the scope as he trains it on just the right spot. Eventually satisfied, he moves beside the apparatus and waits patiently for his victim to appear so he may finish the job that has sent many to their grave prematurely, and many others have deemed impossible. You're mine, Deville, the assassin's robotic voice chuckles from within. You're just another target, waiting to be shot. The FBI's latest and last weapon goes still, almost statuesque, as he waits on his perch like a gargoyle, ready to descend death upon Deville. ~><>...<><~ The grey Cadillac Deville barrels along at a tremendous and extremely dangerous pace, hugging the road for dear life as it whips around a corner, ignoring the stop sign planted there. The man inside is not in any particular hurry to get where he's going, he just likes the thrill of the ride. As if that isn't enough, he's been reflecting on his past in a way only he can. So many people killed... Deville's mind wonders. Not just any people, either... Mainly innocents... Mothers... Fathers... Children. Whoever got in the way was eliminated, that was the way it always worked... No quarter asked nor given. He left the bank a mere twenty minutes ago, and the two briefcases full of money sit triumphantly in the passenger seat. A smile crosses his face. And that's the way it should be. The lesser make way for the greater, and if they don't or they're slow about it, whatever resulting consequences are justified. It's not just common sense, it's the law of nature. Deville pauses momentarily to turn on some music, his smile changing to a full-fledged grin as the opening lyrics for Sympathy for the Devil bounce around the interior. He likes to listen to music while philosophising... It provides a nice backdrop. These morons should actually be thankful of their passing, for in their death they have made another stronger. The weak are the playthings of the strong, small tests which they must overcome to hone their skills. He tears around another corner and narrowly avoids a bunch of kids playing street hockey. They shake their sticks at the back of the car in a laughable threat, and Deville has to struggle against the urge to go back. Does the teacher in school feel sympathy for the books which have taught dutifully and then been discarded? Does the oviparous organism feel remorse for the shell it leaves behind? So why should we feel bad for those we murder? Life is not sacred, nor is it a right. The only thing constant about life is death, and if I bring it to you, count yourself privileged - you've been a small part in building the pinnacle of evolution. Why would anyone feel sorry for your passing? You left something better in your wake. Spotting his destination down the road, Deville begins slowing the car. The music begins sounding more and more intense as it has less engine noise to compete with, and Deville's mind rises along with it. The ignorant mourn death, but only the truly weak feel sorry for those they've killed. Others must fall in order for one to excel... And not only in life or death. Angelo smiles as he realises the seamlessness with which he can relate this to almost anything. It's like the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament... Two-hundred-and-fifty-five men are sacrificed so one man may stand tall. Should we feel sympathy for those who do not win? Of course not, for in their losing they have made us better. Deville pulls up alongside the curb in front of a small downtown office and kills the engine. The house-turned-office was purchased by Deville to give his lawyer a place to stay near the court. The small patch of lawn in front still bears a 'Sold' sign, along with another sign that lists the names of the lawyers who used to share the office... Until they passed away earlier this week. There's only one man left to learn from, Deville continues as he opens the door and steps out into the soft light of the overcast sky. Inphino Blitz will be a wonder to learn from, and his passing will ascertain my position atop the mountain. I will rejoice in his defeat, for without it I would be less. The monstrous form of Hessian appears on the second floor balcony, gripping the railing with two massive claws, as Deville makes his way up the pathway. He is wearing another trench-suit that Deville insists he wear to court, much to the man's chagrin. The red eyes staring down at Deville make him a little uncomfortable, but he maintains the gaze and offers a respectful nod. Deville doesn't see the change in Hessian's face, due to the concealment of the mask, but he hears the wood of the railing begin to splinter. Before his face has time to register the confusion, the hollowly booming voice sounds, seemingly slowing the seconds as it does. 'Down!' is all he shouts, but it seems to go on forever, as if telling an epic story. Deville immediately responds, his instincts trusting the man before his mind has the opportunity to question. He hits the ground hard, rolling through as he does towards a small bush for cover. He hears the silenced sound of the shot half a second after the hole in the grass appears beside him, missing his head by a mere inch. He hears another shot, this one much louder, but doesn't pause to look around until he's behind the bush. He peaks his head around it, scanning the buildings on the other side of the road. 'He's dead,' Hessian's undead voice calls down, and Deville turns a startled look up to the big man, who is standing in the exact same position as he was a moment before. 'Now get up here. We need to talk.' 'What the hell did you shoot him with?' Deville demands as he gets to his feet and scowls at the grass-stains on his suit. 'Your fuckin' mind?' Hessian smiles in response and looks down to his side. A small purple head peaks through the poles of the banister, flashing sharp yellow teeth in a triumphant smile. Minion the Imp presents one small purple hand, fiendishly brandishing the smoking gun. 'Everyone needs friends, Deville,' Hessian calls down, his smile growing as he regards the pair of briefcases. 'Some just cost more than others.' ~><>...<><~ The courtroom is pretty much bare when Hessian and Angelo Deville walk into it, with no spectators or news agencies particularly interested in whatever it is that's going on. That's not to say the news isn't in attendance today, however, as the pair were accosted just outside the doors and had to smash two cameras before being left alone. The men on trial are very young looking, being tried together for the same crime. Their faces beam arrogance and defiance, and their lawyer, a young woman in a smart business suit, leans towards them and whispers harshly. The advice falls on deaf ears. 'Would the accused like to say anything on their behalf before I rule?' The commanding voice emanates from the judicial bench, the Australian accent in it thick and apparent. His eyes regard the two youths compassionately, and the fact that he hopes they give him something to work with is impossible to miss. 'That's him,' Hessian whispers to Deville, cocking his head towards the judge. Deville nods absently. 'Yeah,' one of the kids responds from where he's seated, either too ignorant or blatantly disrespectful to stand up. 'Fuck you!' Every set of eyes in the court go wide, and a satisfied expression crosses the kid's face. It's mirror in that of his friend. His Honour is unamused. 'Very well,' he sighs. 'If you think you can mock and ridicule Lady Justice you leave me no choice. You are not fit for this community, and I will personally see to it that we are free from your disturbing escapades. You are both banned and exiled...' 'That's his way of imposing sentence,' Hessian provides, and Angelo nods again, staring at the man in wonder. The bold-faced approach is refreshing, though at the same time intimidating considering his circumstances. '...For five years...' The judge continues in a soft voice, allowing his gavel to put the exclamation point on the words. The talkative youth jumps up from his seat, obviously shocked. 'It was only public nuisance! You can't do that for public nuisances! This is ridiculous!' The shouts are filled with a tremble and come out as more of a whine than a protest. Deville smiles. So does the judge. The court officer instructs everyone to rise as the judge returns to his quarters, and the bailiff moves forward to take the kids - who are now sobbing - into custody. 'Holy shit,' Deville exclaims to Hessian as they turn to leave. 'Five years for disturbing the peace?' 'He's not very tolerant of arrogance,' Hessian responds simply as they emerge in the hallway, stepping on bits of broken camera. 'So let me do the talking.' ~><>...<><~ 'Enter,' the strangely mellifluous voice calls from beyond the door in response to their rapping. Hessian and Angelo Deville, the judge notes, anxious over this secret meeting. The mass-murdering wrestler with dreams of becoming best in the world. 'Remember tact,' Hessian hisses as he opens the door and the two wrestlers waltz into the judge's chambers. The room is not very large, but the decor within opens it up and instills a sense of openness and comfort. Deville doesn't feel that at the present time, but he still marvels at the huge bookcases lining the walls, all undoubtedly full of charming legal ideals. Giant windows look out onto the downtown core, with its modern architectural wonders and a couple of planes off in the distance. A think couch rests against one wall, and there is even a small bar in one corner, though Deville can't spot any alcohol. Strangely, there is also a large picture of a sheep above the bookcases. The man himself is seated behind an antique oak desk, dressed in a regular white collar shirt. His judicial robe is hanging on the wall. His honest face peers out from beneath fine locks of grey, and the tender lines of experience line it in a pleasant and distinguished way. His eyes are blue and intrusive, seeming to possess the ability to sum up a man and his worth with a single glance. He doesn't rise from his seat as he regards his visitors. 'Angelo Deville,' Hessian begins the introduction in his chilling manner, extending a hand to encompass the judge. 'The Honourable Judge Joseph Argyle.' Angelo steps forward to shake the judge's hand, but finds himself ignored in the same way he did the banker earlier. Content to not ruin everything, he pulls his arm back and runs the hand through his hair instead, trying his damnedest not to look too put out. 'This is highly unorthodox, Hessian,' Argyle says sternly. 'Rather, this is illegal!' He amends. 'The only reason I'm entertaining this is because of how you got screwed earlier in the year... But be quick about it, still... If this gets out the three of us will be hanged.' He finally looks at Angelo Deville, who notes not the slightest indication of friendliness in that stare. 'I know that, Argyle,' Hessian soothes, folding his gloved hands before him. 'We appreciate your time and risk.' 'Yes, thank you,' Deville adds, doing his best to sound humble. The other two glare at him momentarily, so he shuts up and begins idly rotating the briefcases in his hands. 'So what do you want, Hessian?' Argyle asks impatiently, purposely not offering them a place to sit down. 'For you to rule in favour of my friend here,' Hessian states plainly, indicating Angelo. 'The alternative is detrimental to our society, while this man is a champion of the community. Siding with him is the only way to lean on this matter... Anything else would throw the entire system into disrepute.' 'Oh? What makes you say that?' Argyle obviously isn't buying this at face value, as he cocks one eyebrow and practically oozes suspicion. 'For starters,' Hessian begins with a smile. 'All of the support the other side had is gone... They've all mysteriously disappeared.' The red eyes seem to twinkle for a second as Hessian pauses to let that idea sink in. 'There is positively no case against Deville, real or symbolic, just a petty vendetta against his kind.' Argyle still looks doubtful, but he nods for Hessian to continue. 'There will be public outcry if he loses this case, as everyone who doesn't already know he should win will. Everything will be unnecessarily demeaned.' Hessian pauses again, looking expectantly at Argyle. 'Yes, yes,' Argyle snaps impatiently, 'you already eluded to that. Surely this isn't all you have?' Hessian fidgets in the minutest and most indiscernible way, fighting internally to keep this civil and his real feelings on the matter contained. But he can't. 'To put it bluntly, Your Honour, the opposition deserves to die,' the cold simplicity of the statement shocks Deville and Argyle, but not half so much as the intensity of Hessian's voice. When the man wants to be persuasive, one could easily imagine him talking the sun into taking a day off. 'I've faced this man before, as you know, and should've beaten him. I had that opportunity - that right! - stolen from me and I don't want to see anything even remotely similar happening to Angelo Deville.' There is a long moment of silence in which Argyle considers Hessian's words, and Hessian himself regains control of himself, slowly pacing his breathing until it seems like there is none. The seconds tick away as the judge stews over his decision, unsure perhaps for the first time in his life. After what seems like eternity for Angelo Deville, the man slowly shakes his head. 'I'm sorry, Hessian, but that simply isn't compelling enough to bring me onside. I wish I could help you guys, but I can't. Something smells off here.' Hessian and Deville both gape at the man, who looks them both in the eye as he stomps on their balls. 'You're not speaking my language.' Enough of this shit, Deville's his snaps. It's about time to finish this thing... Deville style. 'List-' Hessian begins, but Angelo steps forward and cuts him off. Hessian seems ready to throttle the smaller mobster, but doesn't. Perhaps he's saving it for later, when there are no witnesses. Argyle seems just as shocked as Hessian, but in a less than murderous way. 'Alright... Perhaps this is more your style, old man.' Deville grins as he plops both briefcases down on the desk and opens them up. Hessian's hands fire forward to prevent it, but Angelo undoes the clasps and the tops flip up soundlessly. Untold of amounts of money stare the good judge in the face, and he unconsciously licks his lips before he comes to himself. 'Are you trying to bribe me?' Argyle shouts, the epitome of incredulous. 'Me?! A judge?!' Hessian takes a large step back, shaking his head ruefully and no longer wanting any part in this. Angelo, on the other hand, merely arches one eyebrow and nods slowly. 'Of course,' he responds, crossing his arms in front of him. 'You didn't actually expect me to try and win this thing legally, did you?' Argyle starts shaking with rage as he climbs to his feet and marches slowly around the desk, his face screaming with injured pride. He stops just before Deville, scrutinising the man's eyes for what seems like forever. His calculating eyes narrow, as if peering deeper, then he takes a step back. A large smile crosses his face as he extends his right hand to the mobster. 'I like your style, Deville. This battle is as good as yours.' Deville's smile rivals that of the judge as he excitedly grabs the hand and begins shaking, sealing the bargain. ~><>...<><~ The Golden Turnbuckle Tournament continues spinning on its unfathomable axis like some faraway universe, detached from all things mundane. The struggle rages on within, causing the pulsing and red glowing that has consumed it for time immemorial. The turning stops suddenly, as if frozen in time. The angry red tinge slowly fades and transforms to a mellowing blue hue, and the bubbling ceases. Something has happened. Something has done the unthinkable impossible. Life hereafter is changed and full of the scandalous uncertain, but for the time being there is one scandal which pales and demeans all others. The GTT has made a deal with the Deville.
