GTT · Nightmare on Red Street

5 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: whoo~! Yolanda Milosevic frantically washes her hands in the kitchen sink for the fifth time in twenty minutes. Splashing water on her blanched face, she flops forward onto the counter, supporting herself with knobby elbows. The sound of the running water eases her frazzled nerves, but it can't wash the feeling of dirtiness that has consumed her since witnessing such a horrible crime late last week. Nausea overwhelms her as her mind tries to play the scene over and over again in torturous slow-motion. Forcing the gun to her head with her own hand, he leaned in and whispered something in her ear... Then kissed her cheek and smiled before pulling- smiled... He smiled! Molten bile and half-digested food fires up her throat and explodes into the sink, where it mixes with the water to create the appearance of a whirlpool of wet dog food. The retching and convulsions continue for the better part of a minute, filling the sink halfway with the chunky mixture. Tears stream from her eyes as she wipes one numb, shaking hand over her mouth. Fearing it will provoke another eruption, she runs from the room, fleeing from the revolting sucking sound the sink makes as the goo slides into the pipes. She gasps for air as she moves from the kitchen into the dining room, only a few feet away. Visitors often politely describe her apartment as charming, quaint, or cute, but despite her best efforts to fix it up, the place is doomed to be a dump. Consisting of only a kitchen/dining room mixture and a cramped bedroom, the only reason it isn't overrun with rodents is that there's no room for even a family of rats. Plants hang from the ceiling and furniture is pushed against the walls to provide a more spacious feeling, but the ultimate feeling is still one of suffocation. Yolanda feels this constricting feeling especially as she sags against the only section of bare wall, right beside the front door. Relax! Get control of yourself... You are acting like an American, she scolds herself, putting a special emphasis on the last word so it sounds like 'criminal'. Represent the Empire! Her back goes suddenly rigid, her plain face smooths into a hard shield of impassion, and focussed eyes glare out from beneath their jackets of tears. You are not defeated! He killed her; you are still alive! Her inner-voice booms authoritatively, futilely trying to maintain control over her fears. But what if he saw me? A smaller, weak voice squeaks, quivering with worry. What if he's on his way right now... A loud banging suddenly echoes through the hovel, eliciting a terrified shriek from Yolanda as her thoughts infest reality. Eyes wide with panic and mouth working uselessly, she backs away from the door, distrusting the hushed whispers vibrating through it. A gasp leaps from her throat as she bumps into the kitchen counter and five sharp raps strike the door. 'Ma'am?' A man's muted voice shouts from the hallway, followed by a very uncomfortable pause. Yolanda cringes against the warped wood as the trembling reaches a fever pitch once again. 'Police!' the voice bellows, brimming now with a comforting and protective aura. Milosevic almost collapses as the tenseness mercifully abandons her muscles. Her head stops spinning enough for her to realise she's safe. The epiphany makes her want to sit down and laugh, but instead she runs to the door on wobbly legs, needing desperately to open it before her saviour decides to leave. The deadbolt snaps open with a self-important click, then the door swings open to reveal a pair of large men in tweed jackets. Both have badges in their hands and patronising smiles on their faces. 'Thank you! Thank you!' Yolanda shrieks as she barrels into the closest officer, almost tackling him to the ground with her hug. 'How did you know? ...Thank you!' Tears begin to flow anew as she sobs into the smoke-smelling fabric. The officers exchange a quick glance. ?Uh, ma'am? You called us,' the officer who is not being squeezed to death offers in a jocular voice, and is quickly hushed by a cutting hand motion from his partner. 'Excuse Officer Boutilier,' the first officer speaks softly into the woman's hair, rubbing her back with one hand as he shoots an admonishing glare at Boutilier. 'He's absolutely tactless. Just take your time; relax.' Boutilier rolls his eyes at his partner, then rotates his hands in front of his body, imploring his partner to speed things up. The woman cries into his chest for much longer than either would've preferred, then straightesn up suddenly, looking flustered and awkward. Her head jerks around and she regards her surroundings as if just waking from a dream, then hurries back into the apartment, motioning for the police to follow her. 'You must forgive me,' she says as they come in and shut the door behind them, making a grand effort to keep her voice from wavering. 'I have not done this before. Ever. ...I wish I wasn't now.' The last is an afterthought, spoken more to herself than her guests. Her eyes flicker to the officers to gauge their reaction, and her embarrassment grows as Boutilier's valiant effort to keep from gagging fails miserably. 'Sorry,' he chirps pathetically, 'the smell.' This earns him another disdainful look from his partner, who joins Yolanda in the kitchen. 'Don't fret, miss,' he says, enveloping one of her cold hands in his. She looks up and manages a smile as he continues, 'I'm Officer McLean, and I'm renowned the world over for my patience. Take all the time you need.' His painted smile stretches as Boutilier joins him and nods as if to repeat the pledge. 'I'm sorry,' she apologises again, swallowing hard. 'I'm so scared... Or I was, until you arrived.' She tells herself to force a fake smile before realising that a genuine one is already on her face, and the officers continue returning the goodwill. 'That's normal, ma'am,' grunts Boutilier, 'for people who witness murders.' 'You're doing very well,' McLean continues the soothing. 'And we'll protect you now. You've nothing to fear.' The three wallow in the sappiness for several seconds, then Boutilier gestures for everyone to sit at the kitchen table. 'Oh right!' Yolanda exclaims, bustling towards the table. 'So sorry! Please - sit! Sit!' Everyone sits down and the officers politely refuse an offer for coffee, insisting their jobs keep them alert enough. 'Now would you mind telling us what happened?' Boutilier coaxes, lowering his head so that his eyes appear more sympathetic. She looks down at the table and nods sheepishly, dry-washing her hands in her lap as she thinks of how to go forward. 'I was on my way home from work, listening to the radio like I always do, when I noticed two cars on parked on the side of the road... One of them a police car. As I slowed down to the speed limit I noticed that something was not right,' she looks from one officer to the other slowly, then back again. 'Something was very wrong.' 'Go on,' McLean quietly prompts, nodding his head encouragingly. 'There was a man. A big and evil man. He was out of his car, restraining a small policewoman and... And...' She stutters and almost starts to cry, but a deep breath sorts that out. 'And he was holding her own gun to her head!' The revelation does not have the effect she expected. Both officers look intrigued, yes, but not really shocked as she would have been. 'You mentioned to our operator that you recognised the man. You know who he is?' The question startles her, coming before the story finishes, but she takes it in stride and responds. 'I have seen him on the television. My son, he watches wrestling so I know the names of the stars. ...This star killed that policewoman! He shot her in the head!' Her outrage begins to surface and rise with her voice, and both officers raise their hands in a mollifying gesture. 'I didn't see any more, because I didn't want to stop! I couldn't! He saw me, though... I saw his eyes.' She shudders violently at the memory. 'Whose eyes?' Officer McLean probes, getting a bit anxious himself. 'Angelo Deville,' she spits the name like a curse, gritting her teeth as if trying to keep the words from infecting the air. 'He killed her. I saw it.' Both officers nod sympathetically and lower their eyes to the table. 'We're very glad you called, ma'am,' McLean states as he meets her gaze again, 'but we have to ask that you keep everything you just told us to yourself. It's safer that way.' 'No,' Yolanda protests, whipping her head back and forth. I can't! He saw me! He saw my face! He will kill me!' A quick sob wracks her body and she flops down onto the table, cradling her head in her arms. McLean and Boutilier exchange an agitated look. 'He won't shoot you, miss. We're going to take care of Deville, we just don't want anyone identifying him as a suspect before we do.' Boutilier explains simply, then flinches back from the backhand his partner aims at him. Yolanda slowly rises from the table, looking at them with horrified eyes. 'Why? What are you going to do?' She demands, her eyebrows beginning to thicken with anger. 'Ignore him,' McLean growls. 'He doesn't know what he's talking about. We just need time to secure a few things before we go out and arrest this guy. Evidence, that sort of thing.' The recovery is heroic in it's intent but pitiful in it's effectiveness. Yolanda clambers up from the table and begins moving towards the phone. 'You must arrest him!' She screams as she yanks the phone from the receiver. 'He saw me! He will kill me!' The beeping sounds as she presses 9, then again as she hits 1 the first time. Her finger pauses over the key momentarily, then the beep is pre-empted by a sound like a torpedo being fired under water. The phone drops from her hand and she collapses to the ground, staring in shock at the small hole in her chest. She sticks a finger in and then pulls it out, marvelling at the blood there. 'Goddamnit!' McLean yells as he swipes at the silenced gun in his partner's hand. 'That wasn't necessary!' 'Sure it was,' Boutilier counters, looking abashed and angry at the same time. 'We could've convinced her to keep quiet!' 'She'd have jeopardised the whole thing! Every cop in America would be on the lookout for Deville, and when they found him they'd arrest him! Is that what you want?' Boutilier has taken on the role of lecturer, seizing the upperhand in that instant. 'Of course not,' Officer McLean concedes, 'we're going to kill that murderous son of a bitch. I just wish you'd stop offing everyone along the way.' His glare would likely slice the other's eyes to bits, but he wisely avoids his partner's eyes. Instead of looking at him, he strolls over to the mortally wounded Yolanda and watches amused for a moment as she struggles to reach the phone. Her finger twitches with the movement that could have saved - and ultimately doomed - her life even after the second bullet enters her temple. ~><>...<><~ Angelo Deville steps out into the cold evening air and instinctively wraps his black trenchcoat tighter around himself. His devious blue eyes stare out into the darkness from beneath his trademark slicked-back blonde hair, scouring the neighbourhood for anything of interest. Seeing nothing, he retreats inward as he waits for Tony to arrive. He can't help thinking that the sonofabitch is always late, and something will have to be done about it, before his mind turns to more important matters. I hate hiding, his mind moans the same complaint it's been stuck on since Saturday. But at least the police are coming at me like men this time, instead of hiding behind the armour of society and their badges. It's too bad they'll soon tire of losing and resort to their old tricks. He leans back against the brick wall of Gulliver's Inn, producing a cigar from his breast pocket and lighting it in a cupped hand. He normally wouldn't be caught burning down a place like this, but without knowing how many officers - not to mention forces - are secretly pursuing his carcass, he's avoiding his regular haunts. A small light just above his head makes eery shadows dance about his face, but the street is desolate and deserted of people for it to scare. I wonder if they're resourceful enough to discover my GTT obligations, Deville ponders as he draws back repeatedly on his stogie. Maybe they'll do my opponent a favour and try to shoot me during the match... Putting him out of his misery when their lacklustre aim plugs a slug in him instead. Deville laughs to himself as he considers the prospect of it. It certainly wouldn't be a great loss to the media world... Losing Digital Mortality. The man wastes more radio hours talking about nothing than a whole team of Howard Sterns ever could. Deville's head shakes involuntarily with disgust. Why let the coppers take the credit? I should shoot the fool myself. It's a wonder the man made it this far in the tournament... Hell, it's a wonder the loud-mouthed busybody is still alive. Deville watches intently as a car approaches and slows down, trying to recognise it as Tony's. It isn't, but the car still pulls over to the side of the road just in front of Deville and parks. Oh great... Regulars. Deville tries not to glare at the two men who climb out of the car and begin speaking to one another in a guttural language. Communism... What kind of half-wit could possibly consid- The thought is suddenly cut off by a man's voice. Angelo snaps out of his thoughts and notices the two men are now standing no more than a foot away from him, hands deep in the pockets of their lining-free parkas. Their faces are wide and angular, and both are covered with thick blonde beards. 'Hail, comrade,' the one on the right repeats with a thick Russian accent, and the other simply nods a greeting. Deville's gaze shifts between the two men, who are both roughly his size, then cocks an eyebrow. 'Good evening, gents. ...What do you want?' Deville demands impatiently, far from receptive. 'Are you a flea-infested, democracy loving pig?' the first one smoothly queries in his monotonous voice, looking hypnotised. 'We must burn them alive and feed them to the wolves!' the second one chimes in, his voice goofy and muppetish - but still decidedly Russian - voice. They both grin at Deville from behind glazed eyes, having been recently put under a spell by Digital Mortality which turns them into mindless zombie commies. 'Oh, must you now?' Deville asks rhetorically, moderately irritated at being harassed with such nonsense. 'Tell me, boys... What's so fucking grand about communism? The fact that puppets like you get the same lifestyle as men like me, regardless of what they do?' Deville braces for a violent reaction, but the two men simply continue staring down at him. 'We will follow orders,' the first one pipes up. 'We will die for communism,' the second chants. 'You're damn right you will,' Angelo agrees, nodding, 'if you don't get out of my face.' 'You will bow to Comrade Saunders, dog!' The second one bellows, face drawn back with anger now. 'The chicken guy?' Angelo asks, scrunching his face up in confusion. 'What the hell does KFC have to do with anything?' Angelo seems incredulous at discovering the truth about the international franchise. 'It's a communist plant?' 'Comrade Saunders!' The man shouts, as enraged as his zombie-ness will allow him. 'No more argument! We will tell you about plans,' the other says placidly and Deville's heart lurches into his throat. No! Deville's mind protests as the Russian starts rambling his communist dogma, but it's too late. Can't... Move. Panic seizes him as the other joins in on the litany. The rhetoric spews from their mouths in a constant stream, drowning Deville in bullshit. He struggles to stay awake as they talk about nothing, but resistance is futile. '...And then there will be great things under our empire of equality, where...' the voices zoom in and out of Deville's mind as he inevitably loses consciousness, having been subjected to far too much too much shit-talking. He's only a mortal man, afterall, and there's only so much he can stand. So as the communist drones on relentlessly, unconsciously repeating the same uninspiring words over and over again, Deville succumbs to sleep. Digital Mortality's henchmen grin proudly as Deville falls victim to the plan. They knew it would work. No one could possibly stay conscious through so much foundless and pointless talking. The minions have been trained well. Before falling into slumber, Deville's groggy mind is convinced Comrade Mortality himself made a personal appearance to flawlessly demonstrate the fine art of boring someone to sleep.
