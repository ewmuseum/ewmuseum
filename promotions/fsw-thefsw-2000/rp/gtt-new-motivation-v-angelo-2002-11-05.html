-GTT- New Motivation (v Angelo)

5 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The thirteen hour flight from America to Japan afforded Kimbusa an eternity to decide his course of action. He was still unsure of the approach he would take, but one thing was certain; he is through with Steven Caldera. He had always known that Caldera is a despicable, loathsome human being. But yesterday morning was the first time he had ever really looked Caldera in the eye, and he had seen things far too frightening to contemplate again. Without consideration of the consequences, Kimbusa boarded Caldera’s GCW jetliner and demanded transit to Japan, and he was met with little resistance from the crew. He knew precisely where he was headed. In fact, it could very well have been the first time in a long time that he had any sense of direction, any definite destination in mind. Caldera had seen Epiphany’s failure to motivate Kimbusa, and had sent her home shortly after their meeting. But Caldera would not prevent Kimbusa from finding his love. In truth, Kimbusa was harboring a growing sense of doubt about his plans. He would retreat with Epiphany to the Japanese countryside and start a family, forever free from the American way of life. But he had slowly begun to realize exactly how lofty these goals were; after all, he had only met Epiphany five days ago. But Yoshi had assured him that Epiphany loved him. And his master’s parting words were enough to embolden Kimbusa enough to approach Epiphany. Surely this plan would succeed. Caldera would simply have to find someone else to finish this tournament; Kimbusa was through. They had finally landed on a dirt airstrip not far from Epiphany’s native home, just on the outskirts of a Tokyo suburb. With an incredible surge of excitement and adventure coursing through his veins, Kimbusa stepped out into the air of his home country, and sucked down a deep breath; it was cool, untainted, fresh air. The sweet smell of home. The sweet smell of freedom. With great determination, Kimbusa set to finding Epiphany. Yoshi and his daughter had been highly respected in the area, and a kindly couple sitting on their front porch was kind enough to give directions to the meandering giant. This in itself was a tremendous change that electrified Kimbusa even further; this newfound love had made his eyes pleasant to peer into, and the commonfolk were going out of their way to help him. The dark cloud over his head had disappeared. For the first time, Kimbusa felt like a human being. It had taken Kimbusa no more than twenty minutes to arrive at Epiphany’s front doorstep from the airstrip. With a confident stroke of his hand he pushes his hair away from his face, and delivers a resounding knock to the door. After a moment’s hesitation, the door lightly swings open. Immediately, Kimbusa is taken by the beauty standing before him. Epiphany wears a floral silk dress that conforms perfectly to her curvaceous outline, and her face is perfectly radiant in the morning sun. Her black hair seems silkier than her dress, and it flows freely and softly amid the incoming breeze. Her smile warms Kimbusa’s very heart, until he realizes the extent of the surprise on her face. Her smile suddenly fades to a look of sadness, immediately forcing Kimbusa out of his ecstatic trance and back to reality. Epiphany remains silent as she gracefully turns away from Kimbusa and moves back into the house. With a cautious step, Kimbusa steps through the stoop and into her home. It is a modest structure built in ancient styles. Vases dating to the Ming dynasty occupy the quiet corners of the home, and green plant life hangs from the ceilings. The walls are mere paper, suspended by strings of bamboo. The light is dim but adequate, suiting Kimbusa perfectly. Kimbusa immediately feels at home, not just in the company of Epiphany, but among the décor of his native people. He had been gone for so long that he had forgotten the beauty of the countryside; the house stood alone amid trees and shrubs, the only artificial structure in a place in harmony with nature. But Kimbusa continues to sense some apprehension in Epiphany’s silence. She stops a few feet into the house amid the lightly flapping leaves of a small fern, before slowly turning back to face her massive visitor. “I knew that you’d come,” she states in her perfectly articulated Japanese, just like her father once used. Kimbusa can do little more than bask in the glory of this young girl’s presence. He is so taken by her radiant beauty that he is totally unaware of her melancholy demeanor. “You must go back, Kimbusa,” she states plainly, with a reluctant smile on her face. Kimbusa finally comes to terms with the extent of her sadness. He is entirely confused by this. Yoshi had assured him that Epiphany would love him. Why had she not greeted him with an open embrace like he had imagined? Why was she refusing to give in to the inevitable? “Kimbusa, Mr. Caldera phoned ahead and warned me that you were on your way. He told me what happened…” She trails off, shuddering at the thought of such a heinous confrontation, and the cruelty Caldera had inflicted. “Epiphany,” Kimbusa charges back, speaking in his mentor’s perfect enunciation. “Please… come with me. I will not go back to America. You and I, we can leave this place, we can go somewhere where they’ll never find us. We can be together, you and I. I am done with this wrestling nonsense. I am done with these corrupt American businessmen. I am done with Steven Caldera. Please… come with me.” Epiphany releases a stiff and exasperated sigh, but Kimbusa remains convinced of his ways, his face not cracking an inch. He continues to wear the same odd smile, an unearthly grin never before seen on the face of the assassin. Epiphany is noticeably startled by the extent of the love and the lack of lust in Kimbusa’s eyes. He seemed entirely sincere, and Epiphany reluctantly begins talking him down. “Kimbusa… Kimbusa, you must go back,” she begins flatly, her smile fading to a look of seriousness that confuses Kimbusa further. “No, I will not,” he counters, taking great care not to become too firm with his love. But Epiphany is not to be swayed. Slowly, she begins approaching him, her glowing dark eyes locked upon Kimbusa’s. She stops a few inches before him, close enough for the sweet smell of her perfume to seep into Kimbusa’s senses, taking him higher and further from reality. “Kimbusa, you don’t understand. It can’t be this way right now.” Kimbusa, unwilling to hear of this, drops to his knees before Epiphany. What appears to be a tear wells up in her eye, but she quickly wipes it away before it can be detected. Looking up into her eyes, Kimbusa begins pleading with Epiphany. “Please, come with me. I will have no more of Caldera’s treachery. Come with me.” For a moment it seems as though Epiphany might relent and give in to Kimbusa’s pleas. He reaches towards her and takes hold of her fragile hand with his own, grasping it gingerly and with great care. A sad smile comes across her face, but she quickly snaps her hand away and turns her back to Kimbusa. “You can’t hide from him. He will find you. He will find us. He will not lose you, Kimbusa. You’re too far involved. You must finish the tournament. You must go home.” Kimbusa nimbly snaps up to his feet, and moves closer to Epiphany, gently taking hold of her shoulders. “I am home,” is all he can muster, his conviction and his emotion now running wilder than before. But Epiphany cannot be swayed. “No, Kimbusa. Not yet.” Kimbusa slowly retracts his hands. Had his master deceived him? Perhaps he had been mistaken. Perhaps Epiphany does not love him as Yoshi insisted. Perhaps coming here was a mistake after all. Perhaps Yoshi had spoken of love only to save Kimbusa from the moment; Epiphany felt no love for Kimbusa at all. With a shattered heart, Kimbusa slowly begins backing away from Epiphany, terrified that he had just shot for the sun and missed entirely. For a moment, he finds himself unable to breathe, unable to think, unable to rally his voice to his defense. Epiphany had denied him his one hope of escape; without knowing the ramifications of her decision, she had personally damned Kimbusa back to the hell from whence he came. But a slight glimmer of hope begins twinkling back, as Epiphany turns back to face Kimbusa, an inexplicable glow of compassion in her eyes. With a trembling voice, she whispers back to Kimbusa. “I am only trying to protect you, Kimbusa. My father wished it to be so. Understand that if you do not finish this tournament, if you do not defeat Deville, Steven Caldera will never allow us to be together.” Finally, reality begins to sink in. Indeed, Caldera held surreal control over Kimbusa’s fate. Finally, Epiphany’s words begin to make sense; Caldera was still handling Kimbusa’s dealings with the law. With one stroke of the pen, Caldera could send Kimbusa back to his icy cold prison cell, never to see the light of day nor the light of love again. This can’t be, is all Kimbusa can think to himself. How had he allowed Caldera this much control? How had he been so blind? Caldera was running this entire tournament along his rules, he was arranging things the way he needed them; and Kimbusa is utterly powerless to resist. Once again, Kimbusa drops to his knees. But this time he finds himself on the floor from a lack of strength in his legs, which are no longer firm enough to hold him. Epiphany lightly steps to his side, placing a slender arm over his shoulder. But Kimbusa has moved beyond the quaint home, beyond Epiphany’s comfort. Steven Caldera had won; Kimbusa had no choice. “Please, Kimbusa, call Mr. Caldera back. Tell him you’ll be there to wrestle tonight. Tell him you’ll defeat Deville.” The entire room seems to be spinning, rapidly unraveling before him. For the past twelve hours, Kimbusa had managed to procure only thoughts of a blissful paradise, a heaven on earth consisting of nothing but the bright sun and the company of his true love. But reality had suddenly come crashing down upon Kimbusa, and all he could see before him is a return to the murky, rancid depths of a corrupt world. “How can I do this for him?” Kimbusa mumbles, his voice as empty as his expression. “How can I provide victory to a man so cruel? How can I side with someone so inhuman?” Epiphany is quick to comfort him. “Kimbusa, he fooled us all. You knew from the beginning that you were using him to learn of the business. Well, that’s precisely what he’s done. He taught you exactly what you need to know about that business. You cannot trust anyone but yourself.” “How can this be? How can this be?” Kimbusa continues to mumble intermittently, bewildered and emotionless. Perhaps he simply isn’t as wise as he had thought he was; perhaps Yoshi hadn’t taught him some basic form of perception. He had been deceived, he had been used, and now he was being tormented and taunted by the embodiment of every demon he had sought so hard to destroy. Steven Caldera would force Kimbusa to defeat Angelo Deville, as he knew he could. But in doing so, Kimbusa would be giving the world to the devil himself, all in the name of love. He had come so far, and yet he has so little. The only warmth left in the room is that of Epiphany’s tender touch as she tried her hardest to console Kimbusa. With a faint trace of a smile, Kimbusa look back at Epiphany, who seems overcome with emotion. For a moment, the two share an intensely passionate gaze, holding each other in a comforting mental embrace. Epiphany finally breaks the silence with a voice that seems almost desperate in its weakness. “My father once told me that he believed every person has the chance for salvation. Kimbusa, you have come so far, you have accomplished so much. Don’t let it go to waste. Don’t throw it all away. Be patient, give Caldera what he wants, and then we’ll leave the business, we’ll give Caldera his glory and return home. Please, Kimbusa, go back to America… defeat Angelo Deville.” For a moment, Kimbusa is unable to respond. But he quickly gathers his thoughts and rises to his full height, towering over Epiphany. His eyes suddenly seem stronger, their full power returning slowly. “Angelo Deville was here, Epiphany. He came to kill me. But he made a mistake… he came to kill me, and he failed.” Epiphany seems confused and even a bit frightened by this statement, but Kimbusa utters it with such strength and conviction that her own power returns to her. Kimbusa’s eyes suddenly appear to be flaming, not with the fiery passion of hated, but rather the powerful passion of love. His steps are firm and confident as he moves towards the door, already prepared for his confrontation with Deville. “I will return for you,” he states simply, speaking gently to Epiphany who follows him closely. “It is not your actions that dictate your destiny, Kimbusa. It is your intentions. You will be vindicated for aiding Caldera. You will defeat Deville, and you will do it… for love.” Kimbusa had been steadily heading out the door, pushing his way through his hesitation and towards the plane. But that final word, spoken with such devastating power and compassion, nearly buckles his knees. Kimbusa slowly spins back to face Epiphany one final time, and approaches her once more. The melancholy aura has faded, the sun has returned to Kimbusa’s back. Epiphany stands before him once more as the most magnificent creature he had ever experienced, and her beauty fills him with a sudden boldness he finds impossible to resist. With one quick motion, Kimbusa takes hold of Epiphany and dips her backward, saying his farewell with an electrifying embrace. As his lips meet hers, the entire world fades away, and for a moment, just for a moment… the faces of those he had killed begin smiling back at him. The world seems to revolve around this embrace for an eternity, until Kimbusa finally pulls away. Epiphany recovers her legs and stares back at Kimbusa with a simple glance that empowers Kimbusa’s soul. No words are exchanged, for Kimbusa had finally received the final charge he needed to overcome Deville. Deville was fighting for power. Caldera was fighting for glory. Tonight, Kimbusa enters the ring, fighting for love. As he finally turns away from Epiphany for the last time, he finds the morning air stinging him with a startling realization; he must, he MUST defeat Deville, or he may never see his love again.
