Ode to the Downtrodden

10 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: day after yesterday The back patio of A Little Taste of Hell is not quite so luxurious as the interior, but it still manages to put most restaurants to shame. Encompassing the space equal to a quarter of a football field, the swirled-blue marble deck is all of a level, except in two places, one where there is a large swimming pool and the other for a hot tub. The twenty silver-and-glass tables are scattered all across the floor, surrounding both of the water spectacles. Some have large umbrellas pitched over them to dim the heat brought on by the sun, which has broken forty degrees Celsius with the humidex. Standing at one of these tables is Angelo Deville, eerily cool and dry in a white Armani suit. His short blonde hair is a wind-blown mess, and his blue eyes are dancing before him. That joy is reflected in his broad smile as he looks up into the blinding sun. The reason he's so happy sets her round rump on the chair he is presently pushing in, smoothing her light blue dress underneath her as she does. She sings out a pleasant 'thank you' as he releases her chair and moves to the one on her immediate right. Smiling down at her all the while, he casually removes his jacket, drapes it over the chair-back, and sits down. The snow-white shirt underneath doesn't betray even the hint of sweat. Goddamnit! Let's be smooth with this one, Angelo thinks wryly as he takes in the beautiful creature sitting beside him. He'd kill to run his hands through that voluminous brown hair... To kiss those pouty red lips... To stare into those green eyes as they widened with the guilty pleasure of accepting him. 'What are you thinking?' She teases with an arched eyebrow, distrusting the hungry way his eyes just flashed. Angelo comes back from his thoughts and laughs. 'Forgive me, Maria,' he begins smoothly. 'Your eyes are even more entrancing than the rest of you, and I found myself lost momentarily under their spell.' Angelo says half-truthfully, figuring she'll eat that line up. She does. 'Oh Angelo,' Maria says, pretending to dismiss the comment with a giggle as she reaches over to touch his hand. 'Tell me more about the orphanage,' she pleads again. Deville had told her about his obviously false yet amazingly grandiose plans all the way over here. She ate that up, too. 'Well, I don't really like to go on about myself or my plans,' Angelo lies sweetly, 'but alright.' He leans forward to impress the significance this dream has on his life and grips her hand tighter. He forces the smile from his face and tries to make his eyes water. It doesn't work, but she mistakes it for restraint, which actually wins him more points. 'I've always loved kids. Especially orphans. They're the most precious because they succeed without having the loving support of their parents and family. They have so much affection to share,' he trails off wistfully, selling for all he's worth. He wishes he had time to choreograph all of this, but he just met the woman less than an hour ago. If he didn't have to wing it he'd probably have already scored. 'But so many of them are living and dying on the streets and in run-down orphanages where the threat of mental, physical, and even sexual abuse are real concerns. I... It... It makes me sick. They're children! They deserve love and attention!' He's rolling now, working a glitch into his voice and looking down with feigned self-consciousness. Bringing a hand to his face, she smiles encouragingly. Pretending to force a weak smile, he shakes his head and continues. 'But people don't care. Here we have poor defenceless people who cannot help themselves, and those with the money and power and love to help them... They simply refuse. How can people be so cold?' He asks desperately and she just shakes her head sympathetically. 'Well, somebody had to pick up the slack.' 'This restaurant symbolises their struggle, which is why I named it what I did. I figure it'll net several million dollars annually with the types of stuffed-shirts and big-wigs it caters to, and I'm going to pump it all into the orphanages. Into helping the kids. They deserve it. I know it isn't much, but...' He trails off and raises his arms helplessly. 'Oh Angelo! Don't say that! It's wonderful! If even a handful of people had your... Your nobility... There would be an end to the poor children's suffering!' Maria gushes, now teary-eyed and very emotional. 'You must be the sweetest, kindest man I've ever met...' She trails off with a lick of her lips. 'Yeah, well,' Angelo begins sheepishly, 'I'm really not doing all that much. I just wish I could-' His wistful sentiment is interrupted by a looming shadow that falls over their table. They both look up at the source, expecting to find a waiter or a waitress. Not quite. Standing with his stubby arms crossed over his chest is an obviously mentally handicapped man of about thrity-five, though it's hard to tell. He has one lazy eye and his shoulder-length brown hair is a mess of tangled curls. His mouth hangs open and a bit of drool stains his chin. Clutching his striped suspenders - which are apparently holding up his sweat pants - with both hands, he curls his mouth into what is supposed to be a smile and addresses the middle of the table in his strange but innocent voice. 'Eckoo' me,' he begins, taking one hand from the strap to point at the table. 'Thi' i'my tabo. Admirably, he manages to get it all out and finishes with a heavy smacking of his lips. Angelo looks around at all of the empty tables surrounding him and struggles to restrain his instinctual behaviour. He smiles at Maria and gives her a quick little wink before turning his attention to the newcomer. 'Well hello, friend,' Angelo says in a loud, highly animated, and overly happy voice that almost makes him shudder. 'Those are some absolutely funktacular suspenders you have there.' The man beams proudly at his suspenders, almost forgetting about his table for a moment. Angelo reminds him. 'I'd love to give you this table, but the lady and I are already sitting here, and there are plenty of other tables you can sit at.' He finishes up with a big smile and flourishes his arm at all of the vacant tables before dismissing the guy and turning back to his conquest. She smiles at the way he handled the situation, but she seems to be the only one who is pleased. 'Oo don' under'tan'... My tabo!' The man persists and goes so far as to lean forward and rest his head on the table, much to Deville's ire. 'Wuv dis tabo,' he elaborates and begins snuggling against the table. Deville raises his hand to knock the guy to the ground, but catches a glimpse of Maria out of the corner of his eye and turns the backhand into a compassionate stroking of the greasy hair. 'I'm sorry, buddy, but I'm afraid this table is taken. Now please... Get your head off my table and sit elsewhere.' Angelo says, struggling to keep his temper in rein. He succeeds, but just barely. How did this guy get in here, anyhow?! 'My tabo!' The table-hugger insists, rubbing both of his hands along the glass and leaving smudge-marks. 'Let's just let him have the table, Angelo,' Maria suggests pleasantly, standing up. Angelo stands up too, but for a different reason. 'Sit down, Maria. This man needs to learn that not everything is his,' Angelo says curtly, then smiles and takes the edge off his voice. 'I've the time to teach him.' Reluctantly, and heaving a deep sigh, she sits down. Angelo places one hand underneath the guy's chest and the other on his shoulder, and begins forcing him to a standing position. The man shrieks as soon as pressure is applied, and Angelo jumps back in alarm. He spares a quick glance for Maria, who is also alarmed but mainly sympathetic. 'Hey, c'mon little buddy,' Angelo says. 'What's your problem?' 'My tabo!' He insists in a loud, shrill voice. 'Angelo...' Maria says, a trace of pleading and warning in her voice. 'It's not your table! Knock this off and stand up! You're a grown man, for Christ's sake!' Angelo declares in a raised voice, his patience finally wearing. '...Please...' Maria continues, 'We can move.' 'My tabo! I come hea fo'eva! Go ?way! My tabo!' The man hugging the table shrieks again, his voice obstructed for being against the glass. A puddle of drool has formed under his head. 'The place just opened, pal, you can't have-' Angelo begins through gritted teeth, fighting a losing battle for composure. 'Angelo...' Maria says, standing up. 'My tabo! My tabo!' The shrieking continues, repeating those two words over and over. 'Goddamnit! Get the fuck off my table you ugly little piece of rat shit!' Angelo finally explodes, much to the surprise of Maria, the weird guy, and the other two dozen people eating on the patio. The only sound is the lapping of the pool-water against the wall as everyone stares on in wonder. 'How in the name of Christ did you get in here anyhow?! We don't cater to retards!' There is a collective gasp, the most audible of which coming from Maria, who is staring at Angelo with frightened contempt. 'Shit! When did society get stupid and stop killing you animals at birth?' He demands angrily, seizing the back of the poor guy's suspenders. 'You lying bastard!' Maria shouts, crying, before turning and running back towards the restaurant. Angelo doesn't care. The jig is up. Many of the people at other tables start leaving, too. One or two of the men think to interject, but change their mind due to Angelo's size and demeanour. 'Now look what you did, Corky! Ya little bastard!' Angelo is in a rage now and he rips the man off the table and holds him up in the air before him, staring into his ignorant face. The man shrieks and screams in protest, but Angelo ignores it. 'Damn you halfwits! You always have to come and interfere in the lives of the smart and successful!' 'My taboooooooooooo!' The man bawls like a three-year-old, and Angelo lets out a shout of primal fury. 'Why do we have to put up with these outcasts? They're society's collective shit-stain!' Angelo demands of everyone and no one, spotting the pool and getting an sick idea. He actually smiles at the crying face in front of him as he begins moving towards the pool. 'Y'know what happened to retards back in the days when men were men?' Angelo quizzes him, speaking relatively quietly. 'They used to take you little sonofabitches out to the ocean, seal you in a wooden crate, and send you out to sea. How do you like that?' Angelo asks sadistically, scaring the shit out of the man. The man is wracked by violent, uncontrollable sobs now, but Angelo hoists him overhead nonetheless. 'I don't have a box or the ocean, so this pool will have to do!' Angelo shouts as he gorilla presses him. 'No! Can' swim!' He screams one final protest between sobs. 'Oh, you can?' Angelo asks facetiously. 'That's alright! We'll try it anyhow!' He shouts and throws the handicapped man as far as he can. He hits the pool with a splash and immediately goes under, flailing uselessly for all he's worth. Angelo glares at the drowning man for a moment, then begins stalking toward the restaurant. The crowd regards him with fear and revulsion as he passes, and one brave man dives into the water to fetch the poor victim. Angelo considers going back, but decides against it. Screw it. We don't need a public scandal. Besides, I can take my anger out on that ring-hugging retard tomorrow night at Glory. Thinking of O'Neil causes a sick smile to cross Angelo's face, and as he leaves the half-drowned special person and 40 scandalised others behind, he's actually laughing.
