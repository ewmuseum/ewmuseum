Entering the Swamp

14 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: day before tomorrow Bienvenue a Quebec The green sign proudly welcomes all francophones into the province in bold white lettering. Missing from the sign is the obligatory translation into Canada's other national tongue, English. Both translations can be found on every other sign in every other province of the country. In a sickening twist of irony, those who have spent all of their time in the country lobbying for equality and bilingualism - and precious little else - are the only ones not subjected to it's practice. They are the Quebecois; world-renowned for their unwarranted arrogance and despised for their spurning of all things sensible. 'Welcome to Quebec', my ass, Angelo Deville thinks with distaste as he drives past the sign in his silver '79 Cadillac. Belying it's age, the car's exterior is devoid of imperfections like rust and dents, and the royal blue interior is immaculate with it's absence of cigarette burns and fast-food garbage. From bumper to polished bumper the car reflects the rays of the midday sun in a dazzling and blinding kaleidoscope of light. Deville, his blonde hair slicked back and his Apollonian frame draped in a white suit of the finest silk, leans back into the leather captain's chair and grips the steering wheel a little harder. The sound of the Rolling Stones blasting through his stereo is unable to penetrate the cocoon of anxiety that overwhelmed him the moment he crossed the border. Deville tries to avoid Canada's bastard child whenever possible, but like a metaphor of the province itself, it aggravatingly refuses to leave him alone, demanding his attention at the most inopportune of times. Much as he dislikes the fact, he has business with Quebec today, and will again come Wednesday. His obligations today should probably take precedence in his mind, but he invariably finds his thoughts turning to his other career, working for Five Star Wrestling, and more specifically his next opponent, the self-proclaimed Canadian Criminal, Chris Irving. Damn right he's a criminal, Deville thinks to himself. Every last one of these swamp-singing frogs are criminals. Trying to get everything for nothing and pissing on everyone who's helped them along the way. That little Irving schmuck even had the audacity to spew his filth on the man who brought him into the business, his Uncle Duzzer. Angelo lets his thoughts wrap around that idea for a moment, then he reconsiders his position. Then again, I suppose I can't really blame the kid... What kind of moron could take advice from that over-rated has-been? Hell, what kind of a moron could stand being around the guy for more than thirty seconds? Angelo demands of himself rhetorically, but an answer springs to mind anyway, magnifying his disgust. Only a Quebecker. Angelo notes with surprise that he has allowed himself to accelerate to more than 30 km/h over the speed limit. He resists the urge to brake his way back down to the speed limit and lets the car coast instead, though his mind protests the whole time. Quebec police don't need an excuse to pull someone with Ontario plates over, and they certainly don't need one to lay charges. Deville doesn't need that kind of trouble. He doesn't want any interference in this business. A win is a win is a win, eh? We'll see about that, Chris. There isn't going to be anyone interfering in your match to hand you the win this time. It's just you and me, punk, and you're going to be robbed of- Angelo's thoughts are interrupted by a flashing red light on the dashboard. The check gauges sign winks up at him in lieu of the empty gas tank, slyly mocking his forgetfulness in topping it off before crossing over. He doesn't like spending any time more than is necessary in this sense-forsaken place, even if it's only for a few moments at a gas station. In the land that boasts natives such as Jean Chretien and Pierre Trudeau, any horrible thing could happen at any given moment. Deville curses his absent-mindedness as he drives down the road looking for a fuel stop, his mood getting worse and worse by the second. Mercifully, a Petro station appears up ahead, it's neon sign lighting up it's rates in flagrant disregard of the sun. Seems Quebec feels it needs the sun as much as it needs the rest of Canada. Angelo marvels at the fact that the gas station uses regular numbers on their sign. He would've guessed they used Roman numerals or something, just to piss everyone off. Cursing his luck, Angelo cuts the engine and pulls into the full serve section - there's no way he's getting out of the car. The gas station itself is unremarkable. There are four pumps and a little snack shack about the size of a large kitchen off to Angelo's right, with all manner of goodies advertised along the windows. The building doesn't seem to have any walls, just windows. It is through these that Angelo glares at the three attendants lounging about inside, blatantly ignoring him. He is the only customer at the moment, and after waiting for two minutes he realises they must have spotted the Ontario license plates. Bastards sense us faster than Rosie O'Donnell does an all-you-can-eat bacon buffet, Angelo thinks with a resigned sigh. Mumbling what he believes are French obscenities, Deville gets out of the car and begins to pump his own gas, sharing his glare equally between the Full Serve sign and the three attendants. They pretend not to notice. After filling his tank just shy of overflowing, Angelo fights the temptation to simply drive away and begins stalking over to the shop. A little bell above the door tinkles his arrival as he barges in, and the three clerks look at him as if they've only just noticed his appearance. 'Didn't see me out there, eh?' Angelo spits with obvious contempt as he marches up to the counter. 'Must've blended in with the fucking parking lot... Or maybe the fields across the road.' He continues in that agitated tone, staring daggers at each of the three men in turn. All of them seem to be in their late teens, lanky and dressed identically to one another in their red and black Petro outfits. They share an amused glance with one another, then the one manning the cash register deigns to look up his bill. 'Vingt-sept dollars, monsieur,' the clerk says in an overly smug voice without looking at Angelo. His nametag identifies him as Etien, and Angelo has to swallow the desire to reach over the counter and choke the little bastard. He finds himself wanting to beat all three of these arrogant assholes to death, but as a car pulls up outside he instead reaches for his wallet and heaves another sigh. 'That's twenty-seven, right?' Angelo asks as he thumbs through his wallet. As one of the others scurry out to serve the apparent Francophone pulling into the full serve section, Angelo makes a mental note to break something as he leaves. That kid's back, for instance. 'Vingt-sept dollars, monsier,' the clerk repeats in that same smug tone, still refusing to make eye contact with a lowly Anglophone. Angelo is about to launch into a tirade when the door announcers the newcomer's arrival with a somehow french-sounding tinkle. Angelo turns around to look at him, but the man is already beside him and pushes him aside dismissively with one arm. Angelo is so surprised by the action that he stumbles backward a couple of feet before getting his bearing. 'Son of a bitch!' Angelo shouts as he regains his balance and advances toward the counter. He stops midstride at the terrified look on both clerks' faces, then feels his own eyes widen as the newcomer points a shiny barretta at his face. The gun is perhaps six inches from Deville's nose. He can smell the gunpowder inside. The man is dressed in black khakis, a white tee-shirt, and a ridiculous looking green beret. He screams something at Angelo while indicating the wallet in his hand. Deville, his rage overpowering his shock, struggles to interpret the man's words. Before he can react, the man steps forward and backhands him across the face with the gun. The two clerks inside the store gasp but don't make a move. The third one, standing outside, is completely oblivious. The gunman shouts the same thing again, and Angelo realises he's being robbed. That idea has no time to resonate as it occurs to him that this lowly son of a bitch just pistol whipped him. Raising both arms in a submissive manner, Angelo locks his eyes on those of the gunman. He gets the full satisfaction of seeing the pain bloom there as his free hands snaps up and twists the gun-hand around with a crack. The gunman's hand goes limp as he bends forward and his wrist breaks, sending the gun tumbling to the floor. With a good-old English curse, Deville pulls the would-be criminal forward and drives a knee up into his nose. The latter collapses to his knees as blood gushes all down his face and chest, and Deville lets him fall. Still enraged, Deville bends down and scoops up the gun. The look of relief that had momentarily taken residence on the clerks' faces vanishes as Deville pulls the trigger, putting a bullet through the back of the gunman's head. The sound inside the little booth is loud to the point where it's painful, and the little bell tries to get in on the action as the force from the blast pushes the door open an inch. The clerk outside looks back to the store for the first time and his eyes widen in perplexed horror. As Deville straightens up and regards the other two men in the store, Etien backs up against the wall of cigarettes behind him. The other starts weeping like a child who just dropped his ice cream cone in the dirt. 'Thank you, sir!' Etien stammers in perfect English. Angelo blinks at him and just stares blankly for a second, caught completely off-guard. 'Oh, so now you speak English...' Angelo states rather than asks, the arm with the gun twitching angrily. 'Yes sir. That man was crazy! Who knows what he might have done - he was a criminal! Thank you for saving us!' Etien proclaims, nodding his head vigorously and smiling. The second clerk continues weeping but his head is now bobbing up and down as well. The third one comes running through the door and stops to survey the scene. Angelo can't help but marvel at the kid's stupidity. ''Thank you'?' Angelo queries as he stares down at the gun, trying to control his rage at the situation. 'What gave you the idea I saved you?' Angelo asks, raising both his head and the gun to survey the kid. Before Etien can appreciate the implication of the question, Angelo hammers it home with a bullet. The gunshot again rattles everything in the little store, and Etien slumps to the ground, lifeless, with a brand new hole in the centre of his forehead. The ringing in Angelo's ears prevents him from hearing the tinkling of the bell as the third clerk turns and runs for his life. Mechanically, The Crimson Angel turns the barretta on the crying clerk and pulls the trigger twice, plugging two bullets into his chest. A low gurgling sound escapes his mouth and is followed by a trickle of blood. In a killing-induced state of euphoria, Angelo turns to kill the third clerk and discovers his absence. He spots him outside, just reaching the edge of the road at a full sprint. Angelo considers firing at him from where he is, but the glass might deflect the bullet and send it careening into a gas tank. That wouldn't be good. Instead, he calmly walks outside the little store and raises the gun to shoulder level, aims, and fires. The bullet takes off a chunk of the last clerk's head just as he reaches the other side of the road, spilling the poor adolescent into the ditch, his life tragically cut short. Angelo stands there for a moment, regarding the pistol in his hand with mixed feelings. It's definitely of shoddy workmanship and not up to his standards, but... It got the job done. With a shrug, he places the gun inside his coat and marches back into the store. The ringing in his ears still prevents him from hearing the tinkle-tinkle of the bell. If a car were to show up, he likely wouldn't hear it. He surveys the store, obviously looking for something, and finally settles his eyes on a pair of cameras, one pointing outside and the other in. With casual ease, Deville climbs up onto the counter and removes the film from each one, then places them in his pocket. After one last look around the room to make sure everyone is dead, Angelo hops down from the counter and leave the store. Although he can't hear himself for the ringing, Angelo starts whistling the tune to Sympathy for the Devil as he approaches his car and climbs in. It was a shame that he had to kill those kids, but they saw things they shouldn't have. Plus, it was fun to see their smugness washed away with blood. What was wrong with these people, anyhow? Being arrogant for no reason and pretending to be things they're not. Goddamn Quebec, Angelo thinks as he roars out of the parking lot, leaving the bloody gas station behind him. Couldn't stay around and wait for the cops to show up, after all... Besides, he has an appointment to keep. Goddamn Quebec to Hell.
