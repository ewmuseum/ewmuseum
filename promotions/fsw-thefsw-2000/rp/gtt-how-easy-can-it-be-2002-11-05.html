GTT · How Easy Can It Be?

5 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: whoo~! A young girl sits alone on the curb of the abandoned street, arms crossed over her knees and her head tucked in against them. Her thin back convulses periodically with the sniffles and sobs that wrack her, and the twin black pigtails protruding off of her head bounce in sympathetic vibration. She is wearing a pretty red dress and grips a big green lollipop in her left hand as if it is her hope and saviour. A small pink knapsack shares the curb with her. She stands out from her surroundings like a full-colour animation on a fixed black and white background. The buildings looming up all around her are defiled by careless graffiti and countless gang signs, needles are strewn about the road and sidewalk, and a low sorrowful wailing not of her own echoes through the streets like the faint whistling of a distant train. She is seemingly the only thing in the vicinity with any semblance of innocence left. Seemingly. Why do these things have to happen? her scared voice probes her own mind, echoing the sorrow of her body. Why can't these rich people just leave well enough alone? More questions follow, each as rhetorical as the last. At the tender age of eight, her grammar is impeccable, as is her comprehension of issues beyond the scope of her grade three class. We're not products of our environment, her voice takes on a cold and bitter tone, we're products of how they interact with our environment! How is such ignorance spread? Wrose yet, how does this ignorance breed reality? The pain tears at her little heartstrings and another violent round of sobbing follows. She's upset because her brother arrived home last night in a horrible mood. She could tell he had been crying, but he slapped her when she asked what was wrong. 'Da White Man is what's wrong, Chelsea! Da damn cracka!' is all he would tell her before senselessly breaking all of the lights in the house and tearing out of the house with a large knife. He's in jail now, having taken his anger and frustration out on some hapless victim. It was only later that she found out what had happened. Some white devil comes here and feels a need to boost his ego, and now my brother is in jail and some poor boy is dead! Why? Why can't they leave us alone? We're not all criminals... We're not all bad. The rage and frustration that consumed her brother begins worming it's way into her soul, and she screams wordless anger into her arms. Her anguish has already started mutating, her incomprehension of the situation twisting her mind. Damn white devil! she moans in her mind, then throws her tear-stained face backwards and cries at the sky, 'Damn white devil!' She's shocked by the sound of her own voice, taking on a surreally violent nature. She's even more shocked to discover she likes it... It and the feeling it produces in her stomach. 'Damn white devil!' she cries again, louder this time. In her mind the matter unconsciously escalates, and her next words reflect the inevitable twisting of her being. 'I'm gonna kill that pompous ass motherf-' Her cry is interrupted by a deep voice directly beside her. 'Oh, sweet... A fucking lollipop!' It booms, and Angelo Deville reaches down to snatch the candy from the baby. Her mouth continues gaping as she stares up at the large white man in the white suit, smiling down at her and stealing her lollipop for no reason. He whom she would have killed and felt superior to a moment ago is now her greatest fear when actually confronted. Deville takes a long, exaggerated lick of the lollipop, then walks away down the sidewalk laughing. She stares helplessly as he walks away with her only comfort, impotent with rage. Worse than any petty thug or gangster prowling the streets, the man is cruel because he likes it. He believes the poor are their for his amusement, and uses them as such. And she's helpless to stop it. They're helpless to stop it. 'I'm not your white devil, by the way,' he calls back in a mocking voice. 'I'm the Crimson Angel.' ~><>...<><~ Angelo Deville pulls up short as his foot breaches the small pathway leading up to the shack he managed to procure while waiting for this under-the-table FBI mission to end. He stops because the door is slightly ajar, yet only darkness pours from the interior. He waits a moment and hears nothing, then cautiously and silently moves towards the door. He glances at his watch and notes that it's midnight. Well, at least it can't be cops... They'll all be grabbing coffee for the next six hours. Trying to maintain the espionage, he gently places his fingers on the door and begins to exert only the slightest pressure. In a comic twist of fate that would have Murphy rolling on the floor, the old door immediately shrieks its outrage at being moved. Knowing any cover he had is blown, he whips the door open and jumps into the darkness, trusting the moon and the suddenness of his arrival to give him some sort of advantage. His eyes quickly scan the room and find nothing out of the ordinary, just the unremarkable silhouettes of the furniture the former inhabitants left behind. He's about to relax when he notices a glimmer from his new favourite chair, and he narrows his eyes as he takes another step into the room, trying to make out what it is. After a few seconds it strikes him that someone is sitting there, grinning at him. A full set of gold teeth shine through the darkness, the front five seemingly spelling out the identity of the owner. 'Dummy,' it reads, and rightly so. 'What the fuck do you think you're doing?' Deville demands, outraged. 'You looking to die?' He takes another step into the room and starts as something breaks underfoot. He looks up to where his light used to be, and casts a questioning gaze at the Dummy on the chair. 'And why'd you break my light?' 'The darkness is evil,' a grating voice calls out. 'Or as you would say, black is evil.' The sound of heavy breathing rapes the silence, and Deville takes a moment to recover from the shock of this confrontation. 'What the hell does that mean? You're black?' He asks, holding his arms out to the side in incomprehension. 'And I'm supposed to fear you for it? You think you're some kind of thug or something? Because I've had enough dealings with them to know they're of no consequence.' 'I'm not a thug!' the voice shouts, suddenly irate, as the man pops up from the chair. 'I'm da fuckin' alpha thug, cracka! I'm da fuckin' monsta'!' it continues, the man behind it seemingly forgetting to curb his ebonics and reverting to his real self. 'And I'm gonna kill ya!' 'And why's that?' Deville sighs, sounding halfway amused. 'Because some o' da po-lice be given me money to do it! Dey know who da top-dog is, and dey say it time for you to check da fuck out! So here I am!' Pain's voice grows more and more intense as he continues, waving his arms in front of him and pointing at the floor for some reason. 'Oh really? And just how do you plan to kill me?' Deville queries, crossing his arms over his chest in blatant defiance and disrespect. 'And just how do you plan to kill me?' Pain mimics, flashing his golden grin again. Deville pauses for a moment, waiting for a coherent response, then responds when he realises none is forthcoming. 'Are you on drugs?' 'Are you on drugs?' Pain again parrots him, his brain too tired to think of anything else. It's been a long day. 'Oh, I see...' Deville says with a disgusted shaking of his head. 'You think you can eliminate me by copying everything I do and say... Well, copy this, bitch.' Deville reaches up to his breast pocket, and Pain does the same, still grinning his confident and arrogant grin. Deville pulls out a gun and points it at the intruder, then waits so his opponent can do the same. A look of panic washes over Pain's face. It seems he made a major miscalculation in coming here to take on Angelo Deville. He pats his chest frantically, searching around for a gun. But he has no weapon there, nor anywhere else. Deville smiles as his suspicion that there is no threat is confirmed... The man is obviously unarmed. 'Well,' Deville sings as he cocks the hammer, 'this is going to be a lot easier than I thought.' ~><>...<><~ The Executive Board of Directors for Overseeing the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament sit around their large oak table, dressed to the nines and looking around at each other with pretentious pride. They know they're important... Look at the length of their title, after all. 'Bad news,' says the man at the head, who somehow manages to look exactly the same as everyone else there... Even the women. 'Remember how we suspected Angelo Deville of foul play earlier in the tournament?' Eleven heads nod, and the twelfth sombrely joins in as he waves a piece of plain white paper in front of him. 'Well, we now have proof.' The voice is cold and ominous, but thoroughly saturated with satisfaction. It was time to get down to some serious administration... It was time to enforce the rules. Every face simultaneously breaks into a huge grin, and their eyes all glitter with the prospect of being able to screw someone around. They needed to justify their existence somehow, afterall, and what better way to show how important they are than to reveal someone being heavy-handed. Deville flouted their rules, they knew he did... But they had no proof and so were helpless to do anything. Now it's a different story, however. 'Get Deville on the phone,' the head speaks again. 'Tell him to get down here and explain himself... Then we'll kick him out of the tournament.' Like hyenas at a sumptuous feast, the group throws their collective head back and cackles at the ceiling, delighting in the anticipation of their feast.
