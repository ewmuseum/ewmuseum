The Past: A CBS Special on Jesse Ramey (Part 1)

9 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11-9-02 A man slowly makes his way across the screen in a little room, full of items, and on these items we find pictures of FSW’s “Bad Attitude” Jesse Ramey. The man places his hand upon one of the items and looks into the camera. Man: The past is something some people don’t talk about very often and why would they? All of the things they have done and all of the embarrassing moments that could have been let go so long ago, but you have the small amount of people out there that are looking to ruin everything for them. That little quantity of population that know so much about a person that it’s scary to think just what they could reveal. Today, we learn about a past that is riddled with so many secretes it’s nearly impossible to understand by many. Today, we’ve brought together several people from one man’s past and you’re about to learn the truth of it all. The man slowly makes his way out of the scene as the camera pans in on a certain item located on the table. A small military cap and then sudden the scene switches over to a military institute. Little children are running around everywhere as the man’s voice rings through once again. Man: Not so typical a place to find children, but today is not the same as once before. The era is different and the year is 1987 and little Jesse is closing in on twelve years old, but he has an attitude like most convicted adults and thusly the reason he was sent to the Fort Cornhole Military Boot Camp for Boys by his parents. The scene switches over to a little blue room where we find a man sitting in a chair and his face blacked out. Man: One of Jesse’s former higher ranked officers was willing to speak on the matter at hand, but wasn’t willing to reveal his identity to the public. Officer: Jesse was one of the best cadets we had, but he was so willing to do things to make the others accept him or to just do things to show off in front of everyone that he couldn’t have made it in the Army. We were really pushing for him to go into the Navy or something along those lines, because we just couldn’t put him in line. Something I don’t like to admit, but it’s the truth, that child was just something we couldn’t handle until his sixteenth birthday, when he finally began to settle down and actually began to make his way up the ranks. He would have graduated with high ranking Military honors had he stayed with us, but he wished to attend his regular high school before he went on to college. Man: Though his life was filled of troubled adolescent misbehaviors, Jesse was none the less, a very intellectual child. He was a trained child in many forms of combat through his boot camp years and slowly more of his past will be revealed to us, as we cut for this short commercial break.
