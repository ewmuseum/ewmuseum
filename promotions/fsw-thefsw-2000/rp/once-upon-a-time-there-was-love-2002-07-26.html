Once Upon a Time, There Was Love

26 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07-26-02 He tossed and turned in his sleep, the dreams never left him alone, but they were particularly bad this night. He walked in a field, the amber grains flowing freely in the wind as he did, filling the air with the raspy sound of the grassy shoots hitting one another. He strolled through this endless plain, slowly reaching to pick of an orchid, which had bloomed silently and all alone in front of his eyes. He dropped to his knees and a tear escaped his mind, trickling down his senses and through his reason, to the core of his heart, where it rended him apart from within. He looked at the orchid, so beautiful as it lay upon his large brutish hands, the same hands that had been so gentle before. He smelled the petals and the bright light shone inside his mind, splintering his reality and breaking apart the perception he had of the world. The amber flowing grain had disappeared and he was left with nothing at all but an empty void, compelling him to let the tear flow from his eye. And it did. He looked down at the scarlet drop as it hit his darkened skin, well worn with abuse and age. The scars covered his shoulders, his arms, his chest, his back, almost like a road map of the past, leading every which way and that, detailing his life. They all hurt in one way or another, be it by memory or be it by physical pain. The one drop had flown, and broken his silence that had been far too long established. He could feel a great well open inside of him, as if it were being pumped for the essence of his soul that would flow out from within. “Wicked games indeed my old friend.” He looked up to see his reflection from a Cascading pool that was stained in crimson. He looked within, knowing not why his reflection would talk to him, merely knowing that it did. And he listened. “Too many years it has been since the hole of your heart was opened, has it not been?” “Aye, I think you are correct.” The figure rippled as he tried to grab it, and it followed the ebbing of the water, then disappeared. He could not let it fall away from him again, and without haste, he jumped within the crimson pool, engulfed within the waters that would wash his memory that had gone astray. So many things he wanted to say, but to open his mouth spelled the end of his existence in this plane. He swam forth to try and catch the reflection of himself, still not knowing why it had been, where it had been, or simply if it had been at all. “Follow my friend, and I shall reveal all.” The thought raced through his head, prompting, pleading, begging on bended knee. “Why can’t you tell me now?” The voice cackled and laughed him off, and he began to give in to the crimson darkness, letting it engulf his soul and douse the flicker in his soul. He had lost his way, and the night threatened to take what was his life from him. And he did not care. The hand that reached for him was clad in Semite, and it cared not that the red water would stain it with its dirty hue, it only cared for the being inside, and it would indeed grasp him just as he had given in. Feelings of love and hatred coursed in his veins, propelling him to lay flat, lethargic as the angelic being stood over him. The hand shook itself free of the sick and disturbing water that had fouled the fair silks that covered it, and the face that bent down calmed his nerves. He opened his eyes, the raw eyes that were bloodshot from nights of uneasy sleep, of painful memories. These things had controlled his mind, making him lose his being, but for once, he was not afraid of what he felt. It was so warm, so calm, inviting. The slender fingers were beautiful, and they curled, one by one, leaving just the index finger, which curled itself, beckoning him to stand and follow it. And he did not resist. He stood empowered, following the white figure before him, watching as it rhythmically swayed as it walked, entrancing the mind of the fool who followed. He watched as she walked off the edge of the cliff, and out onto the nothingness that bode for him to fall into, and he followed. Hell, he didn’t care anymore. He was blissful for the first time in too long. She turned and he saw the face. Oh, for all the gods there never was a more beautiful face looking at his own. Her eyes were the exact hue of heaven, and her skin was soft like that of a newborn baby. Her breast was white, her lips contrasting with the deepest shades of red. They looked like they were so very soft. He looked down at the void below him and smiled as he took a step forward, knowing he was throwing it all away, but not caring anymore. The one thing you want more than anything else is the one thing you would die trying to get. The feeling of nothingness was immediately apparent as he fell from the graceful grip of his angel, and into the nothingness and loneliness that would grab him with their demonic claws. He screamed from inside, but no sound would escape his lips, because they were sewn shut by the tapestries that are woven by society. He fought back with everything he could, all his strength, but it was never enough. He would never have his angel again, and as he looked up one last time, the demons rended her too, coloring her breast the same hue as her lips. She beckoned for him to scream forth. And he screamed. The sound woke him from his slumber, and he sat up in a cold sweat, breathing heavily. He looked down at the one who lay next to him and then down to his pillow, where a single crimson droplet has fallen. He left his bed and walked to the mirror, to look within, finding what he expected. A trail of blood had run from his eye down to his cheek, drawing what he had felt from his soul. Loss. Despair. Helplessness. He looked into the mirror for answers, seeing only the man that he had built since then. The mask he had worn, the smile that had replaced his pain, but still there was nothing he could wear to cover the scars. He looked to the sink and turned on the water. It came out in a great hurry only to be swallowed up again by the drain, and he couldn’t help but laugh at the irony. The water slowly swirled before him, draining down into the depths of god knows where, and he smiled. The light that had brought ease to his eyes was doused as he flicked the switch. He walked out of the room to face the day, thinking perhaps it would get better. Maybe the next night he could sleep. Maybe not. He sat down and rested his head in thought, the future battles hardly seemed to compare to these dreams. They tore at hi a little at a time, and even though he was not afraid, he could not hold out forever. Maybe he was just lonely. He fell asleep in the chair, and the dreams came again, but this time it was different. He looked up in the fields, and where the orchid had once bloomed, there now resided a rose. He smiled and smelled the fragrant flower. It called his name, and he knew everything would be okay. The wind beckoned him to follow it over yonder hill, where the sun shone bright, and laughter could be heard as people made merry. He looked up to the sky, and the sun had broken, chasing the clouds away. The wind again called his name. And he went.
