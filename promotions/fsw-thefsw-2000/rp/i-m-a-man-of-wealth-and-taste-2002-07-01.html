I'm a Man of Wealth and Taste

1 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: the day after yesterday The auditorium-sized room is almost as packed with people as it is with cigarette smoke. Hundreds of bodies are crowded around dozens of hanging television monitors, standing in motionless lines, and simply loafing around in small groups, chatting and laughing leisurely. They come from all walks of life; from the filthy rich to the just plain filthy. Despite their social inequalities they all seem to have the same twinkle in their eyes and the same tense eagerness to their bearing. The room actually looks startlingly like a bus terminal, except the bums in their ratty clothing are standing around and glaring suspiciously instead of sleeping on the ground. The once white walls are now covered with a sickening yellow tar, cigarette butts and wads of bubblegum are mashed into the tile, and little windows like those found on cell doors are the object of every lineup. The murmur of the gathered people is like the sound of a beehive in a rainstorm. The only thing that manages to penetrate the din is an amplified high-pitched voice blaring from the loudspeakers mounted on every pillar. ' . . .coming ?round the final bend it's Sleepy Hollow,' the voice shrieks, sounding a lot like an old 33 RPM record turned up to 45 RPM. 'Followed by Dirty Prancing and Grumpy Old Hen . . .!' The excitement in the voice is feigned - the trademark of apathetic sports announcers since the dawn of time. 'So then I said, how ?bout you try whackin' this?' A lanky Italian man states, then bursts out laughing, buckling forward and cradling his stomach with both arms. Despite being dressed in a classic, grey pin-stripe suit with an immaculate white handkerchief poking out of the breast pocket, he still manages to appear sloppy. The dingy fluorescent lighting gleams off the grease holding his mop of black hair in place. 'Ain't that brilliant?' The man asks his companion, straightening up and choking off his laughter at the other man's lack of response. In his laughing brown eyes lives the blank stare of the constantly confused. 'Huh?' Angelo Deville comes back to the present from deep within his own thoughts. 'Oh. Right. That was, uh, perfect. Way to tell him, Anthony.' He says with a grin as he slaps a hand down on Anthony's shoulder, obviously distracted by something else. He's wearing a sharp black suit very similar to Anthony's, and in one hand he's gripping a briefcase of the same colour. His normally discerning blue eyes seem to be looking but not really seeing anything. There is an uncharacteristic blank look to his angular face. 'Her,' Anthony corrects, cocking his head to the left as if to get a better look at his friend. 'Her. Right. That's what I meant,' Angelo amends his previous error, still not fully in the game. At the moment his stare is almost as distant as Anthony's, though it is due to intense internal processing rather than blissful ignorance. 'Is something wrong, Angelo?' Anthony asks, looking genuinely concerned. Angelo turns his attention towards him, really hearing the man for the first time this afternoon. They are standing in one of the many lines, several customers away from the ticket girl. The bum-looking man before them and the unidentifiable blue collar man behind them seem to be paying them no mind, concentrating instead on how they're going to spend the fortune they're bound to win today. Today's the lucky day... It's their turn, isn't it? Angelo blinks at the question and brings himself back to the here and now, then repeats the question to himself. Is something wrong, Angelo? His internal voice probes and all the filing clerks of his memory and the emotional sensors in his mind begin compiling their report. He had just cleaned up that business with Frank... Five Star Wrestling has opened their doors and are hosting their first show on Wednesday... His restaurant would be open for business the following day... How could anything be wrong? Negative, the employees of his mind finally responded with certainty. Right... So why the hell am I so preoccupied? He challenges them, and this time they're silent. He focusses in on Anthony's face and flashes him a good-natured smile, then goes to tell him everything is just peachy. 'Can you believe I'm not even a part of the inaugural show?' That's what comes out instead, and curiously, Angelo doesn't even seem surprised by it. Anthony certainly is. 'What?' He asks in a bewildered tone as he scrunches up his face and scrutinises Angelo more closely. 'Yeah, it's true,' Angelo replies, misunderstanding the meaning of the other's question. 'No interview time, no match, nothing. ' Angelo informs him with the unmistakable sound of incredulous awe in his voice. 'Oh... You're talkin' ?bout your wrestling thing, right?' Anthony catches on and is instantly amused. He decides to try and be tactful, however. 'Didn't you tell me they were eager to sign you? Why would they go through the trouble just to bench you?' He asks, failing. 'How the hell should I know? I haven't heard from any of the front office staff all week, and when they faxed me a list of Glory's competitors, I wasn't included.'Angelo responds vehemently. 'You can bet your ass I'm going to be there, though. If they don't want to give me TV time I'll just have to make my own. ' Angelo states with frenzied conviction, much to the other's dismay. The amusement drains out of Anthony's face for a moment, then something dawns on him. 'Won't that kind of stuff get you fired? I'm sure they didn't mean to leave you off the card. Maybe they're savin' you for somethin' else... Y'know, letting you rest.' Anthony offers, trying to reassure and mollify his friend. 'I've already been resting for a month! I'm about ready to break someone's neck,' Angelo responds with menace, slowly clenching and then releasing both fists. 'I can't stand this inactivity. It's-' 'I said next!' The scratchy voice proclaims as if her time is of the utmost importance and these two slugs have absolutely no business wasting even one second. Anthony and Angelo both turn their attention to the woman glaring at them from behind the window, then quickly hurry across the ten foot wide gap between them and her in an embarrassed shuffle. They don't dare look at the people in line behind them. Anthony gets there first and places a few bets on the favourites, asking stupid questions and doing his best to flirt with the woman. Angelo does his best not to throw up. The woman must be at least fifty years old and she's probably been smoking and doing drugs for about as long. One would think she'd jump at the opportunity for male companionship, but she recognises it for the mockery that it is and glares at Anthony the whole time. 'Have a great one, gorgeous!' Anthony says as he turns to Angelo and winks. Angelo and the woman both shake their heads as Angelo moves up to the window and gives her a sincere smile. 'Who's the longshot in the next race?' He asks innocently, pretending not to know. The mass of wrinkles behind the window rolls her eyes in an almost sympathetic act, sorry to see yet another young sucker ready to throw his paycheque out the window. Angelo simply continues beaming at her, unfazed by her pessimism. 'Heaven's Devil at 25 to 1,' she responds with a sigh. 'But don't bet on ?er, kid. She's never even placed! And she's older than I am!' The hag cackles at her own humour and Angelo continues smiling pleasantly. He brings up his briefcase and rests it on the little ledge in front of the window, turns it sideways so they can both see inside, then opens it up. 'I'd like to place all of this down on that one, then, ma'am... To win.' Angelo says nonchalantly, laughing at the transfixed look on the woman's face as she mind-rapes all those stacks of twenties. She gives her head a shake to compose herself, stringy gray hair whipping about her face, then turns two adoring eyes upon Angelo. 'Please sir, don't be foolish. ...If you split the winnings with me I can give you the best advice around.' She smacks her lips greedily and the adoration is replaced by avarice. 'No,' Angelo states firmly with a shake of his head. 'All of it... On Heaven's Devil. I like the name.' He says with a smile, as if that were the greatest and sanest reason to ever bet on a horse. The woman opens her mouth to protest, but then closes it again and simply shakes her head in resigned disgust. As she begins counting out the money, Angelo turns toward Anthony, who is staring at him like he just placed money on Tyson against Lewis. Angelo grins and the other man grabs him by the shoulders. 'Now I know something is wrong! Are you crazy?' Anthony hisses through his teeth, leaning towards Angelo so only the two of them can hear, then motioning towards the case. 'That's at least a hundred-grand!' 'Crazy like a fox, my friend. Crazy like a fox.' Angelo repeats, then winks at his friend. The gesture does nothing to improve Anthony's state of mind. 'You can't afford to lose that much money!' He hisses, his eyes pleading with Angelo to reconsider before it's too late. 'Sure I can,' Angelo replies calmly. 'Besides, think of how much I'll win. This will finance the first few months of my restaurant with acres and acres to spare. I'd buy in now if I were you.' Anthony seems about to respond but their private meeting is disrupted by the teller, who reaches out and tugs on the back of Angelo's suit. Apparently, for the first time in her trailer-park life, she's speechless. He turns around and she passes him a little folded card, then raises her eyebrows as if waiting for him to open it up. He does and he smiles. 'That's the right amount?' She croaks, getting her voice back. Angelo simply nods to her and tucks the receipt into his breast pocket, then walks off towards the bleachers to watch his money run around the track. 'Sir, wait!' The woman calls in a near panic. Angelo turns around in surprise, certain the jig is up. He smiles pleasantly at the woman, though, and she returns it. 'Don't you want a bodyguard? It's customary with bets this large.' 'No, that's fine,' Angelo says as he turns around and indicates Anthony with his thumb. 'You think I keep this guy around for his charm?' He calls back over his shoulder, and the woman smiles before being pulled back down to her job by the next impatient customer. Angelo and Anthony stroll through the terminal with its forest-fire's amount of smoke, silently avoiding looking at the other. They finally reach a pair of glass double-doors and step out into the fresh air. The shock is immediate and not entirely pleasant. Both men squint at the afternoon sun and the harshness of real air. Little tendrils of smoke grudgingly escape from their jackets, having found refuge there while they were inside. The buzz out here is much louder than in the building, but less constrained, and the announcer's voice is fuller and richer. Anthony points to a couple of vacant spots in the second row off to their right, and the two begin making their way over. The track on which a group of horses are currently running is a mile long, a doughnut of sand and gravel that leaves a cloud of dust behind the competitors. Set out on the grass in the doughnut's hole are many jumps and obstacles for the thrilling 'sport' which is equestrian. They are presently unmolested. The wooden bleachers are another story, however. Hot dogs, spilled soft drinks and beer, and the sort of people you wouldn't let wipe their shoes on your welcome mat defile every inch of the bleachers. Many of them are on their feet, screaming at their horse with maniacal intensity, pumping their arms up and down and waving their hats. The gallop of the steeds is barely discernable above the ruckus. 'This place is disgusting!' Anthony shouts at the top of his lungs as they reach their destination. Angelo squints and bends his head towards him, indicating he didn't hear, so Anthony yells the last word again. Angelo closes his eyes and nods knowingly as he raises a his index finger. The two men look down at the filth whose seats they were planning to steal, and decide they'd rather stand instead. 'I know! But there's money to be had. Lots of money!' Angelo responds with a guttural shout of his own, turning his attention to the finish of the race. Anthony simply mutters to himself and watches as the race ends and a tractor with huge rakes attached to its back begins chugging around the track. They sit in silence as the horses for their race begin lining up behind the starter gate. The noise is considerably less now that there is no one to yell at other than the tractor-guy. They would still have to speak rather loudly to hear one another, though. 'I bet on Mr. Thunderful,' Anthony says with a sideways glance at Angelo, breaking their personal silence. 'He's favoured to win.' 'He's not going to,' Angelo says simply, much to his companion's frustration. 'What makes you so sure?' Anthony asks, humouring his obviously demented friend. The tractor is now making its way back into its little wooden cave; the next race is about to begin. 'I fixed it,' Angelo declares in that same simple tone, still not looking at Anthony. Anthony's scandalised eyes almost bulge right out of his skull and his mouth drops open. He's about to question Angelo when the starter's pistol fires, the gate drops away, and eight angry horses begin tearing around the track. 'And they're off! It's Mr. Thunderful and Mad to the Bone up in front, followed by Scared Crow, Satan's Little Helper, and Lightning Pod!' The announcer screams in his chipmunk voice, getting his fake arousal up once more. 'Bringing up the rear is Smoke King and Lilith's Hammer, and way, way in the back is Heaven's Devil!' The horses basically maintain this order as they go through the first three quarters of the race, with the front pack of five tightening up, followed by a straggling pair, and one loner way in the back. The crowd is once again on their feet, Angelo and Anthony included, and as the horses come around the final bend Anthony receives a gentle poke in the ribs. He looks over to see Angelo grinning and staring straight at the lead pair. He indicates them with a nod of his head. The crowd heaves a collective gasp of horror as Mad to the Bone collides with Mr. Thunderful in a tangle of legs and jockey whips. Mad's jockey manages to fling himself from his horse just as the wreckage begins, but Mr. Thunderful's rider isn't so lucky: he flips over the front of his horse and does a face plant into the gravel. If it wasn't for the noise of the crowd a sickening sound like the starter's pistol would have resounded from his neck. The pack of three just behind the leaders have absolutely no time to stop or turn to avoid the flailing obstacle. The jockeys, submitting to self-preservation and abandoning their chariots of prestige and gold, throw themselves clear and let their horses wipe out in a tangle of broken limbs. Even the two horses coming up behind them barely have time to stop and must turn off the track in either direction. The only horse with time to react is the one way at the back. The one for whom defeat was ascertained. The one pitted at 25 to 1. With steady, clumsy ease, Heaven's Devil avoids the mass of bodies and trots across the line to victory. The anticlimax is astounding. The frenzied buzz of the crowd has died off to a modest hum, the announcer is silent, and all over the track bodies are slowly trying to crawl or make their way to their feet. The lucky ones, at least. Screams and howls of purest agony rise from the fallen jockeys, and shrieks of unintelligible pain bray from their steeds. One man, however, is happy. As teams of paramedics rush onto the field to offer some aid, Angelo Deville smugly rises to his feet and begins making his way back to the building, trying his hardest to look frightfully awestruck. After a moment Anthony runs up beside him, a look of absolute horror in his eyes. He works his jaw uselessly for a moment, trying to will forth words that will not come. He's in the mob, sure, but that was sick. 'You... Anthony finally manages to mutter in shocked tones that make his face seem relaxed and bemused. 'You fixed that?!' The contempt and surprise is practically dripping off the last word. Angelo simply continues walking towards the bettor's building. 'I don't have a problem with cheating, Deville - the country was founded on it - but that went too far. That was crazy! That was... Un-American!' Anthony stammers, incredulous at having witnessed such a monstrosity. Angelo looks at Anthony for the first time since the beginning of the race, but still his pace doesn't slow. 'Now who's talking crazy? That was as American as double penetration!' Angelo states, sickly pleased with himself. Anthony stops in his tracks for a moment, blank-faced, then jogs to catch up to Angelo. 'Do you think the rider of my horse agrees? How much did you pay him to do that? He's probably dead!' Anthony almost shouts the last bit as they reach the glass doors. Angelo whirls on him and gives a warning glare that implies a similar fate isn't far off for him. The man swallows hard but stands his ground. 'I don't give a shit what that asshole thinks. He's the only one that wouldn't be bought... Told me to shove my money up my ass! Serves him right,' Angelo says coldly, looking back at the carnage on the track once more. 'He got what he deserved.' Anthony can only stare. The sound of approaching ambulance sirens can now be heard, seemingly coming from all directions. None of the spectators have moved, rivetted to their seats by the tragedy they have seen. Amidst the wailing of the fallen, sobbing can be heard from multiple sections of the crowd. A few people are even outright wailing their grief at just having witnessed it. Anthony can relate to how they feel, but it goes deeper because he knows the cause. He knows it was set-up. 'But hey,' Angelo beckons, suddenly cheerful, slapping Anthony on the back to knock him out of his daze. 'Cheer up! We won, didn't we?' Angelo flashes Anthony a smile and holds out his hands questioningly, as if to ask what the problem is. As Anthony digests these latest remarks Angelo turns around and disappears through the glass doors, once again putting on a facade of sadness and mourning. Inside the building, Angelo begins his fraudulently sorrowful march towards the teller who sold him the winner. Inside his head, Angelo is rejoicing. It's true what they say... That hollow voice in his mind informs him. Winning is everything.
