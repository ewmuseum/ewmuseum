-GTT- An Induced Relapse (v Angelo)

5 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The door swings open and slams into the wall unrestricted. The crack of doorknob denting plaster echoes down the hallway, rippling down the long black corridor. With a quick flip of the wrist, an exhausted Kimbusa tosses his black duffel bag somewhere into the dim depths of his apartment, and slowly lumbers inside. With an equally careless action, he grabs the door with his left hand and slams it shut with a thud, disregarding his slumbering neighbors. With an audible sigh, Kimbusa moans his way into his Houston high rise home. It had been a hectic week for the big man. He hasn’t slept in days; Steven Caldera roused him from bed for their trip to Japan for Yoshi’s funeral, which in itself was an emotionally draining experience. This was followed immediately by a flight back to America for his match, which was a physical and challenging encounter. It then took him two days to find his way home, all the while being subjected to the nonstop lecturing of his benefactor, and the constant hum of jet engines. It was time for bed. In reality, Kimbusa felt content with the trip. He wanted nothing more than the feel of his cool silk sheets and a warm glass of water. But somewhere deep inside, Kimbusa was at peace. His heart was still flying following his first meeting with Yoshi’s daughter, and he had expended a great deal of anger and frustration on Violence Jack in his wrestling match. Despite the long hours and the nuisance of Caldera, the trip had strengthened him, and his resolve to become successful was at an all time high. But first, it was time for bed. Kimbusa does not bother turning on any lights. He rips his two hundred dollar shirt clear from his chest as he rounds the corner to his room, assuring himself that Caldera could afford to purchase many more. He barely makes it past his door before collapsing forward into bed, and he falls asleep before his head finds the pillow. Hours pass before Kimbusa awakens. Immediately the big man feels reenergized; the mental fog from the previous night has lifted, and the sun in shining in through his window, which he had neglected to cover the night before. A sudden warmth comes over him, and the slightest hint of a smile can even be detected. For a moment, Kimbusa considers remaining in bed, sleeping through the day, and relishing in the warmth of the full sun and his silk bedding. But common sense takes hold rather quickly, ushering Kimbusa to his feet. Kimbusa is on his feet no more than two seconds before a knock on the door resounds throughout the apartment. Kimbusa rubs his face for a moment before the meaning of the sound sinks in. Peering over the edge of his cupped hands, Kimbusa dumbfoundedly stares out into the hallway, unsure of his next move. After a beat, the sounds comes again, this time more assertive… there definitely seems to be someone at his door. With a sigh, Kimbusa shuffles out into the living room to let Caldera in. But as he reaches for the doorknob, a thought occurs to him; Caldera never knocks. Confused, Kimbusa stealthily approaches the door and peeks out the eyehole. His eyes grow wide as he sees the distorted figure outside. With a panicked motion he quickly smoothes his long black hair and adjusts his wrinkled slacks. The knocking sound comes again, perhaps more impatient this time. Realizing that he is without his shirt, Kimbusa quickly searches for a way to improvise. His eyes dart around the room for some way to cover himself, and he finally spots one. Lunging across the room to the window, Kimbusa grabs hold of his beige curtains and frees them with a frustrated jerk, before quickly wrapping himself in the warm material. Without giving one thought to the total ridiculousness of his attire, Kimbusa darts back for the door and swings it open to greet his guest. The young lady standing before him is radiant in her energy and life. Her warmth covers him so completely that he feels he might shed his coverings and bask in the glory of her presence, but once again common sense prevails. For her part, the young woman seems a bit shocked at the form before her. Standing in the doorway is a five hundred pound man wearing winkled black dress pants, and a beige colored curtain with the metal supports still hanging from its front. His hair is messy, his face unshaved. In reality, the sight is not in the least intimidating, at least not to her. Her eyes give her incredulous feelings away, and Kimbusa quickly turns his back to retreat inside. “Please, come in,” is all he can mutter as he shuffles away from the door. Epiphany enters the room and silently clicks the door shut behind her and takes a stance in the relative safety of the entryway. Kimbusa timidly turns to face his guest with a face full of joy, despite his atrocious excuse for clothing. Epiphany merely giggles. “What the hell are you wearing?” she chuckles teasingly. Kimbusa’s face quickly melts away into embarrassment, and he attempts to mutter an excuse. But Epiphany quickly cuts him off. “No, please… it’s fine,” she laughs again, and Kimbusa’s goofy smile returns. “So… what brings you to Houston?” Kimbusa inquires, finally realizing the oddity of Epiphany’s presence. The young girl invites herself inward. She stands no taller than five foot three, a fragile little thing indeed. Her face is radiant beneath a full length of silky black hair, and her deep eyes peer into Kimbusa’s with a sort of intrigue and mystique that leaves Kimbusa breathless. Her clothing is plain but modern looking; a generic black suit coat and black skirt that are beautiful in their simplicity, and display her wonderful form as a pleasant side note. She steps with lightness and spring that seems unnatural to Kimbusa, a silent and graceful movement that is almost angelic. She lightly steps closer to Kimbusa, but turns to take a seat on the sofa. Kimbusa takes the hint and quickly seats himself as well, taking a position in his leather recliner a few feet away. “Your associate, Mr. Caldera, invited me down for a few days. He told me about your success in this tournament, congratulations,” Epiphany states simply. “Thank you,” is all Kimbusa can mutter, still feeling weighted down by the force of this young lady’s radiant energy, breathless amid her beauty. “Father had spoken so highly of you that I came to following American wrestling. I enjoy it very much. I have followed your progress with great interest, and now Mr. Caldera has invited me to become a bit more involved with the industry. I am terribly excited; I’ve never been to a match before.” Kimbusa considers this with a light heart. The prospect of having Epiphany present at the matches is a confusing one. His thoughts immediately trail back to what Caldera had told him in the limo on the way back to America. Caldera had warned him to put this young girl out of his head for the time being and focus on utilizing his power to become victorious. Why, then, had Caldera invited her to take part in them? “Then, you shall accompany us to the next contest?” Kimbusa inquires, now completely confused by his absent benefactor’s actions. “I certainly hope so,” she says, obviously excited at the possibility of being at ringside for an American wrestling match. Kimbusa’s heart begins to fly at the idea, but she continues. “But in the meantime, Mr. Caldera was hoping that I might help prepare you for your next contest.” Kimbusa sighs. That was it. He had all but refused to prepare for his matches. His conscience had been making an impressive comeback, and the more human his opponents seemed, the harder it was to harm them. Had Violence Jack been a man to him, Kimbusa could never have released such brutality. But Violence Jack had been nothing but a mindless robot, and therefore was an easy target for Kimbusa’s frustrations. Needless to say, the prospect of humanizing his next opponent was a worrisome one. “Caldera and I have… conflicting views on this,” Kimbusa begins grimly. “It is not wise nor is it necessary to prepare.” “Oh, but it is,” she shoots back in a very matter of fact manner. Kimbusa is surprised by her insistence; he had not expected her to be so involved with his wrestling affairs. She looks upon him with a rather discerning glare, as if trying to force Kimbusa into submission with her eyes alone. It works. “Very well, then,” Kimbusa concedes. His face actually becomes cheerful as he meets Epiphany’s contented stare with his own. “Who do I face next?” Epiphany states the next lines with a level of ironic glee that is almost unsettling. “His name is Angelo Deville, and he is the favorite to win this entire tournament.” Kimbusa seems confused by this, and his blank stare gives him away. “Do you realize that you’re only two victories away from total success? That goes for Angelo as well. Nobody thinks you can beat him, Kimbusa. Deville is one of, if not the best sports entertainer in the country. And everyone sees him winning the entire tournament, and beating you no less.” A certain level of uncertainty comes over Kimbusa. Epiphany’s radiant glory seems to have faded to something more down to earth, a fact that makes Kimbusa’s objection a bit easier. “What is it about this man that could possibly favor him over me?” Epiphany chuckles in quiet amusement. “You don’t even realize that you’re in the same company as this man, do you?” Kimbusa’s expression remains blank. “Wow, Mr. Caldera wasn’t lying. You do need work.” A sharp, surprised laugh escapes Kimbusa’s lips in spite of himself, and he is joined in a wide smile by his visitor. An awkward moment of silence occupies the next few moments, as Epiphany and Kimbusa study each other’s eyes. Kimbusa prepares to speak again, until from somewhere down the hall outside, a booming voice suddenly appears. Kimbusa and Epiphany both turn their heads towards the front door, listening as the muffled voice approaches. “…Yeah, they sky’s the limit now. We’ve got him on the move. No, don’t worry about that, I’ll take care of it. We’re going all the way here.” The light seeping in under the front door becomes blocked by the presence of two Italian shoes. A click is heard outside before the door swings open, without a single knock to signal the newcomer. Steven Caldera steps into the room looking glorious as ever, dressed in his usual over-the-top attire and a pair of expensive sunglasses. He is greeted by smiles from both Kimbusa and Epiphany, but Caldera quietly pulls his sunglasses off to stare at his rather oddly dressed pupil. “What the hell are you wearing?” Caldera inquires in Kimbusa’s direction. Kimbusa looks down at himself, and quickly realizes that he is still wearing a curtain over his chest. Caldera glances upwards at the bare window behind Kimbusa, and quickly assembles the ridiculous situation. With an incredulous grin Caldera watches as Kimbusa gathers himself and shuffles back into the hallway to change. Epiphany giggles lightly as Kimbusa feels, before standing to greet Caldera. “Hello, Mr. Caldera,” she states lightly, extending her hand to be shaken. Caldera greets her warmly, and takes a seat on the sofa next to her. “I’m sorry I’m late… how are things coming?” Epiphany stares down the hallway towards Kimbusa’s bedroom. “Oh, I think he’s warming up to me,” she coos, smiling with great humor as she waits for Kimbusa’s return. Caldera adjusts his tie and presses on. “Thank you again for coming. I know that with your father’s death you must have business to attend to, and your presence here will do wonders for Kimbusa.” Epiphany remains polite, but does not make eye contact with Caldera. She appears to be uncomfortable with his position so close to her, as she nods in agreement and compliance. Steven Caldera seemed to exude a type of overbearing confidence that was rather unsettling. His hair is groomed all too perfectly and his suit ensemble is too well prepared. His always thick wallet is visible in the breast pocket of his jacket, and his teeth beam with a manufactured whiteness that makes Caldera the epitome of the corrupt American businessman. Epiphany could see why her father had warned her of this man. Yoshi had never met Caldera, but she got the impression that he knew Caldera nonetheless. Before his death, her father had prepared her for her meeting with the duo. To his credit, Yoshi had followed Kimbusa’s progress even more deeply than Epiphany, and he had prepared his daughter well. Caldera, he had said, was an intelligent man and could be a great asset to his former student. Caldera was well educated and knew the American wrestling scene well. To Caldera’s credit, he had gone out of his way to see that Kimbusa had a fair chance in the sports entertainment world, and Kimbusa had thrived. Caldera was a devout businessman who insisted on success in his every venture, and Kimbusa would be no different. Caldera would be a teacher and a provider. But Yoshi had not forgotten to warn his daughter of Caldera the businessman. Sitting next to him, Epiphany gets the feeling that her father was right; Caldera is a relentless thief and a corrupt bureaucrat who viewed Kimbusa as little more than a business venture. Perhaps Caldera was not entirely cold hearted, and perhaps he was glad to be helping Kimbusa gain some semblance of reason and control in his life. But business came first, and Epiphany got the distinct feeling that, should Kimbusa fail, Caldera would disappear rather quickly. She had agreed to come to help ensure Kimbusa’s success. Caldera needed Kimbusa to be victorious for the sake of his own businesses and ventures. Kimbusa needed to succeed to find peace in those dark regions that he has so long now sought to silence. Epiphany had come to ensure Kimbusa’s victory, because only she knew what would become of Kimbusa should he fail. And it is a fate she would not wish upon any living being. Yoshi had told her in no uncertain terms that Kimbusa must not fail. His dying wish was for his daughter to ensure his pupil’s victory. There could be no other way. Kimbusa comes lumbering back out into the living area, this time clad in the rather modest attire of black dress pants and plain white shirt. He is noticeably uncomfortable in these clothes, but makes the best of the situation for the sake of his present company. Epiphany sees an opportunity to distance herself from Caldera, and moves towards Kimbusa. For his part, the Asian Assassin stays still and quiet as Epiphany gingerly adjusts his collar. Kimbusa closes his eyes and finds himself in a state of ecstasy from the scent of Epiphany’s perfume. The smell wafts its way deep inside Kimbusa’s mind, causing him to take off into flight. Kimbusa smiles warmly at Epiphany as she finishes her task, and she smoothly turns and takes a seat in the recliner. Kimbusa remains standing in total silence, still relishing the absolute beauty of Yoshi’s daughter. Caldera notices Kimbusa’s pleasure, and quickly brings him down. “You did great in the last round, Kimbusa, and we’re damn close to accomplishing our goal. You only have two more people to defeat before you become the Golden Turnbuckle Champion. If you can do that, the world will be at our feet. But to win these next two rounds, you must understand the danger presented by the other three men here with you.” Kimbusa forces the smile from his face to placate Caldera, but in truth is far more interested by the energetic glow of Epiphany than the constantly lecturing Caldera. With a sharp nod, Kimbusa assures Caldera that he is paying attention, which in itself is a poorly hidden deception. Nevetheless, Caldera continues. “You’re facing Angelo Deville in the next round, Kimbusa. He’s one of the best talents out there. He’s the top champion in FSW right now. And if you can beat him, that alone will be a tremendous accomplishment.” Kimbusa suddenly snaps to attention. “There is no if,” he insists impatiently, repeating the sentiment he expressed earlier. “I can defeat whoever you present before me. I do not understand this pointless preparation.” He protests with a great deal of annoyance and resentment towards Caldera, but Epiphany turns around in her chair to face him. “Kimbusa, please. Mr. Caldera knows more about this business than you do, and I think it is in your best interest to at least hear him out,” she pleads. Her voice seems positively heavenly, and each word rolls from her wonderfully red lips with a softness that dissolves any protest Kimbusa might have harbored. With a dumbfounded nod, Kimbusa grimaces at Caldera, and allows him to continue. “Angelo is a stellar talent. He graduated from one of the most prestigious wrestling academies in the world with top honors, and he’s a superstar in every aspect. In the ring he’s one of the smoothest, most clever athletes in the world. And, as you’ve no doubt heard, he is also the heavy favorite. And, Kimbusa… the fact that he is favored over a five hundred pound monster is a testament to his skill. Do you understand?” Kimbusa had listened only momentarily, and misses Caldera’s question entirely. Kimbusa’s eyes finally rise away from Epiphany and find an impatient Caldera staring quizzically. Kimbusa instinctively nods at Caldera, but his confusion is transparent. Caldera stands sharply from his seat and maneuvers around the coffee table, coming face to face with Kimbusa. This action holds Kimbusa’s attention, and he stares eye to eye with Caldera in a moment so intense that it causes Epiphany to stand and distance herself from the two. “Listen very carefully,” Caldera begins in a low, methodical voice. “We have a deal. You are going to beat Angelo Deville, do you understand?” Kimbusa simply nods, his face growing more intense. “And the only way you can beat Angelo Deville is by listening to me. Do you understand that?” This obviously angers Kimbusa, almost to the point of another heated outburst of fury… Kimbusa turns away and looks to be heading to cause some destruction… but he stops himself. With an aggravated look he turns back to Caldera and nods again, his entire body now shaking with barely restrained anger. “I’ve got better things to do than stand here and talk to an overgrown ape who won’t listen to a damn thing I say. I’ll be back tomorrow to continue this. And you’d better be ready to learn a thing or two.” Caldera states these last lines with a flair of anger and a patronizing drone that brings Kimbusa to the brink of explosion. Caldera, eyes still fixated on Kimbusa, slowly slips his sunglasses on. With a forced smile he nods his goodbye to Epiphany, who has retreated behind the bar in the corner. Caldera delivers one final grimace to Kimbusa before turning away and taking his leave, slamming the door forcefully behind him. Kimbusa releases a tremendously deep breath, allowing the frustration to pour away. For a moment he can only breathe, as he attempts to pacify his growing anger. But from somewhere behind him comes the light tinkling of glass, and the smooth pour of a liquid. Kimbusa turns to find Epiphany behind his bar, pouring two small saucers of sake. Kimbusa manages to procure a thin smile, and he slowly shuffles towards the bar. Epiphany presents him with a saucer of sake, which he takes with an eager gratitude than induces a smile for Epiphany as well. She opens her mouth to speak for a moment, but Kimbusa’s empty stare silences her. He turns his attention again to her ruby red lips, which seem so soft and tender… She can’t help but turn her head away of embarrassment. After a moment, just as Kimbusa’s anger seems to have subsided… he throws a monstrously hateful punch towards the bar, placing his fist directly through a two hundred dollar bottle of champagne. Glass falls to the floor with a steady tinkling sound, and the champagne fizzes and begins dripping down the wall to the carpet below. But amid the carnage, Kimbusa’s face is the most frightful. Epiphany looks into Kimbusa’s eyes to find them a deep red, burning with the same anger and rage that had plagued him for years. His hulking form seems ready to strike, to destroy, to cause more damage. He glares at Epiphany with a frightening scowl, looking as if he might be privy to releasing his anger upon the most handy and fragile target he sees. But Epiphany is not unsettled. With a slow-moving and cautious hand, she takes hold of Kimbusa’s still clenched fist. Kimbusa’s rage slowly begins to subside at the very touch of her tender fingers. He opens his hand to find it covered in blood, shards of glass still protruding from his flesh. Epiphany tenderly begins plucking the glass from his skin, cleansing the wound with her care. Kimbusa’s eyes quickly return to normal and he finds himself embarrassed by his lack of control. The two share an emotional stare for a moment, as Epiphany grips Kimbusa’s hands with her own, sharing her healing powers with Kimbusa.
