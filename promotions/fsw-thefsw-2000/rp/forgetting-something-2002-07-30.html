Forgetting Something

30 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07-30-02 “Hey kid, you know I just thought of a major problem.” “Yo?” Timo Bolamba, The Perpetual Master of All Things Samoan, and his good buddy Jake Estrada Jr. walk down the mean streets of Philadelphia. A cold wind blows through the air. Timo looks overhead and sees a ray of light breaking through the clouds, and smiles. -Domino, you were right dude- “I was just thinking Jake, you haven’t exactly got a tattoo.” “So?” “So…you are going to go meet these two girls right?” “Uh-huh.” “And.” “And?” Timo stopped and almost slapped his forehead. Then he remembered how dense he can be at times and grinned, baring the pearly whites. Jake looked over to the older man and shrugged. Timo looked as if he had lost himself in space. “And what Timo? “Huh? Oh yeah! You need a tattoo, because the girls are expecting to see one.” Jake stopped walking and shook his head. Timo stopped several paces after and turned around, wondering what the holdup is. Jake stood behind him, kinda watching and waiting as The Samoan turned around and shrugged his shoulders. “What?” “No way man, there is no way I’m doing that.” “Religion?” Jake laughs a bit and shakes his head. Timo was dense in many ways, all the better to make him a great teacher. The Samoan reached out and laughed, well he almost laughed if not for the fact he realized the problem. The kid looked around nervously and tried to chuckle along, not sure what the crazy Timo would do. He stopped laughing and looked shocked as Timo grabbed his shirt by the shoulder area and began dragging him down the street. “Timo…wha…what are you doing?” “It’s called tough love kid. Nobody I train is going to be afraid of needles!” “But!” “Yes, and without a tattoo, you aren’t getting any “butt” at all, comprende?” Jake gave up trying to resist and flogged Timo’s hand off his shoulder. Timo bucked up and walked along, pulling out his wallet and digging inside. Several bums looked on at The Samoan from a back alley they were huddled in. He stopped momentarily and pointed at a woman who had a child. “Timo, what are you doing?” “Watch kid.” The woman and her companion come forward, and Timo hands him several One Hundred dollar bills. The two look at each other in awe at the kindness of the stranger. They try to offer him a small trinket in reward, but Timo looks at the woman after he takes it, and puts it back in her hand, closing it behind himself. “No thank you, just don’t let me catch you spending it on booze. Go buy some clothes, get some food.” The man silently reaches his grimy hand out and Timo takes it, despite the dirt. The man claps him on the shoulder and they walk down the street. Timo watches as they pass a liquor store and head into a small market. A look of wonder passes across Jake’s face. “What was that about?” “Lesson two kid, help those who need it more than you.” Jake looks over at the other bums and scratches his head. “But what about the rest of them?” Timo looks at them and continues walking, even as they all look at him. He has a strange vindictive look upon his face, not common on the heroic Samoan. ‘Well? Why are you walking away? I don’t understand.” “Take a look Jake, they all have bottles in their hands. That family needed the money, the rest of them are just pathetic.” Jake doesn’t fully understand, but he walks with Timo anyhow. The two men continue down the street, and odd calm and quietness around them, not at all like the last couple days. Jake looks over to Timo and shakes his head. “You haven’t always had it so good have you? I mean, I know about the money, the cars, the career, but why do you think those men are pathetic?” Timo keeps walking, he passes a group of people on the street, not paying them mind. He stares forward and ponders his choice of words before speaking. “Because, I used to be one.” “What?” “I don’t like talking about it Jake.” Jake tries to keep up to the pace Timo has set now, sheathing his hands inside of his pockets as a brisk wind picks up. The Samoan seemed to be walking faster and faster now, almost as if he wanted to get back to the gym faster now. “But, you have to some time right?” “No, I don’t.” Timo stops with the gym in view, it loomed in the distance and he had his back turned to it. Jake took in the monolithic building that stood behind the man with a monolithic character and waited for what Timo would say. “Look kid, let’s just say that some things are better left dead okay?” “Okay” “We got a lot to do Jake, I have to train for my match this week, and I also have to call my wife before she kills me. You know….women.” “Yeah I know.” “Hey kid, you know, I just thought of a major problem.” “Yo?” Timo turned to walk towards the building, the concrete below him was cracked and beginning to deteriorate and it irritated him. He looked over to the Latino and he didn’t seem to be having a problem. Maybe it was because he had come from this and wanted to change it, maybe it was just him. “I was just thinking Jake, you haven’t exactly got a tattoo.” “So?” “So…you are going to go meet these two girls right?” “Uh-huh.” “And.” “Aww crap…I was hoping you had forgot.”
