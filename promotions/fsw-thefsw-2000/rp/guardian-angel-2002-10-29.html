Guardian Angel

29 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10-29-02 The era was that of the late seventies, Dr. J was laying down the sky hook, Disco was raging, Punk was beginning. The Cold War was still in full effect, most of the stars from this day and age had not even broken yet. It was the day of Funk, the day of Indulgence…but somewhere, on a small island, things hadn’t really changed much. The efforts of a small sweat stained boy playing under the cerulean skies in the tropical paradise of Samoa do not go unnoticed as the watchful eye of “The Great Samoan” Rufan Bolamba settles upon his only child. The type of pride one can only find inside when watching their child excel flows through him, and he smiles. His great pearl colored teeth shine as a they contrast his dark Samoan skin. He claps as his child performs an Asai Moonsault off of an old and gnarled taproot of a palm tree. On a hammock that sways low to the ground, off in the not so far distance lays Captain Lou Albano, Timo’s godfather, and Rufan’s loyal tag partner. He mumbles about the antics of Samoans, then begins to snore and gurgle in only the way that good ol’ Lou can. “Son, you have skills that impress even me. When I was your age, I could do some of these moves, but you do them with such a fluid action…for a boy of six, you are something else.” “Rufan my boy, you are filling that boy’s head with nonsense again!” Timo turned bright eyed, and looked at the man who walked up the gravel path that led to their house. His ragged left knee hobbled him, and he walked with a cane, but the love in his eyes reflected back unto the small boy with dark locks. “GRANDPA!” The small boy ran up the walkway, scrambling the odd colored rocks hither and tither as he did. He jumped in the air directly into the strong arms of his grandfather. The old man smiled heartily and was soon joined by Rufan. Timo jumped to the ground and made several excited noises. “Armon, my father, it has been a long time. Tell me, do they still call you…”The Hammer”?” The old man smiles and laughs. Rufan was always a kidder, and much like the wild spirit he had once possessed, Rufan now possessed, as did young Timo. The boy was growing so fast, it was really a shame that the old man would not see him again soon. “How was your trip father?” “Bumpy, you would think an old man who is dying could at least fly first class once. You young bucks with your fancy moves…bah it isn’t the same as it used to be.” Rufan shooed away Timo, then turned offering his father a seat at the head of his small table. The two Samoan warriors sat across from another, with as much admiration and respect as the greatest opponents would once have had. “Wrestling?” “Yes…wrestling. Is that what you are teaching the boy?” Rufan nods slowly. “He learns fast, and his skills are just uncann…” “Bah…don’t you think this has gone on long enough?” “Father?” The old man sighs and looks at his knee, then back to Rufan. The old man slides a few stray strands of grey hair aside and puts his cane on the table. He knew the dangers involved, the risks. He had come to say goodbye, but to do one final thing… “You know what you are getting that boy into. He worships you, he follows everything you do, and he would trade his everything to walk out there with you.” “That is why I teach him. You know that Father.” “Yes, yes I do, but I also know that you see something in him, something nobody else but a Samoan warrior can see. You can look into that young unscathed chest of his and see that golden heart beating. You can see what he can do, and you wish to train it as your own.' Rufan watches as his child runs across the lawn, jumping on the rotund belly of Captain Lou, waking him up. Lou smiles and tousles his hair, then gets up for a brief round or two with the kid. “Yes. That is true to some extent.” “Rufan, you have to let him make his own path in life, someday if this is his destiny, he will be the best, just as I was, just as you are. Can you really deal with him being something he isn’t and having to live in your shadow forever?” There is no answer as the glassy eyes of a father watch his young son playfully putting his friend into a headlock. Lou sells the move and makes a tapping motion. Timo stands up and walks around with his hand held up in the air. “He is so innocent.” “Innocent and carefree. You know the life ahead of him. You know he will be hurt, he will be scarred, he will sweat, he will bleed. He will do everything his entire life just to prove to you he is your son. Just like you did…for me.” Armon places a weary hand on Rufan’s shoulder and the younger Samoan helps his father to his feet and into the house. Timo’s laughter can be heard echoing outside, mixed with the cajoling of Captain Lou. The sweet cacophony of their mixed voices echoes in Rufan’s mind as he helps his father sit down. “Do me a favor son, call him inside, I should like to see him one more time before I leave.” Rufan winds his way outside and calls Timo in, the young boy follows his father expectantly, and when he reaches the living room, he hops on his grandpa’s good knee. Rufan leaves the room to let the two have their moment. A slight tear forms at his eye when he hears his father’s voice crack for the first time in his life. “Timo…you are getting so big. Have you been working out like a good wrestler?” “YEAH! I was down a minute ago and Uncle Lou taught me how to do a Full Nelson!” Armon laughs a bit, and coughs. Timo’s smile causes a tear to stain his cheek, and he struggles to continue on. The young boys expectant face is unwavering, calling for his favorite person in the world to teach him again. “You know…I never really got to say this to you, but I am proud of you Timo. You are everything I could have hoped for, and you are so free. One day you will be a true champion, but only if you choose to do so. Do you understand that?” “I think so…Is it like when Dad and Uncle Lou won?” Armon chuckles. How could a six year old understand this game? It was such folly to believe he could influence him at such a young age…yet…he knew perhaps someday Timo would remember. Maybe he would hear his words and become the man he knew he had inside. “My boy, you are the fourth generation of us wrestlers. My father Raku, your great grandfather, wrestled, I wrestled, now your father wrestles. And now, it is your turn, but I want you to make this your own. I want you to know it is your choice, and nobody will ever feel bad if you don’t want to wrestle.” “But I like wrestling. I want to be just like you and Dad! Then I can have my own music and my own belt to carry, they will call me umm…what’s the word grandpa?” Armon coughed again and smiled. He looked into the bright green eyes of his only grandson, the last of his line. He could see depths that would portend the future to his dying frame, and allow him to watch with pride from his heavenly gaze. “The cancer took Grandpa Armon three weeks later. My father never told me that he was gone, he just told me he had went to retire in the Bahamas. I figured it out because to this day I still remember what he told me Jake.” Jake Estrada Jr., Timo’s apprentice and loyal friend sat in a lush chair, much different from the one his grandfather had talked to him in for the last time. This chair was so much more comfortable, yet, Timo was willing to bet it wasn’t as comforting as Armon’s knee. “What did he say?” Timo turned to the wall that his Five Star Wrestling World Championship belt hung upon and looked into the gold. For an instant he could swear he saw the smiling face of Armon, and he smiled himself, letting a tear drop off his cheek. “The word, my Timo, is Champion. That’s what you will be, and I will always be your number one fan.”
