Tracing It Back. What Makes a Magnum Wolf?

20 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11-20-02 'Let's GO! Move the line! We've got a lot of hungry mouths to feed!' The lines were always the same. As long as you could see in each direction, and smelled like a mix of rotting flesh, and processed meat. The usual suspects were there, and each and every person working the soup line knew each and every person coming up to get food. Some of them even knew them by name. Nora Roberts was one of the few people in the building embarassed to be where she was. Most hurried up to the line, and pushed and shoved their way into a position to get food. She was above that. She may be poor, but she was not an indigent. Yet. So she stood, staring at the crowd of animals huddled around the table, begging for food. Her arms crossed over the chests of her two sons, Aaron and Ryan. Both were ragged children, to say the very least. Ryan more-so than Aaron. Ryan's eyes were deep set, and a bluish gray tint. His cheeks had sunken into his face. It was obvious that Ryan had some serious problems. Obvious to everyone except him. Even now, as they watched this horrible scene in the basement of Our Lady of the Rosary Church in downtown Tucson, Arizona, his eyes were wide and bright. He took in everything that went on around him with a sense of excitement. Slowly, the crowd began to disperse to their tables to eat what little food they had managed to get a hold of. Nora patted her youngest son, Aaron, on the back to force him forward to the table. His older brother followed him, both with their hands behind their backs. Nora's eyes followed them for the first few steps. She was so proud of the way they were turning out, even under the conditions they were under. Manners that never faltered. A man at the table stood holding a carving knife, and pointing at the table. 'How many ya got to feed, ma'am?' asked the portly man behind the table. 'Three of us. If there isn't enough, I'm sure I could--' Nora began. 'Aw fooey! We got plenty, lady! Come up here and have some! Whaddya say, little man? Turkey or ham?' the man asked in the direction of Aaron. With eyes full of wonder at the prospect of so much food, Aaron had to choose carefully. He loved Turkey. At least the kind that came in the red and gold packages. He ate that every so often at school, when the other kids didn't want their sandwiches. One thing he would NEVER come to understand... how a kid could just decide he didn't want to eat. It made no sense to Aaron. He shook his head, realizing he was off on another tangent in his mind. Aaron turned around, and faced Ryan, who shot him a look of pure joy. The corners of Ryan's mouth were trembling... about to laugh, cry, smile, something. Aaron sort of shrugged as though he wanted to know what to get. Ryan pointed quickly at the ham, and then put his hand right back behind his back. With a nod, Aaron turned back to the portly man. 'Sir, I will have some ham, please,' stated the 7 year old. The look on the man's face can be described only as shock. For a few seconds after the face began, Aaron thought he did something wrong, and looked warily at his mother. She too didn't understand the face, and began to worry that something had been said that shouldn't have. Her left cheek muscle flexed slightly, drawing her cheek up to her eye. She kind of shrugged. 'I'm sorry for my son, sir. I try to teach him manners... it's just that we don't get many chances to practice what he's supposed to say at times like these... because well, people don't really--' A gesture, waving off any worry from Nora's face, 'No, no, ma'am. Your son was a perfect gentlemen. That's just it. I don't get many pleases or thank yous around here. Just a lot of 'More please', and 'You gave him more than me!'' He smiles slightly, 'It makes the job worth it sometimes to hear those words.' His hand falls into his pocket, and produces two suckers. Blow-Pops. Aaron had seen them in school. Even had one once when he'd answered a question right in social studies. They were the mecca of candy, for sure. He hands one to both Aaron and Ryan, and shares a smile with the two little boys. Ryan's face is the picture of innocence. Though the older of the two, he's spent most of his life in bed, and hasn't experienced the world. His only contact to that world is Aaron. His only friend is Aaron. He looks in awe at the sucker, as it is placed in his hand. He looks at his mother to be sure it's ok to take, and she smiles at her eldest son. A small tear leaves a trail of moisture down her face, through all of the dirt. Her eyes, which have been locked on the floor, meet the man's eyes behind the table. He winks warmly, and her tears begin to flow more intensely. Ryan shoves the sucker into his pocket, and hops up to the counter, where he points at the ham. The server reaches for the fork to dish out the ham, and begins to shovel peices of the meat onto three seperate plates. The Roberts family takes their plates, and begins to move down the table where corn and mashed potatoes await them. As they go, Nora mouths the words 'Thank You' through her tears in the direction of the man. He just looks on at them, almost as though he's in awe of them, and then continues on about his business helping the next family...
