A website federation that ran weekly Glory and Maximum Velocity shows and named pay-per-views from August 2000 into late 2001, then returned in July 2002 with a weekly Glory and two specials, Genesis and Bad Moon Rising, until November 2002. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Five Star Wrestling was created on 23 July 2000, its own history page says, and aired its first show on a Tuesday, 1 August. The earliest card that survives is High Impact on 27 August 2000, staged from Washington’s MCI Center, where the announcers promised that “all the titles will be decided tonight” and seven championships were fought for in a strap match, a three-way ladder match, an iron-man tables match, a no-disqualification match, two four-corner matches and a two-out-of-three-falls main event.

The first run kept a Tuesday Glory, a Friday Maximum Velocity and a named pay-per-view most months, from Total Annihellation and Fallen in the autumn of 2000 to Love Hurts, Evil Intentions, Harsh Addictions and Futile Resistance in 2001. Its history page says it was dark only for the month of January 2001 and came back for a second term, a gap its title histories record as “FSW closes on a forgotten date, all titles stripped.” The last card of the run, Futile Resistance on 3 November 2001, ended with Blitz winning the World Championship in a three-way after a chair shot landed on Stefan Douglas. Twelve write-ups of the run survive; the rest of its shows exist as dates and title changes in its own histories, which close the run with every title “vacated as company closes for ten months.”

A second Five Star Wrestling opened on 3 July 2002 with Glory 1, “under new direction” in the words of its FAQ, as part of Primetime Central, which the FAQ calls “a collaboration of federations who work together for promotional purposes and fun.” A weekly Glory ran through Glory 20 on 27 November, with Genesis in August and Bad Moon Rising in October. The Dead or Alive tournament, played out on Glory in September and October by teams of two, was run to find a new World Champion, and at Bad Moon Rising Timo Bolamba beat his own tournament partner, Mike Marvelous, in the final.

Its second-run cards cut between titled backstage scenes and matches around a commentary desk, and sold their own roster in parody commercials: Azala Zameer at a pizza buffet, Afwa enraged by a grocery store out of his favourite pineapples. On the eve of Thanksgiving the CEO laid on a dinner for the whole roster, and Boda broke one of its turkeys over the head of a man at the Mob Rule table, where Tyler Nelson sat; Mob Rule was the faction the champion was fighting on its last card.

The last card, Glory 20 on 27 November 2002, came from Rupp Arena in Lexington, Kentucky. Bolamba kept the title over Chris Chase, Nelson sprang a contract on him for a title match the next week, and Bolamba answered by beating Nelson bloody and writing Samoan war emblems across his own chest in the blood. The card promised Glory from Conseco Fieldhouse in Indianapolis the next week and Trouble in Paradise seventeen days later; neither survives. The last roleplay is dated 2 December and the last news post 1 December, and no closing statement of its own survives.

Crossover booking with FSW (Five Star Wrestling, 2000–02) (2002)
PCW's Front Office challenged OSW, FSW and RUSH on 29 October 2002 for a spot in its Alliances show: "Four OSW members, four RUSH members and four FSW members to fight against or alongside the PCW roster." The Alliances card of 24 November 2002 stages Team FSW, four wrestlers from this federation's roster, and a PCW commentator calls Mob Rule "FSW's power stable".
