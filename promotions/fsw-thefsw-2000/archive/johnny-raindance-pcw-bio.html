Johnny Raindance Biography Real Name Jonathan Lee Mayweather Nicknames The Son of Thunderbird, JRD, Hurricane Johnny, the Bruce Lee of Wrestling Hometown Great Bull Reservation, South Dakota Birthday May 9, 1977 Height 6' Weight 225 Biography SIGNATURE SPOTS: · Powerdrill Dropkick: Standing on the top turnbuckle, Johnny pushes off with one arm and propels himself into a dropkick, making his body spin horizontally, like a drill, up to 540 degrees (one and one-half full turns.) · When backed-up in a corner for punches chops or such, Johnny switches places with his opponent and delivers several tomahawk chops before backing up, doing a Tatanka war-whoop and hitting a tomahawk missile. · When opponent is on top buckle, Johnny leaps up with him and hits a Frankensteiner. · JRD reverses a suplex/superplex in midair into neck breaker. · Rolls to his feet from a rolling thunder and right into standing moonsault. BIO: Johnny?s father is Stanley ?Plays-His-Ace? Mayweather, Chief of the Great Bull Sioux reservation. Busy with a successful casino (the first reservation Casino in South Dakota,) Stanley had little time for his son. Jon?s mother had died in childbirth, so her father, Raymond Strongbow, raised the boy. If that last name sounds familiar, it?s because Ray?s cousin, Chief Jay Strongbow was a successful WWWF wrestler. Ray would have joined Jay in a tag team had he not been paralyzed from the waist down by a drunk driver. Not one to let a little thing like a crippling injury keep him down, Ray opened a successful wrestling school on the reservation, a school that became his second priority, after taking care of his precocious grandson. Although he grew up around wrestling, Jon never took much interest in it aside from learning the basics one year as a lark. His youth was spent pursuing legitimate athletics and drama. He starred in several school plays and commercials while excelling in the martial arts, track and field and gymnastics. His hero growing up was Bruce Lee, whose brash, graceful charm Jon strove successfully to emulate. Around age sixteen, Jon also took interest in motorcycle culture and bike rallies, working through an entire year to buy his classic Harley road king. His performance in school never matching his dramatic and athletic displays, Jon was unable to get into a good college. Rather than waste his time at a junior college, he decided to go on an indefinite motorcycle tour of North America. This tour lasted two years, during which time Jon worked as a bouncer, martial arts instructor, and construction worker and even spent four months as a circus acrobat. But before the year was through, he was called back home to attend his Grandfather Ray?s deathbed. Ray?s last wish for his directionless grandchild was for Jon to become a professional wrestler. After a month of thinking, Jon was off to Mexico. Jon spent a year or so south of the border, learning the dynamic lucha libre style. Mixing lucha with the more dramatic American/Canadian wrestling style, Jon took the name Juanito Quetzo (Johnny Rain-Serpent) and became one of the biggest draws in Mexico. Jon?s next stop was Japan, where he wasted no time becoming a franchise player in NJPW. Like his hero Bruce Lee, who incorporated the world?s most effective fighting styles into one amazing whole, Johnny mixed the showmanship of North America with high-flying lucha and super-realistic Japanese wrestling to create a totally unique style. The Japanese announcers dubbed this style ?Johnny Tsunami? and the man ?the Bruce Lee of wrestling.? After Japan, Jon headed to the American indies, where he took the name ?Johnny Raindance? and caused a sensation in the wrestling community with ?Tsunami? wrestling. He spent a few years traveling between Mexico, America, Canada and Japan, ruling the indy wrestling scene in whichever country he was in at the time. Ironically, it was Johnny?s charm as an actor and not his skill as a wrestler that got him his first major North American contract. While working security for a Pepsi commercial, JRD was noticed by Britney Spears, who suggested he be written into the spot. The Pepsi commercial was only the first of a long string which netted Johnny both his long-time girlfriend Alyssa Milano and his first PCW contract. But success in PCW was not to be for JRD. Inundated with offers for fame and fortune in Hollywood and frustrated by PCW's backstage politics, JRD left the orginzation several times to pursue his acting career. The pursuit was successful, landing Johnny guest spots on Friends, ER, Dark Angel, Alias, Sex and the City and an entire season of Oz, not to mention a steady stream of successful commercials and a cameo in the second Matrix film. Recently, however, Johnny has become burnt-out with Hollywood life. He made a few calls, said a few good-byes, got on his Hog and rode right into FSW's main office, demanding a meeting with the CEO. A deal was quickly struck, and now, for better or worse, Johnny Raindance is back in the business with which his name is so commonlly associated. Other Feds PCW, WWE Other Fed Awards NJPW Heavyweight Champion. Has won just about every indy title in the US and Mexico. Wrestling Style A unique, innovative martial/arts high-flying hybrid called 'Johnny Tsunami' Finisher Move Thundercall Finisher Description Opponent stands woozy mid-ring. Johnny leaps off of top buckle, flips over in midair, grabs opponent?s head in his extended hands and drives it into the ground when he lands Finisher Setup Move Obsidian Arrow Setup Description Samurai Driver Special Moves Various martial arts punches and kicks, tomahawk chop, monkey-flip, catapult, bulldog (arm extended,) spinning heel kick, hurricanrana, tornado DDT, Frankensteiner, Canoe Drop, Atomic Drop, neck-breaker, standing suplex, Step-over-inzeguri, Sunset flip, rolling thunder, split-legged moonsault, standing moonsault, moonsault, flying body press, Snuka Body Press, dropkick, missile dropkick, spring-board dropkick, springboard back-elbow, Tajiri hand-spring elbow, tomahawk missile.
