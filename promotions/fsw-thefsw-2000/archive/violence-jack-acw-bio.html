Violence Jack Biography Real Name Bruce Shanahan Nicknames God of Extreme; Boston's Bastard Son; Baron of Brutality Hometown Boston, Massachusetts Birthday August 8, 1968 Height 6' Weight 234 Biography Bruce Shanahan lived in a rough slum-region of Boston, Massachusetts. Knowing very little of his family, Bruce grew to respect the codes of brotherhood and loyalty and involved himself in a very tightknit network of friends that became his street allies. It was here that the seeds were planted that would someday spawn his personality trademark: hatred, rage and a volatile mental state. Violence, treachery, and loss were his most common bedfellows and he grew to especially detest those more fortunate in society. At age 20, Bruce collected what little savings he had from his dead-end factory job and used it to travel to Japan. He did this in order to make money and gain a reputation doing what he did best in life: fighting. He entered the notorious deathmatch wrestling circuit of Japan, slowly gaining renown for his uncanny threshold of pain and incredible stamina while waging war night-in and night-out in conditions that would cause the common 'extreme' wrestler to have nightmares. It was sometime during this period that Bruce Shanahan garnered his now-recognized moniker of 'Violence Jack', in tribute to a Japanese anime character of the same name. As for exactly when or how this new nickname was christened upon the scarred native of the USA, nothing is known. Having built a solid reputation around the Orient for his savagery and ruthless nature, Bruce 'Violence Jack' Shanahan returned to the States and entered the American independent wrestling circuit. He spent a couple years toiling across the continent entertaining fans with his rage-filled speeches and in-ring fury. Jack entered several promotions full-time over a period of 5+ years and won a plethora of accolades nationwide. At about this time, Jack and his ICW tag team partner, Chris Havenstorm, were accepted into the WWA federation of Primetime Central as a prominent tag team. Both men also entered separately in the 2nd annual Global Title Tournament as virtual unknowns. V-Jack, an outspoken nobody predicted to fall early without making so much as a scratch in the competition, defeated a rising star of the NTICW company, followed by the soon-to-be Cruiserweight champion of Primetime Championship Wrestling, and then destroyed one of the preliminary tournament favorites the following round. WWA closed shortly thereafter, and when the promotion talent were released as free agents, V-Jack was picked up and given a contract by Primetime Championship Wrestling. It was during this tournament that a new chapter was written in the life of Bruce Shanahan. He was haunted by a series of bleak, grisly revelations during his sleep. Finding conventional medication to be ineffective, Bruce sought out religious counsel by going to his old friend who had been called into the ministry in the Catholic Church. What were originally thought to be visions sent by the supernatural Vampir Nosferatu were believed to be sourced from something far more sinister. Father Randall Craven surmised that Bruce was under a psychic assault by a throng of malevolent demigods spawned from beyond the reaches of known space and fearfully spoken of in tomes of occult origin. These beings, coined the Great Old Ones, were said to hold great power to the point they were touched with demigod status. They reigned on dismal worlds across the cosmos, expansion and power limited by both their foes and a complacent disposition, with several said to be slumbering on Earth itself awaiting the day of their resurrection. Bruce joined an elite underground society/brotherhood called the Blackhelm Coalition, a secretive faction chartered with the intention of preventing the imprisoned semi-deities from awakening and destroying mankind, and thwarting their deranged cults in the process. The connection Bruce had to these beasts was unknown, though one appeared obvious as their pursuing of his soul and mind eclipsed the possibility of his targeting being sheerly random. The reasoning came out later on, and their inconspicuous clash continued across Earth under the veil of conventional military operations and experiments and even terrorist-type attacks. Somewhere along this line, Bruce went over the edge. Seeing the universal horrors of what lies beyond healthy human knowledge took a toll on the already-maddening man from Boston and he was committed to an asylum in upstate New York. When he was inexplicably released by doctors who had only days before claimed he was likely to never escape insanity, Bruce's demeanor had obviously changed. What he had previously stated to fight to his death, he now worshipped at the foot of obscene altars and read of in blasphemous books. Bruce started a vile cult for the reason that (as he said) 'was decreed on me by the Crawling Chaos himself, the Black Pharaoh, Nyarlathotep.' PCW, disturbed and caught off-guard by this extreme shift of attitude, released Jack from his contract with a nationally-televised stunt using a lookalike of the infamous 'God of Extreme'. Contacted almost immediately by Steven Caldera of Five Star Wrestling, Bruce agreed to come to the federation to help Caldera fulfill his greed-inspired wishes. A new chapter is now written in the legacy of Bruce 'Violence Jack' Shanahan as he arrives in the territory of FSW. Here to help his associate Caldera win gold and power, Bruce also aims to spread his new gospel of 'the Old Ones' to all who will willingly listen. And crush all whom deny his words as truth, imparting his wisdom upon their broken bodies. Whether Bruce is truly insane or whether there is a degree of reality to his hideous beliefs is a topic under scrutiny. And it's one Bruce claims 'shall be revealed when the stars are right and Great Cthulhu rises from his undersea temple to revel again.' Other Feds WWIWF; CIWF; GWA; ICW; GWO; WWA; PCW Other Fed Awards Countless independent federation awards; PCW Extreme champion; Sweet 16 of 2nd 128-man Global Title Tournament; Elite 8 of 256-man Golden Turnbuckle Tournament; May and June WT polls #51 and #34 respectively Wrestling Style Combination of brawling and hardcore, coupled with his incredibly high threshold of pain sustained after years of involvement in the Japanese deathmatch circuit. With a direct and brutal wrestling style involving a hybrid of street fighting, weapon usage Finisher Move Detonator (w/ several variations) & R'lyeh Anthem Finisher Description 1) Detonator - Modified hangman's neckbreaker. Jack has mastered the execution of this technique to the point he has created several variations, making it possible to land the maneuver from virtually any position. He has codenamed these unique variations. Special Moves Violentor - V-Jack hurls a chair at his opponent. Instinctually they catch the incoming object, and VJ shoots out a leg and superkicks the chair back into their jaw. The Last Resort - Named after Jack's favorite bar back in Boston, V-Jack locks on a cobra clutch from behind. He then laucnhes the opponent over his head, dropping them on their head and neck in a brutal cobra clutch suplex. Osaka Send-off - Jack gets behind his opponent and drops to a sitting position, uses his full force and weight to spike the back of their head into the mat, floor, etc. Black-Jack - VJ lifts his opponent up as if for a body slam, then abruptly sits down and drops them on their head in a Michinoku Driver. Jack-Hammer - Jack slings one arm across the opponent's chest, resting their jaw on his shoulder. He then leaps slightly into the air and lands in a sitting position, jarring the jaw of the victim on his shoulder in a shoulder jawbreaker. The Carlisle Legacy - Named after Jack's best friend who was gunned down at the age of 19 on the Boston streets. This move is rarely performed, as the potential for injury is great. Typically only performed as a final trump card attack or with the intention of inflicting injury on a hated enemy. Jack sits atop the top rope and executes a double-arm tornado DDT, bringing his opponent down at an unusual angle on their head and neck. Entrance Music Metallica - 'Call of Ktulu'
