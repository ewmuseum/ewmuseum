FSW — Card

Glory 3, 17 July 2002

17 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Darkness. Slowly one single red light begins to flare, silhouetting the profile of a sharp-featured man staring off camera. His most striking feature, his aquiline nose jutting out from in between two dark eyes, half hidden by the brim of a six-gallon cowboy hat. The camera slowly rotates to face the man head on so that half of his face is shrouded in darkness, the other peering slyly into the light. The man is none other than FSW’s legendary veteran, Chief Executive Officer Paul Orion. More commonly known as The Gunslinger. Orion slowly smiles and dips his head. As his carved chin touches his neck “Orion” by Metallica begins to play. The screen fades into grainy black and white clips of Paul Orion in action. Orion: I’ve been here since the beginning. While your mothers were getting their first training bras, hell, before your fathers had even considered the act of conceiving you, I was paying my dues in wrestling rings all across the country. The camera flashes between venues nationwide, from the old American Wrestling Association in the Midwest, to the National Wrestling Alliance in the south, to Pro Wrestling USA. Orion: I hear a lot of you guys’ talk about “the greats. “The veterans”. You guys don’t know shit. You talk about Bob Backlund. I’M the one who discovered him back in Minnesota where he was a four time All-American as an amateur. The screen shows Bob Backlund winning the WWF World Title from “Superstar” Billy Graham in Madison Square Garden, underneath which reads the caption: “1978,” then losing it to the Iron Sheik in MSG in 1983. Orion: You talk about the Von Erich brothers. The tragedy of Kerry, Kevin, and David. Well you know something? I was wrestling their dad Fritz long before they were the apples of his eye. Long before Kerry defeated Ric Flair for the NWA title. The camera shows highlights from the Ric Flair vs. Kerry Von Erich match in 1984. Orion: You talk about Snuka. You talk about his legendary in ring work. Who do you think taught him that? Who do you think developed the flying body press? That was me. The camera shows Snuka leaping off the top of a cage onto his prone opponent with the dreaded “Superfly Splash”. Orion: All of you young guys think you know about the veterans. Rick Steamboat, Paul Orndorff, The Freebirds. But you know something? They all had veterans they looked up to. And you know who those veterans were? Guys like Antonino Rocca. Scenes of Paul Orion battling Rocca play. Orion: Guys like Bruno Sammartino. Paul Orion pressing Bruno above his head flash on screen. Orion: Guys like Lou Thesz, guys like Gorgeous George. I’ve fought them all. None of you have done what I’ve done. NONE of you have seen what I’ve seen… The screen quickly flashes between clips of Orion delivering high impact move after high impact move until the screen goes black and the music stops. The words “The Gunslinger” Paul Orion appear at the top of the screen. A solitary black hat sits beneath the words. At the bottom of the screen, CEO of FSW fade in. Orion: Because I’ve seen it all kids… I’ve seen it all. Jack Brooze is entering the arena as the scene fades to the backstage area. Wearing a contented smile and a confident swagger in his step, he is obviously pleased with his night off from wrestling as he will be this week’s guest commentator. Unfortunately, Brooze’s entrance does not go unnoticed as he is confronted by Leon Chambers whom is holding a steel chair in his right hand. Leon: Hey Brooze, I got a message for ya! Brooze stops in his footsteps and looks around him, cautiously observing his surroundings, expecting to be ambushed. Jack: Oh yeah, from who? Leon: Listen Brooze, don’t be so agitated, if I was going to hit you with this chair, I would do it from behind. Its low I know. But, hey, at least I’m honest. Jack: Just get to the point, I haven’t got all day. Leon walks over to Brooze and looks approvingly at the size of the man. He tilts his head back as far as it will go, just so he can see into the eyes of the big man. Leon: Join us Jack, join me, Big Daveylicious, Mike Marvellous and EOE. All of us, the stars of this company, we have to stick together in order to keep out the jackasses who claim heirs to our throne at the top of the FSW mountain. I don’t usually do this but I am pleading with you to help us rid ‘our home’ of these idiotic losers. Leon gets down on his knee as Jack Brooze appears to ponder on Leon’s proposition before giving his trademark answer. Jack: Not Freakin’ Likely! With one arm, Brooze moves Leon from his path and proceeds to his locker room. Leon is left looking confused – how can anyone reject such an offer? Leon: He’ll come around… guaranteed. The scene cuts in to a raucous Boston crowd inside the jam-packed Fleet Center. The camera focuses on a group of men at ringside with slicked back hair, black suits, and sunglasses. The obvious Angelo Deville “supporters” survey their surroundings, and when one notices the camera’s interest in them, he nods for it to turn away. To avoid any unpleasantries, he is obliged as the camera shifts to a young man holding up a sign which reads “I’m the Mystery Partner” obviously since child labor laws would prevent the young man from working this late at night, negating his claim, the shot lands on tonight’s FSW announce team, Lynn Lane, Sam Collins, and tonight’s guest announcer, Jack Brooze. Lynn: Hello everyone and welcome to this edition of FSW Glory! We are live from a soldout Boston Fleet Center and what a night of action we have planned for you tonight! Among other things, we have tonight’s main-event, where we see the return of “Five-Star” Mike Marvelous to in-ring action, and Timo Bolamba and Johnny Raindance will give it another go-round! Sam: And everyone is abuzz with who Mike’s mystery partner will be! Jack: Hopefully it’s a guy who, like me, can take care of business, and not some goon who’s going to crack a bunch of corny jokes and ruin the sacred name of FSW. Sam: I’m going to take offense to that as a favor to Ming! Jack: Bah! Ming can kiss my a- Lynn: AND to move along, we also will see the said Ming in action tonight against Lo-Key, as well as “Bad Attitude” Jesse Ramey and Asa “Fro” Fountain against a tag team. Jack: What tag team? Sam: You’ll have to wait and find out moron. Jack: You better watch yourself tonight Collins. Lynn: This could be a loooong night. Sam: So Jack, I really have to ask. Are you mentally deficient? Why did you turn down Chambers? Joining your fellow former FSWers would have been the best move of your career! Jack: They are ruining the name of FSW Collins. I’m here to carry the torch of its respect. Those chumps can do what they want, but I’ve got my own way of carrying out business. Lynn: If you two can zip it for a second, we’ve got Robin in the back waiting to talk with Ming. Robin? Jack: You mean that idiot's still in the building with a match scheduled against Lo-Key? Sam: Oh come on, it's all a huge misunderstanding. Lo-Key just hasn't soaked up enough of Ming's greatness to get it yet. The camera switches to the back and finds Ming pacing and muttering, rehearsing his words. Robin Nicely clears her throat and speaks first. Robin: Ming, How do you like your chances in this match with Lo-Key? Ming: Chances! I don't care about my chances. This is a big mistake! I got nothing but love for my boy Lo-Key! Robin: Well, after last week's interference, he doesn't seem to have any for you. Ming: That's what I don't understand! I tried to give him a hand and it worked great! Robin: You don't think he's mad because he doesn't think he needed your help? Ming: Oh no no, of course he didn't need my help. But hey, it's the thought that counts, right? Right? Robin: Uh, right. So what's your strategy? Ming: Nothing but to try and get things cleared up. I just want Lo-Key to understand that his old pal Ming only has his best interest at heart. We're like two peas in a pod, flip sides of the same coin! Like peanut butter and jelly we belong together! Unfortunately these words escape Ming just as Lo-Key walks up behind him. Lo-Key: You belong about as far from me as you can get you little cretin! Ming whirls and grins cheesily. Ming: Lo-Key, old buddy, old... ...[POW] Ming hits the floor and stammers while Lo-Key goes after him. Ming: Anybody.... get the number on that truck...? Ming gets to his feet and runs, with Lo-Key close behind. Ming: Wait! Can't we talk about thiiiiiiiiiiiiiis? As Ming hightails it, the camera switches to the parking area. A huge limo has just pulled up, and stopped just outside of the arena. Lynn: Could that be Mike Marvelous’s mystery partner arriving? Jack: Another lamewad who’s afraid to get his hands dirty in the ring? Why are we even covering this? The plates read “Alpha”. One of the doors in the very end of the limo swings open, and out walks Magnum Wolf. Jack: Oh geeze, it’s Wolf. An even bigger lamewad than I had anticipated. He pulls the sunglasses off of his face, and looks down at his watch. With a very serious face, he looks at the man who has come out to try to get an interview. Magnum: I’m not late, am I? Reporter: Late for what, Mr. Wolf? You have the week off. Magnum: What do you mean I have the week off? They didn’t add me to a match? They didn’t stick me into the card somewhere? They’re having a Glory without Magnum F’N Wolf? Reporter: That’s the long and short of it. Magnum’s eyes narrow, and his right hand goes about even with his head in a motion that can only read as “What the hell is wrong with you?!” He angrily slams the limo’s door, and heads to the trunk. As he rummages around, and pulls out a bag full of wrestling gear, he looks at the camera with disdain. Magnum: After all I’ve done for FSW. After all the work I’ve put into this place… everything that I’ve done as far as PR work for them. The match that I put on where I drug Jack Brooze’s lazy ass around a ring for 15 minutes… and now they decide to reward me by just… LEAVING ME OFF OF A CARD?! Reporter (meekly): Maybe it has something to do with your involvement with that other federation, Mr. Wolf. Wolf stares down the much smaller man. Magnum: Who the hell told you about that?” Reporter: I… I… heard it… somewhere. I don’t know… Wolf tosses the little guy to the ground, and slams the trunk of his limo. He spits in the direction of the reporter, and storms off towards the building. He comes to a huge metal door, and nearly kicks it off its hinges. The scene fades back to ringside. Jack: I REALLY don’t like that guy. Lynn: What’s his deal? It’s not that reporter’s fault that he’s been running around working for other federations on the side! Sam: Now, see, that’s how rumors get started, Lynn! You don’t know what Magnum was doing with EOW. Maybe he was just using them as an advertising tool for FSW! Lynn: The only tool around here is you. Jack: I’ll show him just what FSW is all about, if he thinks it’s going to be that easy for him to get to the top. I’ve already shown him what’s what once in the ring, I can do it again! Sam: Yeah, and then he kicked the bejeebers out of you. Jack: Shut up Collins. Lynn: Ugh, without further ado let’s head to the ring for tonight’s opening match. Willy, take it away. Willy: Our opening contest is a singles matchup scheduled for one fall! Introducing first, from Kemptville, Ontario... Weighing in at 259 lbs... Angelooooooooooooo Devilllllllle! Sympathy for the Devil begins coursing through the arena as Angelo Deville steps out onto the stage amidst a cacophony of boos from the crowd. He pauses as the top of the ramp for a moment, soaking in the hate with a mockingly perplexed look, then saunters down to the ring and slides in. He walks slowly around the ring with his arms outstretched to his sides, as if basking in cheers, until the music stops. Then he turns his attention to the stage. Willy: And his opponent... Hailing from Quebec City, Quebec... And also weighing in at 259 lbs... Chriiiiiiiiiiiiis Irrrrrrviiiiiiiiiing! Sam: Wow... Not only are they both from Canada, they weigh the same? Lynn: ...Canada... tsk. Jack: Lynn! Lynn: What? Jack: You’re a face, remember? Lynn: Oh... Right. The sound of the Rolling Stones is replaced by Eminem as Criminal blasts through the loudspeakers and the words “Canada Rules” appears on the jumbotron. The Canadian Criminal Chris Irving stalks out onto the stage and casts a disapproving eye on the American crowd. They respond in the most convenient way possible: with boos. Irving shakes his head as he walks down to ringside, pausing once or twice to exchange words with a “fan”. He climbs into the ring and begins mounting the turnbuckle, but just as gets his first foot on the rope he’s clotheslined from behind by Deville. The music abruptly stops and the referee calls for the bell. Lynn: And of course, Deville starts us off cheaply. Sam: By any means necessary, Lynn. Jack: Aye aye, Faarooq. Irving collapses face-first into the corner as Deville pummels him with kicks to the back and legs. The referee puts himself between the two wrestlers and makes Deville back off, earning him a few curt words and the threat of a backhand. Sam: That ref better remember who he’s talking to! Jack: Yeah... Deville is making a career out of dealing with FSW’s non-wrestling staff. Deville moves back in and grabs Irving’s arm, then twists it up over his head and back down. Irving begins slapping his shoulder in pain, dancing around as Deville yanks down on the joint. Without warning, Irving suddenly does a complete flip and lands on his feet, untwisting his arm. Before Deville can react, he finds himself tossed over Irving with an armdrag. The Canadian Criminal quickly converts the move into an armbar submission. Lynn: Nice armbar by Irving there. Sam: He’s got a good technical background. ...For a Canadian. The referee asks Deville is he’d like to give up and has to dodge an angry swat from the man’s free hand. After only a few seconds Irving releases the hold and jumps to his feet, backing off and looking at Deville smugly. Deville clambers to his feet and charges forward, but the Criminal is too quick, executing a drop toe hold that makes Deville faceplant into the mat. Irving leans against the ropes as if bored and pretends to dust himself off. Jack: This is gonna cost him... Never give an opponent breathing room; it’s not a smart move. Sam: He’s not a smart man. Jack: After his comments about my home two weeks ago, I’m inclined to agree. Who does he think he is? Sam: Why don’t you go find out? As Deville begins climbing to his feet, Irving springs back into action and sets him up for a vertical suplex. He goes to lift him into the air, but Deville blocks it. Tries again… blocked. Grabbing hold of the other man and letting out a roar, Deville reverses the move into an ugly looking backdrop. Like a rabid animal, Deville rushes in and stomps away at Irving’s neck. Lynn: C’mon ref! What is Deville doing? Putting out a fire on Irving’s neck? After seven or eight vicious boots to his throat, Deville reaches down and grabs Irving’s arm, then viciously yanks him to his feet. Maintaining the momentum, he whips the kid into the ropes and sets himself up for the return. Irving bounces and comes charging back, and Deville catches him as he goes by and executes a tilt-a-whirl backbreaker. Dazed, Irving climbs up to his hands and knees as Deville squats down in front of him and talks a little shit. Irving throws out a fist to punch Deville in the face, but the latter grabs the arm and flips him over with three quarters of a gator roll, then arches his back into a bridge. The referee slides in to count. 1… 2… …Shoulder up! Sam: Oh! Just barely! Jack: What the hell was that move? Lynn: Don’t you follow amateur wrestling, Jack? Jack: Uh... No. Irving manages to wriggle one arm free and throw up a shoulder in the nick of time. Deville jumps to his feet and begins arguing with the ref about the speed of the count. Irving takes advantage of the break and climbs to his feet. Jack: ...Rookies... Sam: They’re not rookies! Jack: To me they are. Lynn: Actually... They’ve both had considerable success in the indy circuit. Jack: Yeah well... Welcome to the big leagues. Deville turns around and Irving cracks him in the jaw, then does it again, and again, pummelling him back into the ropes. As Deville tries to clear the cobwebs, Irving grabs his arm and tries to whip him across the ring, but finds himself heading towards the ropes as the move is reversed. Irving comes running back and Deville drives him to the mat with an Oklahoma slam, then rolls him up for the pin. 1… Sam: He’s got him this time for sure! 2… …Kickout! Lynn: Irving is more resilient than you might think... He can take a beating. Sam: What? I thought he was French... Lynn: So what? Sam: Sometimes you amaze me, Lynn. Irving kicks out and begins climbing to his feet. Deville gets to his feet and is about to continue the assault when he’s distracted by something on stage. The crowd cheers as Jason O’Neil sidles out onto the stage and stands in the center of it, looking down at the ring with supposed indifference. Sam: What the hell is he doing here? Lynn: I guess he doesn’t consider his business with Deville finished! Jack: Perhaps... But this isn’t the time to sort it out. Deville turns his back on Irving and takes a few steps in O’Neil’s direction, pointing a threatening finger and mouthing what one can only assume is the verbalization of those threats. O’Neil flashes a knowing smile as Irving comes from behind and nails Deville with an inverted DDT. Deville looks to be out cold. Irving goes for the cover. Lynn: I think he broke his neck! Jack: That looked... Bad. Sam: Nice word... You’re like a thesaurus over there. Jack: Quiet. 1… 2… …Shoulder Up! Sam: He’s alive! One shoulder inches off the canvas a millisecond before the referee’s hand would’ve hit. Irving climbs to his feet, frustrated, and glares at O’Neil for a moment. O’Neil squats down and continues to watch the match from the stage, so Irving shrugs and turns his attention to Deville. Jack: Why is O’Neil just standing there? Lynn: Scouting the competition? Sam: Trying to get TV time? With arrogant ease, Irving pulls Deville to his feet and gives him a little slap in the face. Deville slumps backward into the ropes and Irving laughs, then struts forward and gives him another playful slap. He looks around at the crowd with a big grin on his face as Deville rests listlessly before him. Jack: He’s having too good of a time... Letting his guard down. Sam: Deville will make him pay...! For America! Lynn: Deville’s Canadian, Sam. Sam: Yeah but... He knows better. Irving goes to give Deville another punch, but Deville suddenly comes to life, hooking his arm and nailing him with the Tommy Gun. After several shots to the back of the neck, Deville releases the arm, letting Irving stumble forward and fall to a knee. Deville begins moving in, but then remembers O’Neil standing up on the stage. With a scowl, he once again turns his back on his opponent and begins moving towards the ramp. Jack: Not in the ring he doesn’t. O’Neil shakes his head and rises to a standing position as Deville leans over the top rope, yelling at O’Neil to get the hell out of there. Irving seems pleasantly surprised by Deville’s refusal to concentrate on the match and wastes no time in taking advantage. He pulls Deville down from the ropes and spins him around. Before the look of surprise can even replace the anger on Deville’s face, Irving picks him up for a flapjack, then turns it into a flip snap powerbomb. Lynn: Ay-Bomb! Ay-Bomb! Jack: Eh? O’Neil turns and disappears backstage as Irving goes for the cover. The ref slides in for the count. Lynn: It’s over! 1… 2… …3! Winner: “The Canadian Criminal” Chris Irving Lynn: He got him! Irving wins! Sam: That sucks... Deville let America down. Lynn: What the? They’re both Canadian, damnit. The referee calls for the bell as Chris Irving jumps to his feet and triumphantly raises his arms. Criminal explodes through the arena once more and is met by a chorus of boos. Ignoring the barely moving Deville, Irving climbs out of the ring and begins sauntering slowly up the ramp, basking in the aftermath of another victory. Jack: Ha! Maybe if Deville were more concerned with “The Canadian Criminal” than Jason O’Neil he wouldn’t have gotten his ass handed to him! Sam: That’s not fair! O’Neil didn’t even interfere and give Deville the chance to lay waste to him! He just stood there like a scarecrow! Lynn: Whatever he was doing, he did what he set out to do as Chris Irving picks up the win over Angelo Deville! Sam: Somebody get that meddler! Jack: Alright... Can it, guys. We’ve gotta go to commercial! Lynn: What the? That’s my gig, Brooze! FSW Genesis - Sunday, August 18th, live and only on Pay-Per-View Lynn: We’re back, and next up…oh, um, ladies and gentlemen, we have something going on outside. Apparently Timo Bolamba has just shown up and- Sam: Pfft. Who cares? Lynn: I care, and I’m sure Jack cares. They both look at Jack for and answer and he shrugs. Sam: See? He doesn’t care either! Now maybe if Mike Marvelous were involved we would be in business! Lynn sighs. Lynn: Regardless of that fact, lets take it to the back for the straight action! The camera feed goes to the back, where Timo is walking to his dressing room. He stops abruptly, and looks up, the camera following his gaze to see none other than Johnny Raindance. The two stare at one another for several moments before Timo tries to speak. Timo: Johnny, you and I have never sat well, but even you cant be so dense as to think I hit you last week. Johnny stands as stiff as a mountain, his only reaction being a quick harumph. Timo: Come on man, you need to look at the big picture here. I don’t like it any more than you do, but we can’t keep trying to kill each other when we are teamed up can we? Johnny squints and leans forward, getting directly in Timo’s face. The Samoan looks back in his eyes, both men practically ready to jump down one anothers throats. Johnny: You might fool some people…but until you can prove it wasn’t you, I don’t want to talk to you. It’s better this way anyhow, that we don’t like one another. I don’t want to have to keep carrying you. Timo snorts and the two actually almost butt heads before Johnny backs off and walks away in the other direction. The camera pans in on Timo and he nods. Timo: You want proof? I will get you proof. Timo walks off down the hall towards the production truck, and the feed feed cuts elsewhere backstage, to find Magnum Wolf storming into the office of “The Gunslinger” Paul Orion. He throws down the bag of wrestling gear he grabbed out of his trunk, and sits down in a chair right across from “The Gunslinger”. Paul looks at Wolf with a sort of smirk on his face, and Magnum likes it not even a little bit. Wolf’s leg is jumping up and down, and he keeps shaking his head back and forth… the agitation is palpable. Paul finally breaks the silence. Paul: What can I do for you today, Mr. Wol--- Magnum: Why aren’t I on the card? Paul: Well, that’s really quite simple, Mr. Wolf. We’ve got a limited amount of time, and a big roster. We’ve got to give everyone a shot at TV time. Magnum: I bet you wouldn’t say that to Big Daveylicious. I bet you wouldn’t say that to Timo Bolamba. I know damn well you wouldn’t say that to Epitome of Evil. So why are you saying it to me, Paul? Why are you telling Magnum Wolf that he gets no face time? I think I know. Paul: Oh you do, do you? Magnum: Yea, I do, smart-ass. Because I don’t have a big enough name in your mind. The world of Wrestling Tattler doesn’t know exactly who Magnum Wolf is, so you don’t give enough of a damn about him to put him on your card. You’d rather put old, low work rate, lazy guys into matches just because they happen to have a big name here in the world you’re working in. Forget that Magnum Wolf is a better wrestler. Forget that Magnum Wolf is more charismatic. Forget that, if given a chance, I could beat any one of those men without breaking so much as a sweat. Forget all of that, Paul. ‘Cause your name is all that matters, right? Paul: Magnum, I really think you’re missing the point here. Magnum: No. I really don’t think I’m missing anything. FSW is going to be my federation. FSW will be the place that I make. I will be the workhorse for your federations…make you millions. And it won’t be any harder than falling off of a log. Once you realize that there’s more to a wrestler than face value, Magnum Wolf will be back at the top of your list. I just hope you figure it all out sooner than later, Mr. Orion. I’m willing to make your promotion as big, or bigger than OSW or PCW… or any other place out there. All you have to do is trust me to do just that. Paul: You have to understand something, here, WOLF. You’re walking in here a smoking gun. You were FIRED from LCW for misconduct. You’re rumored to be running around with some other federation from God knows where when you know DAMN WELL you’re under contract here. How do I know you’re not going to pull some stunts like that again?! Right now, FSW needs those big names, in spite of what you think. You do like you’ve said here today, and prove yourself to me, and you won’t have any trouble finding yourself matches. Magnum: You know what? It’s not even about a freaking match! Just having me on the show adds value to your product! Heck, this argument right now is pulling the shows ratings up! Even if you don’t book me, you could still put me as the third commentator. Paul: Thank you Magnum, but Jack Brooze is commentator this week. Magnum: Jack Brooze!?!?! That no talent bore? I’ve got more appeal in my left pinky finger than him! I thought by interrupting his matches with my enthralling interviews I had done FSW a favor, but I guess not. I guess it’s up to me now to do things on my own. Magnum pushes the chair he is sitting in over, as he stands up. He turns abruptly, and walks calmly out of the room. “The Gunslinger” watches him go with a shake of his head. The scene cuts back to ringside. Lynn: What does that mean? Sam: Hell if I know… but it sure was great watching Mr. Orion sweat! Hehe! Lynn: I hope he fires you. Sam: Bah! My contract is iron-clad. Jack: Can I test to see if your face is? Lynn: Nevertheless, we’re about ready for our next match for the evening, where Jesse Ramey and Asa Fountain will be taking on A Tag Team. Sam: What the hell tag team is it? Lynn: I have word we are going to the back. There are two figures standing in cloudy room of smoke. The mist rises ominously, covering the two figures, who are apparently tonight’s mystery tag team. Voice 1: Tonight we *Cough* make our debut here homes. Voice 2: Here? Where are we? Voice 1: We are at Six-Star Wrestling. Voice 2: Six Stars? *Cough* Voice 1: Uhmm, well it might have been Five. I forgot Voice 2: Man I just....*Cough, Cough, Cough* *The Man falls out coughing of the cloudy smoke* Voice 1: Awwww man, you ruined it!! Here we were being all mysterious and shit, what with the smoke and all, creeping everyone out, and you had to go and ruin it all! Pablo: Sorry...hey Fran what are we called? Francisco: Well I’m Francisco Fumar and your Pablo Diablo duh! Pablo: But don’t we need a team name? Francisco: Oh, um, I think we’re called A Tag Team. Pablo: A Tag Team? Francisco: Yeah. Pablo: Now pass that shit over here. Fran-Man passes the blunt over to Pablo Francisco: Well c'mon man, lets just go to the ring. A Tag Team gets up and walks out the door as the scene cuts back to the announce desk. Sam: THOSE two are our mystery tag team? Lynn: Heh, apparently they took their name from the booking sheet. Since they were told they were going to wrestling against Ramey and Fountain, that meant their name was “A Tag Team.” Jack: You’ve gotta be kidding me. “Hillbilly Rock” by Marty Stewart begins to crack out of the arena’s sound system, letting every Five Star Wrestling fan know exactly who is about to appear in front of their very eyes. Orange and white strobe lights begin to flash on and off, mixing with each other and creating the perfect hue for the entrance of the superstar. Showing up on the entrance stage, a confident stare emerging on his face, “Bad Attitude” Jesse Ramey stands in awe … absorbing the admiration of the sold out crowd. Willy: Ladies and gentleman! Weighing in at two hundred and five pounds, coming to you all straight from Harts, West Virginia! The master of The Attitude Adjustment! He is “Bad Attitude” Jesse Ramey! The fans begin to cheer even louder following the introduction from ring announcer Willy Wilson, as Jesse Ramey takes a historic walk down the ramp way, pausing right at ringside and allowing the fabled FSW announcers to add their take. Sam: I knew something smelled … I just thought the people here were rednecks. Lynn: What type of person he is doesn’t matter Sam … and he isn’t that bad of one to begin with. It’s what kind of athlete he is… Jack: A drunk one… Lynn: Excuse me? Guest commentator Jack Brooze was right. Confident or not, Ramey stands at ringside barely standing. Shaking his head and jumping around, trying to let go of his obvious intoxication, Ramey knows that his number one goal is to finish this match with a victory. But against a mystery team that he knows nothing about … can a win be accomplished while smashed? Sam: I wonder what would happen if we stuck a pin in his stomach. My cup needs a refill. Ignoring the snide remarks, Lynn and Brooze look to the ramp way as “Play That Funky Music” by Wild Cherry replaces “Hillbilly Rock” … signifying the entrance of the most psychedelic hip cat in Five Star Wrestling, Asa Fountain.
