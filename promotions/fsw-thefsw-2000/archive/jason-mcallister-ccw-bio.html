Jason MCAllister Biography Real Name Jason McAllister Nicknames J-Mac, Highway Hometown Iowa City, Iowa Birthday May 31st, 1968 Height 5' 10' Weight 242 Biography Jason McAllister was born the son of an Iowa farmer and grew up learning the values of hard work and determination. An avid athlete in high school. He was the pride of his family when he became the first McAllister to go to college, attending the University of Iowa on a wrestling scholarship. During Jason's sophmore year, he went on to become a national champion at 177. He was excelling in his classes and generally living the American dream. It was all to come crashing down. NCAA watchdogs accused the U of I of violations of Title IX. In a defensive reaction the school did a wholesale chopping of men's scholarships in 'minor' sports (ie, anything other than football or basketball) Jason's scholarship and that of several other wrestler's were pulled in a political gambit by the university that is to the this day called 'unforgivable' by then coach Dan Gable. Jason continued to pursue his family's dream that he graduate from college, but the continued costs were more than the family could bear. At the end of his Junior year, Jason sought out a way to pay his own way and erase the debt. His farming background and familiarity with heavy machinery helped him land a job as a 'long haul' trucker. The hours were long but the pay was good, and Jason discovered that life on the road suited him. His experiences with his scholarship had formed a deep rooted distrust for 'institutions' and 'politics'. In time he paid off his debts, bought his own rig, and enjoyed the independence that being self employed brought him. Eventually McAllister formed a loose alliance with several other independent truckers, forming Rolling Steel Transport. Based in Fort Worth Texas, RST and the group of maverick drivers that composed it became Jason's 'family away from home' and had a direct influence in bringing 'J-Mac' to the squared circle. At an RST shareholders meeting (read beer-drinking and BS session) at Billy Bob's Bar and Grill, the usual rodeo entertainment was being supplanted with an indy wrestling show. Being well into the 5th order of business for the night (read 5th pitcher), Jason noticed that some of the wrestler's moves somewhat reminded him of when he wrestled at Iowa. Offhand he muttered 'I could take those sumbitches'. His friends being the rowdy drunks they were, threw fuel on a lit fire and gathered up 200 dollars to bet against the promoter that Jason could take any man under the fed's banner. The match was set versus the fed's 'Champion'. The champ expected a stumbling drunk, instead he found himself shot on, single-legged and being driven across the mat before he could blink. Jason let the man up for a moment before dumping him with a favorite Greco-Roman throw he'd learned from Dan Gable. The champ was knocked unconscious and counted out as the entire bar roared in approval. That moment and the roar of the crowd hooked McAllister on professional wrestling. He quickly enrolled at a wrestling school and due to his ameteur background was pegged as a 'can't miss' prospect. His instructors nicknamed him 'Highway' both for his driving exploits and for the way he ran over opponents. Jason travelled the indy circuits for several months until he found a place he could call 'home'. Cyberspace Championship Wrestling. CCW's 'come as you are' attitude, no limits philosophy, and cult following appealled to McAllister's independent nature. There were ups and downs, but McAllister's career in CCW was nothing short of spectacular. 2x CCW World Champion 1x CCW Trans-Atlantic Champion 2x CCW Independant Champion 1x CCW Pacific Champion 6x CCW Tag Team Champion (3x w/ John Jupiter, 1x each with Neurosis, Billy Connors, and Tim Inman) 2000: Most Improved Wrestler 2001: Wrestler of the Year CCW Grand Slam Champion/Five Star Champion/Hall of Fame CCW's fortunes turned south as financial difficulties hamstrung the company. Top stars left the fed for greener pastures, further tightening the noose. A risky plan was formed involving an assault on another fed, actions that would secure financing for CCW to rebuild, but the payments weren't made. The cohesion of the assault group diminshed and the results were disasterous. A now weary and disheartened McAllister could only watch as the death knell rang out for CCW. Jason joined Primetime Championship Wrestling for a time, but was put off by what was in his mind an 'institutionalized' approach and 'highly politicized backoffice'. He quickly requested his release and it was granted. McAllister surmised it was time to move on. Jason returned to Iowa, content to live out his remaining days with his wife Kelly, and newborn daughter Emily. His thoughts turned to starting a new passion, building a NASCAR Craftman Truck series team. To raise capital, Jason began giving monthly seminars and wrestling workshops at Core Wrestling School in Tallahassee Florida (Owned by Jason Core and Kyle 'Velocity' Lamen). It was during these sessions that Jason began to refocus on his ameteur background; utilizing a shooting, physical ground attack. McAllister was once again revitalized, and began pushing himself and instructing more, teaching this more rugged style. He was spending more time in Tallahassee instructing, with KVL having been signed to Five Star Wrestling. It was during one of his seminars that he recieved an urgent phone call that brought his world crashing in on him. His wife Kelly was on the phone, blubbering in hysterics. It was completely unintelligible save for three words, 'Emily' and 'not breathing'. Jason rushed home on the first flight available and arrived to find his wife on the living room floor gently rocking the body of their child and whispering softly over and over, 'It's ok, daddy'll be home soon.' The autopsy was inconclusive, and the official cause of death was listed as 'Sudden Infant Death Syndrome' or SIDS. Emily Elizabeth McAllister was only 6 weeks old. Kelly refused to ever live in the house again, perhaps subconciously blaming Jason for what happened because he was gone at the time. In early September she left for her parent's house in Anniston, Alabama. The couple haven't spoken since. Jason grieved in his own stoic way, isolating himself in his home. He thanked well-wishers for their thoughts and prayers, but internally struggled to find a way to make it all right, to regain what he had lost. His decision was to start at the beginning and take it back. Take it all back. With that thought in his mind, he picked up the phone and called Kyle Lamen.
