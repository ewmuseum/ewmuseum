Timo Bolamba Biography Real Name Timotoga Aryno Bolamba Nicknames 'The Samoan Silencer', 'FSW's Samoan Knight', 'The Samoan Ass Kicking Machine', 'The Human Highlight Reel' Hometown Apia, Samoa Birthday 08-28-1974 Height 6'2' Weight 245 Fed Titles FSW World Heavyweight Champion Biography It was a cold and stormy night August the 28th of 1975. My mother was in a great deal of pain, but I was charging through. I was born at exactly 7:34 PM at the Greater Apia Memorial Hospital under the given name of Timotoga Aryno Bolamba, but legend would foretell my face to be remembered by another name…that name was…”The Samoan Silencer”. Ever since I was a young man I can remember wanting to follow in the footsteps of my father, “The Great Samoan” Rufan Bolamba. He wrestled for several years under this guise, mostly with his partner, the legendary Captain Lou Albano. Many a day he would come home when I was a young boy and teach me the gimmicks of wrestling, how to put on a hold, how to wow a crowd, and how to leave the breathless with a move that would warrant a name like “The Human Highlight Reel”. I knew someday I could make it, I knew there would be a day when people made my name once again synonymous with Samoa and great wrestling, but my day was still a long ways off. I began wrestling on the amateur level when I was seven, my father was never more proud of me, but my mother always worried that I would be hurt. Many of the brutes I faced in my day had a great deal of strength over me, but I could win on natural skill and speed. I had several controversial wins that were sparked due to my incredibly unorthodox style. But my style was that nobody had seen before, one that propelled me to success as the years went by, and eventually landed me a full scholarship to The University of Hawaii. While I was there I learned a lot about myself, as well as properly mastering the English language. I met many good looking girls, and I even found one that tickled my fancy enough to sate steadily throughout my stay with the school. Her name was Rachel Lee, she had dusty brown hair, bright blue eyes, and a smile that could melt my heart. But, even as I rode high on a silver lined cloud after winning the national championship for amateur wrestling in my class, tragedy struck me. It was late on a cold night, very cold for the Islands when I received the call from my friend Jim Stevens. He told me that she had been in a car accident, he told me she was hit by a drunken driver, and killed almost instantly. I don’t remember hearing him tell me the mans name, but I do remember finding him in that police station. He looked as if he was already dead, and with good reason, the future would hold a spot for him in my heart, as well as a rage that would never quite subside. I was heartbroken. The loss of Rachel would slowly drive me mad, but not crazy mad. It brought out a certain violent streak in me that could only be subsided by one thing. Packing up my bags late one night , Jim came to me and we discussed joining Independent Fed’s. He and I agreed no matter where we went, we would always work together, and in a way, we always have. We reigned terror on our competiton as “The Tiki Kid” and “Jagged” Jim Stevens, but titles always eluded our grasp. Still the anarachy and blood that ensued from these many years fed my rage, and brought forth a wicked streak that would bring me to drive men through burning tables, hit them with barb wire wrapped bats, and the now famous flaming “Tiki Torch”. I watched men bleed, and I bled myself, I could feel everything being released in those days, everything fed me and kept me strong. Imagine, twenty years old and running the game they called Professional Wrestling. My life wore on, and so did my body, but just as before, tragedy was always just around the corner. One night I was caught outside of the ring, and Jim was knocked off the top rope by a newcomer looking to make a name for himself by taking out a bigger name. He landed on his neck wrong, and now Jim can no longer walk, all thanks to one fools carelessness. I cant remember hurting the man, but I do remember the referee’s pulling me off him as his nose flowed crimson onto the mat and the rhythmic chants of the fans drove me on. I was like a man possessed, and with good reason. I had to watch my best friend’s career end first hand, and not be able to stop it even though I saw it all unfold before my eyes. It drove me into retirement, where I encountered the worst years of my life. I had moved out to Los Angeles shortly after his accident, and I blamed myself mostly. I lived with several losers and degenerates, in a run down rat infested room that nobody ever came to. I worked at a cannery making minimum wage, and in my offtime I had discovered something else, I had discovered the drug Heroin. At first I could take small bits, but then it began to take control of me, making me feel like I needed it more and more, until I spent all I had on the drug. It had totally consumed me, and my life. I had not talked to my mother, my father, or even Jim, and the darkened spiral slowly spun faster, my only hope was for release. I bought a cord of rope from the neighborhood hardware store with my last few dollars and went home to a rickety chair and a bar hanging from the ceiling for me to work out on. As I looked around and fed my neck through the noose, hoping that it would all be over soon. I remembered looking to the window, and thinking about everything I had accomplished and how it had all slipped away, then the chair, which was always quite rickety, broke under my squirmings, and I fell before I could stop myself. I awoke several hours later to find the bar ripped out of the ceiling and a nasty gash on my forehead where it had hit. I looked about the room and eventually stood, finding a note I believe to have wrote on a heroin induced binge, but I will never know. “Timo, you have changed so much. The man I loved is nearly gone, forgive him, let it go, you can be the greatest warrior ever if you just let it go.” It was signed Rachel. I stood up and walked to my telephone, I dialed the numbers slowly and my voice cracked as he picked up the phone. I had but one thing to say to him, and he had but one thing to say to me. “Father, I am ready to come home.” “I think that’s the smartest thing you have ever said my boy.” In several short months, my body felt better than ever, I was in even better condition than when I was in college! I was a growing man now, and my workout regimine knew no boundaries. I would work from before the sun came up to when it left the great Samoan sky. I was bound and determined to make something of my life again, but it all needed to be brought together, and that’s where my mentor came in. Edward “Domino” Dimitri came to my house with the connections of my father and the first thing he taught me is that in life, size does not matter. It’s the will to win, the heart of a champion that will bring a mans destiny out. I quickly learned many moves from him, and I even learned how to compete on a level I didn’t even know existed. Sure I was good as “The Tiki Kid” but god was I better as my new image came about. Domino taught me the move I have used ever since as the end all be all of a match, his own move called “The Fist of The Heavens”. Many people don’t understand my adaptation of it, so I will explain it for everyone. In the early days when Domino was quite young like me, he would use this move, but upon coming down, he would hit his opponent in the chest with his fist. It was a striking blow that knocked them senseless with the impact, and it was effective, to say the least. There was one downfall though, and that was in the summer of his best year, he came down too hard and split the sternum of his opponent, a man named Frank Gillis. After that he had no longer any use for the move, and was not agile enough any more to adapt it. With my legs and strong body, we came up with a new way to use it, and that was finished with a leg drop. It even proved more effective in the ring than the original! I have since won many matches with this maneuver. We even devised a way to use a chair across the body to create even more damage! It was a great time to be me, and the times, well they just got better. I had cleaned up my act, and now had another love, cars, something that would take me through a lot of good times, but that wasn’t the end of my story… I received a call from a man named Freddy Ryder wanting me to be in a little upstart fed called Extreme Wrestling Alliance, and I figured, what the hell, I need the experience. So with my new found skills, and the good wishes of my family, I set out to forge a new destiny. Little did I know where it would take me. EWA lasted a while, but sadly it folded on me, but not before I had the time to shoot a couple commercials for OFF Citronella Tiki Torches. I loved doing camera work, and it followed me through my career. I took my leave of EWA and Fred Ryder, going to a federation known as Primetime Championship Wrestling, where I was really going to show them my stuff. I remember thinking as I walked into the building for the first time and seeing then Continental Champion Chris Sloboda, that I was in WAY over my head, but I met someone there who changed my life. By complete mistake, and on the whim of a Boda joke, I had walked into the females locker room, and there I met Gwenneth Cross, the true love of my life. We quickly formed a bond that made us inseperable, and with confidence flowing out of my ears I went forward to face perhaps the defining point of my career, I went on to face two legends, Kremmen Von Smackdown and Tyler Nelson. My partner Kramer Wild Wing and I were expected to lose…badly, but we didn’t, in fact we gave the two legends such a run that in the end they had to resort to shocking me with a cattle prod to win! Can you imagine that? Tyler Nelson and Kremmen cheating to beat a rookie in his third match? On top of it all, I had wrestled earlier in the night and won the right to be #1 contender to the Rising Star title by beating long time nemesis Akiyama. I went on to indeed gain a victory over the greatest Rising Star Champion Rent-a-Hero, and become Samoa’s first PCW Champion! Not only that, but thanks to the good grace of another legend, Frisco, I won it back and became one of two men to have held the Rising Star title twice! Not too shabby, if you ask me. Along the way I got a new surprise, I had become incredibly wealthy from those commercials, you know the ones with the Tiki Torches, and I was literally rolling in money. I started several foundations for those less fortunate than me, and all the while, the offers for new products just kept coming in. Things were really rolling, and just about that time, I was apporached by Jeremy Howard, Ed, and Miyagawa. They wanted me to join them in their pursuit of winning the coveted Alliances Pay Per View, and who was I to say no? With perhaps the greatest performance of my career, I went into Alliances with three matches. I won the PCW Television Title in the Stairway to Hell match, I was the last man in the way of Alan Kriegman in the Soda Endorsement match, and yes, my team and I won the all coveted Alliances. It was one magical night that was followed by the single biggest mistake of my career. You see, I was approached by a group of enterprising young men that thought we needed a push in the company, and if we couldn’t get one, we would take one. Jeremy was going to give me the bye on the television title, I just knew it. I don’t know why I did it, call it ego, call it greed, but I did it. I stabbed him in the back and turned my back on the fans blindly. So along with Snow, Corey Williams, and Chris Chase, we formed The New Breed. Several months went by and we did indeed dominate the competition on several occasions. I had a plane accident I don’t care to talk about, and Snow won the title, but not all was right. I knew he had cheated to get it, and I decided enough was enough, our rifts had grown and it was time I stood up again for what was right. In a single eye opening moment, he hit me with the belt when my back was turned, and forever changed my career. He had turned on me, just as I had turned on Jeremy, and I now knew what I had done. I would never go back to the wrong side again. My efforts were redoubled, and I fought hard to stave off all comers, not always winning, but I always gave the fans a good show. I never won that title off of Jason, but I did partake in the most fulfilling time of my career, taking in a protégé by the name of Afwa Kilmanjaro, and training him. He learned so fast I couldn’t believe it, but changed were brewing, Tyler Nelson had his grip on me, and I had an offer I just couldn’t refuse. OSW loomed for The Samoan Silencer, but after a brief stint in which I faced Sebastion Toomes, or as he likes to be called, Sea-Bass, I heard about an eruption on the small Samoan island of Kummonnawannalaya. I knew it was my duty to go, and I left OSW, retiring. Now the help has been done, and there is just enough room for a new legend to be born…watch out world because….I’m BAAAAAAAACK!!!
