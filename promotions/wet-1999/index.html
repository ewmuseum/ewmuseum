A ten-handler committee-run federation that ran a European-set weekly card and a flash interview show through the summer of 1999, folding while its own first World Heavyweight Title tournament was still in progress. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Wrestling's Eminently Talented was founded by a ten-person booking committee, formed on IRC and announced on 11 June 1999 as a “traditional e-fed” despite its own acronym — explicitly not a parody promotion, the founding post insists, whatever the chants of “WET! WET! WET!” might suggest. A follow-up post the next day set WET in England, running most of its cards out of a home arena in Manchester, with Wembley Stadium pictured as its own eventual home for an annual dated show, in the manner of a real promotion using a marquee venue for its biggest night.

Twenty singles roster spots were filled by open battle royal ahead of WET's debut card, “First Penetration” (27 June 1999), which seeded a seven-entrant World Heavyweight Championship tournament. WET ran a weekly television card, “Saturday Night Splash,” called by Michael Cardinale and Richard Siegel, opening each broadcast behind a run of house in-fiction stipulations from its own executives — including a running mascot, the WET Beaver.

The title tournament ran into the summer. A four-way semifinal, held 31 July 1999 between “Love Machine” Sean Cox, “Hardball” Dan Edwards, Kuba Rico, and Trevor Copelund, was thrown into chaos when Cox, Edwards, and Rico brawled through the match locked in a private feud from the week before; Copelund, left alone for most of the bout, capitalized with a top rope leg drop to steal the pin and advance to the tournament's final round. Earlier the same broadcast, a debuting heel, “Devious” Devon Case, marked his arrival by urinating on rival Kuba Rico's car in the arena parking lot before going on to face Shotgun Messiah.

WET announced a pay-per-view, “Getting Our Feet WET,” for 29 August 1999, with Copelund's tournament final billed for the main event slot; no broadcast of that show survives, and no document after the 31 July card survives either. No closure statement survives in WET's own record, and whether that pay-per-view, or the World Heavyweight Championship tournament itself, was ever completed isn't known.
