Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Revolution E-Wrestling staged its own debut the week of 30 August 2001 at a Boston venue it called the Revolution Warehouse, where its own recap counted “over 2,500 people... on hand” for the first television taping of its weekly show, Warriors — billed in that same recap as “the debut of ‘Sweetness’ Stevey Love's new promotion.”

That first week's own record staged a tag team called The Foreign Policy, Tommy Kaz and Paulo Sanchez, escalating a brawl with Scott Oakley and Shadowflare across two matches and a beatdown that left all four laid out; a third man, “Regulator” Izzy G, attacked a rookie named Troy Gentry unprompted, explaining only “because he can,” and was named special referee for the rematch to come. A house show in Reading, Massachusetts the same week debuted four more wrestlers — Solitaire, Chris Bevel, Alexander Dreamwalker, and Zane Alexander — and staged the promotion's first Old School match, won by Izzy G over an opponent billed as “Cool Daddy Dave.”

Its own F.A.Q., posted 14 September 2001, described a message-board “open book” role-play federation with no official rankings, minimal in-ring rules (“no countout and no DQ except at the referee's judgment”, while insisting it was “NOT a hardcore federation”), and a staff it said carried “around 15 years” of combined e-wrestling experience. Warriors aired Mondays in a commentary-and-recap format built for syndication, backed by a full slate of house shows, pay-per-views every six to eight weeks, and occasional live Supercards. Three championships ran the promotion: the Revolution Championship as its top belt, an Old School Championship defended under old-rules bouts complete with countouts and disqualifications, and the Revolution Tag Team Championships.

The same day, a companion post opened the Gold Rush, an eight-match single-elimination tournament beginning 24 September 2001 to crown the first Revolution Champion, “under a month to go until our first PPV,” signed by the promotion's owner, “Sweetness” Stevey Love.

By 30 October 2001, ReWF reported that its first pay-per-view had run that weekend and that “the first ever Revolution Champion was crowned on this wild night,” after two months of action, with its roster still open to new applicants.

A further update on 4 December 2001 said the promotion, which it dated back to that August, was “still going strong” under “an expeirenced and dedicated staff,” with a few roster spots left. That post is the last document ReWF's own record carries; who won the Gold Rush and became its first Revolution Champion is not named in any surviving document, and how or when the promotion ended isn't in the surviving record.
