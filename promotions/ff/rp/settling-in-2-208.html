Settling in. 2. Marcellus Cosell March 31, 2003 Downtown “Rent’s $700, due by the first of the month,” said the slimy, hefty, receding man known as Paul as he unlocked a faded and splintered door that was labelled “8A.” The floorboards cracked under his weight as he stepped through the doorway and led his potential tenant into the apartment. “How’s she look to you?” Marcellus Cosell surveyed his surroundings, noting the filth on the floor beneath him. In one corner of the room was a bed with one broken leg and a severely stained mattress. Spray painted on the walls were several obscenities, the mildest of which would make a hardened murderer cringe. On one side of the room was a small kitchen that was just large enough to hold a oven and a refrigerator, the door of which was completely off the hinges. Marcellus took a stroll around the one room apartment, taking notice of the cobwebs that hung dangerously low from the ceiling and the fist-sized holes in the plaster walls. He peered into the bathroom and saw the grime covering the inside of the bathtub and the sink. He had a feeling that if he were to turn one of the sinks on, the substance that came out would hardly bear a resemblance to water.
