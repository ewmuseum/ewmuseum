Erasing the past and creating the future.1 Jamie Pierce February 10, 2003 New Jersey Throughout history, kings were deemed as powerful people that held the greatest riches. You either sided with them, or you could get your fucking head chopped off. I don’t know about you, but I’d choose the first, but other people were different. Some people were taken by jealousy or the quest for righteousness and attempted to overthrow the king. A stupid act if there ever was one…but maybe not. You see, because a man can’t always be powerful, he can always have jealousy. It was funny how much my life changed in two weeks. When I signed the Flatlined Fighting contract, articles printed and comedians started working on their acts. Nobody knew where I headed with the idea, but it was unintentionally headed towards one of biggest jokes of the century. A rockstar signing with a fighting promotion sounded like a Saturday morning cartoon gone wrong, which even left me feeling uneasy about the whole deal. But it had gone over better than expected. What was expected was a huge sum of money lost, my image ruined, and the band having issues. I doubted they heard anything about the contract, but sooner or later they would hear about it. It was only a matter of time before they did, but right then I could care less. And so I let the escalator bring me to the next level with some extra cash in my pocket. The money wasn’t what was important. It was fact that I had a slight bruise forming that got me excited about the whole deal. I hadn’t wasted the past week drinking, instead I spent the week doing something I was made for. Fighting is in my blood, it’s something I wish was never taken away after the death of my Grandfather. People rushing by with suitcases stared as long as they could before they disappeared into the crowds. I swiped my hand away from the bruise and snapped back to reality. Losing myself in thought is something occasional and usually gets me stares in public places, if I don’t get them because of my fame already. The upstairs to the airport came into the view as the escalator was reaching the top. I stepped off and walked between the lines of people hurrying with baggage. Other people got in the way, standing in the middle of the hallways for some reason not known, but they were quickly passed. Today I had to go back to see my wife, and I wouldn’t be missing the flight home. ”Hey you fuckin’ pussy!” At least, if I could help it. A man in his early twenties approached me from the side, walking in line with me as I went. His hair was pressed down in black strands, hanging messily halfway on his forehead. His shirt had a Chinese symbol on it that covered his whole shirt in its entirety and his black cargo pants slid against the ground past his shoes. The man had an ear ring and a nose ring, and if I weren't me then I’d say he looked threatening. But unfortunately for him, I happened to be me that day. This had happened countless times before. Media gets guy to piss celebrity off, guy pisses celebrity off, celebrity punches guy who pissed him off…media gets a story. I looked around for the cameras stationed around me, in order to see where the flashes would come from as soon as I landed a punch, but couldn’t see any. I figured he wore one on him. ”Fuck off.” I mumbled, headed towards the boarding gate. His pace only increased. I looked him in the face only for a second. “I’m not falling for it.” Then immediately set off again. Hopefully if I kept at the pace I was going at then I’d be in California by 12:30, but if I didn’t, then I might have to grab a taxi and stay in a hotel for another night. And I wanted desperately to be home that night. He taunted me while I stared ahead. “You think you’re so fuckin’ tough, huh? Big rockstar fighting, well sorry to say it, but all your fights are scripted! You pussy, having to use your money in order to win!” It didn’t matter what he was saying. Whatever he said was bullshit and that was all I needed to know. I didn’t bother to look towards him again, but after hearing his aggravating voice talking into my ear, I turned around. Knocked the fuck out? No. Instead, a handshake. I patted the man on the back like we were old friends and waved towards the cameras. I showcased a perfect smile and then headed towards the boarding docks, uninterrupted. The man stood shaking his head, in disbelief over what had happened, but the point was that he stood in place. I sat down and waited for my plane seat to be called for. It wouldn’t be long until I’d finally be in California at last. And it wouldn’t be long until the king would fall.
