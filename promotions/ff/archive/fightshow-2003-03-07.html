Wake Up! 8:00 AM Atlantic City, New Jersey With a week’s break from work, the Flatlined staff and personnel were finally able to reunite with their families. It was a bittersweet couple of days for most. Finally, they were able to relax, but try as they may to enjoy their mini-vacation from laboring for the Flatlined CEO Jared Davidson, they couldn’t put their mind at rest. They knew that they couldn’t stay comfortable for long. For next week meant another fightSHOW, and another six months or so of traveling on the road, away from the security of living a life of normalcy at home with their family. Amanda Ling was much higher on the Flatlined food chain, but felt that she may be working herself too hard to make the Lightweight Tournament a success. Her week break wasn’t much of a break at all. In fact, she had never worked harder in her life. In an attempt to mask her slothfulness on Day 1 of 3 of the Lightweight Tournament, she set her alarm clock in the hotel early. It was a much better option for her to spare another two hours of sleep to powder away the unflattering lines that cracked her face. She needed to remain as professional as possible. Sacrificing a few hours of rest was the way to go. *BEEP BEEEP BEEEEP BEEEEEP* As her alarm clock echoed repetitiously, rattling its high-pitched rings in her eardrums, Amanda Ling awoke unpleasantly, and on the wrong side of the bed. Her palm was faced down and ready to slam on the snooze button, but as she turned to do so, her eyes took a glimpse at the alarm clock, and she shot out of bed in an instant. “Shit, not enough time…” This was a big day for Amanda. Today, she would be free of the guilt of seeing a problem, and doing nothing in her power to fix it. Tonight, she will be liberated. But for now, she has a lot of work to do… Fighting Stress [1] “Didn’t think you would show up. Thought you would be busy. You know, with work.” Ah, the sound of sarcasm from a woman. Could anything be better? Well, no. For Ijinza Yebanka, not at all. This whole situation was his fault, yes, but he could do without the sardonic comments and such. He had a fight to compete in. His mind needed to be at absolute ease. Fat chance of that happening, with a furious Fejona Min sitting in his locker-room, the fingers of her right hand drumming against the wooden table. Looking at her, nobody would guess she was incensed. Red t-shirt, tight off-white jeans and black shoes; Fejona was dressed very casually. As if to indicate that she was totally at ease with everything that was going on. “Okay, I deserve that…” Ijinza answered as he closed the door to the locker-room and placed his duffel bag down on the ground. Min snorted at the reply, before folding her arms and crossing her legs. She really was, for the lack of a better word, pissed. “Yeah. What happened on Monday? Huh?” she shot back coldly. Ijinza stood there, not really expecting a direct approach from Fejona. Sure, he had known her for many years now. But then again, women… they're unpredictable, right? “Fejona, I am very sorry. I’ve had a lot of things to do, and fighting stress is never easy. But I know I’m ready. Not a shot on the training, but… I FEEL ready!” Luke explained as he walked towards Fejona, who was listening intently. And she wasn’t really liking what she was hearing. Standing up, Fejona gave Ijinza a glare that spoke a million words, before storming out of the room. Yebanka frowned as he turned to see his cousin slamming the door of the locker-room shut. Realizing that he could do nothing about the situation with Min, Ijinza sat on the floor and opened up his duffel bag. Conflicts had arisen, but he had to get prepared. His pride was at stake. The Child The young man, barely an adult - The Child arrived at the arena for the first time. People didn’t stop. People didn’t stare. People save a few young women in the hallways who wouldn’t believe he was a fighter anyway, didn’t point and giggle. He was not focused on an upcoming fight. He did not even care to do battle on that his night of new beginnings. Esentially he wanted to look and learn and grow just a bit more, maybe enjoy being on the outside of conflict and marvel at the spectacle of two warriors engaged for once. Tonight for the first time, immersement could be substituted – pushed aside for a rare moment of sheer entertainment. Unlike Montgomery Benson who was going to attract attention wherever he went, The Child was invisible compared to the countenance of his new fellows… And why not? At first breath, that hand of shrouds was creations dealing to him, - it is what he knows and what he wants to discard. But tonight he will play by his personal rules and remain unknown because the rules will change. The Child knows that in another 7 days, the situation will be different and he will be at an arena expecting pain and blood and adrenaline and life to flow through every pore and vessel while he shows miracles to the world. He will oppose what was intended for him and expose the rebellion within that is the Rage Machine, the very heart and skill of a superhuman martial artist. Yes, this is no mere child…. but the ever underestimated Child of the Wild West. Fighting Stress [2] The time of reckoning was moments away. Struck awkwardly by the sudden silence that broke among them, Ling began with the small talk once again. “So what are you looking for in there?” “I was… uh…” Xyler spoke slowly as his insecurities about his condition surfaced through the pitch in his voice. Xyler Vayin had a case of schizophrenia. He saw things that weren’t there… some images recurring, others not. It scared him to be consciously aware that he was a prisoner of his own mind, but what scared him more was that he wasn’t normal… and he did his best to prevent that knowledge from spreading to the people around him. In the car, he saw the image of his deceased father, saying to him, “Daddy will be home, son. Daddy will be home.” He knew he wasn’t actually there, but it seemed so tangible, so authentic… so real. “…nothing,” Xyler said aloud. “…nothing at all…” Amanda raised an eyebrow, and jokingly did a mock impression of Xyler, hoping to endear to his humorous side, if at all he had one. “You… uh… nothing, nothing at all?” Xyler didn’t understand the joke. He found it rather insulting, but said nothing about it, suppressing his desire to verbalize his thoughts through a very eerie silence. “You’re not much of a talker, are you?” asked Amanda Ling. “I suppose I’ll leave you alone then.” She left with those parting words, and sped off into the streets in her sleek metallic blue Lexus SC430. “If I’m alone…” whispered Xyler to himself, “then who is that standing there?” He looked off in the distance and saw an Asian male dressed in a black trenchcoat clasp his hands together to form a gun, as he shot rounds of fingerbullets at Amanda Ling’s vehicle below the parking structure. Xyler closed his eyes, and remembered his condition. He had seen many things in his days, but was never sure about what was a figment of his imagination and what was real. He had seen very surreal things, and decided that this was simply one of them. “It’s not real,” Xyler said to himself. “He’s not real.” The Atlantic City nightlife called for him, but he was much too tired to party tonight.
